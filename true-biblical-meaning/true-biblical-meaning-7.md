# True Biblical Meanings — Part 7 (667–777)

> Final installment: what biblical words and expressions originally meant versus modern understanding.

---

## 667. Babylon
- **Modern understanding:** An ancient evil city; symbol of worldly corruption.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *Bavel* (בָּבֶל) — from Akkadian *Bab-ili* (gate of God). In Hebrew wordplay, from *balal* (to confuse).
- **What shifted:** Babylon sees itself as "the gate of God" — the center of civilization and divine access. The Bible renames it "confusion." Revelation's "Babylon" represents any empire that claims to be the gateway to God while actually oppressing and exploiting. It's not a specific future city but a recurring pattern of imperial self-deification.

## 668. Whore of Babylon
- **Modern understanding:** A future evil religion or institution.
- **Original term:** Greek *pornē hē megalē* (πόρνη ἡ μεγάλη) — the great prostitute (Rev. 17:1).
- **What shifted:** Prostitution metaphors in the prophets always describe covenant unfaithfulness — Israel/Judah "prostituting" themselves with foreign gods and empires. The "whore of Babylon" is a political-religious system that sells its loyalty to the highest bidder. For first-century readers, this is Rome (seated on seven hills, Rev. 17:9). The image is of compromised worship serving imperial power.

## 669. Come Out of Her, My People
- **Modern understanding:** Leave sinful institutions; separate from the world.
- **Original term:** Greek *exelthate ex autēs ho laos mou* — come out of her, my people (Rev. 18:4). Echoing Isaiah 48:20, Jeremiah 51:45.
- **What shifted:** The call echoes the Exodus — leaving the empire that enslaves. It's about *disentangling from complicity* with exploitative systems, not physical isolation. You can be geographically inside an empire while refusing its values. The "coming out" is primarily economic and moral, not geographic.

## 670. Maranatha (deeper)
- **Modern understanding:** A worship song title; "come, Lord."
- **Original context:** Paul uses this Aramaic phrase in a Greek letter to a Greek-speaking church (1 Cor. 16:22).
- **What shifted:** Preserving Aramaic in a Greek text means this phrase was so sacred and foundational that translation would diminish it. Like keeping "amen" and "hallelujah" untranslated. It connects Greek-speaking believers back to the original Aramaic-speaking community of Jesus. It's a linguistic bridge to the source.

## 671. New Heaven and New Earth
- **Modern understanding:** God destroying everything and creating from scratch.
- **Original terms:** Greek *ouranon kainon kai gēn kainēn* — a new/renewed heaven and a new/renewed earth (Rev. 21:1). *Kainos* = renewed in quality.
- **What shifted:** *Kainos* (renewed quality) not *neos* (brand new in time). The promise is transformation, not replacement. Like a renovated house — same structure, completely renewed. Romans 8:21 says creation will be "set free from its bondage to corruption" — liberated, not destroyed.

## 672. Behold, I Make All Things New
- **Modern understanding:** Everything is replaced with new things.
- **Original term:** Greek *idou kaina poiō panta* — behold, I am making all things new (Rev. 21:5).
- **What shifted:** Present tense — "I am making" — not future. The renewal is in process *now*, not only at the end. And "all things new" not "all new things." Every existing thing is being made new. Nothing is discarded — everything is transformed. The scope is universal and the process is ongoing.

## 673. Throne Room (Revelation 4-5)
- **Modern understanding:** A vision of heaven; God on a fancy throne.
- **Original context:** The description echoes the Holy of Holies — cherubim, lampstand, sea of glass (laver), incense (prayers), twenty-four elders.
- **What shifted:** John sees the heavenly *temple* — the original of which the earthly temple was a copy. The heavenly reality that the earthly worship system pointed toward. This is not a king's court but a cosmic temple where all creation worships. The throne room is the ultimate Holy of Holies.

## 674. Four Living Creatures
- **Modern understanding:** Bizarre angelic beings around God's throne.
- **Original term:** Greek *tessara zōa* (τέσσαρα ζῷα) — four living ones (Rev. 4:6-8). Combining features of Isaiah's seraphim and Ezekiel's cherubim.
- **What shifted:** The four faces — lion, ox, human, eagle — may represent the four domains of creation: wild animals, domesticated animals, humanity, and birds/sky. Creation itself surrounds and worships the Creator. Early church tradition assigned these faces to the four Gospels (Matthew=human, Mark=lion, Luke=ox, John=eagle).

## 675. Twenty-Four Elders
- **Modern understanding:** Heavenly council members; angels.
- **Original context:** Twenty-four elders on thrones wearing crowns (Rev. 4:4). 24 = 12 (tribes) + 12 (apostles).
- **What shifted:** The twenty-four likely represent the complete people of God — Old and New Covenant unified. They wear *stephanos* (victory wreaths) and sit on thrones, suggesting they are redeemed humans, not angels. The whole people of God — Israel and church together — participate in heavenly worship.

## 676. Scroll with Seven Seals
- **Modern understanding:** A mysterious prophecy book; future events.
- **Original context:** Revelation 5:1 — a scroll sealed with seven seals. In Roman legal practice, a will required seven witnesses and seven seals.
- **What shifted:** The scroll may be God's covenant-will — his plan for creation's inheritance. Only the Lamb can open it because only he has the authority to execute God's testament. When the seals open, the inheritance is distributed. The scroll isn't a timetable of disasters — it's a covenant document being enacted.

## 677. Lamb Standing as Though Slain
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus in heaven still showing his wounds.
- **Original term:** Greek *arnion hestēkos hōs esphagmenon* — a lamb standing as having been slaughtered (Rev. 5:6).
- **What shifted:** The Lamb has seven horns (complete power) and seven eyes (complete sight) — yet bears the marks of slaughter. Power and vulnerability coexist permanently. The marks of suffering are not erased but *glorified*. The slain Lamb standing is the ultimate oxymoron — death alive, victim victorious, weakness as the mode of power.

## 678. Worthy Is the Lamb
- **Modern understanding:** A worship song about Jesus's worthiness.
- **Original term:** Greek *axios to arnion* (ἄξιος τὸ ἀρνίον) — worthy is the lamb (Rev. 5:12).
- **What shifted:** *Axios* means worthy, deserving, having the weight/value required. In Roman imperial context, *axios* was shouted at emperor accessions — "worthy!" Declaring the Lamb *axios* is directly counter-imperial: the slaughtered lamb, not Caesar, deserves the acclamation. It's a political loyalty declaration disguised as worship.

## 679. Lion of Judah / Lamb
- **Modern understanding:** Two titles for Jesus; he's both strong and gentle.
- **Original context:** Rev. 5:5-6 — John is told a *lion* has conquered, but when he looks, he sees a *lamb*.
- **What shifted:** The interpretive switch is the key: you're promised a lion but shown a lamb. Power is redefined. Conquering happens through sacrifice, not violence. Every time you expect the lion (military power), you get the lamb (self-giving love). This redefines victory: the lamb's way of conquering *is* the lion's victory.

## 680. Riders (Four Horsemen)
- **Modern understanding:** Future apocalyptic figures bringing global destruction.
- **Original context:** Revelation 6:1-8. White horse (conquest), red horse (war), black horse (economic injustice), pale horse (death).
- **What shifted:** The four horsemen aren't future predictions — they describe the *constant* pattern of imperial violence: conquest leads to war, which causes economic devastation, which produces mass death. This cycle was Rome's reality and remains humanity's reality. The horsemen are a diagnosis of empire, not a forecast of the future.

## 681. Seal of God
- **Modern understanding:** A mark on believers protecting them in the end times.
- **Original term:** Greek *sphragis* (σφραγίς) — a seal, signet, stamp of ownership (Rev. 7:2-3).
- **What shifted:** Sealing echoes Ezekiel 9:4 where the faithful are marked (*tav* — the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet, which in ancient script looked like a cross/X) on their foreheads before judgment. The seal marks ownership and protection. It contrasts with the *charagma* (mark) of the beast — two competing marks of allegiance.

## 682. 144,000
- **Modern understanding:** A literal number of specially saved people.
- **Original term:** Greek *hekatón tesserakonta tessares chiliades* — 144,000 (Rev. 7:4, 14:1).
- **What shifted:** 144,000 = 12 × 12 × 1,000. Twelve (tribes/apostles) squared (complete people of God) times a thousand (innumerable multitude). It's the complete, full, symbolically infinite people of God from all tribes. Immediately after the 144,000 are listed, John sees "a great multitude that no one could count" (7:9) — the same group described two ways.

## 683. Great Multitude
- **Modern understanding:** A different, larger group than the 144,000.
- **Original term:** Greek *ochlos polys* (ὄχλος πολύς) — a great crowd (Rev. 7:9).
- **What shifted:** This multitude is "from every nation, tribe, people, and language" — universal inclusion. They wear white robes and hold palm branches. The 144,000 (Israel's structured completeness) and the great multitude (universal, uncountable inclusion) are likely the same people viewed from two perspectives — organized and innumerable.

## 684. White Robes
- **Modern understanding:** Heavenly clothing; purity.
- **Original term:** Greek *stolas leukas* (στολὰς λευκάς) — white robes (Rev. 7:9).
- **What shifted:** White robes are victory garments — worn in Roman triumphs by the victorious. They're also priestly garments and resurrection garments. The great multitude wears *stolas* — formal, dignified attire, not just white fabric. These are robes of honor, victory, priesthood, and resurrection identity combined.

## 685. Washed in the Blood
- **Modern understanding:** Made clean by Jesus's sacrifice; a washing metaphor.
- **Original term:** Greek *eplynan tas stolas autōn kai eleukanan autas en tō haimati tou arniou* — they washed their robes and whitened them in the blood of the Lamb (Rev. 7:14).
- **What shifted:** Washing in blood to produce whiteness is a deliberately paradoxical image. Blood stains — it doesn't clean. The paradox is the point: the Lamb's death (blood = life released) produces purity (white). Life poured out becomes the cleansing agent. The logic of the Lamb inverts everything.

## 686. War in Heaven
- **Modern understanding:** An ancient cosmic battle between God and Satan.
- **Original context:** Revelation 12:7-9 — Michael and his angels fight the dragon.
- **What shifted:** The dragon is "thrown down" — not destroyed but relocated from heaven to earth. The victory in heaven is won "by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (12:11) — not by military force but by sacrifice and witness. Heavenly war is won through earthly martyrdom. The weapons of cosmic warfare are testimony and self-giving.

## 687. Woman Clothed with the Sun
- **Modern understanding:** Mary; the church; Israel.
- **Original term:** Greek *gynē peribeblemēnē ton hēlion* — a woman wrapped in the sun (Rev. 12:1).
- **What shifted:** She has twelve stars (tribes), stands on the moon, and gives birth to a male child who will rule the nations. She likely represents the people of God (Israel) giving birth to the Messiah. She then flees to the wilderness (echoing Israel's wilderness journey). She's a composite figure — not one woman but the community that bears the Messiah.

## 688. Dragon's Flood
- **Modern understanding:** The devil attacking the church.
- **Original term:** Greek *hydōr hōs potamon* — water like a river (Rev. 12:15). The dragon spews a flood at the woman.
- **What shifted:** The earth *swallows* the flood to protect the woman (12:16) — echoing the earth swallowing Korah's rebels (Num. 16) and the sea swallowing Egypt's army. Creation itself intervenes to protect God's people. The earth is an active participant in the divine drama, not just a stage.

## 689. Babylon Is Fallen
- **Modern understanding:** A future city will be destroyed.
- **Original term:** Greek *epesen epesen Babylōn hē megalē* — fallen, fallen is Babylon the great (Rev. 18:2).
- **What shifted:** The doubled "fallen, fallen" echoes Isaiah 21:9 about historical Babylon's fall. The announcement is in the *aorist tense* — treating the fall as already accomplished. Babylon's destruction is declared as accomplished fact before it visibly occurs. Faith sees the empire's fall before the empire does.

## 690. Merchants Weeping
- **Modern understanding:** People are sad that a city was destroyed.
- **Original context:** Revelation 18:11-17 — merchants weep because "no one buys their cargo anymore." The cargo list includes "bodies and souls of humans" (18:13).
- **What shifted:** The merchants don't weep for the city's people — they weep for lost *profits*. And their cargo includes human beings. Babylon's economic system commodifies humans. The critique is of an economy built on human trafficking and exploitation. The merchants' grief exposes their values — they mourn revenue, not human suffering.

## 691. Come, Lord Jesus
- **Modern understanding:** The final prayer of the Bible; a plea for the second coming.
- **Original term:** Greek *erchou kyrie Iēsou* (ἔρχου κύριε Ἰησοῦ) — come, Lord Jesus (Rev. 22:20). Greek equivalent of *Maranatha*.
- **What shifted:** This is the Bible's last prayer — matching its earliest preserved prayer (*Maranatha*). The scriptures begin with God walking in the garden seeking humanity ("Where are you?" Gen. 3:9) and end with humanity calling to God ("Come!"). The entire biblical narrative is a story of mutual seeking — God looking for us, us calling for God.

## 692. Amen (Revelation's final)
- **Modern understanding:** The end; closing word.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *amen* — firm, established, reliable (Rev. 22:21).
- **What shifted:** The Bible's last word is *amen* — "this stands firm." After all the prophecy, imagery, warning, and promise, the final word is a declaration of reliability. Everything said is *aman* — trustworthy. The whole narrative — from Genesis 1:1 to Revelation 22:21 — is sealed with a declaration of truth.

## 693. Armageddon
- **Modern understanding:** The final world war; global military destruction.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *Har Megiddo* (הַר מְגִדּוֹ) — Mountain of Megiddo (Rev. 16:16).
- **What shifted:** Megiddo is a real place in Israel where many historic battles occurred (Judges 5, 2 Kings 23). But there's no actual *mountain* at Megiddo — it's a plain. "Har Megiddo" may be deliberately impossible geography — a symbolic battle site, not a literal location. The final battle is theological, not geographical.

## 694. Thousand Years (Millennium)
- **Modern understanding:** A literal 1,000-year reign of Christ on earth.
- **Original term:** Greek *chilia etē* (χίλια ἔτη) — a thousand years (Rev. 20:1-6).
- **What shifted:** In a book where every number is symbolic (7 seals, 12 tribes, 24 elders, 144,000, 666), interpreting 1,000 as the one literal number is inconsistent. 1,000 in biblical usage often means "an extremely long time" or "completeness" (Psalm 50:10 — God owns "cattle on a thousand hills," meaning all cattle). Whether this is literal, symbolic, or both has divided Christianity into pre-, post-, and amillennial camps.

## 695. First Resurrection
- **Modern understanding:** Physical resurrection of believers before the millennium.
- **Original term:** Greek *anastasis hē prōtē* — the first resurrection (Rev. 20:5-6).
- **What shifted:** Whether this is physical resurrection, spiritual rebirth, or martyrs' vindication is debated. Those in the "first resurrection" are called "priests of God" who will "reign with Christ." The emphasis is on priestly identity and co-reign, not just returning from death.

## 696. Second Death
- **Modern understanding:** Going to hell; eternal damnation.
- **Original term:** Greek *ho thanatos ho deuteros* — the second death (Rev. 20:14, 21:8).
- **What shifted:** The "second death" is defined as "the lake of fire." Death and Hades (personified) are thrown into it — death itself dies. The second death is the death *of death*. For those who participate in the first resurrection, the second death "has no authority" over them (Rev. 20:6). It's the final end of the power of death.

## 697. Books Opened (Judgment)
- **Modern understanding:** God's record of everyone's sins.
- **Original context:** Revelation 20:12 — books were opened, and another book was opened — the book of life. The dead were judged "by what was written in the books, according to their deeds."
- **What shifted:** There are *two* types of books: the books of deeds and the book of life. Judgment involves both — deeds matter, but the book of life also matters. The book of life (*biblos tēs zōēs*) is the registry of belonging, not a reward ledger. Two criteria coexist: what you did and whose you are.

## 698. Book of Life
- **Modern understanding:** A list of people going to heaven.
- **Original term:** Greek *biblos tēs zōēs* (βίβλος τῆς ζωῆς) — scroll/book of life (Rev. 20:15).
- **What shifted:** The "book of life" appears in Exodus 32:32 (Moses asks to be blotted out), Psalm 69:28, Daniel 12:1, and Philippians 4:3. It's a *citizenship register* — like a city's roll of residents. Being in the book of life is about belonging to God's city/community. It's identity and citizenship, not a reward list.

## 699. New Name
- **Modern understanding:** Getting a new name in heaven.
- **Original context:** Revelation 2:17 — "a white stone with a new name written on it that no one knows except the one who receives it."
- **What shifted:** White stones were used in ancient courts for acquittal (black = guilty, white = innocent). A *new name* represents a new identity known only to God and the recipient — the truest, deepest self that no one else can define. Your deepest identity is between you and God. No one else gets to name you.

## 700. Overcomers (Revelation 2-3)
- **Modern understanding:** Super-Christians who achieve spiritual excellence.
- **Original term:** Greek *ho nikōn* (ὁ νικῶν) — the one conquering/overcoming (Rev. 2:7,11,17,26; 3:5,12,21).
- **What shifted:** 1 John 5:4-5 defines the *nikē* (victory): "this is the victory (*nikē*) that overcomes the world — our faith. Who is it that overcomes? The one who believes Jesus is the Son of God." Overcoming isn't superhuman achievement — it's *faith*. The "overcomer" is simply the one who trusts and persists. The bar is faithfulness, not heroism.

## 701. Tree of Life (Revelation)
- **Modern understanding:** A heavenly tree; paradise restored.
- **Original context:** Revelation 22:2 — the tree of life bears twelve kinds of fruit, producing fruit each month. Its leaves are "for the healing of the *ethnē* (nations)."
- **What shifted:** The tree from which humanity was exiled (Gen. 3:24) is now accessible. It bears fruit *monthly* — perpetual provision, not seasonal scarcity. And its leaves *heal nations* — the ethnic, cultural, and political divisions of humanity are addressed by the tree. The tree that was guarded is now generously open. Full circle.

## 702. Water of Life
- **Modern understanding:** Heavenly refreshment.
- **Original term:** Greek *hydōr zōēs* (ὕδωρ ζωῆς) — water of life (Rev. 22:1,17).
- **What shifted:** The invitation "let anyone who is thirsty come; let anyone who wishes take the water of life as a gift (*dōrean*)" (Rev. 22:17) is the Bible's final invitation. *Dōrean* means "freely, as a gift." The last word of the Bible's narrative (before the closing) is a free invitation. Not a gatekeeping exercise — an open offer.

## 703. Bright Morning Star
- **Modern understanding:** A title for Jesus; a celestial image.
- **Original term:** Greek *ho astēr ho lampros ho prōinos* — the bright morning star (Rev. 22:16). Also 2 Peter 1:19.
- **What shifted:** The morning star appears before dawn — while it's still dark, announcing that dawn is coming. Jesus as the morning star means he's the sign within the darkness that light is on its way. You don't have to wait for full sunrise to know morning is coming — the morning star is proof enough.

## 704. Root and Offspring of David
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus descends from David's family line.
- **Original term:** Greek *rhiza kai to genos Dauid* — root *and* offspring of David (Rev. 22:16).
- **What shifted:** Jesus is both David's *root* (source, origin — he precedes David) and David's *offspring* (descendant — he comes after David). He's simultaneously the ancestor and the descendant. This paradox echoes Jesus's own question: "How can the Messiah be David's son if David calls him Lord?" (Matt. 22:41-46). He's the origin of what he's born from.

## 705. Spirit and the Bride Say Come
- **Modern understanding:** The church invites people to Jesus.
- **Original context:** Revelation 22:17 — "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.' And let the one who hears say, 'Come.'"
- **What shifted:** The invitation multiplies — Spirit, Bride, and then *everyone who hears*. Each hearer becomes an inviter. The invitation is self-propagating: receiving it automatically means extending it. You don't need ordination or authority to say "Come." Hearing the invitation qualifies you to give it.

## 706. Bride of Christ
- **Modern understanding:** The church; Christians collectively.
- **Original term:** Greek *nymphē* (νύμφη) — bride (Rev. 21:9). The city and the bride are identified as the same.
- **What shifted:** The Bride is also the *city* — the New Jerusalem. This means the Bride isn't just people but an entire *civilization* — a whole way of being human together. The marriage of heaven and earth produces a new social reality. The Bride is a community-civilization, not just a collection of individuals.

## 707. Marriage Supper of the Lamb
- **Modern understanding:** A heavenly banquet; a reward feast.
- **Original term:** Greek *deipnon tou gamou tou arniou* — the dinner of the wedding of the Lamb (Rev. 19:9).
- **What shifted:** *Deipnon* is the main meal — a full feast, not a snack. And it's a *wedding feast* — the most joyful celebration in ancient culture. The culmination of history is not a courtroom (judgment) but a *party* (wedding feast). The final image of God's plan is a celebration of love consummated.

## 708. King of Kings
- **Modern understanding:** A title of supreme authority; Jesus is the highest ruler.
- **Original term:** Greek *basileus basileōn* (βασιλεὺς βασιλέων) — king of kings (Rev. 19:16). Also *kyrios kyriōn* — lord of lords.
- **What shifted:** "King of Kings" was a title used by Persian and Roman emperors. Revelation applies it to Jesus at his return — written on his robe and thigh. The claim is directly counter-imperial: every king who claims ultimate authority is subordinate to this king. No political power is ultimate.

## 709. Sword from His Mouth
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus with a weapon; ready for battle.
- **Original term:** Greek *rhomphaia* (ῥομφαία) — a large broadsword, proceeding from his mouth (Rev. 19:15).
- **What shifted:** The sword comes from his *mouth* — it's his *word*. Jesus's weapon is speech, not metal. He conquers by proclamation, not violence. The rider on the white horse (Rev. 19:11-16) wins through the "sword" of divine declaration. The most powerful force in the universe is God's spoken word — the same power that created everything.

## 710. Robe Dipped in Blood
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus covered in enemies' blood from battle.
- **Original term:** Greek *himation bebammenon haimati* — a garment dipped/dyed in blood (Rev. 19:13). The blood is on the robe *before* the battle.
- **What shifted:** The blood is already on the robe before any battle begins — suggesting it's his *own* blood (from the cross), not enemies' blood. The warrior-king goes into the final battle already wearing the marks of his own sacrifice. His weapon is his word; his battle dress is his own shed blood. Victory through self-sacrifice, not violence.

## 711. True and Faithful Witness
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus is truthful and reliable.
- **Original term:** Greek *ho martys ho pistos kai alēthinos* — the witness who is faithful and true (Rev. 3:14).
- **What shifted:** *Martys* (witness/martyr) + *pistos* (faithful/trustworthy) + *alēthinos* (true/genuine/real). Jesus is the authentic witness — the one whose testimony costs everything and is therefore completely credible. His credibility comes from his willingness to die for his testimony. The witness authenticates himself through sacrifice.

## 712. Conquer (Revelation)
- **Modern understanding:** Military victory; defeating enemies.
- **Original term:** Greek *nikaō* (νικάω) — to conquer, overcome, prevail.
- **What shifted:** Every conquering in Revelation is accomplished through the Lamb's method — sacrifice, testimony, faithfulness unto death (Rev. 12:11). The word *nikaō* is redefined by context: conquering = dying faithfully. The Lamb conquers by being slain. The saints conquer by testifying even to death. Victory is inverted.

## 713. Throne Shared
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus sits on a throne in heaven.
- **Original context:** Revelation 3:21 — "The one who conquers, I will grant to sit with me on my throne, just as I also conquered and sat with my Father on his throne."
- **What shifted:** The throne is *shared* — Jesus shares the Father's throne, then shares his throne with overcomers. Authority cascades downward through sharing, not hoarding. The pattern is: the Father shares with the Son, the Son shares with the faithful. Power in God's economy multiplies by distribution.

## 714. Eating from the Tree of Life
- **Modern understanding:** Eternal life in heaven.
- **Original context:** Revelation 2:7 — "To the one who conquers I will give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the paradise of God."
- **What shifted:** Eating from the tree was forbidden after the fall (Gen. 3:22-24). Now it's *given* as a promise. The original prohibition ("lest you eat and live forever" in a fallen state) is reversed because the eating is now in restored relationship. The tree isn't dangerous anymore because the eater is renewed. Access restored.

## 715. Hidden Manna
- **Modern understanding:** Secret spiritual food; special divine provision.
- **Original term:** Greek *tou manna tou kekrymmenou* — the hidden manna (Rev. 2:17).
- **What shifted:** Tradition held that Jeremiah hid the ark, the manna pot, and the incense altar before Babylon's destruction (2 Maccabees 2:4-8), to be revealed when God gathers his people again. "Hidden manna" connects to this tradition — the messianic age will restore what was hidden. Secret provision preserved through exile.

## 716. Pillar in the Temple
- **Modern understanding:** A stable position in heaven.
- **Original term:** Greek *stylon en tō naō* — a pillar in the temple (Rev. 3:12). "They will never go out of it."
- **What shifted:** A pillar is structural — it holds the building up. Overcomers don't just live in the temple; they *support* it. They're load-bearing members of God's dwelling. And "never go out" reverses the Genesis 3 expulsion. The one expelled from the garden-temple becomes a permanent structural element of the eternal temple.

## 717. Hot, Cold, Lukewarm (Laodicea)
- **Modern understanding:** Be passionate (hot) not indifferent (lukewarm); cold is bad.
- **Original context:** Laodicea had no natural water supply. Hot water came from nearby Hierapolis (hot springs — medicinal). Cold water came from Colossae (refreshing). Laodicea's piped water arrived *lukewarm* and mineral-laden — nauseating.
- **What shifted:** Hot (healing) and cold (refreshing) are *both good*. The problem with lukewarm isn't temperature but *uselessness* — tepid, mineral-filled water that makes you sick. The critique is about being *useful* vs. *useless*, not passionate vs. dispassionate.

## 718. I Stand at the Door and Knock
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus wanting to enter your heart for salvation.
- **Original context:** Revelation 3:20 is addressed to *the Laodicean church* — believers who have pushed Jesus outside.
- **What shifted:** This isn't an evangelistic invitation to non-believers — it's a rebuke of a church that has locked Jesus out. The image is startling: the Lord of the church stands *outside his own church* knocking to get in. The issue isn't initial conversion but ongoing intimacy. A church can be active yet have Christ outside.

## 719. Name Written on Foreheads
- **Modern understanding:** God's mark on believers in heaven.
- **Original context:** Revelation 22:4 — "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads."
- **What shifted:** Seeing God's face (*panim*) and bearing God's name (*shem*) on the forehead reverses every previous limitation. Moses couldn't see God's face. The high priest bore God's name on a plate on his forehead. Now *all* God's people see his face and bear his name. Universal priesthood fully realized. Every believer is the high priest.

## 720. They Will Reign Forever
- **Modern understanding:** Christians will have authority in heaven.
- **Original term:** Greek *basileusousin eis tous aiōnas tōn aiōnōn* — they will reign into the ages of the ages (Rev. 22:5).
- **What shifted:** The vocation given in Genesis 1:26-28 — to reign over creation — is finally fulfilled. Humanity's original assignment (image-bearing dominion) is restored and extended into eternity. The whole story has been about getting humanity to the place where they can properly exercise the dominion they were always meant to have.

## 721. Worship (Revelation)
- **Modern understanding:** Singing praise songs in heaven forever.
- **Original context:** Revelation's worship scenes involve proclamation, prostration, casting crowns, and declaring God's worth and deeds.
- **What shifted:** Worship in Revelation is *political declaration* — asserting who truly rules. "Worthy is the Lamb" counters "worthy is Caesar." Every worship scene is a counter-imperial loyalty oath. Heavenly worship isn't escapist singing — it's the most politically subversive activity possible: declaring that the real king is not the one the world recognizes.

## 722. Elijah Must Come First
- **Modern understanding:** Elijah will literally return before the end times.
- **Original context:** Malachi 4:5 — "I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great day of the LORD." Jesus says John the Baptist was this Elijah (Matt. 11:14, 17:12-13).
- **What shifted:** Jesus identifies John as the fulfillment of the Elijah prophecy — not literal Elijah but one operating in Elijah's spirit and role. Prophecy is fulfilled through pattern and function, not necessarily literal reappearance. The "Elijah" is whoever does what Elijah did: calling people back to covenant faithfulness.

## 723. Moses and Elijah (Transfiguration)
- **Modern understanding:** Two important OT figures appearing with Jesus.
- **Original context:** Moses represents Torah (the Law) and Elijah represents the Prophets — the two divisions of authoritative scripture.
- **What shifted:** Torah and Prophets appear with Jesus and then vanish, leaving "Jesus only" (Mark 9:8). The entire witness of scripture converges on and gives way to Jesus. The Law and Prophets don't disappear — they fulfill their purpose by pointing to him and then stepping back so he can be seen clearly.

## 724. Listen to Him (Transfiguration)
- **Modern understanding:** God telling the disciples to obey Jesus.
- **Original term:** Greek *akouete autou* (ἀκούετε αὐτοῦ) — hear/listen to him (Mark 9:7). Echoing Deuteronomy 18:15 — "a prophet like me... *listen to him*."
- **What shifted:** God's voice from the cloud directly quotes the Moses promise of a future prophet. The declaration identifies Jesus as the *prophet like Moses* whom Deuteronomy predicted. And it comes at the moment Moses himself is present, transferring authority. Moses predicted someone; God says "here he is — listen to *him* now."

## 725. Transfiguration Booths
- **Modern understanding:** Peter randomly suggests building shelters.
- **Original context:** Peter says "let us make three *skēnai* (tents/booths)" — referencing the Feast of Tabernacles (*Sukkot*).
- **What shifted:** *Sukkot* commemorated wilderness dwelling and anticipated the messianic age when God would tabernacle with his people permanently. Peter recognizes the messianic moment and wants to *institutionalize* it — build permanent structures for the event. The response? A cloud (God's mobile presence) overshadows them. God resists being architecturally contained.

## 726. Temptation of Jesus (Stones to Bread)
- **Modern understanding:** The devil tempting Jesus to use power selfishly.
- **Original context:** "If you are the Son of God, command these stones to become bread" (Matt. 4:3).
- **What shifted:** The temptation echoes Israel's wilderness grumbling for bread. Jesus is the new Israel, retesting Israel's tests and passing where they failed. His response ("Man does not live by bread alone but by every word from God's mouth") directly quotes Deuteronomy 8:3 — Moses's teaching about manna. Jesus relives Israel's story and gets it right.

## 727. Pinnacle of the Temple
- **Modern understanding:** The devil testing Jesus's trust in God.
- **Original context:** "Throw yourself down" from the temple's highest point (Matt. 4:5-6).
- **What shifted:** This temptation is about *spectacular display* — using religion for dramatic self-promotion. The devil quotes Psalm 91 (God will protect you) — twisting scripture for self-serving purposes. Jesus's response: "Do not test God." Using God's promises as a performance stunt turns faith into manipulation.

## 728. Kingdoms of the World
- **Modern understanding:** The devil offers Jesus political power.
- **Original context:** "All the kingdoms of the world and their glory — all this I will give you if you worship me" (Matt. 4:8-9).
- **What shifted:** The implication is that the world's kingdoms *are* the devil's to give — political power systems belong to the adversary's domain. Jesus refuses to acquire the kingdoms through allegiance to the wrong power. He'll receive them, but through the cross, not through compromise. The path to authority is through suffering, not shortcuts.

## 729. It Is Written
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus quoting Bible verses in argument.
- **Original term:** Greek *gegraptai* (γέγραπται) — it has been written (and remains written — perfect tense).
- **What shifted:** *Gegraptai* is perfect tense — what was written in the past remains authoritative in the present. When Jesus says "it is written," he's not just citing a verse — he's appealing to a permanently standing authority. The word written in the past has never stopped being in effect. Scripture is present-tense authority from a past-tense writing.

## 730. Fulfill the Law
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus obeyed all the rules so we don't have to.
- **Original term:** Greek *plērosai* (πληρῶσαι) — to fill up, to bring to fullness, to complete (Matt. 5:17).
- **What shifted:** *Plērosai* is the same word used for filling a cup to the brim. Jesus didn't come to end the Law but to *fill it to its fullest meaning*. His teaching doesn't replace Torah — it reveals Torah's deepest intention. "You've heard it said... but I say" isn't contradiction — it's going deeper. He fills the cup that was only partially full.

## 731. Not One Jot or Tittle
- **Modern understanding:** Every word of the Bible is literally binding.
- **Original terms:** Greek *iōta* (ἰῶτα) — the smallest Greek letter. *Keraia* (κεραία) — a horn/serif, the smallest stroke in Hebrew writing.
- **What shifted:** Jesus says not the smallest letter or stroke will pass from the Torah "until everything is accomplished" (*heōs an panta genētai*). The key phrase is "until everything is accomplished." Whether this means "until the end of time" or "until Jesus accomplishes it all" determines whether this passage *reinforces* Torah's permanent authority or *limits* it to Jesus's fulfillment.

## 732. Greater Righteousness
- **Modern understanding:** Christians must be more morally perfect than Pharisees.
- **Original term:** Greek *perisseuē hymōn hē dikaiosynē pleon tōn grammateōn kai Pharisaiōn* — your justice/righteousness must exceed that of the scribes and Pharisees (Matt. 5:20).
- **What shifted:** The Pharisees' *dikaiosynē* was genuinely impressive — detailed Torah observance. Jesus says more is needed. But the Sermon on the Mount that follows redefines *dikaiosynē* — it's not more rules but deeper transformation: not just avoiding murder but anger, not just avoiding adultery but lust. Interior transformation, not just behavioral compliance.

## 733. You Have Heard... But I Say
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus correcting the Old Testament; replacing old rules.
- **Original term:** Greek *ēkousate hoti errethē... egō de legō hymin* — you heard it was said... but I say to you (Matt. 5:21-48).
- **What shifted:** Jesus doesn't say "it was *written*" (*gegraptai*) — he says "it was *said*" (*errethē*). He may be correcting oral traditions and popular interpretations, not Torah itself. His "but I say" goes *deeper* into Torah's intent, not against it. He's the authoritative interpreter, not the replacement.

## 734. Hate Your Enemy
- **Modern understanding:** The OT commanded hatred of enemies.
- **Original context:** Matthew 5:43 — "You heard it said, love your neighbor and hate your enemy."
- **What shifted:** "Love your neighbor" is in Torah (Lev. 19:18). "Hate your enemy" is *nowhere in the OT*. Jesus is correcting a popular *addition* to Torah, not Torah itself. This is significant — he's separating authoritative text from unauthorized interpretation. His radical command (love enemies) is presented as truer Torah, not anti-Torah.

## 735. Eye for Eye (Jesus's reinterpretation)
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus abolished the OT revenge principle.
- **Original context:** Matthew 5:38-39 — Jesus says "do not resist the evil one" (*mē antistēnai tō ponērō*).
- **What shifted:** *Antistēnai* can mean "do not resist violently" or "do not oppose in court" — a legal term. Jesus may not be prohibiting all resistance but prohibiting violent retaliation and litigation. His examples (turn cheek, give cloak, go extra mile) are all *creative non-violent resistance*, not passive acceptance.

## 736. Alms in Secret
- **Modern understanding:** Don't brag about your giving.
- **Original context:** Matthew 6:1-4 — "don't let your left hand know what your right hand is doing."
- **What shifted:** The phrase "left hand/right hand" isn't about hands — it's about self-awareness. Don't even let *yourself* be conscious of your own generosity. The goal isn't hiding from others — it's freedom from the ego's need to track its own virtue. True generosity is so natural it's unconscious.

## 737. Closet Prayer
- **Modern understanding:** Pray privately, not publicly.
- **Original term:** Greek *tameion* (ταμεῖον) — an inner room, a storeroom (Matt. 6:6).
- **What shifted:** The *tameion* was the windowless storage room in a house — the only room with a door that closed. Jesus contrasts this with the street corners where hypocrites pray for visibility. The point isn't that public prayer is wrong — it's that prayer's primary audience is God, not humans. If your prayer has a human audience, your reward is human applause.

## 738. Vain Repetition
- **Modern understanding:** Don't repeat yourself in prayer; keep it short.
- **Original term:** Greek *battalogēsēte* (βατταλογήσητε) — to babble, to pile up empty phrases (Matt. 6:7).
- **What shifted:** *Battalogēsēte* may refer to magical incantations where repetition was believed to compel divine action — "heap up phrases as the pagans do." The issue isn't repetition itself (Jesus prayed repeatedly in Gethsemane) but *mechanical* repetition designed to manipulate God. Prayer isn't a spell where saying the right words enough times forces a response.

## 739. Narrow Way (deeper)
- **Modern understanding:** The path to heaven is exclusive and difficult.
- **Original term:** Greek *tethlimmenē hē hodos* — the *compressed/afflicted* road (Matt. 7:14). From *thlibō* — to press, to compress.
- **What shifted:** The road isn't just narrow — it's *thlibō* (compressed/pressured), from the same root as *thlipsis* (tribulation). The narrow road is the road of tribulation — it's narrow because it involves pressure. This connects directly to Jesus's warning that following him means taking up a cross. The narrowness is the cost of discipleship.

## 740. House on the Rock
- **Modern understanding:** Build your life on faith; believe in Jesus.
- **Original context:** Matthew 7:24-27 — the wise person *hears and does*. The foolish person *hears and doesn't do*.
- **What shifted:** Both builders *hear* Jesus's words. The difference isn't hearing vs. not hearing — it's *doing* vs. *not doing*. The rock isn't abstract faith — it's *practice*. Building on the rock means *living out* what Jesus taught. The entire Sermon on the Mount ends with: knowledge without practice is a house on sand.

## 741. Do Not Judge
- **Modern understanding:** Never evaluate anyone's behavior; all opinions are equally valid.
- **Original term:** Greek *mē krinete* (μὴ κρίνετε) — do not judge/decide/condemn (Matt. 7:1).
- **What shifted:** The context continues: "with the judgment you use, you will be judged." Jesus doesn't prohibit all evaluation — he warns that your standard will be applied to you. It's about *hypocrisy* (remove the plank from your own eye first), not about eliminating moral discernment. Self-examination must precede other-evaluation.

## 742. Plank and Speck
- **Modern understanding:** A funny image about hypocrisy.
- **Original terms:** Greek *dokos* (δοκός) — a building beam, a rafter. *Karphos* (κάρφος) — a tiny splinter, a dry twig.
- **What shifted:** The humor is deliberate and extreme — a construction beam in your eye vs. a splinter in theirs. The absurdity makes it memorable. But the conclusion is nuanced: "first remove the plank from your own eye, *then* you will see clearly to remove the speck from your brother's." He doesn't say don't help — he says get clear-sighted *first*. Self-correction enables genuine helping.

## 743. Pearls Before Swine
- **Modern understanding:** Don't share sacred things with unworthy people.
- **Original term:** Greek *margaritas emprosthen tōn choirōn* — pearls before pigs (Matt. 7:6).
- **What shifted:** Pigs can't digest pearls — they'll trample them and may attack you. The teaching may be about *discernment in communication* — recognizing when sacred truth will be misused or cause harm. It's not about deeming people unworthy but about wisdom in timing and audience. Some truths require preparation to receive.

## 744. Wolves in Sheep's Clothing
- **Modern understanding:** Dangerous people pretending to be nice.
- **Original term:** Greek *lykoi harpages en endymasin probatōn* — ravenous wolves in sheep-clothing (Matt. 7:15).
- **What shifted:** The wolves are specifically *false prophets* — people who claim to speak for God but whose fruit reveals otherwise. "By their fruits you will recognize them" (7:16). The test for false prophets isn't doctrinal correctness but *fruit* — what their teaching produces in practice. Bad fruit reveals predatory nature regardless of theological vocabulary.

## 745. Lord, Lord
- **Modern understanding:** Merely calling yourself a Christian isn't enough.
- **Original term:** Greek *kyrie, kyrie* — Lord, Lord (Matt. 7:21-23). "Not everyone who says *kyrie kyrie* will enter the kingdom."
- **What shifted:** The people Jesus rejects *did miracles in his name* — they prophesied, cast out demons, did mighty works. They had spectacular spiritual *performance*. But Jesus says "I never knew you" — *ginosko* (relational knowing). They had power but not relationship. Activity without intimacy. Performance without partnership.

## 746. Depart from Me
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus sending people to hell.
- **Original term:** Greek *apochōreite ap' emou hoi ergazomenoi tēn anomian* — depart from me, you who work lawlessness (Matt. 7:23).
- **What shifted:** *Anomia* means lawlessness — *without-Torah*, without-instruction. Despite spectacular religious activity, these people operated without genuine Torah (God's instruction). They were lawless while appearing spiritual. The issue is autonomy — doing spiritual things without being under God's actual direction. Self-directed spirituality is *anomia* — even when it looks impressive.

## 747. Seed of Abraham
- **Modern understanding:** Jewish heritage; biological descendants of Abraham.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *zera Avraham* — seed/offspring of Abraham. Greek *sperma Abraam*.
- **What shifted:** Paul argues that the *seed* of Abraham is Christ (Gal. 3:16) and that those who belong to Christ are Abraham's seed (Gal. 3:29). The seed isn't ethnic but covenantal — belonging comes through faith-loyalty (*pistis*), not biology. Abraham's family is reconstituted around faith, not genetics.

## 748. Circumcision of the Heart (Paul)
- **Modern understanding:** Interior spiritual transformation.
- **Original term:** Greek *peritomē kardias en pneumati* — circumcision of the heart in spirit (Rom. 2:29).
- **What shifted:** Paul says "a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, in spirit" — redefining Jewish identity itself. The covenant sign moves from flesh to heart, from external mark to internal reality. This doesn't abolish Jewish identity — it universalizes the *principle* while maintaining the *tradition*.

## 749. Neither Jew nor Greek
- **Modern understanding:** Ethnic differences don't matter in Christianity.
- **Original term:** Greek *ouk eni Ioudaios oude Hellēn* — there is no Jew nor Greek (Gal. 3:28).
- **What shifted:** Paul doesn't say differences *don't exist* — he says they don't determine status "in Christ Jesus." It's not erasure of identity but equalization of standing. Jewish believers don't stop being Jewish; Greek believers don't become Jewish. Both have equal standing in Christ without abandoning their cultural identity.

## 750. All Have Sinned
- **Modern understanding:** Everyone is a terrible sinner; total depravity.
- **Original term:** Greek *pantes hēmarton* (πάντες ἥμαρτον) — all missed the mark (Rom. 3:23).
- **What shifted:** *Hēmarton* (missed the mark) — archery language. Combined with "fall short of the glory (*doxa*) of God" — the *doxa* they lack is the glory-weight-image they were supposed to bear. Humanity has failed to be the image-bearing, glory-reflecting creatures they were designed to be. The problem isn't that humans are monsters — it's that they're underperforming image-bearers.

## 751. Justified Freely
- **Modern understanding:** God declares you innocent for free.
- **Original term:** Greek *dikaioumenoi dōrean tē autou chariti* — being justified/made-right freely by his grace (Rom. 3:24).
- **What shifted:** *Dōrean* (freely, as a gift) + *chariti* (by grace) — double emphasis on unearned. *Dikaioumenoi* (being justified) is present passive participle — *ongoing*, not a one-time declaration. You are *being* made right freely by grace. It's a continuous process of being set right, not a single courtroom verdict.

## 752. Propitiation / Mercy Seat (Romans)
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus satisfied God's wrath on our behalf.
- **Original term:** Greek *hilastērion* (ἱλαστήριον) — mercy seat, place of atonement (Rom. 3:25). The same word used in the Septuagint for the *kapporet* atop the Ark.
- **What shifted:** God put forward Jesus as the *hilastērion* — the contact point where God and humanity meet. This is temple-geometry language: Jesus is the new mercy seat, the place where divine and human converge. Whether emphasis falls on satisfaction (propitiation) or cleansing (expiation) depends on whether you read through Latin or Greek lenses.

## 753. Nothing Can Separate Us
- **Modern understanding:** A comforting promise for hard times.
- **Original context:** Romans 8:38-39 — Paul lists cosmic forces: death, life, angels, rulers, things present, things to come, powers, height, depth.
- **What shifted:** Paul's list includes *archai* (rulers), *dynameis* (powers) — the same political-spiritual forces from Ephesians 6. "Nothing can separate" isn't just a comfort text — it's a defiance of every power structure, political force, and cosmic entity that claims authority over your identity. It's a declaration of absolute security against empire, not just hardship.

## 754. More Than Conquerors
- **Modern understanding:** We're really victorious; super-winners.
- **Original term:** Greek *hypernikōmen* (ὑπερνικῶμεν) — we super-conquer, we over-overcome (Rom. 8:37).
- **What shifted:** *Hypernikōmen* — beyond conquering. But the context is crucial: "in all these things" — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, danger, sword. We super-conquer *through* suffering, not by avoiding it. The victory happens inside the suffering, not after it. The super-conquering is experiencing all that and not being separated from love.

## 755. Mercy Triumphs Over Judgment
- **Modern understanding:** God is more merciful than judgmental.
- **Original term:** Greek *katakauchatai eleos kriseōs* — mercy boasts/triumphs against judgment (James 2:13).
- **What shifted:** *Katakauchatai* means to boast over, to triumph over, to glory against. Mercy doesn't just outweigh judgment — it *exults over* it. Mercy stands over judgment victorious, boasting. In the contest between mercy and judgment, mercy always wins — not by ignoring justice but by exceeding it.

## 756. Faith Without Works Is Dead
- **Modern understanding:** You need to do good things to prove your faith is real.
- **Original term:** Greek *hē pistis chōris ergōn nekra estin* — faith apart from works is dead (James 2:26).
- **What shifted:** James uses *ergōn* (works/deeds) and *pistis* (faith/faithfulness) not as competing categories but as inseparable aspects of one reality. A body without breath is a corpse; faith without action is a corpse. James isn't adding works *to* faith — he's saying faith *without* works was never alive to begin with. Living faith naturally acts.

## 757. Taming the Tongue (James 3 deeper)
- **Modern understanding:** Control what you say; be careful with words.
- **Original context:** James 3:9-10 — "With the tongue we bless the Lord and Father, and with it we curse those who are made in the *likeness* (*homoiōsin*) of God."
- **What shifted:** Cursing a human is cursing a God-image. Every person bears the *homoiōsis* (likeness) of God. Verbal attack on humans is verbal attack on God's image — which is why blessing God and cursing humans from the same mouth is a *contradiction of reality*, not just bad manners.

## 758. Living Stones
- **Modern understanding:** Christians are building blocks of the church.
- **Original term:** Greek *lithoi zōntes* (λίθοι ζῶντες) — living stones (1 Peter 2:5).
- **What shifted:** Stones don't live — that's the point. Dead material is made alive. And these living stones are being built into a "spiritual house" (*oikos pneumatikos*) to be a "holy priesthood." The temple isn't built with dead material anymore but with living people. Every person is simultaneously a stone in the structure and a priest serving in it.

## 759. Royal Priesthood
- **Modern understanding:** A nice title for Christians.
- **Original term:** Greek *basileion hierateuma* — a royal priesthood (1 Peter 2:9). Quoting Exodus 19:6.
- **What shifted:** *Basileion* (royal) + *hierateuma* (priesthood) — every believer is simultaneously royalty and priest. This was originally Israel's vocation (Exod. 19:6) and is now extended to all who belong to Christ. There's no separate priestly class because *everyone* is a priest. And there's no separate royal class because *everyone* is royalty.

## 760. Sojourners and Exiles
- **Modern understanding:** Christians don't belong in this world; heaven is our home.
- **Original term:** Greek *paroikous kai parepidēmous* — resident aliens and temporary residents (1 Peter 2:11).
- **What shifted:** Peter doesn't say "leave the world" — he says live *in* it as resident aliens. You participate in society, honor the emperor (2:17), do good in the community (2:15), but your ultimate allegiance is elsewhere. It's dual citizenship, not withdrawal. You're fully present but not fully absorbed.

## 761. Submission to Authorities (1 Peter)
- **Modern understanding:** Always obey the government without question.
- **Original context:** 1 Peter 2:13-17 — "Submit to every human institution... honor the emperor."
- **What shifted:** Peter writes this while knowing that the same authorities crucified Jesus and will persecute believers. The submission is *strategic* and *qualified* — "for the Lord's sake." When authorities contradict the Lord, Peter himself says "we must obey God rather than humans" (Acts 5:29). Submission to authority is default posture, not absolute rule.

## 762. Suffering Unjustly
- **Modern understanding:** Bad things happen; endure them.
- **Original term:** Greek *paschōn adikōs* — suffering unjustly (1 Peter 2:19-20).
- **What shifted:** Peter says enduring unjust suffering is *charis* (grace/favor) before God. The word for unjust is *adikōs* — without justice. Peter acknowledges the suffering *is* unjust — he doesn't theologize it as deserved or purposeful. But bearing it with integrity has value. The unjustness is named, not rationalized.

## 763. Spirits in Prison
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus preached in hell between death and resurrection.
- **Original term:** Greek *tois en phylakē pneumasin* — the spirits in prison (1 Peter 3:19).
- **What shifted:** This is one of the NT's most debated passages. The "spirits" may be the fallen angels of Genesis 6 (*bene elohim*) imprisoned in Tartarus (2 Peter 2:4), not dead humans. Christ's proclamation (*ekēryxen* — he heralded) may be a victory announcement to defeated powers, not an evangelistic sermon to the dead.

## 764. Baptism Now Saves You
- **Modern understanding:** The water of baptism washes away sins.
- **Original term:** Greek *baptisma... nun sōzei* — baptism now saves you (1 Peter 3:21). Peter immediately adds: "not the removal of dirt from the body but the pledge of a good conscience toward God."
- **What shifted:** Peter explicitly says it's not the *water* that saves — it's the *eperōtēma* (pledge/appeal/answer) of a clear conscience. The Greek *eperōtēma* may mean a pledged response to God's question — like answering "I do" at a covenant ceremony. Baptism saves as a covenant response, not as water magic.

## 765. Love Covers a Multitude of Sins
- **Modern understanding:** If you love enough, your sins are overlooked.
- **Original term:** Greek *agapē kalyptei plēthos hamartiōn* — love covers a multitude of sins (1 Peter 4:8). Quoting Proverbs 10:12.
- **What shifted:** *Kalyptō* means to cover, conceal, hide. In context, Peter is talking about community life — love between believers covers offenses by choosing not to expose, not to gossip, not to amplify. It's about how love handles community conflict: not by airing every grievance but by absorbing many wrongs in silence.

## 766. Fiery Trial
- **Modern understanding:** A difficult test; a hard experience.
- **Original term:** Greek *pyrōsei... pros peirasmon* — the burning/refining for testing (1 Peter 4:12).
- **What shifted:** *Pyrōsis* is a metallurgical term — the fire that tests and refines metal. The "fiery trial" isn't random suffering but a refining process that reveals and purifies what's genuine. "Don't be surprised" (*xenizesthe*) — literally "don't treat it as strange/foreign." Refinement by fire is normal, not exceptional.

## 767. Judgment Begins at God's House
- **Modern understanding:** God judges Christians first, then the world.
- **Original term:** Greek *ho kairos tou arxasthai to krima apo tou oikou tou theou* — the time for judgment to begin from God's household (1 Peter 4:17).
- **What shifted:** *Krima* (judgment/evaluation) starts with those closest to God — not because they're worse but because responsibility is proportional to proximity. Greater knowledge means greater accountability. The household of God is held to a higher standard precisely because they know better.

## 768. Humble Yourselves Under God's Mighty Hand
- **Modern understanding:** Submit to God's authority; don't be proud.
- **Original term:** Greek *tapeinōthēte hypo tēn krataian cheira tou theou* — make yourselves low under the strong hand of God (1 Peter 5:6).
- **What shifted:** "The mighty hand of God" in the OT is an Exodus phrase — God's powerful hand that liberated Israel from Egypt (Exod. 3:19, 6:1). Humbling yourself under God's mighty hand means placing yourself under the same power that *liberates* — not under oppression but under liberating power. Humility is trusting the liberator.

## 769. Cast Your Anxieties
- **Modern understanding:** Don't worry; give your problems to God.
- **Original term:** Greek *pasan tēn merimnan hymōn epirhipsantes ep' auton* — casting all your anxiety/care upon him (1 Peter 5:7). *Epirhiptō* = to throw upon.
- **What shifted:** *Epirhiptō* is a forceful word — to *hurl* your anxieties onto God. It's not passive surrender but active, almost violent transfer. And the reason: "because he *cares* (*melei*) for you." The contrast: your anxiety (*merimna* — divided mind) vs. his care (*melei* — genuine concern). You throw because he catches.

## 770. Roaring Lion
- **Modern understanding:** The devil is dangerous and scary.
- **Original term:** Greek *hōs leōn ōryomenos* — like a roaring lion (1 Peter 5:8). *Peripatei* — walks around.
- **What shifted:** A *roaring* lion is a lion that's *already been detected* — stealth predators don't announce themselves. Peter's warning is about being alert (*nēpsate*, *grēgorēsate* — be sober, be watchful). The adversary's strategy is intimidation through noise. "Resist him, firm in your faith" (5:9) — the proper response to a roaring predator is standing firm, not running.

## 771. Grace Upon Grace
- **Modern understanding:** Abundant grace; lots of blessings.
- **Original term:** Greek *charin anti charitos* — grace *instead of* grace (John 1:16). *Anti* = in place of, in exchange for.
- **What shifted:** *Anti* suggests replacement or succession: one grace replacing another, like waves succeeding waves. It's not just abundance — it's *renewal*. Each grace is replaced by a fresh grace. The old covenant's grace is succeeded by the new covenant's grace. Or: each experience of grace is replaced by a new experience. Waves upon waves, endlessly refreshed.

## 772. Full of Grace and Truth
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus was gracious and truthful; nice and honest.
- **Original term:** Greek *plērēs charitos kai alētheias* — full of grace and truth (John 1:14). This echoes Hebrew *chesed ve'emet* — steadfast love and faithfulness.
- **What shifted:** *Chesed ve'emet* is a standard pairing describing God's character throughout the OT (Exod. 34:6). John says the Logos-made-flesh is *plērēs* (filled to capacity) with the same qualities that define YHWH's character. It's a divine identity claim embedded in character description.

## 773. In the Beginning (John)
- **Modern understanding:** The Gospel of John starts like Genesis; a creation parallel.
- **Original term:** Greek *en archē* (ἐν ἀρχῇ) — in the beginning (John 1:1). Identical to Genesis 1:1 LXX.
- **What shifted:** John deliberately opens with Genesis 1's opening words, then surpasses them — the Logos was *already there* "in the beginning." Genesis says "in the beginning God created." John says "in the beginning the Logos *already existed*." The Logos precedes creation — he's not created at the beginning but present before it.

## 774. Life Was the Light of Humans
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus brings spiritual enlightenment.
- **Original term:** Greek *hē zōē ēn to phōs tōn anthrōpōn* — the life was the light of humanity (John 1:4).
- **What shifted:** The sequence is important: *life* produces *light*, not the other way around. Understanding (light) comes from encounter with life, not from information. You don't think your way to life — you live your way to understanding. The order is experiential: first life, then illumination.

## 775. Came to His Own
- **Modern understanding:** Jesus came to the Jews and they rejected him.
- **Original term:** Greek *eis ta idia ēlthen, kai hoi idioi auton ou parelabon* — he came to his own things/place, and his own people did not receive him (John 1:11).
- **What shifted:** *Ta idia* (his own things) and *hoi idioi* (his own people) — the Logos comes to his own creation (his own things) and his own people. The rejection is cosmic and specific: the universe's creator enters his own creation and the creation doesn't recognize him. It's not just Jewish rejection — it's cosmic non-recognition.

## 776. Power to Become Children of God
- **Modern understanding:** God gives believers the ability to be his children.
- **Original term:** Greek *exousian tekna theou genesthai* — authority/right to become children of God (John 1:12).
- **What shifted:** *Exousia* (authority/right/freedom) — not just ability but *legal right*. And *genesthai* (to become) — it's a process, not instant. Believing gives you the *right* to *become* — it's an authorized journey of becoming, not instant arrival. You receive the authority; you grow into the identity.

## 777. Born of God
- **Modern understanding:** A spiritual rebirth event; the moment of conversion.
- **Original term:** Greek *ek theou egennēthēsan* — from God they were born (John 1:13). John specifies: "not from blood, nor from the will of the flesh, nor from the will of man, but from God."
- **What shifted:** Three negations define what divine birth is *not*: not from *blood* (not ethnic/genealogical), not from *flesh's will* (not human desire/effort), not from *man's will* (not human decision/arrangement). Being born of God is entirely God's initiative — it cannot be manufactured by biology, desire, or planning. The source is *ek theou* — from God alone. Human origin stories don't determine divine identity. You are born from the same source that spoke light into existence.
