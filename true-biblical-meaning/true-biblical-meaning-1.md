# True Biblical Meanings — Part 1 (1–111)

> A guide to what biblical words and expressions originally meant in Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek — versus how they are commonly understood through modern translations.

---

## 1. Hell
- **Modern understanding:** A single fiery underworld of eternal punishment for the wicked.
- **Original terms:** Three distinct words — *Sheol* (Hebrew: the grave, the underworld where all dead go), *Hades* (Greek: equivalent of Sheol), and *Gehenna* (Greek/Aramaic: the Valley of Hinnom, an actual burning trash dump outside Jerusalem). A fourth term, *Tartarus*, appears once in 2 Peter 2:4 referring to a holding place for fallen angels.
- **What shifted:** All four were collapsed into one English word "Hell," creating a unified doctrine of eternal fiery punishment that doesn't exist in any single original term.

## 2. Eternal / Everlasting
- **Modern understanding:** Infinite, never-ending, without limit in time.
- **Original term:** Greek *aiōnios* (αἰώνιος), from *aiōn* — an age, era, or epoch.
- **What shifted:** *Aiōnios* more accurately means "pertaining to an age" or "age-lasting." "Eternal punishment" in Matthew 25:46 could read "age-lasting correction." This single translation choice determines whether Christian theology produces eternal torment or universal reconciliation.

## 3. Virgin (Isaiah 7:14)
- **Modern understanding:** A woman who has never had sexual intercourse.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *almah* (עַלְמָה) — a young woman of marriageable age. Hebrew has a specific word for virgin: *bethulah* (בְּתוּלָה).
- **What shifted:** The Septuagint translators chose the Greek *parthenos* (virgin), and Matthew 1:23 picked up this Greek rendering. The entire virgin birth doctrine rests on a Greek translation choice that arguably exceeded the scope of the Hebrew original.

## 4. Lucifer
- **Modern understanding:** The proper name of Satan before his fall from heaven.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *helel ben shachar* (הֵילֵל בֶּן־שָׁחַר) — "shining one, son of the dawn." Latin *lucifer* simply means "light-bearer" or "morning star."
- **What shifted:** Isaiah 14:12 is addressing the King of Babylon in a political taunt. Jerome used the Latin word *lucifer* in the Vulgate, and over centuries it was reinterpreted as a proper name for a fallen angel. The entire Satan origin story most Christians know was built from a Latin translation of political satire.

## 5. Elohim
- **Modern understanding:** A singular name for the one God.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *Elohim* (אֱלֹהִים) — grammatically plural, meaning "gods," "divine beings," or "powers." Used for God but also for angels, judges, and other spiritual beings.
- **What shifted:** Psalm 82 says "God (*Elohim*) stands in the council of gods (*elohim*)." Deuteronomy 32:8 in the Dead Sea Scrolls assigns nations to the "sons of God" (*bene elohim*). The original Hebrew worldview was a divine hierarchy with Yahweh as supreme, not strict monotheism from the start.

## 6. Soul
- **Modern understanding:** An immaterial, immortal essence trapped inside a physical body.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *nephesh* (נֶפֶשׁ) — a living being, life-breath, throat, appetite, creature. Greek *psychē* (ψυχή) similarly means life or self.
- **What shifted:** Body-soul dualism comes from Plato, not the Bible. In Genesis, God makes Adam a "living *nephesh*" — a living creature, not a body containing a soul. You don't *have* a nephesh, you *are* a nephesh.

## 7. Church
- **Modern understanding:** A religious building or institution with organized hierarchy.
- **Original term:** Greek *ekklēsia* (ἐκκλησία) — a called-out assembly, a civic gathering of citizens for deliberation.
- **What shifted:** *Ekklēsia* was a political term. Translating it as "church" (from Germanic *kirche/kyriakon*) imported centuries of institutional and architectural meaning back into texts where Jesus was describing a grassroots movement of people.

## 8. Repent / Repentance
- **Modern understanding:** To feel guilt and sorrow for sins, to do penance.
- **Original term:** Greek *metanoia* (μετάνοια) — a change of mind, a shift in perception and understanding. Hebrew *shuv* (שׁוּב) — to turn, to return.
- **What shifted:** Latin *paenitentia* (penance/punishment) introduced guilt and self-punishment connotations. The originals describe a cognitive reorientation — changing your entire way of seeing — not an emotional guilt response.

## 9. Love (flattened)
- **Modern understanding:** A single English word covering all forms of affection.
- **Original terms:** Greek distinguishes *agapē* (unconditional/self-giving), *phileō* (brotherly affection), *erōs* (romantic desire), and *storgē* (familial bond). Hebrew uses *ahavah* (אַהֲבָה) broadly but context differentiates.
- **What shifted:** In John 21:15-17, Jesus switches between *agapaō* and *phileō* when asking Peter "do you love me?" — a nuanced conversation entirely invisible in English translation.

## 10. Worship
- **Modern understanding:** Singing songs, attending religious services, spiritual devotion.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *hishtachavah* (הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה) — to bow down, prostrate oneself. Greek *proskyneō* (προσκυνέω) — to kiss toward, to prostrate before.
- **What shifted:** Both terms describe a physical gesture of honor given to kings, elders, and even strangers — not exclusively religious devotion. Many "worship" passages are simply describing cultural respect gestures.

## 11. Sin
- **Modern understanding:** A moral crime against God, an inherent stain of guilt.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *chata* (חָטָא) — to miss the mark (archery term). Greek *hamartia* (ἁμαρτία) — same meaning, missing the target.
- **What shifted:** The shift from "you missed the target" to "you're guilty/corrupted" changed the entire psychological framework. Missing a target implies recalibration and another attempt; being a "sinner" implies a broken identity.

## 12. Faith
- **Modern understanding:** Believing correct doctrinal statements to be true; intellectual assent.
- **Original term:** Greek *pistis* (πίστις) — trust, loyalty, faithfulness, allegiance. Hebrew *emunah* (אֱמוּנָה) — firmness, steadfastness, reliability.
- **What shifted:** *Pistis* is relational and active — ongoing trust and allegiance. "Do you believe Jesus is the Son of God?" was not a quiz — it was "are you loyal to this king?" The faith-vs-works debate may be largely a translation artifact.

## 13. Logos (The Word)
- **Modern understanding:** A spoken word or verbal statement.
- **Original term:** Greek *logos* (λόγος) — reason, logic, ordering principle, rational structure of reality. In Stoic philosophy, the *Logos* was the cosmic intelligence behind all existence.
- **What shifted:** John 1:1 saying "In the beginning was the Logos" was a massive philosophical claim to Greek-speaking audiences. Translating it as "Word" domesticates it into something verbal when the original is cosmological.

## 14. Kingdom of God
- **Modern understanding:** Heaven; the afterlife destination for believers.
- **Original term:** Greek *basileia tou theou* (βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ) — the reign, rule, or active governance of God. *Basileia* refers to a *reign*, not a *realm*.
- **What shifted:** Jesus says "the kingdom of God is among/within you" (Luke 17:21) — describing a present reality of divine governance, not a future location. The spatial/afterlife reading redirected the entire message from "here and now" to "later and elsewhere."

## 15. Forgiveness
- **Modern understanding:** An internal emotional act of letting go of resentment.
- **Original term:** Greek *aphesis* (ἄφεσις) — release, liberation, letting go, cancellation of debts. Used for releasing prisoners and cancelling financial obligations.
- **What shifted:** The Lord's Prayer literally says "release us from our debts as we release our debtors." The economic/social dimension was primary. This concrete socioeconomic language got spiritualized into a purely internal emotional act.

## 16. Righteousness
- **Modern understanding:** Personal moral purity; being a good person.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *tsedaqah* (צְדָקָה) — justice, equity, setting things right. Greek *dikaiosynē* (δικαιοσύνη) — justice, fairness.
- **What shifted:** "Righteousness" became individual and moral; the original is communal and structural. "Blessed are those who hunger for *dikaiosynē*" means people desperate for justice in society, not people trying to achieve personal moral perfection.

## 17. Servant (hiding Slave)
- **Modern understanding:** An employee, a willing helper.
- **Original term:** Greek *doulos* (δοῦλος) — slave, one owned by another with no independent rights.
- **What shifted:** Paul calls himself a *doulos* of Christ. Translations softened this to "servant" for cultural comfort, especially during and after the transatlantic slave trade. The original metaphor is radical surrender and total belonging.

## 18. Angel
- **Modern understanding:** A winged, glowing, humanoid supernatural being.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *malakh* (מַלְאָךְ) — messenger. Greek *angelos* (ἄγγελος) — messenger.
- **What shifted:** Sometimes the "messenger" is supernatural, sometimes human, sometimes ambiguous. The winged glowing image is artistic tradition. Biblical *seraphim* and *cherubim* are described as multi-winged, multi-faced cosmic beings — nothing like Renaissance paintings.

## 19. Adam
- **Modern understanding:** The proper name of the first individual man.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *adam* (אָדָם) — human, humankind, earthling (from *adamah* — earth/soil/ground).
- **What shifted:** Genesis 1-2 initially reads "the human" not "a man named Adam." It functions as a proper name only later. Whether the narrative is about a specific individual or an archetype of humanity itself has massive implications for original sin theology.

## 20. Spirit of God (Genesis 1:2)
- **Modern understanding:** The Holy Spirit hovering above primordial waters.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *ruach elohim* (רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים) — spirit/wind/breath of God. *Elohim* can function as a superlative, making it "mighty wind."
- **What shifted:** *Ruach* can mean spirit, wind, or breath. *Merachefet* (hovered) is the same verb used for an eagle hovering over its nest (Deut. 32:11). The creation scene reads quite differently as "a mighty wind swept over the waters" versus "God's Spirit hovered."

## 21. Prayer
- **Modern understanding:** Hands folded, eyes closed, quietly accepting God's will.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *palal* (פָּלַל) — to judge, intercede, argue, make a case. Root of *tefillah* (prayer).
- **What shifted:** Abraham argues with God over Sodom. Jacob wrestles God. Moses challenges God's decisions. Biblical prayer was dialogue, negotiation, even confrontation — a far cry from passive submission.

## 22. Commandments / Torah
- **Modern understanding:** Rigid legal rules demanding obedience under threat of punishment.
- **Original terms:** *Mitzvot* (מִצְווֹת) — commandments. *Torah* (תּוֹרָה) — instruction, teaching, aim (from *yarah* — to shoot, to aim).
- **What shifted:** Torah means "aim" or "instruction." Paired with *chata* (sin = missing the mark), you get a beautiful archery metaphor: Torah is the aim, sin is missing it. The legal/judicial framing of "law and crime" destroys this.

## 23. Covenant
- **Modern understanding:** A contract or agreement between parties.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *berit* (בְּרִית) — involves cutting (*karat berit* — to cut a covenant). Genesis 15 has God passing between cut animal pieces.
- **What shifted:** This was a blood-oath ritual where both parties said "may I be torn apart like these animals if I break this." Calling it a "testament" (as in Old/New Testament) makes it sound like a document, not a life-and-death pact.

## 24. Peace / Shalom
- **Modern understanding:** Absence of conflict; calm and quiet.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *shalom* (שָׁלוֹם) — wholeness, completeness, flourishing, nothing missing, nothing broken, total well-being — material, relational, and spiritual.
- **What shifted:** When Jesus says "peace I leave with you," he's offering *shalom* — total restoration of reality. It's not "calm down" — it's "I'm making all things whole."

## 25. New (as in New Covenant/Testament)
- **Modern understanding:** Brand new replacement for the old.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *chadash* (חָדָשׁ) — can mean new or renewed/refreshed. Greek *kainos* (καινός) — fresh, renewed, qualitatively new (vs. *neos* — new in time).
- **What shifted:** Whether the covenant is a replacement or a renewal of the original fundamentally changes how Christianity relates to Judaism. *Kainos* leans toward "renewed quality" not "totally different."

## 26. Worship in Spirit and Truth (John 4:24)
- **Modern understanding:** Being emotionally sincere during worship services.
- **Original terms:** Greek *pneuma* (πνεῦμα) — spirit/breath/wind. *Alētheia* (ἀλήθεια) — truth, reality, unveiling, unconcealment (related to *lanthano* — to be hidden).
- **What shifted:** Jesus tells the Samaritan woman that encounter with God is non-localized and unmediated — not this mountain, not Jerusalem. He's dismantling the entire temple system, not giving worship style advice.

## 27. Holy
- **Modern understanding:** Morally pure, sinless, pious.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *qadosh* (קָדוֹשׁ) — set apart, other, distinct, separated.
- **What shifted:** When God is called *qadosh*, it means utterly different — not "really good." The ground around the burning bush is *qadosh* — not morally superior ground, but charged, dangerous, other. Holiness has more in common with high voltage than with moral perfection.

## 28. Glory
- **Modern understanding:** Bright light, golden glow, visual splendor.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *kavod* (כָּבוֹד) — weight, heaviness, substance, gravitas.
- **What shifted:** The "glory of God" is God's *weight* — his density of presence. The tabernacle was filled with *kavod* so heavy the priests couldn't stand (1 Kings 8:11). This isn't a light show — it's a gravitational event.

## 29. Blessed
- **Modern understanding:** Happy, fortunate, favored by God.
- **Original terms:** Greek *makarios* (μακάριος) — used in Greek culture for the state of the gods, an enviable condition. Hebrew *ashrei* (אַשְׁרֵי) — to walk straight, to be on the right path.
- **What shifted:** "Blessed are the poor in spirit" isn't "happy are the sad people." It's closer to "on the right path are those who recognize their emptiness." It's directional, not emotional.

## 30. Flesh
- **Modern understanding:** The physical body, especially its sinful desires.
- **Original term:** Greek *sarx* (σάρξ) — in Paul's usage, the entire human operating system oriented toward self-preservation, ego-driven survival mode.
- **What shifted:** Paul isn't a Platonist. You can be "in the flesh" while meditating. You can be "in the spirit" while doing manual labor. The body isn't the enemy — unconscious automated self-interest is.

## 31. Heart
- **Modern understanding:** The seat of emotions, feelings, romance.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *lev/levav* (לֵב/לְבָב) — the mind, will, intellect, decision-making center.
- **What shifted:** "Love the Lord with all your heart" means with your entire cognitive apparatus. When Pharaoh's heart is "hardened," his decision-making faculty is locked. The emotional reading is modern and misleading.

## 32. Fear of God
- **Modern understanding:** Being terrified of God's punishment.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *yirah* (יִרְאָה) — a spectrum from terror to awe to reverence to wonder.
- **What shifted:** "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom" uses *yirah* as standing before something so vast it reorganizes your priorities — the feeling at the edge of a canyon, not the feeling of an abused child before an angry parent.

## 33. Jealous God
- **Modern understanding:** A petty, insecure deity who can't handle competition.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *qanna* (קַנָּא) — zealous, passionate, fiercely devoted.
- **What shifted:** When Exodus says God is *qanna*, it's the intensity of a committed partner — not the insecurity of a controlling personality. "Passionately devoted" captures the original far better.

## 34. Wrath of God
- **Modern understanding:** God actively punishing people in rage.
- **Original term:** Greek *orgē* (ὀργή) — anger, but in Romans 1:24-28, the "wrath" is described as God "giving them over" — stepping back, allowing consequences.
- **What shifted:** The wrath may not be active punishment but withdrawal — the natural entropy that occurs when the source of order stops intervening. Less lightning bolt, more letting go.

## 35. Believe In
- **Modern understanding:** Intellectual assent to a factual claim.
- **Original term:** Greek *pisteuō eis* (πιστεύω εἰς) — to believe *into*, to entrust oneself to, to enter into relational union with.
- **What shifted:** You don't "believe in" Jesus the way you believe in gravity. *Pisteuō eis* is relational movement — entering, merging, participating. John's whole theology is about mutual indwelling.

## 36. Salvation
- **Modern understanding:** Going to heaven after death; being saved from hell.
- **Original terms:** Greek *sōtēria* (σωτηρία) — rescue, deliverance, healing, wholeness, preservation. *Sōzō* (σῴζω) — to save, heal, make whole.
- **What shifted:** When Jesus says "your faith has saved (*sōzō*) you" to someone he physically healed, it's the same word used for spiritual salvation. The texts make no hard distinction between physical healing, psychological restoration, and spiritual rescue.

## 37. Apostle
- **Modern understanding:** A religious title for one of the twelve disciples.
- **Original term:** Greek *apostolos* (ἀπόστολος) — one sent with authority, an envoy, ambassador, commissioned agent.
- **What shifted:** This is diplomatic/military language, not a religious title. Paul claiming to be an *apostolos* is claiming to be an authorized representative of a king, carrying the king's own authority. Roman officials would have understood the political implications.

## 38. Gospel
- **Modern understanding:** A religious message about personal salvation.
- **Original term:** Greek *euangelion* (εὐαγγέλιον) — good news, specifically the announcement of a new emperor's enthronement, a military victory, or a royal birth.
- **What shifted:** When Mark opens with "the *euangelion* of Jesus Christ," he's directly co-opting Caesar's terminology. This is not "here's a nice spiritual message" — it's "there's a new emperor, and it's not Augustus." Subversive political speech.

## 39. Meek
- **Modern understanding:** Weak, timid, passive, doormat.
- **Original term:** Greek *praus* (πραΰς) — gentle strength, power under control. Used to describe a warhorse trained to respond to the lightest touch.
- **What shifted:** "Blessed are the meek" means those with enormous strength who exercise perfect self-regulation — a stallion that could trample you but chooses gentleness. The opposite of weakness.

## 40. End of the World
- **Modern understanding:** The physical destruction of planet Earth.
- **Original term:** Greek *synteleia tou aiōnos* (συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος) — the consummation/completion of the age.
- **What shifted:** Many scholars argue Jesus in Matthew 24 described the end of the Second Temple era (fulfilled in 70 AD), not planetary destruction. Apocalyptic genre uses cosmic language for political upheaval — stars falling means regime change, not astronomy.

## 41. Create (Genesis 1)
- **Modern understanding:** *Creatio ex nihilo* — creating everything from absolute nothing.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *bara* (בָּרָא) — to shape, form, fashion. Used elsewhere for working with existing material.
- **What shifted:** Genesis 1:2 starts with waters already present (*tohu vabohu*). The text may describe God ordering chaos rather than conjuring matter from nothing. The *ex nihilo* doctrine was formalized centuries later under Greek philosophical influence.

## 42. Image of God
- **Modern understanding:** Humans look like God, or carry an immaterial soul-property.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *tselem* (צֶלֶם) — image, statue, representative figure. *Demut* (דְּמוּת) — likeness, resemblance.
- **What shifted:** In the Ancient Near East, an "image" of a god was a statue placed in a territory to represent that god's rule. Humans as God's *tselem* means we are functional representatives — authorized agents, not passive carriers of a spiritual property.

## 43. Good (in Creation)
- **Modern understanding:** Morally good, ethically perfect.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *tov* (טוֹב) — functional, fit for purpose, operating as intended.
- **What shifted:** A hammer is *tov* when it hammers well. Creation being *tov* means it works, it's ordered. It's an engineering assessment, not a moral one. This shifts the "fall" narrative from moral degradation to functional breakdown.

## 44. Know
- **Modern understanding:** To possess factual information about something.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *yada* (יָדַע) — to know through direct encounter, experience, and intimacy.
- **What shifted:** "Adam knew Eve" is the famous example, but *yada* is used for knowing God too. Biblical epistemology is relational rather than propositional — you don't know God by studying facts. You *yada* God through encounter.

## 45. Obey
- **Modern understanding:** To comply with commands; submit to authority.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *shema* (שָׁמַע) — to hear, listen, pay attention, and by extension, respond.
- **What shifted:** The Shema (Deut 6:4) is "Hear, O Israel." Biblical obedience isn't blind compliance — it's attentive listening that naturally produces response, like a musician attuned to a conductor.

## 46. Garden of Eden
- **Modern understanding:** A beautiful nature park where the first humans lived.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *gan* (גַּן) — enclosed garden. *Eden* (עֵדֶן) — delight, abundance. Description matches Ancient Near Eastern temple architecture.
- **What shifted:** Eastward entrance, river flowing out, gold and precious stones, a divine being walking within. Adam is placed there to *avad* (serve) and *shamar* (guard) — the exact word pair used for Levitical priestly duties. Adam is the first priest in the first temple.

## 47. Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil
- **Modern understanding:** A tree that reveals moral right and wrong.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *tov vara* (טוֹב וָרָע) — a merism (two opposites meaning "everything"), like "searching high and low."
- **What shifted:** The tree isn't about moral knowledge — it's about autonomous total knowledge, the capacity to determine reality independently of God. The "fall" is an authority seizure, not an apple incident.

## 48. Rib (Genesis 2:21)
- **Modern understanding:** God took one of Adam's rib bones.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *tsela* (צֵלָע) — side, half, column. Used 40+ times in the OT and never means "rib" anywhere else.
- **What shifted:** The Septuagint translates it as *pleura* (side). God takes a *side* of the human and builds another — suggesting the splitting of a unified being into two complementary halves, not the removal of a small bone.

## 49. Serpent in Eden
- **Modern understanding:** Satan disguised as a snake.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *nachash* (נָחָשׁ) — serpent, but also "shining one" (related to *nechoshet* — bronze/copper). Described as *arum* (עָרוּם) — cunning/shrewd — the same word used positively for wisdom in Proverbs.
- **What shifted:** The text never identifies the serpent as Satan. That connection was made centuries later (Revelation 12, ~95 AD). The original story is far more mysterious and ambiguous.

## 50. Firmament
- **Modern understanding:** The sky, the atmosphere (metaphorical).
- **Original term:** Hebrew *raqia* (רָקִיעַ) — something beaten out, hammered flat, like sheet metal.
- **What shifted:** The biblical cosmology describes a solid dome over the earth holding back waters above. God opens windows in it for rain (Gen. 7:11). This was the literal cosmological model of the Ancient Near East, not poetic metaphor.

## 51. LORD (all caps)
- **Modern understanding:** A title of respect and authority.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *YHWH* (יהוה) — the divine name, appearing ~6,800 times. Likely from *hayah* (to be) — "I Am Who I Am" or "I Will Be What I Will Be."
- **What shifted:** The name is dynamic, processual, about becoming. Replacing it with the feudal title "Lord" imports hierarchical power dynamics over existential mystery.

## 52. Israel
- **Modern understanding:** A nation, a country, a people group.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *Yisrael* (יִשְׂרָאֵל) — "he struggles with God" or "God struggles."
- **What shifted:** Given to Jacob after wrestling a divine being and refusing to let go. The nation is literally named "Wrestles-With-God." Doubt and contention are built into the identity of God's chosen people.

## 53. Prophet
- **Modern understanding:** One who predicts the future.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *navi* (נָבִיא) — likely from Akkadian root meaning "to call" — one who is called, or one who calls out.
- **What shifted:** Prophets primarily addressed the present — confronting injustice, corruption, and unfaithful leaders. Future prediction was a tiny fraction of the job. Isaiah and Amos were social activists, not fortune tellers.

## 54. Sacrifice
- **Modern understanding:** Killing an animal to appease an angry God.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *korban* (קָרְבָּן) — from *karav* (קָרַב) — to come close, to draw near, to approach.
- **What shifted:** Sacrifices were mechanisms for drawing near to God's presence — proximity technology, not appeasement payments. The entire sacrificial system is about restoring closeness, not settling debts.

## 55. Atonement
- **Modern understanding:** Punishment paid for sin; penal substitution.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *kippur* (כִּפּוּר) from *kaphar* (כָּפַר) — to cover, smear, coat (same word used for pitching Noah's ark).
- **What shifted:** The penal substitution model (someone must be punished) is a medieval legal framework. The original is relational repair — covering the breach between divine and human to restore contact.

## 56. Curse (Genesis 3)
- **Modern understanding:** God cursed all of humanity after the fall.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *arar* (אָרַר) — to curse, bind with a curse.
- **What shifted:** God curses the *nachash* (serpent) and the *adamah* (ground) — never Adam or Eve directly. Consequences are described for humans, but the curse-word is applied only to the serpent and soil. Humans are not themselves cursed.

## 57. Blood
- **Modern understanding:** A symbol of death, violence, and the price of sin.
- **Original insight:** Leviticus 17:11 — "the life (*nephesh*) of the flesh is in the blood." Blood represents *life*, not death.
- **What shifted:** When blood is applied to the altar, life is being offered and released. The cross isn't primarily about how brutal the death was — it's about life being poured out. Life released, not punishment absorbed.

## 58. Testament
- **Modern understanding:** A formal label for the two halves of the Bible.
- **Original situation:** Latin *testamentum* means a will — a document taking effect at death. Hebrew *berit* (covenant) is between living parties.
- **What shifted:** "Old and New Testaments" implies the old is a dead document replaced by the new. "Old and New Covenants" (or Prior and Renewed) preserves the living relational character.

## 59. Pharisees
- **Modern understanding:** Hypocritical villains, the enemies of Jesus.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *perushim* (פְּרוּשִׁים) — "separated ones." They were the populist movement making Torah accessible to common people.
- **What shifted:** Jesus's theology is actually closest to Pharisaic teaching. His debates with them are more like intra-family arguments. The vilification of Pharisees in later Christian reading has deep antisemitic roots.

## 60. The Word Became Flesh (John 1:14)
- **Modern understanding:** God took on a human body.
- **Original term:** Greek *sarx* (σάρξ) — flesh, raw meat, mortal vulnerable meat — not *sōma* (σῶμα) — organized, dignified body.
- **What shifted:** John deliberately chose the most visceral, undignified word for physical existence. The Logos didn't take on a noble form — it became *meat*. This is deliberately shocking and anti-Gnostic.

## 61. Grace
- **Modern understanding:** God's unmerited favor; a theological concept about being forgiven for free.
- **Original term:** Greek *charis* (χάρις) — gift, favor, generosity, also charm and beauty. In the Greco-Roman world, *charis* operated within a reciprocal patronage system.
- **What shifted:** In Roman society, *charis* implied a gift that created a relationship of mutual obligation — not a one-way free pass. Paul's use of *charis* is subversive because he claims God's patronage is available to all, not just elites. It's political redistribution of divine favor.

## 62. Demon
- **Modern understanding:** A fallen angel, a malevolent supernatural being with horns and pitchforks.
- **Original term:** Greek *daimonion* (δαιμόνιον) — a spirit or divine being, not inherently evil. In Greek culture, *daimōn* could be benevolent, neutral, or malevolent.
- **What shifted:** The blanket demonization of all *daimonia* as evil was a later development. The Hebrew concept behind many "demon" passages is *shedim* (שֵׁדִים) — likely borrowed from Akkadian *shedu*, which were protective spirits. The entire demonology was reframed.

## 63. Devil
- **Modern understanding:** The supreme evil being, ruler of hell, Satan.
- **Original term:** Greek *diabolos* (διάβολος) — slanderer, accuser, one who throws accusations across (from *dia* + *ballō*). Hebrew *satan* (שָׂטָן) — adversary, accuser, prosecutor.
- **What shifted:** In Job, the *satan* is a member of God's court acting as a prosecuting attorney — with God's permission. The evolution from courtroom function to independent evil ruler happened gradually over centuries.

## 64. Heaven
- **Modern understanding:** The eternal destination of saved souls; paradise in the sky.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *shamayim* (שָׁמַיִם) — the sky, the expanse above. Greek *ouranos* (οὐρανός) — the sky, the heavens.
- **What shifted:** The Bible describes three "heavens" — atmosphere, space, and God's dwelling. The final hope in Revelation isn't humans going to heaven — it's heaven coming down to earth (Rev. 21:2). The destination theology is actually reversed from the original.

## 65. Baptize
- **Modern understanding:** A religious ritual of sprinkling water or brief immersion.
- **Original term:** Greek *baptizō* (βαπτίζω) — to immerse completely, to submerge, to dunk, to dip. Used in secular Greek for dyeing cloth (fully saturating it with color) and for sinking ships.
- **What shifted:** The word implies total saturation, not a symbolic sprinkle. Being "baptized into Christ" carries the image of being completely submerged and saturated — dyed through with a new identity.

## 66. Mystery
- **Modern understanding:** Something unknown, puzzling, or intellectually unsolvable.
- **Original term:** Greek *mystērion* (μυστήριον) — a secret now being revealed, an initiation into hidden knowledge, something previously concealed that is being disclosed.
- **What shifted:** In Paul's usage, a *mystērion* isn't something you can't figure out — it's something that *was* hidden but is *now* being unveiled. The mystery of Christ is not a puzzle but a revelation in progress.

## 67. Power
- **Modern understanding:** Force, ability, control over others.
- **Original term:** Greek *dynamis* (δύναμις) — inherent power, capability, potency, miraculous force. Root of "dynamite."
- **What shifted:** *Dynamis* is often paired with God's activity and refers to transformative energy, not coercive control. When Paul says the gospel is the *dynamis* of God, he's describing explosive transformative capability, not political authority.

## 68. Authority
- **Modern understanding:** The right to command; hierarchical power.
- **Original term:** Greek *exousia* (ἐξουσία) — freedom of action, liberty, right, permission, delegated authority.
- **What shifted:** *Exousia* is about freedom and permission, not domination. When Jesus speaks "with *exousia*," it means he speaks with inherent freedom and right — not that he's pulling rank. The coercive connotation came later.

## 69. Parable
- **Modern understanding:** A simple moral story with a clear lesson.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *mashal* (מָשָׁל) — proverb, riddle, allegory, comparison, taunt, dark saying. Greek *parabolē* (παραβολή) — something thrown alongside for comparison.
- **What shifted:** Jesus explicitly says parables are meant to conceal as much as reveal (Mark 4:11-12). They're not simple children's stories with tidy morals — they're designed to destabilize assumptions and provoke wrestling with meaning.

## 70. Truth
- **Modern understanding:** Factual accuracy; correct propositional statements.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *emet* (אֶמֶת) — firmness, reliability, trustworthiness, faithfulness. Greek *alētheia* (ἀλήθεια) — unconcealment, unveiling, disclosure.
- **What shifted:** Hebrew *emet* is relational (is this person trustworthy?) not propositional (is this statement factually correct?). When Jesus says "I am the truth," he's saying "I am the faithful, reliable, trustworthy one" — not "I am the correct set of doctrines."

## 71. World
- **Modern understanding:** Planet Earth; the physical globe.
- **Original term:** Greek *kosmos* (κόσμος) — order, arrangement, system, adornment (root of "cosmetics"). Also *aiōn* (αἰών) — an age, era, epoch.
- **What shifted:** When John says "God so loved the *kosmos*" or "do not love the *kosmos*," the word means the arranged system of human society, not the physical planet. "Do not love the world" means "do not be captivated by the system," not "hate nature."

## 72. Temptation
- **Modern understanding:** Enticement to do something morally wrong.
- **Original term:** Greek *peirasmos* (πειρασμός) — testing, trial, proving, examination. From *peirazō* — to test, prove, examine.
- **What shifted:** The Lord's Prayer's "lead us not into temptation" uses *peirasmos* — it's asking "don't bring us into a trial/test," not "keep us from wanting to do bad things." Testing is about proving quality, not moral entrapment.

## 73. Perfect
- **Modern understanding:** Flawless, without any defects, morally spotless.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *tamim* (תָּמִים) — complete, whole, sound, finished. Greek *teleios* (τέλειος) — having reached its goal, mature, complete, fully developed.
- **What shifted:** "Be perfect as your Father is perfect" (Matt. 5:48) means "be complete/mature," not "never make a mistake." It's a call to full development, not flawlessness.

## 74. Patience
- **Modern understanding:** Quietly waiting without complaint.
- **Original term:** Greek *makrothymia* (μακροθυμία) — literally "long-tempered" (long + passion/fury). Also *hypomonē* (ὑπομονή) — endurance, steadfast persistence under pressure.
- **What shifted:** Biblical patience isn't passive waiting — it's active endurance. *Makrothymia* means having a long fuse before anger. *Hypomonē* means standing firm under crushing weight. Both are active, not passive.

## 75. Hope
- **Modern understanding:** Wishful thinking; hoping something might happen.
- **Original term:** Greek *elpis* (ἐλπίς) — confident expectation, assured anticipation. Hebrew *tiqvah* (תִּקְוָה) — a cord, a rope, something you hold onto (same word Rahab used for the scarlet rope).
- **What shifted:** Biblical hope is not "I hope it doesn't rain." It's the certainty of holding onto a rope thrown to you from solid ground. *Tiqvah* being a physical cord makes hope tangible and anchored.

## 76. Mercy
- **Modern understanding:** Not punishing someone who deserves it.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *chesed* (חֶסֶד) — loyal love, covenant faithfulness, steadfast devotion, lovingkindness. Greek *eleos* (ἔλεος) — compassion, pity.
- **What shifted:** *Chesed* is far richer than "mercy." It's the loyal, unfailing love that persists because of covenant commitment. It's not merely withholding punishment — it's active, pursuing, relentless devotion.

## 77. Compassion
- **Modern understanding:** Feeling sorry for someone.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *rachamim* (רַחֲמִים) — from *rechem* (רֶחֶם) — womb. Gut-level, visceral, maternal ache.
- **What shifted:** When the Bible says God has compassion, it uses a womb-word. God's compassion is described as the fierce, visceral, protective ache a mother feels for the child of her body. It's not sympathy from a distance.

## 78. Praise
- **Modern understanding:** Singing worship songs; saying nice things about God.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew has multiple words — *halal* (הָלַל) — to shine, to boast, to rave (root of hallelujah). *Yadah* (יָדָה) — to throw, to extend hands. *Zamar* (זָמַר) — to pluck, to make music. *Tehillah* (תְּהִלָּה) — to sing spontaneously.
- **What shifted:** Each Hebrew praise word describes a different physical action — shining, throwing hands out, plucking strings, raving wildly, bowing. Biblical praise was intensely embodied and varied, not a single uniform activity.

## 79. Humble / Humility
- **Modern understanding:** Thinking lowly of yourself; self-deprecation.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *anav* (עָנָו) — lowly, afflicted, poor, dependent on God. Greek *tapeinos* (ταπεινός) — low position, of low status.
- **What shifted:** Humility isn't self-deprecation — it's accurate self-assessment and awareness of dependence. Moses is called the most *anav* man (Num. 12:3) — not self-hating, but clear-eyed about his position relative to God.

## 80. Wicked
- **Modern understanding:** Extremely evil; morally villainous.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *rasha* (רָשָׁע) — guilty, in the wrong, unjust, one whose actions create social disorder.
- **What shifted:** *Rasha* is legal/social terminology — one who is in the wrong in a dispute, whose behavior destabilizes the community. It's about social disruption more than cartoonish evil.

## 81. Selah
- **Modern understanding:** Largely ignored; often assumed to mean "amen" or "pause."
- **Original term:** Hebrew *selah* (סֶלָה) — unknown meaning, possibly a musical instruction. Likely from *salal* — to lift up, to exalt.
- **What shifted:** Appearing 71 times in Psalms and 3 times in Habakkuk, *selah* may be an instruction to raise the volume, insert an instrumental interlude, or elevate the intensity. The honest answer is that no one is certain.

## 82. Minister / Ministry
- **Modern understanding:** A clergy member; professional religious leadership.
- **Original terms:** Greek *diakonos* (διάκονος) — a table servant, waiter, one who serves food. *Diakonia* (διακονία) — the act of serving, especially waiting tables.
- **What shifted:** When Paul calls himself a *diakonos*, he's calling himself a waiter. The term was deliberately undignified — the opposite of the professional clerical class it eventually produced.

## 83. Tithe
- **Modern understanding:** Giving 10% of income to a church.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *maaser* (מַעֲשֵׂר) — a tenth, a tenth portion of agricultural produce.
- **What shifted:** The tithe system was agricultural, not monetary. It functioned as a communal food redistribution system for Levites, foreigners, orphans, and widows (Deut. 14:28-29). It was a social safety net, not a church budget line item.

## 84. Amen
- **Modern understanding:** A closing word for prayers, meaning "the end" or "I agree."
- **Original term:** Hebrew *amen* (אָמֵן) — from *aman* — firm, established, reliable, trustworthy. "So be it" or "this is firm/true."
- **What shifted:** *Amen* is an affirmation of solidity and truth — "this stands firm." Jesus uses it as an opener ("Amen, amen I say to you") — asserting reliability before speaking, not closing afterward.

## 85. Altar
- **Modern understanding:** A table at the front of a church.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *mizbeach* (מִזְבֵּחַ) — from *zavach* (זָבַח) — to slaughter. A place of slaughter.
- **What shifted:** An altar was specifically a place where animals were killed and their blood/fat offered. The modern church "altar" has no slaughter function — the word has been entirely domesticated.

## 86. Priest
- **Modern understanding:** A religious official who leads services and administers sacraments.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *kohen* (כֹּהֵן) — one who stands before God, mediates between divine and human, handles sacred objects, and maintains the temple.
- **What shifted:** Israelite priests were a hereditary class (Levitical/Aaronic) with specific physical duties — handling blood, maintaining fire, diagnosing skin diseases (Leviticus 13). They were more like sacred technicians than spiritual counselors.

## 87. Clean / Unclean
- **Modern understanding:** Morally good / morally bad; hygienic vs. dirty.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *tahor* (טָהוֹר) — ritually pure, in a state fit for encountering the sacred. *Tamei* (טָמֵא) — ritually impure, in a state unfit for sacred space.
- **What shifted:** Clean/unclean had nothing to do with morality or hygiene. Childbirth made you *tamei* — not because it was sinful but because contact with the powerful forces of life and death required a reset period before re-entering sacred space.

## 88. Sabbath
- **Modern understanding:** Sunday church attendance; a day of religious obligation.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *shabbat* (שַׁבָּת) — from *shavat* — to cease, to stop, to rest. The seventh day, Saturday, not Sunday.
- **What shifted:** The shift from Saturday to Sunday happened gradually in early Christianity. But more importantly, *shabbat* is designed as a weekly act of counter-cultural resistance — refusing to let productivity define your worth. It's economic protest, not religious obligation.

## 89. Abomination
- **Modern understanding:** Something God especially hates; the worst possible sin.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *toevah* (תּוֹעֵבָה) — something culturally taboo, a boundary violation, often related to pagan worship practices or social boundary-crossing. Also *sheqets* (שֶׁקֶץ) — detestable, used specifically for forbidden foods.
- **What shifted:** *Toevah* is used for eating shrimp, dishonest business practices, and various cult practices. It's a category of boundary violation, not a moral superlative. Context determines severity.

## 90. Gentile
- **Modern understanding:** A non-Jewish person, an outsider to God's people.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *goy* (גּוֹי) — nation, people. Plural *goyim* — the nations. Greek *ethnos* (ἔθνος) — a people group, nation, ethnic group.
- **What shifted:** *Goy* simply means "nation" — Israel is also called a *goy* in several passages (Gen. 12:2). The exclusively negative "outsider" connotation developed over time. *Ethnos* is the root of "ethnic" — it just means a people group.

## 91. Gospel of the Kingdom
- **Modern understanding:** A message about getting to heaven after death.
- **Original terms:** Greek *euangelion tēs basileias* — the good news of the reign/rule.
- **What shifted:** Jesus's primary message was that God's active governance was breaking into the present world order. This was a revolutionary political announcement, not a personal afterlife insurance policy. The "kingdom" message was about transformed reality *now*.

## 92. Resurrect / Resurrection
- **Modern understanding:** Souls going to heaven; spiritual continuation after death.
- **Original term:** Greek *anastasis* (ἀνάστασις) — literally "standing up again," a bodily rising from death. *Egeirō* (ἐγείρω) — to wake up, to rise.
- **What shifted:** The early Jewish and Christian concept was emphatically physical — bodily resurrection, not disembodied souls floating upward. The Greek philosophical preference for escaping the body influenced later theology away from the original physicality.

## 93. Flesh and Blood
- **Modern understanding:** Human nature; being a physical person.
- **Original term:** The Hebrew/Aramaic idiom *basar vadam* (בָּשָׂר וָדָם) and Greek *sarx kai haima* (σὰρξ καὶ αἷμα) — mortal human existence in its frailty and limitation.
- **What shifted:** When Paul says "flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom" (1 Cor. 15:50), he doesn't mean physical bodies are excluded — he means mortal, perishable existence must be transformed. The body isn't rejected; it's upgraded.

## 94. Inheritance
- **Modern understanding:** Something received after someone dies; a heavenly reward.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *nachalah* (נַחֲלָה) — portion, allotment, territory, share. Greek *klēronomia* (κληρονομία) — lot, allotment, inheritance.
- **What shifted:** In the OT, inheritance is primarily about *land* — physical territory. The "inheritance of the saints" isn't an abstract spiritual reward but participation in the renewed creation. It's earthy, not ethereal.

## 95. Justification
- **Modern understanding:** Being declared legally innocent; having your criminal record wiped clean.
- **Original term:** Greek *dikaiōsis* (δικαίωσις) — the act of making right, setting things in proper order, restoring to right relationship.
- **What shifted:** The courtroom metaphor dominates modern understanding, but *dikaiōsis* is broader — it's about restoring right relationships and proper order. It's relational restoration, not just legal acquittal.

## 96. Sanctification
- **Modern understanding:** A gradual moral improvement process; becoming sinless.
- **Original term:** Greek *hagiasmos* (ἁγιασμός) — the process of being set apart, dedicated, consecrated for a purpose.
- **What shifted:** Connected to *qadosh* (holy = set apart), sanctification is about being dedicated and purposed, not necessarily about moral perfection. A tool set apart for a specific task is "sanctified" for that use.

## 97. Redemption
- **Modern understanding:** Being saved from sin through Jesus's sacrifice.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *padah* (פָּדָה) — to ransom, to buy back. *Gaal* (גָּאַל) — to act as kinsman-redeemer, to reclaim family property, to restore what was lost. Greek *apolytrōsis* (ἀπολύτρωσις) — release through ransom, liberation.
- **What shifted:** Redemption is marketplace and family language — buying back a slave, reclaiming lost family land, liberating a prisoner. The kinsman-redeemer (*goel*) concept is central in Ruth. It's about concrete restoration of status and belonging.

## 98. Crucifixion (theological term)
- **Modern understanding:** A religious symbol; the mechanism of salvation.
- **Original reality:** Greek *stauros* (σταυρός) — an upright stake or cross. Roman crucifixion was the most shameful form of execution, reserved for slaves, rebels, and enemies of the state.
- **What shifted:** The cross was a political instrument of terror — Rome's message: "this is what happens to those who challenge us." Early Christians claiming a crucified man was Lord was not spiritual poetry — it was direct political defiance of Roman power.

## 99. Suffering
- **Modern understanding:** Something to be avoided; punishment from God; or a test of faith.
- **Original term:** Greek *paschō* (πάσχω) — to experience, to undergo, to be affected by (root of "passion"). Hebrew *anah* (עָנָה) — to be afflicted, pressed down.
- **What shifted:** *Paschō* is about undergoing experience — not exclusively negative. The "passion of Christ" doesn't mean "the punishment of Christ" but "the experience of Christ." Suffering is framed as transformative passage, not mere punishment.

## 100. Paradise
- **Modern understanding:** Heaven; the final destination of souls.
- **Original term:** Greek *paradeisos* (παράδεισος) — borrowed from Persian *pairi-daēza* — an enclosed royal garden, a king's park.
- **What shifted:** When Jesus tells the thief "today you will be with me in *paradeisos*" (Luke 23:43), the word evokes a king's private garden — a place of intimacy with the ruler, not a celestial location. It's relational access, not geographic destination.

## 101. Revelation
- **Modern understanding:** The last book of the Bible; end-times prediction; scary future events.
- **Original term:** Greek *apokalypsis* (ἀποκάλυψις) — an uncovering, an unveiling, a disclosure of what was hidden.
- **What shifted:** The book of Revelation is not primarily about predicting the future — it's about *unveiling* present spiritual realities behind visible political events. The genre (apocalyptic literature) uses symbolic imagery to expose hidden truths about power, not to provide a timeline.

## 102. Antichrist
- **Modern understanding:** A single future world leader who opposes God.
- **Original term:** Greek *antichristos* (ἀντίχριστος) — one who is against or in place of Christ. The prefix *anti-* can mean "opposed to" or "instead of."
- **What shifted:** The word appears only in 1 John and 2 John — never in Revelation. John says "many antichrists have already come" (1 John 2:18) and defines them as anyone denying Jesus as Christ. It's a present-tense category, not a single future individual.

## 103. Rapture
- **Modern understanding:** Believers being suddenly snatched up to heaven before tribulation.
- **Original term:** The word "rapture" doesn't appear in the Bible. It derives from Latin *rapturo* in the Vulgate for 1 Thessalonians 4:17, translating Greek *harpagēsometha* (ἁρπαγησόμεθα) — "caught up, seized, snatched."
- **What shifted:** The passage uses royal welcome imagery — citizens going out to *meet* a returning king and escorting him back into the city. The movement is toward earth, not away from it. The modern rapture doctrine was developed in the 1830s by John Nelson Darby.

## 104. Tribulation
- **Modern understanding:** A future seven-year period of global catastrophe.
- **Original term:** Greek *thlipsis* (θλίψις) — pressure, affliction, distress, crushing (like grapes in a winepress).
- **What shifted:** Jesus says "in this world you will have *thlipsis*" (John 16:33) — present tense, ongoing reality. Paul describes his own experiences as *thlipsis*. The word describes normal pressure of faithful living in a hostile system, not a future apocalyptic period.

## 105. Miracle
- **Modern understanding:** A supernatural violation of natural laws.
- **Original terms:** Greek *sēmeion* (σημεῖον) — a sign, indicator, pointer. *Teras* (τέρας) — a wonder, marvel. *Dynamis* (δύναμις) — a powerful act, display of capability.
- **What shifted:** The NT uses three different words where English uses one. A *sēmeion* points beyond itself. A *teras* provokes wonder. A *dynamis* displays power. They're not about violating physics — they're about revealing reality.

## 106. Sin Offering
- **Modern understanding:** An animal killed as punishment in the sinner's place.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *chattat* (חַטָּאת) — the same word as "sin" (*chata*). More accurately: a purification offering or de-sinning offering.
- **What shifted:** The *chattat* wasn't about punishing the animal instead of the person. It was a purification ritual — the blood cleansed the sacred space contaminated by human sin. The sanctuary needed cleaning, not God needing payment.

## 107. Woe
- **Modern understanding:** A curse or condemnation, expressing God's anger.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *hoy* (הוֹי) — a funeral cry, a lamentation, "alas!" Greek *ouai* (οὐαί) — an expression of grief, sorrow, pity.
- **What shifted:** When Jesus says "woe to you, Pharisees," the tone is closer to grief and lament — "how tragic for you" — than to "I condemn you." It's the sound of a funeral, not a verdict.

## 108. Idol / Idolatry
- **Modern understanding:** Worshipping statues; a primitive religious practice.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *pesel* (פֶּסֶל) — a carved image. *Elil* (אֱלִיל) — a worthless thing, a nothing. *Teraphim* (תְּרָפִים) — household gods.
- **What shifted:** The prophets don't just condemn worshipping statues — they mock the *concept* of reducing the infinite to something manageable. *Elil* literally means "nothing" — idols are nothings people treat as somethings. The critique is epistemological, not just religious.

## 109. Wisdom
- **Modern understanding:** Being smart; making good decisions; general intelligence.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *chokmah* (חָכְמָה) — skill, craftsmanship, practical expertise, the ability to navigate reality successfully. Greek *sophia* (σοφία) — wisdom, philosophical insight.
- **What shifted:** In Proverbs, *chokmah* is personified as a woman present at creation (Prov. 8). *Chokmah* includes practical skills — Bezalel is filled with *chokmah* for metalwork and weaving (Exodus 31:3). Wisdom is craftsmanship for living, not abstract intelligence.

## 110. Confess / Confession
- **Modern understanding:** Admitting guilt for wrongdoing; telling a priest your sins.
- **Original terms:** Hebrew *yadah* (יָדָה) — to throw out, to extend, to declare openly (also means "to praise"). Greek *homologeō* (ὁμολογέω) — to say the same thing, to agree, to declare publicly.
- **What shifted:** *Homologeō* literally means "same-word" — to align your words with reality. Confession isn't primarily about guilt — it's about honest declaration, aligning what you say with what is true. The same word family covers both confessing sin and confessing faith.

## 111. Name
- **Modern understanding:** A label for identification; what someone is called.
- **Original term:** Hebrew *shem* (שֵׁם) — name, reputation, character, nature, essence, authority.
- **What shifted:** In Hebrew thought, a name *is* the person's nature. When God changes someone's name (Abram → Abraham, Jacob → Israel), their nature is being transformed. "In the name of Jesus" doesn't mean "using Jesus as a magic word" — it means "in the character, nature, and authority of Jesus." Acting in someone's *shem* means acting as their authorized representative carrying their full identity.
