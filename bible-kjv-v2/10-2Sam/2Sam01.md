# 2 Samuel 1

[1] After the death of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, when David had returned from striking down the ⟦amalekites|Amaleqim|Amalekites⟧, David remained two days in ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧.

[2] On the third day, behold, a man came from ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧'s camp, with his clothes torn and earth on his head. When he came to David, he fell to the ground and did homage.

[3] David said to him, "Where do you come from?" He said to him, "I have escaped from the camp of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧."

[4] David said to him, "How did the matter go? Please tell me." He answered, "The people have fled from the battle; many of the people also have fallen and are dead; ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ his son are also dead."

[5] David said to the young man who told him, "How do you know that ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and his son ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ are dead?"

[6] The young man who told him said, "By chance I happened to be on Mount Gilboa; and behold, ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ was leaning on his spear, and the chariots and the horsemen were pressing hard after him.

[7] When he looked behind him, he saw me and called to me. I answered, 'Here I am.'

[8] He said to me, 'Who are you?' I answered him, 'I am an ⟦amalekites|Amaleqi|Amalekite⟧.'

[9] He said to me, 'Please stand over me and kill me, for anguish has seized me, though my life is still in me.'

[10] So I stood over him and killed him, because I was sure that he could not live after he had fallen. I took the crown that was on his head and the armlet that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."

[11] Then David took hold of his clothes and tore them; so did all the men who were with him.

[12] They mourned and wept and fasted until evening for ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and for ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ his son, for the people of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ and for the house of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, because they had fallen by the sword.

[13] David said to the young man who told him, "Where are you from?" He answered, "I am the son of a foreigner, an ⟦amalekites|Amaleqi|Amalekite⟧."

[14] David said to him, "How were you not afraid to put out your hand to destroy the ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧ of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧?"

[15] David called one of the young men and said, "Go near, fall upon him." So he struck him down, and he died.

[16] David said to him, "Your blood be on your own head, for your own mouth has testified against you, saying, 'I have killed the ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧ of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.'"

[17] David sang this lament over ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and over ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ his son.

[18] He ordered that the men of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ be taught it — the Bow — behold, it is written in the Book of ⟦jashar|Yashar|Jashar⟧:

[19] "Your glory, O ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, is slain on your high places!
How the mighty have fallen!

[20] Tell it not in ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧;
proclaim it not in the streets of ⟦ashkelon|Ashqelon|Ashkelon⟧;
lest the daughters of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ rejoice,
lest the daughters of the uncircumcised triumph.

[21] Mountains of Gilboa —
may there be no dew or rain upon you,
nor fields yielding tribute;
for there the shield of the mighty was defiled,
the shield of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, not anointed with oil.

[22] From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty,
the bow of ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ did not turn back,
and the sword of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ did not return empty.

[23] ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧, beloved and delightful in life,
and in death they were not divided;
swifter than eagles,

[24] Daughters of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, weep for ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧,
who clothed you in scarlet with delights,

[25] How the mighty have fallen in the midst of battle!
⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ lies slain on your high places.

[26] I grieve for you, my brother ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧;

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"bracelet" → "armlet"** (v10) — Hebrew אֶצְעָדָה *etz'adah*. Royal arm-band.
- **"Book of Jasher" → "Book of Yashar"** (v18).
- **"Askelon" → "Ashkelon"** (v20) — standard modern spelling.
- **"vilely cast away" → "defiled"** (v21).
- **"stranger" → "foreigner"** (v13).
- **"use of the bow" / "the Bow"** (v18) — Hebrew קָשֶׁת *qeshet* — possibly the lament is called "The Bow" (from v22), or the Judahites were to be taught archery via the lament's example.

### Notes
- **The Amalekite's false report** — he claims to have killed Saul at his request. Contradicts 1 Sam 31 (Saul fell on his sword). Either (a) Amalekite fabricates to earn David's gratitude; (b) 1 Sam 31 tells a different death-moment. Most interpreters: Amalekite is lying.
- **David executes the messenger** — "anointed of Yahweh" principle again. David does not reward the death of the king, even a rival. Sets up David's pattern of vengeance on those who killed Saul's family (2 Sam 4).
- **Lament for Saul and Jonathan** (v19–27) — one of the greatest laments in world literature. David's personal grief + public eulogy. No hint of triumph.
- **"Tell it not in Gath"** (v20) — becomes proverbial phrase for mourning concealment. Mic 1:10 echoes.
- **"Your love to me was wonderful, surpassing the love of women"** (v26) — intimacy-language for the Jonathan-David friendship. Much debated modern interpretation.
- **Davidic Bow** (v18) — possibly the lament is titled *ha-qeshet* ("the bow"); taught to Judah as a song. Preserved in the lost *Book of Yashar*.
