# 2 Samuel 13

[1] And it came to pass after this that ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ the son of David had a beautiful sister, and her name was Tamar; and Amnon the son of David loved her.

[2] And Amnon was distressed, so as to make himself sick, for the sake of Tamar his sister, for she was a virgin, and it was too wonderful in the eyes of Amnon to do anything to her.

[3] And Amnon had a friend, and his name was ⟦jonadab|Yonadav|Jonadab⟧ the son of ⟦shimeah|Shimah|Shimeah⟧ the brother of David; and ⟦jonadab|Yonadav|Jonadab⟧ was a very wise man.

[4] And he said to him, "Why are you so low, son of the king, morning by morning? Will you not tell me?" And Amnon said to him, "Tamar, the sister of ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ my brother, I love."

[5] And ⟦jonadab|Yonadav|Jonadab⟧ said to him, "Lie on your bed and make yourself sick, and your father will come to see you, and you shall say to him, 'Let Tamar my sister come, please, and make me eat bread, and let her make the food before my eyes, so that I may see and eat from her hand.'"

[6] And Amnon lay down and made himself sick, and the king came to see him, and Amnon said to the king, "Let Tamar my sister come, please, and make two cakes before my eyes, and I will eat from her hand."

[7] And David sent to Tamar, to the house, saying, "Go, please, to the house of Amnon your brother and make the food for him."

[8] And Tamar went to the house of Amnon her brother, and he was lying down. And she took the dough and kneaded it and made cakes before his eyes and boiled the cakes.

[9] And she took the pan and poured out before him, and he refused to eat. And Amnon said, "Bring out every man from me." And every man went out from him.

[10] And Amnon said to Tamar, "Bring the food into the chamber, and I will eat from your hand." And Tamar took the cakes that she had made and brought them to Amnon her brother into the chamber.

[11] And she brought them near to him to eat, and he took hold of her and said to her, "Come, lie with me, my sister."

[12] And she said to him, "No, my brother, do not humble me, for it is not done so in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧. Do not do this outrage.

[13] And I, where would I carry my reproach? And you, you would be like one of the fools in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧. And now, speak, please, to the king, for he will not hold me back from you."

[14] And he was not willing to listen to her voice, and he was stronger than she and humbled her and lay with her.

[15] And Amnon hated her with a very great hatred, for greater was the hatred with which he hated her than the love with which he had loved her. And Amnon said to her, "Arise, go."

[16] And she said to him, "No, on account of this great evil, more than the other that you did with me, to send me away." And he was not willing to listen to her.

[17] And he called his young man, his minister, and said, "Send this one away from me, please, outside, and lock the door after her."

[18] And upon her was a long tunic, for so the daughters of the king, the virgins, would wear robes. And his minister brought her outside and locked the door after her.

[19] And Tamar took ashes upon her head, and the long tunic that was upon her she tore; and she put her hand on her head and went, going and crying out.

[20] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ her brother said to her, "Has Amnon your brother been with you? And now, my sister, be silent; he is your brother. Do not set your heart to this thing." And Tamar dwelt, and desolate, in the house of ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ her brother.

[21] And King David heard all these things, and he burned with anger greatly.

[22] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ did not speak with Amnon, from evil to good, for ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ hated Amnon, on account of his having humbled Tamar his sister.

[23] And it came to pass after two years of days that ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ had shearers in ⟦baal-hazor|Baal-Chatzor|Baal-hazor⟧, which is beside ⟦ephraim-town|Efrayim|Ephraim⟧, and ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ called all the sons of the king.

[24] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ came to the king and said, "Behold, please, your servant has shearers. Let the king, please, and his servants go with your servant."

[25] And the king said to ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧, "No, my son, let us not all go, please, and we will not be heavy upon you." And he pressed upon him, and he was not willing to go, and he blessed him.

[26] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ said, "And if not, let Amnon my brother go with us, please." And the king said to him, "Why should he go with you?"

[27] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ pressed upon him, and he sent with him Amnon and all the sons of the king.

[28] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ commanded his young men, saying, "See, please, when the heart of Amnon is good with the wine, and I say to you, 'Strike Amnon,' then you shall put him to death. Do not fear. Is it not I who have commanded you? Be strong and be sons of ability."

[29] And the young men of ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ did to Amnon as ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ had commanded. And all the sons of the king arose and rode, each man on his mule, and fled.

[30] And it came to pass, while they were on the way, that the report came to David, saying, "⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ has struck down all the sons of the king, and not one of them remains."

[31] And the king arose and tore his garments and lay on the earth, and all his servants were standing by with torn garments.

[32] And ⟦jonadab|Yonadav|Jonadab⟧ the son of ⟦shimeah|Shimah|Shimeah⟧ the brother of David answered and said, "Let not my lord say, 'All the young men, the sons of the king, they have put to death,' for Amnon alone is dead; for upon the mouth of ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ it has been set from the day of his humbling Tamar his sister.

[33] And now, let not my lord the king set a word to his heart, saying, 'All the sons of the king are dead,' but Amnon alone is dead."

[34] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ fled. And the young man, the watchman, lifted up his eyes and saw, and behold, many people were coming from the way behind him, from the side of the mountain.

[35] And ⟦jonadab|Yonadav|Jonadab⟧ said to the king, "Behold, the sons of the king have come; according to the word of your servant, so it has been."

[36] And it came to pass, as he finished speaking, that behold, the sons of the king came, and they lifted up their voice and wept; and also the king and all his servants wept a very great weeping.

[37] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ fled and went to Talmai the son of Ammihud, the king of Geshur. And he mourned over his son all the days.

[38] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ fled and went to Geshur, and he was there three years.

[39] And David the king ceased to go out to ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧, for he was comforted over Amnon, for he was dead.

## Changes

- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"had a fair sister" → "had a beautiful sister"** (v1) — Hebrew יָפָה.
- **"Amnon was so vexed, that he fell sick for his sister Tamar" → "Amnon was distressed, so as to make himself sick, for the sake of Tamar his sister"** (v2) — Hebrew וַיֵּצֶר לְאַמְנוֹן, literally "it was narrow for Amnon"; לְהִתְחַלּוֹת, the reflexive of "be sick" ("make himself sick"; the same form in v5, 6, where it is done on purpose). **"Amnon thought it hard for him to do any thing to her" → "it was too wonderful in the eyes of Amnon to do anything to her"** — Hebrew וַיִּפָּלֵא בְּעֵינֵי, "it was beyond (him) in the eyes of": the verb of Gen 18:14; here "out of reach".
- **"a very subtil man" → "a very wise man"** (v3) — Hebrew חָכָם, the ordinary word "wise"; what kind of wisdom it was the story shows.
- **"Why art thou, being the king's son, lean from day to day?" → "Why are you so low, son of the king, morning by morning?"** (v4) — Hebrew דַּל, "low, weak, poor" ("poor" in the word list; here it would say "without money", so "low" is used); בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר, "in the morning, in the morning".
- **"Jonadab"** (v5) — the name is written here in its longer form, יְהוֹנָדָב (Jehonadab); in v3, 32, 35 יוֹנָדָב. **"give me meat, and dress the meat in my sight" → "make me eat bread, and let her make the food before my eyes"** — Hebrew וְתַבְרֵנִי לֶחֶם … הַבִּרְיָה: the rare verb בָּרָה "eat" (12:17; causative here, as 3:35) and its noun בִּרְיָה "food", found only in this chapter (v5, 7, 10); the verb is used of food taken by one who is sick or mourning (3:35).
- **"make me a couple of cakes" → "make two cakes"** (v6, 8, 10) — Hebrew וּתְלַבֵּב … שְׁתֵּי לְבִבוֹת, verb and noun of one root, the root of לֵבָב "heart" ("heart-cakes"); the word is found only here and what kind of food it was is not known. **"did bake the cakes" → "boiled the cakes"** (v8) — Hebrew בִּשֵּׁל, "boil, cook".
- **"she took a pan"** (v9) — Hebrew הַמַּשְׂרֵת, found only here; "pan" is the old understanding. **"Have out all men from me" → "Bring out every man from me"** — Hebrew הוֹצִיאוּ כָל־אִישׁ מֵעָלַי, "make every man go out from upon me".
- **"he took hold of her"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיַּחֲזֶק־בָּהּ, of the root "be strong" (v14 "he was stronger than she", v28 "be strong").
- **"do not force me … he forced her" → "do not humble me … humbled her"** (v12, 14, 22, 32) — Hebrew עִנָּה, "afflict, bring low, humble": the verb of Gen 34:2; Deut 22:24, 29. The word itself does not say force; that is said here by "he was not willing to listen to her voice, and he was stronger than she" (v14). **"no such thing ought to be done in Israel" → "it is not done so in Israel"** — Hebrew לֹא־יֵעָשֶׂה כֵן (Gen 34:7). **"this folly" → "this outrage"** — Hebrew נְבָלָה, the deed of the נָבָל, the base fool (Gen 34:7; Judg 19:23; 20:6).
- **"whither shall I cause my shame to go?" → "where would I carry my reproach?"** (v13) — Hebrew אוֹלִיךְ, "make go"; חֶרְפָּה "reproach". **"as one of the fools in Israel"** — Hebrew הַנְּבָלִים, of one root with "outrage" in v12: base, godless men. **"he will not withhold me from thee" → "he will not hold me back from you"** — Hebrew מָנַע.
- **"being stronger than she, forced her, and lay with her" → "he was stronger than she and humbled her and lay with her"** (v14) — Hebrew וַיֶּחֱזַק מִמֶּנָּה וַיְעַנֶּהָ וַיִּשְׁכַּב אֹתָהּ, three verbs as they stand. The last is pointed with the object marker, literally "he lay her" (as Gen 34:2), not "with her" (אִתָּהּ).
- **"hated her exceedingly" → "hated her with a very great hatred"** (v15) — Hebrew שִׂנְאָה גְּדוֹלָה מְאֹד; שָׂנֵא "hate" and אָהֵב "love" (v1, 4, 15) are the plain words.
- **"There is no cause: this evil in sending me away is greater than the other that thou didst unto me" → "No, on account of this great evil, more than the other that you did with me, to send me away"** (v16) — Hebrew אַל־אוֹדֹת הָרָעָה הַגְּדוֹלָה הַזֹּאת מֵאַחֶרֶת אֲשֶׁר־עָשִׂיתָ עִמִּי לְשַׁלְּחֵנִי, word for word "no (do not) — on account of this great evil, than the other that you did with me — to send me away". The sentence is broken and difficult; it is translated as printed. The sense commonly taken is "No, my brother, for this evil, to send me away, is greater than the other that you did with me" (so in part the Septuagint, from memory).
- **"his servant that ministered unto him" → "his young man, his minister"** (v17, 18) — Hebrew נַעֲרוֹ מְשָׁרְתוֹ. **"Put now this woman out from me" → "Send this one away from me, please, outside"** — Hebrew שִׁלְחוּ (plural) … אֶת־זֹאת, "this one" (feminine), with no word "woman"; שִׁלַּח is the verb of v16. **"bolt the door" → "lock the door"**.
- **"a garment of divers colours" → "a long tunic"** (v18, 19) — Hebrew כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים, the garment of Joseph (Gen 37:3, 23, 32) and found nowhere else. כְּתֹנֶת is a tunic; the meaning of פַּסִּים is uncertain. The best-supported sense takes it from פַּס, the flat of the hand or foot: a tunic reaching to the palms and the soles. "Of many colours" is the rendering of the Septuagint in Genesis (from memory). One rendering is kept in both places, without certainty. **"for with such robes were the king's daughters that were virgins apparelled" → "for so the daughters of the king, the virgins, would wear robes"** — Hebrew כִּי כֵן תִּלְבַּשְׁןָ … מְעִילִים: the last word, "robes" (מְעִיל, the outer robe), fits loosely beside the tunic, and it is often changed to "from of old" (מֵעוֹלָם); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"put ashes on her head" → "took ashes upon her head"** (v19) — Hebrew וַתִּקַּח. **"went on crying" → "went, going and crying out"** — Hebrew וַתֵּלֶךְ הָלוֹךְ וְזָעָקָה.
- **"Amnon"** (v20) — written here only אֲמִינוֹן (Aminon). **"hold now thy peace … regard not this thing" → "be silent … Do not set your heart to this thing"** — Hebrew הַחֲרִישִׁי; אַל־תָּשִׁיתִי אֶת־לִבֵּךְ. **"So Tamar remained desolate" → "And Tamar dwelt, and desolate"** — Hebrew וַתֵּשֶׁב תָּמָר וְשֹׁמֵמָה, with an "and" before "desolate" that English would leave out; שֹׁמֵמָה is "desolate, laid waste" (the word of a ruined land).
- **"he was very wroth" → "he burned with anger greatly"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיִּחַר לוֹ מְאֹד, literally "it burned to him greatly". The Masoretic Text ends the verse here. The Septuagint and a Samuel scroll from Qumran add "and he did not grieve the spirit of Amnon his son, for he loved him, for he was his firstborn" (from memory); it is not in the Masoretic Text and not in the verse.
- **"neither good nor bad" → "from evil to good"** (v22) — Hebrew לְמֵרָע וְעַד־טוֹב, "from evil and to good": not a word of any kind. **"because he had forced" → "on account of his having humbled"** — Hebrew עַל־דְּבַר אֲשֶׁר עִנָּה, "on account of the matter that he humbled".
- **"after two full years" → "after two years of days"** (v23) — Hebrew לִשְׁנָתַיִם יָמִים. **"sheepshearers" → "shearers"** — Hebrew גֹּזְזִים; no "sheep" is named. **"invited" → "called"** — Hebrew וַיִּקְרָא.
- **"lest we be chargeable unto thee" → "and we will not be heavy upon you"** (v25) — Hebrew וְלֹא נִכְבַּד עָלֶיךָ. **"he pressed him" → "he pressed upon him"** (v25, 27) — Hebrew וַיִּפְרָץ־בּוֹ, as printed: the verb "break out" (as 1 Sam 28:23); many read וַיִּפְצַר "urged".
- **"when Amnon's heart is merry with wine" → "when the heart of Amnon is good with the wine"** (v28) — Hebrew כְּטוֹב לֵב. **"Smite Amnon; then kill him" → "'Strike Amnon,' then you shall put him to death"** — Hebrew הַכּוּ … וַהֲמִתֶּם. **"be courageous, and be valiant" → "Be strong and be sons of ability"** — Hebrew חִזְקוּ וִהְיוּ לִבְנֵי־חָיִל.
- **"tidings came" → "the report came"** (v30) — Hebrew הַשְּׁמֻעָה, "what is heard". **"hath slain" → "has struck down"** — Hebrew הִכָּה.
- **"lay on the earth; and all his servants stood by with their clothes rent"** (v31) — Hebrew אַרְצָה "to the earth"; קְרֻעֵי בְגָדִים "torn of garments".
- **"by the appointment of Absalom this hath been determined" → "upon the mouth of Absalom it has been set"** (v32) — Hebrew עַל־פִּי אַבְשָׁלוֹם הָיְתָה שׂוּמָה, word for word; שׂוּמָה is a form found only here ("a thing set, settled"; uncertain).
- **"take the thing to his heart" → "set a word to his heart"** (v33) — Hebrew אַל־יָשֵׂם … אֶל־לִבּוֹ דָּבָר; דָּבָר is "word" or "thing".
- **"the young man that kept the watch" → "the young man, the watchman"** (v34) — Hebrew הַנַּעַר הַצֹּפֶה. **"there came much people by the way of the hill side behind him" → "many people were coming from the way behind him, from the side of the mountain"** — Hebrew מִדֶּרֶךְ אַחֲרָיו מִצַּד הָהָר, word for word; "behind him" is difficult, and the Septuagint has a longer text naming a road (from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"wept very sore" → "wept a very great weeping"** (v36) — Hebrew בְּכִי גָּדוֹל מְאֹד.
- **"Ammihud"** (v37) — the reading tradition (qere); the written text (ketiv) has "Ammihur". **"And David mourned for his son every day" → "And he mourned over his son all the days"** — the Hebrew does not name who mourned; "David" was supplied. הִתְאַבֵּל "mourn"; כָּל־הַיָּמִים "all the days".
- **"And the soul of king David longed to go forth unto Absalom" → "And David the king ceased to go out to Absalom"** (v39) — Hebrew וַתְּכַל דָּוִד הַמֶּלֶךְ לָצֵאת אֶל־אַבְשָׁלוֹם. The verse is difficult: the verb is feminine and "David the king" is masculine, so a feminine subject seems to be missing (the KJV supplies "the soul of"; a Samuel scroll from Qumran is reported to read "the spirit of the king", from memory). The verb as pointed is כִּלָּה, "finish, bring to an end, cease"; the related sense "be spent with longing" (Ps 84:2) gives "longed to go out to Absalom". "Go out to" can be friendly or hostile ("go out against"). The verse is translated as printed with the verb's ordinary sense, and nothing is supplied; the next chapter (14:1, 24, 28) shows that the king did not bring Absalom back of himself. **"he was comforted concerning Amnon, seeing he was dead" → "he was comforted over Amnon, for he was dead"**.
- **"servant(s)"** (v24, 31, 35, 36) — Hebrew עֶבֶד, "slave"; of a king's men and of the speaker before the king "servant".

### Notes
- **Tamar** — Absalom's full sister (3:3); Amnon is David's firstborn (3:2).
- **"Speak to the king"** (v13) — whether such a marriage could have been allowed is not said here (Lev 18:9; Deut 27:22 forbid it).
- **The king** (v21) — he burned with anger; nothing more is told of what he did.
- **Two years** (v23) — Absalom said nothing (v22) and gave the command at the shearing (v28).
- **Geshur** (v37–38) — Talmai was the father of Absalom's mother (3:3).
