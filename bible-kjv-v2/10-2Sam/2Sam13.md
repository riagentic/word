# 2 Samuel 13

[1] After this, Absalom son of David had a beautiful sister, whose name was Tamar; and Amnon son of David loved her.

[2] Amnon was so tormented that he made himself sick for Tamar his sister, for she was a virgin, and it seemed impossible to Amnon to do anything to her.

[3] But Amnon had a friend whose name was Jonadab, the son of Shimeah, David's brother; Jonadab was a very crafty man.

[4] He said to him, "Why are you, the king's son, so haggard morning after morning? Will you not tell me?" Amnon said to him, "I love Tamar, my brother Absalom's sister."

[5] Jonadab said to him, "Lie down on your bed and pretend to be sick; when your father comes to see you, say to him, 'Please let my sister Tamar come and give me bread to eat, and prepare the food in my sight, that I may see it and eat it from her hand.'"

[6] So Amnon lay down and pretended to be sick. When the king came to see him, Amnon said to the king, "Please let my sister Tamar come and make a couple of cakes in my sight, that I may eat from her hand."

[7] David sent home to Tamar, saying, "Go now to your brother Amnon's house and prepare food for him."

[8] Tamar went to her brother Amnon's house; he was lying down. She took dough, kneaded it, made cakes in his sight, and baked the cakes.

[9] She took the pan and emptied it before him, but he refused to eat. Amnon said, "Send out every man from me." So every man went out from him.

[10] Amnon said to Tamar, "Bring the food into the inner chamber, that I may eat from your hand." Tamar took the cakes she had made and brought them into the inner chamber to Amnon her brother.

[11] When she brought them near him to eat, he seized her and said to her, "Come, lie with me, my sister."

[12] She answered him, "No, my brother, do not violate me; for such a thing is not done in Israel; do not do this outrage.

[13] As for me, where could I take my shame? And as for you, you would be as one of the outrageous fools in Israel. Now therefore please speak to the king, for he will not withhold me from you."

[14] But he would not listen to her voice; being stronger than she was, he violated her and lay with her.

[15] Then Amnon hated her with very great hatred, so that the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her. Amnon said to her, "Get up! Go!"

[16] She said to him, "No, my brother! For this wrong in sending me away is greater than the other that you did to me." But he would not listen to her.

[17] He called the young man who served him and said, "Put this woman out of my presence and bolt the door after her."

[18] She had on a long-sleeved robe, for such were the garments the king's virgin daughters wore. His servant put her out and bolted the door after her.

[19] Tamar put ashes on her head, tore the long-sleeved robe that she wore, laid her hand on her head, and went away, crying aloud as she went.

[20] Absalom her brother said to her, "Has Amnon your brother been with you? Now be silent, my sister; he is your brother; do not take this to heart." So Tamar dwelt, a desolate woman, in her brother Absalom's house.

[21] When King David heard of all these things, he was very angry. [But he would not punish Amnon his son, because he loved him, since he was his firstborn.]

[22] Absalom spoke to Amnon neither good nor bad, for Absalom hated Amnon because he had violated his sister Tamar.

[23] After two full years, Absalom had sheepshearers at Baal-hazor, which is beside Ephraim; Absalom invited all the king's sons.

[24] Absalom came to the king and said, "Behold now, your servant has sheepshearers; please let the king and his servants go with your servant."

[25] The king said to Absalom, "No, my son, let us not all go, lest we be burdensome to you." He pressed him, but he would not go; yet he blessed him.

[26] Then Absalom said, "If not, please let my brother Amnon go with us." The king said to him, "Why should he go with you?"

[27] But Absalom pressed him, and he let Amnon and all the king's sons go with him.

[28] Absalom commanded his young men, "Watch now, when Amnon's heart is merry with wine, and when I say to you, 'Strike Amnon,' then kill him. Do not fear; have not I myself commanded you? Be courageous and valiant."

[29] The young men of Absalom did to Amnon as Absalom had commanded. Then all the king's sons arose, and every man mounted his mule and fled.

[30] While they were on the way, the report came to David: "Absalom has struck down all the king's sons, and not one of them is left."

[31] Then the king arose, tore his garments, and lay on the earth; all his servants stood by with torn garments.

[32] Jonadab the son of Shimeah, David's brother, spoke up and said, "Let not my lord suppose that they have killed all the young men, the king's sons, for Amnon alone is dead. For by the command of Absalom this has been determined from the day Amnon violated his sister Tamar.

[33] Now therefore let not my lord the king take the thing to heart, thinking that all the king's sons are dead; for Amnon alone is dead."

[34] But Absalom fled. The young man who kept watch lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, much people were coming from the road behind him, by the side of the hill.

[35] Jonadab said to the king, "Behold, the king's sons have come; as your servant said, so it is."

[36] As soon as he had finished speaking, behold, the king's sons came and lifted up their voices and wept; the king also and all his servants wept very bitterly.

[37] But Absalom fled and went to Talmai son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. David mourned for his son every day.

[38] So Absalom fled and went to Geshur, and was there three years.

[39] King David's soul longed to go out to Absalom; for he had been consoled concerning Amnon, seeing that he was dead.

## Changes

- **"subtil" → "crafty"** (v3) — archaic.
- **"folly" → "outrage"** (v12) — *nevalah*.
- **"garment of divers colours" → "long-sleeved robe"** (v18, 19) — Hebrew כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים *ketonet passim*. Same term as Joseph's "coat" (Gen 37:3). Likely ankle-length / long-sleeved royal garment.
- **"force" / "forced" → "violate" / "violated"** (v12, 14, 22, 32) — Hebrew *innah*.
- **MT v21 omits the phrase about Amnon being firstborn**, but DSS (4QSamᵃ) and LXX preserve it. Added in brackets as context.

### Notes
- **The fulfillment begins** — Nathan's prophecy of "sword shall not depart from your house" (12:10) starts unfolding. Amnon's sin echoes David's sin with Bathsheba.
- **Tamar's careful speech** (v12–13) — tries to reason Amnon out of rape. Proposes legitimate marriage ("speak to the king") — an attempted escape.
- **Amnon's inversion of love to hatred** (v15) — classic pattern of rape: desire gratified turns to revulsion. Double wrong: rape + dismissal.
- **David's failure** (v21) — very angry but takes no action. Compromised moral authority after Bathsheba affair. Protects firstborn Amnon.
- **Absalom's revenge** (v22–29) — waits two years, then murders Amnon at sheepshearing feast. Deliberate, long-planned.
- **Geshur exile** (v37–38) — Absalom flees to his maternal grandfather (David's father-in-law Talmai). Three-year exile.
- **David's divided heart** (v39) — mourns Amnon; longs for Absalom. The tension will erupt in ch 14.
