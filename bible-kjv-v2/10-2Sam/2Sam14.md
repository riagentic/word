# 2 Samuel 14

[1] And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ the son of ⟦zeruiah|Tzeruyah|Zeruiah⟧ knew that the heart of the king was upon ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧.

[2] And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ sent to ⟦tekoa|Teqoa|Tekoa⟧ and took from there a wise woman, and he said to her, "Mourn, please, and put on, please, garments of mourning, and do not anoint yourself with oil, and be like a woman who these many days has been mourning over one dead.

[3] And you shall come to the king and speak to him according to this word." And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ put the words in her mouth.

[4] And the woman of ⟦tekoa|Teqoa|Tekoa⟧ said to the king — and she fell on her face to the earth and bowed down — and she said, "Save, O king!"

[5] And the king said to her, "What is it with you?" And she said, "Truly I am a widow woman, and my husband died.

[6] And your maidservant had two sons, and the two of them struggled in the field, and there was no deliverer between them, and the one struck the other and put him to death.

[7] And behold, the whole clan has risen against your maidservant, and they have said, 'Give the one who struck down his brother, and we will put him to death for the soul of his brother whom he killed, and we will destroy the heir also.' And they will quench my coal that is left, so as not to set for my husband a name and a remnant on the face of the ground."

[8] And the king said to the woman, "Go to your house, and I will give command concerning you."

[9] And the woman of ⟦tekoa|Teqoa|Tekoa⟧ said to the king, "On me, my lord the king, be the iniquity, and on the house of my father; and the king and his throne are clear."

[10] And the king said, "The one who speaks to you, bring him to me, and he shall no more again touch you."

[11] And she said, "Let the king, please, remember ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that the redeemer of blood may not ruin yet more, and they shall not destroy my son." And he said, "As ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ lives, not a hair of your son shall fall to the earth."

[12] And the woman said, "Let your maidservant, please, speak a word to my lord the king." And he said, "Speak."

[13] And the woman said, "And why have you devised a thing like this against the people of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧? And in speaking this word the king is as one guilty, in that the king does not bring back his driven-away one.

[14] For we shall surely die, and are like the waters poured out to the earth, which are not gathered; and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ does not lift up a soul, and he devises devices so that one driven away may not be driven away from him.

[15] And now, that I have come to speak this word to the king my lord, it is because the people made me afraid; and your maidservant said, 'Let me speak, please, to the king; perhaps the king will do the word of his slave woman.

[16] For the king will hear, to deliver his slave woman from the palm of the man, to destroy me and my son together from the inheritance of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.'

[17] And your maidservant said, 'Let the word of my lord the king, please, be for rest, for as an angel of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, so is my lord the king, to hear the good and the evil.' And may ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ be with you."

[18] And the king answered and said to the woman, "Do not hide from me, please, a thing that I am asking you." And the woman said, "Let my lord the king speak, please."

[19] And the king said, "Is the hand of ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ with you in all this?" And the woman answered and said, "By the life of your soul, my lord the king, there is no going to the right or to the left from all that my lord the king has spoken; for your servant ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧, he commanded me, and he put in the mouth of your maidservant all these words.

[20] In order to turn about the face of the matter your servant ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ did this thing. And my lord is wise, according to the wisdom of an angel of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, to know all that is on the earth."

[21] And the king said to ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧, "Behold, please, I have done this thing; and go, bring back the young man, ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧."

[22] And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ fell on his face to the earth and bowed down and blessed the king. And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ said, "Today your servant knows that I have found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, in that the king has done the word of your servant."

[23] And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ arose and went to Geshur and brought ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[24] And the king said, "Let him turn to his house, and my face he shall not see." And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ turned to his house, and the face of the king he did not see.

[25] And like ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ there was no man so beautiful in all ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, to be greatly praised; from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him.

[26] And when he shaved his head — and it was at the end of every year that he shaved it, for it was heavy on him, and he shaved it — he weighed the hair of his head: two hundred shekels by the weight of the king.

[27] And there were born to ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ three sons and one daughter, and her name was Tamar; she was a woman beautiful in appearance.

[28] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ dwelt in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ two years of days, and the face of the king he did not see.

[29] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ sent to ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧, to send him to the king, and he was not willing to come to him. And he sent yet a second time, and he was not willing to come.

[30] And he said to his servants, "See, the portion of ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ is next to mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it on fire." And the servants of ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ set the portion on fire.

[31] And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ arose and came to ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧, to the house, and said to him, "Why have your servants set the portion that is mine on fire?"

[32] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ said to ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧, "Behold, I sent to you, saying, 'Come here, and let me send you to the king, saying, "Why have I come from Geshur? It would be good for me were I still there."' And now, let me see the face of the king, and if there is iniquity in me, then let him put me to death."

[33] And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ came to the king and told him. And he called ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧, and he came to the king and bowed down to him on his face to the earth before the king, and the king kissed ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 20) — Hebrew *Elohim*; in v17, 20 with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"the king's heart was toward Absalom" → "the heart of the king was upon Absalom"** (v1) — Hebrew עַל, "upon": the word does not say whether his heart was for Absalom or against him (עַל is also "against", v7, 13).
- **"feign thyself to be a mourner" → "Mourn, please"** (v2) — Hebrew הִתְאַבְּלִי, the ordinary reflexive form "mourn" (v2 end; 13:37); "feign" is not in the word. **"fetched" → "took"**. **"a woman that had a long time mourned for the dead" → "a woman who these many days has been mourning over one dead"** — Hebrew זֶה יָמִים רַבִּים; מֵת "a dead one".
- **"And when the woman of Tekoah spake to the king, she fell" → "And the woman of Tekoa said to the king — and she fell … — and she said"** (v4) — Hebrew וַתֹּאמֶר "said" twice, as printed; many Hebrew copies and the versions have "came" (וַתָּבֹא) for the first (from memory). הַתְּקֹעִית is "the Tekoite (woman)". **"did obeisance" → "bowed down"**. **"Help, O king" → "Save, O king"** — Hebrew הוֹשִׁעָה.
- **"What aileth thee?" → "What is it with you?"** (v5) — Hebrew מַה־לָּךְ, "what to you?". **"I am indeed a widow woman, and mine husband is dead" → "Truly I am a widow woman, and my husband died"** — Hebrew אֲבָל "truly".
- **"thy handmaid" → "your maidservant" / "his slave woman"** (v6, 7, 12, 15, 17, 19 / v15, 16) — Hebrew שִׁפְחָה "maidservant" and אָמָה "slave woman"; the woman uses both of herself.
- **"they two strove together in the field, and there was none to part them" → "the two of them struggled in the field, and there was no deliverer between them"** (v6) — Hebrew וַיִּנָּצוּ, "fight with the hands"; מַצִּיל "one who delivers". **"smote the other, and slew him" → "struck the other and put him to death"**.
- **"the whole family" → "the whole clan"** (v7) — Hebrew מִשְׁפָּחָה. **"Deliver him that smote his brother, that we may kill him, for the life of his brother whom he slew" → "Give the one who struck down his brother, and we will put him to death for the soul of his brother whom he killed"** — Hebrew תְּנִי "give"; בְּנֶפֶשׁ "for the soul of". **"shall not leave to my husband neither name nor remainder upon the earth" → "so as not to set for my husband a name and a remnant on the face of the ground"** — Hebrew שִׂים (qere), שְׁאֵרִית "remnant", הָאֲדָמָה "the ground".
- **"I will give charge concerning thee" → "I will give command concerning you"** (v8) — Hebrew אֲצַוֶּה.
- **"the king and his throne be guiltless" → "the king and his throne are clear"** (v9) — Hebrew נָקִי, with no verb.
- **"he shall not touch thee any more" → "he shall no more again touch you"** (v10) — Hebrew וְלֹא־יֹסִיף עוֹד לָגַעַת, "he shall not add again to touch".
- **"that thou wouldest not suffer the revengers of blood to destroy any more" → "that the redeemer of blood may not ruin yet more"** (v11) — Hebrew מֵהַרְבַּת (qere) גֹּאֵל הַדָּם לְשַׁחֵת, literally "from the redeemer of blood's making much to ruin". גֹּאֵל הַדָּם is "the redeemer of blood" (Num 35:19): the kinsman whose duty it is to take the life of the killer; "avenger" is not the word. שִׁחֵת is "ruin"; "destroy" in the next clause is הִשְׁמִיד (as v7, 16). **"there shall not one hair of thy son fall to the earth" → "not a hair of your son shall fall to the earth"** — an oath, literally "if there falls of the hair of your son to the earth".
- **"Wherefore then hast thou thought such a thing" → "And why have you devised a thing like this"** (v13) — Hebrew חָשַׁבְתָּה, the verb of v14 ("devises devices", וְחָשַׁב מַחֲשָׁבוֹת). **"for the king doth speak this thing as one which is faulty, in that the king doth not fetch home again his banished" → "And in speaking this word the king is as one guilty, in that the king does not bring back his driven-away one"** — Hebrew וּמִדַּבֵּר הַמֶּלֶךְ הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה כְּאָשֵׁם, word for word "and from the king's speaking this word, as one guilty": difficult. מִדַּבֵּר is pointed as a participle ("speaking", as Num 7:89) and can also be read as "from speaking"; "the king is" is supplied. נִדְּחוֹ is "his one who is thrust out, driven away" (Deut 30:4).
- **"For we must needs die, and are as water spilt on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again; neither doth God respect any person: yet doth he devise means, that his banished be not expelled from him" → "For we shall surely die, and are like the waters poured out to the earth, which are not gathered; and Elohim does not lift up a soul, and he devises devices so that one driven away may not be driven away from him"** (v14) — the verse is given word for word. כִּי־מוֹת נָמוּת, "dying we shall die". וְלֹא־יִשָּׂא אֱלֹהִים נֶפֶשׁ, "and Elohim does not lift up a soul": the words are plain and the sense is disputed. It is commonly taken as "Elohim does not take away a life" (נָשָׂא "lift, bear, take away"), that is, he does not make an end of the one driven away; the KJV took it as "does not show regard to a person" (that is elsewhere נָשָׂא פָּנִים, "lift up the face"). וְחָשַׁב מַחֲשָׁבוֹת, "and he devises devices (thinks thoughts)": the subject is not named again and is most naturally Elohim; some take it as "but (the king) should devise". לְבִלְתִּי יִדַּח מִמֶּנּוּ נִדָּח, "so that a driven-away one may not be driven away from him", verb and noun of one root. The earlier text ("But Elohim does not take away life; he devises plans so that a banished one is not expelled from his presence") was a smoothing; "but", "plans" and "presence" are not in the Hebrew.
- **"it is because the people have made me afraid"** (v15) — Hebrew כִּי יֵרְאֻנִי הָעָם. The speech of v15–17 turns back to the woman's own case; where it stands in her speech is difficult, and it is left as printed.
- **"out of the hand of the man that would destroy me and my son together" → "from the palm of the man, to destroy me and my son together"** (v16) — Hebrew מִכַּף הָאִישׁ לְהַשְׁמִיד, with no "that would" (it is commonly supplied; the Septuagint has "who seeks", from memory); כַּף "palm". **"the inheritance of Elohim"** — Hebrew נַחֲלַת אֱלֹהִים.
- **"The word of my lord the king shall now be comfortable" → "Let the word of my lord the king, please, be for rest"** (v17) — Hebrew יִהְיֶה־נָּא … לִמְנוּחָה, "for a rest, a resting place". **"as an angel of God" → "as an angel of Elohim"** (v17, 20) — Hebrew כְּמַלְאַךְ הָאֱלֹהִים; מַלְאָךְ is "messenger". With the article the words can also be read "as the angel of Elohim"; the wording of 1 Sam 29:9 is kept. **"to discern good and bad" → "to hear the good and the evil"** — Hebrew לִשְׁמֹעַ. **"therefore the LORD thy God will be with thee" → "And may Yahweh your Elohim be with you"** — Hebrew יְהִי, a wish.
- **"Hide not from me … the thing that I shall ask thee"** (v18) — Hebrew דָּבָר, "a word, a thing".
- **"As thy soul liveth … none can turn to the right hand or to the left" → "By the life of your soul … there is no going to the right or to the left"** (v19) — Hebrew חֵי־נַפְשְׁךָ; אִם־אִשׁ לְהֵמִין וּלְהַשְׂמִיל, literally "if there is to go right and to go left": the "if" of an oath means "there is not"; אִשׁ is an unusual spelling of יֵשׁ "there is".
- **"To fetch about this form of speech" → "In order to turn about the face of the matter"** (v20) — Hebrew לְבַעֲבוּר סַבֵּב אֶת־פְּנֵי הַדָּבָר. **"to know all things that are in the earth" → "to know all that is on the earth"** — or "in the land".
- **"I have done this thing"** (v21) — Hebrew עָשִׂיתִי, a perfect: the thing is granted as he speaks. **"bring the young man Absalom again" → "bring back the young man, Absalom"**.
- **"thanked the king" → "blessed the king"** (v22) — Hebrew וַיְבָרֶךְ. **"the request of his servant" → "the word of your servant"** — the qere "your servant"; the ketiv "his servant".
- **"Let him turn to his own house"** (v24) — Hebrew יִסֹּב.
- **"there was none to be so much praised as Absalom for his beauty" → "like Absalom there was no man so beautiful in all Israel, to be greatly praised"** (v25) — Hebrew אִישׁ־יָפֶה … לְהַלֵּל מְאֹד.
- **"when he polled his head, (for it was at every year's end that he polled it …)" → "when he shaved his head — and it was at the end of every year that he shaved it …"** (v26) — Hebrew גִּלַּח "shave"; מִקֵּץ יָמִים לַיָּמִים, literally "from the end of days to the days": "days" stands for a year (1 Sam 27:7). **"two hundred shekels after the king's weight" → "two hundred shekels by the weight of the king"** — Hebrew בְּאֶבֶן הַמֶּלֶךְ, "by the stone of the king". The number is that of the Masoretic Text; the Septuagint has "a hundred" in some copies (from memory). A shekel is a weight.
- **"a woman of a fair countenance" → "a woman beautiful in appearance"** (v27) — Hebrew יְפַת מַרְאֶה.
- **"two full years" → "two years of days"** (v28) — Hebrew שְׁנָתַיִם יָמִים (13:23).
- **"Joab's field is near mine" → "the portion of Joab is next to mine"** (v30, 31) — Hebrew חֶלְקָה, "a portion (of field)"; אֶל־יָדִי, "at my hand". **"set it on fire"** — the qere וְהַצִּיתוּהָ; the ketiv has "and I will set it on fire".
- **"it had been good for me to have been there still" → "It would be good for me were I still there"** (v32) — Hebrew טוֹב לִי עֹד אֲנִי־שָׁם, "good for me, still I there". **"let him kill me" → "let him put me to death"** — Hebrew הֵמִית.
- **"bowed himself on his face to the ground"** (v33) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ לוֹ עַל־אַפָּיו אַרְצָה; אַפַּיִם is "nostrils, face" (v4).
- **"servant(s)"** (v19, 20, 22, 30, 31) — Hebrew עֶבֶד, "slave"; of a king's men and of the speaker before a superior "servant".

### Notes
- **The woman of Tekoa** (v2–20) — her words are Joab's (v3, 19); the case she tells is like Nathan's in chapter 12, a story on which the king gives judgment before he knows it is his own.
- **v13–17** — the hard places of her speech are left as the Hebrew has them; see the Changes for each.
- **Absalom's hair** (v26) — see 18:9.
- **Three sons** (v27) — 18:18 says "I have no son"; the text does not explain.
- **The king's face** (v24, 28, 32–33) — two years in Jerusalem without seeing it.
