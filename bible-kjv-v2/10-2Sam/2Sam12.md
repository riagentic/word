# 2 Samuel 12

[1] Yahweh sent Nathan to David. He came to him and said to him, "There were two men in a certain city, one rich and the other poor.

[2] The rich man had very many flocks and herds;

[3] but the poor man had nothing except one little ewe lamb that he had bought and raised. It grew up with him and with his children; it ate of his food and drank from his cup and lay in his bosom, and was to him like a daughter.

[4] A traveler came to the rich man, and he was unwilling to take one of his own flock or herd to prepare for the wayfarer who had come to him; but he took the poor man's lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him."

[5] David's anger burned greatly against the man, and he said to Nathan, "As Yahweh lives, the man who has done this deserves to die!

[6] He shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing and had no pity."

[7] Nathan said to David, "You are the man! Thus says Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel: 'I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul.

[8] I gave you your master's house and your master's wives into your bosom, and gave you the house of Israel and of Judah; and if that were too little, I would add to you as much more.

[9] Why have you despised the word of Yahweh, to do what is evil in his eyes? You have struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword and have taken his wife to be your wife; you have killed him with the sword of the children of Ammon.

[10] Now therefore the sword shall never depart from your house, because you have despised me and have taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.'

[11] Thus says Yahweh: 'Behold, I will raise up evil against you out of your own house; I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he shall lie with your wives in the sight of this very sun.

[12] For you did it in secret; but I will do this thing before all Israel and before the sun.'"

[13] David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against Yahweh." Nathan said to David, "Yahweh also has put away your sin; you shall not die.

[14] But because by this deed you have utterly spurned Yahweh, the child born to you shall surely die."

[15] Nathan went to his house. Yahweh struck the child that Uriah's wife bore to David, and it became very sick.

[16] David therefore sought *Elohim* on behalf of the child; David fasted and went in and lay all night on the ground.

[17] The elders of his house stood by him, to raise him up from the ground; but he would not, nor would he eat food with them.

[18] On the seventh day the child died. The servants of David were afraid to tell him that the child was dead; for they said, "Behold, while the child was yet alive, we spoke to him, and he would not listen to our voice; how then can we say to him that the child is dead? He may do himself harm."

[19] But when David saw that his servants were whispering together, David perceived that the child was dead. David said to his servants, "Is the child dead?" They said, "He is dead."

[20] Then David arose from the ground, washed, anointed himself, and changed his clothes; he went into the house of Yahweh and worshiped. Then he came to his own house, and when he asked, they set food before him, and he ate.

[21] His servants said to him, "What is this thing you have done? You fasted and wept for the child while it was alive, but when the child died, you arose and ate food."

[22] He said, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept, for I said, 'Who knows whether Yahweh will be gracious to me, that the child may live?'

[23] But now he is dead. Why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me."

[24] David comforted his wife Bathsheba; he went in to her and lay with her; and she bore a son, and he called his name Solomon. Yahweh loved him,

[25] and sent a word by the hand of Nathan the prophet; so he called his name Jedidiah ("Beloved of Yahweh"), because of Yahweh.

[26] Joab fought against Rabbah of the children of Ammon and captured the royal city.

[27] Joab sent messengers to David and said, "I have fought against Rabbah; I have also captured the city of waters.

[28] Now therefore gather the rest of the people together and encamp against the city and take it, lest I take the city and it be called by my name."

[29] So David gathered all the people together and went to Rabbah, fought against it, and captured it.

[30] He took the crown of Milcom from upon his head; its weight was a talent of gold, with a precious stone. It was placed on David's head. He brought forth the spoil of the city in great abundance.

[31] The people who were in it he brought out and set to work with saws and iron picks and iron axes, and made them labor at the brick-kilns. So he did to all the cities of the children of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"king's crown" → "crown of Milcom"** (v30) — Hebrew *atereth malkam*. Either "their king" or the Ammonite god "Milcom." Given the huge weight (a talent = ~75 pounds), likely a god-statue's crown — unwearable by a human, but symbolic of appropriation.
- **"put under saws, etc." → "set to work with saws"** (v31) — Hebrew *vayasem... ba-megerah*. Debated: (1) tortured them with saws / iron picks, or (2) put them to forced labor with those tools. Modern translations increasingly favor labor; KJV's lurid reading softened here (same verb in 1 Chr 20:3 supports "forced labor" reading).
- **"Jedidiah" → "*Yedid-yah* — Beloved of Yahweh"** (v25) — etymology.
- **"blaspheme" → "utterly spurned"** (v14) — Hebrew נִאֵץ נִאַצְתָּ *ni'atz ni'atzta*. Dead Sea Scrolls preserve fuller reading: "you have caused the enemies of Yahweh utterly to scorn." Follows MT short form.

### Notes
- **Nathan's parable** — brilliant prophetic diplomacy. David condemns himself unknowingly. "You are the man" reveals.
- **Fourfold restitution** (v6) — David later loses four sons: the infant, Amnon, Absalom, Adonijah. Self-pronounced sentence.
- **Sword on house** (v10) — prophetic consequence. Amnon murdered (ch 13), Absalom killed (ch 18), civil war, more.
- **"Your neighbor will lie with your wives"** — fulfilled by Absalom (ch 16:21–22).
- **David's repentance** (v13) — immediate, unqualified. Pss 32, 51 traditionally assigned here.
- **Solomon / Jedidiah** — two names, one significance: the child Yahweh loves. Despite the scandalous origin, Solomon is chosen.
- **Joab's fidelity-check** (v26–28) — insists on David's presence for the conquering blow. Loyal servant.
