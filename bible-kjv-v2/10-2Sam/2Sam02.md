# 2 Samuel 2

[1] After this David inquired of Yahweh, "Shall I go up into any of the cities of Judah?" Yahweh said to him, "Go up." David said, "Where shall I go up?" He said, "To Hebron."

[2] So David went up there, and his two wives also, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite.

[3] David also brought up the men who were with him, every man with his household; and they lived in the towns of Hebron.

[4] The men of Judah came, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah. It was told David, "The men of Jabesh-gilead were the ones who buried Saul."

[5] David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and said to them, "May you be blessed by Yahweh, because you showed this loyalty to Saul your lord and buried him.

[6] Now may Yahweh show loyalty and faithfulness to you. I also will reward you with good because you have done this thing.

[7] Now therefore let your hands be strengthened, and be valiant; for your lord Saul is dead, and the house of Judah has anointed me king over them."

[8] But Abner son of Ner, commander of Saul's army, had taken Ishbosheth son of Saul and brought him over to Mahanaim.

[9] He made him king over Gilead, over the Ashurites, over Jezreel, over Ephraim, over Benjamin, and over all Israel.

[10] Ishbosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he began to reign over Israel, and he reigned two years. But the house of Judah followed David.

[11] The length of time David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

[12] Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ishbosheth son of Saul went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

[13] Joab son of Zeruiah and the servants of David also went out; they met by the pool of Gibeon and sat down, one on one side of the pool and the other on the other side of the pool.

[14] Abner said to Joab, "Let the young men arise and hold a contest before us." Joab said, "Let them arise."

[15] So they arose and passed over by number: twelve for Benjamin and for Ishbosheth son of Saul, and twelve of the servants of David.

[16] Each caught his opponent by the head and thrust his sword into his opponent's side, so they fell down together. Therefore that place was called Helkath-hazzurim ("Field of Flint-Edges") in Gibeon.

[17] The battle that day was very fierce; Abner and the men of Israel were defeated before the servants of David.

[18] The three sons of Zeruiah were there — Joab, Abishai, and Asahel. Asahel was swift of foot as a gazelle of the field.

[19] Asahel pursued Abner; he did not turn aside to the right hand or to the left from following Abner.

[20] Then Abner looked behind him and said, "Is it you, Asahel?" He answered, "It is I."

[21] Abner said to him, "Turn aside to your right hand or to your left, and seize one of the young men and take his spoil." But Asahel would not turn aside from following him.

[22] Abner again said to Asahel, "Turn aside from following me; why should I strike you to the ground? How then could I lift up my face to Joab your brother?"

[23] But he refused to turn aside. So Abner struck him in the belly with the butt of his spear, so that the spear came out at his back; and he fell there and died on the spot. All who came to the place where Asahel fell and died stood still.

[24] But Joab and Abishai pursued Abner. When the sun was going down, they came to the hill of Ammah, which lies before Giah by the way of the wilderness of Gibeon.

[25] The children of Benjamin gathered together behind Abner and formed a single band; they stood on the top of a hill.

[26] Then Abner called to Joab, "Shall the sword devour forever? Do you not know that it will be bitter in the end? How long will you refuse to tell the people to turn from pursuing their brothers?"

[27] Joab said, "As *Elohim* lives, if you had not spoken, then surely in the morning the people would have gone up, each one from following his brother."

[28] So Joab blew the trumpet, and all the people stopped and no longer pursued Israel or fought anymore.

[29] Abner and his men walked all that night through the Arabah; they crossed the Jordan, marched through all Bithron, and came to Mahanaim.

[30] Joab returned from following Abner; when he had gathered all the people together, there were missing from David's servants nineteen men and Asahel.

[31] But the servants of David had struck down of Benjamin and of Abner's men so that 360 men died.

[32] They took up Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb, which was in Bethlehem. Joab and his men marched all night; the day broke upon them at Hebron.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"captain" → "commander"** (v8).
- **"roe" → "gazelle"** (v18).
- **"hinder end of the spear" → "butt of his spear"** (v23).
- **"fifth rib" → "belly"** (v23) — Hebrew חֹמֶשׁ *chomesh* = under-the-ribs / belly. Debated; likely the soft-tissue region.
- **"kindness" → "loyalty"** (v5, 6) — *chesed*.

### Notes
- **Civil war** — Saul's commander Abner installs Ishbosheth ("man of shame," likely Esh-baal "man of Baal" modified). David rules Judah; Ishbosheth nominally the north.
- **Hebron** — David's capital for 7.5 years. Southern Judahite center.
- **Jabesh-gilead diplomacy** (v5–7) — David honors those who honored Saul. Political and personal.
- **Twelve-on-twelve contest** (v14–16) — possibly ritual combat / ordeal. All 24 die simultaneously; no decision. Triggers full battle.
- **Asahel's death** — Abner kills David's nephew reluctantly. Creates blood-feud with Joab.
- **Joab's character** introduced — ruthless loyalist-warrior.
- **"Shall the sword devour forever?"** (v26) — Abner's plea ends the day's fighting.
