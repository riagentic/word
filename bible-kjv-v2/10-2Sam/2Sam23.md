## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 3) — Hebrew *Elohim*. **"God" → "El"** (v5) — Hebrew אֵל *El*.
- **"David the son of Jesse said, and the man who was raised up on high … said" → "The declaration of David the son of Jesse, and the declaration of the mighty man who was raised up on high"** (v1) — Hebrew נְאֻם, "declaration", the word used of a prophet's oracle (Num 24:3, 15); גֶּבֶר "mighty man". הֻקַם עָל is rare: "was raised up — on high"; עָל as a noun or adverb, "height, above", stands only a few times. Some read "whom the Most High raised up", and a Hebrew manuscript from Qumran is said to read "whom El raised up" (from memory); the verse keeps the text as printed.
- **"the sweet psalmist of Israel" → "the pleasant one of the songs of Israel"** (v1) — Hebrew נְעִים זְמִרוֹת יִשְׂרָאֵל, literally "pleasant of the songs of Israel": נָעִים "pleasant, lovely"; זְמִרוֹת "songs, music" (from זִמֵּר "make music"). The phrase is rare and its sense is not certain: "the one made pleasant by the songs of Israel", "the singer of Israel's songs", or (taking זִמְרָה as "strength, protection") "the beloved of the Protector of Israel".
- **"The Spirit of the LORD spake by me, and his word was in my tongue" → "The spirit of Yahweh has spoken by me, and his utterance is on my tongue"** (v2) — Hebrew דִּבֶּר־בִּי, "spoke in / by / with me"; מִלָּה "utterance" is another word than דָּבָר "word".
- **"He that ruleth over men must be just, ruling in the fear of God" → "One who rules over man, a righteous one, who rules in the fear of Elohim"** (v3) — Hebrew מוֹשֵׁל בָּאָדָם צַדִּיק מוֹשֵׁל יִרְאַת אֱלֹהִים: two participles with no verb; "must be" is not in the Hebrew. אָדָם is "man, mankind". "In" is supplied: the Hebrew has "ruling the fear of Elohim". Where the saying ends is not marked in the Hebrew.
- **"And he shall be as the light of the morning, when the sun riseth, even a morning without clouds; as the tender grass springing out of the earth by clear shining after rain" → "and like the light of morning when the sun dawns, a morning without thick clouds; from brightness, from rain, vegetation from the earth"** (v4) — Hebrew וּכְאוֹר בֹּקֶר יִזְרַח־שָׁמֶשׁ בֹּקֶר לֹא עָבוֹת מִנֹּגַהּ מִמָּטָר דֶּשֶׁא מֵאָרֶץ. The verse has no verb but "dawns"; "when" is supplied. The last line is three phrases side by side, each with מִן "from" ("from brightness, from rain, vegetation from the earth"); "he shall be", "springing" and "after" are not written. The sense is commonly taken as "through brightness after rain the vegetation comes from the earth". Difficult; nothing is smoothed.
- **"Although my house be not so with God; yet he hath made with me an everlasting covenant … although he make it not to grow" → "For not so is my house with El; for an everlasting covenant he has set for me … for he does not make it sprout"** (v5) — Hebrew כִּי־לֹא־כֵן בֵּיתִי עִם־אֵל כִּי בְרִית עוֹלָם שָׂם לִי עֲרוּכָה בַכֹּל וּשְׁמֻרָה כִּי־כָל־יִשְׁעִי וְכָל־חֵפֶץ כִּי־לֹא יַצְמִיחַ. Four clauses begin with כִּי "for"; two of them have "not". The Hebrew has no mark of a question, and the verse gives the words as they stand. Many read the first and the last clause as questions ("Is not my house so with El? … will he not make it sprout?"), which gives the opposite sense; the KJV reads "although … yet … although". The reader should know that the sense is disputed. "It" is supplied. בְּרִית עוֹלָם is literally "a covenant of the age". עֲרוּכָה "arranged, set in order"; שְׁמֻרָה "kept"; חֵפֶץ "delight"; יַצְמִיחַ "make sprout".
- **"But the sons of Belial shall be all of them as thorns thrust away, because they cannot be taken with hands" → "And Beliyyaal (worthlessness) is like a thorn thrust away, all of them, for not with the hand are they taken"** (v6) — Hebrew וּבְלִיַּעַל alone, the common noun "worthlessness" (no "sons of", no "men"); קוֹץ "thorn" is singular; כֻּלָּהַם "all of them". מֻנָד "thrust away, chased off". יִקָּחוּ is active, "they take": people do not take them with the hand.
- **"must be fenced with iron and the staff of a spear; and they shall be utterly burned with fire in the same place" → "is filled with iron and the shaft of a spear, and with fire they are surely burned in the seat"** (v7) — Hebrew יִמָּלֵא "is filled" (commonly understood "arms himself"); עֵץ חֲנִית "the wood of a spear"; שָׂרוֹף יִשָּׂרְפוּ "burning they are burned". בַּשָּׁבֶת is difficult: "in the sitting, in the seat" (from יָשַׁב), taken as "on the spot, where they are"; the same word stands in the next verse.
- **"mighty men" → "mighty ones"** (v8, 9, 16, 17, 22) — Hebrew גִּבֹּרִים.
- **"The Tachmonite that sat in the seat, chief among the captains; the same was Adino the Eznite: he lift up his spear against eight hundred, whom he slew at one time" → "Josheb-basshebeth a Tahchemonite, head of the officers; he was Adino the Eznite; against eight hundred slain at one time"** (v8) — Hebrew יֹשֵׁב בַּשֶּׁבֶת תַּחְכְּמֹנִי רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁלִשִׁי הוּא עֲדִינוֹ הָעֶצְנִי (qere; ketiv העצנו) עַל־שְׁמֹנֶה מֵאוֹת חָלָל בְּפַעַם אֶחָת (qere; ketiv אחד). The verse is difficult and is given as the Masoretic Text prints it. יֹשֵׁב בַּשֶּׁבֶת is given as a name; as words it is "one sitting in the seat" (so the KJV). הַשָּׁלִשִׁי is taken from שָׁלִישׁ "officer" (literally "third man"); it is also read "the three". עֲדִינוֹ הָעֶצְנִי is given as a name, "Adino the Eznite"; the words are not known elsewhere. The sentence has no verb: "against eight hundred slain"; "he lift up his spear" in the KJV is supplied. The parallel 1 Chr 11:11 reads differently: "Jashobeam the son of a Hachmonite, head of the thirty (qere: the officers); he roused his spear against three hundred slain at one time". Nothing is taken over from Chronicles: neither the name, nor "roused his spear", nor the number.
- **"the son of Dodo the Ahohite" → "the son of Dodo, the son of an Ahohite"** (v9) — Hebrew בֶּן־דֹּדוֹ (qere; ketiv דדי "Dodai") בֶּן־אֲחֹחִי. **"one of the three mighty men"** — qere הַגִּבֹּרִים. **"when they defied the Philistines" → "when they reproached the Philistines"** — Hebrew בְּחָרְפָם בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים. **"were gone away" → "went up"** — Hebrew וַיַּעֲלוּ; whether they went up to battle or went away is not said.
- **"smote the Philistines until his hand was weary" → "struck among the Philistines until his hand was tired"** (v10) — Hebrew וַיַּךְ בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים; יָגְעָה. **"wrought a great victory" → "worked a great salvation"** (v10, 12) — Hebrew תְּשׁוּעָה גְדוֹלָה. **"only to spoil" → "only to strip"** — Hebrew לְפַשֵּׁט.
- **"the Philistines were gathered together into a troop" → "were gathered into a band"** (v11) — Hebrew לַחַיָּה, as pointed "to the band" (חַיָּה in this sense also v13); it is often read as a place name, "at Lehi" (Judg 15:9). **"a piece of ground" → "a portion of the field"** — Hebrew חֶלְקַת הַשָּׂדֶה. "A Hararite" has no article in the Hebrew (הָרָרִי).
- **"defended it" → "delivered it"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיַּצִּילֶהָ.
- **"three of the thirty chief went down, and came to David in the harvest time" → "three of the thirty heads went down and came at harvest to David"** (v13) — qere שְׁלֹשָׁה "three" (ketiv: "thirty"); Hebrew אֶל־קָצִיר, literally "to harvest", which is difficult. **"the troop of the Philistines pitched" → "the band of the Philistines was camping"** — Hebrew חַיַּת פְּלִשְׁתִּים.
- **"in an hold … the garrison" → "in the stronghold … the outpost"** (v14) — Hebrew בַּמְּצוּדָה; מַצַּב.
- **"David longed" → "David craved"** (v15) — Hebrew וַיִּתְאַוֶּה. **"Oh that one would give me drink" → "Who will give me water to drink"** — Hebrew מִי יַשְׁקֵנִי מַיִם, a wish in the form of a question. **"the well of Bethlehem, which is by the gate" → "the pit of Bethlehem that is in the gate"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew בֹּאר, pointed as בּוֹר "pit, cistern" and written with the letters of בְּאֵר "well"; the same word in v20. בַּשָּׁעַר is "in the gate".
- **"brake through the host" → "split into the camp"** (v16) — Hebrew וַיִּבְקְעוּ … בְּמַחֲנֵה. **"would not drink thereof, but poured it out unto the LORD" → "was not willing to drink it, and he poured it out to Yahweh"** — Hebrew וַיַּסֵּךְ, the verb for pouring out a drink offering.
- **"Be it far from me, O LORD, that I should do this: is not this the blood of the men that went in jeopardy of their lives?" → "Far be it from me, Yahweh, from my doing this! The blood of the men who went with their souls?"** (v17) — Hebrew חָלִילָה לִּי יְהוָה מֵעֲשֹׂתִי זֹאת הֲדַם הָאֲנָשִׁים הַהֹלְכִים בְּנַפְשׁוֹתָם. The question has no verb in Samuel (1 Chr 11:19 has "shall I drink"); it is left as it stands. בְּנַפְשׁוֹתָם is "with / at the price of their souls".
- **"chief among three. And he lifted up his spear against three hundred, and slew them" → "head of the three; and he roused his spear against three hundred slain"** (v18) — qere הַשְּׁלֹשָׁה (ketiv השלשי, the word of v8). Hebrew עוֹרֵר אֶת־חֲנִיתוֹ, "roused his spear" (wielded it); "and slew them" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"Was he not most honourable of three? therefore he was their captain: howbeit he attained not unto the first three" → "He was indeed honored above the three, and he became their commander; and as far as the three he did not come"** (v19) — Hebrew מִן־הַשְּׁלֹשָׁה הֲכִי נִכְבָּד, literally "from the three, is it that he was honored?"; הֲכִי is taken as "indeed". The verse is difficult as it stands (honored above the three, yet not coming as far as the three); some versions have "the thirty" in the first place (from memory). "First" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"the son of a valiant man, of Kabzeel, who had done many acts" → "the son of a man of ability, great in deeds, from Kabzeel"** (v20) — qere אִישׁ־חַיִל (ketiv: אִישׁ־חַי "a living man"); רַב־פְּעָלִים.
- **"two lionlike men of Moab" → "the two Ariels of Moab"** (v20) — Hebrew אֵת שְׁנֵי אֲרִאֵל מוֹאָב, "the two ariel of Moab". The meaning of אֲרִאֵל is not known: it is taken as "lion of El" (a champion), as a proper name, or as "altar hearth" (Ezek 43:15). The Greek has "the two sons of Ariel" (from memory); "sons of" is not in the Hebrew and was removed from the verse.
- **"a lion in the midst of a pit in time of snow" → "the lion in the midst of the pit on the day of the snow"** (v20) — the three nouns have the article; qere הָאֲרִי.
- **"a goodly man" → "a man of appearance"** (v21) — qere אִישׁ מַרְאֶה (ketiv אשר מראה): a man to look at; 1 Chr 11:23 has "a man of measure, five cubits". **"a staff" → "the rod"** — Hebrew בַּשָּׁבֶט. **"plucked the spear" → "robbed the spear"** — Hebrew וַיִּגְזֹל, "took by robbery, tore away".
- **"had the name among three mighty men" → "had a name among the three mighty ones"** (v22).
- **"more honourable than the thirty, but he attained not to the first three. And David set him over his guard" → "Above the thirty he was honored, and to the three he did not come; and David set him to his bodyguard"** (v23) — Hebrew מִן־הַשְּׁלֹשִׁים נִכְבָּד; אֶל־מִשְׁמַעְתּוֹ, literally "to his hearing, his bidding": those who obey his call (uncertain).
- **Names of the thirty** (v24–39) — each name follows the Hebrew as printed. v24 בֵּית לָחֶם, "Bethlehem", with no preposition. v29 "the sons of Benjamin" (בְּנֵי). v30 "a Pirathonite": no article. v32 בְּנֵי יָשֵׁן יְהוֹנָתָן, "the sons of Jashen, Jonathan": the words stand side by side; how they belong together is not clear (1 Chr 11:34 has "the sons of Hashem the Gizonite, Jonathan the son of Shageh the Hararite"). v33 the second gentilic is written הָארָרִי ("the Ararite"), the first הַהֲרָרִי "the Hararite"; both are given as "Hararite", one name in two spellings. v35 "Hezrai" is the qere (ketiv: "Hezro"). v36 "Bani the Gadite" (1 Chr 11:38 has "Mibhar the son of Hagri"). v37 "weapon-bearer" is the qere, singular (ketiv: plural); literally "bearer of the gear". The list in 1 Chr 11:26–47 differs in many names and is longer.
- **"thirty and seven in all" → "in all, thirty-seven"** (v39) — Hebrew כֹּל שְׁלֹשִׁים וְשִׁבְעָה. The total is given as printed; how the thirty-seven are counted (the names of v24–39, the three, Abishai, Benaiah) is not said.

### Notes
- **The last words of David** (v1–7) — a poem in the form of a declaration (נְאֻם). Several lines are difficult; see Changes.
- **"An everlasting covenant"** (v5) — compare 2 Sam 7.
- **The three** (v8–12) — Josheb-basshebeth, Eleazar, Shammah.
- **The water of Bethlehem** (v13–17) — David pours it out to Yahweh and calls it the blood of the men.
- **The thirty** (v24–39) — the parallel list is 1 Chr 11:26–47.
- **Uriah the Hittite** (v39) — the last name in the list (2 Sam 11).
