# 2 Samuel 11

[1] In the spring, at the time when kings go out to battle, David sent Joab with his servants and all Israel; they ravaged the children of Ammon and besieged Rabbah. But David remained at Jerusalem.

[2] One evening David arose from his bed and walked on the roof of the king's house. From the roof he saw a woman bathing; the woman was very beautiful in appearance.

[3] David sent and inquired about the woman. One said, "Is not this Bathsheba daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?"

[4] David sent messengers and took her; she came to him, and he lay with her (she had just purified herself from her uncleanness); then she returned to her house.

[5] The woman conceived and sent word to David, saying, "I am pregnant."

[6] David sent word to Joab, "Send me Uriah the Hittite." So Joab sent Uriah to David.

[7] When Uriah came to him, David inquired how Joab was doing, how the people were faring, and how the war was going.

[8] David said to Uriah, "Go down to your house and wash your feet." Uriah went out of the king's house, and a present from the king was sent after him.

[9] But Uriah slept at the door of the king's house with all the servants of his lord; he did not go down to his house.

[10] When it was told David, "Uriah did not go down to his house," David said to Uriah, "Have you not come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?"

[11] Uriah said to David, "The ark and Israel and Judah are dwelling in booths; my lord Joab and the servants of my lord are encamped in the open field. Shall I then go to my house to eat and drink and lie with my wife? As you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this thing."

[12] David said to Uriah, "Remain here today also, and tomorrow I will send you away." So Uriah remained in Jerusalem that day and the next.

[13] David invited him, and he ate and drank in his presence, and David made him drunk. But in the evening he went out to lie on his bed with the servants of his lord; he did not go down to his house.

[14] In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by the hand of Uriah.

[15] He wrote in the letter, "Set Uriah in the forefront of the hardest fighting, and draw back from him, that he may be struck down and die."

[16] While Joab was besieging the city, he assigned Uriah to a place where he knew there were valiant men.

[17] The men of the city came out and fought with Joab; some of the people — some of the servants of David — fell; Uriah the Hittite also died.

[18] Joab sent and told David all the news about the war.

[19] He commanded the messenger, "When you have finished telling all the news of the war to the king,

[20] then, if the king's anger rises, and he says to you, 'Why did you go so near the city to fight? Did you not know that they would shoot from the wall?

[21] Who struck down Abimelech son of Yerubbesheth? Did not a woman throw an upper millstone on him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez? Why did you go so near the wall?' — then you shall say, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead also.'"

[22] So the messenger went, and came and told David all that Joab had sent him to tell.

[23] The messenger said to David, "The men gained an advantage against us and came out against us in the field, but we drove them back up to the entrance of the gate.

[24] The archers shot at your servants from the wall; some of the king's servants are dead; your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead also."

[25] David said to the messenger, "Thus shall you say to Joab: 'Let not this matter displease you, for the sword devours one as well as another. Press your attack harder against the city and overthrow it.' And encourage him."

[26] When the wife of Uriah heard that Uriah her husband was dead, she mourned for her husband.

[27] When the mourning was over, David sent and brought her to his house; she became his wife and bore him a son. But the thing that David had done was evil in the eyes of Yahweh.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"after the year was expired" → "in the spring"** (v1) — Hebrew *li-teshuvat ha-shanah*. Turn of the year — early spring campaign season.
- **"mess of meat" → "present"** (v8) — Hebrew מַשְׂאַת *mas'at*. Gift, sent from the king's table.
- **"Jerubbesheth" → "Yerubbesheth"** (v21) — alternate form of Jerubbaal (Gideon), with *bosheth* substitution for *baal*.

### Notes
- **"In the spring, when kings go out" but David stays home** — narrative irony. David's absence from war becomes occasion for sin.
- **Bathsheba's bath** — may have been ritual purification bath after menstruation (v4 "purified from her uncleanness" suggests this). Establishes that the child is certainly David's.
- **Uriah the Hittite** — one of David's mighty men (ch 23:39). Loyal foreign soldier.
- **Uriah's integrity** (v11) — soldier-discipline: refuses to enjoy domestic life while comrades are in the field. Implicitly, the ark accompanied the army, so Uriah also cannot enter his house for ritual-covenantal reasons.
- **David's cover-up escalates** — summoning, dinner, drunkenness, and finally a death-letter carried by the victim himself. Ethical descent.
- **Joab's complicity** — obeys without apparent hesitation. Adjusts tactics to ensure Uriah's death, sacrificing others with him.
- **"Evil in the eyes of Yahweh"** (v27) — divine verdict pronounced. Sets up Nathan's confrontation in ch 12.
- **Abimelech reference** (v21) — Judg 9; David sees the parallel (ignoble death by woman's weapon).
