# 2 Samuel 11

[1] And it came to pass at the return of the year, at the time when the kings go out, that David sent ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ and his servants with him and all ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, and they ruined the sons of Ammon and besieged Rabbah. And David was dwelling in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[2] And it came to pass at the time of the evening that David arose from upon his bed and walked about on the roof of the house of the king, and he saw a woman washing, from upon the roof, and the woman was very good in appearance.

[3] And David sent and sought after the woman. And he said, "Is this not ⟦bathsheba|Bat-Sheva|Bathsheba⟧ the daughter of Eliam, the wife of ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ the ⟦hittite|Chitti|Hittite⟧?"

[4] And David sent messengers and took her, and she came to him, and he lay with her — and she was sanctifying herself from her uncleanness — and she returned to her house.

[5] And the woman conceived, and she sent and told David and said, "I am with child."

[6] And David sent to ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧: "Send to me ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ the ⟦hittite|Chitti|Hittite⟧." And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ sent ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ to David.

[7] And ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ came to him, and David asked about the ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧ of ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ and about the ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧ of the people and about the ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧ of the war.

[8] And David said to ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧, "Go down to your house and wash your feet." And ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ went out from the house of the king, and there went out after him a portion from the king.

[9] And ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ lay at the entrance of the house of the king with all the servants of his lord, and he did not go down to his house.

[10] And they told David, saying, "⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ did not go down to his house." And David said to ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧, "Have you not come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?"

[11] And ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ said to David, "The ark and ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ and ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ are dwelling in booths, and my lord ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ and the servants of my lord are camping on the face of the field; and I, shall I come into my house to eat and to drink and to lie with my wife? By your life and by the life of your soul, I will not do this thing."

[12] And David said to ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧, "Dwell here today also, and tomorrow I will send you away." And ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ dwelt in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ on that day and on the morrow.

[13] And David called him, and he ate before him and drank, and he made him drunk. And he went out in the evening to lie on his bed with the servants of his lord, and to his house he did not go down.

[14] And it came to pass in the morning that David wrote a letter to ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ and sent it by the hand of ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧.

[15] And he wrote in the letter, saying, "Put ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ over against the face of the strong battle, and turn back from after him, and he shall be struck down and die."

[16] And it came to pass, as ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ was keeping watch on the city, that he put ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ at the place where he knew that men of ability were.

[17] And the men of the city went out and fought with ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧, and there fell some of the people, of the servants of David, and ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ the ⟦hittite|Chitti|Hittite⟧ also died.

[18] And ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ sent and told David all the matters of the battle.

[19] And he commanded the messenger, saying, "When you have finished speaking all the matters of the battle to the king,

[20] then it shall be, if the fury of the king rises and he says to you, 'Why did you draw near to the city to fight? Did you not know that they would shoot from upon the wall?

[21] Who struck down ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ the son of ⟦jerubbesheth|Yerubbeshet|Jerubbesheth⟧? Did not a woman throw upon him an upper millstone from upon the wall, and he died at ⟦thebez|Tevetz|Thebez⟧? Why did you draw near to the wall?' — then you shall say, 'Your servant ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ the ⟦hittite|Chitti|Hittite⟧ is dead also.'"

[22] And the messenger went, and he came and told David all that ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧ had sent him for.

[23] And the messenger said to David, "The men prevailed over us and came out to us into the field, and we were upon them as far as the entrance of the gate.

[24] And the archers shot at your servants from upon the wall, and some of the servants of the king died, and your servant ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ the ⟦hittite|Chitti|Hittite⟧ is dead also."

[25] And David said to the messenger, "Thus shall you say to ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧: 'Let not this thing be evil in your eyes, for the sword devours now this one and now that one. Make strong your battle against the city and throw it down.' And make him strong."

[26] And the wife of ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ heard that ⟦uriah|Uriyah|Uriah⟧ her husband was dead, and she lamented over her husband.

[27] And the mourning passed, and David sent and gathered her to his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. And the thing that David had done was evil in the eyes of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"after the year was expired, at the time when kings go forth to battle" → "at the return of the year, at the time when the kings go out"** (v1) — Hebrew לִתְשׁוּבַת הַשָּׁנָה, "at the return of the year": the turn of the year; "in the spring" was an explanation and is removed. "To battle" is not in the Hebrew. The word for "the kings" is written here with an א (המלאכים, the letters of "the messengers") and pointed as "the kings"; the pointing is translated. 1 Chr 20:1 has the plain spelling "the kings".
- **"destroyed the children of Ammon" → "ruined the sons of Ammon"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיַּשְׁחִתוּ, the verb "ruin"; בְּנֵי "sons of". **"tarried still at Jerusalem" → "was dwelling in Jerusalem"** — Hebrew יוֹשֵׁב, "sitting, dwelling"; the same verb in v11, 12.
- **"in an eveningtide" → "at the time of the evening"** (v2) — Hebrew לְעֵת הָעֶרֶב. **"from the roof he saw a woman washing herself" → "he saw a woman washing, from upon the roof"** — Hebrew רֹחֶצֶת "washing" (the verb of v8); the Hebrew order is kept. **"very beautiful to look upon" → "very good in appearance"** — Hebrew טוֹבַת מַרְאֶה מְאֹד.
- **"enquired after the woman" → "sought after the woman"** (v3) — Hebrew וַיִּדְרֹשׁ לָאִשָּׁה. **"And one said" → "And he said"** — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר, with no subject named: the one who was asked, or David himself.
- **"for she was purified from her uncleanness" → "and she was sanctifying herself from her uncleanness"** (v4) — Hebrew וְהִיא מִתְקַדֶּשֶׁת מִטֻּמְאָתָהּ, a participle of the verb "sanctify" (קדשׁ) in a clause set beside the others; when and how this was is not said by the words. The three verbs before it stand as they are: "he took her, and she came to him, and he lay with her".
- **"I am with child"** (v5) — Hebrew הָרָה אָנֹכִי.
- **"demanded of him how Joab did, and how the people did, and how the war prospered" → "asked about the shalom of Joab and about the shalom of the people and about the shalom of the war"** (v7) — Hebrew לִשְׁלוֹם three times, "peace, well-being": the usual words for asking after someone, here also of the war.
- **"a mess of meat from the king" → "a portion from the king"** (v8) — Hebrew מַשְׂאַת הַמֶּלֶךְ, "a portion of the king": something lifted and carried from his table (the word of Gen 43:34), or a gift.
- **"slept at the door" → "lay at the entrance"** (v9, 13) — Hebrew שָׁכַב "lie", the verb of v4, 11; פֶּתַח "entrance".
- **"Camest thou not from thy journey?" → "Have you not come from a journey?"** (v10) — Hebrew מִדֶּרֶךְ, "from a way".
- **"abide in tents" → "are dwelling in booths"** (v11) — Hebrew בַּסֻּכּוֹת, "in the booths" (shelters of branches); the word can also be read as the place name Succoth. **"in the open fields" → "on the face of the field"**. **"as thou livest, and as thy soul liveth, I will not do this thing" → "By your life and by the life of your soul, I will not do this thing"** — Hebrew חַיֶּךָ וְחֵי נַפְשֶׁךָ אִם־אֶעֱשֶׂה, literally "your life and the life of your soul, if I do this thing": the "if" of an oath means "I will not".
- **"Tarry here" → "Dwell here"; "abode" → "dwelt"** (v12) — Hebrew יָשַׁב, the verb of v1, 11. **"that day, and the morrow"** — the Masoretic verse division puts "and on the morrow" at the end of v12.
- **"he did eat and drink before him"** (v13) — Hebrew לְפָנָיו "before him".
- **"a letter"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew סֵפֶר, "book": any written document.
- **"Set ye Uriah in the forefront of the hottest battle, and retire ye from him, that he may be smitten, and die" → "Put Uriah over against the face of the strong battle, and turn back from after him, and he shall be struck down and die"** (v15) — Hebrew הָבוּ … אֶל־מוּל פְּנֵי הַמִּלְחָמָה הַחֲזָקָה, literally "give Uriah to over against the face of the strong battle"; וְנִכָּה וָמֵת "and he shall be struck and die".
- **"when Joab observed the city" → "as Joab was keeping watch on the city"** (v16) — Hebrew בִּשְׁמוֹר … אֶל־הָעִיר, "in Joab's keeping (watch) toward the city". **"valiant men" → "men of ability"** — Hebrew אַנְשֵׁי־חַיִל.
- **"the things concerning the war" → "the matters of the battle"** (v18, 19) — Hebrew דִּבְרֵי הַמִּלְחָמָה, "the words / matters of the war". מִלְחָמָה is "war" in v7 and "battle" of the single fight in v15, 18, 19, 25: one Hebrew word.
- **"if so be that the king's wrath arise" → "if the fury of the king rises"** (v20) — Hebrew חֵמָה "fury" (literally "heat"). **"knew ye not that they would shoot"** — Hebrew אֵת אֲשֶׁר־יֹרוּ, "that which they shoot".
- **"Who smote Abimelech" → "Who struck down Abimelech"** (v21) — Hebrew הִכָּה "strike"; he was killed. **"a piece of a millstone" → "an upper millstone"** — Hebrew פֶּלַח רֶכֶב, literally "a millstone of riding": the stone that rides on the lower one (Judg 9:53). **"Jerubbesheth"** — Judg 9:1 has Jerubbaal (Gideon); here the name is written with *bosheth* "shame" in place of *baal*.
- **"shewed David all that Joab had sent him for"** (v22) — Hebrew אֵת כָּל־אֲשֶׁר שְׁלָחוֹ יוֹאָב, "all that Joab had sent him"; "for" is supplied.
- **"Surely the men prevailed against us"** (v23) — the speech opens with כִּי ("for", "indeed", or only the mark of the words spoken); it is not translated. **"we were upon them"** — Hebrew וַנִּהְיֶה עֲלֵיהֶם, word for word.
- **"the shooters shot" → "the archers shot"** (v24) — the reading tradition (qere) וַיֹּרוּ הַמּוֹרִים, "and the shooters shot" (one root; no bow is named); the written text (ketiv) spells both words with an א.
- **"Let not this thing displease thee" → "Let not this thing be evil in your eyes"** (v25) — Hebrew אַל־יֵרַע בְּעֵינֶיךָ אֶת־הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה: the same words come back in v27, "the thing … was evil in the eyes of Yahweh" (וַיֵּרַע הַדָּבָר … בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה). **"the sword devoureth one as well as another" → "the sword devours now this one and now that one"** — Hebrew כָזֹה וְכָזֶה תֹּאכַל הֶחָרֶב, literally "like this and like this the sword eats". **"make thy battle more strong against the city, and overthrow it: and encourage thou him" → "Make strong your battle against the city and throw it down.' And make him strong"** — Hebrew הַחֲזֵק … וְחַזְּקֵהוּ, one root twice; the last word is spoken to the messenger.
- **"she mourned for her husband" → "she lamented over her husband"** (v26) — Hebrew סָפַד, the loud wailing for the dead. "Her husband" is first אִישָׁהּ ("her man") and then בַּעְלָהּ ("her owner, husband"): two words.
- **"when the mourning was past, David sent and fetched her to his house" → "the mourning passed, and David sent and gathered her to his house"** (v27) — Hebrew וַיַּעֲבֹר הָאֵבֶל; וַיַּאַסְפָהּ, "gathered her", the verb of taking someone into a house. **"the thing that David had done displeased the LORD" → "the thing that David had done was evil in the eyes of Yahweh"** — Hebrew וַיֵּרַע … בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה.
- **"servants"** (v1, 9, 11, 13, 17, 21, 24) — Hebrew עֶבֶד, "slave"; of a king's men "servant".

### Notes
- **Bathsheba** — daughter of Eliam, wife of Uriah (v3); the text does not say what she thought or wished.
- **Uriah the Hittite** — named among David's mighty ones (23:39).
- **The letter** (v14–15) — carried by Uriah himself.
- **Abimelech** (v21) — Judg 9:50–54.
- **"Evil in the eyes of Yahweh"** (v27) — the only judgment the chapter gives; chapter 12 follows from it.
