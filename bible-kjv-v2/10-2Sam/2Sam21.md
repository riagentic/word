# 2 Samuel 21

[1] There was a famine in the days of David for three years, year after year; and David sought the face of Yahweh. Yahweh said, "There is bloodguilt on Saul and on his house, because he put the Gibeonites to death."

[2] So the king called the Gibeonites and spoke to them. (Now the Gibeonites were not of the children of Israel, but of the remnant of the Amorites; the children of Israel had sworn to them, but Saul had sought to strike them down in his zeal for the children of Israel and Judah.)

[3] David said to the Gibeonites, "What shall I do for you? With what shall I make atonement, that you may bless the inheritance of Yahweh?"

[4] The Gibeonites said to him, "It is not a matter of silver or gold between us and Saul or his house; neither is it for us to put anyone to death in Israel." He said, "What you say, I will do for you."

[5] They said to the king, "The man who consumed us and planned to destroy us, so that we should be eliminated from all the territory of Israel —

[6] let seven of his sons be given to us, that we may hang them up before Yahweh at Gibeah of Saul, the chosen of Yahweh." The king said, "I will give them."

[7] But the king spared Mephibosheth son of Jonathan son of Saul, because of the oath of Yahweh that was between them, between David and Jonathan son of Saul.

[8] The king took the two sons of Rizpah daughter of Aiah, whom she had borne to Saul — Armoni and Mephibosheth — and the five sons of Merab daughter of Saul, whom she had borne to Adriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite;

[9] he handed them over to the Gibeonites, and they hanged them on the mountain before Yahweh. The seven of them fell together; they were put to death in the days of harvest, at the beginning, at the start of barley harvest.

[10] Rizpah daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and spread it out for herself on the rock, from the beginning of harvest until rain fell from heaven on them; she did not allow the birds of the sky to rest on them by day, nor the wild animals by night.

[11] It was told David what Rizpah daughter of Aiah, the concubine of Saul, had done.

[12] David went and took the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son from the men of Jabesh-gilead, who had stolen them from the public square of Beth-shan, where the Philistines had hung them up on the day the Philistines killed Saul at Gilboa.

[13] He brought up from there the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son, and they gathered the bones of those who had been hanged.

[14] They buried the bones of Saul and Jonathan his son in the country of Benjamin at Zela, in the tomb of Kish his father; they did all that the king had commanded. After that *Elohim* responded to the plea for the land.

[15] The Philistines had war again with Israel; David went down with his servants, and they fought against the Philistines. David grew weary;

[16] and Ishbi-benob, one of the descendants of the Rephaim, whose spear weighed three hundred shekels of bronze, girded with a new sword, thought to kill David.

[17] But Abishai son of Zeruiah came to his aid and struck down the Philistine and killed him. Then David's men swore to him, saying, "You shall no longer go out with us to battle, lest you quench the lamp of Israel."

[18] After this there was again war with the Philistines at Gob; then Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Saph, who was one of the descendants of the Rephaim.

[19] There was again war with the Philistines at Gob; Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite struck down [Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam].

[20] There was again war at Gath, where there was a man of great stature, who had six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in number; he also had been born to the Rephaim.

[21] When he defied Israel, Jonathan son of Shimei, David's brother, struck him down.

[22] These four were descended from the Rephaim in Gath; they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Michal" → "Merab"** (v8) — MT reads "Michal," but Michal was childless (6:23); Merab, the elder sister, married Adriel (1 Sam 18:19). Modern translations emend to "Merab" as scribal error. Noted here.
- **"brother of Goliath"** (v19) — MT reads that Elhanan "killed Goliath" (not "the brother of Goliath"). 1 Chr 20:5 clarifies: "Elhanan son of Jair struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath." Textual harmonization. Here: MT reading with bracketed understanding.
- **"giants" → "Rephaim"** (v16, 18, 20, 22).
- **"brass" → "bronze"** (v16).

### Notes
- **Saul's Gibeonite massacre** — not recorded in 1 Samuel but alluded to here. Saul's zeal led to breach of the Joshua-era covenant (Josh 9).
- **Blood-guilt across generations** — the land suffers for Saul's unatoned offense. Only atoning reparation satisfies Yahweh.
- **Seven sons hanged** — two of Rizpah, five of Merab. Gruesome but framed as necessary reparation.
- **Rizpah's vigil** — months-long watch over the bodies. Perhaps prompts David to finally gather remains of Saul and Jonathan.
- **Four giants** (v16–22) — David's mighty men kill four Rephaim. Completion of anti-giant campaign begun with Goliath.
- **"Light of Israel"** (v17) — David's identity as covenant-light. Later 1 Kgs 11:36, 15:4 — the "lamp" given perpetually to David's line.
