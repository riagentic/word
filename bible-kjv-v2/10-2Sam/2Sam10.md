# 2 Samuel 10

[1] After this the king of the children of Ammon died, and Hanun his son became king in his place.

[2] David said, "I will show kindness to Hanun son of Nahash, as his father showed kindness to me." So David sent his servants to console him concerning his father. David's servants came into the land of the children of Ammon.

[3] But the princes of the children of Ammon said to Hanun their lord, "Do you think that David is honoring your father because he has sent you comforters? Has not David sent his servants to you in order to search the city, to scout it out, and to overthrow it?"

[4] So Hanun seized David's servants, shaved off half of each one's beard, cut their garments in the middle up to their buttocks, and sent them away.

[5] When it was told David, he sent to meet them, for the men were greatly shamed. The king said, "Remain at Jericho until your beards grow, and then return."

[6] When the children of Ammon saw that they had made themselves odious to David, the children of Ammon sent and hired the Arameans of Beth-rehob and the Arameans of Zobah — twenty thousand foot soldiers — and the king of Maacah with a thousand men, and the men of Tob, twelve thousand men.

[7] When David heard of it, he sent Joab and all the army of the mighty men.

[8] The children of Ammon came out and drew up in battle array at the entrance of the gate; and the Arameans of Zobah and of Rehob, with the men of Tob and Maacah, were by themselves in the open country.

[9] When Joab saw that the battle-line was against him both in front and behind, he chose some of the best men in Israel and drew them up against the Arameans.

[10] The rest of the people he delivered into the hand of Abishai his brother, and he drew them up against the children of Ammon.

[11] Joab said, "If the Arameans are too strong for me, then you shall help me; but if the children of Ammon are too strong for you, then I will come to help you.

[12] Be of good courage, and let us be strong for our people and for the cities of our *Elohim*; and may Yahweh do what is good in his eyes."

[13] So Joab and the people with him drew near for battle against the Arameans, and they fled before him.

[14] When the children of Ammon saw that the Arameans had fled, they also fled before Abishai and entered the city. Joab returned from fighting against the children of Ammon and came to Jerusalem.

[15] When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they gathered themselves together.

[16] Hadadezer sent and brought out the Arameans who were beyond the River; they came to Helam, with Shobach the commander of Hadadezer's army at their head.

[17] When it was told David, he gathered all Israel together and crossed the Jordan and came to Helam. The Arameans drew up in battle array against David and fought with him.

[18] The Arameans fled before Israel; David killed of the Arameans seven hundred chariot-teams and forty thousand horsemen, and struck down Shobach the commander of their army, who died there.

[19] When all the kings who were servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with Israel and became their servants. So the Arameans feared to help the children of Ammon any more.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Syrians" → "Arameans"** — as elsewhere.
- **"Hadarezer" → "Hadadezer"** (v16) — variant; same person as ch 8.

### Notes
- **Diplomatic incident** — David's gesture of kindness to the new Ammonite king is misinterpreted and insulted.
- **Beard shaving + clothes cut** — double humiliation. Beards symbolized honor; exposing buttocks shamed the messengers.
- **Two-phase war** — Ammonite/Aramean alliance defeated in two stages. David's empire consolidates.
- **Joab's generalship** (v9–12) — tactical split-force deployment; mutual-support principle. Pragmatic theology: "do what is good in his eyes."
- **Israel's position** — dominant regional power. Sets up ch 11's Ammonite siege during which the Bathsheba affair occurs.
