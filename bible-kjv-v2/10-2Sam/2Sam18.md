# 2 Samuel 18

[1] David mustered the people who were with him and set over them commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.

[2] David sent out the people: a third under the command of ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧, a third under the command of ⟦abishai|Avishai|Abishai⟧ son of ⟦zeruiah|Tzeruyah|Zeruiah⟧, ⟦joab|Yoav|Joab⟧'s brother, and a third under the command of Ittai the ⟦gittite|Gitti|Gittite⟧. ## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"dale" → "valley"** (v18).
- **"Cushi" → "the Cushite"** (v21) — ethnic designation, not a name.
- **"succour" → "ready to help"** (v3).

### Notes
- **David stays in Mahanaim** — people prevent him from battle. His emotional state would endanger all.
- **"Deal gently with Absalom"** — David's fatherly instruction. Tragically overruled by Joab.
- **Absalom in the oak** — his long hair caught in the tree. Ironic: the pride of his appearance becomes his undoing.
- **Joab defies the king** — kills Absalom against orders. Pragmatic judgment: Absalom alive would continue rebellion. But moral and political consequences fester.
- **Three darts through the heart** — Joab's own handiwork, then ten armor-bearers. Overkill emphasizes determination.
- **Absalom's monument** — traditionally identified with a pillar in the Kidron Valley (though that monument is Hellenistic-period).
- **Ahimaaz eager but informationless** — David's priestly courier runs first but does not say what he knows.
- **Cushite delivers the hard news** — a foreigner carries the death-word.
- **David's lament** — one of the most poignant in scripture. Father's grief unabated despite political necessity of his son's death.
