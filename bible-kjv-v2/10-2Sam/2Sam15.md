# 2 Samuel 15

[1] After this Absalom got himself a chariot and horses and fifty men to run before him.

[2] Absalom would rise early and stand beside the way to the gate; whenever a man had a case that was to come to the king for judgment, Absalom would call to him and say, "From what city are you?" When he said, "Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel,"

[3] Absalom would say to him, "Behold, your claims are good and right, but there is no one deputed by the king to hear you."

[4] Absalom would also say, "If only I were appointed judge in the land! Then every man who has a lawsuit or case might come to me, and I would give him justice."

[5] Whenever anyone came near to do homage to him, he would put out his hand, take hold of him, and kiss him.

[6] Absalom behaved in this way toward all Israel who came to the king for judgment. So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.

[7] At the end of four years Absalom said to the king, "Please let me go and pay my vow that I have vowed to Yahweh, in Hebron.

[8] For your servant vowed a vow while I dwelt at Geshur in Aram, saying, 'If Yahweh indeed brings me back to Jerusalem, then I will serve Yahweh.'"

[9] The king said to him, "Go in peace." So he arose and went to Hebron.

[10] But Absalom sent secret messengers throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "As soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet, then say, 'Absalom is king at Hebron!'"

[11] With Absalom went two hundred men from Jerusalem who had been invited; they went in their innocence, and they knew nothing of the matter.

[12] While Absalom was offering the sacrifices, he sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's counselor, from his city Giloh. The conspiracy grew strong, and the people kept increasing with Absalom.

[13] A messenger came to David, saying, "The hearts of the men of Israel have gone after Absalom."

[14] Then David said to all his servants who were with him at Jerusalem, "Arise, let us flee, for there will be no escape for us from Absalom. Make haste to depart, lest he overtake us quickly and bring harm down upon us and strike the city with the edge of the sword."

[15] The king's servants said to the king, "Behold, your servants are ready to do whatever my lord the king decides."

[16] The king went out, and all his household with him; the king left ten women, concubines, to look after the house.

[17] The king went out, and all the people after him, and halted at the last house.

[18] All his servants passed on beside him, and all the Cherethites, all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites — six hundred men who had come with him from Gath — passed on before the king.

[19] Then the king said to Ittai the Gittite, "Why should you go with us? Return and stay with the king; for you are a foreigner and also an exile from your place.

[20] Since you came but yesterday, should I today make you wander about with us, when I myself go wherever I can? Return and take your brothers back with you; may lovingkindness and faithfulness be with you."

[21] Ittai answered the king, "As Yahweh lives, and as my lord the king lives, wherever my lord the king shall be, whether for death or for life, there your servant will be."

[22] David said to Ittai, "Go, pass on." So Ittai the Gittite passed on, and all his men, and all the little ones who were with him.

[23] All the country wept aloud as all the people passed on; the king also himself crossed over the wadi Kidron, and all the people passed on toward the way of the wilderness.

[24] Zadok was also there, and all the Levites with him, bearing the ark of the covenant of *Elohim*. They set down the ark of *Elohim*, and Abiathar came up, until all the people had finished passing out of the city.

[25] The king said to Zadok, "Carry the ark of *Elohim* back into the city. If I find favor in the eyes of Yahweh, he will bring me back and let me see both it and his dwelling;

[26] but if he says thus, 'I have no delight in you,' behold, here I am; let him do to me what is good in his eyes."

[27] The king also said to Zadok the priest, "Are you not a seer? Return to the city in peace, and your two sons with you, Ahimaaz your son and Jonathan son of Abiathar.

[28] See, I will wait at the fords of the wilderness until word comes from you to inform me."

[29] Zadok and Abiathar carried the ark of *Elohim* back to Jerusalem, and they remained there.

[30] David went up the ascent of the Mount of Olives, weeping as he went, with his head covered and walking barefoot; all the people who were with him covered their heads, and went up weeping as they went.

[31] It was told David, "Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom." David said, "O Yahweh, please turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness."

[32] When David came to the summit, where *Elohim* was worshiped, behold, Hushai the Archite came to meet him with his tunic torn and dust on his head.

[33] David said to him, "If you pass on with me, you will be a burden to me.

[34] But if you return to the city and say to Absalom, 'I will be your servant, O king; as I have been your father's servant in time past, so now I will be your servant,' then you may defeat the counsel of Ahithophel for me.

[35] Are not Zadok and Abiathar the priests with you there? Whatever you hear from the king's house, tell it to Zadok and Abiathar the priests.

[36] Behold, their two sons are with them there, Ahimaaz the son of Zadok, and Jonathan the son of Abiathar; by them you shall send to me whatever you hear."

[37] So Hushai, David's friend, came to the city just as Absalom was entering Jerusalem.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"forty years" → "four years"** (v7) — MT reads "forty," but LXX and Peshitta read "four." Forty years is impossibly long given the narrative chronology; "four" is the likely original.
- **"stranger" → "foreigner"** (v19).
- **"mercy and truth" → "lovingkindness and faithfulness"** (v20) — *chesed ve-emet*.

### Notes
- **Absalom's campaign** — patient 4-year political gathering. "Stole the hearts" idiom: populist appeals.
- **Hebron** — Absalom's birthplace (3:2–3) and David's first capital. Symbolic choice of rebellion-center.
- **Ahithophel joins** — David's top counselor defects. Possibly Bathsheba's grandfather (23:34 suggests); personal grudge over David's sin.
- **David's flight** — leaves Jerusalem without fighting. Prevents bloodshed in the city.
- **Ittai the Gittite** — remarkable loyalty from a Philistine convert. Echoes Ruth: "where you go, I go."
- **Ark sent back** (v25) — David refuses to treat the ark as magic. If Yahweh brings him back, good; if not, he accepts. Theological maturity.
- **Hushai the spy** (v32–37) — David's counter-intelligence in Jerusalem. Sets up the contest of counselors in ch 17.
- **Pss 3, 63** — traditionally linked to David's flight from Absalom.
