# 2 Samuel 15

[1] And it came to pass after this that ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ made for himself a chariot and horses, and fifty men running before him.

[2] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ would rise early and stand beside the way of the gate. [12] And ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ sent for ⟦ahithophel|Achitofel|Ahithophel⟧ the ⟦gilonite|Giloni|Gilonite⟧, the counselor of David, from his city, from Giloh, while he sacrificed the sacrifices. [32] And it came to pass, as David was coming to the top, where one bows down to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that behold, to meet him came ⟦hushai|Chushai|Hushai⟧ the ⟦archite|Arki|Archite⟧, his tunic torn and ground on his head.

[37] And ⟦hushai|Chushai|Hushai⟧, the friend of David, came to the city, and ⟦absalom|Avshalom|Absalom⟧ was coming to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v24, 25, 29, 32) — Hebrew *Elohim*; in v24, 25, 29 with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"prepared him chariots and horses, and fifty men to run before him" → "made for himself a chariot and horses, and fifty men running before him"** (v1) — Hebrew מֶרְכָּבָה, one chariot; רָצִים, a participle, "running" (the runners of a king, 1 Sam 22:17).
- **"rose up early, and stood" → "would rise early and stand"** (v2, 5) — the Hebrew forms tell of what he did again and again. **"beside the way of the gate"** — Hebrew עַל־יַד, literally "at the hand of". **"a controversy" → "a case"** (v2, 4) — Hebrew רִיב, a quarrel brought to law.
- **"thy matters are good and right; but there is no man deputed of the king to hear thee" → "your words are good and straight, but you have no one who hears, from the king"** (v3) — Hebrew דְבָרֶךָ "your words" (what he has to say in his case); נְכֹחִים "straight"; וְשֹׁמֵעַ אֵין־לְךָ מֵאֵת הַמֶּלֶךְ, literally "and a hearer there is none for you from the king". "Deputed" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"Oh that I were made judge" → "Who will set me as judge"** (v4) — Hebrew מִי־יְשִׂמֵנִי שֹׁפֵט, a question that is a wish: "who will set me judge?" **"I would do him justice" → "I would justify him"** — Hebrew וְהִצְדַּקְתִּיו, "declare him righteous", give the verdict for him. "To me" stands first in the Hebrew.
- **"to do him obeisance" → "to bow down to him"** (v5) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה; the same verb in v32 of bowing down to Elohim.
- **"stole the hearts" → "stole the heart"** (v6) — Hebrew לֵב, singular; so also v13. In v13 "the men of Israel" is the singular אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל, "the man of Israel", as a body.
- **"after forty years" → "at the end of forty years"** (v7) — Hebrew מִקֵּץ אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה: the Masoretic Text has "forty" and is translated as it stands. The number is difficult in the story (from what it is counted is not said); the Syriac, the Vulgate and a part of the Greek tradition have "four" (from memory), and the earlier form of this chapter followed them.
- **"pay my vow" → "repay my vow"** (v7) — Hebrew שִׁלֵּם, "make whole, repay". The accents join "in Hebron" to the whole sentence; it can be taken with "let me go and repay" or with "vowed".
- **"If the LORD shall bring me again indeed" → "If Yahweh will surely bring me back"** (v8) — the reading tradition (qere) is יָשׁוֹב יְשִׁיבֵנִי, literally "returning he brings me back"; the written text (ketiv) ישיב has the same sense. **"Syria" → "Aram"**.
- **"Go in peace" → "Go in shalom"** (v9, 27) — Hebrew שָׁלוֹם.
- **"spies"** (v10) — Hebrew מְרַגְּלִים. **"the sound of the trumpet" → "the voice of the shofar"** — Hebrew קוֹל הַשֹּׁפָר. **"Absalom reigneth"** — Hebrew מָלַךְ, a perfect: "has become king".
- **"that were called; and they went in their simplicity" → "called ones, and going in their integrity"** (v11) — Hebrew קְרֻאִים, invited guests (1 Sam 9:13); לְתֻמָּם, "in their integrity": without guile, knowing nothing.
- **"sent for Ahithophel"** (v12) — the Hebrew has וַיִּשְׁלַח … אֶת־אֲחִיתֹפֶל … מֵעִירוֹ, literally "sent Ahithophel … from his city": he sent and had him brought from his city. The wording is difficult; the Greek has "sent and called" (from memory). **"the people increased continually" → "the people went on growing more numerous"** — Hebrew הוֹלֵךְ וָרָב, "going and many" (as 3:1).
- **"a messenger" → "the one who told"** (v13) — Hebrew הַמַּגִּיד. **"are after Absalom"** — Hebrew הָיָה … אַחֲרֵי, "has come to be after".
- **"we shall not else escape from Absalom" → "there will be no escape for us from the face of Absalom"** (v14) — Hebrew פְלֵיטָה מִפְּנֵי. **"make speed to depart, lest he overtake us suddenly, and bring evil upon us" → "Hasten to go, lest he hasten and overtake us and thrust the evil upon us"** — one verb, מִהַר, twice; הִדִּיחַ "thrust, drive"; הָרָעָה has the article. **"smite … with the edge of the sword"** — literally "to the mouth of the sword".
- **"Behold, thy servants are ready to do whatsoever my lord the king shall appoint" → "According to all that my lord the king chooses — behold, your servants"** (v15) — Hebrew כְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר־יִבְחַר … הִנֵּה עֲבָדֶיךָ; "are ready to do" is not in the Hebrew. "Servants" is the word "slaves" (עֲבָדִים), as everywhere of the king's men in this chapter.
- **"after him" → "at his feet"** (v16, 17) — Hebrew בְּרַגְלָיו; in v18 "at his foot", בְרַגְלוֹ. **"women, which were concubines"** — Hebrew נָשִׁים פִּלַגְשִׁים, "women, concubines". **"to keep the house"** — Hebrew שָׁמַר.
- **"tarried in a place that was far off" → "stood at the far house"** (v17) — Hebrew בֵּית הַמֶּרְחָק, "the house of the distance": the last house, or a place name (Beth-hammerhak).
- **"passed on"** (v18, 22–24, 33) — one Hebrew verb, עָבַר, runs through the chapter; in v23 of the king in the wadi Kidron it is "passing over" (עֹבֵר בְּנַחַל, "passing in the wadi"). In v18 the verbs are participles: the troops file past the king. The accents put the Cherethites and Pelethites with the servants.
- **"Wherefore goest thou also with us? return to thy place, and abide with the king: for thou art a stranger, and also an exile" → "Why do you go, you also, with us? Return and dwell with the king, for you are a foreigner, and you are also an exile, to your place"** (v19) — Hebrew נָכְרִי "foreigner"; "the king" is Absalom. The last words, וְגַם־גֹּלֶה אַתָּה לִמְקוֹמֶךָ, are difficult: literally "and also an exile are you to your place" — an exile as regards his own place, or (so the KJV) "to your place" belongs with "return". The Greek and the Vulgate have "from your place" (from memory).
- **"Whereas thou camest but yesterday, should I this day make thee go up and down with us? seeing I go whither I may" → "Yesterday was your coming, and today shall I make you wander with us, to go, while I am going where I am going?"** (v20) — Hebrew תְּמוֹל בּוֹאֶךָ; the reading tradition (qere) אֲנִיעֲךָ, the hifil of נוּעַ "sway, roam" (the written text has another form of the same verb); וַאֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ עַל אֲשֶׁר־אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ, literally "and I am going upon where I am going".
- **"take back thy brethren: mercy and truth be with thee" → "bring back your brothers with you — chesed and truth"** (v20) — Hebrew וְהָשֵׁב אֶת־אַחֶיךָ עִמָּךְ חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת. The last two words stand without a verb and the sentence is difficult; the accents put "with you" with "your brothers". The Greek has a longer text, "and may the Lord do with you mercy and truth" (from memory). The Masoretic Text is translated as it stands. חֶסֶד is *chesed*; אֱמֶת "truth".
- **"As the LORD liveth, and as my lord the king liveth" → "As Yahweh lives, and by the life of my lord the king"** (v21) — Hebrew חַי־יְהוָה וְחֵי אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ: two forms, חַי "living" of Yahweh and חֵי "the life of" of a man. The oath runs כִּי אִם … כִּי, "surely … surely"; "whether for death or for life" is literally "if for death, if for life".
- **"all the country wept" → "all the land was weeping"** (v23) — Hebrew הָאָרֶץ. **"with a loud voice"** — literally "a great voice". **"toward the way of the wilderness" → "facing the way of the wilderness"** — Hebrew עַל־פְּנֵי־דֶרֶךְ אֶת־הַמִּדְבָּר, literally "upon the face of the way, the wilderness"; the wording is difficult (אֶת before "the wilderness").
- **"they set down the ark of God; and Abiathar went up"** (v24) — Hebrew וַיַּצִּקוּ, pointed as a form of יָצַק "pour", used as "set down" (Josh 7:23); וַיַּעַל אֶבְיָתָר can be read "and Abiathar went up" or "and Abiathar offered up". The verse is difficult and is translated as it stands. **"until all the people had done passing" → "had come to an end of passing on"** — Hebrew עַד־תֹּם.
- **"Carry back" → "Bring back"; "he will bring me again, and shew me both it, and his habitation" → "he will bring me back and let me see it and his habitation"** (v25) — one verb, הֵשִׁיב, of the ark and of David (also v8, 20, 29); נָוֵהוּ "his habitation" (the abode of a shepherd). "It" can also be read "him".
- **"behold, here am I" → "here I am"** (v26) — Hebrew הִנְנִי, literally "behold me". **"as seemeth good unto him" → "as is good in his eyes"**.
- **"Art not thou a seer?" → "Do you see?"** (v27) — Hebrew הֲרוֹאֶה אַתָּה, a question with the participle of "see" (as Ezek 8:6): "Are you seeing?" The same word is the noun "seer" (1 Sam 9:9), so that it can be read "Are you a seer?"; "not" is not in the Hebrew. **"your two sons with you"** — "your" and "you" are plural: Zadok and Abiathar.
- **"I will tarry in the plain of the wilderness" → "I am lingering in the plains of the wilderness"** (v28) — the reading tradition (qere) בְּעַרְבוֹת "in the plains of"; the written text (ketiv) בעברות, "at the fords of" (17:16 has "the plains" without a second reading). **"to certify me" → "to tell me"**.
- **"they tarried there" → "they dwelt there"** (v29) — Hebrew יָשַׁב.
- **"the ascent of mount Olivet" → "the ascent of the Olives"** (v30) — Hebrew מַעֲלֵה הַזֵּיתִים, "the going-up of the olive trees"; the Hebrew has no "mount" here, so the place-name token ("the Mount of Olives") was taken out. **"and wept as he went up" → "going up and weeping"** — Hebrew עֹלֶה וּבוֹכֶה; at the end עָלֹה וּבָכֹה.
- **"And one told David" → "And David — one told him"** (v31) — Hebrew וְדָוִד הִגִּיד, literally "And David told": "David" stands first and the verb has no other subject. It is taken as "and as for David, one told"; the Greek and other versions have "and it was told to David" (from memory). **"turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness" → "Make foolish … the counsel of Ahithophel"** — Hebrew סַכֶּל. עֵצָה is "counsel" in every place in chapters 15–17, and יָעַץ "to counsel".
- **"the top of the mount, where he worshipped God" → "the top, where one bows down to Elohim"** (v32) — Hebrew הָרֹאשׁ "the head, the top"; יִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה is an imperfect, "where one bows down" or "where he used to bow down". **"with his coat rent, and earth upon his head" → "his tunic torn and ground on his head"** — Hebrew כֻּתָּנְתּוֹ, אֲדָמָה.
- **"I will be thy servant, O king; as I have been thy father's servant hitherto, so will I now also be thy servant" → "Your servant, O king, I will be; the servant of your father I was from of old, and now I am your servant"** (v34) — the Hebrew is broken: עַבְדְּךָ אֲנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶהְיֶה עֶבֶד אָבִיךָ וַאֲנִי מֵאָז וְעַתָּה וַאֲנִי עַבְדֶּךָ, literally "your servant I, O king, will be; the servant of your father, and I from then; and now, and I your servant". **"defeat" → "make void"** — Hebrew הֵפֵר, as of a vow (Num 30:8) and of a covenant.
- **"what thing soever" → "every word"** (v35, 36) — Hebrew דָּבָר. In v36 "you shall send" and "you hear" are plural: Hushai and the priests.
- **"Ahimaaz Zadok's son, and Jonathan Abiathar's son" → "Ahimaaz of Zadok and Jonathan of Abiathar"** (v36) — Hebrew לְצָדוֹק, לְאֶבְיָתָר, "belonging to"; "son" is not repeated.
- **"and Absalom came into Jerusalem" → "and Absalom was coming to Jerusalem"** (v37) — Hebrew יָבֹא, an imperfect: he was on his way in as Hushai arrived.

### Notes
- **Absalom's campaign** — a long gathering of followers at the gate. "Stole the heart" (v6): he took by guile what was the king's.
- **Hebron** — Absalom's birthplace (3:2–3) and David's first capital. Symbolic choice of rebellion-center.
- **Ahithophel joins** — David's top counselor defects. Possibly Bathsheba's grandfather (23:34 suggests); personal grudge over David's sin.
- **David's flight** — leaves Jerusalem without fighting. Prevents bloodshed in the city.
- **Ittai the Gittite** — remarkable loyalty from a man of Gath. Echoes Ruth: "where you go, I go."
- **Ark sent back** (v25) — David refuses to treat the ark as magic. If Yahweh brings him back, good; if not, he accepts. Theological maturity.
- **Hushai the spy** (v32–37) — David's counter-intelligence in Jerusalem. Sets up the contest of counselors in ch 17.
- **Pss 3, 63** — traditionally linked to David's flight from Absalom.
