# 2 Samuel 24

[1] Again the anger of Yahweh was kindled against Israel, and he incited David against them, saying, "Go, number Israel and Judah."

[2] The king said to Joab the commander of the army who was with him, "Go through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, and number the people, that I may know the number of the people."

[3] Joab said to the king, "May Yahweh your *Elohim* add to the people a hundredfold as many as they are, while the eyes of my lord the king still see it. But why does my lord the king delight in this thing?"

[4] But the king's word prevailed against Joab and against the commanders of the army. So Joab and the commanders of the army went out from the presence of the king to number the people of Israel.

[5] They crossed the Jordan and began from Aroer, from the city that is in the middle of the wadi of Gad, toward Jazer.

[6] They came to Gilead, and to the land of Tahtim-hodshi; they came to Dan-jaan and went around to Sidon.

[7] They came to the stronghold of Tyre, and to all the cities of the Hivites and of the Canaanites; they went out to the Negev of Judah at Beersheba.

[8] When they had gone through the whole land, they came to Jerusalem at the end of nine months and twenty days.

[9] Joab gave the number of the enrollment of the people to the king: there were in Israel 800,000 valiant men who drew the sword; and the men of Judah were 500,000 men.

[10] David's heart struck him after he had counted the people. David said to Yahweh, "I have sinned greatly in what I have done. Now, O Yahweh, please take away the iniquity of your servant, for I have acted very foolishly."

[11] When David arose in the morning, the word of Yahweh came to the prophet Gad, David's seer, saying,

[12] "Go and say to David, 'Thus says Yahweh: Three things I offer you; choose one of them, that I may do it to you.'"

[13] So Gad came to David and told him, and said to him, "Shall three years of famine come upon you in your land? Or will you flee three months before your enemies while they pursue you? Or shall there be three days' pestilence in your land? Now consider and decide what answer I shall return to him who sent me."

[14] David said to Gad, "I am in great distress. Let us fall into the hand of Yahweh, for his mercies are great; but let me not fall into the hand of man."

[15] So Yahweh sent a pestilence on Israel from morning until the appointed time; there died of the people from Dan to Beersheba seventy thousand men.

[16] When the Messenger stretched out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, Yahweh relented from the evil, and said to the Messenger who was destroying the people, "It is enough; now stay your hand." The Messenger of Yahweh was at the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite.

[17] When David saw the Messenger who was striking the people, he spoke to Yahweh and said, "Behold, I have sinned, and I have done wickedly; but these sheep, what have they done? Let your hand, I pray, be against me and against my father's house."

[18] Gad came that day to David and said to him, "Go up, raise an altar to Yahweh on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite."

[19] David went up at the word of Gad, as Yahweh commanded.

[20] Araunah looked and saw the king and his servants coming toward him; Araunah went out and bowed to the king with his face to the ground.

[21] Araunah said, "Why has my lord the king come to his servant?" David said, "To buy the threshing floor from you, to build an altar to Yahweh, that the plague may be stayed from the people."

[22] Araunah said to David, "Let my lord the king take and offer up whatever is good in his eyes. See, the oxen for the burnt offering, and the threshing sledges and the yokes of the oxen for the wood.

[23] All this, O king, Araunah gives to the king." And Araunah said to the king, "May Yahweh your *Elohim* accept you."

[24] But the king said to Araunah, "No, but I will surely buy it from you at a price; I will not offer burnt offerings to Yahweh my *Elohim* that cost me nothing." So David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver.

[25] David built there an altar to Yahweh and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. So Yahweh heeded the plea for the land, and the plague was averted from Israel.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"moved David" → "incited David"** (v1) — Hebrew הִסִית *hesit*. 1 Chr 21:1 attributes the incitement to *Satan* (the adversary); 2 Sam to Yahweh. Theological difficulty — OT often says Yahweh did what we would ascribe to proximate causes.
- **"seven years" → "three years"** (v13) — MT reads "seven"; LXX and 1 Chr 21:12 read "three." The parallel three/three/three structure argues for "three."
- **"strait" → "distress"** (v14) — archaic.
- **"angel" → "Messenger"** (v16, 17).
- **"repented him of the evil" → "relented from the evil"** (v16) — *nacham*.

### Notes
- **The census sin** — debated. Was it (1) pride in military strength, (2) failure to pay the census-tax (Ex 30:12), (3) lack of trust in Yahweh? Joab's protest (v3) shows it was recognized as problematic even then.
- **Three-choice test** (v13) — Gad presents three plagues. David chooses pestilence (falling into Yahweh's hand).
- **70,000 die** — massive loss. Far worse than famine or military defeat.
- **"Angel of Yahweh" halted** (v16) — at the future temple site.
- **Araunah's generosity declined** — David insists on paying. "I will not offer that which costs me nothing" — foundational principle of costly worship.
- **Temple site purchased** (v24) — this threshing floor of Araunah becomes the site of Solomon's temple (2 Chr 3:1). Mount Moriah connection (Gen 22). The book of Samuel ends at the sacred ground where the temple will rise.
