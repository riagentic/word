# Matthew 13

[1] In that day ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, going out of the house, was sitting beside the sea;

[2] and many crowds were gathered together to him, so that he, stepping into a boat, sat down, and all the crowd stood on the shore.

[3] And he spoke many things to them in parables, saying:

"Behold, the sower went out to sow.

[4] And as he sowed, some fell beside the way, and the birds, coming, devoured them.

[5] And others fell upon the rocky places, where they did not have much earth, and immediately they sprang up, because of not having depth of earth;

[6] but when the sun rose, they were scorched, and because of not having root they withered.

[7] And others fell upon the thorns, and the thorns came up and choked them.

[8] And others fell upon the good earth and were giving fruit, some a hundred, some sixty, some thirty.

[9] He who has ears, let him hear."

[10] And approaching, the disciples said to him, "Why do you speak to them in parables?"

[11] And he answered and said to them, "Because to you it has been given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of the heavens, but to those it has not been given.

[12] For whoever has, to him it will be given, and he will be made to abound; but whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.

[13] Because of this I speak to them in parables, because seeing they do not see, and hearing they do not hear, nor do they understand.

[14] And in them is filled up the prophecy of ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧, which says:

"'With hearing you will hear and by no means understand,
and looking you will look and by no means see.

[15] For the heart of this people was made thick,
and with the ears they heard heavily,
and their eyes they shut,
lest they should see with the eyes
and hear with the ears
and understand with the heart
and turn, and I will heal them.'

[16] But blessed are your eyes, because they see, and your ears, because they hear.

[17] For amen, I say to you that many prophets and righteous ones desired to see what you see and did not see, and to hear what you hear and did not hear.

[18] "You, therefore, hear the parable of the one who sowed:

[19] When anyone hears the word of the kingdom and does not understand, the evil one comes and snatches what has been sown in his heart; this is the one sown beside the way.

[20] And the one sown upon the rocky places, this is the one who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy;

[21] but he has no root in himself, but is for a time; and when affliction or persecution comes on account of the word, immediately he is made to stumble.

[22] And the one sown into the thorns, this is the one who hears the word, and the anxiety of the age and the deceit of riches choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

[23] And the one sown upon the good earth, this is the one who hears the word and understands, who indeed bears fruit and makes, some a hundred, some sixty, some thirty."

[24] Another parable he set before them, saying, "The kingdom of the heavens was likened to a man who sowed good seed in his field.

[25] But while the men slept, his enemy came and sowed darnel over it in the midst of the wheat, and went away.

[26] And when the blade sprouted and made fruit, then the darnel appeared also.

[27] And approaching, the slaves of the master of the house said to him, 'Lord, did you not sow good seed in your field? From where, then, does it have darnel?'

[28] And he said to them, 'An enemy, a man, did this.' And the slaves say to him, 'Do you want us, then, to go and collect them?'

[29] But he says, 'No, lest, collecting the darnel, you uproot the wheat together with them.

[30] Let both grow together until the harvest; and in the time of the harvest I will say to the reapers, Collect first the darnel and bind them into bundles to burn them up, but the wheat gather into my barn.'"

[31] Another parable he set before them, saying, "The kingdom of the heavens is like a grain of mustard, which a man took and sowed in his field;

[32] which is smaller than all the seeds, but when it has grown it is greater than the garden herbs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of heaven come and dwell in its branches."

[33] Another parable he spoke to them: "The kingdom of the heavens is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three seahs of flour, until the whole was leavened."

[34] All these things ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ spoke in parables to the crowds, and without a parable he was speaking nothing to them,

[35] so that what was spoken through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying:

"I will open my mouth in parables;
I will utter things hidden from the foundation."

[36] Then, leaving the crowds, he came into the house. And his disciples approached him, saying, "Make plain to us the parable of the darnel of the field."

[37] And he answered and said, "The one who sows the good seed is the Son of Man;

[38] and the field is the world; and the good seed, these are the sons of the kingdom; and the darnel are the sons of the evil one,

[39] and the enemy who sowed them is the devil; and the harvest is the consummation of the age, and the reapers are angels.

[40] Therefore just as the darnel is collected and burned up with fire, so it will be in the consummation of the age:

[41] the Son of Man will send his angels, and they will collect out of his kingdom all the stumbling blocks and those who do lawlessness,

[42] and they will cast them into the furnace of fire; there will be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.

[43] Then the righteous will shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father. He who has ears, let him hear.

[44] "The kingdom of the heavens is like a treasure hidden in the field, which a man found and hid, and from his joy he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field.

[45] "Again, the kingdom of the heavens is like a man, a merchant, seeking good pearls;

[46] and finding one pearl of great price, he went away and has sold all that he had, and bought it.

[47] "Again, the kingdom of the heavens is like a dragnet cast into the sea and gathering out of every kind;

[48] which, when it was filled, they drew up upon the shore, and sitting down they collected the good into containers, but the rotten they cast out.

[49] So it will be in the consummation of the age: the angels will go out and will set apart the evil from the midst of the righteous,

[50] and they will cast them into the furnace of fire; there will be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.

[51] "Did you understand all these things?" They say to him, "Yes."

[52] And he said to them, "Because of this every scribe who has been made a disciple to the kingdom of the heavens is like a man, a master of a house, who puts forth out of his treasure things new and old."

[53] And it came to pass, when ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ finished these parables, that he moved on from there.

[54] And coming into his fatherland he was teaching them in their synagogue, so that they were astonished and said, "From where does this one have this wisdom and the powers?

[55] Is not this the son of the carpenter? Is not his mother called ⟦mary|Miryam|Mary⟧, and his brothers ⟦james|Yaaqov|James⟧ and ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ and ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ and ⟦jude|Yehudah|Judas⟧?

[56] And his sisters, are they not all with us? From where, then, does this one have all these things?"

[57] And they were being made to stumble in him. But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to them, "A prophet is not without honor except in his fatherland and in his house."

[58] And he did not do many powers there, because of their unbelief.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (vv1, 34, 53, 57) — Hebrew name preserved; KJV "Jesus" in vv36, 51 is absent from the critical text.
- **"kingdom of heaven" → "kingdom of the heavens"** (vv11, 24, 31, 33, 44, 45, 47, 52) — Greek ἡ βασιλεία τῶν οὐρανῶν, plural and article kept.
- **"a sower" → "the sower"** (v3) — Greek ὁ σπείρων with the article, as Mark 4:3. Where Matthew's Greek is the Greek of Mark 4, the wording of Mark is used: "beside the way", "rocky places", "earth", "scorched", "withered", "fruit".
- **"some seeds … fell" → "some fell … others fell"** (vv4–8) — Greek ἃ μέν … ἄλλα δέ, neuter plurals without a noun (Mark 4:4–7 has the singular). "Fruit" (καρπόν), not "grain"; "a hundred, sixty, thirty" without "fold".
- **"ears to hear" → "ears"** (vv9, 43) — Greek ὁ ἔχων ὦτα ἀκουέτω (WH, NA28); Tregelles and the Byzantine text add ἀκούειν "to hear".
- **"he shall have more abundance" → "he will be made to abound"** (v12) — Greek περισσευθήσεται, a passive of περισσεύω "abound".
- **"seeing see not" kept** (vv13–17) — Greek βλέπω throughout, "see"; only in v14 it stands against ἴδητε, and there the two are "look" and "see" as in Mark 4:12. In v17 "to see" is ἰδεῖν and "what you see" is βλέπετε.
- **"is fulfilled" → "is filled up"** (v14) — Greek ἀναπληροῦται, the compound, a present; αὐτοῖς "in them, for them".
- **"By hearing ye shall hear, and shall not understand" → "With hearing you will hear and by no means understand"** (vv14–15) — Isa 6:9–10, translated from Matthew's Greek, which is that of the Septuagint. οὐ μή "by no means"; ἐπαχύνθη "was made thick, fat"; βαρέως ἤκουσαν "heard heavily"; ἐκάμμυσαν "shut"; ἰάσομαι is a future indicative, "and I will heal them".
- **"verily" → "amen"; "righteous men" → "righteous ones"; "have desired" → "desired"** (v17) — Greek ἀμήν; δίκαιοι; ἐπεθύμησαν, an aorist.
- **"the parable of the sower" → "the parable of the one who sowed"** (v18) — Greek τοῦ σπείραντος, an aorist participle (WH, Treg, NA28); the Byzantine text has the present σπείροντος.
- **"catcheth away" → "snatches"** (v19) — Greek ἁρπάζει. The sentence begins with a genitive absolute, "everyone hearing … and not understanding".
- **"he which received seed by the way side" → "the one sown beside the way"** (vv19–23) — Greek ὁ … σπαρείς, a masculine passive participle: the hearer himself is "the one sown".
- **"dureth for a while … tribulation … is offended" → "is for a time … affliction … is made to stumble"** (v21) — Greek πρόσκαιρός ἐστιν; θλῖψις; σκανδαλίζεται, as Mark 4:17.
- **"the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches" → "the anxiety of the age and the deceit of riches"** (v22) — Greek ἡ μέριμνα τοῦ αἰῶνος (WH, Treg, NA28); the Byzantine text and the printed SBLGNT add τούτου "of this age". ἄκαρπος γίνεται can be "it (the word) becomes unfruitful", as Mark 4:19, or "he becomes unfruitful".
- **"bringeth forth" → "makes"** (v23) — Greek ποιεῖ; ὃ μέν … ὃ δέ are neuters, "some … some", as in v8.
- **"is likened" → "was likened"** (v24) — Greek ὡμοιώθη, an aorist passive; in vv31, 33, 44, 45, 47 ὁμοία ἐστίν "is like". παρέθηκεν "set before".
- **"tares" → "darnel"** (vv25–40) — Greek ζιζάνια *zizania*, darnel (*Lolium temulentum*), a weed that looks like wheat until the ear forms. The Greek word is a plural, hence "them". ἐπέσπειρεν "sowed over, sowed on top" (WH, Treg, NA28); the Byzantine text has ἔσπειρεν "sowed".
- **"servants … householder … Sir" → "slaves … master of the house … Lord"** (vv27–28, 52) — Greek δοῦλοι; οἰκοδεσπότης; Κύριε.
- **"An enemy hath done this" → "An enemy, a man, did this"** (v28) — Greek ἐχθρὸς ἄνθρωπος, two nouns; λέγουσιν, φησιν are presents, kept.
- **"gather" → "collect" / "gather"** (vv28–30, 40–41, 47–48) — Greek συλλέγω "collect"; συνάγω "gather" (v30 of the wheat, v47 of the net). In v30 ἕως "until" (WH, Treg, NA28; the printed SBLGNT has μέχρι, the same sense); κατακαῦσαι "burn up".
- **"the least of all seeds … the greatest among herbs" → "smaller than all the seeds … greater than the garden herbs"** (v32) — Greek μικρότερον, μεῖζον, comparatives, as Mark 4:31–32. κατασκηνοῦν "dwell" (as Mark 4:32; literally "pitch a tent").
- **"three measures of meal" → "three seahs of flour"** (v33) — Greek σάτα τρία, the Aramaic form of Hebrew סְאָה *seah*, a dry measure of about 13 liters.
- **"from the foundation of the world" → "from the foundation"** (v35) — Greek ἀπὸ καταβολῆς (WH, Treg, SBLGNT); NA28 and the Byzantine text add κόσμου "of the world". The quotation is Ps 78:2, translated from Matthew's Greek; ἐρεύξομαι "I will pour forth, utter".
- **"Declare unto us" → "Make plain to us"** (v36) — Greek Διασάφησον (WH, Treg, NA28), as 18:31.
- **"the end of the world" → "the consummation of the age"** (vv39–40, 49) — Greek συντέλεια (τοῦ) αἰῶνος; in v39 without articles. "End" is τέλος; αἰών is "age", not the world (κόσμος, v38).
- **"burned" → "burned up"** (v40) — Greek κατακαίεται (WH, NA28); Tregelles and the Byzantine text have καίεται "burned". "Of this age" at the end is only in the Byzantine text.
- **"all things that offend, and them which do iniquity" → "all the stumbling blocks and those who do lawlessness"** (v41) — Greek πάντα τὰ σκάνδαλα, of one root with σκανδαλίζω "make stumble" (vv21, 57); ἀνομία "lawlessness".
- **"a furnace of fire: there shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth" → "the furnace of fire; there will be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth"** (vv42, 50) — Greek with the articles; ἐκεῖ "there".
- **"hideth … selleth … buyeth"** (v44) — the Greek has "hid" (aorist), then presents: "goes and sells … and buys". ἀπὸ τῆς χαρᾶς αὐτοῦ "from his joy" (or "from joy over it").
- **"a merchant man, seeking goodly pearls" → "a man, a merchant, seeking good pearls"** (vv45–46) — Greek ἀνθρώπῳ ἐμπόρῳ (Treg, NA28, RP; WH without "man"); καλούς "good"; πολύτιμον "of great price" (as John 12:3); πέπρακεν is a perfect, "has sold", beside the aorist "bought".
- **"a net … gathered of every kind" → "a dragnet … gathering out of every kind"** (vv47–48) — Greek σαγήνη, the large dragnet; the Greek has no word "fish". σαπρά "rotten" (as 12:33); ἄγγη "containers".
- **"sever the wicked from among the just" → "set apart the evil from the midst of the righteous"** (v49) — Greek ἀφοριοῦσιν; πονηρούς; δικαίων.
- **"instructed unto the kingdom" → "made a disciple to the kingdom"** (v52) — Greek μαθητευθεὶς τῇ βασιλείᾳ (WH, Treg, NA28), a dative: "to" or "for" the kingdom. ἐκβάλλει "puts forth" (as 12:35).
- **"he departed thence" → "he moved on from there"** (v53) — Greek μετῆρεν *metēren*, "removed" (as 19:1). Καὶ ἐγένετο "And it came to pass".
- **"his own country" → "his fatherland"** (vv54, 57) — Greek πατρίς *patris*, as Mark 6:1, 4. "Astonished" (ἐκπλήσσεσθαι).
- **"mighty works" → "powers"** (vv54, 58) — Greek δυνάμεις, as 11:20.
- **"Joses" → "Joseph"** (v55) — Greek Ἰωσήφ (WH, Treg, NA28); the Byzantine text has Ἰωσῆς, the form of Mark 6:3. "The son of the carpenter": Mark 6:3 has "the carpenter".
- **"they were offended in him" → "they were being made to stumble in him"** (v57) — Greek ἐσκανδαλίζοντο ἐν αὐτῷ, an imperfect, as Mark 6:3. "In his own country": the Greek has "in the fatherland" (WH, Treg, NA28; the Byzantine text adds αὐτοῦ "his").

### Notes
- **v14-15** quotes Isa 6:9-10.
- **v35** quotes Ps 78:2.
