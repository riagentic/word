## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved. The name stands where the Greek has it (v1, 6, 11, 12, 16, 21, 24, 27, 31, 42).
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv31, 43) — Greek θεός *theos*; divine name preserved. Here Matthew writes "kingdom of *Elohim*" (βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ), not his usual "kingdom of the heavens".
- **"the Lord" → "*Yahweh*"** (v9, 42) — Greek κύριος without the article, standing in these quotations for the name YHWH (Ps 118:26, 23).
- **"baptism" → "immersion"** (v25) — Greek βάπτισμα *baptisma*; same correction as ch. 3.
- **"And" / present tenses kept** (throughout) — the καί and δέ at the start of sentences and the Greek present tenses ("says", v13, 16, 19, 31, 42; "say", v31, 41) are kept as they are.
- **"were come to Bethphage, unto the mount of Olives"** (v1) — Greek εἰς Βηθφαγὴ εἰς τὸ Ὄρος τῶν Ἐλαιῶν (WH Treg NA28), twice εἰς; "then" is τότε.
- **"over against you, and straightway ye shall find an ass tied … loose them, and bring them unto me" → "opposite you, and immediately you will find a donkey tied … having loosed them, bring them to me"** (v2) — Greek κατέναντι; εὐθέως; ὄνον δεδεμένην, a female donkey ("with her"); λύσαντες ἀγάγετέ μοι, a participle and an imperative with no object: "them" is supplied twice. πῶλος is the young of a horse or a donkey. Mark 11:2 has the colt only.
- **"The Lord hath need of them; and straightway he will send them" → "'The Lord has need of them'; and immediately he will send them"** (v3) — Greek Ὁ κύριος αὐτῶν χρείαν ἔχει, as Mark 11:3; the words can also be taken "their lord (owner) has need". εὐθὺς δὲ ἀποστελεῖ αὐτούς, a future (WH Treg NA28): the one who is asked will send them. The Greek does not show where the words to be said end.
- **"All this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet" → "Now this has come to pass that what was spoken through the prophet might be fulfilled"** (v4) — Greek Τοῦτο δὲ γέγονεν, a perfect; "all" (RP ὅλον) is not in the critical text; διὰ τοῦ προφήτου, "through".
- **"Tell ye the daughter of Sion … meek, and sitting upon an ass, and a colt the foal of an ass" → "Say to the daughter of Tziyon … meek, and mounted upon a donkey, and upon a colt, the son of a beast of burden"** (v5) — Greek Εἴπατε τῇ θυγατρὶ Σιών (Isa 62:11), then Zech 9:9 as Matthew gives it: πραῢς καὶ ἐπιβεβηκὼς ἐπὶ ὄνον καὶ ἐπὶ πῶλον υἱὸν ὑποζυγίου. ἐπιβεβηκώς is a perfect, "having mounted"; ἐπί stands twice (WH Treg NA28); υἱὸν ὑποζυγίου is "son of an animal under the yoke". In the Hebrew the two lines name one animal twice; the Greek printed here has "a donkey and … a colt", and the verse keeps it. The words "righteous and saving is he" of Zech 9:9 are not in Matthew.
- **"went, and did as Jesus commanded them, and brought the ass" → "having gone and done even as Yeshua directed them, brought the donkey"** (v6–7) — Greek πορευθέντες … καὶ ποιήσαντες … ἤγαγον: one sentence; συνέταξεν (WH Treg NA28).
- **"put on them their clothes, and they set him thereon" → "put upon them the garments, and he sat upon them"** (v7) — Greek ἐπέθηκαν ἐπ' αὐτῶν τὰ ἱμάτια, without "their" (WH Treg NA28); ἐπεκάθισεν ἐπάνω αὐτῶν, "he sat on top of them": "them" can be the two animals or the garments; the Greek does not say which.
- **"a very great multitude spread their garments … cut down branches … and strawed them" → "most of the crowd spread their own garments … were cutting branches … and were spreading them"** (v8) — Greek ὁ δὲ πλεῖστος ὄχλος, a superlative: "the greatest part of the crowd" (so here, with "others" following) or "the very great crowd"; ἑαυτῶν τὰ ἱμάτια; ἔκοπτον, ἐστρώννυον, imperfects; ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ, "in the way" (Mark 11:8 εἰς τὴν ὁδόν).
- **"the multitudes that went before, and that followed, cried" → "the crowds going before him and those following were crying out"** (v9) — Greek οἱ προάγοντες αὐτόν (WH Treg NA28); ἔκραζον, imperfect. Ὡσαννά is Hebrew *hoshia-na*, "save, please" (Ps 118:25), left untranslated as Matthew leaves it. "In the highest": ἐν τοῖς ὑψίστοις, plural, "in the highest places".
- **"all the city was moved" → "all the city quaked"** (v10) — Greek ἐσείσθη, the verb of an earthquake (σεισμός), as 27:51; 28:4.
- **"the multitude said, This is Jesus the prophet of Nazareth" → "the crowds were saying, This is the prophet Yeshua, the one from Natzrat"** (v11) — Greek ἔλεγον; ὁ προφήτης Ἰησοῦς ὁ ἀπὸ Ναζαρέθ (WH Treg NA28).
- **"went into the temple of God, and cast out all them that sold … moneychangers … doves" → "entered into the temple, and cast out all those selling … money changers … the doves"** (v12) — "of God" (RP) is not in the critical text; ἐξέβαλεν, the verb used of demons; τὰς περιστεράς with the article. "Temple" is ἱερόν, the whole temple precinct, throughout the chapter.
- **"And said unto them … ye have made it a den of thieves" → "and he says to them … you are making it a cave of robbers"** (v13) — Greek λέγει; ποιεῖτε, a present (WH Treg NA28; RP ἐποιήσατε; Mark 11:17 "you have made"); σπήλαιον λῃστῶν (Jer 7:11): σπήλαιον is a cave; λῃστής is a robber or bandit who uses violence, not a thief (κλέπτης). "For all the nations" (Mark 11:17) is not in Matthew.
- **"the blind and the lame" → "blind and lame"** (v14) — Greek τυφλοὶ καὶ χωλοί, without articles.
- **"chief priests" → "high priests"** (v15, 23, 45) — Greek ἀρχιερεῖς, one word with "high priest".
- **"the wonderful things … the children crying" → "the marvelous things … the boys who were crying out"** (v15) — Greek τὰ θαυμάσια, of the root of "marvel" (v20) and "marvelous" (v42); τοὺς παῖδας τοὺς κράζοντας (WH Treg NA28): παῖς is a boy or, more widely, a child (also a servant); "boys" as 2:16. "Were indignant": ἠγανάκτησαν.
- **"have ye never read, Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings thou hast perfected praise" → "did you never read, Out of the mouth of infants and nursing ones you fitted praise for yourself"** (v16) — Greek οὐδέποτε ἀνέγνωτε, a simple past; νηπίων καὶ θηλαζόντων; κατηρτίσω, a middle form of καταρτίζω "fit, put in order" (Heb 10:5): "you prepared for yourself". The wording is that of Ps 8:2 in the Septuagint (8:3 there); the Hebrew has "you founded strength".
- **"he left them, and went out of the city … and he lodged there" → "leaving them behind, he went out outside the city … and spent the night there"** (v17) — Greek καταλιπών; ἐξῆλθεν ἔξω; ηὐλίσθη, "passed the night" (properly in the open or in a courtyard).
- **"in the morning as he returned into the city, he hungered" → "early, going back to the city, he was hungry"** (v18) — Greek Πρωΐ, as Mark 11:20; ἐπανάγων (Treg NA28 RP); ἐπείνασεν, as Mark 11:12.
- **"a fig tree in the way … Let no fruit grow on thee henceforward for ever. And presently the fig tree withered away" → "one fig tree by the way … Let there be fruit from you no more forever. And the fig tree withered instantly"** (v19) — Greek συκῆν μίαν, "one (a single) fig tree"; ἐπὶ τῆς ὁδοῦ, "on / by the way"; Μηκέτι ἐκ σοῦ καρπὸς γένηται, a prohibition (Treg NA28 RP; Mark 11:14 has a wish, "may no one eat"); εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα, literally "unto the age"; παραχρῆμα "instantly" (v19, 20), a different word from "immediately" (v2, 3).
- **"when the disciples saw it … How soon is the fig tree withered away!" → "the disciples, seeing, marveled … How did the fig tree wither instantly?"** (v20) — Greek ἰδόντες, without an object; Πῶς παραχρῆμα ἐξηράνθη ἡ συκῆ; a question in the printed text; the words can also be an exclamation, "How instantly the fig tree withered!"
- **"Jesus answered and said … Verily … ye shall not only do this which is done to the fig tree … Be thou removed, and be thou cast … it shall be done" → "answering, Yeshua said … Amen … not only will you do the thing of the fig tree … Be taken up and be cast … it will come to pass"** (v21) — Greek ἀποκριθεὶς … εἶπεν; ἀμήν; τὸ τῆς συκῆς, literally "the (thing) of the fig tree"; Ἄρθητι καὶ βλήθητι, as Mark 11:23; γενήσεται. "Doubt": διακριθῆτε, as Jas 1:6.
- **"all things, whatsoever ye shall ask in prayer, believing, ye shall receive"** (v22) — kept; Greek ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ, "in the prayer"; πιστεύοντες, a participle, not "if you have faith".
- **"By what authority" kept** (v23, 24, 27) — Greek ἐν ποίᾳ ἐξουσίᾳ, literally "in what kind of authority".
- **"I also will ask you one thing, which if ye tell me, I in like wise will tell you" → "I also will ask you one word, which if you tell me, I also will tell you"** (v24) — Greek λόγον ἕνα; κἀγώ twice. Mark 11:29 has no "also".
- **"whence was it? from heaven, or of men? And they reasoned with themselves" → "from where was it? From heaven or from men? And they were reasoning among themselves"** (v25) — Greek πόθεν ἦν; ἐξ ἀνθρώπων, as ἐξ οὐρανοῦ; διελογίζοντο ἐν ἑαυτοῖς (WH Treg NA28), "in / among themselves" (Mark 11:31 πρὸς ἑαυτούς).
- **"we fear the people; for all hold John as a prophet" → "we fear the crowd, for all hold Yochanan as a prophet"** (v26) — Greek ὄχλον; ὡς προφήτην ἔχουσιν. In Matthew the sentence is finished by the speakers; in Mark 11:32 it breaks off.
- **"We cannot tell. And he said unto them, Neither tell I you" → "We do not know. He also said to them, Neither do I tell you"** (v27) — Greek Οὐκ οἴδαμεν; ἔφη αὐτοῖς καὶ αὐτός; λέγω, a present.
- **"A certain man had two sons … Son, go work to day in my vineyard" → "A man had two children … Child, go, work today in the vineyard"** (v28) — Greek τέκνα, τέκνον; "my" (RP) is not in the critical text. "Today" can go with "go" or with "work". "And approaching": the verse follows καὶ προσελθών (Treg NA28 RP); WH and the SBLGNT have no καί. "What do you think?": Τί ὑμῖν δοκεῖ, literally "what seems to you?"
- **"I will not: but afterward he repented, and went" → "I do not want to; but afterward, regretting it, he went"** (v29) — Greek Οὐ θέλω; μεταμεληθείς, "regret" (as 2 Cor 7:8), not the word "repent" (μετανοέω); also v32. "Went" (v29, 30) is ἀπῆλθεν, "went off".
- **"I go, sir: and went not" → "I, lord; and he did not go"** (v30) — Greek Ἐγώ, κύριε: "I", with no verb, a word of assent; κύριε is "lord". "The second": δευτέρῳ (WH Treg RP; NA28 ἑτέρῳ, "the other").
- **The order of the two sons** (v29–31) — the verse follows Treg NA28 RP: the first says no and goes, the second says "I, lord" and does not go, and the answer is "The first" (NA28 RP). WH has the two answers in the reverse order (the first says "I, lord" and does not go) and the answer "The latter" (ὕστερος, WH Treg). The sense of the parable is the same.
- **"Whether of them twain did the will of his father? They say unto him" → "Which of the two did the will of the father? They say"** (v31) — Greek τοῦ πατρός; "to him" (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"Verily … the publicans and the harlots go into the kingdom of God before you" → "Amen … the toll collectors and the prostitutes are going before you into the kingdom of Elohim"** (v31) — Greek προάγουσιν ὑμᾶς, the verb of v9; it says they go ahead, not whether the others follow. τελῶναι are collectors of tolls (τέλος); πόρναι as 1 Cor 6:15.
- **"ye, when ye had seen it, repented not afterward, that ye might believe him" → "you, seeing, did not even regret it afterward, so as to believe him"** (v32) — Greek ἰδόντες, without an object; οὐδὲ μετεμελήθητε (WH Treg NA28); τοῦ πιστεῦσαι αὐτῷ.
- **"There was a certain householder, which planted a vineyard, and hedged it round about, and digged a winepress in it … let it out to husbandmen, and went into a far country" → "There was a man, a master of a house, who planted a vineyard and put a fence around it and dug a winepress in it … let it out to farmers, and went away from home"** (v33) — Greek Ἄνθρωπος ἦν οἰκοδεσπότης (WH Treg NA28); φραγμὸν αὐτῷ περιέθηκεν; ληνόν, the winepress itself (Mark 12:1 ὑπολήνιον, the vat under it); γεωργοῖς, "workers of the ground" (as 2 Tim 2:6), here tenants; ἀπεδήμησεν: the Greek has no "far country". The wording echoes Isa 5:1–2.
- **"when the time of the fruit drew near, he sent his servants … that they might receive the fruits of it" → "when the time of the fruits drew near, he sent his slaves … to receive his fruits"** (v34) — Greek δούλους (v34, 35, 36); τοὺς καρποὺς αὐτοῦ: "his fruits" or "its fruits" (of the vineyard).
- **"took his servants" → "taking his slaves"** (v35, 39) — Greek λαβόντες.
- **"last of all he sent unto them his son … They will reverence my son" → "afterward he sent to them his son … They will respect my son"** (v37) — Greek ὕστερον, the word of v29, 32; ἐντραπήσονται, as Mark 12:6.
- **"let us seize on his inheritance" → "let us … have his inheritance"** (v38) — Greek σχῶμεν (WH Treg NA28), "have, get"; RP κατάσχωμεν, "seize". "Among themselves": ἐν ἑαυτοῖς.
- **"cast him out of the vineyard, and slew him"** (v39) — Greek ἐξέβαλον ἔξω … καὶ ἀπέκτειναν, in this order (Mark 12:8: killed, then cast out).
- **"He will miserably destroy those wicked men … which shall render him the fruits in their seasons" → "Bad men — he will badly destroy them … who will render to him the fruits in their times"** (v41) — Greek Κακοὺς κακῶς ἀπολέσει αὐτούς, a play on one word: "bad (men), badly he will destroy them"; ἀποδώσουσιν, "pay what is owed" (as 22:21); καιροῖς, as v34. In Mark 12:9 Yeshua himself gives the answer.
- **"Did ye never read in the scriptures, The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner" → "Did you never read in the scriptures: The stone which the builders rejected, this became the head of the corner"** (v42) — Greek word for word as Mark 12:10–11 (Ps 118:22–23 in the Septuagint). "Head of the corner" is the chief stone at a corner.
- **"this is the Lord's doing" → "from Yahweh this came to be"** (v42) — Greek παρὰ κυρίου ἐγένετο αὕτη; αὕτη, "this", is feminine, following the Hebrew.
- **"Therefore say I unto you, The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and given to a nation bringing forth the fruits thereof" → "Because of this I say to you that the kingdom of Elohim will be taken away from you and will be given to a nation making its fruits"** (v43) — Greek διὰ τοῦτο; ἀρθήσεται; ἔθνει, "a nation" (the word of "the nations"); ποιοῦντι τοὺς καρπούς, "making the fruits", as 3:8; 7:17: bearing them.
- **"whosoever shall fall on this stone shall be broken: but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder" → "the one falling upon this stone will be shattered; but upon whomever it falls, it will winnow him"** (v44) — Greek συνθλασθήσεται, "be crushed together, shattered"; λικμήσει, from λικμάω "winnow, scatter as chaff"; here it is often rendered "crush" or "grind to powder". The verse is in WH Treg NA28 RP; the margin of WH omits it.
- **"they perceived that he spake of them" → "knew that he was speaking about them"** (v45) — Greek ἔγνωσαν, as Mark 12:12; λέγει, a present.
- **"when they sought to lay hands on him, they feared the multitude, because they took him for a prophet" → "seeking to take hold of him, they feared the crowds, since they held him for a prophet"** (v46) — Greek κρατῆσαι; τοὺς ὄχλους, plural; ἐπεὶ εἰς προφήτην αὐτὸν εἶχον (WH Treg NA28): εἰς "for" here, ὡς "as" in v26.
- **"multitudes" → "crowds"** (v8, 9, 11, 26, 46) — Greek ὄχλος.
- **"publicans" → "toll collectors"** (v31–32) — Greek τελῶναι *telōnai*.
- **"husbandmen" → "farmers"** (vv33–41) — Greek γεωργοί *geōrgoi*, as Mark 12:1.

### Notes

- **v9 *Hoshia-na*** — Hebrew "Save now!" (from Ps 118:25).
- **v13** quotes Isa 56:7 + Jer 7:11.
- **v16** quotes Ps 8:2.
- **v42** quotes Ps 118:22-23.
