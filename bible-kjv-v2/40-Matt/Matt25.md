# Matthew 25

[1] "Then the kingdom of the heavens will be likened to ten virgins who, having taken their own torches, went out to meet the bridegroom.

[2] Now five of them were foolish and five sensible.

[3] For the foolish, having taken their torches, did not take oil with them,

[4] but the sensible took oil in the vessels with their own torches.

[5] Now while the bridegroom delayed, they all slumbered and were sleeping.

[6] But in the middle of the night a cry has come: 'Behold, the bridegroom! Come out to meet him.'

[7] Then all those virgins arose and put their own torches in order.

[8] And the foolish said to the sensible, 'Give us of your oil, because our torches are going out.'

[9] But the sensible answered, saying, 'Lest it by no means suffice for us and you — go rather to those who sell, and buy for yourselves.'

[10] And as they were going away to buy, the bridegroom came, and the ready ones went in with him to the wedding, and the door was shut.

[11] And afterward the rest of the virgins also come, saying, 'Lord, Lord, open to us.'

[12] But he, answering, said, 'Amen, I say to you, I do not know you.'

[13] Be awake therefore, because you do not know the day nor the hour.

[14] "For as a man going away from home called his own slaves and delivered to them his possessions,

[15] and to one he gave five talents, and to another two, and to another one, to each according to his own power, and he went away from home.

[16] Immediately the one who had received the five talents went and worked with them and gained five others;

[17] likewise the one with the two gained two others.

[18] But the one who had received the one went away and dug the earth and hid his lord's silver.

[19] Now after much time the lord of those slaves comes and settles an account with them.

[20] And the one who had received the five talents, approaching, brought five other talents, saying, 'Lord, five talents you delivered to me; see, I gained five other talents.'

[21] His lord said to him, '**Well done, good and faithful slave. Over a few things you were faithful; over many I will appoint you. Enter into the joy of your lord.**'

[22] And the one with the two talents, also approaching, said, 'Lord, two talents you delivered to me; see, I gained two other talents.'

[23] His lord said to him, 'Well done, good and faithful slave. Over a few things you were faithful; over many I will appoint you. Enter into the joy of your lord.'

[24] "And the one who had received the one talent, also approaching, said, 'Lord, I came to know you, that you are a hard man, reaping where you did not sow and gathering from where you did not scatter;

[25] and being afraid, I went away and hid your talent in the earth. See, you have what is yours.'

[26] But his lord, answering, said to him, 'Evil and slothful slave, you knew that I reap where I did not sow and gather from where I did not scatter?

[27] Then you ought to have put my silver pieces with the bankers, and I, coming, would have carried off what is mine with interest.

[28] Take therefore the talent from him and give it to the one who has the ten talents.

[29] For to everyone who has, it will be given, and he will be made to abound; but the one who does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.

[30] And cast out the useless slave into the outer darkness. There will be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.'

[31] "But when the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, then he will sit on the throne of his glory.

[32] And all the nations will be gathered together before him, and he will set them apart from one another, as the shepherd sets apart the sheep from the goats,

[33] and he will set the sheep at his right hand, but the goats at the left.

[34] Then the King will say to those at his right hand, 'Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom made ready for you from the foundation of the world.

[35] For I hungered and you gave me to eat, I thirsted and you gave me to drink, I was a stranger and you gathered me in,

[36] naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me, I was in prison and you came to me.'

[37] Then the righteous will answer him, saying, 'Lord, when did we see you hungering and feed you, or thirsting and give you to drink?

[38] And when did we see you a stranger and gather you in, or naked and clothe you?

[39] And when did we see you sick or in prison and come to you?'

[40] And answering, the King will say to them, '**Amen, I say to you, inasmuch as you did it to one of these my brothers, the least, you did it to me.**'

[41] "Then he will say also to those at the left, 'Go from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire made ready for the devil and his angels.

[42] For I hungered and you did not give me to eat, I thirsted and you did not give me to drink,

[43] I was a stranger and you did not gather me in, naked and you did not clothe me, sick and in prison and you did not visit me.'

[44] Then they also will answer, saying, 'Lord, when did we see you hungering or thirsting or a stranger or naked or sick or in prison, and did not serve you?'

[45] Then he will answer them, saying, 'Amen, I say to you, inasmuch as you did not do it to one of these least, neither did you do it to me.'

[46] And these will go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous into eternal life."

## Changes

- **"kingdom of heaven" → "kingdom of the heavens"** (v1) — Greek ἡ βασιλεία τῶν οὐρανῶν, plural with the article. "Will be likened" is the future passive ὁμοιωθήσεται.
- **"lamps" → "torches"** (v1, 3, 4, 7, 8) — Greek λαμπάς, a torch (as John 18:3; Rev 4:5); the household lamp is λύχνος (Matt 5:15). The oil is for torches of oil-soaked cloth, or the word is used here of oil lamps (so KJV, and Acts 20:8 of the lights in a room).
- **"wise" → "sensible"** (v2, 4, 8, 9) — Greek φρόνιμος; "foolish" is μωρός.
- **"to meet"** (v1, 6) — two nouns, εἰς ὑπάντησιν (v1) and εἰς ἀπάντησιν (v6), with no difference in meaning.
- **"slumbered and slept" → "slumbered and were sleeping"** (v5) — Greek ἐνύσταξαν (aorist: they nodded off) καὶ ἐκάθευδον (imperfect: they went on sleeping). "Delayed" is χρονίζοντος, "taking time".
- **"at midnight there was a cry made" → "in the middle of the night a cry has come"** (v6) — Greek μέσης νυκτός; γέγονεν is a perfect. Mark 13:35 has the single word μεσονύκτιον, "at midnight". The Byzantine text adds "comes" after "the bridegroom".
- **"arose"** (v7) — Greek ἠγέρθησαν, the passive of ἐγείρω ("were roused"). **"trimmed" → "put in order"** — Greek ἐκόσμησαν, "put in order, adorn"; what was done to the torches is not said.
- **"Not so; lest there be not enough" → "Lest it by no means suffice"** (v9) — Greek Μήποτε οὐ μὴ ἀρκέσῃ, a broken sentence: "lest" with the strong double negative; nothing stands before it ("Not so" is supplied in the KJV). The Byzantine text has the single negative οὐκ. "Those who sell" is the participle τοὺς πωλοῦντας.
- **"marriage" → "wedding"** (v10) — Greek τοὺς γάμους, plural, the wedding festivities (as John 2:1).
- **"came" → "come"** (v11) — Greek ἔρχονται, present; so also "comes and settles" (v19, ἔρχεται, συναίρει).
- **"Verily" → "Amen"** (v12, 40, 45) — Greek ἀμήν, left untranslated.
- **"Watch" → "Be awake"** (v13) — Greek γρηγορεῖτε, as Mark 13:35, 37. The Byzantine text adds "in which the Son of Man comes".
- **"For the kingdom of heaven is as a man" → "For as a man"** (v14) — Greek Ὥσπερ γὰρ ἄνθρωπος: the comparison has no main clause ("the kingdom of heaven is" is supplied in the KJV); the sentence is left as it stands, as in Mark 13:34.
- **"travelling into a far country" → "going away from home"** (v14, 15) — Greek ἀποδημῶν, ἀπεδήμησεν, as Mark 12:1; 13:34.
- **"servants" → "slaves"** (v14, 19, 21, 23, 26, 30) — Greek δοῦλος.
- **"goods" → "possessions"** (v14) — Greek τὰ ὑπάρχοντα. "Delivered" (v14, 20, 22) is παραδίδωμι, the verb of 26:2.
- **"talents" kept** (v15–28) — Greek τάλαντον, a weight of silver (about 6,000 denarii, some twenty years of a laborer's wages); not an ability.
- **"ability" → "power"** (v15) — Greek δύναμις.
- **"and straightway took his journey. Then he … went" → "and he went away from home. Immediately the one … went"** (v15–16) — Greek εὐθέως stands at the end of v15 in the printed text and begins the sentence of v16 (WH, NA28). With δέ after πορευθείς (Tregelles, Byzantine) it belongs to v15: "and he went away from home immediately".
- **"traded with the same" → "worked with them"** (v16) — Greek ἠργάσατο ἐν αὐτοῖς. **"made" → "gained"** (v16, 17, 20, 22) — Greek ἐκέρδησεν (the Byzantine text has ἐποίησεν, "made", in v16).
- **"digged in the earth" → "dug the earth"** (v18) — Greek ὤρυξεν γῆν (the Byzantine text: ἐν τῇ γῇ, "in the earth"). **"money" → "silver"** (v18), **"silver pieces"** (v27) — Greek τὸ ἀργύριον; in v27 the plural τὰ ἀργύρια (WH, NA28; Tregelles and the Byzantine text have the singular).
- **"lord"** (v18–26) — Greek κύριος, the same word as the address "Lord" (κύριε, v11, 20, 22, 24, 37, 44); "master" would be δεσπότης.
- **"reckoneth" → "settles an account"** (v19) — Greek συναίρει λόγον, singular.
- **"came and brought" → "approaching, brought"** (v20, 22, 24) — Greek προσελθών.
- **"behold" → "see"** (v20, 22, 25) — Greek ἴδε; "Behold" (v6) is ἰδού.
- **"Well done"** (v21, 23) — Greek Εὖ, "well!". **"I will make thee ruler" → "I will appoint you"** — Greek καταστήσω. "A few things … many" are plurals (ὀλίγα, πολλῶν).
- **"I knew thee" → "I came to know you"** (v24) — Greek ἔγνων (γινώσκω); "you knew" in v26 is ᾔδεις (οἶδα). **"where thou hast not strawed" → "from where you did not scatter"** (v24, 26) — Greek ὅθεν οὐ διεσκόρπισας; no "seed" in the Greek.
- **"wicked" → "evil"** (v26) — Greek πονηρός. The sentence is printed as a question.
- **"exchangers" → "bankers"** (v27) — Greek τραπεζῖται, "table men" (money changers who took deposits). **"received" → "carried off"** — Greek ἐκομισάμην ἄν (κομίζομαι, as 2 Cor 5:10; Heb 11:19), here "got back". "Put" is βαλεῖν, "throw". **"usury" → "interest"** — Greek τόκος.
- **"he shall have abundance" → "he will be made to abound"** (v29) — Greek περισσευθήσεται, passive. Compare Mark 4:25.
- **"unprofitable" → "useless"** (v30) — Greek ἀχρεῖος. **"cast out"** — Greek ἐκβάλετε. **"the weeping and the gnashing of teeth"** — both nouns have the article (ὁ κλαυθμὸς καὶ ὁ βρυγμὸς τῶν ὀδόντων). "There" (ἐκεῖ) means in that place.
- **"holy angels" → "angels"** (v31) — "holy" is only in the Byzantine text.
- **"separate" → "set apart"** (v32) — Greek ἀφορίζω, twice. "Them" is masculine (αὐτούς), though "nations" is neuter: the persons are meant.
- **"goats"** (v32, 33) — Greek ἔριφος (v32) and its diminutive ἐρίφιον (v33), properly a kid, a young goat.
- **"on his right hand … on the left"** (v33, 34, 41) — Greek ἐκ δεξιῶν, ἐξ εὐωνύμων; "hand" is not in the Greek.
- **"prepared" → "made ready"** (v34, 41) — Greek ἡτοιμασμένην, ἡτοιμασμένον, perfect participles.
- **"I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat" → "I hungered and you gave me to eat"** (v35, 42) — Greek ἐπείνασα … ἐδώκατέ μοι φαγεῖν; likewise "thirsted", "gave me to drink" (ἐποτίσατε).
- **"took me in" → "gathered me in"** (v35, 38, 43) — Greek συνάγω, the verb of v24, 26 ("gathering") and v32 ("will be gathered together").
- **"sick"** (v36, 39, 43, 44) — Greek ἀσθενέω, ἀσθενής, "be weak, sick". **"came unto thee" kept** (v39) — Greek ἤλθομεν πρός σε, as v36; "visited" (v36, 43) is ἐπισκέπτομαι.
- **"Inasmuch as"** (v40, 45) — Greek ἐφ᾽ ὅσον, "to the extent that" (as Rom 11:13). **"the least of these my brethren" → "these my brothers, the least"** (v40) — the Greek order: τούτων τῶν ἀδελφῶν μου τῶν ἐλαχίστων. "It" is supplied.
- **"Depart" → "Go"** (v41) — Greek πορεύεσθε. "Cursed" is a perfect participle (κατηραμένοι, "who have been cursed").
- **"minister unto" → "serve"** (v44) — Greek διακονέω.
- **"everlasting punishment … life eternal" → "eternal punishment … eternal life"** (v46) — Greek αἰώνιος both times (and of the fire, v41): one word, one rendering, "of the age, eternal". **"punishment"** — Greek κόλασις: punishment, chastisement; in older Greek the word is used of pruning and of correction, and Aristotle (Rhetoric 1.10) sets κόλασις, for the sake of the one who suffers it, against τιμωρία, for the sake of the one who inflicts it; in the Greek of this time it is the ordinary word for punishment (1 John 4:18; the verb in Acts 4:21; 2 Pet 2:9).

### Notes
- **v21, 23** — the two answers of the lord are word for word the same in Greek.
- **v14–30** — compare Mark 13:34 (a man away from home who gives his slaves authority) and the parable of the minas, Luke 19:12–27.
