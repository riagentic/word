## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** — Hebrew name he was actually called (Greek *Iesous* renders Hebrew *Yeshua* = "Yahweh saves"); name explained in v21.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** — Greek *Christos* translates Hebrew *Mashiach* = "Anointed."
- **"Holy Ghost" → "*Ruach HaQodesh*"** — Hebrew term preserved.
- **"Mary" → "*Miryam*"** at first use (then anglicized).
- **"The book of the generation" → "The book of the origin"; "the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise" → "the origin of *Yeshua* the *Mashiach* was thus"** (v1, 18) — Greek γένεσις in both verses: origin, coming into being, birth; Βίβλος γενέσεως is the wording of Gen 2:4; 5:1 in the Septuagint. The Byzantine text (RP) has γέννησις, "birth", in v18. Treg omits "*Yeshua*" in v18.
- **"begat" → "begot"** (v2–16) — Greek ἐγέννησεν, 39 times, one rendering. δέ, "and", joins each link; v2 begins without it.
- **"of Thamar" kept; "of Rachab", "of Ruth" kept** (v3, 5) — Greek ἐκ τῆς, "out of": the mother.
- **"of her that had been the wife of Urias" → "of her of *Uriyah*"** (v6) — Greek ἐκ τῆς τοῦ Οὐρίου, "of the (woman) of Uriah": the Greek has neither her name nor the word "wife". "David the king" a second time (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"Asa" / "Amon"** (v7–8, 10) — Greek Ἀσάφ, Ἀμώς (WH Treg NA28), "Asaph", "Amos"; RP has Ἀσά, Ἀμών, the names of the kings in 1 Kgs 15:8; 2 Kgs 21:18.
- **"about the time they were carried away to Babylon" → "at the deportation to Babylon"** (v11, 12, 17) — Greek ἐπὶ τῆς μετοικεσίας Βαβυλῶνος: μετοικεσία is a change of dwelling-place; Βαβυλῶνος is a plain genitive, "of Babylon".
- **"of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" → "of whom was begotten *Yeshua*, the one called *Mashiach*"** (v16) — Greek ἐξ ἧς ἐγεννήθη Ἰησοῦς ὁ λεγόμενος χριστός. ἧς is feminine singular: "of whom" is Miryam alone. ἐγεννήθη is the passive of the verb "begot" of v2–16 (of a mother it is "bear": "was born").
- **"When as his mother Mary was espoused" → "His mother Miryam having been betrothed"** (v18) — Greek μνηστευθείσης τῆς μητρὸς αὐτοῦ, a participle clause. πρὶν ἢ συνελθεῖν αὐτούς, "before they came together".
- **"with child" kept** (v18, 23) — Greek ἐν γαστρὶ ἔχουσα / ἕξει, literally "having / will have in the womb" (as Mark 13:17).
- **"of the Holy Ghost" → "of *Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v18, 20) — Greek ἐκ πνεύματος ἁγίου, without the article both times; ἐκ, "out of", is the preposition used of the mothers in v3, 5, 6, 16.
- **"a just man" → "righteous"; "not willing" → "not wanting"; "was minded" → "wished"; "to put her away privily" → "to send her away secretly"** (v19) — Greek δίκαιος ὤν; μὴ θέλων; ἐβουλήθη; λάθρᾳ ἀπολῦσαι: ἀπολύω is "send away", the verb of 5:31–32 and Mark 10:2–12 (to divorce).
- **"make her a publick example" → "make a show of her"** (v19) — Greek δειγματίσαι, "expose, make a show of" (as Col 2:15); RP has παραδειγματίσαι.
- **"while he thought on these things" → "when he had pondered these things"** (v20) — Greek ἐνθυμηθέντος, an aorist.
- **"to take unto thee Mary thy wife" → "to take Miryam your wife"** (v20, 24) — Greek παραλαβεῖν Μαρίαν τὴν γυναῖκά σου: no "as"; she is already called his wife. παραλαμβάνω is "take along" in 2:13–21; here it is taking her to himself.
- **"that which is conceived in her" → "that which has been begotten in her"** (v20) — Greek τὸ ἐν αὐτῇ γεννηθέν, the verb of v2–16.
- **"all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet" → "all this has come to pass that what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled"** (v22) — Greek γέγονεν, a perfect; ὑπὸ κυρίου, "by" the Lord (without the article); διὰ τοῦ προφήτου, "through" the prophet.
- **"a virgin" → "the virgin"** (v23) — Greek ἡ παρθένος, with the article: "virgin". The quotation is Isa 7:14 as the Greek of Matthew has it; the Hebrew there has הָעַלְמָה, "the young woman", and "she will call" (Matthew: καλέσουσιν, "they will call").
- **"which being interpreted is, God with us" → "which is, being translated, 'With us *Elohim*'"** (v23) — Greek ὅ ἐστιν μεθερμηνευόμενον Μεθ' ἡμῶν ὁ θεός, in the order of the Hebrew name; the Greek has no "is" ("With us is *Elohim*").
- **"being raised from sleep" → "roused from sleep"; "had bidden him" → "prescribed to him"** (v24) — Greek ἐγερθείς, passive; προσέταξεν (as Mark 1:44).
- **"knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son" → "was not knowing her until she bore a son"** (v25) — Greek οὐκ ἐγίνωσκεν, an imperfect; ἕως οὗ, "until", says nothing about the time after. "a son" WH Treg NA28; "her firstborn son" (τὸν υἱὸν αὐτῆς τὸν πρωτότοκον) is the Byzantine reading (RP).

### Notes
- **Four women in genealogy** (Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, Bathsheba): Gentile or scandalous, hinting at Gospel's scope and grace.
- **v23** — quotes Isa 7:14.
- **v20** — Treg and RP spell the name Μαριάμ here; WH NA28 Μαρίαν.
