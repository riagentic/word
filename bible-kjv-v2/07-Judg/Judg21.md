## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"repented them" → "had compassion"** (v6, 15) — Hebrew *nacham*. Turned / relented.
- **"wept sore" → "wept bitterly"** (v2) — modernized.
- **"utterly destroy" → "devote to destruction"** (v11) — *cherem*.

### Notes
- **Rash oath problem** — Israel had sworn not to give daughters to Benjamin. Now they need wives for 600 Benjamite survivors. Two solutions:
  1. **Jabesh-gilead massacre** (v8–12) — a city that didn't join the war against Benjamin is destroyed; 400 virgin women taken as wives.
  2. **Shiloh kidnapping** (v19–23) — 200 more wives taken by ambush from the annual feast at Shiloh.
- **Legalistic evasion** — the oath's letter is preserved (they didn't *give* daughters) but the spirit is twisted (women are seized). Classic judges-era casuistry.
- **Jabesh-gilead connection** — these women become the ancestors of many Benjamites. Later 1 Sam 11 records Saul (a Benjamite) rescuing Jabesh-gilead; the bond persists. 1 Sam 31 Jabesh-gilead recovers Saul's body.
- **Refrain** (v25) — the book closes with the refrain: "no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Transition to the monarchic era.
- **Dark mirror of Judges** — what began with purposeful judges delivering Israel ends with incest-adjacent and kidnapping stories. The whole book traces a descending arc: early judges are heroic; late narratives are morally broken. The implicit conclusion: only a king can right this.
