# Judges 21

[1] The men of Israel had sworn at Mizpah, "None of us will give his daughter to Benjamin as a wife."

[2] The people came to Bethel and sat there until evening before *Elohim*; they lifted up their voices and wept bitterly.

[3] They said, "O Yahweh, *Elohim* of Israel, why has this come to pass in Israel, that there should today be one tribe lacking in Israel?"

[4] On the next day the people rose early, built an altar there, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings.

[5] The children of Israel said, "Who of all the tribes of Israel did not come up to the assembly to Yahweh?" For they had made a great oath concerning anyone who did not come up to Yahweh at Mizpah, saying, "He shall surely be put to death."

[6] The children of Israel had compassion on Benjamin their brother, and said, "One tribe has been cut off from Israel today.

[7] What shall we do about wives for those who remain, since we have sworn by Yahweh not to give them any of our daughters as wives?"

[8] They said, "Which one of the tribes of Israel did not come up to Mizpah to Yahweh?" And behold, no one had come to the camp from Jabesh-gilead, to the assembly.

[9] For when the people were mustered, behold, none of the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead were there.

[10] So the congregation sent 12,000 of the most valiant men there and commanded them, "Go and strike the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead with the edge of the sword, with the women and the little ones.

[11] This is what you shall do: every male and every woman who has known a man by lying with him you shall devote to destruction."

[12] They found among the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead 400 young virgins who had not known a man by lying with him; they brought them to the camp at Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan.

[13] The whole congregation sent messengers to speak to the children of Benjamin who were at the rock of Rimmon, and proclaimed peace to them.

[14] Benjamin returned at that time, and they gave them the women whom they had saved alive of the women of Jabesh-gilead; but they were not enough for them.

[15] The people had compassion on Benjamin, because Yahweh had made a breach in the tribes of Israel.

[16] The elders of the congregation said, "What shall we do for wives for those who remain, since the women of Benjamin are destroyed?"

[17] They said, "There must be an inheritance for those survivors of Benjamin, that a tribe may not be blotted out from Israel.

[18] But we cannot give them wives from our daughters, for the children of Israel have sworn, 'Cursed is the one who gives a wife to Benjamin.'"

[19] So they said, "Behold, there is the yearly feast of Yahweh at Shiloh, which is north of Bethel, on the east of the highway that goes up from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebonah."

[20] They commanded the children of Benjamin, "Go and lie in wait in the vineyards;

[21] watch, and if the daughters of Shiloh come out to dance in the dances, then come out of the vineyards and each of you seize his wife from the daughters of Shiloh, and go to the land of Benjamin.

[22] When their fathers or their brothers come to complain to us, we will say to them, 'Grant them graciously to us, because we did not take for each man his wife in battle, and because you did not give them to them — otherwise you would now be guilty.'"

[23] The children of Benjamin did so; they took wives according to their number from the dancers whom they carried off. They went and returned to their inheritance, rebuilt the cities, and dwelt in them.

[24] The children of Israel departed from there at that time, each one to his tribe and to his clan; they went out from there each one to his inheritance.

[25] In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"repented them" → "had compassion"** (v6, 15) — Hebrew *nacham*. Turned / relented.
- **"wept sore" → "wept bitterly"** (v2) — modernized.
- **"utterly destroy" → "devote to destruction"** (v11) — *cherem*.

### Notes
- **Rash oath problem** — Israel had sworn not to give daughters to Benjamin. Now they need wives for 600 Benjamite survivors. Two solutions:
  1. **Jabesh-gilead massacre** (v8–12) — a city that didn't join the war against Benjamin is destroyed; 400 virgin women taken as wives.
  2. **Shiloh kidnapping** (v19–23) — 200 more wives taken by ambush from the annual feast at Shiloh.
- **Legalistic evasion** — the oath's letter is preserved (they didn't *give* daughters) but the spirit is twisted (women are seized). Classic judges-era casuistry.
- **Jabesh-gilead connection** — these women become the ancestors of many Benjamites. Later 1 Sam 11 records Saul (a Benjamite) rescuing Jabesh-gilead; the bond persists. 1 Sam 31 Jabesh-gilead recovers Saul's body.
- **Refrain** (v25) — the book closes with the refrain: "no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Transition to the monarchic era.
- **Dark mirror of Judges** — what began with purposeful judges delivering Israel ends with incest-adjacent and kidnapping stories. The whole book traces a descending arc: early judges are heroic; late narratives are morally broken. The implicit conclusion: only a king can right this.
