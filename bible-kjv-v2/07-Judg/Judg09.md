# Judges 9

[1] Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem, to his mother's brothers, and spoke to them and to the whole clan of his mother's ancestral house, saying,

[2] "Speak, please, in the hearing of all the lords of Shechem: 'Which is better for you — that all seventy of the sons of Jerubbaal rule over you, or that one man rule over you?' Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh."

[3] His mother's brothers spoke these words on his behalf in the hearing of all the lords of Shechem, and their hearts inclined to follow Abimelech, for they said, "He is our brother."

[4] They gave him seventy pieces of silver from the temple of Baal-berith, and with them Abimelech hired worthless and reckless men, who followed him.

[5] He went to his father's house at Ophrah and killed his brothers, the sons of Jerubbaal — seventy men — on one stone; but Jotham the youngest son of Jerubbaal was left, because he hid himself.

[6] All the lords of Shechem and all Beth-millo gathered together; they went and made Abimelech king, by the oak of the pillar that was at Shechem.

[7] When they told Jotham of it, he went and stood on the top of Mount Gerizim, and lifted up his voice and cried out, saying to them:

"Listen to me, lords of Shechem,
that *Elohim* may listen to you.

[8] The trees once went to anoint a king over themselves.
They said to the olive tree, 'Reign over us.'

[9] But the olive tree said to them,
'Shall I leave my richness,
by which gods and men are honored,
and go to sway over the trees?'

[10] So the trees said to the fig tree,
'Come, you, reign over us.'

[11] But the fig tree said to them,
'Shall I leave my sweetness and my good fruit,
and go to sway over the trees?'

[12] Then the trees said to the vine,
'Come, you, reign over us.'

[13] But the vine said to them,
'Shall I leave my new wine,
which cheers gods and men,
and go to sway over the trees?'

[14] Then all the trees said to the bramble,
'Come, you, reign over us.'

[15] And the bramble said to the trees,
'If in truth you are anointing me as king over you,
come, take refuge in my shade;
but if not, let fire come out of the bramble
and devour the cedars of Lebanon.'

[16] "Now therefore, if you have acted truly and sincerely in making Abimelech king, and if you have dealt well with Jerubbaal and with his house, and have done to him as his hands deserved —

[17] (for my father fought for you and risked his life to deliver you out of the hand of Midian;

[18] but you have risen up against my father's house this day, have killed his sons — seventy men — on one stone, and have made Abimelech, the son of his maidservant, king over the lords of Shechem because he is your brother) —

[19] if then you have acted truly and sincerely with Jerubbaal and his house this day, rejoice in Abimelech and let him also rejoice in you.

[20] But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and devour the lords of Shechem and Beth-millo; and let fire come out from the lords of Shechem and from Beth-millo and devour Abimelech."

[21] Jotham ran away and fled; he went to Beer and dwelt there because of Abimelech his brother.

[22] When Abimelech had ruled over Israel three years,

[23] *Elohim* sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the lords of Shechem, and the lords of Shechem dealt treacherously with Abimelech,

[24] so that the violence done to the seventy sons of Jerubbaal might come back, and their blood be laid on Abimelech their brother, who killed them, and on the lords of Shechem, who aided him in the killing of his brothers.

[25] The lords of Shechem set men in ambush against him on the mountaintops, and they robbed everyone who passed by them along the road; and it was told Abimelech.

[26] Gaal son of Ebed came with his kinsmen, passing over to Shechem; and the lords of Shechem put their confidence in him.

[27] They went out into the fields and gathered the grapes of their vineyards, trod them, and held a festival; they went into the house of their god, ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.

[28] Gaal son of Ebed said, "Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? Is he not the son of Jerubbaal, and Zebul his officer? Serve the men of Hamor, the father of Shechem! Why should we serve him?

[29] Would that this people were under my authority! Then I would remove Abimelech." He said to Abimelech, "Gather your army and come out."

[30] When Zebul, the governor of the city, heard the words of Gaal son of Ebed, his anger was kindled.

[31] He sent messengers to Abimelech secretly, saying, "Behold, Gaal son of Ebed and his kinsmen have come to Shechem, and they are stirring up the city against you.

[32] Now therefore, at night go up — you and the people who are with you — and lie in wait in the field.

[33] In the morning, as soon as the sun rises, you shall rise early and rush upon the city. And behold, when he and the people with him come out against you, you shall do to them whatever your hand finds to do."

[34] Abimelech and all the people with him rose up by night, and they lay in wait against Shechem in four companies.

[35] Gaal son of Ebed went out and stood at the entrance of the gate of the city. Abimelech and the people with him rose up from the ambush.

[36] When Gaal saw the people, he said to Zebul, "Behold, people are coming down from the mountaintops!" But Zebul said to him, "You see the shadow of the mountains as if they were men."

[37] Gaal spoke again: "Look, people are coming down by the heart of the land, and another company is coming by way of the Diviners' Oak."

[38] Then Zebul said to him, "Where now is your bluster, you who said, 'Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?' Is this not the people you despised? Go out now and fight with them."

[39] Gaal went out before the lords of Shechem and fought with Abimelech.

[40] Abimelech pursued him, and he fled before him; many fell wounded, even to the entrance of the gate.

[41] Abimelech stayed at Arumah, and Zebul drove out Gaal and his kinsmen, so that they could not dwell at Shechem.

[42] On the next day the people went out into the field, and it was told Abimelech.

[43] He took his forces, divided them into three companies, and lay in wait in the field. When he saw that the people were coming out of the city, he rose against them and struck them down.

[44] Abimelech and the company that was with him rushed forward and stood at the entrance of the gate of the city, while the other two companies rushed upon all who were in the fields and struck them down.

[45] Abimelech fought against the city all that day; he captured the city and killed the people who were in it; he razed the city and sowed it with salt.

[46] When all the lords of the tower of Shechem heard of it, they entered the stronghold of the house of *Elohim*-berith.

[47] It was told Abimelech that all the lords of the tower of Shechem had gathered together.

[48] Abimelech went up to Mount Zalmon, he and all the people with him; he took an axe in his hand, cut down a branch from the trees, lifted it, laid it on his shoulder, and said to the people with him, "What you have seen me do, hurry and do as I have done."

[49] So every one of the people likewise cut down his branch and followed Abimelech; they laid them against the stronghold and set it on fire with those inside; so all the people of the tower of Shechem also died — about a thousand men and women.

[50] Then Abimelech went to Thebez, encamped against Thebez, and captured it.

[51] But there was a strong tower within the city, and all the men and women, and all the lords of the city fled there, shut themselves in, and went up to the roof of the tower.

[52] Abimelech came to the tower, fought against it, and approached the door of the tower to burn it with fire.

[53] A certain woman threw an upper millstone on Abimelech's head and crushed his skull.

[54] He called hurriedly to the young man who carried his armor and said to him, "Draw your sword and kill me, so that no one may say of me, 'A woman killed him.'" So his young man thrust him through, and he died.

[55] When the men of Israel saw that Abimelech was dead, each one departed to his home.

[56] Thus *Elohim* repaid the evil of Abimelech, which he had done to his father, by killing his seventy brothers.

[57] Also *Elohim* repaid on their heads all the evil of the men of Shechem; and upon them came the curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"men of Shechem" → "lords of Shechem"** (throughout) — Hebrew בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם *ba'alei Shechem*. "Lords / citizens" — city rulers, not commoners.
- **"communed with" → "spoke to"** (v1).
- **"vain and light persons" → "worthless and reckless men"** (v4) — Hebrew רֵיקִים וּפֹחֲזִים *reiqim u-fochazim*.
- **"plain of the pillar" → "oak of the pillar"** (v6) — Hebrew אֵלוֹן מֻצָּב *elon mutzav*. Sacred tree and pillar at Shechem.
- **"adventured his life" → "risked his life"** (v17).
- **"Meonenim" → "Diviners' Oak"** (v37) — Hebrew מְעוֹנְנִים *me'onenim* = "diviners."
- **"hold" → "stronghold"** (v46, 49) — Hebrew צְרִיחַ *tzeriach*.
- **"all to brake" → "crushed"** (v53) — archaic "all to" = completely.

### Notes
- **Abimelech's coup** — son of a Shechemite concubine (8:31), he kills his 70 half-brothers on one stone (the fulfillment of "sons of the maidservant" usurping legitimate inheritance).
- **Jotham's parable** (v7–15) — the "trees choosing a king." Useful fruit-trees refuse; only the worthless bramble accepts. Classic anti-monarchy fable: fit leaders reject power; only worthless seekers-of-office accept.
- **Fire from the bramble** (v15, 20) — prophetic curse: Abimelech will burn the Shechemites, and the Shechemites will destroy Abimelech. Both come true.
- **"Sent an evil spirit"** (v23) — divine-send to cause dissolution. Compare 1 Sam 16:14 (Saul's evil spirit).
- **Salt on the razed city** (v45) — symbolic de-consecration: the city will not be rebuilt. Compare Deut 29:23.
- **Millstone from a woman** (v53) — ironic reversal of Abimelech's pride. A woman kills him; he begs to be finished off to hide the shame. Years later 2 Sam 11:21 remembers him.
- **Jotham's curse fulfilled** (v56–57) — the narrative explicitly notes: *Elohim* worked through the events to repay both Abimelech and Shechem.
