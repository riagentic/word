# Judges 17

[1] There was a man of the hill country of ⟦ephraim|Efrayim|Ephraim⟧, whose name was ⟦micah|Michah|Micah⟧.

[13] Then ⟦micah|Michah|Micah⟧ said, "Now I know that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will do me good, because I have a ⟦levites|Levi|Levite⟧ as my priest."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"shekels" → "pieces"** (v2, 3, 4, 10) — Hebrew *keseph* (silver). The unit is implied.
- **"graven / molten image" → "carved / cast image"** (v3, 4).
- **"founder" → "silversmith"** (v4) — Hebrew צוֹרֵף *tzoreph*. Metal-worker.
- **"house of gods" → "shrine"** (v5) — Hebrew בֵּית אֱלֹהִים *beit elohim*. Household shrine.
- **"teraphim" → "household gods"** (v5) — Hebrew תְּרָפִים *teraphim*. Domestic-cult figurines (cf. Gen 31 Rachel's teraphim).
- **"victuals" → "food"** (v10).

### Notes
- **Epilogue narrative begins** — Judges 17–21 forms a two-story appendix: the story of Micah's shrine and the Danite migration (17–18); and the Benjamite civil war (19–21). Both illustrate the religious and moral collapse of pre-monarchical Israel.
- **"No king in Israel / everyone did right in his own eyes"** (v6) — key refrain of the appendix (also 18:1, 19:1, 21:25). Anticipates the monarchy.
- **Micah's silver** — 1,100 shekels. Possibly echoing Delilah's bribe (16:5). Stolen, cursed, returned, consecrated to idolatry — entire comic-tragic sequence.
- **"Consecrated to Yahweh to make an image"** — contradictory. The mother sees no conflict between Yahweh-worship and image-making. Characteristic of pre-monarchical religion.
- **Wandering Levite** — unemployed Levites in the pre-temple era; Micah hires one for household priesthood. Later tradition identifies him as a descendant of Moses (18:30), which would make the idolatry particularly scandalous.
- **"A Levite as my priest"** (v13) — syncretistic presumption: idols + Levite = divine favor. The whole episode satirizes Israel's religious confusion.
