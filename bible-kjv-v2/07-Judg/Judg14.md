# Judges 14

[1] And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ went down to Timnah, and he saw a woman in Timnah, of the daughters of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧.

[2] And he went up and told his father and his mother, and he said, "A woman I have seen in Timnah, of the daughters of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧; and now, take her for me as a wife."

[3] And his father and his mother said to him, "Is there not among the daughters of your brothers and among all my people a woman, that you are going to take a wife from the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧, the uncircumcised?" And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ said to his father, "Her take for me, for she is right in my eyes."

[4] And his father and his mother did not know that it was from ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, for he was seeking an occasion from the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧. And at that time the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ were ruling over ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[5] And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ went down, and his father and his mother, to Timnah, and they came as far as the vineyards of Timnah; and behold, a young lion of the lions, roaring to meet him.

[6] And the spirit of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ rushed upon him, and he tore it open as one tears open a kid, and there was not anything in his hand; and he did not tell his father and his mother what he had done.

[7] And he went down and spoke to the woman, and she was right in the eyes of ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧.

[8] And he returned after some days to take her, and he turned aside to see the fallen body of the lion; and behold, a swarm of bees in the body of the lion, and honey.

[9] And he scraped it out into his palms and went on, going and eating; and he went to his father and to his mother and gave to them, and they ate; and he did not tell them that from the body of the lion he had scraped out the honey.

[10] And his father went down to the woman, and ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ made a feast there, for so the young men used to do.

[11] And it came to pass, when they saw him, that they took thirty friends, and they were with him.

[12] And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ said to them, "Let me riddle you a riddle, please. If you surely tell it to me in the seven days of the feast and find it out, then I will give you thirty linen wraps and thirty changes of garments.

[13] And if you are not able to tell me, then you, you shall give me thirty linen wraps and thirty changes of garments." And they said to him, "Riddle your riddle, and let us hear it."

[14] And he said to them:

"From the eater came out something to eat,
and from the strong came out something sweet."

And they were not able to tell the riddle for three days.

[15] And it came to pass on the seventh day that they said to the wife of ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧, "Entice your husband, and let him tell us the riddle, lest we burn you and the house of your father with fire. Was it to dispossess us that you called us? Was it not?"

[16] And the wife of ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ wept upon him and said, "You only hate me, and you do not love me. The riddle you have riddled to the sons of my people, and to me you have not told it." And he said to her, "Behold, to my father and to my mother I have not told it, and to you shall I tell it?"

[17] And she wept upon him the seven days that they had the feast; and it came to pass on the seventh day that he told her, for she had straitened him; and she told the riddle to the sons of her people.

[18] And the men of the city said to him on the seventh day, before the sun went in:

"What is sweeter than honey,
and what is stronger than a lion?"

And he said to them:

"If you had not plowed with my heifer,
you would not have found out my riddle."

[19] And the spirit of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ rushed upon him, and he went down to ⟦ashkelon|Ashqelon|Ashkelon⟧ and struck down thirty men of them, and he took what was stripped off them and gave the changes to those who had told the riddle. And his anger burned, and he went up to the house of his father.

[20] And the wife of ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ became his friend's, who had been a friend to him.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton kept.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"he came up, and told" → "he went up and told"** (v2) — Hebrew וַיַּעַל; the chapter keeps "go down" to Timnah and Ashkelon (v1, 5, 7, 10, 19) and "go up" home (v2, 19). **"I have seen a woman" → "A woman I have seen"** — the object stands first. **"get her for me to wife" → "take her for me as a wife"** (v2, 3, 8) — Hebrew לָקַח "take".
- **"among the daughters of thy brethren, or among all my people" → "among the daughters of your brothers and among all my people"** (v3) — Hebrew אַחֶיךָ "your brothers" (kinsmen); עַמִּי "my people", though father and mother both speak. **"of the uncircumcised Philistines" → "from the Philistines, the uncircumcised"** — the adjective follows with its own article.
- **"Get her for me; for she pleaseth me well" → "Her take for me, for she is right in my eyes"** (v3, 7) — Hebrew אוֹתָהּ קַח־לִי, with "her" first; כִּי־הִיא יָשְׁרָה בְעֵינָי, the verb of יָשָׁר "upright, straight": she is the right one in his eyes. The book's last words use the same phrase: "each man did what was right in his eyes" (17:6; 21:25).
- **"that it was of the LORD, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines" → "that it was from Yahweh, for he was seeking an occasion from the Philistines"** (v4) — Hebrew כִּי מֵיְהוָה הִיא כִּי־תֹאֲנָה הוּא־מְבַקֵּשׁ מִפְּלִשְׁתִּים, word for word. "It" is feminine (the matter). "He" is not named: Yahweh, who has just been named, or Samson. תֹּאֲנָה is found only here: an occasion, an opening (for a quarrel); מִן is "from".
- **"had dominion over" → "were ruling over"** (v4) — Hebrew participle מֹשְׁלִים.
- **"a young lion roared against him" → "a young lion of the lions, roaring to meet him"** (v5) — Hebrew כְּפִיר אֲרָיוֹת "a young lion of lions" (as "a kid of the goats"); שֹׁאֵג לִקְרָאתוֹ, a participle, "roaring to meet him". The verse says "they came" and then "to meet him".
- **"the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him" → "the spirit of Yahweh rushed upon him"** (v6, 19) — Hebrew וַתִּצְלַח עָלָיו רוּחַ יְהוָה. The verb צָלַח with עַל is "rush upon, break in upon" (15:14; 1 Sam 10:6, 10; 16:13); elsewhere the same verb is "prosper". The book has other verbs of the spirit in 3:10; 11:29 ("was upon"), 6:34 ("clothed") and 13:25 ("began to stir"). רוּחַ is "spirit", "wind" and "breath".
- **"rent him as he would have rent a kid" → "tore it open as one tears open a kid"** (v6) — Hebrew וַיְשַׁסְּעֵהוּ כְּשַׁסַּע הַגְּדִי, literally "as the tearing open of the kid". **"he had nothing in his hand" → "there was not anything in his hand"** — Hebrew וּמְאוּמָה אֵין בְּיָדוֹ.
- **"talked with the woman" → "spoke to the woman"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיְדַבֵּר לָאִשָּׁה.
- **"after a time" → "after some days"** (v8) — Hebrew מִיָּמִים, literally "from days", as 11:4.
- **"the carcase of the lion" → "the fallen body of the lion" / "the body of the lion"** (v8, 9) — two words: מַפֶּלֶת, "what has fallen" (from נָפַל "fall"), and גְּוִיָּה "body". The usual word for a carcass (נְבֵלָה) is not used.
- **"a swarm of bees" kept** (v8) — Hebrew עֲדַת דְּבוֹרִים; עֵדָה is the word for the "congregation" of Israel: a gathered company.
- **"he took thereof in his hands, and went on eating" → "he scraped it out into his palms and went on, going and eating"** (v9) — Hebrew וַיִּרְדֵּהוּ אֶל־כַּפָּיו: רָדָה, used of taking honey only here (another verb than רָדָה "have dominion"); כַּף "palm"; וַיֵּלֶךְ הָלוֹךְ וְאָכֹל "and he went, going and eating".
- **"for so used the young men to do" kept** (v10) — Hebrew יַעֲשׂוּ, a form of what is usually done; בַּחוּרִים "young men" (chosen ones, men in their prime; נַעַר is also "young man"). מִשְׁתֶּה "feast" is from שָׁתָה "drink".
- **"they brought thirty companions to be with him" → "they took thirty friends, and they were with him"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיִּקְחוּ … וַיִּהְיוּ אִתּוֹ; מֵרֵעַ "friend, companion", the word of v20 and 15:2, 6.
- **"I will now put forth a riddle unto you" → "Let me riddle you a riddle, please"** (v12, 13, 16) — Hebrew אָחוּדָה־נָּא לָכֶם חִידָה: the verb and the noun are one root (חוּד חִידָה), and English can keep it.
- **"if ye can certainly declare it me … and find it out" → "If you surely tell it to me … and find it out"** (v12) — Hebrew הַגֵּד תַּגִּידוּ, the infinitive absolute with its verb ("telling you tell"); הִגִּיד "tell" throughout the chapter (v2, 6, 9, 12–17, 19); מָצָא "find" (v12, 18).
- **"thirty sheets and thirty change of garments" → "thirty linen wraps and thirty changes of garments"** (v12, 13) — Hebrew סְדִינִים, a piece of fine linen worn as a wrap (Isa 3:23; Prov 31:24); חֲלִפֹת בְּגָדִים "changes of garments".
- **"Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness" → "From the eater came out something to eat, / and from the strong came out something sweet"** (v14) — Hebrew מֵהָאֹכֵל יָצָא מַאֲכָל / וּמֵעַז יָצָא מָתוֹק: two lines of three words each. "Eater" and "something to eat" are one root (אֹכֵל … מַאֲכָל), and each line has the same sounds at its head and its end (*me-ha-okhel … ma'akhal*; *me-az … matoq*). עַז is "strong", also "fierce"; מָתוֹק is the adjective "sweet".
- **"they could not in three days expound" → "they were not able to tell the riddle for three days"** (v14) — Hebrew לְהַגִּיד "tell".
- **"on the seventh day" kept** (v15) — the Masoretic Text has בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי "on the seventh day". The Septuagint and the Syriac have "on the fourth day" (cited from memory), which fits the "three days" of v14 more easily; the Masoretic Text is kept, and v17 says she wept "the seven days".
- **"have ye called us to take that we have? is it not so?" → "Was it to dispossess us that you called us? Was it not?"** (v15) — Hebrew הַלְיָרְשֵׁנוּ קְרָאתֶם לָנוּ הֲלֹא: יָרַשׁ with a person as object, "take over what is his, dispossess"; the "you" is plural. The question ends with הֲלֹא "is it not?"; some Hebrew manuscripts read הֲלֹם "here" (cited from memory).
- **"wept before him" → "wept upon him"** (v16, 17) — Hebrew וַתֵּבְךְּ עָלָיו: "upon him", on his neck, or "over / at him".
- **"Thou dost but hate me" → "You only hate me"** (v16) — Hebrew רַק־שְׂנֵאתַנִי וְלֹא אֲהַבְתָּנִי, perfects of verbs of feeling. **"the children of my people" → "the sons of my people"** (v16, 17).
- **"because she lay sore upon him" → "for she had straitened him"** (v17) — Hebrew הֱצִיקַתְהוּ, the hifil of צוּק "press, bring into straits", as 16:16.
- **"before the sun went down" → "before the sun went in"** (v18) — Hebrew בְּטֶרֶם יָבֹא הַחַרְסָה; חֶרֶס is a rare word for the sun (Job 9:7), and "go in" is the Hebrew for its setting.
- **"What is sweeter than honey? and what is stronger than a lion?" (v18)** — Hebrew מַה־מָּתוֹק מִדְּבַשׁ / וּמֶה עַז מֵאֲרִי: two lines that take up "sweet" and "strong" from the riddle; "and" joins them. The answer has the form of a question.
- **"If ye had not plowed with my heifer, ye had not found out my riddle" kept** (v18) — Hebrew לוּלֵא חֲרַשְׁתֶּם בְּעֶגְלָתִי / לֹא מְצָאתֶם חִידָתִי: two lines that end on the same sound (*be-eglati … chidati*). "My heifer" is his wife.
- **"slew thirty men of them, and took their spoil, and gave change of garments unto them which expounded the riddle" → "struck down thirty men of them, and he took what was stripped off them and gave the changes to those who had told the riddle"** (v19) — Hebrew חֲלִיצוֹתָם, what is stripped from a slain man (2 Sam 2:21), from חָלַץ "pull off"; הַחֲלִיפוֹת "the changes" (v12, 13): the two words sound alike.
- **"his anger was kindled" → "his anger burned"** (v19) — Hebrew וַיִּחַר אַפּוֹ; אַף is "nose", and so "anger".
- **"But Samson's wife was given to his companion, whom he had used as his friend" → "And the wife of Samson became his friend's, who had been a friend to him"** (v20) — Hebrew וַתְּהִי אֵשֶׁת שִׁמְשׁוֹן לְמֵרֵעֵהוּ אֲשֶׁר רֵעָה לוֹ, word for word: "became … to his friend" is the way of saying that a woman becomes a man's wife; "was given" is not written (15:2, 6 say who gave her). רֵעָה is a verb made from the noun: "who had been friend to him". Which of the thirty he was, and what his office was at the feast, is not said.

### Notes
- **A wife from the Philistines** — against the word of his father and mother (v3). V4 says of it "it was from Yahweh" and gives the reason in words that leave "he" unnamed.
- **The lion and the honey** — the riddle rests on what only Samson has seen (v6, 9: "he did not tell").
- **The riddle told** — the wife's weeping brings out what he had told no one; so again in 16:16–17.
- **Ashkelon** (v19) — one of the five Philistine cities, on the coast; the thirty garments for the wager are taken from thirty men struck down there.
- **The wife** (v20) — she becomes another man's; ch 15 follows from it.
- **The *nazir*** — the chapter does not use the word. It tells of vineyards (v5), of the body of a dead lion (v8–9) and of a feast (v10) without comment.
