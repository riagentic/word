## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"harlot" → "prostitute"** (v1).
- **"strange woman" → "another woman"** (v2) — Hebrew *achereth*. Different woman.
- **"vain men" → "worthless men"** (v3) — Hebrew *reiqim*. Desperate / landless men; compare David's band 1 Sam 22:2.
- **"captain" → "commander"** (v6, 11).
- **"Spirit of the Lord" → "spirit of Yahweh"** (v29).
- **"Red sea" → "Sea of Reeds"** (v16).
- **"timbrels" → "tambourines"** (v34) — Hebrew תֻּפִּים *tuppim*. Hand drums.
- **"lament" → "commemorate"** (v40) — Hebrew לְתַנּוֹת *letannot*. Debated: lament / recount / celebrate. Likely a combination ritual.

### Notes
- **Jephthah's troubled background** (v1–3) — illegitimate son, driven out, outlaw leader. He is not the ideal judge; he is what the society's brokenness produces.
- **Diplomatic argument** (v12–27) — Jephthah's history-theology is extensive:
  - Israel avoided Moab/Ammon (didn't take their land)
  - The disputed territory was taken from Sihon, not Moab/Ammon
  - "Three hundred years" — first explicit chronology
  - "Chemosh your god / Yahweh our *Elohim*" — acknowledging that Ammon's god gave them their territory; Yahweh gave Israel theirs. Henotheistic framework.
- **Jephthah's vow** (v30–31) — tragic narrative. The Hebrew *asher* can be read "whoever" or "whatever" — the ambiguity likely intentional. "What comes out first" suggests an animal, but "from the door" suggests human.
- **The daughter's fate** (v34–40) — debated:
  - (1) Literal human sacrifice — the text's plain reading.
  - (2) Perpetual virginity consecration (the text emphasizes "she had not known a man" v39 — rabbinic tradition interpreted so).
  - Either way: tragic. A father's rash vow consumes the innocent daughter.
- **The yearly commemoration** (v39–40) — four days annually, the daughters of Israel mourn / celebrate Jephthah's daughter. One of the rare women-led rituals in Israel.
- **Heb 11:32** lists Jephthah among heroes of faith — perhaps for his deliverance, not his vow.
