## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew *Elohim*. In v8 the same word is "gods".
- **The song** (v2–31) — very old Hebrew poetry, with many words found only here and several lines whose meaning is not certain. The Masoretic Text is translated as it is pointed and divided; each line of the translation is one line (colon) of the Hebrew, by the accents; where the sense is doubtful the best-supported rendering stands in the verse and the doubt is named below. No reading of the old versions and no conjecture is in the verses.
- **"Then sang Deborah and Barak" → "And Deborah sang, and Barak"** (v1) — Hebrew וַתָּשַׁר, a feminine singular: Deborah sang, and Barak with her.
- **"Praise ye the LORD for the avenging of Israel" → "When loose locks were let loose in Israel … bless Yahweh"** (v2) — Hebrew בִּפְרֹעַ פְּרָעוֹת בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, literally "in the letting loose of loose things in Israel". The verb פָּרַע is "let loose" (of the hair of the head, Num 5:18; of a people, Exod 32:25); the noun פְּרָעוֹת stands again only in Deut 32:42 ("the loose locks of the enemy"). The best-supported sense is the unbound hair of men vowed to war. Others: "when leaders took the lead" (so the Greek, cited from memory) or "for the avenging". Uncertain. בָּרֲכוּ is "bless".
- **"when the people willingly offered themselves"** (v2, 9) — Hebrew הִתְנַדֵּב, to offer oneself freely.
- **"O ye kings … O ye princes" → "kings … rulers"** (v3) — Hebrew רֹזְנִים, a poetic word for men of weight, rulers. "I" stands twice in the Hebrew (אָנֹכִי לַיהוָה אָנֹכִי אָשִׁירָה): "I, to Yahweh, I, I will sing". **"I will sing praise" → "I will make music"** — Hebrew אֲזַמֵּר.
- **"the earth trembled, and the heavens dropped, the clouds also dropped water" → "the earth quaked, the heavens also dropped, the thick clouds also dropped water"** (v4) — Hebrew רָעָשָׁה "quaked"; גַּם "also" stands twice; עָבִים "thick clouds". **"out of the field of Edom" → "from the field of Edom"** — שְׂדֵה. "Marched" is צָעַד, "step, stride".
- **"The mountains melted from before the LORD, even that Sinai" → "Mountains flowed from the face of Yahweh, this Sinai"** (v5) — Hebrew נָזְלוּ, pointed as a form of נָזַל "flow"; the same consonants can be read as "quaked" (so the Greek, cited from memory). זֶה סִינַי is "this Sinai", or an old title, "the One of Sinai" (said of Yahweh). מִפְּנֵי is "from the face of".
- **"the highways were unoccupied, and the travellers walked through byways" → "the paths ceased, and those who walked the tracks walked by winding paths"** (v6) — Hebrew חָדְלוּ אֳרָחוֹת, "paths ceased"; the same consonants can be read "caravans ceased". נְתִיבוֹת "tracks", אֳרָחוֹת עֲקַלְקַלּוֹת "crooked, winding paths".
- **"The inhabitants of the villages ceased, they ceased in Israel" → "The villagers ceased in Israel, they ceased"** (v7, 11) — Hebrew פְּרָזוֹן, found only in these two verses. It is commonly connected with פְּרָזוֹת "open, unwalled villages" (Ezek 38:11) and פְּרָזִי "dweller in the open country": the people of the open land. Others: "warriors", "leaders". Uncertain; one rendering in both verses.
- **"until that I Deborah arose, that I arose a mother in Israel" → "until I arose, Deborah, I arose, a mother in Israel"** (v7) — Hebrew שַׁקַּמְתִּי, as pointed a first person, "I arose" (with the old relative שַׁ). The same form can be an old second person feminine, "you arose".
- **"They chose new gods; then was war in the gates" → "He chose new gods; then there was war at the gates"** (v8) — Hebrew יִבְחַר אֱלֹהִים חֲדָשִׁים, a singular verb with no subject named: "he" (Israel) "chose new gods" (cf. Deut 32:17); it can also be read "Elohim chose new ones". אָז לָחֶם שְׁעָרִים is literally "then war of gates"; לָחֶם as pointed is found only here and is taken from the root of "fight" (לָחַם); with other vowels the letters are "bread". Very uncertain. **"was there a shield or spear seen" → "Was a shield seen, or a spear"** — Hebrew מָגֵן אִם־יֵרָאֶה וָרֹמַח, literally "a shield, if it was seen, and a spear": a question that expects "no".
- **"the governors of Israel" → "the commanders of Israel"** (v9, 14) — Hebrew חוֹקְקֵי, מְחֹקְקִים, from חָקַק "inscribe, decree": those who give orders.
- **"Speak, ye that ride on white asses, ye that sit in judgment, and walk by the way" → "You who ride on tawny she-donkeys, you who sit on cloths, and you who walk on the way: speak!"** (v10) — Hebrew אֲתֹנוֹת "she-donkeys"; צְחֹרוֹת, found only here: a light colour, "tawny" or "white"; uncertain. מִדִּין is taken as a plural of מַד "garment, cloth" (3:16): saddle cloths or carpets; "in judgment" reads it from another root (דִּין). Uncertain. שִׂיחוּ is "speak, muse on it"; the verb stands last in the Hebrew.
- **"They that are delivered from the noise of archers in the places of drawing water" → "From the voice of those who divide, between the drawing places"** (v11) — Hebrew מִקּוֹל מְחַצְצִים בֵּין מַשְׁאַבִּים. מְחַצְצִים is found only here and its meaning is not known: from חָצַץ "divide" (those who share out water, flocks or spoil), or from חֵץ "arrow" ("archers"), or "singers, players". "They that are delivered" is not in the Hebrew. מַשְׁאַבִּים are places where water is drawn. מִן is "from" (or "louder than"). Very uncertain.
- **"there shall they rehearse the righteous acts of the LORD" → "there they recount the righteous acts of Yahweh"** (v11) — Hebrew יְתַנּוּ, "recount, repeat" (11:40); צִדְקוֹת, the plural of "righteousness": his righteous deeds. "Of his villagers": פִּרְזֹנוֹ, as v7.
- **"utter a song" → "speak a song"** (v12) — Hebrew דַּבְּרִי־שִׁיר; the sound plays on the name Deborah. **"lead thy captivity captive" → "take captive your captives"** — Hebrew וּשֲׁבֵה שֶׁבְיְךָ.
- **"Then he made him that remaineth have dominion over the nobles among the people: the LORD made me have dominion over the mighty" → "Then he gave a survivor dominion over the nobles, the people; Yahweh gave me dominion among the mighty ones"** (v13) — Hebrew אָז יְרַד שָׂרִיד לְאַדִּירִים עָם יְהוָה יְרַד־לִי בַּגִּבּוֹרִים. The verb is pointed יְרַד both times, a form taken from רָדָה "have dominion" (so the old Jewish reading and the KJV); the accents end the first line with "people" and begin the second with "Yahweh". With other vowels (יָרַד) and another division the same letters say "Then a remnant went down to the nobles; the people of Yahweh went down for me among the mighty" — "went down" is the verb of v11 and v14, and most recent translations read so. The Masoretic pointing and division are translated; the verse is one of the most uncertain of the song. "The people" stands loosely beside "the nobles" in the Hebrew. אַדִּירִים "nobles" (the majestic, mighty: v25) and גִּבּוֹרִים "mighty ones" are two words.
- **"Out of Ephraim was there a root of them against Amalek" → "From Ephraim, their root is in Amalek"** (v14) — Hebrew מִנִּי אֶפְרַיִם שָׁרְשָׁם בַּעֲמָלֵק, word for word; no verb. What is meant is not certain (cf. 12:15, "the hill country of the Amalekite" in Ephraim). **"after thee, Benjamin, among thy people" → "after you, Benjamin, among your peoples"** — Hebrew בַּעֲמָמֶיךָ, a plural.
- **"they that handle the pen of the writer" → "those who draw with the rod of the scribe"** (v14) — Hebrew מֹשְׁכִים בְּשֵׁבֶט סֹפֵר: מָשַׁךְ "draw" (4:6, 7); שֵׁבֶט "rod, staff"; סֹפֵר "scribe", one who writes or counts: the officer who musters the men. "Pen" is not the Hebrew word.
- **"the princes of Issachar were with Deborah; even Issachar, and also Barak: he was sent on foot into the valley" → "my princes in Issachar were with Deborah, and Issachar, so Barak; into the valley he was sent at his feet"** (v15) — Hebrew וְשָׂרַי, pointed "my princes"; with other vowels "the princes of". וְיִשָּׂשכָר כֵּן בָּרָק is literally "and Issachar, so Barak". שֻׁלַּח בְּרַגְלָיו is "was sent (let loose) at his feet": behind him, as 4:10.
- **"For the divisions of Reuben there were great thoughts of heart" → "In the divisions of Reuben great were the resolves of heart"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew פְּלַגּוֹת, "divisions" (of the tribe) or "streams". The two verses differ by one letter: חִקְקֵי־לֵב "resolves (decrees) of heart" in v15, חִקְרֵי־לֵב "searchings of heart" in v16; and "in" (בְּ) in v15, "at / for" (לְ) in v16. Both are kept as they stand.
- **"Why abodest thou among the sheepfolds, to hear the bleatings of the flocks?" → "Why did you sit between the sheepfolds, to hear the pipings of the droves?"** (v16) — Hebrew הַמִּשְׁפְּתַיִם, a dual, only here and in Gen 49:14; its meaning is not certain ("sheepfolds", "saddlebags", "hearths"). שְׁרִקוֹת are whistlings or pipings (to or for the flocks).
- **"Gilead abode beyond Jordan: and why did Dan remain in ships? Asher continued on the sea shore, and abode in his breaches" → "Gilead abode across the Jordan; and Dan, why does he sojourn in ships? Asher sat at the shore of the seas, and by his inlets he abides"** (v17) — three verbs: שָׁכַן "abide", גּוּר "sojourn", יָשַׁב "sit, dwell". חוֹף יַמִּים as Gen 49:13. מִפְרָצָיו is found only here: from פָּרַץ "breach", the breaks in the coast, inlets or landing places. Uncertain.
- **"a people that jeoparded their lives unto the death" → "a people that scorned its soul, to die"** (v18) — Hebrew חֵרֵף נַפְשׁוֹ לָמוּת: חֵרֵף is "reproach, scorn"; נֶפֶשׁ "soul". **"in the high places of the field" → "on the heights of the field"** — מְרוֹמֵי שָׂדֶה.
- **"they took no gain of money" → "gain of silver they did not take"** (v19) — Hebrew בֶּצַע כֶּסֶף: בֶּצַע is gain cut off for oneself, plunder.
- **"They fought from heaven; the stars in their courses fought against Sisera" → "From the heavens they fought; the stars from their highways fought with Sisera"** (v20) — Hebrew מְסִלּוֹת, "raised roads"; עִם "with" (against). The accents divide after "they fought".
- **"The river of Kishon swept them away, that ancient river" → "The wadi Kishon swept them away, the ancient wadi"** (v21) — Hebrew נַחַל, as 4:7, 13: here the stream in flood. גְּרָפָם "swept them away" is found only here. קְדוּמִים is found only here: from קֶדֶם, "of ancient days" (or "that goes to meet, onrushing"). Uncertain. **"O my soul, thou hast trodden down strength" → "You shall tread, my soul, with strength"** — Hebrew תִּדְרְכִי נַפְשִׁי עֹז, word for word "you tread, my soul, strength": "tread on with strength", or "tread down the strong". Uncertain.
- **"Then were the horsehoofs broken by the means of the pransings, the pransings of their mighty ones" → "Then the heels of the horse hammered, from the gallopings, the gallopings of his mighty ones"** (v22) — Hebrew הָלְמוּ "hammered, struck", the verb of v26; עִקְּבֵי־סוּס "the heels of the horse"; דַּהֲרוֹת, only here: a rushing gallop (cf. Nah 3:2); אַבִּירָיו "his mighty ones": his stallions. "His" is singular.
- **"Curse ye Meroz, said the angel of the LORD, curse ye bitterly" → "'Curse Meroz,' said the angel of Yahweh, 'surely curse'"** (v23) — Hebrew אֹרוּ אָרוֹר, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "curse, cursing". מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה: the word means "messenger". Meroz is not known. **"against the mighty" → "among the mighty ones"** — Hebrew בַּגִּבּוֹרִים, as v13: "among", "with" or "against".
- **"Blessed above women shall Jael … be" → "Blessed above women be Jael"** (v24) — Hebrew תְּבֹרַךְ מִנָּשִׁים, a wish; מִן is "more than". "In the tent": women who live in tents.
- **"she brought forth butter in a lordly dish" → "in a bowl of nobles she brought near curds"** (v25) — Hebrew סֵפֶל אַדִּירִים, "a bowl of nobles" (the word of v13); חֶמְאָה "curds"; הִקְרִיבָה "brought near".
- **"She put her hand to the nail, and her right hand to the workmen's hammer; and with the hammer she smote Sisera, she smote off his head, when she had pierced and stricken through his temples" → "Her hand she stretched out to the peg, and her right hand to the hammer of the toilers; and she hammered Sisera, she crushed his head, and she struck through and pierced his temple"** (v26) — Hebrew תִּשְׁלַחְנָה, an unusual form of "stretch out"; הַלְמוּת עֲמֵלִים "the hammer of toilers" (הַלְמוּת only here); וְהָלְמָה "hammered"; מָחֲקָה, only here, "crushed" (uncertain); מָחֲצָה "struck through"; חָלְפָה "passed through, pierced". Four verbs of the blow, each kept.
- **"At her feet he bowed, he fell, he lay down … there he fell down dead" → "Between her feet he stooped, he fell, he lay … there he fell, destroyed"** (v27) — Hebrew בֵּין רַגְלֶיהָ, "between her feet"; כָּרַע "bend the knee, stoop"; שָׁדוּד "devastated, destroyed", stronger than "dead".
- **"The mother of Sisera looked out at a window, and cried through the lattice" → "Through the window she looked down and wailed, the mother of Sisera, through the lattice"** (v28) — the Hebrew order. וַתְּיַבֵּב is found only here: a shrill cry. **"Why is his chariot so long in coming? why tarry the wheels of his chariots?" → "Why is his chariot long in coming? Why do the beats of his chariots delay?"** — Hebrew בֹּשֵׁשׁ, as Exod 32:1; פַּעֲמֵי, "steps, beats" (hoofbeats).
- **"Her wise ladies answered her, yea, she returned answer to herself" → "The wise ones of her princesses answer her; she also returns her sayings to herself"** (v29) — Hebrew חַכְמוֹת שָׂרוֹתֶיהָ; שָׂרָה is the feminine of "prince".
- **"Have they not sped? have they not divided the prey; to every man a damsel or two" → "Are they not finding, dividing the spoil? A womb, two wombs, for the head of a mighty man"** (v30) — Hebrew רַחַם רַחֲמָתַיִם, "a womb, two wombs": a coarse word for captive women, in the mouth of Sisera's mother; it is not softened. לְרֹאשׁ גֶּבֶר is "for the head of a man" (גֶּבֶר, a man in his strength).
- **"a prey of divers colours, a prey of divers colours of needlework, of divers colours of needlework on both sides, meet for the necks of them that take the spoil" → "spoil of dyed stuffs for Sisera, spoil of dyed stuffs, embroidery, a dyed stuff, two embroideries, for the necks of the spoil"** (v30) — Hebrew צְבָעִים, dyed cloths (צֶבַע only here); רִקְמָה "embroidery", רִקְמָתַיִם its dual, "two embroideries" (or "embroidered on both sides"). The last words, לְצַוְּארֵי שָׁלָל, are word for word "for the necks of spoil": what it means is not certain ("for the necks of the spoilers"; "for the neck of the queen" and "for my neck" are corrections of the text). The Hebrew is kept as it stands.
- **"So let all thine enemies perish, O LORD: but let them that love him be as the sun when he goeth forth in his might" → "So may all your enemies perish, Yahweh; and those who love him be as the going out of the sun in its might"** (v31) — the Hebrew turns from "your" to "him" within the verse; it is kept. **"the land had rest" → "the land was quiet"** — Hebrew שָׁקַט, the book's standing sentence (3:11, 30); it is prose and stands outside the song.

### Notes
- **One of the oldest texts in the Bible** — the "Song of Deborah" is commonly dated early (12th–11th century BC) on the ground of its archaic Hebrew.
- **Triumph and taunt** — the song celebrates: (1) Yahweh the warrior (v4–5, 20–21); (2) the tribes that came (Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir, Zebulun, Issachar, Naphtali); (3) it questions those that did not (Reuben, Gilead, Dan, Asher) and curses Meroz; (4) Jael's deed; (5) Sisera's mother waiting.
- **Tribal roll call** (v14–18) — who came to battle, who stayed home. Judah, Simeon and Levi are not named.
- **"The stars fought"** (v20) — the heavens and the wadi Kishon together against the chariots of Sisera.
- **Sisera's mother** (v28–30) — the women at home expect spoil and captive women. The hearers know what has happened in the tent.
- **Forty years of quiet** (v31) — the same as after Othniel (3:11).
