## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Praise ye the Lord for the avenging" → "When leaders lead ... bless Yahweh"** (v2) — Hebrew בִּפְרֹעַ פְּרָעוֹת *bi-fro'a pera'ot*. Obscure; translations vary: "when leaders lead" / "when long hair is loose" (as warriors) / "when avengers arise." Reflects the song's archaic poetic Hebrew.
- **"asses" → "donkeys"** (v10).
- **"pransings" → "galloping"** (v22) — modernized.
- **"lordly dish" → "lordly bowl"** (v25).
- **"damsel" → "womb"** (v30) — Hebrew רַחַם *racham*. Literally "womb" used here crudely for captive-woman; the dehumanizing objectification from Sisera's mother's perspective.
- **"divers colours" → "dyed garments"** (v30).
- **"breaches" → "inlets"** (v17) — archaic.
- **"angel of the Lord" → "Messenger of Yahweh"** (v23).
- **"Meroz" (v23)** — unknown location; cursed for non-participation.

### Notes
- **One of the oldest texts in the Bible** — the "Song of Deborah" is linguistically dated to the 12th–11th century BC, preserving archaic Hebrew. Earlier than most of the Torah's narrative prose.
- **Triumph and taunt** — the song celebrates: (1) Yahweh the warrior (v4–5, 20–21); (2) participating tribes (Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir, Zebulun, Issachar, Naphtali); (3) rebuking non-participating tribes (Reuben, Gilead, Dan, Asher, Meroz); (4) Jael's kill; (5) Sisera's mother in pathetic anticipation.
- **Tribal roll call** (v14–18) — who came to battle, who stayed home. A snapshot of Israel's political fractiousness.
- **"Stars fought"** (v20) — the heavens + Kishon flood = cosmic / environmental intervention. Yahweh controls weather and cosmos; chariots useless.
- **Sisera's mother scene** (v28–30) — poetic irony: the women at home expect spoils and captive-slaves. The song's listeners know the dark truth.
- **Forty years rest** (v31) — same as Othniel. The pattern: 40-40-80-40-40... progressively shorter cycles in subsequent judges.
