# Judges 19

[1] And it came to pass in those days, when there was no king in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, that there was a man, a ⟦levites|Levi|Levite⟧, sojourning in the remote parts of the hill country of ⟦ephraim|Efrayim|Ephraim⟧; and he took for himself a woman, a concubine, from ⟦bethlehem|Beit Lechem|Bethlehem⟧ of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧.

[2] And his concubine played the prostitute against him, and she went from him to the house of her father, to ⟦bethlehem|Beit Lechem|Bethlehem⟧ of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, and she was there for a time, four months.

[3] And her husband arose and went after her, to speak to her heart, to bring her back; and his young man was with him, and a pair of donkeys. And she brought him into the house of her father, and the father of the girl saw him and rejoiced to meet him.

[4] And his father-in-law, the father of the girl, held him, and he dwelt with him three days; and they ate and drank and lodged there.

[5] And it came to pass on the fourth day that they rose early in the morning, and he arose to go; and the father of the girl said to his son-in-law, "Sustain your heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward you shall go."

[6] And they sat and ate, the two of them together, and drank; and the father of the girl said to the man, "Be willing, please, and lodge, and let your heart be good."

[7] And the man arose to go, and his father-in-law pressed him, and he turned back and lodged there.

[8] And he rose early in the morning on the fifth day to go, and the father of the girl said, "Sustain your heart, please, and linger until the day declines." And the two of them ate.

[9] And the man arose to go, he and his concubine and his young man; and his father-in-law, the father of the girl, said to him, "Behold, please, the day has slackened toward evening; lodge, please. Behold, the day is declining; lodge here, and let your heart be good; and you shall rise early tomorrow for your way, and you shall go to your tent."

[10] But the man was not willing to lodge, and he arose and went and came as far as opposite ⟦jebus|Yevus|Jebus⟧ — that is ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧; and with him was a pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.

[11] They were by ⟦jebus|Yevus|Jebus⟧, and the day had gone far down; and the young man said to his lord, "Come, please, and let us turn aside to this city of the ⟦jebusites|Yevusi|Jebusite⟧ and lodge in it."

[12] And his lord said to him, "We will not turn aside to a city of the foreigner, who are not of the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧; and we will pass on as far as ⟦gibeah|Givah|Gibeah⟧."

[13] And he said to his young man, "Come, and let us come near to one of the places, and we will lodge in ⟦gibeah|Givah|Gibeah⟧ or in Ramah."

[14] And they passed on and went, and the sun set on them beside ⟦gibeah|Givah|Gibeah⟧, which belongs to ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧.

[15] And they turned aside there to go in to lodge in ⟦gibeah|Givah|Gibeah⟧; and he went in and sat in the square of the city, and there was no man gathering them into the house to lodge.

[16] And behold, an old man came from his work, from the field, in the evening; and the man was from the hill country of ⟦ephraim|Efrayim|Ephraim⟧, and he was sojourning in ⟦gibeah|Givah|Gibeah⟧; and the men of the place were ⟦benjaminite|Bnei Yemini|Benjaminites⟧.

[17] And he lifted up his eyes and saw the man, the wayfarer, in the square of the city; and the old man said, "Where are you going, and from where do you come?"

[18] And he said to him, "We are passing on from ⟦bethlehem|Beit Lechem|Bethlehem⟧ of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ as far as the remote parts of the hill country of ⟦ephraim|Efrayim|Ephraim⟧. From there am I, and I went as far as ⟦bethlehem|Beit Lechem|Bethlehem⟧ of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧; and I am going to the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, and there is no man gathering me into the house.

[19] And there is both straw and fodder for our donkeys, and there is also bread and wine for me and for your slave woman and for the young man with your servants; there is no lack of any thing."

[20] And the old man said, "⟦shalom|Shalom|Peace⟧ to you; only, all your lack is on me; only do not lodge in the square."

[21] And he brought him into his house and gave fodder to the donkeys; and they washed their feet and ate and drank.

[22] They were making their heart good, and behold, the men of the city, men of the ⟦belial|sons of Beliyyaal|sons of worthlessness⟧, surrounded the house, pounding on the door; and they said to the man, the owner of the house, the old man, saying, "Bring out the man who came to your house, and let us know him."

[23] And the man, the owner of the house, went out to them and said to them, "No, my brothers, do not do evil, please; after this man has come into my house, do not do this outrage.

[24] Behold, my daughter, the virgin, and his concubine; let me, please, bring them out, and humble them, and do to them what is good in your eyes; and to this man you shall not do the thing of this outrage."

[25] And the men were not willing to listen to him; and the man took hold of his concubine and brought her out to them, outside; and they knew her and dealt ruthlessly with her all the night until the morning, and they let her go as the dawn went up.

[26] And the woman came at the turning of the morning and fell at the entrance of the house of the man where her lord was, until the light.

[27] And her lord arose in the morning and opened the doors of the house and went out to go on his way; and behold, the woman, his concubine, fallen at the entrance of the house, and her hands on the threshold.

[28] And he said to her, "Arise, and let us go." And there was no one answering. And he took her on the donkey, and the man arose and went to his place.

[29] And he came into his house and took the knife and took hold of his concubine and cut her in pieces, by her bones, into twelve pieces, and sent her into all the territory of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[30] And it was so, that everyone who saw said, "There has not been and there has not been seen a thing like this, from the day when the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ came up from the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ to this day. Set yourselves upon it, take counsel, and speak."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v18) — the Tetragrammaton kept.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"when there was no king in Israel" kept** (v1) — Hebrew וּמֶלֶךְ אֵין בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, literally "and a king there was not in Israel", a clause of circumstance inside "And it came to pass in those days … that there was a man". The same statement stands in 17:6; 18:1; 21:25.
- **"a certain Levite" → "a man, a Levite"** (v1) — Hebrew אִישׁ לֵוִי. **"on the side of mount Ephraim" → "in the remote parts of the hill country of Ephraim"** (v1, 18) — Hebrew יַרְכְּתֵי "the two flanks", the far end; הַר is the one word "mountain", here of a region.
- **"took to him a concubine" → "took for himself a woman, a concubine"** (v1) — Hebrew אִשָּׁה פִילֶגֶשׁ, two nouns side by side; פִּילֶגֶשׁ is a wife of lower standing. In v3 he is "her husband" (אִישָׁהּ, literally "her man"), her father is his "father-in-law" (חֹתֵן, v4, 7, 9) and he the "son-in-law" (חָתָן, v5).
- **"Bethlehemjudah" → "Bethlehem of Judah"** (v1, 2, 18) — Hebrew בֵּית לֶחֶם יְהוּדָה, a genitive: the Bethlehem that belongs to Judah.
- **"played the whore against him" → "played the prostitute against him"** (v2) — Hebrew וַתִּזְנֶה עָלָיו, the verb זָנָה with עַל "against / upon"; this combination stands only here. The Masoretic Text is translated. The Septuagint is commonly cited as reading "she became angry with him" (from memory); nothing more is said of what she did.
- **"four whole months" → "for a time, four months"** (v2) — Hebrew יָמִים אַרְבָּעָה חֳדָשִׁים, literally "days, four months".
- **"to speak friendly unto her" → "to speak to her heart"** (v3) — Hebrew לְדַבֵּר עַל־לִבָּהּ, as Gen 34:3; 50:21. **"to bring her again" → "to bring her back"** (v3) — the reading tradition (qere) לַהֲשִׁיבָהּ "to bring her back" is translated; the written form (ketiv) להשיבו is "to bring him / it back" (her heart, or himself to her).
- **"servant" → "young man"; "damsel" → "girl"** (v3–19) — Hebrew נַעַר, of an attendant, and its feminine נַעֲרָה, used of the concubine in her father's house (v3–9).
- **"retained him" → "held him"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיֶּחֱזַק־בּוֹ, literally "was strong on him": held on to him. **"abode" → "dwelt"** — Hebrew יָשַׁב.
- **"Comfort thine heart" → "Sustain your heart"** (v5, 8) — Hebrew סְעָד לִבְּךָ, as Gen 18:5. "You shall go" (v5) is plural, of the whole party.
- **"Be content, I pray thee, and tarry all night, and let thine heart be merry" → "Be willing, please, and lodge, and let your heart be good"** (v6, 9, 22) — Hebrew הוֹאֶל־נָא וְלִין וְיִטַב לִבֶּךָ: לוּן is "lodge" (spend the night) throughout the chapter; יָטַב לֵב is literally "the heart is good" (of ease and gladness at a meal; so also Ruth 3:7).
- **"he lodged there again" → "he turned back and lodged there"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיָּשָׁב וַיָּלֶן, two verbs; "turned back and lodged" may also say only "lodged again".
- **"and they tarried until afternoon" → "and linger until the day declines"** (v8) — Hebrew וְהִתְמַהְמְהוּ is pointed as an imperative plural and so belongs to the father's speech; עַד־נְטוֹת הַיּוֹם is literally "until the stretching out (turning) of the day".
- **"the day draweth toward evening … the day groweth to an end" → "the day has slackened toward evening … the day is declining"** (v9) — Hebrew רָפָה הַיּוֹם לַעֲרֹב, literally "the day has gone slack to become evening"; and חֲנוֹת הַיּוֹם, literally "the camping of the day" (the verb חָנָה "pitch camp"), found only here. The first "lodge" is plural, the second singular. **"go home" → "go to your tent"** — Hebrew לְאֹהָלֶךָ.
- **"would not tarry" → "was not willing to lodge"** (v10) — Hebrew לֹא־אָבָה; so also v25. **"over against Jebus" → "as far as opposite Jebus"** — Hebrew עַד־נֹכַח.
- **"the day was far spent" → "the day had gone far down"** (v11) — Hebrew רַד מְאֹד; the form רַד is unusual and is taken from יָרַד "go down".
- **"master" → "lord"** (v11, 12, 26, 27) — Hebrew אָדוֹן, of the young man's lord and of the woman's (v26, 27, plural of rank).
- **"the city of a stranger, that is not of the children of Israel" → "a city of the foreigner, who are not of the sons of Israel"** (v12) — Hebrew עִיר נָכְרִי אֲשֶׁר לֹא־מִבְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל הֵנָּה: נָכְרִי is singular ("a foreigner", of the people as one), and the clause ends with הֵנָּה, which is "they" (feminine plural) or "here"; the grammar is loose and is left so.
- **"the sun went down upon them" → "the sun set on them"** (v14) — Hebrew וַתָּבֹא לָהֶם הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ, literally "the sun went in for them".
- **"street" → "square"** (v15, 17, 20) — Hebrew רְחוֹב, the open place of a town. **"there was no man that took them into his house" → "there was no man gathering them into the house"** (v15, 18) — Hebrew מְאַסֵּף … הַבַּיְתָה: the verb אָסַף "gather", as Josh 20:4; "his" is not written.
- **"Benjamites" → "Bnei Yemini"** (v16) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יְמִינִי, "sons of Yemini".
- **"a wayfaring man" → "the man, the wayfarer"** (v17) — Hebrew הָאִישׁ הָאֹרֵחַ, with the article.
- **"I am now going to the house of the LORD" → "I am going to the house of Yahweh"** (v18) — Hebrew וְאֶת־בֵּית יְהוָה אֲנִי הֹלֵךְ; אֶת marks the house as the goal (or says "with / by the house of Yahweh I walk"). The Septuagint is commonly cited as reading "to my house" (from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"thy handmaid … thy servants" → "your slave woman … your servants"** (v19) — Hebrew אֲמָתֶךָ (אָמָה "slave woman") and עֲבָדֶיךָ, plural (the word is "slaves"): polite speech of himself and his party before the old man. **"no want of any thing" → "no lack of any thing"**; in v20 the same noun מַחְסוֹר: "all your lack is on me".
- **"Peace be with thee" → "*Shalom* to you"** (v20) — Hebrew שָׁלוֹם לָךְ; no verb.
- **"gave provender unto the asses" → "gave fodder to the donkeys"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיָּבָול, from בָּלַל "mix": he mixed fodder for them.
- **"certain sons of Belial" → "men of the sons of Beliyyaal"** (v22) — Hebrew אַנְשֵׁי בְנֵי־בְלִיַּעַל; בְּלִיַּעַל is a common noun, "worthlessness", not a name. **"beset the house round about" → "surrounded the house"** — Hebrew נָסַבּוּ, the verb of Gen 19:4. **"beat at the door" → "pounding on the door"** — Hebrew מִתְדַּפְּקִים, a reflexive form: beating again and again, or pushing one another.
- **"the master of the house" → "the owner of the house"** (v22, 23) — Hebrew בַּעַל הַבַּיִת.
- **"that we may know him" → "and let us know him"** (v22) — Hebrew וְנֵדָעֶנּוּ, the verb יָדַע "know", the same form of words as Gen 19:5 ("and let us know them"); in v25 "they knew her" is the same verb, of sexual intercourse.
- **"Nay, my brethren, nay, I pray you, do not so wickedly" → "No, my brothers, do not do evil, please"** (v23) — Hebrew אַל־אַחַי אַל־תָּרֵעוּ נָא, as Gen 19:7.
- **"folly" / "so vile a thing" → "this outrage" / "the thing of this outrage"** (v23, 24) — Hebrew נְבָלָה, the deed of the נָבָל, the base fool: an act that breaks what holds a people together (Gen 34:7; Deut 22:21; Josh 7:15). In v24 דְּבַר הַנְּבָלָה הַזֹּאת is literally "the word / thing of this outrage".
- **"my daughter a maiden" → "my daughter, the virgin"** (v24) — Hebrew בִּתִּי הַבְּתוּלָה. **"them I will bring out now" → "let me, please, bring them out"** — Hebrew אוֹצִיאָה־נָּא, as Gen 19:8. The pronouns "them" in this verse are masculine in form.
- **"humble ye them" kept** (v24) — Hebrew וְעַנּוּ אוֹתָם, the verb עִנָּה (piel), literally "afflict, bring low"; of a woman it says that she is brought low by the act, not by itself how (Deut 22:24, 29). **"do with them what seemeth good unto you" → "do to them what is good in your eyes"** — Hebrew הַטּוֹב בְּעֵינֵיכֶם, as Gen 19:8.
- **"would not hearken" → "were not willing to listen"; "took" → "took hold of"** (v25, 29) — Hebrew וַיַּחֲזֵק בְּ (hifil of חָזַק "be strong"): he seized her; the same verb in v29.
- **"abused her" → "dealt ruthlessly with her"** (v25) — Hebrew וַיִּתְעַלְּלוּ־בָהּ, the verb of Exod 10:2; Num 22:29; 1 Sam 31:4: to deal with someone as one pleases, without mercy. **"when the day began to spring, they let her go" → "they let her go as the dawn went up"** — Hebrew שִׁלַּח "send away, let go"; the qere כַּעֲלוֹת "as … went up" is translated (ketiv בעלות "at the going up").
- **"in the dawning of the day" → "at the turning of the morning"** (v26) — Hebrew לִפְנוֹת הַבֹּקֶר. **"door" → "entrance"** (v26, 27) — Hebrew פֶּתַח; "the doors" (v27) is דַּלְתוֹת. **"till it was light" → "until the light"**.
- **"was fallen down" → "fallen"** (v27) — Hebrew נֹפֶלֶת, a participle: lying fallen. The verse does not say whether she is alive.
- **"Up, and let us be going. But none answered" → "Arise, and let us go. And there was no one answering"** (v28) — Hebrew וְאֵין עֹנֶה. The Hebrew does not say here that she was dead; the Septuagint is commonly cited as adding "for she was dead" (from memory). In 20:5 the man says "and she died".
- **"a knife" → "the knife"** (v29) — Hebrew הַמַּאֲכֶלֶת, with the article: the large knife for cutting up meat (Gen 22:6, 10). **"divided her, together with her bones, into twelve pieces" → "cut her in pieces, by her bones, into twelve pieces"** — Hebrew וַיְנַתְּחֶהָ לַעֲצָמֶיהָ: נִתַּח is the verb of נֵתַח "piece", used of cutting up a sacrificial animal (Lev 1:6); לַעֲצָמֶיהָ is literally "according to her bones", limb by limb. **"coasts" → "territory"** — Hebrew גְּבוּל "border", and the land within it.
- **"And it was so, that all that saw it said" → "And it was so, that everyone who saw said"** (v30) — Hebrew וְהָיָה … וְאָמַר, forms of what happened again and again: whoever saw would say. **"consider of it" → "Set yourselves upon it"** — Hebrew שִׂימוּ־לָכֶם עָלֶיהָ, literally "put for yourselves upon it": the object ("your heart") is not written. **"take advice" → "take counsel"** — Hebrew עֻצוּ.

### Notes
- **Parallel to Sodom** — the Gibeah story echoes Gen 19 in its words: strangers arrive at evening; a sojourner takes them in; the men of the city surround the house and ask to "know" the guest; the host offers women instead. In Gen 19 the messengers strike the men blind; here no one steps in.
- **The Levite** — the man who came "to speak to her heart" (v3) takes hold of her and brings her out (v25). His words in v28 and the silence that follows are told without comment.
- **Dismemberment** (v29) — twelve pieces sent into all the territory of Israel: a summons. Compare 1 Sam 11:7 (Saul cuts up oxen to summon Israel).
- **"There has not been … a thing like this"** (v30) — the text marks the deed as unheard of since the coming up from Egypt.
- **No judge, no king** — chs 17–21 have no judge. The statement "there was no king in Israel" (17:6; 18:1; 19:1; 21:25) frames them.
- **Hos 9:9; 10:9** speak of "the days of Gibeah".
