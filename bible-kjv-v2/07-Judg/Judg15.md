# Judges 15

[1] After some time, at the time of wheat harvest, Samson visited his wife with a young goat. He said, "I will go in to my wife into the chamber." But her father would not let him go in.

[2] Her father said, "I really thought that you utterly hated her, so I gave her to your companion. Is not her younger sister more beautiful than she? Please take her instead."

[3] Samson said to them, "This time I shall be blameless regarding the Philistines when I do them harm."

[4] Samson went and caught three hundred foxes; he took torches, turned them tail to tail, and put a torch between every pair of tails.

[5] When he had lit the torches, he let the foxes go into the standing grain of the Philistines; he burned up the sheaves, the standing grain, and also the vineyards and olive groves.

[6] Then the Philistines said, "Who has done this?" They answered, "Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite, because he took his wife and gave her to his companion." So the Philistines came up and burned her and her father with fire.

[7] Samson said to them, "Since you act in this way, I will surely take revenge on you, and after that I will quit."

[8] He struck them leg and thigh with a great slaughter. Then he went down and lived in the cleft of the rock of Etam.

[9] The Philistines went up and encamped in Judah and spread out in Lehi.

[10] The men of Judah said, "Why have you come up against us?" They answered, "We have come up to bind Samson, to do to him as he has done to us."

[11] Then three thousand men of Judah went down to the cleft of the rock of Etam and said to Samson, "Do you not know that the Philistines rule over us? What is this you have done to us?" He said to them, "As they did to me, so I have done to them."

[12] They said to him, "We have come down to bind you, that we may give you into the hand of the Philistines." Samson said to them, "Swear to me that you will not fall upon me yourselves."

[13] They said to him, "No, we will only bind you and give you into their hand; we will surely not kill you." So they bound him with two new cords and brought him up from the rock.

[14] When he came to Lehi, the Philistines shouted as they met him; and the spirit of Yahweh rushed upon him, and the cords that were on his arms became like flax that has caught fire, and his bonds melted off his hands.

[15] He found a fresh jawbone of a donkey; he reached out his hand and took it, and with it he struck down a thousand men.

[16] Samson said:

"With the jawbone of a donkey,
heap upon heap!
With the jawbone of a donkey
I have struck down a thousand men!"

[17] When he had finished speaking, he threw the jawbone from his hand; and that place was called Ramath-lehi — "Jawbone Hill."

[18] He was very thirsty, and he called on Yahweh and said, "You have given this great deliverance into the hand of your servant. Now shall I die of thirst and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised?"

[19] So *Elohim* split open the hollow place that is at Lehi, and water came out of it. When he drank, his spirit returned and he revived. Therefore he named it En-hakkore ("the spring of him who called"), which is at Lehi to this day.

[20] He judged Israel in the days of the Philistines twenty years.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"corn" → "grain"** (v5).
- **"hip and thigh" → "leg and thigh"** (v8) — Hebrew שׁוֹק עַל־יָרֵךְ *shoq al yarekh*. Archaic English idiom.
- **"ass" → "donkey"** (v15, 16).
- **"sore athirst" → "very thirsty"** (v18) — archaic.
- **Hebrew wordplay in v16** — "heap upon heap" is *chamor chamorotayim*, playing on *chamor* (donkey) and *chomer* (heap).

### Notes
- **Samson's feud** — the Philistines burn his wife + father-in-law; he massacres in retaliation; they besiege Judah; he kills 1,000 with a donkey jawbone.
- **Foxes and firebrands** (v4–5) — ingenious arson. 300 torch-trailing foxes scattered through wheat fields at harvest time. Ecological devastation.
- **Judah delivers him up** (v11–13) — Judah is a vassal to the Philistines; does not defend Samson. Three thousand Judahites bind one Israelite to hand him to the oppressor. The tribes do not support their own judge.
- **Jawbone of a donkey** — donkey bones are bone-dry and can break easily. His 1,000-kill with this "unclean" weapon underscores miraculous strength.
- **Samson's prayer** (v18) — rare moment of prayer. Yahweh answers by providing water. A glimpse of a relationship beyond brute exploits.
- **"Twenty years"** (v20) — Samson's judgeship. Repeated at 16:31 — closing formula of his career.
