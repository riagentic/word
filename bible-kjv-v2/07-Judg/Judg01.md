## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v7) — Hebrew *Elohim*, in the mouth of a Canaanite king.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. v30, 31 and 33 begin without it, as in the Hebrew. **"children of" → "sons of"** — Hebrew בְּנֵי. **"Now after the death of Joshua it came to pass" → "And it came to pass after the death of Joshua"** (v1) — וַיְהִי, as Josh 1:1.
- **"the Canaanites … them" → "the Canaanite … him"** (throughout) — the Hebrew names each people in the singular with the article (הַכְּנַעֲנִי, הַפְּרִזִּי, הַיְבוּסִי, הָאֱמֹרִי, הָאָשֵׁרִי) and often goes on in the singular ("against him", v1; "did not dispossess him", v28, 32). So also the tribes: "Judah … his hand" (v2), "in his midst" (v29, 30). Where the Hebrew turns to the plural ("into their hand", v4; "they came to be", v30) the plural is kept. "The Hittites" (v26) is a plural in the Hebrew.
- **"asked the LORD" → "asked of Yahweh"** (v1) — Hebrew שָׁאַל בְּ, to ask by means of: to seek a decision from Yahweh (Num 27:21). **"against the Canaanites" → "to the Canaanite"** (v1, 10, 11) — Hebrew אֶל "to"; "against him" (v1, 3, 5, 8, 9) is בְּ after "fight". **"first"** — Hebrew בַּתְּחִלָּה, "at the beginning".
- **"Come up with me into my lot" → "Go up with me into my lot"** (v3) — Hebrew עָלָה, the verb of v1, 2, 4; גּוֹרָל is "lot": the land that fell to him by the lot. "The territory allotted to me" is an explanation.
- **"they slew of them in Bezek ten thousand men" → "they struck them down at Bezek, ten thousand men"** (v4) — Hebrew הִכָּה "strike"; "strike down" where killing is meant (v4, 5, 10, 17). In v8, 12, 25 a city is the object: "strike".
- **"caught him" → "took hold of him"** (v6) — Hebrew אָחַז. **"his thumbs and his great toes"** (v6, 7) — Hebrew בְּהֹנוֹת יָדָיו וְרַגְלָיו, literally "the thumbs of his hands and of his feet": one Hebrew word, בֹּהֶן, for the thumb and the great toe.
- **"threescore and ten" → "seventy"** (v7).
- **"gathered their meat under my table" → "were gathering under my table"** (v7) — Hebrew הָיוּ מְלַקְּטִים, "were gathering / picking up", with no object; "their meat", "scraps" are supplied. **"requited" → "repaid"** — Hebrew שִׁלֵּם.
- **"had taken it" → "captured it"** (v8, 12, 13, 18) — Hebrew לָכַד. **"set the city on fire"** (v8) — Hebrew שִׁלְּחוּ בָאֵשׁ, literally "sent away in the fire"; the object stands first.
- **"in the mountain, and in the south, and in the valley" → "in the hill country and the Negev and the Shephelah"** (v9) — Hebrew הָהָר, הַנֶּגֶב, הַשְּׁפֵלָה: the three regions of Judah, as Josh 15:21, 33, 48. הַר is "mountain" and, of a region, "hill country" (v19, 34). The Hebrew has one "in".
- **"Now the name of Hebron before was Kirjatharba" → "and the name of Hebron formerly was Kiriath-arba"** (v10, 11, 23) — Hebrew לְפָנִים; as Josh 15:15.
- **v10–15 and Josh 15:13–19** — the same account, in the same words where the Hebrew is the same. Differences of the Hebrew that are kept: here Judah goes to Hebron and "they" strike down the three (v10), in Josh 15:14 Caleb dispossesses them (so here in v20); "he went" (v11) for "he went up"; "the one younger than he" (v13) is only here; "the field" with the article (v14), "a field" there; "Give me" is הָבָה here and תְּנָה there (two verbs, one English word); "to him" (v15) and "Caleb" as the named subject are only here; "upper" and "lower" are singular forms here (גֻּלֹּת עִלִּית … תַּחְתִּית), plural there.
- **"Caleb's younger brother" → "the brother of Caleb, the one younger than he"** (v13) — Hebrew אֲחִי כָלֵב הַקָּטֹן מִמֶּנּוּ, literally "the small one from him". The Hebrew does not say whether Othniel or Kenaz is the brother of Caleb. **"took it" → "captured it"**.
- **"she moved him to ask of her father a field: and she lighted from off her ass … What wilt thou?" → "she incited him to ask from her father the field; and she alighted from the donkey … What ails you?"** (v14) — Hebrew הֵסִית "incite, urge on"; "when she came" is בְּבוֹאָהּ, "at her coming" ("to him" is not in the Hebrew). וַתִּצְנַח is a verb found only here, in Josh 15:18 and in Judg 4:21 (of the peg going down into the ground); "came down" is the sense the context gives; uncertain. מַה־לָּךְ is literally "What to you?"
- **"thou hast given me a south land; give me also springs of water" → "the land of the Negev you have given me; and you shall give me basins of water"** (v15) — Hebrew אֶרֶץ הַנֶּגֶב נְתַתָּנִי, with two objects: "you have given me the land of the Negev", or "you have given me away to the land of the Negev" (a dry land). "Also" is not in the Hebrew. גֻּלֹּת is the plural of גֻּלָּה "bowl, basin": basins or pools of water; "springs" is the old rendering. It may be a place name ("Gulloth-maim", "Upper Gulloth", "Lower Gulloth"). Uncertain.
- **"the children of the Kenite, Moses' father in law, went up out of the city of palm trees" → "the sons of the Kenite, the father-in-law of Moses, went up from the city of the palm trees"** (v16) — Hebrew וּבְנֵי קֵינִי חֹתֵן מֹשֶׁה: "Kenite" has no article ("the sons of a Kenite", or "of Keni" as a name); "the father-in-law of Moses" is said of that man. No name is given here (Hobab in 4:11). עִיר הַתְּמָרִים is "the city of the palm trees", as Deut 34:3 (there said of Jericho).
- **"which lieth in the south of Arad; and they went and dwelt among the people" → "which is in the Negev of Arad; and he went and dwelt with the people"** (v16) — Hebrew בְּנֶגֶב עֲרָד: "in the south of Arad" or "in the Negev of Arad" (the part of the Negev by Arad). The two last verbs are singular in the Hebrew (וַיֵּלֶךְ וַיֵּשֶׁב): "he", the Kenite, as one. "The people" is הָעָם, Hebrew as it stands; a reading "the Amalekite" (cf. 1 Sam 15:6) is found in some witnesses to the Greek (cited from memory) and is not in the verse.
- **"utterly destroyed it" → "devoted it to destruction"** (v17) — Hebrew הֶחֱרִים: gave it over to Yahweh beyond recall. The name Hormah (חָרְמָה) is from the same root. **"the name of the city was called Hormah" → "he called the name of the city Hormah"** — Hebrew וַיִּקְרָא, an active singular with no subject named: "he" (Judah) or "one called".
- **"with the coast thereof" → "and its territory"** (v18) — Hebrew וְאֶת־גְּבוּלָהּ, "and its border": the border and the land within it. The Greek translation has "did not take possession of" here (cited from memory), in line with v19 and 3:3; the Hebrew says "captured".
- **"he drave out the inhabitants of the mountain; but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley" → "he took possession of the hill country; for there was no dispossessing the inhabitants of the valley"** (v19) — Hebrew וַיֹּרֶשׁ אֶת־הָהָר כִּי לֹא לְהוֹרִישׁ. One verb, הוֹרִישׁ (the causative of יָרַשׁ "possess"): with a land as object "take possession of", with persons "dispossess", take another's place and holding. The second clause has no finite verb, literally "for not to dispossess": the Hebrew does not say "could not" (Josh 15:63; 17:12 have that verb). כִּי is "for" both times. **"chariots of iron"** — Hebrew רֶכֶב בַּרְזֶל.
- **"drive out" / "expelled" → "dispossess"** (v19–21, 27–33) — Hebrew הוֹרִישׁ in every place; one English word through the chapter. In v27 the first objects are towns ("Beth-shean and its daughters"), the later ones their inhabitants; the one verb governs both.
- **"as Moses said" → "as Moses had spoken"** (v20) — Hebrew דִּבֶּר. **"the three sons of Anak" → "the three sons of the Anak"** — the Hebrew has the article, as Josh 15:14.
- **"the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin" → "the Jebusite has dwelt with the sons of Benjamin"** (v21) — as Josh 15:63, where the same is said of the sons of Judah. The object stands first in the Hebrew.
- **"they also went up against Bethel" → "went up, they also, to Bethel"** (v22) — the Hebrew has no preposition before "Bethel".
- **"sent to descry Bethel" → "had Bethel explored"** (v23) — Hebrew וַיָּתִירוּ בְּ, the causative of תּוּר "explore" (Num 13): made a search at Bethel. "Sent" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"the spies" → "the watchers"** (v24) — Hebrew הַשֹּׁמְרִים, from שָׁמַר "keep, watch": those keeping watch on the city. "Spies" is another word.
- **"the entrance into the city" → "the way into the city"** (v24, 25) — Hebrew מְבוֹא הָעִיר, "the going-in of the city": the place where one can get in. **"we will shew thee mercy" → "we will do chesed with you"** — Hebrew וְעָשִׂינוּ עִמְּךָ חָסֶד.
- **"they let go the man and all his family" → "the man and all his clan they let go"** (v25) — Hebrew מִשְׁפָּחָה "clan"; שִׁלַּח "send away, let go".
- **"her towns" → "its daughters"** (v27) — Hebrew בְּנוֹתֶיהָ: the settlements that belong to a city, as Josh 17:11. "The inhabitants of Dor": the written text has the singular (ישב), the reading tradition the plural (יֹשְׁבֵי); the meaning is the same.
- **"the Canaanites would dwell in that land" → "the Canaanite undertook to dwell in this land"** (v27, 35) — Hebrew וַיּוֹאֶל … לָשֶׁבֶת: resolved, set himself to dwell; as Josh 17:12.
- **"they put the Canaanites to tribute, and did not utterly drive them out" → "he put the Canaanite to forced labor; but he surely did not dispossess him"** (v28) — Hebrew מַס is forced labor, not tribute (v30, 33, 35: הָיָה לָמַס, "came to be for forced labor"). וְהוֹרֵישׁ לֹא הוֹרִישׁוֹ is the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "and dispossessing he did not dispossess him": "surely did not"; it can also be taken as "did not dispossess him completely". As Josh 17:13. The verbs are singular: Israel as one.
- **"dwelt in Gezer among them" → "dwelt in his midst in Gezer"** (v29, 30) — Hebrew בְּקִרְבּוֹ, singular: in the midst of Ephraim, of Zebulun.
- **"Zidon" → "Sidon"** (v31).
- **"forced the children of Dan into the mountain: for they would not suffer them" → "pressed the sons of Dan into the hill country, for he did not allow him"** (v34) — Hebrew לָחַץ "press, squeeze", the verb rendered "oppress" elsewhere (2:18; 4:3); here it has a direction ("toward the hill country"), so the literal sense stands. לֹא נְתָנוֹ is literally "did not give him", singular.
- **"the hand of the house of Joseph prevailed" → "the hand of the house of Joseph was heavy"** (v35) — Hebrew וַתִּכְבַּד.
- **"the coast of the Amorites was from the going up to Akrabbim, from the rock, and upward" → "the border of the Amorite was from the ascent of Akrabbim, from the crag and upward"** (v36) — Hebrew מֵהַסֶּלַע, "from the crag", with the article; it may be a place name ("Sela"). Some witnesses to the Greek read "the Edomite" for "the Amorite" (cited from memory); the Hebrew is kept.

### Notes
- **Opening inventory of failure** — Judges begins where Joshua left off but from a different angle. Where Joshua 21 emphasized covenant fulfillment, Judges 1 catalogs what was *not* done. Tribe by tribe, the failure to fully dispossess the Canaanites.
- **Adoni-bezek's ironic fate** (v5–7) — his own cruelty returns on him. Possibly a local king-title like Adoni-zedek of Jerusalem.
- **Othniel and Achsah** (v12–15) — repeats Josh 15:16–19. Sets up Othniel as first judge (Judg 3).
- **Kenites** (v16) — Moses' in-law clan; they go with Judah into the southern wilderness. Heber the Kenite's wife Jael will kill Sisera (Judg 4–5).
- **Judah takes Jerusalem** (v8); Benjamin does not dispossess the Jebusites (v21). Jerusalem sits in limbo till David.
- **Catalog of incomplete conquest** (v27–36) — the key theme of Judges. Each tribe fails; the Canaanite remnant becomes a snare, fulfilling Joshua's warnings (Josh 23:12–13).
