# Judges 4

[1] The children of Israel again did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, after Ehud died.

[2] So Yahweh sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned at Hazor; the commander of his army was Sisera, who dwelt in Harosheth-hagoyim.

[3] The children of Israel cried out to Yahweh, for Sisera had nine hundred iron chariots and had oppressed the children of Israel harshly for twenty years.

[4] Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time.

[5] She used to sit under the Palm of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the children of Israel came up to her for judgment.

[6] She sent and summoned Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh-naphtali, and said to him, "Has not Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel commanded you, 'Go, gather troops at Mount Tabor, taking ten thousand men with you from the children of Naphtali and from the children of Zebulun?

[7] I will draw out to you at the wadi Kishon Sisera, the commander of Jabin's army, with his chariots and his multitude, and I will give him into your hand.'"

[8] Barak said to her, "If you will go with me, I will go; but if you will not go with me, I will not go."

[9] She said, "I will surely go with you; nevertheless, there will be no glory for you on the course you are taking, for Yahweh will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman." Deborah arose and went with Barak to Kedesh.

[10] Barak summoned Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh, and ten thousand men went up at his heels; Deborah went up with him.

[11] Now Heber the Kenite had separated himself from the Kenites — from the children of Hobab, Moses' father-in-law — and had pitched his tent as far as the oak in Zaanannim, which is near Kedesh.

[12] They told Sisera that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up to Mount Tabor.

[13] So Sisera called out all his chariots — nine hundred iron chariots — and all the troops with him, from Harosheth-hagoyim to the wadi Kishon.

[14] Deborah said to Barak, "Up! For this is the day on which Yahweh has given Sisera into your hand. Has not Yahweh gone out before you?" So Barak went down from Mount Tabor with ten thousand men after him.

[15] Yahweh threw Sisera and all his chariots and all his army into panic before Barak with the edge of the sword; and Sisera got down from his chariot and fled on foot.

[16] Barak pursued the chariots and the army to Harosheth-hagoyim, and all the army of Sisera fell by the edge of the sword; not a man was left.

[17] Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.

[18] Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him, "Turn aside, my lord, turn aside to me; do not be afraid." So he turned aside to her, into the tent, and she covered him with a rug.

[19] He said to her, "Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty." So she opened a skin of milk, gave him a drink, and covered him again.

[20] He said to her, "Stand at the entrance of the tent, and if any man comes and asks you, 'Is anyone here?' say, 'No.'"

[21] But Jael, Heber's wife, took a tent peg and took a hammer in her hand; she went softly to him and drove the peg into his temple, and it pierced through into the ground. He was fast asleep from weariness. So he died.

[22] And behold, as Barak pursued Sisera, Jael came out to meet him and said to him, "Come, and I will show you the man whom you are seeking." He went into her tent, and behold, Sisera lay dead, with the peg in his temple.

[23] So on that day *Elohim* subdued Jabin king of Canaan before the children of Israel.

[24] The hand of the children of Israel pressed harder and harder upon Jabin king of Canaan, until they had destroyed Jabin king of Canaan.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Harosheth of the Gentiles" → "Harosheth-hagoyim"** (v2, 13, 16) — Hebrew חֲרֹשֶׁת הַגּוֹיִם *Charoshet ha-goyim*. Proper name; *goyim* = "nations."
- **"captain of whose host" → "commander of his army"** (v2).
- **"discomfited" → "threw into panic"** (v15).
- **"nail" → "peg" / "tent peg"** (v21, 22) — Hebrew יָתֵד *yated* = tent-peg.
- **"bottle of milk" → "skin of milk"** (v19).
- **"mantle" → "rug"** (v18) — Hebrew שְׂמִיכָה *semikhah*. Thick cover / rug.

### Notes
- **Deborah** — the only female judge. Both prophetess and civic-judicial figure. Unique in ancient-Near-Eastern records.
- **Barak's hesitation** (v8) — his condition produces Deborah's prophecy: victory will go "by the hand of a woman." Barak's honor-loss (v9). Heb 11:32 nonetheless includes Barak among the faithful.
- **Jael** (v17–22) — the woman. Wife of Heber, who had peace with Jabin. Her shocking act violates hospitality-code but serves Yahweh. Celebrated in ch 5's song.
- **"Iron chariots"** (v3, 13) — standard Canaanite technological advantage. Sisera's army is powerful on the flat Megiddo plain; Yahweh's miracle (cf. 5:20–21) neutralizes this.
- **Wadi Kishon** (v7, 13) — runs through the Jezreel valley, past Mt Tabor. Rain and flood will turn it against the chariots.
- **Hospitality inversion** — Sisera expects shelter; receives death. Same culture-inversion as Rahab (where the spy in need received aid).
