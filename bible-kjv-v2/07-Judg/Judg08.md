## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"did chide with him sharply" → "contended with him sharply"** (v1).
- **"abated" → "subsided"** (v3).
- **"faint, yet pursuing" → "exhausted yet still pursuing"** (v4).
- **"threescore and seventeen" → "seventy-seven"** (v14) — modernized.
- **"ornaments ... on the camels' necks" → "crescents"** (v21, 26) — Hebrew שַׂהֲרֹנִים *saharonim*. Crescent-shaped lunar ornaments, possibly Midianite moon-god amulets.
- **"collars" → "pendants"** (v26) — Hebrew נְטִיפוֹת *netifot*. Drop / pendant ornaments.
- **"upbraid me" → "taunted me"** (v15) — modernized.
- **"discomfited all the host" → "threw the entire camp into panic"** (v12).
- **"taught the men of Succoth" (v16)** — grim irony; he "instructed" them with thorns.
- **"Ishmaelites" (v24)** — Midianites equated with Ishmaelites, as in Gen 37:25–28. Ethnic overlap.

### Notes
- **Ephraimite complaint** (v1–3) — Ephraim's tribal jealousy, later seen in Judg 12 with Jephthah (more fatally). Gideon diplomatically flatters them.
- **Succoth and Penuel** (v5–17) — Transjordan Israelite cities that refused aid. Gideon punishes them brutally on return. Rough justice: these were his own people.
- **Zebah and Zalmunna** — Midianite kings. Punning names: *Zevach* = "sacrifice," *Tzalmunna* = "shelter refused."
- **Blood revenge** (v18–21) — Gideon discovers the kings killed his own brothers at Tabor. Personal vendetta overlaid on national deliverance.
- **Kingship refused** (v22–23) — iconic text. Yahweh is king; human ruler rejected. But Gideon's later behavior (many wives, concubine, gold ephod) is monarchical.
- **Gold ephod** (v24–27) — ambiguous. An ephod was a priestly vestment; this one functioned as a divinatory / cultic object. Israel "whored after it" — it became an idol. First step toward dissolution.
- **Baal-berith** (v33) — "Baal of the Covenant," Shechem's local deity. The Israelites promptly covenant with Baal after Gideon's death.
- **Seventy sons** (v30) — Gideon's harem-size foreshadows royal / non-ideal behavior. Sets up Abimelech's coup in ch 9.
