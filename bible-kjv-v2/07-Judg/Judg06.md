## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"trumpet" → "horn"** (v34) — Hebrew שׁוֹפָר *shofar* = ram's horn, not a metal trumpet.
- **"strong holds" → "strongholds"** (v2) — modernized spelling.
- **"grasshoppers" → "locusts"** (v5) — Hebrew אַרְבֶּה *arbeh*. Locusts specifically.
- **"bullock" → "bull"** (v25, 26, 28).
- **"Jehovah-shalom" → "*Yahweh-shalom*"** (v24) — modernized divine name.
- **"came upon Gideon" → "clothed Gideon"** (v34) — Hebrew לָבְשָׁה *lavshah*. Distinctive idiom for radical divine empowerment (also 1 Chr 12:18, 2 Chr 24:20). The spirit "clothes" / puts on the person.
- **"my Lord" → "*Adonai*"** (v15) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי *Adonai* (the divine title), where v13 has אֲדֹנִי *adoni*, "my lord."

### Notes
- **Midianite oppression** — seven-year cycles of locust-like raiding. Israel impoverished, driven to caves.
- **Gideon's call** (v11–24) — elaborate theophany in a winepress. Gideon is threshing wheat hidden. The ironic appellation "mighty man of valor" spurs self-reflection.
- **Gideon's doubt** — repeated throughout: excuse of weakness (v15), request for sign (v17–22), fleece-tests (v36–40). The narrative paints him as hesitant, demanding reassurance.
- **Altar of Baal torn down** (v25–32) — Gideon's first command is domestic, not military: tear down the altar on his own father's property. Joash defends him with pragmatic theology: if Baal is real, let him act.
- **"Let Baal contend"** — Jerubbaal becomes Gideon's standing nickname (cf. 1 Sam 12:11, 2 Sam 11:21 where "Jerub-besheth" replaces Baal with *bosheth* "shame").
- **Fleece test** (v36–40) — doubly reversed: first wet-fleece dry-ground, then dry-fleece wet-ground. Gideon gains assurance through repeated sign-request.
