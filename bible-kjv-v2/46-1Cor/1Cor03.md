# 1 Corinthians 3

[1] And I, brothers, was not able to speak to you as to spiritual ones, but as to ones of flesh, as to infants in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[2] I gave you milk to drink, not food, for you were not yet able. But not even now are you yet able,

[3] for you are still fleshly. For where there is jealousy and strife among you, are you not fleshly and walking according to man?

[4] For when one says, "I am of ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧," and another, "I of Apollos," are you not men?

[5] What then is Apollos? And what is ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧? Servants through whom you believed, and to each as the Lord gave.

[6] I planted, Apollos watered, but ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ was making it grow;

[7] so that neither the one who plants is anything nor the one who waters, but ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ who makes it grow.

[8] Now the one who plants and the one who waters are one, but each will receive his own wage according to his own labor,

[9] for we are ⟦elohim|Elohim's|God's⟧ fellow workers; you are ⟦elohim|Elohim's|God's⟧ field, ⟦elohim|Elohim's|God's⟧ building.

[10] According to the grace of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ that was given to me, as a wise master builder I laid a foundation, and another builds upon it. But let each one look how he builds upon it;

[11] for another foundation no one is able to lay besides the one that is laid, which is ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[12] But if anyone builds upon the foundation gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, straw,

[13] the work of each will become manifest, for the day will make it clear, because it is revealed in fire, and the work of each, of what sort it is, the fire itself will test.

[14] If the work of anyone that he built upon it will remain, he will receive a wage;

[15] if the work of anyone will be burned up, he will suffer loss, but he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire.

[16] Do you not know that **you are ⟦elohim|Elohim's|God's⟧ sanctuary and the Spirit of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ dwells in you**?

[17] If anyone ruins the sanctuary of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, this one ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will ruin; for the sanctuary of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is holy, which you are.

[18] Let no one utterly beguile himself; if anyone thinks he is wise among you in this age, let him become a fool, that he may become wise,

[19] for the wisdom of this world is foolishness with ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧. For it is written: "**He who catches the wise in their craftiness**";

[20] and again: "**⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The Lord⟧ knows the reasonings of the wise, that they are vain**."

[21] So then let no one boast in men; for all things are yours,

[22] whether ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ or Apollos or ⟦cephas|Kefa|Cephas⟧ or world or life or death or things present or things to come, all are yours,

[23] and you are ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach's|Christ's⟧, and ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ is ⟦elohim|Elohim's|God's⟧.

## Changes

- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (throughout) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*; divine name preserved.
- **"Cephas" → "*Kefa*"** (v22) — Aramaic כֵּיפָא *Kefa* ("rock"); preserved in Aramaic form.
- **"as unto carnal" → "as to ones of flesh"; "ye are yet carnal" → "you are still fleshly"** (v1, 3) — Greek σαρκίνοις (v1; WH, Treg, NA28), "made of flesh" (as 2 Cor 3:3), and σαρκικοί (v3, twice), "fleshly" (as 2 Cor 1:12): two words of the root σάρξ "flesh"; the KJV's Greek has σαρκικοῖς in v1 as well (RP). The Greek has no "people".
- **"babes" → "infants"** (v1) — Greek νηπίοις, as Eph 4:14; Heb 5:13.
- **"I have fed you with milk, and not with meat" → "I gave you milk to drink, not food"** (v2) — Greek γάλα ὑμᾶς ἐπότισα, οὐ βρῶμα; ποτίζω "give to drink" is the verb "watered" in v6–8. βρῶμα is "food"; the Greek has no "solid".
- **"ye were not able to bear it, neither yet now are ye able" → "you were not yet able. But not even now are you yet able"** (v2) — Greek οὔπω γὰρ ἐδύνασθε. ἀλλʼ οὐδὲ ἔτι νῦν δύνασθε; the Greek has no "bear it" and no "ready".
- **"envying, and strife, and divisions" → "jealousy and strife"** (v3) — Greek ζῆλος καὶ ἔρις (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows + καὶ διχοστασίαι (RP). ζῆλος is "zeal", here against one another, hence "jealousy" (as 2 Cor 12:20).
- **"walk as men" → "walking according to man"** (v3) — Greek κατὰ ἄνθρωπον περιπατεῖτε; περιπατέω is "walk"; the Greek has no "only" and no "behaving".
- **"I am of Paul" kept** (v4) — Greek Ἐγὼ μέν εἰμι Παύλου, as 1:12; the second saying has no verb.
- **"are ye not carnal?" → "are you not men?"** (v4) — Greek οὐκ ἄνθρωποί ἐστε; (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows οὐχὶ σαρκικοί (RP). The Greek has no "merely".
- **"Who then is Paul, and who is Apollos, but ministers" → "What then is Apollos? And what is *Paulus*? Servants"** (v5) — Greek τί, "what", and Apollos first (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows τίς, "who", the other order and ἀλλʼ ἤ, "but" (RP). διάκονοι is "servants".
- **"even as the Lord gave to every man" → "and to each as the Lord gave"** (v5) — Greek καὶ ἑκάστῳ ὡς ὁ κύριος ἔδωκεν: δίδωμι "give"; the Greek has no "assigned".
- **"God gave the increase" → "*Elohim* was making it grow"; "God that giveth the increase" → "*Elohim* who makes it grow"** (v6, 7) — Greek ηὔξανεν, imperfect, beside two simple past tenses ("planted", "watered"); ὁ αὐξάνων θεός. αὐξάνω is "make grow" (as 2 Cor 9:10); the Greek has no object ("it" is supplied: English needs one) and no noun "growth". The Greek has no "only" in v7.
- **"reward" → "wage"; "labour" kept** (v8, 14) — Greek μισθός, pay for work (as Jas 5:4), the same word in v8 and v14; κόπος.
- **"we are labourers together with God" → "we are *Elohim's* fellow workers"** (v9) — Greek θεοῦ γάρ ἐσμεν συνεργοί, a plain genitive: fellow workers who belong to God, or who work with God.
- **"God's husbandry" → "*Elohim's* field"; "God's building" kept** (v9) — Greek γεώργιον, tilled land; οἰκοδομή, here the structure (as Eph 2:21), elsewhere the act of "building up" (2 Cor 10:8).
- **"wise masterbuilder" kept; "I have laid the foundation" → "I laid a foundation"** (v10) — Greek σοφὸς ἀρχιτέκτων: σοφός is "wise" as in all of chapters 1–3, not "skilled"; θεμέλιον ἔθηκα, a simple past tense (WH, Treg, NA28) and no article.
- **"buildeth thereon" → "builds upon it"; "take heed" → "look"** (v10) — Greek ἐποικοδομεῖ, "builds upon", four times in v10–14; the Greek has no object ("it" is supplied). βλεπέτω as 1:26.
- **"other foundation can no man lay than that is laid" → "another foundation no one is able to lay besides the one that is laid"** (v11) — Greek δύναται; παρὰ τὸν κείμενον.
- **"upon this foundation" → "upon the foundation"; "stubble" → "straw"** (v12) — Greek ἐπὶ τὸν θεμέλιον (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows + τοῦτον (RP). The materials are in the accusative: they are what is built. καλάμη is the stalk of grain.
- **"shall be made manifest" → "will become manifest"; "shall declare it" → "will make it clear"** (v13) — Greek φανερὸν γενήσεται; δηλώσει, the verb of 1:11. "The day" has no capital in the Greek, and the verb has no object ("it" is supplied).
- **"it shall be revealed by fire" → "it is revealed in fire"** (v13) — Greek ἐν πυρὶ ἀποκαλύπτεται, present tense. The subject is not named: the day, or the work.
- **"the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is" → "the work of each, of what sort it is, the fire itself will test"** (v13) — Greek τὸ πῦρ αὐτὸ δοκιμάσει (WH, Treg, NA28 have αὐτό, "itself"; NIV and RP omit it). The Greek has no "has done".
- **"If any man's work abide" → "If the work of anyone … will remain"** (v14) — Greek μενεῖ, printed as a future; without the accent, which the old manuscripts lack, it can also be the present μένει, "remains". μένω is "abide" of persons, "remain" of a thing as here; the Greek has no "survives" and no "on the foundation".
- **"he shall suffer loss" kept; "yet so as by fire" → "yet so as through fire"** (v15) — Greek ζημιωθήσεται (as Phil 3:8); οὕτως δὲ ὡς διὰ πυρός. The Greek has no "only".
- **"the temple of God" → "*Elohim's* sanctuary"** (v16) — Greek ναὸς θεοῦ, without the article: "a sanctuary of God" or "God's sanctuary". ναός is the temple building itself ("temple" is ἱερόν, the whole precinct, 9:13). "You" is plural throughout v16–17.
- **"If any man defile the temple of God, him shall God destroy" → "If anyone ruins the sanctuary of *Elohim*, this one *Elohim* will ruin"** (v17) — Greek φθείρει, φθερεῖ: one verb twice, placed side by side, which the KJV renders by two. φθείρω is "corrupt, ruin, destroy" (2 Cor 7:2; 11:3 "corrupt"); here "ruin" fits both the sanctuary and the one who is punished, where "corrupt" would wrongly suggest that Elohim makes someone morally corrupt.
- **"which temple ye are" → "which you are"** (v17) — Greek οἵτινές ἐστε ὑμεῖς; the Greek does not repeat ναός. The relative is plural: it can take up "sanctuary" or "holy".
- **"deceive" → "utterly beguile"** (v18) — Greek ἐξαπατάτω, as 2 Cor 11:3.
- **"If any man among you seemeth to be wise in this world" → "if anyone thinks he is wise among you in this age"** (v18) — Greek εἴ τις δοκεῖ σοφὸς εἶναι ἐν ὑμῖν ἐν τῷ αἰῶνι τούτῳ: δοκεῖ is "thinks" or "seems"; "among you" follows "wise" in the Greek; "in this age" can go with "wise" or with "let him become a fool".
- **"that he may be wise" → "that he may become wise"** (v18) — Greek ἵνα γένηται σοφός, the verb of "let him become".
- **"He taketh the wise in their own craftiness" → "He who catches the wise in their craftiness"** (v19) — Greek ὁ δρασσόμενος, a participle with the article: the quotation is not a full sentence, and the verse leaves it so.
- **"the thoughts of the wise" → "the reasonings of the wise"** (v20) — Greek τοὺς διαλογισμούς (as Jas 2:4); μάταιοι is "vain". The Greek has Κύριος, "Lord", without the article; in Ps 94:11 it is Yahweh.
- **"Therefore let no man glory in men" → "So then let no one boast in men"** (v21) — Greek ὥστε; καυχάσθω as 1:29, 31.
- **"or the world" → "or world"; "all are yours" kept** (v22) — the Greek has no articles in the list; the second "are" is supplied (WH, Treg, NA28 have only πάντα ὑμῶν; RP adds ἐστιν).
- **"ye are Christ's; and Christ is God's" kept** (v23) — Greek ὑμεῖς δὲ Χριστοῦ, Χριστὸς δὲ θεοῦ: plain genitives without verbs ("are", "is" are supplied).

### Notes
- **v19** quotes Job 5:13.
- **v20** quotes Ps 94:11.
