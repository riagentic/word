# 1 Corinthians 8

[1] Now concerning the things sacrificed to idols: we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up.

[2] If anyone thinks he has come to know anything, he has not yet come to know as he ought to know;

[3] but if anyone loves ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, this one has been known by him.

[4] Concerning then the eating of the things sacrificed to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that **there is no ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ but one**.

[5] For even if indeed there are so-called gods, whether in heaven or on earth, just as there are many gods and many lords,

[6] yet to us there is **one ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, the Father, from whom are all things and we unto him, and one Lord, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, through whom are all things and we through him**.

[7] But the knowledge is not in all; and some, by the custom of the idol until now, eat as a thing sacrificed to an idol, and their conscience, being weak, is defiled.

[8] But food will not present us to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; neither if we do not eat are we in lack, nor if we eat do we abound.

[9] But see that this right of yours does not somehow become a cause of stumbling to the weak.

[10] For if anyone sees you, the one having knowledge, reclining in an idol's temple, will not his conscience, he being weak, be built up to eat the things sacrificed to idols?

[11] For the one who is weak is perishing in your knowledge, the brother because of whom ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ died.

[12] And thus sinning against the brothers and striking their conscience while it is weak, you sin against ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[13] Therefore, if food makes my brother stumble, I will by no means eat meat forever, that I may not make my brother stumble.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v6) — Greek *Iēsous* transliterates Hebrew/Aramaic *Yeshua*.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (vv6, 11, 12) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **v4 *Shema* echo** — "there is no *Elohim* but one" echoes Deut 6:4's שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל *Shema Yisrael*; the *Shema* notation is preserved in the Notes rather than altered in the text.
- **"things offered unto idols" → "the things sacrificed to idols"** (v1, 4, 7, 10) — Greek εἰδωλόθυτον, from εἴδωλον "idol" and θύω "sacrifice"; the word itself does not say "food" or "meat".
- **"we all have knowledge" kept, without quotation marks** (v1, 4) — many take "we all have knowledge", "an idol is nothing in the world" and "there is no *Elohim* but one" as sayings of the Corinthians that Paul takes up; the Greek does not mark them.
- **"Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth" → "Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up"** (v1) — Greek ἡ γνῶσις φυσιοῖ, ἡ δὲ ἀγάπη οἰκοδομεῖ; the Greek has no "this". "Builds up" is the verb of v10.
- **"if any man think that he knoweth any thing, he knoweth nothing yet" → "If anyone thinks he has come to know anything, he has not yet come to know"** (v2) — Greek ἐγνωκέναι τι, οὔπω ἔγνω (WH Treg NA28), from γινώσκω "come to know", the verb of "known" in v3; RP reads εἰδέναι … οὐδέπω οὐδὲν ἔγνωκεν, "knows … has known nothing yet".
- **"the same is known of him" → "this one has been known by him"** (v3) — Greek οὗτος ἔγνωσται ὑπʼ αὐτοῦ, a perfect; the Greek has "by him", not the name a second time.
- **"an idol is nothing in the world" kept** (v4) — Greek οὐδὲν εἴδωλον ἐν κόσμῳ, without a verb. It is either "an idol is nothing in the world" or, parallel to the next clause (οὐδεὶς θεός, "no *Elohim*"), "there is no idol in the world". The Greek has no "real existence".
- **"there is none other God but one" → "there is no *Elohim* but one"** (v4) — Greek οὐδεὶς θεὸς εἰ μὴ εἷς (WH Treg NA28); "other" (ἕτερος) is a later addition (RP).
- **"For though there be that are called gods" → "For even if indeed there are so-called gods"** (v5) — Greek καὶ γὰρ εἴπερ εἰσὶν λεγόμενοι θεοί.
- **"(as there be gods many, and lords many,)" → "just as there are many gods and many lords"** (v5) — Greek ὥσπερ εἰσὶν θεοὶ πολλοὶ καὶ κύριοι πολλοί; no quotation marks in the verse.
- **"of whom are all things, and we in him" → "from whom are all things and we unto him"** (v6) — Greek ἐξ οὗ τὰ πάντα καὶ ἡμεῖς εἰς αὐτόν: ἐκ "out of, from", εἰς "into, unto". The sentence has no verb in the Greek; "there is" and "are" are supplied, and no "exist".
- **"by whom are all things, and we by him" → "through whom are all things and we through him"** (v6) — Greek διʼ οὗ … διʼ αὐτοῦ.
- **"there is not in every man that knowledge" → "the knowledge is not in all"** (v7) — Greek οὐκ ἐν πᾶσιν ἡ γνῶσις.
- **"with conscience of the idol unto this hour" → "by the custom of the idol until now"** (v7) — Greek τῇ συνηθείᾳ ἕως ἄρτι τοῦ εἰδώλου (WH Treg NA28): συνήθεια is custom, being used to something. The KJV follows συνειδήσει, "conscience" (RP). "Until now" can go with "custom" or with "eat". The verb "eat" has no object in the Greek; "it" is not supplied.
- **"meat commendeth us not to God" → "food will not present us to *Elohim*"** (v8) — Greek παραστήσει, future (WH Treg NA28; RP has the present): "set beside, present", as 2 Cor 4:14; "commend" is another verb (συνίστημι).
- **"for neither, if we eat, are we the better; neither, if we eat not, are we the worse" → "neither if we do not eat are we in lack, nor if we eat do we abound"** (v8) — the verse follows the order of WH and NA28, without "for": οὔτε ἐὰν μὴ φάγωμεν, ὑστερούμεθα, οὔτε ἐὰν φάγωμεν, περισσεύομεν. SBLGNT and RP have "for" (γάρ) and the two halves the other way round; Treg has the order of the verse with περισσευόμεθα. ὑστερέω and περισσεύω as elsewhere ("be in lack", "abound").
- **"take heed lest" → "see that … not"** (v9) — Greek βλέπετε μή πως.
- **"this liberty of yours" → "this right of yours"** (v9) — Greek ἡ ἐξουσία ὑμῶν αὕτη; ἐξουσία is "authority" (7:37) and of the root of "is permitted" (6:12). "Right" here as in chapter 9, which takes the word up.
- **"stumblingblock" → "cause of stumbling"** (v9) — Greek πρόσκομμα, what one strikes the foot against; a different root from "make stumble" (σκανδαλίζω) in v13.
- **"sit at meat" → "reclining"** (v10) — Greek κατακείμενον, lying at a meal.
- **"shall not the conscience of him which is weak be emboldened to eat" → "will not his conscience, he being weak, be built up to eat"** (v10) — Greek οἰκοδομηθήσεται, the verb of "love builds up" (v1), here in a bitter sense. "Weak" is said of the man (ἀσθενοῦς ὄντος), not of the conscience.
- **"And through thy knowledge shall the weak brother perish" → "For the one who is weak is perishing in your knowledge, the brother"** (v11) — Greek ἀπόλλυται γὰρ ὁ ἀσθενῶν ἐν τῇ σῇ γνώσει, ὁ ἀδελφός (WH Treg NA28): a statement in the present tense; ἐν is "in" or "by". RP has "and … will perish" and "upon your knowledge". The verb is "perish", not "is destroyed".
- **"for whom Christ died" → "because of whom the *Mashiach* died"** (v11) — Greek διʼ ὃν, "on account of whom".
- **"wound their weak conscience" → "striking their conscience while it is weak"** (v12) — Greek τύπτοντες, "strike, beat"; ἀσθενοῦσαν is a participle, "being weak".
- **"if meat make my brother to offend" → "if food makes my brother stumble"** (v13) — Greek βρῶμα, "food" (as v8); σκανδαλίζει, as 2 Cor 11:29.
- **"I will eat no flesh while the world standeth" → "I will by no means eat meat forever"** (v13) — Greek οὐ μὴ φάγω κρέα εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα: a strong denial; κρέα is "meat" (plural, "pieces of meat"); εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα is literally "unto the age".

### Notes
- **v4** echoes Deut 6:4 (*Shema*).
- **v10** — "you" in v10–11 is singular; in v9 and v12 it is plural.
