# 1 Corinthians 6

[1] Does any of you, having a matter against the other, dare to go to law before the unrighteous, and not before the saints?

[2] Or do you not know that the saints will judge the world? And if the world is judged by you, are you unworthy of the least tribunals?

[3] Do you not know that we will judge angels, to say nothing of things pertaining to this life?

[4] If then you have tribunals pertaining to this life, those who are despised in the church — do you seat these?

[5] I say it to your shame. Is it so, that there is not among you any wise man who will be able to judge between his brothers,

[6] but brother goes to law with brother, and that before unbelievers?

[7] Already then it is actually a defeat for you, that you have lawsuits with one another. Why are you not rather wronged? Why are you not rather deprived?

[8] But you yourselves wrong and deprive, and that brothers.

[9] Or do you not know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧? Do not be deceived: neither the sexually immoral nor idolaters nor adulterers nor soft men nor men who lie with males

[10] nor thieves nor the covetous, not drunkards, not revilers, not extortioners will inherit the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[11] And these things some of you were; but you washed yourselves, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and in the Spirit of our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[12] All things are permitted to me, but not all things are profitable. All things are permitted to me, but I will not be brought under the authority of anything.

[13] Foods for the belly, and the belly for foods; but ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will abolish both this and these. But the body is not for sexual immorality, but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body;

[14] and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ both raised the Lord and will raise us up through his power.

[15] Do you not know that your bodies are members of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧? Shall I then take away the members of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and make them members of a prostitute? May it not be!

[16] Or do you not know that the one who is joined to the prostitute is one body? For "**the two**," it says, "**will be one flesh**."

[17] But the one who is joined to the Lord is one spirit.

[18] Flee sexual immorality. Every sin, whatever a man may do, is outside the body, but the one who commits sexual immorality sins against his own body.

[19] Or do you not know that your body is a sanctuary of the ⟦ruach-haqodesh|Ruach HaQodesh|Holy Spirit⟧ in you, which you have from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧? And you are not your own,

[20] for you were bought with a price. **Glorify ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, then, in your body.**

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*; divine name preserved.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v11) — Greek *Iēsous* transliterates Hebrew/Aramaic *Yeshua*.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (vv11, 15) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"church"** (v4) — Greek ἐκκλησία *ekklēsia*, "assembly/congregation," not an institution.
- **"Holy Ghost" → "*Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v19) — Greek τὸ ἅγιον πνεῦμα *to hagion pneuma* renders the Hebrew רוּחַ הַקֹּדֶשׁ *Ruach HaQodesh* ("Spirit of Holiness"); the Hebrew title is used here as the culturally authentic form in which Paul, a Pharisee, would have conceptualized this.
- **"Dare any of you … go to law" kept** (v1, 6) — Greek κρίνεσθαι, κρίνεται, the middle voice of κρίνω "judge" (v2–3): "have oneself judged, go to law". The Greek has no "when" and no "grievance": πρᾶγμα is "a matter".
- **"the unjust" → "the unrighteous"** (v1) — Greek τῶν ἀδίκων, the word of v9; "wrong" in v7–8 is its verb (ἀδικέω).
- **"Do ye not know" → "Or do you not know"** (v2, 16) — Greek ἢ οὐκ οἴδατε (WH Treg NA28); RP has no "or".
- **"shall be judged by you" → "is judged by you"** (v2) — Greek ἐν ὑμῖν κρίνεται, present tense; ἐν ὑμῖν is "by you", "among you" or "before you".
- **"unworthy to judge the smallest matters" → "unworthy of the least tribunals"** (v2) — Greek ἀνάξιοί ἐστε κριτηρίων ἐλαχίστων. κριτήριον is a court of judgment; it can also mean a case that is judged. The Greek has no verb "to judge" here.
- **"how much more things that pertain to this life" → "to say nothing of things pertaining to this life"** (v3) — Greek μήτιγε βιωτικά; βιωτικός is from βίος, daily life and its means. The verse is one question in the printed text.
- **"judgments of things pertaining to this life" → "tribunals pertaining to this life"** (v4) — Greek βιωτικὰ κριτήρια, the word of v2. No "such cases" in the Greek.
- **"set them to judge who are least esteemed in the church" → "those who are despised in the church — do you seat these?"** (v4) — Greek τοὺς ἐξουθενημένους ἐν τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ, τούτους καθίζετε; ἐξουθενέω is "count as nothing, despise". καθίζετε is "seat (as judges)"; the form is a question or a statement ("you seat") or a command ("seat"). The printed text has a question. Who the despised are (judges outside the church, or its least members) the Greek does not say.
- **"I speak to your shame" → "I say it to your shame"** (v5) — Greek πρὸς ἐντροπὴν ὑμῖν λέγω.
- **"that there is not a wise man among you? no, not one" → "that there is not among you any wise man"** (v5) — Greek οὐκ ἔνι ἐν ὑμῖν οὐδεὶς σοφός (WH Treg NA28); RP has σοφὸς οὐδὲ εἷς, "a wise man, not even one".
- **"to judge between his brethren" → "to judge between his brothers"** (v5) — Greek διακρῖναι ἀνὰ μέσον τοῦ ἀδελφοῦ αὐτοῦ, literally "between his brother", in the singular; English needs two parties. διακρίνω is "discern" elsewhere in the letter; here it is deciding between parties, so "judge between". No "dispute" in the Greek.
- **"Now therefore there is utterly a fault among you" → "Already then it is actually a defeat for you"** (v7) — Greek ἤδη μὲν οὖν ὅλως ἥττημα ὑμῖν ἐστιν. ὅλως as 5:1; ἥττημα is the noun of ἡττάομαι "be defeated".
- **"because ye go to law one with another" → "that you have lawsuits with one another"** (v7) — Greek κρίματα ἔχετε μεθʼ ἑαυτῶν. κρίμα is "sentence" elsewhere; here the plural means legal actions, so "lawsuits".
- **"Why do ye not rather take wrong? why do ye not rather suffer yourselves to be defrauded?" → "Why are you not rather wronged? Why are you not rather deprived?"** (v7) — Greek ἀδικεῖσθε, ἀποστερεῖσθε: passive, "are wronged", or middle, "let yourselves be wronged". ἀποστερέω is "deprive", as 7:5; "defraud" is another word (πλεονεκτέω).
- **"and that your brethren" → "and that brothers"** (v8) — Greek καὶ τοῦτο ἀδελφούς; the Greek has no "your own".
- **"fornicators" → "the sexually immoral"** (v9) — Greek πόρνοι, as 5:9–11.
- **"effeminate" → "soft men"** (v9) — Greek μαλακοί, "soft": used of soft clothing (Matt 11:8), and of men who are soft, unmanly; in this place, between "adulterers" and "men who lie with males", it is often taken of the passive partners of such men. The verse gives the word itself.
- **"abusers of themselves with mankind" → "men who lie with males"** (v9) — Greek ἀρσενοκοῖται, from ἄρσην "male" and κοίτη "bed"; as 1 Tim 1:10. The Greek has two words (μαλακοί, ἀρσενοκοῖται), not one phrase.
- **"nor … nor" → "not … not"** (v10) — Greek οὐ μέθυσοι, οὐ λοίδοροι, οὐχ ἅρπαγες (WH Treg NA28). The words are those of 5:10–11.
- **"And such were some of you" → "And these things some of you were"** (v11) — Greek καὶ ταῦτά τινες ἦτε; ταῦτα is neuter, "these things".
- **"but ye are washed" → "but you washed yourselves"** (v11) — Greek ἀπελούσασθε, a middle voice and a past tense: "you washed yourselves" or "you had yourselves washed"; the next two verbs are passive. "But" (ἀλλά) stands three times.
- **"in the name of the Lord Jesus" → "in the name of the Lord *Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (v11) — the verse follows Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ (Treg NA28); Ἰησοῦ alone is read by SBLGNT and RP; WH has ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ, "our Lord *Yeshua* the *Mashiach*".
- **"by the Spirit" → "in the Spirit"** (v11) — Greek ἐν τῷ πνεύματι, the same ἐν as "in the name".
- **"lawful" → "permitted"; "expedient" → "profitable"** (v12) — Greek ἔξεστιν, as 2 Cor 12:4; συμφέρει, as 2 Cor 12:1. The verse has no quotation marks: many take "All things are permitted to me" (and "Foods for the belly …", v13) as sayings of the Corinthians that Paul answers; the Greek does not mark them.
- **"I will not be brought under the power of any" → "I will not be brought under the authority of anything"** (v12) — Greek ἐξουσιασθήσομαι, the verb of ἐξουσία "authority" and of the same root as ἔξεστιν "is permitted"; as 7:4. ὑπό τινος is "by anything" or "by anyone".
- **"Meats" → "Foods"; "shall destroy both it and them" → "will abolish both this and these"** (v13) — Greek βρώματα; καταργήσει, "abolish" as elsewhere; καὶ ταύτην καὶ ταῦτα. The Greek has no verb "is meant".
- **"fornication" → "sexual immorality"** (v13, 18) — Greek πορνεία.
- **"hath both raised up the Lord, and will also raise up us by his own power" → "both raised the Lord and will raise us up through his power"** (v14) — Greek ἤγειρεν … ἐξεγερεῖ, two verbs of one root; διὰ τῆς δυνάμεως αὐτοῦ.
- **"shall I then take" → "Shall I then take away"** (v15) — Greek ἄρας, from αἴρω, "take up, take away"; the verb of 5:2.
- **"an harlot" → "a prostitute"** (v15, 16) — Greek πόρνη, of the root of πορνεία; v16 has the article, "the prostitute".
- **"God forbid" → "May it not be!"** (v15) — Greek μὴ γένοιτο; no "God" in the Greek.
- **"for two, saith he, shall be one flesh" → "For 'the two,' it says, 'will be one flesh'"** (v16) — Greek Ἔσονται γάρ, φησίν, οἱ δύο εἰς σάρκα μίαν, literally "will be unto one flesh". φησίν is "it says" (the Scripture) or "he says"; the Greek has no "as it is written". "Body" (σῶμα) and "flesh" (σάρξ) are two words. The Greek has no "with her" (v16) and no "with him" (v17), and "is", not "becomes".
- **"Every sin that a man doeth" → "Every sin, whatever a man may do"** (v18) — Greek πᾶν ἁμάρτημα ὃ ἐὰν ποιήσῃ ἄνθρωπος; ἁμάρτημα is the sinful deed. The Greek has no "other".
- **"he that committeth fornication" → "the one who commits sexual immorality"** (v18) — Greek ὁ πορνεύων. "Against" is εἰς, "into, against", as 8:12.
- **"the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God" → "a sanctuary of the *Ruach HaQodesh* in you, which you have from *Elohim*"** (v19) — Greek ναὸς τοῦ ἐν ὑμῖν ἁγίου πνεύματος, οὗ ἔχετε ἀπὸ θεοῦ; ναός "sanctuary" (as 3:16–17) has no article. "Which" follows the neuter word πνεῦμα; the gender is grammatical. "Your body" is singular with a plural "your" (τὸ σῶμα ὑμῶν).
- **"and ye are not your own?" → "And you are not your own,"** (v19) — the printed text ends the question after "from *Elohim*"; the rest is a statement that runs on into v20.
- **"therefore glorify God" → "Glorify *Elohim*, then"** (v20) — Greek δοξάσατε δὴ τὸν θεόν; δή is an urging "then, now".
- **"in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's" → "in your body"** (v20) — the longer ending is a later addition (RP); WH, Treg and NA28 do not have it.

### Notes
- **v16** quotes Gen 2:24, in the wording of the Septuagint.
- **v20** — "with a price" (τιμῆς) is a plain genitive of price; the same sentence in 7:23.
