# 1 Corinthians 13

[1] If I speak in the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become sounding brass or a clanging cymbal.

[2] And if I have prophecy and know all the mysteries and all the knowledge, and if I have all the faith, so as to remove mountains, but have not love, I am nothing.

[3] And if I give away all my possessions as food, and if I deliver up my body that I may boast, but have not love, I am profited nothing.

[4] **Love is patient, love is kind, love is not jealous, it does not brag, it is not puffed up,**

[5] **it does not behave unbecomingly, does not seek the things of itself, is not provoked, does not reckon the evil,**

[6] **does not rejoice over unrighteousness, but rejoices together with the truth;**

[7] **it bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.**

[8] Love never falls. But whether there are prophecies, they will be abolished; whether tongues, they will cease; whether knowledge, it will be abolished.

[9] For we know in part and we prophesy in part;

[10] but when the perfect comes, that which is in part will be abolished.

[11] When I was an infant, I spoke as an infant, I thought as an infant, I reckoned as an infant; now that I have become a man, I have abolished the things of the infant.

[12] For now we see through a mirror, in a riddle, but then face to face; now I know in part, but then I shall know fully, even as I also was fully known.

[13] But now there abides faith, hope, love, these three; but **the greatest of these is love**.

## Changes

- **"charity" → "love"** (throughout) — ἀγάπη *agape* = self-giving, unconditional love; KJV's "charity" was the 1611 English equivalent of *agape* (from Latin *caritas*), but in modern English "charity" has narrowed to mean donations/almsgiving. "Love" is the correct modern translation.
- **"with the tongues" → "in the tongues"** (v1) — Greek ταῖς γλώσσαις, a dative without a preposition, as "speak in a tongue" in chapter 14.
- **"I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal" → "I have become sounding brass or a clanging cymbal"** (v1) — Greek γέγονα, a perfect; the Greek has no "as". χαλκὸς ἠχῶν is "sounding bronze (or copper)": the Greek names the metal, not an instrument. ἀλαλάζον is "crying aloud" (the verb of Mark 5:38).
- **"the gift of prophecy" → "prophecy"; "understand" → "know"; "all mysteries, and all knowledge" → "all the mysteries and all the knowledge"; "all faith" → "all the faith"** (v2) — Greek ἔχω προφητείαν, εἰδῶ τὰ μυστήρια πάντα καὶ πᾶσαν τὴν γνῶσιν, πᾶσαν τὴν πίστιν: no "gift" or "powers" in the Greek, and the articles are kept.
- **"bestow all my goods to feed the poor" → "give away all my possessions as food"** (v3) — Greek ψωμίσω, "feed by morsels (ψωμίον)"; the Greek does not name those who are fed.
- **"give my body to be burned" → "deliver up my body that I may boast"** (v3) — Greek παραδῶ, "deliver up" (as Gal 2:20). The verse follows καυχήσωμαι, "that I may boast" (WH NA28); the other reading is καυθήσωμαι / καυθήσομαι, "that I may be burned" (Treg RP; the printed SBLGNT text). The two words differ by one letter.
- **"it profiteth me nothing" → "I am profited nothing"** (v3) — Greek οὐδὲν ὠφελοῦμαι, a passive.
- **"suffereth long, and is kind" → "is patient, love is kind"** (v4) — Greek μακροθυμεῖ, χρηστεύεται ἡ ἀγάπη: "love" stands three times in the verse. The printed punctuation is followed; the words can also be divided "Love is patient, is kind; love is not jealous".
- **"envieth not" → "is not jealous"** (v4) — Greek οὐ ζηλοῖ, the verb rendered "be zealous" in 12:31; 14:1, 39: one word for zeal and for jealousy.
- **"vaunteth not itself" → "does not brag"; "is not puffed up" kept** (v4) — Greek περπερεύεται (only here in the New Testament), φυσιοῦται (as 4:6, 18–19; 5:2; 8:1).
- **"Doth not behave itself unseemly" → "does not behave unbecomingly"** (v5) — Greek οὐκ ἀσχημονεῖ, the verb of 7:36; no "rude" in the Greek.
- **"seeketh not her own" → "does not seek the things of itself"** (v5) — Greek οὐ ζητεῖ τὰ ἑαυτῆς; no "way" and no "insist" in the Greek.
- **"is not easily provoked" → "is not provoked"** (v5) — Greek οὐ παροξύνεται, "is not sharpened, stirred up" (the noun in Heb 10:24); the Greek has no "easily".
- **"thinketh no evil" → "does not reckon the evil"** (v5) — Greek οὐ λογίζεται τὸ κακόν, with the article: "does not put the evil to account" (as 2 Cor 5:19) or "does not think out evil". The same verb in v11.
- **"Rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth" → "does not rejoice over unrighteousness, but rejoices together with the truth"** (v6) — Greek ἐπὶ τῇ ἀδικίᾳ, συγχαίρει δὲ τῇ ἀληθείᾳ: the second verb is "rejoice with".
- **"Beareth all things" → "bears all things"** (v7) — Greek στέγει, "covers" or "bears" (9:12; 1 Thess 3:1, 5); "endures" is ὑπομένει.
- **"never faileth" → "never falls"** (v8) — Greek οὐδέποτε πίπτει.
- **"they shall fail" / "it shall vanish away" / "shall be done away" / "I put away" → "be abolished" / "I have abolished"** (v8, 10, 11) — Greek καταργέω four times, one verb; "they will cease" (παύσονται) is a different verb.
- **"that which is in part" kept** (v10) — Greek τὸ ἐκ μέρους, the phrase of v9 and v12.
- **"child" → "infant"; "I understood as a child, I thought as a child" → "I thought as an infant, I reckoned as an infant"** (v11) — Greek νήπιος, a small child, not yet of age (as Gal 4:1, 3); ἐφρόνουν, ἐλογιζόμην.
- **"when I became a man, I put away childish things" → "now that I have become a man, I have abolished the things of the infant"** (v11) — Greek ὅτε γέγονα ἀνήρ, κατήργηκα τὰ τοῦ νηπίου: "when", with two perfects.
- **"through a glass, darkly" → "through a mirror, in a riddle"** (v12) — Greek διʼ ἐσόπτρου ἐν αἰνίγματι; αἴνιγμα is a riddle, an obscure saying (Num 12:8 in the Septuagint).
- **"then shall I know even as also I am known" → "then I shall know fully, even as I also was fully known"** (v12) — Greek ἐπιγνώσομαι καθὼς καὶ ἐπεγνώσθην: the compound verb ("full knowledge", ἐπίγνωσις) against the simple γινώσκω before it; the second form is an aorist passive.
- **"And now abideth" → "But now there abides"** (v13) — Greek νυνὶ δὲ μένει, a singular verb with three subjects.
- **"the greatest of these" kept** (v13) — Greek μείζων δὲ τούτων, the comparative form, "greater": used for the superlative in the Greek of this time ("the greatest of these"), or "greater than these".

### Notes
- "Love" (*agape*) here, famously rendered "charity" in KJV, denotes self-giving love, not mere affection.
- **v4–7** — one sentence in Greek: fifteen verbs with "love" as subject.
