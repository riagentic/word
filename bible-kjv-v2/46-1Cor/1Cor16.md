# 1 Corinthians 16

[1] Now concerning the collection for the saints: as I ordained for the churches of Galatia, so do you also.

[2] On the first day of every week let each of you put aside by himself, storing up whatever he is prospered in, so that there be no collections then, when I come.

[3] And when I arrive, whomever you approve through letters, these I will send to carry your grace to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧;

[4] and if it is worthy of my going also, they will go with me.

[5] But I will come to you when I have passed through ⟦macedonia|Makedonia|Macedonia⟧, for I am passing through ⟦macedonia|Makedonia|Macedonia⟧,

[6] and perhaps I will abide with you, or even spend the winter, that you may send me forward wherever I go.

[7] For I do not want to see you now in passing, for I hope to remain on with you some time, if the Lord permits.

[8] But I will remain on in ⟦ephesus|Ephesos|Ephesus⟧ until ⟦shavuot|Shavuot|Pentecost⟧;

[9] for a door has opened to me, great and effective, and there are many opposers.

[10] Now if ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧ comes, see that he may be with you without fear, for he works the work of the Lord, as I also do;

[11] let no one therefore despise him. But send him forward in peace, that he may come to me, for I wait for him with the brothers.

[12] Now concerning Apollos the brother: I exhorted him much that he should come to you with the brothers, and it was not at all the will that he should come now; but he will come when he has opportunity.

[13] **Be awake, stand fast in the faith, be men, be made mighty.**

**[14] Let all your things be done in love.**

[15] Now I exhort you, brothers — you know the household of Stephanas, that it is the firstfruits of Achaia, and that they set themselves to service for the saints —

[16] that you also be subject to such ones, and to everyone who works together and labors.

[17] And I rejoice over the coming of Stephanas and Fortunatus and ⟦achaicus|Achaikos|Achaicus⟧, because these filled up your lack,

[18] for they refreshed my spirit and yours. Recognize therefore such ones.

[19] The churches of Asia greet you. Aquila and Prisca greet you much in the Lord, with the church at their house.

[20] All the brothers greet you. Greet one another with a holy kiss.

[21] The greeting with my own hand, ⟦paul|of Paulus|of Paul⟧.

[22] If anyone does not have affection for the Lord, let him be accursed. **Marana tha.**

[23] The grace of the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ be with you.

[24] My love be with you all in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" / "Christ Jesus" → "*Yeshua*" / "*Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v23, 24) — systematic. "Christ" in v22–23 and "Amen" in v24 are the later text (RP), not in the critical text.
- **"Pentecost" → "*Shavuot*"** (v8) — Greek ἡ πεντηκοστή, "the fiftieth (day)", the Greek name of the Feast of Weeks.
- **"as I have given order to the churches" → "as I ordained for the churches"** (v1) — Greek διέταξα, as 7:17; 11:34.
- **"Upon the first day of the week" → "On the first day of every week"** (v2) — Greek κατὰ μίαν σαββάτου, "on (each) one of the sabbath", the Jewish way of counting the days of the week; κατά is "each".
- **"lay by him in store, as God hath prospered him" → "put aside by himself, storing up whatever he is prospered in"** (v2) — Greek παρʼ ἑαυτῷ τιθέτω θησαυρίζων ὅ τι ἐὰν εὐοδῶται: "by himself" is "at his own home"; εὐοδῶται is a passive, "is led on a good way, prospers" (3 John 2); "God" is not in the Greek, and neither is "something".
- **"that there be no gatherings when I come" → "so that there be no collections then, when I come"** (v2) — Greek λογεῖαι, the plural of "collection" (v1); τότε, "then".
- **"whomsoever ye shall approve by your letters, them will I send" → "whomever you approve through letters, these I will send"** (v3) — Greek οὓς ἐὰν δοκιμάσητε διʼ ἐπιστολῶν, τούτους πέμψω. The printed punctuation joins "through letters" to "approve"; it can also go with "I will send" ("these I will send with letters"). The Greek has no "your".
- **"your liberality" → "your grace"** (v3) — Greek τὴν χάριν ὑμῶν: χάρις, "grace", here of the gift (as 2 Cor 8:4, 6–7, 19).
- **"if it be meet that I go also" → "if it is worthy of my going also"** (v4) — Greek ἐὰν δὲ ἄξιον ᾖ τοῦ κἀμὲ πορεύεσθαι.
- **"for I do pass through Macedonia" → "for I am passing through Macedonia"** (v5) — Greek διέρχομαι, a present, of what he is about to do; the Greek has no "intend".
- **"abide" kept; "tarry" → "remain on"** (v6, 7, 8) — Greek παραμενῶ, ἐπιμεῖναι, ἐπιμενῶ, compounds of μένω (as Phil 1:24–25): παραμένω "abide" with the persons (v6), ἐπιμένω "remain (on)" in every place.
- **"bring me on my journey" / "conduct him forth" → "send me forward" / "send him forward"** (v6, 11) — Greek προπέμπω, as 2 Cor 1:16; no "help" in the Greek.
- **"by the way" → "in passing"** (v7) — Greek ἐν παρόδῳ; the Greek has no "just".
- **"a great door and effectual is opened unto me, and there are many adversaries" → "a door has opened to me, great and effective, and there are many opposers"** (v9) — Greek θύρα … ἀνέῳγεν μεγάλη καὶ ἐνεργής: ἐνεργής, "working, effective" (Phlm 6), said of the door; no "for work". ἀντικείμενοι as Phil 1:28.
- **"see that he may be with you without fear" kept** (v10) — Greek βλέπετε ἵνα ἀφόβως γένηται πρὸς ὑμᾶς.
- **"I look for him with the brethren" → "I wait for him with the brothers"** (v11) — Greek ἐκδέχομαι. "With the brothers" can go with "I" or with "him".
- **"I greatly desired him to come" → "I exhorted him much that he should come"** (v12) — Greek πολλὰ παρεκάλεσα αὐτόν; παρακαλέω as in v15 and 1:10.
- **"his will was not at all to come" → "it was not at all the will that he should come now"** (v12) — Greek πάντως οὐκ ἦν θέλημα, without "his": the will of Apollos, or the will of God.
- **"Watch ye" → "Be awake"; "quit you like men, be strong" → "be men, be made mighty"** (v13) — Greek γρηγορεῖτε (as 1 Thess 5:6), στήκετε (as 1 Thess 3:8), ἀνδρίζεσθε ("act as men", from ἀνήρ), κραταιοῦσθε, a passive (as Eph 3:16).
- **"Let all your things be done with charity" → "Let all your things be done in love"** (v14) — Greek πάντα ὑμῶν ἐν ἀγάπῃ γινέσθω.
- **"I beseech you" → "I exhort you"** (v15) — Greek παρακαλῶ; the sentence breaks off and is taken up in v16 ("that you also …").
- **"they have addicted themselves to the ministry of the saints" → "they set themselves to service for the saints"** (v15) — Greek εἰς διακονίαν τοῖς ἁγίοις ἔταξαν ἑαυτούς: τάσσω, "set in place, appoint". ἀπαρχή is "firstfruits" (15:20, 23); no "converts" in the Greek. οἰκία, "household", is a different word from "house" in v19 and 1:16.
- **"that ye submit yourselves unto such, and to every one that helpeth with us, and laboureth" → "that you also be subject to such ones, and to everyone who works together and labors"** (v16) — Greek ὑποτάσσησθε (as 14:34; 15:27–28), παντὶ τῷ συνεργοῦντι καὶ κοπιῶντι; the Greek has no "with us".
- **"that which was lacking on your part they have supplied" → "these filled up your lack"** (v17) — Greek τὸ ὑμέτερον ὑστέρημα οὗτοι ἀνεπλήρωσαν (as Phil 2:30): what was lacking on their side, or his lack of them. παρουσία is "coming" and "presence".
- **"acknowledge ye them that are such" → "Recognize therefore such ones"** (v18) — Greek ἐπιγινώσκετε, as 14:37.
- **"salute" → "greet"; "the church that is in their house" → "the church at their house"** (v19–20) — Greek ἀσπάζομαι; κατʼ οἶκον αὐτῶν, as Phlm 2. "Much" is πολλά; in the printed text the verb is singular (WH NA28; plural Treg RP), which does not change the translation. Πρίσκα is the critical text; "Priscilla" is RP.
- **"The salutation of me Paul with mine own hand" → "The greeting with my own hand, of Paulus"** (v21) — Greek ὁ ἀσπασμὸς τῇ ἐμῇ χειρὶ Παύλου, without a verb (as Col 4:18; 2 Thess 3:17).
- **"love not" → "does not have affection for"** (v22) — Greek οὐ φιλεῖ: φιλέω "have affection for", not ἀγαπάω, the verb of "love" (ἀγάπη) in chapter 13 and in v24.
- **"Anathema" → "accursed"** (v22) — Greek ἀνάθεμα, a thing devoted to destruction (as Gal 1:8–9); translated rather than transliterated.
- **"Maran-atha" → "Marana tha"** (v22) — the Greek text gives Aramaic words in Greek letters and does not translate them; the verse does the same. The printed text divides Μαράνα θά, "Our Lord, come!" (NA28); the other division is Μαρὰν ἀθά, "Our Lord has come" (WH Treg RP). The old manuscripts have no word division.

### Notes
- **v22** — *Marana tha* is Aramaic; the same prayer in Greek in Rev 22:20, "Come, Lord *Yeshua*".
