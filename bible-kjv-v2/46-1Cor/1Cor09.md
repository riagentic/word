# 1 Corinthians 9

[1] Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord?

[2] If to others I am not an apostle, yet at least to you I am; for you are the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.

[3] My defense to those who examine me is this.

[4] Have we no right to eat and to drink?

[5] Have we no right to lead about a sister as wife, as also the rest of the apostles and the brothers of the Lord and ⟦cephas|Kefa|Cephas⟧?

[6] Or do only I and ⟦barnabas|Bar-Nabba|Barnabas⟧ have no right not to work?

[7] Who ever serves as a soldier at his own wages? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat its fruit? Or who shepherds a flock and does not eat of the milk of the flock?

[8] Do I speak these things according to man, or does not the ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧ also say these things?

[9] For in the ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧ of ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ it is written, "**You shall not muzzle a threshing ox**." Is it for the oxen that ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ cares,

[10] or does he say it altogether because of us? For because of us it was written, that the one who plows ought to plow in hope, and the one who threshes, in hope of partaking.

[11] If we sowed spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we shall reap your fleshly things?

[12] If others partake of the right over you, do not we rather?

But we did not use this right, but we bear all things, that we may not give any hindrance to the gospel of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[13] Do you not know that those who work at the sacred things eat the things from the temple, those who attend at the altar have their share with the altar?

[14] So also the Lord ordained for those who proclaim the gospel to live from the gospel.

[15] But I have not used any of these things. And I did not write these things that it should be so done in my case; for it is good for me rather to die than — no one shall empty my boast.

[16] For if I preach the gospel, I have no boast, for necessity is laid upon me; for woe is to me if I do not preach the gospel.

[17] For if I do this willingly, I have a wage; but if unwillingly, I have been entrusted with a stewardship.

[18] What then is my wage? That, preaching the gospel, I may make the gospel without charge, so as not to use to the full my right in the gospel.

[19] For being free from all, I enslaved myself to all, that I might gain the more;

[20] **and to the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ I became as a ⟦jew|Yehudi|Jew⟧, that I might gain ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧; to those under law as under law, not being myself under law, that I might gain those under law;**

[21] to those without law as without law, not being without law of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ but within law of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, that I might gain those without law.

**[22] To the weak I became weak, that I might gain the weak; to all I have become all things, that by all means I might save some.**

[23] And I do all things because of the gospel, that I may become a fellow sharer of it.

[24] Do you not know that those who run in a stadium all run, but one receives the prize? So run, that you may lay hold of it.

[25] And everyone who struggles exercises self-control in all things: those, then, that they may receive a corruptible crown, but we an incorruptible.

[26] I therefore so run, as not uncertainly; so I box, as not beating air;

[27] but I bruise my body and lead it into slavery, lest somehow, having proclaimed to others, I myself should become unapproved.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v1) — Greek *Iēsous* transliterates Hebrew/Aramaic *Yeshua*.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (vv12, 21) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv9, 21) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Cephas" → "*Kefa*"** (v5) — Aramaic כֵּיפָא *Kefa* ("rock"); preserved in original Aramaic form.
- **"law" → "Torah"** (vv8, 9) — Greek νόμος *nomos* here explicitly refers to the Torah (Paul cites Deut 25:4); rendered "Torah" to preserve the specific referent rather than the abstract "law."
- **"my work" kept** (v1) — Greek τὸ ἔργον μου, "my work"; not "workmanship".
- **"them that do examine me" → "those who examine me"** (v3) — Greek τοῖς ἐμὲ ἀνακρίνουσιν, a present participle: they are examining him now. "This" (αὕτη) stands last in the sentence and can point back to v1–2 or forward to v4 and what follows.
- **"power" → "right"** (v4, 5, 6, 12, 18) — Greek ἐξουσία, "authority, right" (as 2 Thess 3:9); the same word as "authority" in 11:10. The questions of v4–6 begin with μὴ οὐκ, "is it that we have no …?".
- **"a sister, a wife" → "a sister as wife"** (v5) — Greek ἀδελφὴν γυναῖκα, two nouns side by side: "a sister" (a fellow believer) "as wife"; γυνή is "woman" or "wife". The Greek has no word "believing".
- **"to forbear working" → "not to work"** (v6) — Greek μὴ ἐργάζεσθαι; nothing in the Greek says "for a living".
- **"at his own charges" → "at his own wages"** (v7) — Greek ἰδίοις ὀψωνίοις, a soldier's pay or rations (as 2 Cor 11:8).
- **"feedeth a flock" → "shepherds a flock"; "eateth not of the milk of the flock" kept** (v7) — Greek ποιμαίνει ποίμνην … ἐκ τοῦ γάλακτος τῆς ποίμνης οὐκ ἐσθίει; "flock" stands twice and the verb is "eat". "Or who" follows ἢ τίς (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28); the SBLGNT prints τίς alone.
- **"as a man" → "according to man"** (v8) — Greek κατὰ ἄνθρωπον.
- **"the same also" → "also say these things"** (v8) — Greek καὶ ὁ νόμος ταῦτα οὐ λέγει; ταῦτα "these things" twice in the verse.
- **"Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth out the corn" → "You shall not muzzle a threshing ox"** (v9) — Greek Οὐ κημώσεις βοῦν ἀλοῶντα, four words (as 1 Tim 5:18); "threshes" in v10 is the same verb. Westcott-Hort print φιμώσεις, with the same meaning.
- **"Doth God take care for oxen? Or saith he it altogether for our sakes?"** (v9–10) — one question in Greek: "Is it for the oxen that *Elohim* cares, or does he say it altogether because of us?" πάντως ("altogether") is the word rendered "by all means" in v22. "He" can also be "it" (the Torah).
- **"that he that thresheth in hope should be partaker of his hope" → "and the one who threshes, in hope of partaking"** (v10) — Greek καὶ ὁ ἀλοῶν ἐπ' ἐλπίδι τοῦ μετέχειν; the KJV follows the longer later text. ὅτι ("that") can also be "because". The Greek has no word "crop".
- **"carnal things" → "fleshly things"** (v11) — Greek τὰ σαρκικά, from σάρξ "flesh"; not "material". ὑμῖν is "for you" or "to you".
- **"this power over you" → "the right over you"** (v12) — Greek τῆς ὑμῶν ἐξουσίας, literally "of your right": the right over them (so most), or a right they grant.
- **"suffer all things" → "bear all things"** (v12) — Greek πάντα στέγομεν (as 13:7; 1 Thess 3:1).
- **"lest we should hinder" → "that we may not give any hindrance to"** (v12) — Greek ἵνα μή τινα ἐγκοπὴν δῶμεν.
- **"they which minister about holy things live of the things of the temple" → "those who work at the sacred things eat the things from the temple"** (v13) — Greek οἱ τὰ ἱερὰ ἐργαζόμενοι τὰ ἐκ τοῦ ἱεροῦ ἐσθίουσιν: ἱερά "sacred things" and ἱερόν "temple" are one word; the verb is "eat".
- **"are partakers with the altar" → "have their share with the altar"** (v13) — Greek τῷ θυσιαστηρίῳ συμμερίζονται, "share with the altar"; a different word from "partake" (v10, 12). The Greek has no "and" between the two halves.
- **"hath the Lord ordained that they which preach the gospel should live" → "the Lord ordained for those who proclaim the gospel to live"** (v14) — Greek διέταξεν (as Gal 3:19) with a dative and an infinitive, and καταγγέλλουσιν (as Phil 1:17–18); a different verb from "preach the gospel" (εὐαγγελίζομαι, v16, 18).
- **"it were better for me to die, than that any man should make my glorying void" → "it is good for me rather to die than — no one shall empty my boast"** (v15) — Greek καλὸν γάρ μοι μᾶλλον ἀποθανεῖν ἤ — τὸ καύχημά μου οὐδεὶς κενώσει: the sentence breaks off after "than" and a new one begins. The KJV follows the later text (ἵνα τις κενώσῃ), which smooths the break. κενόω "empty" as Phil 2:7.
- **"that it should be so done unto me" → "in my case"** (v15) — Greek ἐν ἐμοί, "in me".
- **"nothing to glory of" → "no boast"** (v16) — Greek καύχημα, as v15.
- **"yea, woe is unto me" → "for woe is to me"** (v16) — Greek γάρ, "for", three times in the verse.
- **"against my will, a dispensation of the gospel is committed unto me" → "unwillingly, I have been entrusted with a stewardship"** (v17) — Greek ἄκων, οἰκονομίαν πεπίστευμαι; the Greek has no "of the gospel" and no "still".
- **"reward" → "wage"** (v17, 18) — Greek μισθός, pay for work; a different word from the soldier's "wages" in v7 (ὀψώνια).
- **"the gospel of Christ" → "the gospel"** (v18) — "of Christ" is absent from Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28.
- **"that I abuse not my power" → "so as not to use to the full my right"** (v18) — Greek καταχρήσασθαι, a stronger form of "use" (χράομαι, v12, 15): "use up, use to the full".
- **"though I be free" → "being free"; "made myself servant" → "enslaved myself"** (v19) — Greek ὤν, a participle; ἐδούλωσα, from δοῦλος "slave".
- **"gain the more" kept** (v19) — Greek τοὺς πλείονας, "the more", "the greater number"; κερδαίνω "gain" five times in v19–22.
- **"under the law" → "under law"; + "not being myself under law"** (v20) — Greek ὑπὸ νόμον, without the article, four times. The clause μὴ ὢν αὐτὸς ὑπὸ νόμον is in Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28 and is missing from the later text the KJV follows.
- **"without law" kept** (v21) — Greek ἄνομος, elsewhere "lawless"; here it means those who do not have the law, not evildoers.
- **"being not without law to God, but under the law to Christ" → "not being without law of *Elohim* but within law of the *Mashiach*"** (v21) — Greek μὴ ὢν ἄνομος θεοῦ ἀλλ' ἔννομος Χριστοῦ: two genitives, and ἔννομος is "in law, within law"; not "under" (ὑπό, v20).
- **"as weak" → "weak"; "I am made all things to all men" → "to all I have become all things"** (v22) — the critical text has no ὡς "as" here; γέγονα is a perfect; the Greek has no word "men". πᾶσιν … πάντα … πάντως: "all" three times.
- **"And this I do" → "And I do all things"** (v23) — Greek πάντα (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28).
- **"that I might be partaker thereof with you" → "that I may become a fellow sharer of it"** (v23) — Greek ἵνα συγκοινωνὸς αὐτοῦ γένωμαι: a fellow sharer of the gospel. The Greek has no "with you", no "with them" and no "blessings".
- **"run in a race" → "run in a stadium"** (v24) — Greek ἐν σταδίῳ.
- **"that ye may obtain" → "that you may lay hold of it"** (v24) — Greek καταλάβητε (as Phil 3:12), a different verb from "receives" (λαμβάνει); "it" is supplied.
- **"every man that striveth for the mastery is temperate" → "everyone who struggles exercises self-control"** (v25) — Greek ἀγωνιζόμενος, of a contestant in the games; ἐγκρατεύεται, as ἐγκράτεια "self-control".
- **"corruptible crown … incorruptible" kept** (v25) — Greek φθαρτὸν στέφανον … ἄφθαρτον; the crown is the victor's wreath.
- **"not as uncertainly … not as one that beateth the air" → "as not uncertainly … as not beating air"** (v26) — Greek ὡς οὐκ ἀδήλως … ὡς οὐκ ἀέρα δέρων.
- **"I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection" → "I bruise my body and lead it into slavery"** (v27) — Greek ὑπωπιάζω, "strike under the eye, beat black and blue"; δουλαγωγῶ, "lead as a slave".
- **"when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway" → "having proclaimed to others, I myself should become unapproved"** (v27) — Greek κηρύξας, of a herald; ἀδόκιμος, "not standing the test" (as 2 Cor 13:5–7).

### Notes
- **v9** quotes Deut 25:4.
- **v14** — compare Matt 10:10; Luke 10:7.
