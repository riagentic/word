## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Greek Ἰησοῦς *Iēsous* is a transliteration of Hebrew/Aramaic יֵשׁוּעַ *Yeshua* ("Yahweh saves"); he was called *Yeshua* by those who knew him.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (throughout) — Greek Χριστός *Christos* = Hebrew מָשִׁיחַ *Mashiach*, "Anointed One." Preserving the Hebrew term keeps the royal-priestly-prophetic anointing concept explicit.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek θεός *theos* renders Hebrew אֱלֹהִים *Elohim*. The underlying divine name is preserved rather than flattened to the generic English "God."
- **"baptized" → "immersed"** (vv13–17) — Greek βαπτίζω *baptizō* means literally "to immerse/dip"; "baptize" is a transliteration, not a translation. "Immerse" renders the Greek verb accurately.
- **"Cephas" → "*Kefa*"** (v12) — Aramaic כֵּיפָא *Kefa* ("rock/stone"), the name Yeshua gave Simon; Greek *Kēphas* is itself a transliteration of Aramaic. Preserved in its Aramaic form.
- **"called to be an apostle" → "a called apostle"; "called to be saints" → "called saints"** (v1, 2) — Greek κλητὸς ἀπόστολος, κλητοῖς ἁγίοις: an adjective and a noun; the Greek has no "to be". The same word in v24 ("the called"); the verb in v9, the noun in v26 ("calling").
- **"our brother" → "the brother"** (v1) — Greek ὁ ἀδελφός, without "our" (as 2 Cor 1:1).
- **Order of v2** — the verse follows τῇ οὔσῃ ἐν Κορίνθῳ, ἡγιασμένοις ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ, "that is in Korinthos, to those sanctified in the *Mashiach* *Yeshua*" (WH, NA28, RP); the printed SBLGNT has the two phrases in the other order (Treg). The meaning is the same.
- **"with all that in every place call upon the name … both theirs and ours" → "with all who call upon the name … in every place, theirs and ours"** (v2) — Greek ἐν παντὶ τόπῳ αὐτῶν καὶ ἡμῶν. "Theirs and ours" can go with "place" (their place and ours) or with "Lord" (their Lord and ours); the Greek does not repeat "Lord", and the verse leaves it open.
- **"on your behalf" → "concerning you"; "by Jesus Christ" → "in the *Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v4) — Greek περὶ ὑμῶν; ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ.
- **"ye are enriched by him, in all utterance" → "you were made rich in him, in all word"** (v5) — Greek ἐπλουτίσθητε ἐν αὐτῷ, a past tense; λόγος is "word" throughout (v17, 18; 2:1, 4, 13; 4:19–20).
- **"the testimony of Christ" kept** (v6) — Greek τὸ μαρτύριον τοῦ Χριστοῦ, a plain genitive: the testimony about him or the testimony he gave. ἐν ὑμῖν is "in you" or "among you".
- **"ye come behind in no gift" → "you are in lack in no gift"** (v7) — Greek ὑστερεῖσθαι, as 2 Cor 11:9. χάρισμα "gift" is of the root of χάρις "grace" (v4).
- **"waiting for the coming" → "awaiting the revelation"** (v7) — Greek ἀπεκδεχομένους τὴν ἀποκάλυψιν; "coming" is παρουσία.
- **"that ye may be blameless" → "blameless"** (v8) — Greek ἀνεγκλήτους, one word joined to "you"; the Greek has no "that ye may be". "Confirm" (βεβαιόω) is the verb of v6.
- **"God is faithful, by whom" → "Faithful is *Elohim*, through whom"** (v9) — Greek πιστὸς ὁ θεὸς διʼ οὗ.
- **"I beseech you" → "I exhort you"; "by the name" → "through the name"** (v10) — Greek παρακαλῶ, in this letter "exhort" where it urges (also 4:16; 4:13 "appeal"); διὰ τοῦ ὀνόματος.
- **"that ye all speak the same thing" → "that you all say the same thing"** (v10) — Greek ἵνα τὸ αὐτὸ λέγητε; the Greek has no "agree".
- **"divisions" → "schisms"** (v10) — Greek σχίσματα, "splits, tears" (the word for a tear in a garment, Matt 9:16); a different word from "divided" in v13 (μερίζω).
- **"perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment" → "restored in the same mind and in the same opinion"** (v10) — Greek κατηρτισμένοι, "mended, fitted together, restored" (as 2 Cor 13:11; the word for mending nets, Matt 4:21); γνώμη is "opinion" (as 2 Cor 8:10).
- **"it hath been declared unto me" → "it was made clear to me"** (v11) — Greek ἐδηλώθη, the verb of 3:13.
- **"them which are of the house of Chloe" → "those of Chloe"** (v11) — Greek τῶν Χλόης; the Greek has neither "house" nor "people".
- **"contentions" → "strifes"** (v11) — Greek ἔριδες, plural of ἔρις "strife" (3:3).
- **"I am of Paul" kept** (v12) — Greek Ἐγὼ μέν εἰμι Παύλου, a plain genitive, "I belong to"; the Greek has no "follow". The verb stands only in the first saying.
- **"Is Christ divided?" → "Has the *Mashiach* been divided?"** (v13) — Greek μεμέρισται, perfect tense. The old manuscripts have no punctuation: it can also be a statement, "The *Mashiach* has been divided."
- **"for you" → "on your behalf"; "in the name" → "into the name"** (v13, 15) — Greek ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν; εἰς τὸ ὄνομα.
- **"I thank God" → "I give thanks to *Elohim*"** (v14) — the verse follows εὐχαριστῶ τῷ θεῷ (Treg, RP, NA28); the printed SBLGNT (WH, NIV) reads only εὐχαριστῶ, "I give thanks".
- **"that I had baptized in mine own name" → "that you were immersed into my name"** (v15) — Greek ἐβαπτίσθητε (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows ἐβάπτισα (RP).
- **"household" → "house"; "besides" → "for the rest"** (v16) — Greek οἶκον; λοιπόν. The Greek has no brackets.
- **"not with wisdom of words" → "not in wisdom of word"** (v17) — Greek οὐκ ἐν σοφίᾳ λόγου, singular; the Greek has no "eloquent".
- **"be made of none effect" → "be emptied"** (v17) — Greek κενωθῇ (as Phil 2:7); the Greek has no "of its power".
- **"the preaching of the cross" → "the word of the cross"** (v18) — Greek ὁ λόγος ὁ τοῦ σταυροῦ.
- **"foolishness" kept; "foolish" / "foolish things"** (v18, 20, 21, 23, 25, 27) — Greek μωρία, μωραίνω, μωρός: one root throughout chapters 1–4 (the noun "fool" in 3:18; 4:10).
- **"them that perish" → "those who are perishing"; "which are saved" → "who are being saved"** (v18) — Greek ἀπολλυμένοις, σῳζομένοις, present participles (as 2 Cor 2:15).
- **"will bring to nothing the understanding of the prudent" → "the understanding of those who understand I will set aside"** (v19) — Greek τὴν σύνεσιν τῶν συνετῶν ἀθετήσω: noun and adjective of one root; ἀθετέω is "set aside" (Gal 2:21). The quotation is given as the Greek has it.
- **"Where is the wise? where is the scribe?" → "Where is a wise man? Where is a scribe?"** (v20) — Greek ποῦ σοφός; ποῦ γραμματεύς; without articles and without a verb ("is" is supplied).
- **"the disputer of this world" → "a debater of this age"; "of this world" → "of the world"** (v20) — Greek τοῦ αἰῶνος τούτου, "age"; the second is τοῦ κόσμου (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows + τούτου, "this" (RP).
- **"by wisdom" → "through the wisdom"** (v21) — Greek διὰ τῆς σοφίας, with the article: the wisdom just named, or its own wisdom.
- **"it pleased God by the foolishness of preaching" → "*Elohim* was pleased through the foolishness of the proclamation"** (v21) — Greek εὐδόκησεν ὁ θεός; τοῦ κηρύγματος is the thing proclaimed, a noun; the Greek has no "we".
- **"For the Jews require a sign" → "Since both *Yehudim* ask for signs"** (v22) — Greek ἐπειδὴ καὶ Ἰουδαῖοι σημεῖα αἰτοῦσιν: the same "since" as v21, no articles, and σημεῖα plural (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows σημεῖον (RP). The sentence runs on through v24.
- **"we preach" → "we proclaim"** (v23) — Greek κηρύσσομεν, the verb of "proclamation" (v21); "preach the gospel" in v17 is εὐαγγελίζεσθαι.
- **"a stumblingblock" → "a stumbling block"** (v23) — Greek σκάνδαλον, the trigger of a trap: what makes one stumble or take offense (as Gal 5:11); the noun of σκανδαλίζω "make stumble".
- **"unto the Greeks foolishness" → "to nations foolishness"** (v23) — Greek ἔθνεσιν (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows Ἕλλησιν (RP).
- **"unto them which are called" → "to the called themselves"; "the power of God, and the wisdom of God" → "*Elohim's* power and *Elohim's* wisdom"** (v24) — Greek αὐτοῖς δὲ τοῖς κλητοῖς; θεοῦ δύναμιν καὶ θεοῦ σοφίαν, "of God" first both times and no articles.
- **"the foolishness of God … the weakness of God" → "the foolish thing of *Elohim* … the weak thing of *Elohim*"** (v25) — Greek τὸ μωρόν, τὸ ἀσθενές: neuter adjectives, not the nouns of v18 and 2:3.
- **"For ye see your calling" → "For look at your calling"** (v26) — Greek βλέπετε, a command or a statement; the form is the same.
- **"how that not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called" → "that there are not many wise according to flesh, not many powerful, not many well-born"** (v26) — the Greek has no verb ("there are" is supplied; the KJV supplies "are called"); κατὰ σάρκα is "according to flesh"; δυνατοί "powerful", of the root of "power" (v18, 24); εὐγενεῖς "well-born".
- **"to confound" → "that he might put to shame"; "the things which are mighty" → "the strong things"** (v27) — Greek ἵνα καταισχύνῃ; τὰ ἰσχυρά, the word of v25 ("stronger"). "The wise" is masculine (τοὺς σοφούς), the rest neuter.
- **"base things" → "the low-born things"; "yea, and things which are not, to bring to nought" → "the things that are not, that he might abolish"** (v28) — Greek τὰ ἀγενῆ, the opposite of "well-born" (v26); the printed text has no "and" before τὰ μὴ ὄντα (Treg, NA28; WH and RP have καί); καταργέω is "abolish" (also 2:6).
- **"That no flesh should glory in his presence" → "so that no flesh may boast before *Elohim*"** (v29) — Greek ὅπως μὴ καυχήσηται πᾶσα σὰρξ ἐνώπιον τοῦ θεοῦ: "all flesh … not", and "before God", not "in his presence".
- **"of him are ye" → "from him you are"** (v30) — Greek ἐξ αὐτοῦ.
- **"who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption" → "who became wisdom to us from *Elohim*, both righteousness and sanctification and redemption"** (v30) — Greek ἐγενήθη σοφία ἡμῖν ἀπὸ θεοῦ, δικαιοσύνη τε καὶ ἁγιασμὸς καὶ ἀπολύτρωσις: "wisdom" stands apart, and the three nouns joined by τε καί follow it.
- **"He that glorieth, let him glory" → "He who boasts, let him boast"** (v31) — Greek καυχάομαι, as v29. The sentence begun by "that" has no verb of its own. The Greek has κύριος, "Lord"; the wording is that of Jer 9:24, where the one to boast in is Yahweh.

### Notes
- **v19** quotes Isa 29:14.
- **v31** echoes Jer 9:24.
