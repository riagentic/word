# Job 30

[1] "And now they laugh at me, those younger than I in days,
whose fathers I rejected
to set with the dogs of my flock.

[2] Even the power of their hands — why is it to me?
On them vigor has perished.

[3] In want and in famine, barren,
those who gnaw the dry land,
yesternight, devastation and desolation;

[4] those who pluck saltwort by the shrub,
and the root of broom trees is their bread.

[5] From the midst they are driven out;
they shout over them as over the thief,

[6] to abide in the dread place of the wadis,
holes of the dust and rocks.

[7] Among the shrubs they bray;
under the nettles they are joined together.

[8] Sons of a fool, also sons of no name,
they have been smitten out of the land.

[9] And now I have become their song,
and I am to them for a byword.

[10] They abominate me, they keep far from me,
and from my face they do not withhold spit.

[11] For he has loosed my cord and afflicted me,
and the bridle from before my face they have let go.

[12] On the right hand the brood rise up;
my feet they send away,
and they cast up against me the paths of their calamity.

[13] They tear up my path;
for my ruin they are of profit;
there is no helper for them.

[14] Like a wide breach they come;
under the devastation they roll themselves on.

[15] Terrors are turned upon me;
it pursues my nobility like the wind,
and like a thick cloud my salvation has passed away.

[16] And now upon me my soul is poured out;
days of affliction take hold of me.

[17] Night pierces my bones from upon me,
and those that gnaw me do not lie down.

[18] By great power my garment disguises itself;
like the mouth of my tunic it girds me.

[19] He has thrown me into the clay,
and I am made like dust and ashes.

[20] I cry for help to you, and you do not answer me;
I stand, and you gaze at me.

[21] You are turned to be cruel to me;
with the might of your hand you bear a grudge against me.

[22] You lift me up to the wind, you make me ride;
and you melt away my sound wisdom.

[23] For I know: to death you will bring me back,
and to the house appointed for all living.

[24] Surely he does not stretch out a hand against a heap of ruins,
if in his disaster there is a cry for help for them.

[25] Did I not weep for the one hard of day?
My soul grieved for the needy.

[26] For I waited for good, and evil came;
and I hoped for light, and gloom came.

[27] My inward parts have boiled and have not been still;
days of affliction came to meet me.

[28] Darkened I go about, without the sun;
I have stood up in the assembly, I cry for help.

[29] A brother I have become to jackals,
and a friend to the daughters of the ostrich.

[30] My skin has grown black from upon me,
and my bone burns from heat.

[31] And my lyre has become mourning,
and my pipe the voice of those who weep.

## Changes

- **"But now" → "And now"** (v1, 9, 16) — Hebrew וְעַתָּה each time. **"have me in derision" → "laugh at me"** — שָׂחֲקוּ עָלַי. **"younger than I" → "younger than I in days"** — לְיָמִים "in days" is written (as 32:6). **"I would have disdained" → "I rejected"** — מָאַסְתִּי, perfect, the verb "reject". "Have" in "to have set" is not written: לָשִׁית "to set".
- **"whereto might the strength of their hands profit me, in whom old age was perished?" → "Even the power of their hands — why is it to me? On them vigor has perished"** (v2) — Hebrew גַּם "even"; כֹּחַ "power"; לָמָּה לִּי, literally "why to me", = of what use to me. כֶּלַח is found only here and in 5:26; "vigor" (full strength, or ripe age) is uncertain.
- **"For want and famine they were solitary; fleeing into the wilderness in former time desolate and waste" → "In want and in famine, barren, those who gnaw the dry land, yesternight, devastation and desolation"** (v3) — Hebrew גַּלְמוּד "barren, stony" (the word of 3:7; 15:34); הָעֹרְקִים, from עָרַק, found only here and in v17, "gnaw" (KJV "flee", from the Aramaic sense); צִיָּה "dry land". אֶמֶשׁ is elsewhere "last night" (Gen 19:34; 31:29); what it means here is uncertain (KJV "in former time"; others "gloom"). שׁוֹאָה וּמְשֹׁאָה, two forms of one root, "devastation and desolation" (Zeph 1:15).
- **"Who cut up mallows by the bushes, and juniper roots for their meat" → "those who pluck saltwort by the shrub, and the root of broom trees is their bread"** (v4) — Hebrew מַלּוּחַ, a plant named from מֶלַח "salt" (found only here; the kind is uncertain); שִׂיחַ "shrub" (singular); רְתָמִים "broom trees" (as 1 Kgs 19:4); לַחְמָם "their bread".
- **"They were driven forth from among men, (they cried after them as after a thief;)" → "From the midst they are driven out; they shout over them as over the thief"** (v5) — Hebrew גֵּו "midst" (an Aramaic word; "men" is not written); יָרִיעוּ עָלֵימוֹ "they shout over / against them"; כַּגַּנָּב "as the thief".
- **"To dwell in the clifts of the valleys, in caves of the earth, and in the rocks" → "to abide in the dread place of the wadis, holes of the dust and rocks"** (v6) — Hebrew בַּעֲרוּץ נְחָלִים, literally "in the dreaded (place) of wadis" (uncertain; KJV "clifts"); לִשְׁכֹּן, the verb שָׁכַן "abide"; חֹרֵי עָפָר "holes of dust"; כֵּפִים "rocks" (Jer 4:29).
- **"under the nettles they were gathered together" → "they are joined together"** (v7) — Hebrew חָרוּל, a weed of uncertain kind (Prov 24:31; Zeph 2:9); יְסֻפָּחוּ, pual of סָפַח "join, attach".
- **"They were children of fools, yea, children of base men: they were viler than the earth" → "Sons of a fool, also sons of no name, they have been smitten out of the land"** (v8) — Hebrew בְּנֵי־נָבָל "sons of a fool" (singular), בְּנֵי בְלִי־שֵׁם "sons of without-name"; נִכְּאוּ, nifal of נָכָא "smite", found only here: "beaten out, scourged out" of the land. KJV "viler" takes it from another root.
- **"And now am I their song, yea, I am their byword" → "And now I have become their song, and I am to them for a byword"** (v9) — Hebrew נְגִינָתָם "their song" (a song sung to strings; here a mocking song); לְמִלָּה, מִלָּה "word, utterance": what is said about him.
- **"they flee far from me, and spare not to spit in my face" → "they keep far from me, and from my face they do not withhold spit"** (v10) — Hebrew תִּעֲבוּנִי "they abominate me"; רָחֲקוּ "keep far"; וּמִפָּנַי לֹא־חָשְׂכוּ רֹק, literally "and from my face they do not withhold spit": spitting in his face, or spitting at the sight of him.
- **"Because he hath loosed my cord … they have also let loose the bridle before me" → "For he has loosed my cord and afflicted me, and the bridle from before my face they have let go"** (v11) — qere יִתְרִי "my cord"; the ketiv יתרו is "his cord" (then: "he has loosed his cord", of a bow string). The subject "he" is not named. עִנָּה "afflict"; שִׁלֵּחוּ "they let go" (piel of שָׁלַח; the same verb is "send away" in v12).
- **"Upon my right hand rise the youth; they push away my feet, and they raise up against me the ways of their destruction" → "On the right hand the brood rise up; my feet they send away, and they cast up against me the paths of their calamity"** (v12) — Hebrew עַל־יָמִין "on the right hand" ("my" is not written); פִּרְחַח, found only here, from פָּרַח "sprout": "brood" (uncertain); שִׁלֵּחוּ "send away" (as v11); סָלַל "cast up" (a raised road, a siege ramp); אָרְחוֹת אֵידָם "the paths of their calamity".
- **"They mar my path, they set forward my calamity, they have no helper" → "They tear up my path; for my ruin they are of profit; there is no helper for them"** (v13) — Hebrew נָתְסוּ, found only here (taken as נָתַץ "break down"); לְהַוָּתִי יֹעִילוּ, literally "for my ruin they profit"; לֹא עֹזֵר לָמוֹ "no helper to them": either "no one helps them" (they need no help) or "no one checks them"; the Hebrew is kept.
- **"They came upon me as a wide breaking in of waters: in the desolation they rolled themselves upon me" → "Like a wide breach they come; under the devastation they roll themselves on"** (v14) — Hebrew כְּפֶרֶץ רָחָב "like a wide breach" ("of waters" and "upon me" are not written); שֹׁאָה "devastation", the word of v3; הִתְגַּלְגָּלוּ "they roll themselves".
- **"they pursue my soul as the wind: and my welfare passeth away as a cloud" → "it pursues my nobility like the wind, and like a thick cloud my salvation has passed away"** (v15) — Hebrew הָהְפַּךְ, a singular verb with the plural בַּלָּהוֹת "terrors"; תִּרְדֹּף "it pursues" (the terror; also readable "you pursue"); נְדִבָתִי "my nobility, my dignity" (not "soul"); עָב "thick cloud"; יְשֻׁעָתִי "my salvation" (KJV "welfare").
- **"poured out upon me" → "upon me"** (v16) — Hebrew עָלַי kept. **"have taken hold upon me" → "take hold of me"** — the verb is imperfect; עֳנִי "affliction".
- **"My bones are pierced in me in the night season: and my sinews take no rest" → "Night pierces my bones from upon me, and those that gnaw me do not lie down"** (v17) — Hebrew לַיְלָה עֲצָמַי נִקַּר מֵעָלָי: נִקַּר "bore, pierce" is active, singular; its subject is "night" or an unnamed "he". וְעֹרְקַי, the participle of עָרַק "gnaw" (v3): "my gnawers", the pains (KJV "sinews"); לֹא יִשְׁכָּבוּן "do not lie down".
- **"By the great force of my disease is my garment changed: it bindeth me about as the collar of my coat" → "By great power my garment disguises itself; like the mouth of my tunic it girds me"** (v18) — Hebrew בְּרָב־כֹּחַ "by great power" ("of my disease" is not written); יִתְחַפֵּשׂ, hitpael of חָפַשׂ "disguise oneself" (1 Kgs 22:30): the garment is changed out of shape; כְּפִי כֻתָּנְתִּי "like the mouth (the neck opening) of my tunic"; יַאַזְרֵנִי "it girds me" (or "he girds me").
- **"He hath cast me into the mire, and I am become like dust and ashes" → "He has thrown me into the clay, and I am made like dust and ashes"** (v19) — Hebrew הֹרָנִי, hifil of יָרָה "throw"; חֹמֶר "clay" (the "mortar" of the brick passages, Gen 11:3; Exod 1:14; "clay" in Job, 27:16); וָאֶתְמַשֵּׁל "and I am made like".
- **"I cry unto thee, and thou dost not hear me: I stand up, and thou regardest me not" → "I cry for help to you, and you do not answer me; I stand, and you gaze at me"** (v20) — Hebrew אֲשַׁוַּע "cry for help"; תַעֲנֵנִי "answer me" (not "hear"); וַתִּתְבֹּנֶן בִּי "and you gaze at me" — the Hebrew has no "not" in the second line (KJV supplies it).
- **"Thou art become cruel to me: with thy strong hand thou opposest thyself against me" → "You are turned to be cruel to me; with the might of your hand you bear a grudge against me"** (v21) — Hebrew תֵּהָפֵךְ "you are turned" (the root of "turned" in v15); עֹצֶם "might"; תִשְׂטְמֵנִי, שָׂטַם "bear a grudge against".
- **"and dissolvest my substance" → "and you melt away my sound wisdom"** (v22) — qere תּוּשִׁיָּה "sound wisdom" (5:12; 6:13; 12:16; the sense is uncertain: sound counsel, success, lasting help); the verse has "you melt me … sound wisdom": "my" is supplied. The ketiv תשוה is often read תְּשֻׁוָּה "in the roar (of the storm)", whence "toss me about in the storm"; the qere is kept. "On it" after "ride" is not written.
- **"thou wilt bring me to death" → "to death you will bring me back"** (v23) — Hebrew תְּשִׁיבֵנִי, hifil of שׁוּב "return". **"the house appointed for all living" kept** — בֵּית מוֹעֵד, the word of "appointed time" and of "tent of meeting".
- **"Howbeit he will not stretch out his hand to the grave, though they cry in his destruction" → "Surely he does not stretch out a hand against a heap of ruins, if in his disaster there is a cry for help for them"** (v24) — the meaning of the verse is uncertain. Hebrew אַךְ "surely / only"; בְּעִי "against (or: in) a heap of ruins" (not "the grave"); בְּפִידוֹ "in his disaster"; לָהֶן "for them" (feminine) or "therefore"; שׁוּעַ "cry for help". Many read it as a question: "does not one in a heap of ruins stretch out a hand, and in his disaster cry for help?"
- **"Did not I weep for him that was in trouble? was not my soul grieved for the poor?" → "Did I not weep for the one hard of day? My soul grieved for the needy"** (v25) — Hebrew אִם־לֹא, a question in the first line only (it may also be read as an oath, "surely I wept"); לִקְשֵׁה־יוֹם "for the hard of day"; עָגְמָה, found only here, "grieved"; אֶבְיוֹן "needy".
- **"When I looked for good, then evil came unto me" → "For I waited for good, and evil came; and I hoped for light, and gloom came"** (v26) — Hebrew כִּי "for"; קִוִּיתִי "I waited for"; וַאֲיַחֲלָה "and I hoped"; אֹפֶל "gloom" (as אֲפֵלָה).
- **"My bowels boiled, and rested not: the days of affliction prevented me" → "My inward parts have boiled and have not been still; days of affliction came to meet me"** (v27) — Hebrew מֵעַי "my inward parts"; רֻתְּחוּ pual "were made to boil"; דָּמּוּ "be still"; קִדְּמֻנִי "came to meet me".
- **"I went mourning without the sun: I stood up, and I cried in the congregation" → "Darkened I go about, without the sun; I have stood up in the assembly, I cry for help"** (v28) — Hebrew קֹדֵר "dark, darkened" (of one in mourning garb or with darkened skin); חַמָּה "sun" (literally "heat"; "without heat" is also read); קָהָל "assembly"; אֲשַׁוֵּעַ "I cry for help", as v20.
- **"dragons" → "jackals"; "owls" → "the daughters of the ostrich"** (v29) — Hebrew תַּנִּים "jackals" (not תַּנִּין); בְּנוֹת יַעֲנָה "daughters of the ostrich" (the bird is not certain; "ostrich" is the common reading); רֵעַ "friend".
- **"My skin is black upon me, and my bones are burned with heat" → "My skin has grown black from upon me, and my bone burns from heat"** (v30) — Hebrew שָׁחַר מֵעָלָי "has grown black from upon me"; עַצְמִי "my bone", singular; חָרָה "burns"; חֹרֶב "heat" (as Gen 31:40).
- **"My harp also is turned to mourning, and my organ into the voice of them that weep" → "And my lyre has become mourning, and my pipe the voice of those who weep"** (v31) — Hebrew וַיְהִי לְאֵבֶל "and it became mourning"; כִּנּוֹר "lyre"; עוּגָב "pipe" (a wind instrument; the kind is uncertain).

### Notes
- **Reversal of ch 29** — total collapse of Job's honor.
- **Mocked by the dregs** — those whose fathers he would not set with his dogs now laugh at him.
- **Outcasts of the dry land** (v3–8) — they live in wadis and holes, on saltwort and broom roots.
- **The one who afflicts** (v11, 19–23) — "he" in v11 and v19, "you" from v20: Job turns to address him directly.
- **"Jackals and the daughters of the ostrich"** — creatures of the desert, known for their cries.
- **Musical instruments turned to lament** — whole life inverted.
