## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"I abhor myself" → "I recant"** (v6) — Hebrew *em'as*; "I reject [what I said]" — object implicit. Modern scholarship often prefers this over self-loathing reading.
- **"captivity" → "fortunes"** (v10) — *shevut*; reversal of circumstances.
- **"Jemima / Kezia / Keren-happuch"** — poetic name-meanings: dove, cassia (perfume spice), horn of antimony (eye-makeup). All beauty/feminine names.

### Notes
- **Job's surrender** (v2-6) — recognizes transcendence, recants his claims.
- **"I had heard... now my eye sees"** (v5) — transformation from hearsay theology to direct encounter.
- **Yahweh vindicates Job against friends** (v7) — surprise twist: Job, despite his accusations, spoke "rightly"; the friends, despite their orthodoxy, did not.
- **Sacrifice through Job** — friends restored to favor via Job's intercession. Reconciliation pattern.
- **Double restoration** (v10) — twice the flocks/property.
- **Seven sons, three daughters** — same count as beginning; children not literally doubled (as property was). The daughters uniquely named; they inherit equally with sons (unusual legally). Only daughters' beauty noted — dignified honor.
- **Full life** — 140 years, four generations. Classic patriarchal closure.
- **Theological conclusion** — The book does not explain suffering; it presents Yahweh's engaged sovereignty and Job's repentance. Mystery remains, but relationship is restored.
