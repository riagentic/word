# Job 20

[1] Then ⟦zophar|Tzofar|Zophar⟧ the ⟦naamathite|Naamati|Naamathite⟧ answered and said:

[2] "Therefore my disquieting thoughts answer me,
because of my inward agitation.

[3] I hear a rebuke that insults me,
and the spirit of my understanding answers me.

[4] Do you not know this from of old,
since man was placed on earth,

[5] that the rejoicing of the wicked is brief,
the joy of the godless but a moment?

[6] Though his loftiness mount to the heavens
and his head reach to the clouds,

[7] he will perish forever like his own dung;
those who have seen him will say, 'Where is he?'

[8] He flies away like a dream and is not found;
he is chased away like a vision of the night.

[9] The eye that saw him sees him no more;
his place no longer beholds him.

[10] His sons seek the favor of the poor;
his hands restore his wealth.

[11] His bones are full of his youthful vigor,
but it will lie down with him in the dust.

[12] Though evil is sweet in his mouth
and he hides it under his tongue,

[13] though he is loath to let it go
and keeps it still in his mouth,

[14] yet his food in his bowels is turned;
it is the venom of cobras within him.

[15] He swallows down riches, and vomits them up again;
⟦el|El|God⟧ drives them out of his belly.

[16] He sucks the poison of cobras;
the viper's fangs kill him.

[17] He shall not look upon the streams,
rivers flowing with honey and curds.

[18] He will restore his gain and not swallow it down;
his restitution shall match his wealth,
and he will have no joy of it.

[19] For he has crushed and abandoned the poor;
he has seized a house that he did not build.

[20] Because he knew no quiet in his belly,
he will save nothing of his desires.

[21] Nothing was left of what he devoured;
therefore his prosperity will not last.

[22] In his full sufficiency, distress will come upon him;
all the force of misery will come on him.

[23] To fill his belly to the full,
⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will send his burning wrath upon him
and rain it down on him as his food.

[24] He will flee from an iron weapon;
a bronze arrow will pierce him through.

[25] It is drawn out, it comes from his back;
the glittering point comes out of his liver;
terrors are upon him.

[26] Total darkness is laid up for his treasures;
a fire not fanned will consume him;
what is left in his tent will be devoured.

[27] The heavens will reveal his iniquity;
the earth will rise up against him.

[28] The possessions of his house will be carried off,
dragged away in the day of his wrath.

[29] This is the portion of the wicked man from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,
the heritage decreed for him by ⟦el|El|God⟧."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"God" → "El"** (v15, 29) — Hebrew אֵל *El*, not *Elohim*.
- **"hypocrite" → "godless"** (v5) — *chanef*.
- **"asps" → "cobras"** (v14, 16) — *petanim*.
- **"bow of steel" → "bronze arrow"** (v24) — *qeshet nechushah*; bronze bow (steel unknown at composition date).
- **"secret places" → "treasures"** (v26) — *tzafun*.
- **"gall" → "liver"** (v25) — *merorah*.

### Notes
- **Zophar's second speech** — full of traditional imprecations on the wicked.
- **Dream imagery** (v8) — wicked man as fleeting, like a dream.
- **Food turned to poison** (v14-16) — evil-enjoyed becomes internal torment.
- **Rivers of honey-curds denied** (v17) — covenant-land blessings withheld.
- **Indirect charge** — Zophar clearly casting Job as the wicked man.
