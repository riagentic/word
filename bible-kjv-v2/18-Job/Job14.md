# Job 14

[1] "Man born of woman is of few days
and full of trouble.

[2] He comes forth like a flower and withers;
he flees like a shadow and does not last.

[3] Yet you fix your eyes on such a one
and bring him into judgment with you!

[4] Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean?
No one.

[5] Since his days are determined,
and the number of his months is with you,
and you have appointed his bounds that he cannot pass,

[6] look away from him that he may rest,
till he shall accomplish, as a hired laborer, his day.

[7] For there is hope for a tree —
if it is cut down, it will sprout again;
its fresh shoots will not fail.

[8] Though its root grows old in the earth
and its stock dies in the ground,

[9] at the scent of water it will bud
and put forth branches like a young plant.

[10] But man dies and is laid low;
man breathes his last, and where is he?

[11] As waters fail from a lake
and a river wastes away and dries up,

[12] so man lies down and does not rise again;
till the heavens are no more, they will not awake,
nor be roused out of their sleep.

[13] Oh that you would hide me in Sheol,
that you would conceal me until your wrath is past,
that you would appoint me a set time and remember me!

[14] If a man dies, shall he live again?
All the days of my hard service I would wait,
till my renewal should come.

[15] You would call, and I would answer you;
you would long for the work of your hands.

[16] But now you number my steps;
do you not keep watch over my sin?

[17] My transgression is sealed up in a bag;
you plaster over my iniquity.

[18] But a falling mountain crumbles,
and a rock is moved from its place;

[19] the waters wear away the stones;
the floods wash away the soil of the earth;
so you destroy the hope of man.

[20] You prevail forever against him, and he passes;
you change his countenance and send him away.

[21] His sons come to honor, and he does not know;
they are brought low, and he does not perceive it.

[22] Only his own flesh feels pain,
and his own soul mourns for himself."

## Changes

- **"grave" → "Sheol"** (v13).
- **"appointed time" → "hard service"** (v14) — *tzava*.
- **"change" → "renewal"** (v14) — *chalifah*.
- **"sewest up" → "plaster over"** (v17) — *tiphlu*; covering-over.

### Notes
- **Human mortality meditation** — brief life, inevitable death.
- **Tree vs man** — tree has hope of regrowth; *adam* once dead lies down permanently.
- **"If a man die, shall he live again?"** (v14) — fleeting question; immediately answered by Job's hope of being hidden, remembered, and renewed. First glimmer of resurrection-hope in Job.
- **"You would long for the work of your hands"** (v15) — touching: the Creator-creature bond reconstituted.
- **Waterworn stones** (v19) — slow erosion; Job's hope ground down.
- **Posthumous unawareness** (v21) — Sheol is not observation post for the living world.
