# Job 33

[1] "But now, O Job, hear my speech;
give ear to all my words.

[2] Behold, I open my mouth;
my tongue speaks in my palate.

[3] My words come from my upright heart;
my lips speak knowledge sincerely.

[4] The spirit of *Elohim* has made me,
the breath of Shaddai gives me life.

[5] If you can, answer me;
set your words in order before me; take your stand.

[6] Behold, I am toward *Elohim* as you are;
I too was pinched from clay.

[7] Behold, no terror of me need frighten you;
my pressure shall not be heavy on you.

[8] Surely you have spoken in my hearing,
and I have heard the sound of your words:

[9] 'I am pure, without transgression;
I am clean, and there is no iniquity in me.

[10] Behold, he finds occasions against me;
he counts me as his enemy.

[11] He puts my feet in the stocks;
he watches all my paths.'

[12] Behold, in this you are not right; I answer you:
Eloah is greater than man.

[13] Why do you contend with him,
that he does not answer any of his words?

[14] For *Elohim* speaks once,
twice, yet man does not perceive it.

[15] In a dream, a vision of the night,
when deep sleep falls on men,
while they slumber on their beds —

[16] then he opens the ears of men
and seals their instruction,

[17] that he may turn man aside from his deed,
and hide pride from man —

[18] keeping back his soul from the pit,
his life from passing away by the sword.

[19] He is chastened also with pain on his bed,
and with continual strife in his bones,

[20] so that his life makes bread disgusting to him,
and his soul favored food.

[21] His flesh wastes away so that it cannot be seen;
his bones, once hidden, stick out.

[22] His soul draws near the pit,
his life to those who bring death.

[23] If there is a Messenger for him, an interpreter,
one among a thousand,
to declare to man what is right for him —

[24] and he is gracious to him, and says,
'Deliver him from going down to the pit;
I have found a ransom' —

[25] his flesh becomes fresher than a child's;
he returns to the days of his youthful vigor.

[26] He prays to Eloah, and he accepts him;
he sees his face with shouts of joy;
he restores to man his righteousness.

[27] He sings before men, saying,
'I sinned, and perverted what was right,
but it was not repaid to me.

[28] He has redeemed my soul from going into the pit,
and my life shall see the light.'

[29] Behold, all these things *Elohim* does —
twice, three times with a man —

[30] to bring back his soul from the pit,
that he may be enlightened with the light of the living.

[31] Pay attention, O Job; listen to me;
be silent, and I will speak.

[32] If you have anything to say, answer me;
speak, for I desire to justify you.

[33] If not, listen to me;
be silent, and I will teach you wisdom."

## Changes

- **"I am formed out of the clay" → "I too was pinched from clay"** (v6) — Hebrew *qoratzti*; pinched off as from a lump.

### Notes
- **Elihu's first speech proper** — promises to "justify" Job if he can defend himself.
- **Human equality** (v6) — Elihu stands as Job's equal, not on divine authority's side like the friends.
- **God speaks multiple ways** (v14-22) — dreams, then pain. Instructive purposes.
- **Role of the *melitz*** (v23-28) — mediator, interpreter, bringing ransom. Notable theological concept: the *mal'ak*-*melitz* who rescues.
- **Chastening discipline** — suffering as teaching, not mere retribution.
- **Third speaker** distinct from earlier three in theology: offers suffering as pedagogy and mediation.
