# Job 33

[1] "And yet hear, please, ⟦job|Iyov|Job⟧, my utterances,
and give ear to all my words.

[2] Behold now, I have opened my mouth;
my tongue has spoken in my palate.

[3] My sayings are the uprightness of my heart,
and the knowledge of my lips they utter purely.

[4] The spirit of ⟦el|El|God⟧ has made me,
and the breath of ⟦shaddai|Shaddai|the Almighty⟧ gives me life.

[5] If you are able, answer me;
arrange before me, take your stand.

[6] Behold, I am like you toward ⟦el|El|God⟧;
from clay I too was pinched off.

[7] Behold, my dread shall not terrify you,
and my pressure shall not be heavy on you.

[8] Surely you have said in my ears,
and I heard the voice of utterances:

[9] 'Pure am I, without transgression; clean am I,
and I have no iniquity.

[10] Behold, he finds oppositions against me;
he reckons me as his enemy.

[11] He puts my feet in the stocks;
he keeps all my paths.'

[12] Behold, in this you are not righteous; I will answer you:
for ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧ is greater than mortal man.

[13] Why have you contended against him,
that he does not answer any of his words?

[14] For ⟦el|El|God⟧ speaks once,
and twice; he does not watch it.

[15] In a dream, a vision of the night,
when deep sleep falls on men,
in slumberings on the bed,

[16] then he uncovers the ear of men,
and on their discipline he seals,

[17] to remove man from a deed,
and pride from a mighty man he covers.

[18] He withholds his soul from the pit,
and his life from passing over by the missile.

[19] And he is reproved with pain on his bed,
and the multitude of his bones is enduring.

[20] And his life makes bread loathsome to him,
and his soul food of craving.

[21] His flesh is consumed out of sight,
and laid bare are his bones that were not seen.

[22] And his soul draws near to the pit,
and his life to those who put to death.

[23] If there is over him a messenger, an interpreter,
one from a thousand,
to tell to man his uprightness,

[24] and he shows him favor and says,
'Ransom him from going down to the pit;
I have found a ransom,'

[25] his flesh grows fresher than youth;
he returns to the days of his youthful vigor.

[26] He pleads with ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧, and he is pleased with him,
and he sees his face with shouting,
and he returns to mortal man his righteousness.

[27] He sings to men and says,
'I have sinned, and what was upright I perverted,
and it was not equal to me.'

[28] He has ransomed his soul from passing over into the pit,
and his life shall look on the light.

[29] Behold, all these ⟦el|El|God⟧ works,
twice, three times, with a man,

[30] to bring back his soul from the pit,
to be lighted with the light of life.

[31] Give heed, ⟦job|Iyov|Job⟧; listen to me;
be silent, and I will speak.

[32] If there are utterances, answer me;
speak, for I delight to justify you.

[33] If not, you, listen to me;
be silent, and I will teach you wisdom."

## Changes

- **"God" → "El"** (v4, 6, 14, 29) — Hebrew אֵל *El*, not *Elohim*. **"God" → "Eloah"** (v12, 26) — Hebrew אֱלוֹהַּ. **"the Almighty" → "Shaddai"** (v4) — Hebrew שַׁדַּי; the meaning of the name is not certain.
- **Tenses** (throughout) — the poem mixes the Hebrew perfect, the imperfect and the form with וַ; they are given as English present or past by the sense, and each וְ / וַ at the head of a line is kept as "and".
- **"Wherefore, Job, I pray thee, hear my speeches" → "And yet hear, please, Iyov, my utterances"** (v1) — Hebrew וְאוּלָם "and yet, however"; נָא "please"; מִלָּה "utterance" (Aramaic-like word frequent in Job, beside דָּבָר "word"; so v8, 32 and in Job 34–35).
- **"my tongue hath spoken in my mouth" → "in my palate"** (v2) — Hebrew בְחִכִּי, "in my palate". הִנֵּה־נָא "behold now".
- **"My words shall be of the uprightness of my heart: and my lips shall utter knowledge clearly" → "My sayings are the uprightness of my heart, and the knowledge of my lips they utter purely"** (v3) — Hebrew יֹשֶׁר־לִבִּי אֲמָרָי, a clause without a verb, literally "the uprightness of my heart — my sayings" ("are" supplied); אֵמֶר "saying". דַעַת שְׂפָתַי "the knowledge of my lips" is the object; בָּרוּר "purely" (root בָּרַר "purify").
- **"set thy words in order before me, stand up" → "arrange before me, take your stand"** (v5) — Hebrew עֶרְכָה with no object ("your words" or "your case" is understood, not written); הִתְיַצָּבָה "take your stand". הֲשִׁיבֵנִי "answer me" is literally "make return to me".
- **"I am according to thy wish in God's stead" → "I am like you toward El"** (v6) — Hebrew כְפִיךָ לָאֵל, literally "as your mouth, to El"; כְּפִי "according to the mouth of" is "like, as". KJV's "according to thy wish in God's stead" reads "mouth" as "word, wish" and supplies "stead". **"formed out of the clay" → "from clay I too was pinched off"** — Hebrew קֹרַצְתִּי, "nipped, pinched off" as from a lump; חֹמֶר "clay" (the potter's clay, not the builder's mortar of the Exodus row).
- **"my terror … neither shall my hand be heavy" → "my dread … my pressure"** (v7) — Hebrew אֵמָתִי "my dread" (dread of me); אַכְפִּי is found only here, "my pressure" (uncertain; others read כַּפִּי "my palm, hand", as KJV "my hand"; MT kept).
- **"in mine hearing … the voice of thy words, saying" → "in my ears … the voice of utterances"** (v8) — Hebrew בְאָזְנָי; קוֹל מִלִּין, "a voice of utterances" without "your"; no "saying".
- **"I am clean … I am innocent; neither is there iniquity in me" → "Pure am I … clean am I, and I have no iniquity"** (v9) — Hebrew זַךְ "pure"; חַף, only here, "clean" (lit. "washed off, scraped clean"; uncertain); וְלֹא עָוֹן לִי "and there is no iniquity to me". The lines follow the accents: the main break falls after חַף אָנֹכִי "clean am I".
- **"occasions against me … counteth me for his enemy" → "oppositions against me … reckons me as his enemy"** (v10) — Hebrew תְּנוּאוֹת, "oppositions" (as Num 14:34; uncertain: "pretexts"); חָשַׁב "reckon".
- **"he marketh all my paths" → "he keeps all my paths"** (v11) — Hebrew יִשְׁמֹר, "keep, guard": here keeping a close eye on.
- **"in this thou art not just" → "in this you are not righteous"** (v12) — Hebrew לֹא־צָדַקְתָּ; "in" is supplied for זֹאת "this". **"man" → "mortal man"** — Hebrew אֱנוֹשׁ (also v26). יִרְבֶּה "is great, is much" with מִן "than".
- **"for he giveth not account of any of his matters" → "that he does not answer any of his words"** (v13) — Hebrew כִּי כָל־דְּבָרָיו לֹא יַעֲנֶה, literally "that all his words he does not answer"; "his" may be God's words or man's: left open. כִּי may also be "for".
- **"yea twice, yet man perceiveth it not" → "and twice; he does not watch it"** (v14) — Hebrew בְאַחַת … וּבִשְׁתַּיִם, literally "in one … and in two" ("once … twice", or "in one way … in two"); לֹא יְשׁוּרֶנָּה "he does not watch it": "man" is not written; the subject may be the man who is spoken to, or El.
- **"Then he openeth the ears of men, and sealeth their instruction" → "then he uncovers the ear of men, and on their discipline he seals"** (v16) — Hebrew יִגְלֶה אֹזֶן "uncovers the ear" (to reveal something); וּבְמֹסָרָם יַחְתֹּם "and on (in) their discipline he seals". מֹסָר is taken as מוּסָר "discipline" (as the pointing has it); others take it from מוֹסֵר "bond".
- **"That he may withdraw man from his purpose, and hide pride from man" → "to remove man from a deed, and pride from a mighty man he covers"** (v17) — Hebrew לְהָסִיר אָדָם מַעֲשֶׂה, literally "to remove man — a deed" ("from" is supplied; many read מִמַּעֲשֵׂהוּ "from his deed"; MT kept). אָדָם "man" and גֶּבֶר "mighty man" stand apart; כִּסָּה "cover".
- **"He keepeth back his soul … and his life from perishing by the sword" → "He withholds his soul … from passing over by the missile"** (v18) — Hebrew יַחְשֹׂךְ "withhold"; עָבַר "pass over" (= perish); שֶׁלַח "missile, weapon" (uncertain; also taken as "channel", a stream of the dead). שַׁחַת "pit" (also v22, 24, 28, 30).
- **"He is chastened also … and the multitude of his bones with strong pain" → "And he is reproved … and the multitude of his bones is enduring"** (v19) — Hebrew וְהוּכַח, the passive of הוֹכִיחַ "reprove". The qere וְרוֹב is "and the multitude"; the ketiv ריב is "and the strife (of his bones)". אֵתָן "enduring, constant"; with the qere the line is hard ("all his bones are still firm", or "constant in pain"): left as it stands.
- **"his life abhorreth bread, and his soul dainty meat" → "his life makes bread loathsome to him, and his soul food of desire"** (v20) — Hebrew זִהֲמַתּוּ (only here), "makes it loathsome to him"; מַאֲכַל תַּאֲוָה "food of craving" (תַּאֲוָה, what the appetite craves, here without blame; = dainty food).
- **"his bones that were not seen stick out" → "laid bare are his bones that were not seen"** (v21) — qere וְשֻׁפּוּ "and they are laid bare" (ketiv ושפי, a noun "bareness"). יִכֶל מֵרֹאִי "is consumed from sight".
- **"to the destroyers" → "to those who put to death"** (v22) — Hebrew לַמְמִתִים, "to those who cause death"; some read לְמֵתִים "to the dead" (MT kept).
- **"If there be a messenger with him, an interpreter, one among a thousand, to shew unto man his uprightness" → "If there is over him a messenger, an interpreter, one from a thousand, to tell to man his uprightness"** (v23) — word for word. Hebrew עָלָיו "over him, upon him" (also "for him, on his behalf"); מַלְאָךְ "messenger" (the word for "angel": who is meant is not said); מֵלִיץ "interpreter" (as Gen 42:23; also "spokesman, go-between"); יָשְׁרוֹ "his uprightness": whose — the man's or the messenger's — is not said; "what is right for him" (earlier wording) was an explanation and is removed.
- **"Then he is gracious unto him, and saith, Deliver him from going down to the pit: I have found a ransom" → "and he shows him favor and says, 'Ransom him … I have found a ransom'"** (v24) — word for word. Hebrew וַיְחֻנֶּנּוּ "and he shows him favor" (חָנַן); the speaker is not named. פְּדָעֵהוּ is an unusual form with ע: taken from פָּדָה "ransom" (the verb of v28), uncertain (KJV "deliver"). כֹּפֶר "ransom" (the price that covers a life) is another root than פָּדָה; English has one word for both (CZ keeps them apart).
- **"fresher than a child's … the days of his youth" → "fresher than youth … his youthful vigor"** (v25) — Hebrew רֻטֲפַשׁ, only here, "grow fresh, plump" (uncertain); מִנֹּעַר "than youth"; עֲלוּמִים "youthful vigor".
- **"He shall pray unto God, and he will be favourable unto him: and he shall see his face with joy: for he will render unto man his righteousness" → "He pleads with Eloah, and he is pleased with him, and he sees his face with shouting, and he returns to mortal man his righteousness"** (v26) — Hebrew עָתַר "plead with"; רָצָה "be pleased with"; תְּרוּעָה "shouting"; וַיָּשֶׁב "and he returns" (KJV "for" is "and"). Who sees whose face is not said.
- **"He looketh upon men, and if any say, I have sinned, and perverted that which was right, and it profited me not" → "He sings to men and says, 'I have sinned, and what was upright I perverted, and it was not equal to me'"** (v27) — Hebrew יָשֹׁר, taken from שׁוּר "sing" (KJV from שׁוּר "look, watch", the verb of v14; the form fits both); "if any" is not in the Hebrew: וַיֹּאמֶר "and says". וְלֹא־שָׁוָה לִי "and it was not equal to me": it was not paid back to me in full, or it was not worth it to me.
- **"He will deliver his soul from going into the pit" → "He has ransomed his soul from passing over into the pit"** (v28) — qere נַפְשׁוֹ … וְחַיָּתוֹ "his soul … his life"; the ketiv has "my soul … my life", which keeps the man speaking. פָּדָה "ransom" (perfect). בָּאוֹר תִּרְאֶה "shall look on the light".
- **"Lo, all these things worketh God oftentimes with man" → "Behold, all these El works, twice, three times, with a man"** (v29) — Hebrew פַּעֲמַיִם שָׁלוֹשׁ "two times, three"; גֶּבֶר "man" (it stands alone here; "mighty man" only in v17, beside אָדָם).
- **"the light of the living" → "the light of life"** (v30) — Hebrew בְּאוֹר הַחַיִּים, "life" or "the living". לֵאוֹר "to be lighted".
- **"Mark well … hearken unto me: hold thy peace" → "Give heed … listen to me; be silent"** (v31, 33) — Hebrew הַקְשֵׁב; שְׁמַע־לִי; הַחֲרֵשׁ. In v33 אַתָּה "you" stands before the verb.
- **"If thou hast any thing to say" → "If there are utterances"** (v32) — Hebrew אִם־יֵשׁ־מִלִּין. **"I desire to justify thee" → "I delight to justify you"** — Hebrew חָפַצְתִּי.
- **"I shall teach thee wisdom"** (v33) — Hebrew אֲאַלֶּפְךָ, from אִלֵּף "teach" (a word of Job and Proverbs; הוֹרָה and לִמֵּד are also "teach").

### Notes
- **Elihu's first speech proper** — he offers to "justify" Job if Job can answer (v32).
- **Human equality** (v6) — Elihu stands as Job's equal: both pinched off from clay.
- **El speaks in more than one way** (v14–22) — dreams, then pain on the sick bed.
- **The messenger and the interpreter** (v23–24) — the Hebrew does not say who the messenger is, nor who says "Ransom him"; the verse is left as it stands.
