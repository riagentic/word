# Job 1

[1] There was a man in the land of Uz whose name was Job. That man was blameless and upright, one who feared *Elohim* and turned away from evil.

[2] Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

[3] His livestock was seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred female donkeys — with a very large household. This man was the greatest of all the children of the east.

[4] His sons would go and feast in their houses, each on his day; they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them.

[5] When the days of feasting had run their course, Job would send and sanctify them; he would rise early in the morning and offer burnt offerings according to the number of them all. For Job said, "Perhaps my sons have sinned and cursed *Elohim* in their hearts." Thus Job did continually.

[6] Now there was a day when the sons of *Elohim* came to present themselves before Yahweh; the adversary also came among them.

[7] Yahweh said to the adversary, "From where do you come?" the adversary answered Yahweh, "From roaming the earth and walking up and down on it."

[8] Yahweh said to the adversary, "Have you considered my servant Job? For there is none like him on earth — a blameless and upright man, one who fears *Elohim* and turns away from evil."

[9] Then the adversary answered Yahweh, "Does Job fear *Elohim* for nothing?

[10] Have you not put a hedge around him, around his house, and around all that he has, on every side? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his livestock has spread out in the land.

[11] But stretch out your hand now and touch all that he has, and he will surely curse you to your face."

[12] Yahweh said to the adversary, "Behold, all that he has is in your power; only against him do not stretch out your hand." So the adversary went out from the presence of Yahweh.

[13] There was a day when his sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother's house,

[14] and a messenger came to Job and said, "The oxen were plowing and the donkeys were feeding beside them,

[15] when the Sabeans fell upon them and took them away; they struck down the young men with the edge of the sword. I alone have escaped to tell you."

[16] While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The fire of *Elohim* fell from heaven and burned up the sheep and the young men, and consumed them. I alone have escaped to tell you."

[17] While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The Chaldeans formed three raiding bands, made a raid on the camels, and took them; they struck down the young men with the edge of the sword. I alone have escaped to tell you."

[18] While he was still speaking, another came and said, "Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother's house,

[19] and behold, a great wind came across the wilderness and struck the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young men, and they are dead. I alone have escaped to tell you."

[20] Then Job arose, tore his robe, shaved his head, fell on the ground, and worshiped.

[21] He said:

"Naked I came from my mother's womb,
and naked I shall return there.
Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away.
Blessed be the name of Yahweh."

[22] In all this Job did not sin, nor did he charge *Elohim* with wrong.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"eschewed / escheweth" → "turned away from / turns away from"** (v1, 8).
- **"sons of God" → "sons of *Elohim*"** (v6) — Hebrew *bene ha-Elohim*; heavenly council members.
- **"charged God foolishly" → "charged *Elohim* with wrong"** (v22) — *tiflah*; unfitting speech.

### Notes
- **Uz** — location uncertain; Edom/Arabia area. Job is a non-Israelite patriarch, set vaguely in patriarchal era.
- **Prose prologue (1-2)** + poetic dialogues (3-41) + prose epilogue (42). Sandwich structure.
- ***Ha-satan*** — in Job, not yet the developed "Satan" figure of intertestamental and NT. He's a role in the divine council — the "adversary/prosecutor." Acts with Yahweh's permission throughout.
- **Euphemism of "bless" for "curse"** — scribal practice: unwilling to write *qallel* ("curse") in relation to *Elohim*. *Berakh* substituted.
- **Seven / three pairing** (sons/daughters; sheep/camels thousands) — complete/perfect numbers.
- **"Fire of *Elohim* from heaven"** (v16) — lightning attributed to divine source.
- **Four disasters, four messengers** — symmetrical catastrophe.
- **"Naked I came... Yahweh gave, Yahweh has taken"** (v21) — foundational wisdom-hymn; invoked in countless funerals.
