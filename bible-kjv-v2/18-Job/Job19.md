# Job 19

[1] Then Job answered and said:

[2] "How long will you torment my soul
and break me in pieces with words?

[3] These ten times you have reproached me;
you are not ashamed to wrong me.

[4] And even if it is true that I have erred,
my error remains with me alone.

[5] If indeed you magnify yourselves against me
and make my disgrace an argument against me,

[6] know then that Eloah has wronged me
and closed his net around me.

[7] Behold, I cry 'Violence!' but I am not answered;
I cry aloud, but there is no justice.

[8] He has walled up my way so that I cannot pass,
and he has set darkness upon my paths.

[9] He has stripped my glory from me
and taken the crown from my head.

[10] He breaks me down on every side, and I am gone;
my hope he has uprooted like a tree.

[11] His wrath is kindled against me;
he counts me as one of his adversaries.

[12] His troops come together;
they raise up their way against me,
and encamp around my tent.

[13] He has put my brothers far from me;
my acquaintances are entirely estranged from me.

[14] My kinsmen have failed me;
my intimate friends have forgotten me.

[15] Those who dwell in my house and my maidservants
regard me as a stranger;
I have become a foreigner in their sight.

[16] I summon my servant, and he gives me no answer;
I must beg him with my mouth.

[17] My breath is repulsive to my wife;
I am loathsome to the sons of my own mother.

[18] Even young children despise me;
when I rise, they speak against me.

[19] All my intimate friends abhor me;
those whom I loved have turned against me.

[20] My bones cling to my skin and to my flesh;
I have escaped with the skin of my teeth.

[21] Have pity on me, have pity on me, O my friends,
for the hand of Eloah has struck me!

[22] Why do you, like *Elohim*, pursue me?
Why are you not satisfied with my flesh?

[23] Oh that my words were now written!
Oh that they were inscribed in a scroll —

[24] engraved with an iron pen and lead
in the rock forever!

[25] For I know that my redeemer — lives,
and at the last he shall stand upon the earth;

[26] and after my skin has been destroyed thus,
yet in my flesh I shall see Eloah,

[27] whom I shall see for myself,
whom my eyes shall behold, and not another.
My inmost being is consumed within me.

[28] If you say, 'How we will pursue him,
since the root of the matter is in him,'

[29] be afraid of the sword for yourselves,
for wrath brings the punishments of the sword,
that you may know there is judgment."

## Changes

- **"the children of mine own body" → "the sons of my own mother"** (v17) — Hebrew *benei vitni*; literally "sons of my belly," figuratively his full-brothers / household.
- **"Why... are not satisfied with my flesh?" → "Why are you not satisfied with my flesh?"** (v22) — cannibalistic pursuit imagery.
- **"in my flesh shall I see God" → "in my flesh I shall see Eloah"** (v26) — Hebrew ambiguous: *mi-bsari* — "from my flesh" / "apart from my flesh" / "in my flesh." Preposition allows multiple readings; traditional rendering retained.

### Notes
- **Social death** — family, servants, friends all abandon Job.
- **"Skin of my teeth"** (v20) — only the gums covering his teeth remain — phrase has entered English proverb.
- **Famous "Redeemer" passage** (v25-27) — most celebrated in Job. *Go'el* is legal kinsman-advocate. Job affirms: some defender lives who will establish him. Christian tradition reads messianically (Handel's Messiah).
- **"Whether after my skin"** — whether this is resurrection hope, or vindication before death, is debated. The phrase is ambiguous in Hebrew. Either way, Job confidently expects vindication.
- **"In my flesh I shall see Eloah"** — whether in the flesh or apart from it, direct vision of God is the climax. Deeply personal: "my eyes... and not another."
- **Inscription wish** (v23-24) — Job wants his case preserved on rock forever. Ironically fulfilled by the biblical book itself.
- **Warns friends** (v28-29) — their false judgment will bring judgment on them. Prefigures the epilogue.
