# Job 5

[1] "Call, please: is there one who answers you?
And to which of the holy ones will you turn?

[2] For the fool vexation kills,
and the simple one jealousy puts to death.

[3] I, I have seen a fool taking root,
and I laid a curse on his habitation suddenly.

[4] His sons are far from salvation,
and they are crushed in the gate, and there is no deliverer.

[5] Whose harvest the hungry eats,
and even out of the thorns he takes it,
and the snare pants after their wealth.

[6] For trouble does not go out from the dust,
and from the ground toil does not spring up;

[7] for man is born to toil,
and the sons of flame fly high.

[8] But as for me, I would seek ⟦el|El|God⟧,
and to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ I would set my cause,

[9] who does great things, and there is no searching,
wonderful deeds till there is no number,

[10] who gives rain on the face of the earth,
and sends waters on the face of the open country,

[11] to set the lowly on high,
and the darkened are set high in salvation.

[12] He makes void the devices of the shrewd,
and their hands do not achieve success.

[13] He captures the wise in their shrewdness,
and the counsel of the twisted is hastened.

[14] By day they meet darkness,
and as in the night they feel about at noon.

[15] And he saves from the sword, from their mouth,
and from the hand of the strong, the needy.

[16] And there is hope for the poor,
and injustice shuts its mouth.

[17] Behold, happy is the mortal man whom ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧ reproves,
and the discipline of ⟦shaddai|Shaddai|the Almighty⟧ do not reject.

[18] For he causes pain, and he binds up;
he strikes through, and his hands heal.

[19] In six distresses he will deliver you,
and in seven evil shall not touch you.

[20] In famine he ransoms you from death,
and in war from the hands of the sword.

[21] From the whip of the tongue you shall be hidden,
and you shall not fear devastation when it comes.

[22] At devastation and at dearth you shall laugh,
and of the living things of the earth do not be afraid.

[23] For with the stones of the field is your covenant,
and the living things of the field are made to be at peace with you.

[24] And you shall know that your tent is ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧,
and you shall visit your habitation and shall not miss.

[25] And you shall know that your seed is many,
and your offspring as the plants of the earth.

[26] You shall come in vigor to the grave,
as stacked grain goes up in its time.

[27] Behold, this — we have examined it; so it is.
Hear it, and you, know it for yourself."

## Changes

- **"God … God" → "El … Elohim"** (v8) — Hebrew אֵל *El*, then אֱלֹהִים *Elohim*; **"God" → "Eloah"**, **"the Almighty" → "Shaddai"** (v17) — Hebrew אֱלוֹהַּ, שַׁדַּי. The four names stand as written.
- **"Call now, if there be any that will answer thee; and to which of the saints wilt thou turn?" → "Call, please: is there one who answers you? And to which of the holy ones will you turn?"** (v1) — Hebrew קְרָא־נָא; הֲיֵשׁ עוֹנֶךָּ "is there one answering you?"; קְדֹשִׁים "holy ones" (who they are is not said).
- **"For wrath killeth the foolish man, and envy slayeth the silly one" → "For the fool vexation kills, and the simple one jealousy puts to death"** (v2) — Hebrew כִּי־לֶאֱוִיל יַהֲרָג־כָּעַשׂ: the object stands first, marked by לְ, and the verse keeps that order (nothing supplied). כַּעַשׂ "vexation" (the word Job takes up in 6:2); הָרַג "kill", הֵמִית "put to death"; קִנְאָה "jealousy". אֱוִיל "fool"; פֹּתֶה "simple one" (one who is easily enticed).
- **"I have seen the foolish taking root" → "I, I have seen a fool taking root"** (v3) — Hebrew אֲנִי־רָאִיתִי, the pronoun written beside the verb (as 6:24). **"but suddenly I cursed his habitation" → "and I laid a curse on his habitation suddenly"** — Hebrew וָאֶקּוֹב, the verb of Num 23:8 (קָבַב "lay a curse on", beside אָרַר "curse"); "but" is not written. נָוֶה "habitation" (lit. "pasture, abode"; so v24).
- **"His children are far from safety … neither is there any to deliver them" → "His sons are far from salvation … and there is no deliverer"** (v4) — Hebrew בָּנָיו "his sons"; יֶשַׁע "salvation" (so v11); וְאֵין מַצִּיל. The verse is two lines in the Hebrew.
- **"and taketh it even out of the thorns, and the robber swalloweth up their substance" → "and even out of the thorns he takes it, and the snare pants after their wealth"** (v5) — Hebrew וְאֶל־מִצִּנִּים יִקָּחֵהוּ, literally "and to from thorns he takes it": obscure. וְשָׁאַף צַמִּים חֵילָם: צַמִּים is "snare" in 18:9, and so it is rendered here; many read צְמֵאִים "the thirsty" (a change of the Hebrew), KJV "the robber"; uncertain. שָׁאַף "pant after" (as 7:2). חַיִל "wealth".
- **"Although affliction cometh not forth of the dust, neither doth trouble spring out of the ground; yet man is born unto trouble, as the sparks fly upward" → "For trouble does not go out from the dust, and from the ground toil does not spring up; for man is born to toil, and the sons of flame fly high"** (v6, 7) — Hebrew כִּי … כִּי, "for … for"; אָוֶן "trouble" (the word of 4:8, "wickedness", here of what befalls), עָמָל "toil". בְּנֵי־רֶשֶׁף "sons of flame" (sparks, or birds; רֶשֶׁף is also the name of a god, Resheph); יַגְבִּיהוּ עוּף, literally "make high to fly". "As" is not written: the two lines stand side by side.
- **"I would seek unto God, and unto God would I commit my cause" → "But as for me, I would seek El, and to Elohim I would set my cause"** (v8) — Hebrew אוּלָם אֲנִי "but I"; דָּרַשׁ אֶל "seek to"; אָשִׂים "set, put"; דִּבְרָה "cause" (from דָּבָר).
- **"Which doeth great things and unsearchable; marvellous things without number" → "who does great things, and there is no searching, wonderful deeds till there is no number"** (v9) — Hebrew וְאֵין חֵקֶר (חֵקֶר "searching", noun of חָקַר "examine", v27); נִפְלָאוֹת "wonderful deeds"; עַד־אֵין מִסְפָּר.
- **"upon the earth … upon the fields" → "on the face of the earth … on the face of the open country"** (v10) — Hebrew עַל־פְּנֵי twice; חוּצוֹת "the places outside" (elsewhere "streets"; not שָׂדֶה "field").
- **"that those which mourn may be exalted to safety" → "and the darkened are set high in salvation"** (v11) — Hebrew וְקֹדְרִים שָׂגְבוּ יֶשַׁע: קֹדְרִים "dark ones" (the dress and face of mourners; the verb of 6:16), a finite verb, not "that … may".
- **"He disappointeth the devices of the crafty, so that their hands cannot perform their enterprise" → "He makes void the devices of the shrewd, and their hands do not achieve success"** (v12) — Hebrew מֵפֵר "makes void" (as of a vow, Num 30); עֲרוּמִים "shrewd" (as Gen 3:1). תּוּשִׁיָּה "success" (uncertain: "sound wisdom", "resource"; so 6:13); תַעֲשֶׂינָה, literally "make, do".
- **"He taketh the wise in their own craftiness: and the counsel of the froward is carried headlong" → "He captures the wise in their shrewdness, and the counsel of the twisted is hastened"** (v13) — Hebrew לֹכֵד "captures"; עָרְמָה "shrewdness" (noun of עָרוּם, v12); נִפְתָּלִים "twisted" (פָּתַל, 2 Sam 22:27); נִמְהָרָה, nifal of מִהַר "hasten". 1 Cor 3:19 quotes the first line in a Greek form of its own.
- **"grope in the noonday" → "feel about at noon"** (v14) — Hebrew יְמַשְׁשׁוּ (as Deut 28:29).
- **"But he saveth the poor from the sword, from their mouth, and from the hand of the mighty" → "And he saves from the sword, from their mouth, and from the hand of the strong, the needy"** (v15) — Hebrew וַיֹּשַׁע מֵחֶרֶב מִפִּיהֶם, word for word: "from the sword" and "from their mouth" stand side by side (no "sword of their mouth"); אֶבְיוֹן "needy" stands last in the verse.
- **"So the poor hath hope, and iniquity stoppeth her mouth" → "And there is hope for the poor, and injustice shuts its mouth"** (v16) — Hebrew וַתְּהִי, "and there is"; דַּל "poor"; עֹלָתָה, a form of עַוְלָה "injustice" (6:29, 30); קָפְצָה "shut".
- **"happy is the man whom God correcteth: therefore despise not thou the chastening of the Almighty" → "happy is the mortal man whom Eloah reproves, and the discipline of Shaddai do not reject"** (v17) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵי "happy"; אֱנוֹשׁ "mortal man"; הוֹכִיחַ "reprove"; מוּסָר "discipline"; מָאַס "reject"; "therefore" is not written (וּ "and").
- **"For he maketh sore, and bindeth up: he woundeth, and his hands make whole" → "For he causes pain, and he binds up; he strikes through, and his hands heal"** (v18) — Hebrew יַכְאִיב; מָחַץ "strike through" (as Deut 32:39); "his hands" is the qere וְיָדָיו; the ketiv וידו is the singular "his hand".
- **"He shall deliver thee in six troubles: yea, in seven there shall no evil touch thee" → "In six distresses he will deliver you, and in seven evil shall not touch you"** (v19) — Hebrew צָרוֹת "distresses"; רָע "evil".
- **"In famine he shall redeem thee from death: and in war from the power of the sword" → "In famine he ransoms you from death, and in war from the hands of the sword"** (v20) — Hebrew פָּדְךָ, a perfect ("he has ransomed you"), of what is sure; פָּדָה "ransom" (גָּאַל is "redeem"); מִידֵי "from the hands of".
- **"Thou shalt be hid from the scourge of the tongue" → "From the whip of the tongue you shall be hidden"** (v21) — Hebrew בְּשׁוֹט, literally "in the whip"; תֵּחָבֵא, nifal ("be hidden" or "hide yourself"). **"destruction" → "devastation"** (v21, 22) — Hebrew שֹׁד.
- **"At destruction and famine thou shalt laugh: neither shalt thou be afraid of the beasts of the earth" → "At devastation and at dearth you shall laugh, and of the living things of the earth do not be afraid"** (v22) — Hebrew כָּפָן "dearth" (an Aramaic word, also 30:3; רָעָב "famine" is v20); חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ "the living things of the earth" (the word says "living", not "beasts"); אַל־תִּירָא, a prohibition.
- **"For thou shalt be in league with the stones of the field: and the beasts of the field shall be at peace with thee" → "For with the stones of the field is your covenant, and the living things of the field are made to be at peace with you"** (v23) — Hebrew בְרִיתֶךָ "your covenant"; הָשְׁלְמָה, hofal of שָׁלֵם, "are made to be at peace" (no `shalom` token: the noun is not written).
- **"thy tabernacle shall be in peace; and thou shalt visit thy habitation, and shalt not sin" → "your tent is shalom, and you shall visit your habitation and shall not miss"** (v24) — Hebrew כִּי־שָׁלוֹם אָהֳלֶךָ, literally "that shalom is your tent" ("in" is not written); אֹהֶל "tent"; פָּקַד "visit"; וְלֹא תֶחֱטָא: חָטָא is "sin", from "miss the mark" (Judg 20:16); here "you will miss nothing" or "you will not sin": both senses are in the word.
- **"Thou shalt know also that thy seed shall be great, and thine offspring as the grass of the earth" → "And you shall know that your seed is many, and your offspring as the plants of the earth"** (v25) — Hebrew זַרְעֶךָ "your seed"; צֶאֱצָאֶיךָ "your offspring" (lit. "what comes out of you"); עֵשֶׂב "plant", not "grass".
- **"Thou shalt come to thy grave in a full age, like as a shock of corn cometh in in his season" → "You shall come in vigor to the grave, as stacked grain goes up in its time"** (v26) — Hebrew בְכֶלַח (only here and 30:2; uncertain: "vigor" or "ripe age"); קֶבֶר "grave"; גָּדִישׁ "stacked grain" (as Exod 22:6); עָלָה "go up" (to the threshing floor).
- **"Lo this, we have searched it, so it is; hear it, and know thou it for thy good" → "Behold, this — we have examined it; so it is. Hear it, and you, know it for yourself"** (v27) — Hebrew חֲקַרְנוּהָ (חָקַר "examine"); וְאַתָּה דַע־לָךְ, literally "and you, know for yourself".

### Notes
- **Eliphaz continues** — points to universal providence. Man born to toil, beside the "sons of flame" that fly high (v7) — famous image.
- **"Six... seven distresses"** (v19) — numerical parallelism; encompassing.
- **Promise pattern** — if Job submits to divine discipline, restoration. The ground of Job's problem: he already is innocent; friends' counsel assumes the opposite.
- **1 Cor 3:19 cites v13** — Paul quotes the first line of this verse.
