# Job 3

[1] After this, Job opened his mouth and cursed his day.

[2] Job spoke and said:

[3] "Let the day perish on which I was born,
and the night that said, 'A man-child is conceived.'

[4] Let that day be darkness;
let Eloah not seek it from above,
nor let light shine upon it.

[5] Let darkness and deep darkness — deep shadow — claim it;
let a cloud dwell upon it;
let all that blackens the day terrify it.

[6] As for that night — let gloom seize it;
let it not be joined to the days of the year,
let it not enter the number of the months.

[7] Behold, let that night be barren;
let no cry of joy enter it.

[8] Let those who curse the day curse it,
those who are ready to rouse Leviathan.

[9] Let the stars of its twilight be dark;
let it wait for light and have none;
let it not see the eyelids of dawn —

[10] because it did not shut the doors of my mother's womb,
to hide trouble from my eyes.

[11] Why did I not die at birth,
come out from the womb and expire?

[12] Why did the knees receive me?
Why the breasts, that I should nurse?

[13] For now I would be lying down in quiet;
I would be asleep; then I would be at rest,

[14] with kings and counselors of the earth
who built ruins for themselves,

[15] or with officials who had gold,
who filled their houses with silver.

[16] Or why was I not as a hidden stillborn,
as infants who never saw light?

[17] There the wicked cease from raging;
there the weary are at rest.

[18] There prisoners are at ease together;
they do not hear the voice of the taskmaster.

[19] The small and the great are there,
and the slave is free from his master.

[20] Why is light given to one in misery,
and life to the bitter in soul —

[21] who long for death, but it does not come,
and dig for it more than for hidden treasures,

[22] who rejoice exceedingly
and are glad when they find the grave?

[23] Why is light given to a man whose way is hidden,
and whom Eloah has hedged in?

[24] For my sighing comes as my bread,
and my groans are poured out like water.

[25] The dread I feared has come upon me,
and what I was afraid of has come to me.

[26] I am not at ease, not quiet, not at rest;
and trouble has come."

## Changes

- **"princes" → "officials"** (v15).
- **"untimely birth" → "stillborn"** (v16) — *nefel*.
- **"Eloah" — retained** (v4, 23) — poetic singular of *Elohim*, frequent in Job.

### Notes
- **Curse of his birth-day** — ultimate rejection of being. Job does not curse Yahweh (keeping his integrity) but curses his own existence.
- **Reverse-creation poetry** (v4-9) — darkness over day, barren over night. Uncreation-language echoes Gen 1.
- **Rousers of Leviathan** — professional mourners/magicians said to rouse the chaos-monster. Job wishes they'd curse his day into nothingness.
- **Sheol-equality** (v17-19) — death flattens all hierarchies. Egalitarian but grim consolation.
- **"Eyelids of dawn"** — beautiful poetic phrase for daybreak.
- **"Dread I feared has come"** (v25) — Job had anticipated his vulnerability.
- **Opens the poetic dialogues** — tone is fierce lament, not stoic acceptance.
