# Job 3

[1] After this ⟦job|Iyov|Job⟧ opened his mouth and cursed his day.

[2] And ⟦job|Iyov|Job⟧ answered and said:

[3] "Let the day perish on which I was born,
and the night that said, 'A man has been conceived.'

[4] That day — let it be darkness;
let ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧ not seek it from above,
and let no daylight shine on it.

[5] Let darkness and the shadow of death redeem it;
let a cloud abide on it;
let the blackenings of the day terrify it.

[6] That night — let gloom take it;
let it not rejoice among the days of the year;
into the number of the months let it not come.

[7] Behold, that night — let it be barren;
let no ringing cry come into it.

[8] Let those who curse the day lay a curse on it,
those who are ready to rouse ⟦leviathan|Livyatan|Leviathan⟧.

[9] Let the stars of its twilight be dark;
let it wait for light, and there is none;
and let it not see the eyelids of the dawn,

[10] because it did not shut the doors of my belly
and hide toil from my eyes.

[11] Why did I not die from the womb,
come out from the belly and expire?

[12] Why did knees come to meet me,
and why breasts, that I should suck?

[13] For now I would have lain down and been quiet;
I would have slept; then there would be rest for me,

[14] with kings and counselors of the earth,
who build ruins for themselves,

[15] or with princes who had gold,
who filled their houses with silver.

[16] Or as a hidden miscarriage I would not be,
as children who did not see light.

[17] There the wicked cease from agitation,
and there the weary of strength rest.

[18] Together prisoners are at ease;
they do not hear the voice of the taskmaster.

[19] The small and the great are there,
and the slave is free from his master.

[20] Why does he give light to the toiling one,
and life to the bitter of soul,

[21] who await death, and it is not,
and dig for it more than for hidden treasures,

[22] who rejoice unto joy,
exult when they find a grave,

[23] to a man whose way is hidden,
and around whom ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧ has put a hedge?

[24] For before my bread my sighing comes,
and my roarings are poured out like water.

[25] For I dreaded a dread, and it has come upon me,
and what I was afraid of comes to me.

[26] I was not untroubled, and I was not quiet, and I did not rest,
and agitation came."

## Changes

- **"Job spake, and said" → "And Job answered and said"** (v2) — Hebrew וַיַּעַן אִיּוֹב וַיֹּאמַר, the formula that opens every speech in the book; no one has spoken to him. **"cursed his day"** (v1) — Hebrew קִלֵּל "curse" (lit. "make light of").
- **"Let the day perish wherein I was born" → "on which I was born"** (v3) — Hebrew יוֹם אִוָּלֶד בּוֹ. **"There is a man child conceived" → "A man has been conceived"** — Hebrew הֹרָה גָבֶר; גֶּבֶר "man" (the word says a male, not a child).
- **"Let that day be darkness" → "That day — let it be darkness"** (v4) — the Hebrew sets "that day" first, as "that night" in v6, 7. **"regard it" → "seek it"** — Hebrew דָּרַשׁ. **"light" → "daylight"** — Hebrew נְהָרָה, a word found only here, beside אוֹר "light" (v9, 16, 20).
- **"stain it" → "redeem it"** (v5) — Hebrew יִגְאָלֻהוּ, the verb גָּאַל "redeem": darkness is to claim the day back as its own. Some take it from a second root גָּאַל "defile" ("stain it", KJV). **"the shadow of death"** — Hebrew צַלְמָוֶת, pointed as צֵל "shadow" + מָוֶת "death"; many read צַלְמוּת "deep darkness" (another pointing). **"a cloud dwell upon it" → "a cloud abide on it"** — Hebrew עֲנָנָה, a feminine form of עָנָן "cloud" found only here; שָׁכַן "abide". **"the blackness of the day" → "the blackenings of the day"** — Hebrew כִּמְרִירֵי יוֹם, found only here: from כָּמַר "be black" (an eclipse or darkening), or כְּ + מְרִירִים "like the bitternesses of a day"; uncertain.
- **"let darkness seize upon it" → "let gloom take it"** (v6) — Hebrew אֹפֶל, of one root with אֲפֵלָה "gloom"; לָקַח "take". **"let it not be joined unto the days of the year" → "let it not rejoice among the days of the year"** — the Masoretic Text points אַל־יִחַדְּ from חָדָה "rejoice"; "be joined" (יֵחַד from יָחַד) is another pointing of the same letters, and many follow it.
- **"Lo, let that night be solitary" → "Behold, that night — let it be barren"** (v7) — Hebrew הִנֵּה; גַּלְמוּד "barren, stony" (of a woman Isa 49:21). **"joyful voice" → "ringing cry"** — Hebrew רְנָנָה.
- **"curse it" → "lay a curse on it"** (v8) — Hebrew יִקְּבֻהוּ from קָבַב, beside אֹרְרֵי־יוֹם from אָרַר "curse": two verbs. Some change יוֹם "day" to יָם "sea" ("those who curse the Sea"); the Masoretic Text is kept. **"who are ready to raise up their mourning" → "who are ready to rouse Leviathan"** — Hebrew עֹרֵר לִוְיָתָן "rouse Leviathan"; the KJV's "their mourning" is a guess.
- **"twilight"** (v9) — Hebrew נֶשֶׁף, the dusk (or the dawn twilight). **"look for light, but have none" → "wait for light, and there is none"** — Hebrew יְקַו־לְאוֹר וָאַיִן. **"the dawning of the day" → "the eyelids of the dawn"** — Hebrew עַפְעַפֵּי־שָׁחַר.
- **"my mother's womb" → "my belly"** (v10) — Hebrew דַּלְתֵי בִטְנִי "the doors of my belly", the belly in which he lay; "mother" is not written. **"sorrow" → "toil"** — Hebrew עָמָל. The negation governs both verbs: "did not shut … and hide".
- **"from the womb? why did I not give up the ghost when I came out of the belly?" → "from the womb, come out from the belly and expire?"** (v11) — Hebrew מֵרֶחֶם … מִבֶּטֶן יָצָאתִי וְאֶגְוָע; רֶחֶם "womb", בֶּטֶן "belly", גָּוַע "expire".
- **"Why did the knees prevent me?" → "Why did knees come to meet me"** (v12) — Hebrew קִדְּמוּנִי בִרְכָּיִם, without the article; קִדֵּם "come to meet". מַדּוּעַ "why" (v12) and לָמָּה "why" (v11, 20): two words, one English.
- **"For now should I have lain still and been quiet, I should have slept: then had I been at rest" → "For now I would have lain down and been quiet; I would have slept; then there would be rest for me"** (v13) — Hebrew שָׁכַבְתִּי וְאֶשְׁקוֹט … אָז יָנוּחַ לִי, the last impersonal: "there would be rest for me".
- **"which built desolate places" → "who build ruins"** (v14) — Hebrew הַבֹּנִים חֳרָבוֹת, a participle; חָרְבָּה "ruin": what they build, or ruins they rebuild; not decided.
- **"princes"** (v15) — Hebrew שָׂרִים. **"that had gold"** — lit. "gold was theirs".
- **"Or as an hidden untimely birth I had not been" → "Or as a hidden miscarriage I would not be"** (v16) — Hebrew נֵפֶל "a falling" (a miscarriage); עֹלְלִים "children".
- **"cease from troubling" → "cease from agitation"** (v17, 26) — Hebrew רֹגֶז, noun of רָגַז "be agitated" (turmoil, raging): one word in v17 and v26. **"the weary be at rest" → "the weary of strength rest"** — Hebrew יְגִיעֵי כֹחַ.
- **"There the prisoners rest together" → "Together prisoners are at ease"** (v18) — Hebrew יַחַד stands first; שַׁאֲנַן "be at ease". **"the oppressor" → "the taskmaster"** — Hebrew נֹגֵשׂ.
- **"The small and great are there"** (v19) — Hebrew שָׁם הוּא, lit. "there he (is)": perhaps "are the same there". **"the servant" → "the slave"** — Hebrew עֶבֶד; "his master" is a plural form, אֲדֹנָיו.
- **"Wherefore is light given to him that is in misery" → "Why does he give light to the toiling one"** (v20) — Hebrew יִתֵּן "he gives", the subject not named; עָמֵל "toiling, wretched", adjective of עָמָל "toil" (v10).
- **"long for death, but it cometh not" → "await death, and it is not"** (v21) — Hebrew הַמְחַכִּים לַמָּוֶת וְאֵינֶנּוּ; חִכָּה "await" (קִוָּה "wait for" in v9). **"dig for it"** — the form וַיַּחְפְּרֻהוּ is a wayyiqtol in a chain of participles.
- **"rejoice exceedingly, and are glad" → "rejoice unto joy, exult"** (v22) — Hebrew הַשְּׂמֵחִים אֱלֵי־גִיל יָשִׂישׂוּ: שָׂמַח "rejoice", גִּיל "joy", שׂוּשׂ "exult". Some repoint גִּיל as גַּל "heap" (of stones over a grave): "who rejoice at the heap"; the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"Why is light given to a man" → "to a man"** (v23) — the verse continues the question of v20; גֶּבֶר "man". **"hedged in" → "around whom … has put a hedge"** — Hebrew וַיָּסֶךְ אֱלוֹהַּ בַּעֲדוֹ, the words of the adversary in 1:10 (שַׂכְתָּ בַעֲדוֹ, "put a hedge around him"): there a protection, here a prison.
- **"For my sighing cometh before I eat" → "For before my bread my sighing comes"** (v24) — Hebrew לִפְנֵי לַחְמִי "before my bread" (at my food, or in place of it). **"my roarings"** — Hebrew שַׁאֲגֹתַי, the word of the lion's roar.
- **"For the thing which I greatly feared is come upon me" → "For I dreaded a dread, and it has come upon me"** (v25) — Hebrew פַּחַד פָּחַדְתִּי, cognate; אָתָה "come" (poetic) beside בּוֹא "come": one English word. **"comes to me"** — יָבֹא is an imperfect.
- **"I was not in safety, neither had I rest, neither was I quiet; yet trouble came" → "I was not untroubled, and I was not quiet, and I did not rest, and agitation came"** (v26) — Hebrew שָׁלַוְתִּי … שָׁקַטְתִּי … נָחְתִּי, three verbs in their order (KJV changed it); וַיָּבֹא רֹגֶז "and agitation came" (the word of v17).
- **Eloah kept** (v4, 23) — Hebrew אֱלוֹהַּ, the singular form, frequent in Job; never levelled to *Elohim*.

### Notes
- **Curse of his birth-day** — ultimate rejection of being. Job does not curse Yahweh (keeping his integrity) but curses his own existence.
- **Reverse-creation poetry** (v4-9) — darkness over day, barren over night. Uncreation-language echoes Gen 1 ("let there be light" — "let it be darkness", v4).
- **Rousers of Leviathan** — those who curse the day are pictured as able to rouse Leviathan, the sea monster (41:1–34); whether professional cursers or magicians are meant is not said.
- **Equality in death** (v17-19) — death flattens all hierarchies. Egalitarian but grim consolation.
- **"Eyelids of the dawn"** — the first rays of daybreak.
- **"I dreaded a dread"** (v25) — Job had anticipated his vulnerability.
- **Opens the poetic dialogues** — tone is fierce lament, not stoic acceptance.
