# Job 39

[1] "Do you know when the mountain goats give birth?
Do you observe the calving of the deer?

[2] Can you number the months that they fulfill,
or know the time when they give birth?

[3] They crouch down, bring forth their young,
and cast out their offspring.

[4] Their young grow strong, thrive in the open;
they go forth and do not return.

[5] Who has let the wild donkey go free?
Who has loosed the bonds of the swift donkey,

[6] whose home I have made the steppe,
and the salt land his dwellings?

[7] He scorns the tumult of the city;
he does not hear the shouts of the driver.

[8] He ranges the mountains as his pasture
and searches for every green thing.

[9] Is the wild ox willing to serve you?
Will he spend the night at your feeding-trough?

[10] Can you bind him in the furrow with ropes?
Will he harrow the valleys after you?

[11] Will you trust him because his strength is great?
Will you leave to him your labor?

[12] Will you trust him to return your grain,
to gather it to your threshing floor?

[13] The wings of the ostrich flap joyously,
but are they the pinions and plumage of the stork?

[14] For she leaves her eggs to the earth
and warms them on the sand,

[15] forgetting that a foot may crush them
or a wild beast may trample them.

[16] She treats her young harshly, as if they were not hers;
though her labor be in vain, she has no fear,

[17] because ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧ made her forget wisdom
and gave her no share in understanding.

[18] When she arouses herself to flee,
she laughs at the horse and his rider.

[19] Do you give the horse his might?
Do you clothe his neck with a mane?

[20] Do you make him leap like the locust?
His majestic snorting is terrifying.

[21] He paws in the valley and rejoices in his strength;
he goes out to meet weapons.

[22] He laughs at fear and is not dismayed;
he does not turn back from the sword.

[23] Upon him rattle the quiver,
the flashing spear and the javelin.

[24] With fierceness and fury he swallows the ground;
he cannot stand still when the ⟦shofar|shofar|trumpet⟧ sounds.

[25] When the ⟦shofar|shofar|trumpet⟧ sounds he says, 'Aha!'
he smells the battle from afar,
the thunder of the captains and the war shout.

[26] Is it by your understanding that the hawk soars
and spreads his wings toward the south?

[27] Is it at your command that the eagle mounts up
and makes his nest on high?

[28] He dwells on the cliff and lodges there,
on the rocky crag, a stronghold.

[29] From there he spies out the prey;
his eyes see it from afar.

[30] His young ones suck up blood,
and where the slain are, there is he."

## Changes

- **"unicorn" → "wild ox"** (v9, 10) — *re'em*; aurochs or similar extinct bovine.
- **"peacocks... ostrich" → "ostrich... stork"** (v13) — Hebrew *renanim... chasidah*; scholars debate; rendering provides parallel of ostrich as primary subject.
- **"thunder" → "mane"** (v19) — Hebrew *ra'mah*; noisy/thundering, but here of the horse's flowing/quivering neck-hair.

### Notes
- **Zoological survey** — untameable animals (mountain goat, deer, wild donkey, wild ox, ostrich, horse, hawk, eagle). Divine delight in wildness.
- **Wild donkey free** (v5-8) — rejects civilization.
- **Wild ox unharnessable** — strength not for human service.
- **Ostrich foolish** (v13-18) — dispositional silliness, yet outruns horses.
- **War-horse** (v19-25) — poetic masterpiece; glory in battle.
- **Eagle** (v27-30) — soaring predator. Ends with eagles' young drinking blood — carnivorous ecology as Yahweh's province.
- **Theme: creation has a wildness outside human utility or ethics.** Yahweh glories in it.
