# Job 9

[1] Then Job answered and said:

[2] "Truly I know that it is so;
but how can man be righteous before *Elohim*?

[3] If one wanted to dispute with him,
he could not answer him one in a thousand.

[4] He is wise in heart and mighty in strength.
Who has hardened himself against him and escaped unhurt?

[5] He removes mountains, and they do not know it,
when he overturns them in his anger.

[6] He shakes the earth out of its place,
and its pillars tremble.

[7] He commands the sun, and it does not rise;
he seals up the stars.

[8] He alone stretches out the heavens
and treads upon the waves of the sea.

[9] He made the Ash, Orion, and Kimah — the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades —
and the chambers of the south.

[10] He does great things beyond searching,
wonders without number.

[11] Behold, he passes by me, and I do not see him;
he moves on, but I do not perceive him.

[12] Behold, he snatches away; who can turn him back?
Who will say to him, 'What are you doing?'

[13] Eloah does not turn back his anger;
beneath him the helpers of Rahab — the sea-monster — stoop.

[14] How then can I answer him,
choose my words to argue with him?

[15] Though I were righteous, I could not answer him;
I must plead with my judge for mercy.

[16] If I called, and he answered me,
I would not believe that he was listening to my voice.

[17] For he crushes me with a tempest
and multiplies my wounds without cause.

[18] He will not let me catch my breath,
but fills me with bitterness.

[19] If it is a matter of strength — behold, he is mighty!
If of justice — who can summon him?

[20] Though I am righteous, my own mouth will condemn me;
though I am blameless, he will prove me perverse.

[21] I am blameless! I do not know myself;
I despise my life.

[22] It is all one; therefore I say,
he destroys the blameless and the wicked.

[23] When the scourge kills suddenly,
he mocks the despair of the innocent.

[24] The earth is given into the hand of the wicked;
he covers the faces of its judges —
if it is not he, then who?

[25] My days are swifter than a courier;
they flee away, they see no good.

[26] They go by like reed boats,
like an eagle swooping on prey.

[27] If I say, 'I will forget my complaint,
I will change my face and be cheerful,'

[28] I dread all my sufferings,
for I know you will not hold me innocent.

[29] I shall be counted guilty —
why then do I labor in vain?

[30] If I wash myself with snow water
and cleanse my hands with lye,

[31] yet you will plunge me into the pit,
and my own clothes will abhor me.

[32] For he is not a man, as I am, that I might answer him,
that we should come together in judgment.

[33] There is no arbiter between us,
who might lay his hand upon us both.

[34] Let him take his rod away from me,
and let not his dread terrify me.

[35] Then I would speak and not fear him,
for it is not so with me in myself."

## Changes

- **"post" → "courier"** (v25) — *ratz*.
- **"swift ships" → "reed boats"** (v26) — *oniyyot evek*; papyrus skiffs.

### Notes
- **Cosmic-judicial struggle** — Job's dilemma: how argue with *Elohim* who controls everything?
- **Cosmic poetry** (v5-10) — mountains, earth, sun, stars all at Yahweh's command.
- **Rahab** — sea-monster defeated at creation (Ps 89:10, Isa 51:9). Chaos-beings submit to El.
- **"He destroys the blameless and the wicked"** (v22) — direct denial of friends' retribution theology. Wilderness-complaint at divine indiscrimination.
- **No umpire** (v33) — Job longs for a mediator who could stand between him and *Elohim*. Later Christian reading sees messianic anticipation.
- **"Wash with snow water"** (v30) — no amount of purification can make Job righteous before one who pre-counts him guilty.
