# Job 21

[1] Then Job answered and said:

[2] "Listen carefully to my speech,
and let this be your consolation.

[3] Bear with me, that I may speak;
and after I have spoken, you may mock.

[4] As for me, is my complaint against man?
Why then should I not be impatient?

[5] Look at me and be appalled;
lay your hand on your mouth.

[6] When I remember, I am dismayed;
trembling seizes my flesh.

[7] Why do the wicked live,
grow old, and mount in power?

[8] Their offspring are established before them in their presence,
their descendants before their eyes.

[9] Their houses are safe from fear;
no rod of Eloah is on them.

[10] Their bull mates and does not fail;
their cow calves and does not miscarry.

[11] They send out their little ones like a flock;
their children dance.

[12] They raise their voices to the tambourine and lyre;
they rejoice at the sound of the flute.

[13] They spend their days in prosperity,
and in peace they go down to Sheol.

[14] They say to *Elohim*, 'Depart from us;
we desire not the knowledge of your ways.

[15] What is Shaddai, that we should serve him?
What profit should we have if we pray to him?'

[16] Behold, their good is not in their hand;
the counsel of the wicked is far from me.

[17] How often is the lamp of the wicked put out?
How often does their calamity come upon them?
Does Eloah apportion pains in his anger?

[18] They are as stubble before the wind,
as chaff that the storm carries away.

[19] 'Eloah stores up the iniquity for his children' —
let him repay it to the man himself, that he may know it.

[20] Let his own eyes see his ruin;
let him drink from the wrath of Shaddai.

[21] For what is his pleasure in his house after him,
when the number of his months is cut off?

[22] Can one teach *Elohim* knowledge,
when it is he who judges those on high?

[23] One man dies in his full vigor,
wholly at ease and content,

[24] his thighs filled out with fat,
the marrow of his bones moist.

[25] Another dies in bitterness of soul,
never having tasted good.

[26] They lie down alike in the dust,
and the worms cover them.

[27] Behold, I know your thoughts,
and the schemes by which you would wrong me.

[28] For you say, 'Where is the house of the noble?
Where is the tent in which the wicked lived?'

[29] Have you not asked travelers?
Do you not accept their evidence,

[30] that the evil man is spared in the day of calamity,
that he is rescued in the day of wrath?

[31] Who declares his way to his face?
Who repays him for what he has done?

[32] When he is carried to the grave,
watch is kept over his tomb.

[33] The clods of the valley are sweet to him;
everyone follows after him,
as those before him were innumerable.

[34] How then will you comfort me with vanities?
Your answers remain falsehoods."

## Changes

- **"grave" → "Sheol"** (v13).
- **"timbrel... organ" → "tambourine... flute"** (v12) — *toph... ugav*.
- **"prince" → "noble"** (v28) — *nadiv*.

### Notes
- **Job's counter-evidence** — observation shows wicked prosper, die in peace.
- **Direct challenge to retribution** — empirical contradiction of friends' orthodoxy.
- **Wicked's rejection of El** (v14-15) — classic atheist-skeptic voice quoted.
- **"Eloah stores iniquity for his children"** (v19) — quotes conventional wisdom only to reject: fair repayment requires the man himself pays.
- **Death flattens all** (v23-26) — the fat-easy death and the bitter death both end in dust.
- **Travelers' testimony** (v29) — wider observation confirms: wicked often escape wrath.
- **Sentimental funeral** (v32-33) — even the wicked get pleasant burials. Clods sweet over him.
- **"Your answers remain falsehoods"** — devastating judgment.
