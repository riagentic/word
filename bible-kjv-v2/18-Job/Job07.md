# Job 7

[1] "Has not man — mortal man — a hard service on earth?
Are not his days like the days of a hired laborer?

[2] Like a slave who longs for the shadow,
and like a hireling who looks for his wages,

[3] so I have been allotted months of emptiness,
and wearisome nights are appointed me.

[4] When I lie down I say,
'When shall I rise?'
but the night drags on,
and I am full of tossings till dawn.

[5] My flesh is clothed with worms and clods of dust;
my skin breaks open and runs.

[6] My days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle,
and come to an end without hope.

[7] Remember that my life is a breath;
my eye will never again see good.

[8] The eye that sees me shall see me no more;
your eyes shall be upon me, and I shall be gone.

[9] As a cloud fades and vanishes,
so he who goes down to Sheol shall not come up.

[10] He shall return no more to his house;
his place shall know him no more.

[11] Therefore I will not restrain my mouth;
I will speak in the anguish of my spirit;
I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.

[12] Am I the Sea, or the dragon,
that you set a guard over me?

[13] When I say, 'My couch will comfort me,
my bed will ease my complaint,'

[14] then you scare me with dreams
and terrify me with visions,

[15] so that my soul chooses strangling,
death, rather than my bones.

[16] I loathe my life; I would not live forever.
Let me alone; my days are a breath.

[17] What is man, that you make so much of him,
that you set your heart upon him,

[18] that you examine him every morning,
test him every moment?

[19] How long will you not look away from me,
not let me alone until I swallow my spittle?

[20] If I have sinned, what have I done to you,
you watcher of men?
Why have you made me your target,
so that I have become a burden to myself?

[21] Why do you not pardon my transgression
and take away my iniquity?
For now I shall lie down in the dust;
you shall seek me, but I shall not be."

## Changes

- **"appointed time" → "hard service"** (v1) — *tzava*; military-service term.
- **"grave" → "Sheol"** (v9).
- **"whale" → "the dragon"** (v12) — *tannin*; sea-monster, not whale.
- **"sea" → "the Sea"** (v12) — *Yam*; the cosmic sea, often personified.
- **"mark against thee" → "target"** (v20) — *miphga*.
- **"preserver of men" → "watcher of men"** (v20) — *notzer ha-adam*; observer, not preserver.
- **"my life" → "my bones"** (v15) — *atzmotai*.

### Notes
- **Yam and Tannin** (v12) — Canaanite chaos-monsters; in Ugaritic myth Baal defeats them. Job sarcastically asks if he's a primeval monster requiring divine surveillance.
- **"What is *enosh*?"** (v17) — parodies Ps 8's adoring question. There: wonder at God's care. Here: complaint about God's obsessive scrutiny.
- **Address shifts to *Elohim* directly** — Job ignores the friends and argues upward.
- **First direct complaint against Elohim** in the dialogues: excessive divine attention is itself burden.
- **Death-wish** (v15-16, 21) — "sleep in the dust"; Sheol as blessed escape from divine gaze.
