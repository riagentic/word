# Job 41

[1] "Can you draw out ⟦leviathan|Livyatan|Leviathan⟧ with a fishhook,
or press down his tongue with a cord?

[2] Can you put a rope in his nose
or pierce his jaw with a hook?

[3] Will he make many pleas to you?
Will he speak soft words to you?

[4] Will he make a covenant with you
to take him as a servant forever?

[5] Will you play with him as with a bird,
or put him on a leash for your girls?

[6] Will traders bargain over him?
Will they divide him among the merchants?

[7] Can you fill his skin with harpoons,
or his head with fishing spears?

[8] Lay your hand upon him;
remember the battle — you will not do it again!

[9] Behold, hope of capturing him is false;
one is cast down at the mere sight of him.

[10] No one is fierce enough to rouse him;
who then can stand before me?

[11] Who has given to me, that I should repay?
Whatever is under the whole heaven is mine.

[12] I will not keep silence concerning his limbs,
nor his mighty strength, nor his comely frame.

[13] Who can strip off his outer garment?
Who can come within his double coat of mail?

[14] Who can open the doors of his face?
Around his teeth is terror.

[15] His back is made of rows of shields,
shut up closely as with a seal.

[16] One is so near another
that no air can come between them.

[17] They are joined one to another;
they clasp and cannot be separated.

[18] His sneezings flash forth light;
his eyes are like the eyelids of dawn.

[19] Out of his mouth go flaming torches;
sparks of fire leap out.

[20] Out of his nostrils comes forth smoke,
as from a boiling pot and burning reeds.

[21] His breath kindles coals,
and a flame goes forth from his mouth.

[22] In his neck dwells strength,
and dismay dances before him.

[23] The folds of his flesh cleave fast together;
they are cast upon him and cannot be moved.

[24] His heart is hard as stone,
hard as the lower millstone.

[25] When he raises himself up, the mighty are afraid;
at the crashing they are beside themselves.

[26] Though the sword reaches him, it cannot avail,
nor the spear, the dart, or the javelin.

[27] He counts iron as straw,
and bronze as rotten wood.

[28] The arrow cannot make him flee;
slingstones become stubble to him.

[29] Clubs are counted as stubble;
he laughs at the rattle of the javelin.

[30] His underparts are like sharp potsherds;
he spreads himself like a threshing sledge on the mire.

[31] He makes the deep boil like a pot;
he makes the sea like a pot of ointment.

[32] Behind him he leaves a shining wake;
one would think the deep to be white-haired.

[33] On earth there is not his like,
a creature made without fear.

[34] He beholds everything that is high;
he is king over all the proud beasts."

## Changes

- **"habergeon" → "javelin"** (v26).
- **"neesings" → "sneezings"** (v18) — same word.
- **"brass" → "bronze"** (v27).
- **"children of pride" → "proud beasts"** (v34) — Hebrew *benei-shachatz*; "sons of pride" = proud creatures.

### Notes
- **Leviathan** — retains mythological overtones in Job. Crocodile most commonly identified historically; fire-breathing and cosmic scale suggest mythic chaos-monster (cf. Ps 74:14, Isa 27:1).
- **Uncapturable** — cannot be hooked, spoken to, made covenant with, played with like a pet.
- **Armored, fire-breathing** — hyperreal description; dragons of sea-myth.
- **Divine sovereignty** — "Who has given to me, that I should repay?" (v11) — Yahweh owes no human. Cited Rom 11:35.
- **Closes Yahweh's speeches** — culminates the creation-wonder argument.
- **No direct answer to Job** — theophany itself is the answer. Trust based on creation-relation.
