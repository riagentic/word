# Job 13

[1] "Behold, my eye has seen all this;
my ear has heard and understood.

[2] What you know, I also know;
I am not inferior to you.

[3] But I would speak to Shaddai;
I desire to argue my case with *Elohim*.

[4] But you plaster lies;
you are all worthless physicians.

[5] Oh that you would be completely silent!
That would be your wisdom.

[6] Hear now my argument;
listen to the pleadings of my lips.

[7] Will you speak falsely for *Elohim*?
Will you speak deceitfully for him?

[8] Will you show partiality toward him?
Will you plead the case for *Elohim*?

[9] Will it be good when he examines you?
Can you deceive him as one deceives a man?

[10] He will surely rebuke you
if you secretly show partiality.

[11] Will not his majesty terrify you,
and the dread of him fall upon you?

[12] Your platitudes are proverbs of ashes;
your defenses are defenses of clay.

[13] Be silent and let me alone, that I may speak;
let come on me what may.

[14] Why do I take my flesh in my teeth,
and put my life in my hand?

[15] Behold, though he kill me, yet will I wait for him;
I will argue my ways before his face.

[16] This also will be my salvation,
that a godless man cannot come before him.

[17] Listen carefully to my speech;
let my declaration be in your ears.

[18] Behold, I have set my case in order;
I know that I am in the right.

[19] Who is he who will contend with me?
For then I would be silent and expire.

[20] Only two things do not do to me,
then I will not hide myself from your face:

[21] withdraw your hand far from me,
and let not the dread of you make me afraid.

[22] Then call, and I will answer,
or let me speak, and answer me.

[23] How many are my iniquities and sins?
Make me know my transgression and my sin.

[24] Why do you hide your face
and hold me as your enemy?

[25] Will you drive a fluttering leaf?
Will you pursue dry chaff?

[26] For you write bitter things against me
and make me inherit the iniquities of my youth.

[27] You put my feet in the stocks
and watch all my paths;
you set a mark on the soles of my feet.

[28] Man wastes away like something rotten,
like a garment that the moth has eaten."

## Changes

- **"the Almighty" → "Shaddai"** (v3) — Hebrew שַׁדַּי *Shaddai*; ancient divine name; preserved in Job as one of the book's signature divine titles.
- **"God" → "El"** (vv3, 7, 8) — Hebrew אֵל *El*; Job addresses and speaks of *El* (short divine name) distinctly from *Elohim*.
- **"Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him" → "though he kill me, yet will I wait for him"** (v15) — Hebrew Ketiv/Qere: Ketiv (written) *lo* = "I will not wait"; Qere (read) *lo* = "in him I will hope/wait." Most modern translations follow Qere. Defiant hope either way.
- **"hypocrite" → "godless"** (v16) — *chanef*.
- **"forgers of lies" → "plaster lies"** (v4) — *tophelei shaqer*.
- **"bodies to bodies of clay" → "defenses of clay"** (v12) — *gabbei-chomer*; better "defenses/ramparts of clay."

### Notes
- **Defiant faith** (v15) — "Though he kill me, yet I will wait" — Job refuses surrender to slander. He demands a hearing even at cost of life.
- **"You plaster lies"** — friends as plasterers covering truth with lies.
- **Job turns from friends to Elohim** (v20ff) — wants direct audience: withdraw heavy hand, answer without terror.
- **"How many my sins?"** — direct challenge: enumerate my wrongs, then judge.
- **"Leaf... chaff"** — Job's frailty contrasted with divine persecution.
- **Rotten garment** (v28) — transition to 14.
