# Acts 25

[1] So Festus, having set foot in the province, after three days went up to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ from Caesarea,

[2] and the high priests and the first men of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ made disclosure to him against ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, and they were appealing to him,

[3] asking a favor against him, that he would send for him to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, making an ambush to do away with him along the way.

[4] So then Festus answered that ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ was being kept at Caesarea, and that he himself was about to go out shortly.

[5] "So let those among you who are powerful," he says, "having gone down together, accuse him, if there is anything amiss in the man."

[6] And having stayed among them not more than eight or ten days, having gone down to Caesarea, on the morrow, having sat down upon the judgment seat, he commanded ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ to be brought.

[7] And when he had arrived, the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ who had come down from ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ stood around him, bringing down many and heavy charges which they had no strength to show forth,

[8] while ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ defended himself: "Neither against the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ nor against the temple nor against Caesar have I sinned in anything."

[9] But Festus, wanting to lay up a favor with the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, answering ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, said, "Do you want, having gone up to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, to be judged there concerning these things before me?"

[10] But ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ said, "I am standing before the judgment seat of Caesar, where I must be judged. ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ I have wronged in nothing, as you also know quite well.

[11] If then I am doing wrong and have practiced anything worthy of death, I do not refuse to die; but if there is nothing of the things of which these accuse me, no one can grant me to them. **I call upon Caesar.**"

[12] Then Festus, having spoken together with the council, answered, "You have called upon Caesar; to Caesar you shall go."

[13] And when some days had passed, Agrippa the king and ⟦bernice|Bernike|Bernice⟧ arrived at Caesarea, having greeted Festus.

[14] And as they were staying there more days, Festus laid before the king the things concerning ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, saying, "There is a certain man left behind by Felix, a prisoner,

[15] concerning whom, when I was in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, the high priests and the elders of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ made disclosure, asking for a condemnation against him;

[16] to whom I answered that it is not a custom for Romans to grant any man before the accused has the accusers face to face and receives a place for defense concerning the accusation.

[17] So when they had come together here, having made no delay, on the next day, having sat down upon the judgment seat, I commanded the man to be brought;

[18] concerning whom the accusers, having stood up, were bringing no charge of the evil things that I was surmising,

[19] but they had certain questions against him concerning their own fear of the divinities and concerning a certain ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ who had died, whom ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ asserted to be alive.

[20] And I, being at a loss as to the questioning concerning these things, was saying whether he would wish to go to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ and there be judged concerning these things.

[21] But when ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ called to be kept for the determination of the Augustus, I commanded him to be kept until I should send him up to Caesar."

[22] And Agrippa to Festus: "I myself also was wishing to hear the man." "Tomorrow," he says, "you shall hear him."

[23] So on the morrow, when Agrippa and ⟦bernice|Bernike|Bernice⟧ had come with much pageantry and had entered into the hall of hearing, with both chiliarchs and the men of eminence of the city, and when Festus had commanded, ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ was brought.

[24] And Festus says, "King Agrippa, and all men who are present with us, you behold this one, concerning whom all the multitude of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ petitioned me, both in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ and here, crying that he must not live any longer.

[25] But I grasped that he had practiced nothing worthy of death; and when this one himself called upon the Augustus, I judged to send him,

[26] concerning whom I have nothing certain to write to the lord; therefore I brought him forth before you, and most of all before you, King Agrippa, so that, the examination having taken place, I may have something to write;

[27] for it seems to me unreasonable, sending a prisoner, not also to signify the charges against him."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** — no direct occurrence in this chapter; consistent policy.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v19) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"Holy Ghost" / Spirit** — no explicit occurrence in this chapter.
- **"Festus" / "*Porcius Festus*"** — Porcius Festus, successor to Felix as procurator of Judea c. 59–62 CE; retained as proper name.
- **"Agrippa" / "King Agrippa"** — Herod Agrippa II, great-grandson of Herod the Great; the last of the Herodian line to hold power; retained.
- **"Bernice"** — sister of Agrippa II; noted for her relationship with him and later Titus; retained.
- **"I appeal unto Caesar" → "I call upon Caesar"** (v11, 12, 21, 25) — Greek Καίσαρα ἐπικαλοῦμαι *Kaisara epikaloumai*; ἐπικαλέομαι is the verb "call upon" (2:21; 7:59; 9:14); here it is Paul's legal appeal to the emperor (Nero at this time); pivotal moment of the book.
- **"Caesar" / "Augustus"** — Greek Καῖσαρ *Kaisar* (v8, 10–12, 21) and ὁ Σεβαστός (v21, 25), "the revered one", the Greek for the title Augustus; both refer to Nero (54–68 CE).
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
- **"when Festus was come into the province" → "Festus, having set foot in the province"** (v1) — Greek ἐπιβὰς τῇ ἐπαρχείᾳ. The sentence runs on through v3.
- **"the high priest and the chief of the Jews informed him … and besought him" → "the high priests and the first men of the Jews made disclosure to him … and they were appealing to him"** (v2) — Greek οἱ ἀρχιερεῖς (WH, Treg, NA28; RP singular); οἱ πρῶτοι as 13:50; ἐνεφάνισαν as 24:1; παρεκάλουν, imperfect.
- **"desired favour … laying wait in the way to kill him" → "asking a favor … making an ambush to do away with him along the way"** (v3) — Greek αἰτούμενοι χάριν (χάρις, "grace"); ἐνέδραν ποιοῦντες; ἀνελεῖν as 9:23.
- **"that Paul should be kept at Caesarea, and that he himself would depart shortly thither" → "that Paul was being kept at Caesarea, and that he himself was about to go out shortly"** (v4) — Greek τηρεῖσθαι, present; εἰς Καισάρειαν (WH, Treg, NA28), literally "unto Caesarea"; μέλλειν ἐν τάχει ἐκπορεύεσθαι; no "thither".
- **"which among you are able, go down with me, and accuse this man, if there be any wickedness in him" → "those among you who are powerful, … having gone down together, accuse him, if there is anything amiss in the man"** (v5) — Greek δυνατοί, "powerful" (men of power, or those able to come); συγκαταβάντες, no "with me"; ἄτοπον (WH, Treg, NA28), "out of place"; φησίν is a present, "he says".
- **"more than ten days" → "not more than eight or ten days"** (v6) — Greek οὐ πλείους ὀκτὼ ἢ δέκα (WH, Treg, NA28). "The next day" → "on the morrow": τῇ ἐπαύριον. "Judgment seat" kept: βήματος, as 12:21.
- **"stood round about, and laid many and grievous complaints against Paul, which they could not prove" → "stood around him, bringing down many and heavy charges which they had no strength to show forth"** (v7) — Greek περιέστησαν αὐτόν (WH, Treg, NA28); βαρέα αἰτιώματα καταφέροντες; ἴσχυον; ἀποδεῖξαι, "show forth" (2:22), that is, prove.
- **"While he answered for himself … have I offended any thing at all" → "while Paul defended himself … have I sinned in anything"** (v8) — Greek τοῦ Παύλου ἀπολογουμένου (WH, Treg, NA28); τι ἥμαρτον, the verb "sin".
- **"willing to do the Jews a pleasure … Wilt thou go up" → "wanting to lay up a favor with the Jews … Do you want, having gone up"** (v9) — Greek χάριν καταθέσθαι, as 24:27; Θέλεις … ἀναβάς; κριθῆναι, "to be judged".
- **"where I ought to be judged: to the Jews have I done no wrong, as thou very well knowest" → "where I must be judged. Jews I have wronged in nothing, as you also know quite well"** (v10) — Greek δεῖ; Ἰουδαίους without article; ἠδίκησα (NA28, RP; 2–2, NA28 decides), WH, Treg ἠδίκηκα, perfect; κάλλιον ἐπιγινώσκεις, a comparative, "know better" (than you admit), with the verb "recognize, know fully".
- **"if I be an offender, or have committed … I refuse not to die … no man may deliver me unto them" → "If then I am doing wrong and have practiced … I do not refuse to die … no one can grant me to them"** (v11) — Greek εἰ μὲν οὖν (WH, Treg, NA28) ἀδικῶ, present; πέπραχα; χαρίσασθαι, "grant as a favor" (3:14), the root of χάρις in v3, 9.
- **"when he had conferred with the council, answered, Hast thou appealed unto Caesar?" → "having spoken together with the council, answered, 'You have called upon Caesar'"** (v12) — Greek συλλαλήσας μετὰ τοῦ συμβουλίου, the governor's board of advisers (not the Sanhedrin). The words can be read as a question or as a statement; the printed text has a statement.
- **"king Agrippa and Bernice came unto Caesarea to salute Festus" → "Agrippa the king and Bernice arrived at Caesarea, having greeted Festus"** (v13) — Greek κατήντησαν; ἀσπασάμενοι (WH, Treg, NA28), an aorist participle: literally "having greeted"; the greeting belongs to the arrival.
- **"when they had been there many days, Festus declared Paul's cause unto the king … left in bonds" → "as they were staying there more days, Festus laid before the king the things concerning Paul … left behind … a prisoner"** (v14) — Greek πλείους ἡμέρας διέτριβον; ἀνέθετο as Gal 2:2; τὰ κατὰ τὸν Παῦλον; καταλελειμμένος … δέσμιος.
- **"informed me, desiring to have judgment against him" → "made disclosure, asking for a condemnation against him"** (v15) — Greek ἐνεφάνισαν, no "me"; καταδίκην (WH, Treg, NA28), "a judgment against" (a different word from κατάκριμα); RP δίκην.
- **"It is not the manner of the Romans to deliver any man to die … have licence to answer for himself concerning the crime laid against him" → "it is not a custom for Romans to grant any man … receives a place for defense concerning the accusation"** (v16) — Greek ἔθος Ῥωμαίοις; χαρίζεσθαι as v11; "to die" (εἰς ἀπώλειαν) is Byzantine; τόπον ἀπολογίας; ἐγκλήματος.
- **"without any delay on the morrow I sat" → "having made no delay, on the next day, having sat down"** (v17) — Greek ἀναβολὴν μηδεμίαν ποιησάμενος τῇ ἑξῆς καθίσας. "When they had come together": συνελθόντων αὐτῶν (Treg, NA28, RP; WH has no αὐτῶν; no change in English).
- **"Against whom when the accusers stood up, they brought none accusation of such things as I supposed" → "concerning whom the accusers, having stood up, were bringing no charge of the evil things that I was surmising"** (v18) — Greek περὶ οὗ; αἰτίαν ἔφερον (WH, Treg, NA28); ὧν ἐγὼ ὑπενόουν πονηρῶν (WH, Treg, NA28; RP omits "evil").
- **"of their own superstition, and of one Jesus, which was dead" → "concerning their own fear of the divinities and concerning a certain Jesus who had died"** (v19) — Greek δεισιδαιμονίας, "fear of the divine powers": the word can be respectful ("religion") or scornful ("superstition") and the verse leaves it open; θρησκεία "religion" is another word (26:5). τεθνηκότος, a perfect participle.
- **"because I doubted of such manner of questions, I asked him whether he would go" → "I, being at a loss as to the questioning concerning these things, was saying whether he would wish to go"** (v20) — Greek ἀπορούμενος … τὴν περὶ τούτων ζήτησιν (as 15:2); ἔλεγον εἰ βούλοιτο.
- **"had appealed to be reserved unto the hearing of Augustus … till I might send him" → "called to be kept for the determination of the Augustus … until I should send him up"** (v21) — Greek ἐπικαλεσαμένου τηρηθῆναι; διάγνωσιν, the noun of διαγινώσκω (24:22); τοῦ Σεβαστοῦ, with the article; ἀναπέμψω (WH, Treg, NA28), "send up". "Kept" stands twice (τηρηθῆναι, τηρεῖσθαι).
- **"Then Agrippa said unto Festus, I would also hear the man myself. To morrow, said he" → "And Agrippa to Festus: 'I myself also was wishing to hear the man.' 'Tomorrow,' he says"** (v22) — the Greek has no verb of saying (WH, Treg, NA28; RP ἔφη); Ἐβουλόμην is an imperfect, "I was wishing": either a wish he has had for some time, or a polite "I would wish"; φησίν, present.
- **"with great pomp, and was entered into the place of hearing, with the chief captains, and principal men of the city, at Festus' commandment Paul was brought forth" → "with much pageantry and had entered into the hall of hearing, with both chiliarchs and the men of eminence of the city, and when Festus had commanded, Paul was brought"** (v23) — Greek φαντασίας, "show, display"; ἀκροατήριον; χιλιάρχοις without article; τοῖς κατʼ ἐξοχήν; κελεύσαντος; ἤχθη.
- **"Festus said … ye see this man, about whom all the multitude of the Jews have dealt with me" → "Festus says … you behold this one, concerning whom all the multitude of the Jews petitioned me"** (v24) — Greek φησιν; θεωρεῖτε τοῦτον; ἐνέτυχον (Treg, NA28, RP; WH singular), the verb "intercede" (Rom 8:27), here of pleading against someone; its noun ἔντευξις is "petition". "Ought not" → "must not": μὴ δεῖν.
- **"when I found that he had committed nothing worthy of death, and that he himself hath appealed to Augustus, I have determined to send him" → "I grasped that he had practiced nothing worthy of death; and when this one himself called upon the Augustus, I judged to send him"** (v25) — Greek κατελαβόμην (WH, Treg, NA28), as 10:34; πεπραχέναι; ἔκρινα πέμπειν; "him" is supplied (in RP it stands).
- **"unto my lord … I have brought him forth before you, and specially before thee, O king Agrippa, that, after examination had" → "to the lord … I brought him forth before you, and most of all before you, King Agrippa, so that, the examination having taken place"** (v26) — Greek τῷ κυρίῳ, no "my": a title of the emperor; προήγαγον as 12:6; μάλιστα; τῆς ἀνακρίσεως γενομένης, the noun of ἀνακρίνω "examine" (24:8).
- **"to send a prisoner, and not withal to signify the crimes laid against him" → "sending a prisoner, not also to signify the charges against him"** (v27) — Greek πέμποντα … μὴ καὶ … σημᾶναι; αἰτίας, "charges" as v18.
