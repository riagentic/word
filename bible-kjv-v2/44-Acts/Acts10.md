# Acts 10

[2] a devout man who feared ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ with all his household, gave alms generously to the people, and prayed continually to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[4] And he stared at him in terror and said, "What is it, Lord?" And he said to him, "Your prayers and your alms have ascended as a memorial before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[9] The next day, as they were on their journey and approaching the city, ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ went up on the housetop about the sixth hour to pray.

[21] And ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ went down to the men and said, "I am the one you are looking for. What is the reason for your coming?"

[23] So he invited them in to be his guests.

[24] And on the following day they entered Caesarea. Cornelius was expecting them and had called together his relatives and close friends.

[25] When ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ entered, Cornelius met him and fell down at his feet and worshiped him.

[26] But ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ lifted him up, saying, "Stand up; I too am a man."

[30] And Cornelius said, "Four days ago, about this hour, I was praying in my house at the ninth hour, and behold, a man stood before me in bright clothing

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v38) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v36) — Hebrew messianic title.
- **"Holy Ghost" → "*Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v38, 44, 45, 47) — consistent rendering.
- **"Peter" / "*Kefa*"** (v5, 9, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 21, 25, 26, 32, 34, 44, 45, 46, 47) — Aramaic name used consistently throughout.
- **"baptized" → "immersed"** (v47, 48) — Greek *baptizo* = "to immerse"; consistent rendering.
- **"shalom" / "peace"** (v36) — Greek εἰρήνη *eirēnē* here clearly renders Hebrew שָׁלוֹם *shalom* (covenant peace, wholeness); rendered "peace" in the text.
- **"God is no respecter of persons" → "*Elohim* shows no partiality"** (v34) — modernized from archaic "respecter of persons."
- **Gentile inclusion theme** — theologically central; *Kefa's* vision and Cornelius episode mark the first Gentile reception of the *Ruach HaQodesh*.
