# Acts 26

[1] And Agrippa said to ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, "It is permitted you to speak concerning yourself." Then ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having stretched out his hand, was defending himself:

[2] "Concerning all the things of which I am charged by ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, King Agrippa, I have counted myself blessed, being about to defend myself before you today,

[3] most of all as you are a knower of all the customs and also questions that are among ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧; therefore I beseech you to hear me patiently.

[4] "So then my manner of life from youth, which from the beginning came to be among my nation and in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, all ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ know,

[5] knowing me beforehand, from the first, if they want to testify, that according to the most exact sect of our religion I lived a ⟦pharisee|Parush|Pharisee⟧.

[6] And now I stand being judged upon hope of the promise made by ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ unto our fathers,

[7] unto which our twelve tribes, worshiping in earnestness night and day, hope to arrive; concerning which hope I am charged by ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, O king.

[8] Why is it judged unbelievable among you, if ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ raises the dead?

[9] "So then I thought to myself that I must practice many things contrary to the name of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ the ⟦nazarenes|Notzri|Nazorean⟧;

[10] which I also did in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, and many of the saints I myself shut up in prisons, having received the authority from the high priests, and when they were being done away with I cast a vote against them,

[11] and throughout all the synagogues, punishing them often, I was compelling them to blaspheme, and being exceedingly maddened against them I was persecuting them even as far as the cities outside.

[12] "In which things, as I was going to ⟦damascus|Dammeseq|Damascus⟧ with authority and commission of the high priests,

[13] at midday along the way I saw, O king, a light from heaven, above the brightness of the sun, shining around me and those going with me;

[14] and when we had all fallen down to the ground, I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, '**⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against goads.**'

[15] And I said, 'Who are you, Lord?' And the Lord said, 'I am ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, whom you are persecuting;

[16] but rise and stand upon your feet; for unto this I appeared to you, to appoint you an assistant and a witness both of the things in which you saw me and of the things in which I will appear to you,

[17] delivering you out of the people and out of the nations, unto whom I send you

[18] **to open their eyes, to turn from darkness to light and from the authority of Satan to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a lot among those who have been sanctified by faith, the faith in me**.'

[19] "Therefore, King Agrippa, I did not become disobedient to the heavenly vision,

[20] but to those in ⟦damascus|Dammeseq|Damascus⟧ first and also in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, and through all the country of ⟦judea|Yehudah|Judea⟧, and to the nations I was reporting that they should repent and turn to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, practicing works worthy of the repentance.

[21] On account of these things ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, having apprehended me in the temple, were trying to slay me.

[22] Having therefore attained help, that from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, unto this day I stand testifying to both small and great, saying nothing outside the things which both the prophets and ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ spoke of as about to come to pass:

[23] whether the ⟦mashiach|Mashiach|Christ⟧ is subject to suffering, whether he, first from a resurrection of the dead, is about to proclaim light both to the people and to the nations."

[24] And as he was saying these things in his defense, Festus says with a loud voice, "You are mad, ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧; the many letters are turning you round to madness."

[25] But ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧: "I am not mad," he says, "most excellent Festus, but I speak forth words of truth and of sober-mindedness.

[26] For the king knows about these things, to whom also I speak, speaking boldly; for I am not persuaded that any of these things escapes him, not one; for this has not been practiced in a corner.

[27] Do you believe, King Agrippa, the prophets? I know that you believe."

[28] And Agrippa to ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧: "In a little you are persuading me to make ⟦christian|a Christianos|a Christian⟧."

[29] And ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧: "I would pray to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ that, both in a little and in a great deal, not only you but also all who hear me today might become such as I also am, apart from these chains."

[30] And the king rose up, and the governor, and ⟦bernice|Bernike|Bernice⟧, and those sitting with them,

[31] and having withdrawn, they were speaking to one another, saying, "This man practices nothing worthy of death or of chains."

[32] And Agrippa said to Festus, "This man could have been released, if he had not called upon Caesar."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v6, 8, 18, 20, 22, 29) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v9, 15) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v23) — Hebrew messianic title.
- **"Holy Ghost" / Spirit** — no explicit *Ruach HaQodesh* occurrence in this chapter.
- **"the Hebrew tongue" → "the Hebrew language"** (v14) — Greek τῇ Ἑβραΐδι διαλέκτῳ; as in 21:40 and 22:2, the language of the Hebrews, perhaps Aramaic rather than classical Hebrew.
- **"Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me"** — Paul's third account of the Damascus Road event; archaic language modernized throughout.
- **"it is hard for thee to kick against the pricks" → "It is hard for you to kick against goads"** (v14) — Greek σκληρόν σοι πρὸς κέντρα λακτίζειν; κέντρα (plural, without article) are goads, pointed sticks to prod oxen; "pricks" is archaic.
- **"Christian" → retained** (v28) — Greek Χριστιανόν *Christianon* is the term Agrippa uses; see the v28 line below.
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
- **"Thou art permitted to speak for thyself … stretched forth the hand, and answered for himself" → "It is permitted you to speak concerning yourself … having stretched out his hand, was defending himself"** (v1) — Greek Ἐπιτρέπεταί σοι; περὶ σεαυτοῦ (Treg, NA28; 2–2, NA28 decides), "concerning yourself"; WH, RP ὑπὲρ σεαυτοῦ, "on behalf of yourself". ἀπελογεῖτο, imperfect.
- **"I think myself happy … because I shall answer for myself this day before thee touching all the things whereof I am accused of the Jews" → "Concerning all the things of which I am charged by Jews … I have counted myself blessed, being about to defend myself before you today"** (v2) — Greek ἐγκαλοῦμαι, "have a charge brought against one" (Rom 8:33; its noun ἔγκλημα, 25:16), a different verb from κατηγορέω "accuse"; ὑπὸ Ἰουδαίων, without article (so v3, 7, 21); ἥγημαι, perfect; μακάριον; μέλλων.
- **"because I know thee to be expert in all customs and questions which are among the Jews" → "as you are a knower of all the customs and also questions that are among Jews"** (v3) — Greek γνώστην ὄντα σε, an accusative that hangs loose in the sentence; "because I know" is not in the Greek. "You" after "beseech" is supplied (in RP it stands).
- **"which was at the first among mine own nation at Jerusalem, know all the Jews" → "which from the beginning came to be among my nation and in Jerusalem, all Jews know"** (v4) — Greek τὴν ἀπʼ ἀρχῆς γενομένην ἐν τῷ ἔθνει μου ἔν τε Ἱεροσολύμοις (WH, Treg, NA28 have τε, "and"); πάντες Ἰουδαῖοι (WH, Treg, SBLGNT; NA28, RP "all the Jews"). βίωσις is "the life one has lived".
- **"Which knew me from the beginning, if they would testify, that after the most straitest sect" → "knowing me beforehand, from the first, if they want to testify, that according to the most exact sect"** (v5) — Greek προγινώσκοντες, the verb "foreknow" (Rom 8:29), here of knowing from an earlier time; ἄνωθεν, "from above", here "from the first"; ἀκριβεστάτην, the superlative of ἀκριβής (24:22); αἵρεσιν, "sect" as 24:5; θρησκείας, "religion" as Jas 1:26.
- **"I stand and am judged for the hope" → "I stand being judged upon hope"** (v6) — Greek ἐπʼ ἐλπίδι, without article; εἰς τοὺς πατέρας ἡμῶν (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"Unto which promise our twelve tribes, instantly serving God day and night, hope to come" → "unto which our twelve tribes, worshiping in earnestness night and day, hope to arrive"** (v7) — Greek τὸ δωδεκάφυλον ἡμῶν, one word, "our twelve-tribe people"; ἐν ἐκτενείᾳ; λατρεῦον, "worship" in the sense of serving God; no "God" in the Greek; καταντῆσαι. "Charged by Jews, O king": ὑπὸ Ἰουδαίων, βασιλεῦ (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"Why should it be thought a thing incredible with you, that God should raise the dead?" → "Why is it judged unbelievable among you, if God raises the dead?"** (v8) — Greek κρίνεται; εἰ … ἐγείρει, a present indicative. νεκρούς has no article.
- **"I verily thought with myself, that I ought to do" → "So then I thought to myself that I must practice"** (v9) — Greek ἔδοξα ἐμαυτῷ, "I seemed to myself"; δεῖν; πρᾶξαι. "Of Nazareth" → "the Nazorean": Greek τοῦ Ναζωραίου, as 6:14.
- **"in prison … from the chief priests … when they were put to death, I gave my voice against them" → "in prisons … from the high priests … when they were being done away with I cast a vote against them"** (v10) — Greek ἐν φυλακαῖς, plural; ἀρχιερέων; ἀναιρουμένων as 9:23; κατήνεγκα ψῆφον, "I brought down a pebble", the pebble used in voting (Rev 2:17).
- **"I punished them oft in every synagogue, and compelled them to blaspheme … exceedingly mad … even unto strange cities" → "throughout all the synagogues, punishing them often, I was compelling them to blaspheme … exceedingly maddened … even as far as the cities outside"** (v11) — Greek ἠνάγκαζον, imperfect: it does not say that he succeeded ("I tried to compel"); ἐμμαινόμενος, the root of "mad" in v24–25; εἰς τὰς ἔξω πόλεις.
- **"Whereupon as I went" → "In which things, as I was going"** (v12) — Greek Ἐν οἷς.
- **"in the way a light from heaven … shining round about me and them which journeyed with me" → "along the way … a light from heaven … shining around me and those going with me"** (v13) — Greek κατὰ τὴν ὁδόν; οὐρανόθεν; περιλάμψαν; πορευομένους.
- **"I heard a voice speaking unto me, and saying" → "I heard a voice saying to me"** (v14) — Greek λέγουσαν πρός με (WH, Treg, NA28); "speaking … and" is Byzantine. "To the earth" → "to the ground": εἰς τὴν γῆν, as 9:4.
- **"And he said" → "And the Lord said"** (v15) — Greek ὁ δὲ κύριος εἶπεν (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"I have appeared unto thee for this purpose, to make thee a minister and a witness both of these things which thou hast seen" → "unto this I appeared to you, to appoint you an assistant and a witness both of the things in which you saw me"** (v16) — Greek ὤφθην, "I was seen"; προχειρίσασθαι as 22:14; ὑπηρέτην as 13:5; ὧν τε εἶδές με (WH, NA28; 2–2, NA28 decides), Treg, RP without "me".
- **"Delivering thee from the people, and from the Gentiles, unto whom now I send thee" → "delivering you out of the people and out of the nations, unto whom I send you"** (v17) — Greek ἐξαιρούμενος, as 7:10; 12:11 (the verb can also mean "choosing out"); ἐκ twice (WH, Treg, NA28); "now" is Byzantine.
- **"and to turn them from darkness to light, and from the power of Satan … inheritance among them which are sanctified by faith that is in me" → "to turn from darkness to light and from the authority of Satan … a lot among those who have been sanctified by faith, the faith in me"** (v18) — Greek τοῦ ἐπιστρέψαι, without object: "that they may turn" or "to turn them"; ἐξουσίας; κλῆρον as 1:17; 8:21; πίστει τῇ εἰς ἐμέ, which can go with "sanctified" or with "receive".
- **"Whereupon … I was not disobedient" → "Therefore … I did not become disobedient"** (v19) — Greek Ὅθεν; οὐκ ἐγενόμην ἀπειθής; ὀπτασίᾳ, "vision" (a different word from ὅραμα).
- **"But shewed first unto them of Damascus, and at Jerusalem, and throughout all the coasts of Judaea, and then to the Gentiles … and do works meet for repentance" → "but to those in Damascus first and also in Jerusalem, and through all the country of Judea, and to the nations I was reporting … practicing works worthy of the repentance"** (v20) — Greek ἀπήγγελλον (WH, Treg, NA28), imperfect; πᾶσάν τε τὴν χώραν τῆς Ἰουδαίας (WH, Treg, NA28) is a bare accusative, with no preposition; "through" is supplied (RP has εἰς); πράσσοντας; τῆς μετανοίας, with the article.
- **"the Jews caught me in the temple, and went about to kill me" → "Jews, having apprehended me in the temple, were trying to slay me"** (v21) — Greek Ἰουδαῖοι, without article (WH, Treg, NA28); συλλαβόμενοι (WH, Treg, RP; NA28 adds ὄντα, "when I was"); ἐπειρῶντο; διαχειρίσασθαι as 5:30.
- **"Having therefore obtained help of God, I continue unto this day, witnessing … saying none other things than those which the prophets and Moses did say should come" → "Having therefore attained help, that from God, unto this day I stand testifying … saying nothing outside the things which both the prophets and Moses spoke of as about to come to pass"** (v22) — Greek ἐπικουρίας τυχὼν τῆς ἀπὸ τοῦ θεοῦ; ἕστηκα; μαρτυρόμενος; οὐδὲν ἐκτός; μελλόντων γίνεσθαι.
- **"That Christ should suffer, and that he should be the first that should rise from the dead, and should shew light" → "whether the Mashiach is subject to suffering, whether he, first from a resurrection of the dead, is about to proclaim light"** (v23) — Greek εἰ … εἰ, "if, whether": the points under question; many take it as "that". παθητός, "capable of suffering, subject to suffering"; πρῶτος ἐξ ἀναστάσεως νεκρῶν, without articles; καταγγέλλειν.
- **"as he thus spake for himself, Festus said … Paul, thou art beside thyself; much learning doth make thee mad" → "as he was saying these things in his defense, Festus says … You are mad, Paul; the many letters are turning you round to madness"** (v24) — Greek ἀπολογουμένου; φησιν (WH, Treg, NA28), present; Μαίνῃ, as 12:15; τὰ πολλὰ γράμματα, "the many letters": writings, or learning (John 7:15); εἰς μανίαν περιτρέπει.
- **"But he said, I am not mad, most noble Festus; but speak forth the words of truth and soberness" → "But Paul: 'I am not mad,' he says, 'most excellent Festus, but I speak forth words of truth and of sober-mindedness'"** (v25) — Greek ὁ δὲ Παῦλος (WH, Treg, NA28) … φησίν; κράτιστε as 24:3; σωφροσύνης as 1 Tim 2:9; ῥήματα without article.
- **"before whom also I speak freely: for I am persuaded that none of these things are hidden from him; for this thing was not done in a corner" → "to whom also I speak, speaking boldly; for I am not persuaded that any of these things escapes him, not one; for this has not been practiced in a corner"** (v26) — Greek παρρησιαζόμενος as 13:46; λανθάνειν γὰρ αὐτόν τι τούτων οὐ πείθομαι οὐθέν (τι: Treg, NA28, RP; WH omits; no change in English), with a doubled negative; λανθάνω as 2 Pet 3:5; πεπραγμένον.
- **"Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian" → "In a little you are persuading me to make a Christian"** (v28) — Greek Ἐν ὀλίγῳ με πείθεις Χριστιανὸν ποιῆσαι (WH, Treg, NA28; RP γενέσθαι, "to become"), translated word for word. ἐν ὀλίγῳ is "in a little" (time, or effort), not "almost". ποιῆσαι, "to make", can be read: "you are persuading me, so as to make me a Christian"; "you are persuading me to act the Christian"; or "you persuade yourself that you have made me a Christian". The Greek has no verb of saying, and the words can be a statement or a question; the printed text has a statement. Whether Agrippa speaks in earnest or with irony is not said.
- **"I would to God, that not only thou … were both almost, and altogether such as I am, except these bonds" → "I would pray to God that, both in a little and in a great deal, not only you … might become such as I also am, apart from these chains"** (v29) — Greek Εὐξαίμην ἂν τῷ θεῷ, the verb "pray" (2 Cor 13:7); καὶ ἐν ὀλίγῳ καὶ ἐν μεγάλῳ (WH, Treg, NA28; RP πολλῷ), taking up Agrippa's "in a little"; γενέσθαι; δεσμῶν as Phil 1:7. No verb of saying (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"And when he had thus spoken, the king rose up" → "And the king rose up"** (v30) — the first words are Byzantine.
- **"when they were gone aside, they talked between themselves … doeth nothing worthy of death or of bonds" → "having withdrawn, they were speaking to one another … practices nothing worthy of death or of chains"** (v31) — Greek ἀναχωρήσαντες; ἐλάλουν; πράσσει.
- **"might have been set at liberty, if he had not appealed unto Caesar" → "could have been released, if he had not called upon Caesar"** (v32) — Greek Ἀπολελύσθαι ἐδύνατο; ἐπεκέκλητο, as 25:11.
