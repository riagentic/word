# Acts 23

[1] And ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having gazed at the ⟦sanhedrin|Sanhedrin|council⟧, said, "Men, brothers, I have lived as a citizen to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in all good conscience until this day."

[2] But the high priest ⟦ananias|Chananyah|Ananias⟧ ordered those standing by him to strike his mouth.

[3] Then ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ said to him, "⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is about to strike you, whitewashed wall! And do you sit judging me according to the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧, and contrary to the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ command me to be struck?"

[4] And those standing by said, "Do you revile the high priest of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧?"

[5] And ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ said, "I did not know, brothers, that he is high priest; for it is written, '**You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people**.'"

[6] But ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having come to know that the one part is of ⟦sadducees|Tzedukim|Sadducees⟧ and the other of ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧, was crying out in the ⟦sanhedrin|Sanhedrin|council⟧, "Men, brothers, I am a ⟦pharisee|Parush|Pharisee⟧, a son of ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧; concerning hope and resurrection of the dead I am being judged."

[7] And when he had said this, there arose a discord of the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ and ⟦sadducees|Tzedukim|Sadducees⟧, and the multitude was split.

[8] For ⟦sadducees|Tzedukim|Sadducees⟧ say that there is no resurrection, nor angel nor spirit, but ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ confess both.

[9] And there arose a great cry, and some of the scribes of the part of the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧, having risen, were contending fiercely, saying, "We find nothing evil in this man; but if a spirit spoke to him, or an angel —"

[10] And as much discord was arising, the chiliarch, having feared lest ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ be torn apart by them, commanded the troop to go down and snatch him out of their midst, and to lead him into the barracks.

[11] And on the following night the Lord, having stood by him, said, "Be of good courage, for **as you solemnly testified the things about me in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, so you must testify also in ⟦rome|Roma|Rome⟧**."

[12] And when day came, the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, having made a gathering, cursed themselves, saying that they would neither eat nor drink until they killed ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧.

[13] And there were more than forty who made this conspiracy;

[14] who, having approached the high priests and the elders, said, "With a curse we cursed ourselves to taste nothing until we kill ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧.

[15] Now therefore you, with the ⟦sanhedrin|Sanhedrin|council⟧, make disclosure to the chiliarch, that he may bring him down to you, as being about to determine more accurately the things about him; and we, before he draws near, are ready to do away with him."

[16] But the son of ⟦paul|Paulus's|Paul's⟧ sister, having heard of the ambush, having arrived and entered into the barracks, reported to ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧.

[17] And ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having called to himself one of the centurions, said, "Lead this young man away to the chiliarch, for he has something to report to him."

[18] So he, having taken him along, brought him to the chiliarch and says, "The prisoner ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having called me to himself, requested me to bring this young man to you, as he has something to speak to you."

[19] And the chiliarch, having laid hold of his hand and withdrawn, was inquiring privately, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

[20] And he said, "The ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ agreed to request you that tomorrow you bring ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ down to the ⟦sanhedrin|Sanhedrin|council⟧, as though it were about to inquire something more accurately about him.

[21] You therefore, do not be persuaded by them, for more than forty men of them are lying in ambush for him, who cursed themselves neither to eat nor to drink until they do away with him; and now they are ready, awaiting the promise from you."

[22] So the chiliarch sent the young man away, having charged him to tell no one "that you disclosed these things to me."

[23] And having called to himself a certain two of the centurions, he said, "Make ready two hundred soldiers, that they may go as far as Caesarea, and seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen, from the third hour of the night,"

[24] and to provide beasts, so that having set ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ on them they might save him through to Felix the governor,

[25] having written a letter having this pattern:

[26] "Claudius Lysias to the most excellent governor Felix, greeting.

[27] This man, having been apprehended by the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ and being about to be done away with by them, I, having come upon them with the troop, delivered, having learned that he is a Roman;

[28] and wishing to come to know the cause for which they were bringing a charge against him, I brought him down into their ⟦sanhedrin|Sanhedrin|council⟧;

[29] whom I found to be charged concerning questions of their ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧, but having no accusation worthy of death or of chains.

[30] And when it was disclosed to me that there would be a plot against the man, at once I sent him to you, having charged the accusers also to speak against him before you."

[31] So the soldiers, according to the orders given them, having taken up ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, brought him by night to Antipatris;

[32] and on the morrow, having let the horsemen go away with him, they returned to the barracks;

[33] who, having entered into Caesarea and handed the letter to the governor, presented ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ also to him.

[34] And having read it and asked from what province he is, and having learned by inquiry that he is from ⟦cilicia|Kilikia|Cilicia⟧,

[35] "I will hear you fully," he said, "when your accusers also arrive"; having commanded him to be guarded in ⟦herod|Herodes's|Herod's⟧ praetorium.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 3, 4) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Holy Ghost" / Spirit** — no explicit *Ruach HaQodesh* occurrence in this chapter.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** — no direct occurrence in narrative text; referenced as "the Lord" in v11.
- **"Torah"** (v3, 29) — Greek νόμος; "contrary to the Torah" is παρανομῶν, one word, "acting against the law". In v29 the word stands in the Roman's letter ("their law").
- **"Felix"** (v24, 26) — Marcus Antonius Felix, Roman procurator of Judea; retained as proper name.
- **"Lysias" / "Claudius Lysias"** (v26) — the chiliarch, a Roman military tribune; retained.
- **"Antipatris"** (v31) — ancient city of *Afek* renamed by Herod after his father Antipater; retained.
- **v5** — OT quotation: Ex 22:28 ("You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people").
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
- **"earnestly beholding the council, said, Men and brethren, I have lived in all good conscience before God" → "having gazed at the Sanhedrin, said, 'Men, brothers, I have lived as a citizen to God in all good conscience'"** (v1) — Greek ἀτενίσας; Ἄνδρες ἀδελφοί (also v6); πεπολίτευμαι τῷ θεῷ, the verb of Phil 1:27, "live as a citizen", with a dative: "to God" or "for God".
- **"commanded them that stood by him to smite him on the mouth" → "ordered those standing by him to strike his mouth"** (v2) — Greek ἐπέταξεν (κελεύω "command" stands in v3); τύπτειν αὐτοῦ τὸ στόμα.
- **"God shall smite thee, thou whited wall: for sittest thou to judge me" → "God is about to strike you, whitewashed wall! And do you sit judging me"** (v3) — Greek Τύπτειν σε μέλλει; τοῖχε κεκονιαμένε, as Matt 23:27; καὶ σὺ κάθῃ κρίνων, a participle.
- **"Revilest thou God's high priest?" kept as "Do you revile the high priest of God?"** (v4) — Greek λοιδορεῖς, a present, no "would".
- **"that he was the high priest" → "that he is high priest"** (v5) — Greek ὅτι ἐστὶν ἀρχιερεύς, without article. "The ruler" → "a ruler": Ἄρχοντα, without article; Exod 22:28 (22:27 in the Greek and Hebrew numbering) as the Greek prints it.
- **"perceived that the one part were Sadducees … he cried out" → "having come to know that the one part is of Sadducees … was crying out"** (v6) — Greek Γνούς; ἔκραζεν, an imperfect (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"the son of a Pharisee: of the hope and resurrection of the dead I am called in question" → "a son of Pharisees; concerning hope and resurrection of the dead I am being judged"** (v6) — Greek υἱὸς Φαρισαίων, plural (WH, Treg, NA28); περὶ ἐλπίδος καὶ ἀναστάσεως νεκρῶν, without articles: "hope and resurrection", perhaps "the hope of the resurrection"; κρίνομαι, "judge". ἐγώ before κρίνομαι: NA28, SBLGNT, RP (2–2, NA28 decides); WH, Treg omit; no difference in English.
- **"when he had so said, there arose a dissension between the Pharisees and the Sadducees: and the multitude was divided" → "when he had said this, there arose a discord of the Pharisees and Sadducees, and the multitude was split"** (v7) — εἰπόντος, "had said" (Treg, NA28); WH and the printed SBLGNT read λαλοῦντος, "as he was speaking this". στάσις as 15:2 (also v10); ἐσχίσθη as 14:4.
- **"neither angel, nor spirit: but the Pharisees confess both" kept** (v8) — Greek μήτε ἄγγελον μήτε πνεῦμα; ὁμολογοῦσιν τὰ ἀμφότερα, "both": resurrection on the one side, angel and spirit on the other; no "them all".
- **"the scribes that were of the Pharisees' part arose, and strove" → "some of the scribes of the part of the Pharisees, having risen, were contending fiercely"** (v9) — Greek τινὲς τῶν γραμματέων (WH, Treg, NA28); διεμάχοντο, an imperfect. "A great cry" kept: κραυγὴ μεγάλη.
- **"but if a spirit or an angel hath spoken to him, let us not fight against God" → "but if a spirit spoke to him, or an angel —"** (v9) — the sentence breaks off in the Greek (WH, Treg, NA28): εἰ δὲ πνεῦμα ἐλάλησεν αὐτῷ ἢ ἄγγελος; "let us not fight against God" is Byzantine. The words can also be read as a question: "What if a spirit spoke to him, or an angel?"
- **"when there arose a great dissension, the chief captain, fearing lest Paul should have been pulled in pieces of them, commanded the soldiers to go down, and to take him by force" → "as much discord was arising, the chiliarch, having feared lest Paul be torn apart by them, commanded the troop to go down and snatch him"** (v10) — Greek γινομένης (WH, NA28); φοβηθείς; διασπασθῇ; τὸ στράτευμα, the body of soldiers ("army" in Rev 19), also v27; ἁρπάσαι, as 8:39.
- **"Be of good cheer, Paul: for as thou hast testified of me in Jerusalem, so must thou bear witness also at Rome" → "Be of good courage, for as you solemnly testified the things about me in Jerusalem, so you must testify also in Rome"** (v11) — Greek Θάρσει ("Paul" is Byzantine); διεμαρτύρω τὰ περὶ ἐμοῦ … μαρτυρῆσαι: two verbs of one root; εἰς both times, "unto Jerusalem … unto Rome".
- **"certain of the Jews banded together, and bound themselves under a curse" → "the Jews, having made a gathering, cursed themselves"** (v12, also v14, 21) — Greek ποιήσαντες συστροφὴν οἱ Ἰουδαῖοι (WH, Treg, NA28), as 19:40; ἀνεθεμάτισαν ἑαυτούς, "put themselves under a curse" (ἀνάθεμα), as Mark 14:71.
- **"conspiracy" kept** (v13) — Greek συνωμοσίαν, "a swearing together".
- **"chief priests" → "high priests"; "We have bound ourselves under a great curse, that we will eat nothing until we have slain Paul" → "With a curse we cursed ourselves to taste nothing until we kill Paul"** (v14) — Greek ἀρχιερεῦσιν; Ἀναθέματι ἀνεθεματίσαμεν, noun and verb of one root, no "great"; γεύσασθαι, as 20:11; ἀποκτείνωμεν.
- **"signify to the chief captain that he bring him down unto you to morrow, as though ye would enquire something more perfectly concerning him … to kill him" → "make disclosure to the chiliarch, that he may bring him down to you, as being about to determine more accurately the things about him … to do away with him"** (v15) — Greek ἐμφανίσατε; "tomorrow" is Byzantine; μέλλοντας διαγινώσκειν ἀκριβέστερον; ἀνελεῖν (also v21, 27).
- **"heard of their lying in wait, he went and entered into the castle, and told Paul" → "having heard of the ambush, having arrived and entered into the barracks, reported to Paul"** (v16) — Greek τὴν ἐνέδραν (WH, Treg, NA28); παραγενόμενος; ἀπήγγειλεν (also v17, 19).
- **"Bring this young man" → "Lead this young man away"** (v17) — Greek ἄπαγε. "Young man" is νεανίαν here and νεανίσκον in v18 (Treg, NA28; WH and the printed SBLGNT νεανίαν) and v22; both are "young man".
- **"said … prayed me to bring this young man unto thee, who hath something to say unto thee" → "says … requested me to bring this young man to you, as he has something to speak to you"** (v18) — Greek φησίν, a present; ἠρώτησεν; λαλῆσαι. "Having taken him along": παραλαβών.
- **"took him by the hand, and went with him aside privately, and asked him" → "having laid hold of his hand and withdrawn, was inquiring privately"** (v19) — Greek ἐπιλαβόμενος τῆς χειρὸς αὐτοῦ; ἀναχωρήσας; κατʼ ἰδίαν ἐπυνθάνετο.
- **"have agreed to desire thee … as though they would enquire somewhat of him more perfectly" → "agreed to request you … as though it were about to inquire something more accurately about him"** (v20) — Greek συνέθεντο, as John 9:22; μέλλον, neuter, of the Sanhedrin (NA28, SBLGNT; 2–2, NA28 decides); WH and Treg read μέλλων, "as though you were about to"; RP μέλλοντα.
- **"do not thou yield unto them … which have bound themselves with an oath … looking for a promise from thee" → "do not be persuaded by them … who cursed themselves … awaiting the promise from you"** (v21) — Greek μὴ πεισθῇς; ἐνεδρεύουσιν, the verb of "ambush" (v16); προσδεχόμενοι τὴν ἀπὸ σοῦ ἐπαγγελίαν, "promise", the usual word; no word "consent".
- **"let the young man depart, and charged him, See thou tell no man that thou hast shewed these things to me" → "sent the young man away, having charged him to tell no one 'that you disclosed these things to me'"** (v22) — Greek ἀπέλυσε; παραγγείλας μηδενὶ ἐκλαλῆσαι ὅτι ταῦτα ἐνεφάνισας πρὸς ἐμέ: the Greek passes from reported speech to the chiliarch's own words, and the verse keeps this.
- **"two centurions … to go to Caesarea, and horsemen threescore and ten, and spearmen two hundred, at the third hour of the night" → "a certain two of the centurions … that they may go as far as Caesarea, and seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen, from the third hour of the night"** (v23) — Greek τινας δύο; ἕως Καισαρείας; ἀπὸ τρίτης ὥρας. δεξιολάβους is a rare word, literally "taking with the right hand"; its exact sense (spearmen, slingers, light-armed guards) is not known.
- **"And provide them beasts, that they may set Paul on, and bring him safe" → "and to provide beasts, so that having set Paul on them they might save him through"** (v24) — Greek κτήνη τε παραστῆσαι, an infinitive: the Greek passes from the chiliarch's own words to reported speech; διασώσωσι, as 27:43.
- **"he wrote a letter after this manner" → "having written a letter having this pattern"** (v25) — Greek γράψας, a participle; ἔχουσαν (WH, Treg, NA28) τὸν τύπον τοῦτον.
- **"sendeth greeting" → "greeting"** (v26) — Greek χαίρειν, as 15:23. "Most excellent": κρατίστῳ.
- **"was taken of the Jews, and should have been killed of them: then came I with an army, and rescued him, having understood that he was a Roman" → "having been apprehended by the Jews and being about to be done away with by them, I, having come upon them with the troop, delivered, having learned that he is a Roman"** (v27) — Greek συλλημφθέντα, as 1:16; μέλλοντα ἀναιρεῖσθαι; ἐπιστάς; ἐξειλάμην, as 12:11 ("him" after it is Byzantine); μαθών; Ῥωμαῖος, with no word "citizen".
- **"when I would have known the cause wherefore they accused him, I brought him forth" → "wishing to come to know the cause for which they were bringing a charge against him, I brought him down"** (v28) — Greek βουλόμενος ἐπιγνῶναι (WH, Treg, NA28); ἐνεκάλουν (also v29), not κατηγορέω "accuse"; κατήγαγον.
- **"Whom I perceived to be accused of questions of their law, but to have nothing laid to his charge worthy of death or of bonds" → "whom I found to be charged concerning questions of their law, but having no accusation worthy of death or of chains"** (v29) — Greek εὗρον; ζητημάτων, as 15:2; ἔγκλημα; δεσμῶν.
- **"when it was told me how that the Jews laid wait for the man, I sent straightway to thee, and gave commandment to his accusers also to say before thee what they had against him. Farewell" → "when it was disclosed to me that there would be a plot against the man, at once I sent him to you, having charged the accusers also to speak against him before you"** (v30) — Greek μηνυθείσης, as John 11:57; ἐπιβουλῆς, as 20:3; ἐξαυτῆς (WH, NA28; Treg ἐξ αὐτῶν, "from among them"); "by the Jews" and "Farewell" are Byzantine; "him" after "sent" is supplied. λέγειν πρὸς αὐτόν (WH, Treg, SBLGNT), "to speak against him"; NA28 and RP add τά, "to say the things against him".
- **"as it was commanded them, took Paul" → "according to the orders given them, having taken up Paul"** (v31) — Greek κατὰ τὸ διατεταγμένον αὐτοῖς, "according to what had been ordered them": διατάσσω of an everyday order, "give orders" (as 20:13; 24:23; "ordain" of a standing rule, Gal 3:19); ἀναλαβόντες.
- **"they left the horsemen to go with him" → "having let the horsemen go away with him"** (v32) — Greek ἐάσαντες … ἀπέρχεσθαι (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"when they came to Caesarea, and delivered the epistle" → "having entered into Caesarea and handed the letter"** (v33) — Greek εἰσελθόντες; ἀναδόντες.
- **"when the governor had read the letter, he asked of what province he was. And when he understood" → "having read it and asked from what province he is, and having learned by inquiry"** (v34) — "the governor" is Byzantine, "it" is supplied; ἐπερωτήσας; πυθόμενος, the verb "inquire". The sentence runs on into v35.
- **"I will hear thee … when thine accusers are also come. And he commanded him to be kept in Herod's judgment hall" → "I will hear you fully … when your accusers also arrive; having commanded him to be guarded in Herod's praetorium"** (v35) — Greek Διακούσομαι, "hear through"; παραγένωνται; κελεύσας, a participle (WH, Treg, NA28); φυλάσσεσθαι; πραιτωρίῳ, the Latin word, as Mark 15:16.

### Notes
