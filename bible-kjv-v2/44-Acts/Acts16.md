# Acts 16

[1] And he arrived also at Derbe and at Lystra. And behold, a certain disciple was there, ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧ by name, son of a faithful Jewish woman, but of a ⟦greek|Yevani|Greek⟧ father,

[2] who was testified to by the brothers in Lystra and ⟦iconium|Ikonion|Iconium⟧.

[3] This one ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ wanted to go out with him, and having taken him he circumcised him because of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ who were in those places, for they all knew that his father was a ⟦greek|Yevani|Greek⟧.

[4] And as they were going through the cities, they were delivering to them, to guard, the decrees that had been judged by the apostles and elders who were in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[5] So the churches were being made strong in the faith and were abounding in number day by day.

[6] And they went through the Phrygian and Galatian country, having been hindered by the ⟦ruach-haqodesh|Ruach HaQodesh|Holy Spirit⟧ from speaking the word in Asia;

[7] and having come opposite Mysia, they were trying to go into Bithynia, and the Spirit of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ did not let them;

[8] and having passed by Mysia, they went down to Troas.

[9] And a vision through the night was seen by ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧: a certain man, a ⟦macedonians|Makedon|Macedonian⟧, was standing and appealing to him and saying, "Having crossed over into ⟦macedonia|Makedonia|Macedonia⟧, help us."

[10] And when he saw the vision, immediately we sought to go out into ⟦macedonia|Makedonia|Macedonia⟧, concluding that ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ had called us to himself to preach the gospel to them.

[11] So, having put out to sea from Troas, we ran a straight course to ⟦samothrace|Samothrake|Samothrace⟧, and on the following day to Neapolis,

[12] and from there to ⟦philippi|Philippoi|Philippi⟧, which is a first city of the district of ⟦macedonia|Makedonia|Macedonia⟧, a colony. And we were in this city, staying some days.

[13] And on the day of the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ we went out outside the gate beside a river, where we supposed a place of prayer to be, and having sat down we were speaking to the women who had come together.

[14] And a certain woman, Lydia by name, a seller of purple of the city of ⟦thyatira|Thyateira|Thyatira⟧, revering ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, was hearing, whose heart the Lord opened up to give heed to the things spoken by ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧.

[15] And when she was ⟦immerse|immersed|baptized⟧, and her house, she appealed, saying, "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, having entered into my house, remain." And she constrained us.

[16] And it came to pass, as we were going to the place of prayer, that a certain slave woman having a spirit, a python, met us, who was providing much business to her lords by divining.

[17] This woman, following after ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ and us, was crying out, saying, "These men are slaves of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ the Most High, who proclaim to you a way of salvation."

[18] And this she was doing for many days. But ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, being greatly vexed, and having turned, said to the spirit, "I charge you in the name of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ to come out from her." And it came out in that very hour.

[19] But her lords, having seen that the hope of their business had gone out, having laid hold of ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ and ⟦silas|Sila|Silas⟧, drew them into the marketplace before the rulers,

[20] and having brought them to the magistrates, they said, "These men are utterly troubling our city, being ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧,

[21] and they proclaim customs which it is not permitted for us to accept nor to do, being Romans."

[22] And the crowd rose up together against them, and the magistrates, having torn their garments off them, were commanding to beat them with rods;

[23] and having laid many blows upon them, they threw them into prison, charging the jailer to keep them securely;

[24] who, having received such a charge, threw them into the inner prison and made their feet secure in the stocks.

[25] And about midnight ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ and ⟦silas|Sila|Silas⟧, praying, were singing hymns to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and the prisoners were listening to them;

[26] and suddenly there came a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the jail were shaken; and instantly all the doors were opened, and the chains of all were loosened.

[27] And the jailer, having come out of sleep and seen the doors of the prison opened, having drawn the sword was about to do away with himself, supposing the prisoners to have escaped.

[28] But ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ called with a loud voice, saying, "Do nothing bad to yourself, for we are all here."

[29] And having asked for lights, he leaped in, and having become trembling he fell down before ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ and ⟦silas|Sila|Silas⟧,

[30] and having led them forth outside, he said, "Lords, what must I do that I may be saved?"

[31] And they said, "**Believe upon the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, and you will be saved, you and your house.**"

[32] And they spoke to him the word of the Lord, with all those in his house.

[33] And having taken them along in that hour of the night, he bathed them from the blows, and he was ⟦immerse|immersed|baptized⟧, he and all his, instantly,

[34] and having brought them up into the house, he set a table before them, and exulted with all his house, having believed ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[35] And when day had come, the magistrates sent the rod-bearers, saying, "Release those men."

[36] And the jailer reported these words to ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧: "The magistrates have sent that you be released; now therefore, going out, go in peace."

[37] But ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ said to them, "Having beaten us publicly, uncondemned, men who are Romans, they threw us into prison; and now do they cast us out secretly? No indeed; but let them come themselves and lead us out."

[38] And the rod-bearers reported these words to the magistrates; and they were afraid, having heard that they are Romans,

[39] and having come, they appealed to them, and having led them out they were requesting them to go away from the city.

[40] And having gone out from the prison, they entered in to Lydia, and having seen the brothers they encouraged them and went out.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v10, 14, 17, 25, 34) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Holy Ghost" / "Spirit of Jesus"** (v6, 7) — Greek τοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος; τὸ πνεῦμα Ἰησοῦ, "the Spirit of *Yeshua*" (WH, Treg, NA28; RP has no "of Jesus").
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v7, 18, 31) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v18) — Hebrew messianic title.
- **"baptized" → "immersed"** (v15, 33) — Greek *baptizo* = "to immerse"; consistent rendering.
- **"Neapolis"** (v11) — Greek Νέαν Πόλιν, two words (WH, Treg, NA28), "New City"; the name is kept.
- **"a spirit of divination" → "a spirit, a python"** (v16) — Greek πνεῦμα πύθωνα (WH, Treg, NA28): two nouns side by side, "a spirit, a python". Python was the serpent of Delphi, and the word came to be used of soothsayers and ventriloquists. RP reads πύθωνος, "a spirit of Python". No word "divination" stands here; "by divining" is μαντευομένη.
- **"Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ" → "Believe upon the Lord *Yeshua*"** (v31) — Greek ἐπὶ τὸν κύριον Ἰησοῦν, "upon", as 9:42; 11:17; "Christ" is Byzantine. "House" is οἶκος (v15, 31, 34) and οἰκία (v32); no separate word "household" or "family".
- **"being Romans" kept** (v21, 37, 38) — Greek Ῥωμαίοις οὖσιν, ἀνθρώπους Ῥωμαίους ὑπάρχοντας, Ῥωμαῖοί εἰσιν; the word "citizens" is not in the Greek.
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
- **"Then came he" → "And he arrived"** (v1) — Greek Κατήντησεν, as Eph 4:13; Phil 3:11; the name "Paul" is not in the Greek. "And behold" (καὶ ἰδού) is kept. "Which was a Jewess, and believed" → "a faithful Jewish woman": γυναικὸς Ἰουδαίας πιστῆς; πιστός is "faithful", here also "believing". "But his father was a Greek" → "but of a Greek father": πατρὸς δὲ Ἕλληνος, a genitive.
- **"was well reported of" → "was testified to"** (v2) — Greek ἐμαρτυρεῖτο, as 6:3; 10:22; no "well".
- **"Him would Paul have to go forth with him" → "This one Paul wanted to go out with him"** (v3) — Greek τοῦτον … σὺν αὐτῷ ἐξελθεῖν; the name "Timothy" is not repeated.
- **"they delivered them the decrees for to keep, that were ordained" → "they were delivering to them, to guard, the decrees that had been judged"** (v4) — Greek παρεδίδοσαν, imperfect (the verb of παράδοσις "tradition"); φυλάσσειν; τὰ δόγματα τὰ κεκριμένα, the verb of 15:19 ("I judge").
- **"established" → "being made strong"; "increased in number daily" → "were abounding in number day by day"** (v5) — Greek ἐστερεοῦντο, as 3:7, 16; ἐπερίσσευον; καθʼ ἡμέραν, as 2:46–47.
- **"Phrygia and the region of Galatia" → "the Phrygian and Galatian country"** (v6) — Greek τὴν Φρυγίαν καὶ Γαλατικὴν χώραν (WH, Treg, NA28), one article for both: two adjectives with one noun, or "Phrygia and the Galatian country". "Forbidden" → "hindered": κωλυθέντες. The participle can give the reason for the route or what happened on it.
- **"After they were come to Mysia, they assayed … but the Spirit suffered them not" → "having come opposite Mysia, they were trying … and the Spirit of *Yeshua* did not let them"** (v7) — Greek κατὰ τὴν Μυσίαν, "down along, over against"; ἐπείραζον, imperfect of πειράζω ("tempt, try"); καί, "and"; εἴασεν, as 14:16.
- **"came down" → "went down"** (v8) — Greek κατέβησαν.
- **"a vision appeared to Paul in the night; There stood a man of Macedonia, and prayed him" → "a vision through the night was seen by Paul: a certain man, a Macedonian, was standing and appealing to him"** (v9) — Greek ὅραμα διὰ νυκτὸς … ὤφθη (as 13:31); ἀνὴρ Μακεδών τις ἦν ἑστὼς καὶ παρακαλῶν. "Come over … and help" → "Having crossed over …, help": Διαβὰς … βοήθησον, a participle and one imperative.
- **"after he had seen the vision, immediately we endeavoured" → "when he saw the vision, immediately we sought"** (v10) — the name "Paul" is not in the Greek; ἐζητήσαμεν. From here the narrative says "we". "Assuredly gathering that the Lord had called us" → "concluding that God had called us to himself": συμβιβάζοντες, "putting together" (9:22 "proving"); προσκέκληται, "has called to himself", as 13:2; ὁ θεός (WH, Treg, NA28), RP ὁ κύριος.
- **"Therefore loosing from Troas, we came with a straight course" → "So, having put out to sea from Troas, we ran a straight course"** (v11) — Greek Ἀναχθέντες, as 13:13; εὐθυδρομήσαμεν, one verb. "So" is οὖν (WH, Treg, RP); NA28 δέ, "and". "The next day" → "on the following day": τῇ ἐπιούσῃ, as 7:26.
- **"which is the chief city of that part of Macedonia, and a colony" → "which is a first city of the district of Macedonia, a colony"** (v12) — Greek ἥτις ἐστὶν πρώτη τῆς μερίδος Μακεδονίας πόλις, κολωνία (WH, Treg). The words can also be taken "which is the first city of the district, of Macedonia"; Philippi was not the capital. NA28 prints πρώτης μερίδος τῆς, "a city of the first district of Macedonia", an editor's emendation which no Greek manuscript has. κολωνία is the Latin word *colonia*; the word "Roman" is not in the Greek. "We were … abiding" → "we were …, staying": ἦμεν … διατρίβοντες.
- **"on the sabbath we went out of the city by a river side, where prayer was wont to be made" → "on the day of the *Shabbat* we went out outside the gate beside a river, where we supposed a place of prayer to be"** (v13) — Greek τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῶν σαββάτων, as 13:14; ἔξω τῆς πύλης (WH, Treg, NA28; RP "the city"); οὗ ἐνομίζομεν προσευχὴν εἶναι (WH, Treg, NA28; RP ἐνομίζετο προσευχὴ εἶναι, "where prayer was accustomed to be"). προσευχή is the word "prayer"; among the Jews it was also the name of a house or place of prayer, and so here and in v16. "Spake" → "were speaking": ἐλαλοῦμεν, imperfect.
- **"which worshipped God, heard us" → "revering God, was hearing"** (v14) — Greek σεβομένη τὸν θεόν, as 13:43, 50; ἤκουεν, imperfect, without object. "Opened" → "opened up": διήνοιξεν, as 7:56. "That she attended unto" → "to give heed to": προσέχειν, as 8:6.
- **"her household" → "her house"; "besought us" → "appealed"; "come into my house, and abide there" → "having entered into my house, remain"** (v15) — Greek ὁ οἶκος αὐτῆς; παρεκάλεσεν, without object; εἰσελθόντες … μένετε, the verb μένω ("abide" of persons), here of a stay in a place: "remain". "Constrained" kept: παρεβιάσατο, as Luke 24:29.
- **"a certain damsel possessed with a spirit of divination" → "a certain slave woman having a spirit, a python"** (v16) — Greek παιδίσκην τινά, as 12:13; ἔχουσαν, "having". "Brought her masters much gain by soothsaying" → "was providing much business to her lords by divining": ἐργασίαν … παρεῖχεν (ἐργασία is work, business, and the profit from it; "gain" is κέρδος), as 19:24–25; τοῖς κυρίοις, the plural of "lord", which the jailer uses in v30.
- **"followed" → "following after"; "the servants of the most high God, which shew unto us the way of salvation" → "slaves of God the Most High, who proclaim to you a way of salvation"** (v17) — Greek κατακολουθοῦσα; δοῦλοι; καταγγέλλουσιν; ὑμῖν (WH, Treg, NA28), RP ἡμῖν "to us"; ὁδὸν σωτηρίας has no article.
- **"being grieved" → "being greatly vexed"; "I command thee" → "I charge you"; "the same hour" → "in that very hour"** (v18) — Greek διαπονηθείς, as 4:2; Παραγγέλλω; αὐτῇ τῇ ὥρᾳ. The words ἐπιστρέψας τῷ πνεύματι εἶπεν can be read "having turned, said to the spirit" or "having turned to the spirit, said".
- **"the hope of their gains was gone" → "the hope of their business had gone out"** (v19) — Greek ἐξῆλθεν, the verb of v18 ("it came out"). "Caught" → "having laid hold of": ἐπιλαβόμενοι. "Drew" kept: εἵλκυσαν, as John 21:6. "Rulers": ἄρχοντας.
- **"magistrates" kept** (v20, 22, 35, 36, 38) — Greek στρατηγοί, the word rendered "captain" of the temple (4:1; 5:24, 26). In a Roman colony it is the Greek title of the two chief officials (Latin *duoviri*, *praetores*), who are not soldiers; "captains" would say the wrong thing here.
- **"do exceedingly trouble our city" → "are utterly troubling our city"** (v20) — Greek ἐκταράσσουσιν, a strengthened form of ταράσσω "trouble" (15:24). The Greek order sets "being *Yehudim*" last, against "being Romans" (v21).
- **"teach customs, which are not lawful for us to receive, neither to observe" → "proclaim customs which it is not permitted for us to accept nor to do"** (v21) — Greek καταγγέλλουσιν; οὐκ ἔξεστιν; παραδέχεσθαι οὐδὲ ποιεῖν.
- **"the multitude rose up together" → "the crowd rose up together"; "rent off their clothes, and commanded to beat them" → "having torn their garments off them, were commanding to beat them with rods"** (v22) — Greek ὁ ὄχλος; περιρήξαντες αὐτῶν τὰ ἱμάτια: the garments of Paul and Silas; ἐκέλευον, imperfect; ῥαβδίζειν, the verb of ῥάβδος "rod" (v35 ῥαβδοῦχοι).
- **"had laid many stripes" → "having laid many blows"; "cast" → "threw"; "to keep them safely" → "to keep them securely"** (v23–24) — Greek πληγάς, as 2 Cor 6:5; ἔβαλον, ἔβαλεν; ἀσφαλῶς, and v24 ἠσφαλίσατο "made secure" is its verb. "Jailer" is δεσμοφύλαξ, "keeper of prisoners". "Stocks" is τὸ ξύλον, "the wood", the word rendered "tree" in 5:30; 10:39; 13:29.
- **"prayed, and sang praises unto God" → "praying, were singing hymns to God"; "heard them" → "were listening to them"** (v25) — Greek προσευχόμενοι ὕμνουν; ἐπηκροῶντο.
- **"the prison" → "the jail"; "immediately" → "instantly"; "every one's bands were loosed" → "the chains of all were loosened"** (v26) — Greek δεσμωτηρίου (φυλακή "prison" in v23, 24, 27, 37, 40), as 5:21, 23; παραχρῆμα; τὰ δεσμὰ ἀνέθη.
- **"awaking out of his sleep" → "having come out of sleep"; "would have killed himself" → "was about to do away with himself"; "had been fled" → "to have escaped"** (v27) — Greek ἔξυπνος γενόμενος; ἤμελλεν ἑαυτὸν ἀναιρεῖν, the verb of 9:23; ἐκπεφευγέναι. "The sword": τὴν μάχαιραν (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"cried" → "called"; "Do thyself no harm" → "Do nothing bad to yourself"** (v28) — Greek ἐφώνησεν; Μηδὲν πράξῃς σεαυτῷ κακόν (πράσσω, "practice"). The verse follows the word order μεγάλῃ φωνῇ ὁ Παῦλος (NA28); RP φωνῇ μεγάλῃ ὁ Παῦλος, Treg φωνῇ μεγάλῃ Παῦλος, WH Παῦλος μεγάλῃ φωνῇ. No difference in English.
- **"called for a light, and sprang in, and came trembling" → "having asked for lights, he leaped in, and having become trembling"** (v29) — Greek αἰτήσας φῶτα, plural; εἰσεπήδησεν, as 14:14; ἔντρομος γενόμενος, as 7:32. The name "the jailer" is not repeated in the Greek.
- **"Sirs" → "Lords"; "what must I do to be saved" → "what must I do that I may be saved"** (v30) — Greek Κύριοι, the word of v16, 19 and of "the Lord" in v31; ἵνα σωθῶ.
- **"to all that were in his house" → "with all those in his house"** (v32) — Greek σύν (WH, Treg, NA28), RP καί. "The word of the Lord": τοῦ κυρίου (Treg, NA28, RP); WH τοῦ θεοῦ.
- **"took them the same hour" → "having taken them along in that hour"; "washed their stripes" → "bathed them from the blows"; "all his, straightway" → "all his, instantly"** (v33) — Greek παραλαβών; ἔλουσεν ἀπὸ τῶν πληγῶν (λούω, John 13:10); οἱ αὐτοῦ πάντες, "all those of him"; παραχρῆμα.
- **"into his house, he set meat before them, and rejoiced, believing in God with all his house" → "into the house, he set a table before them, and exulted with all his house, having believed God"** (v34) — Greek εἰς τὸν οἶκον (WH, NA28; Treg, RP add "his"); παρέθηκεν τράπεζαν; ἠγαλλιάσατο; πεπιστευκὼς τῷ θεῷ, a dative: "believed God". πανοικεί, "with the whole house", stands between the two verbs and can go with either.
- **"the serjeants" → "the rod-bearers"; "Let those men go" → "Release those men"** (v35, 38) — Greek ῥαβδούχους, "rod-holders", the Greek name of the Roman lictors who carried the rods; Ἀπόλυσον, the verb "send away", as 4:21.
- **"told this saying" → "reported these words"; "to let you go" → "that you be released"; "depart" → "going out"** (v36) — Greek ἀπήγγειλεν τοὺς λόγους τούτους (NA28, RP; WH, Treg have no "these"); ἵνα ἀπολυθῆτε; ἐξελθόντες.
- **"They have beaten us openly uncondemned, being Romans, and have cast us into prison; and now do they thrust us out privily? nay verily" → "Having beaten us publicly, uncondemned, men who are Romans, they threw us into prison; and now do they cast us out secretly? No indeed"** (v37) — Greek Δείραντες, as 5:40; δημοσίᾳ; ἀνθρώπους Ῥωμαίους ὑπάρχοντας; ἐκβάλλουσιν; λάθρᾳ; οὐ γάρ. "Fetch us out" → "lead us out": ἐξαγαγέτωσαν, as v39 and 5:19.
- **"told" → "reported"; "words": ῥήματα** (v38) — Greek ἀπήγγειλαν; in v36 "words" is λόγους.
- **"besought them" → "appealed to them"; "desired them to depart out of" → "were requesting them to go away from"** (v39) — Greek παρεκάλεσαν, the verb of v9, 15, 40; no word "apologize"; ἠρώτων ἀπελθεῖν ἀπό (WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"entered into the house of Lydia" → "entered in to Lydia"; "comforted" → "encouraged"; "departed" → "went out"** (v40) — Greek εἰσῆλθον πρὸς τὴν Λυδίαν; παρεκάλεσαν, as 15:32 (also "exhorted"); ἐξῆλθαν.
