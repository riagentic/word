# Ezekiel 19

[1] And you, take up a lamentation for the princes of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧,

[2] and say:

What a lioness was your mother among lions!
She couched in the midst of young lions,
rearing her cubs.

[3] And she brought up one of her cubs;
he became a young lion,
and he learned to catch prey;
he devoured men.

[4] The nations heard about him;
he was caught in their pit,
and they brought him with hooks
to the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[5] When she saw that she waited in vain,
that her hope was lost,
she took another of her cubs
and made him a young lion.

[6] He prowled among the lions;
he became a young lion,
and he learned to catch prey;
he devoured men,

[7] and seized their widows.
He laid waste their cities,
and the land was appalled and all who were in it
at the sound of his roaring.

[8] Then the nations set against him
from provinces on every side;
they spread their net over him;
he was taken in their pit.

[9] With hooks they put him in a cage
and brought him to the king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧;
they brought him into custody,
that his voice should no more be heard
on the mountains of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[10] Your mother was like a vine in a vineyard,
planted by the water,
fruitful and full of branches
by reason of abundant water.

[11] Its strong stems became
rulers' scepters;
it towered aloft
among the thick boughs;
it was seen in its height
with the mass of its branches.

[12] But the vine was plucked up in fury,
cast down to the ground;
the east wind dried up its fruit;
they were stripped off and withered.
As for its strong stem, fire consumed it.

[13] Now it is planted in the wilderness,
in a dry and thirsty land.

[14] And fire has gone out from the stem of its shoots,
has consumed its fruit,
so that there remains in it no strong stem,
no scepter for ruling.

This is a lamentation and has become a lamentation.

## Changes

- **Poetry formatted with blockquote indentation** (vv2-14) — KJV prose; the entire chapter is a *qinah* (lamentation) in 3:2 meter — a dirge form.
- **"take thou up a lamentation for the princes of Israel" → "take up a lamentation for the princes of Israel"** (v1) — modernized.
- **"whelps" → "cubs"** (vv2, 3, 5) — Hebrew גּוּרֶיהָ *gureha* = her cubs; "cubs" more natural than "whelps."
- **"brought him with chains" → "brought him with hooks"** (v4) — Hebrew בַּחַחִים *bachachim* = with hooks; the nose-hooks used to lead captives (cf. 2 Kgs 19:28); "hooks" more accurate.
- **"he knew their desolate palaces" → "seized their widows"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיֵּדַע אַלְמְנוֹתָיו *vayyeda' almnotav* = he knew their widows (i.e., violated their widows); "widows" more accurate; KJV "desolate palaces" misreads *almenot* = widows as *armonot* = palaces.
- **"they put him in ward in chains" → "with hooks they put him in a cage"** (v9) — Hebrew וַיִּתְּנוּהוּ בַּסּוּגַר בַּחַחִים *vayyittenohu bassugar bachachim* = they put him in a cage with hooks; *sugar* = cage/enclosure.
- **"unto holds" → "into custody"** (v9) — Hebrew מְצוּדוֹת *metzudot* = strongholds/custody; "custody" more natural.
- **"vine in thy blood" → "vine in a vineyard"** (v10) — Hebrew כְּגֶפֶן בְּדָמְךָ *kegefen bedamkha*; "in your blood" is unclear; "in a vineyard" (*kerem*) is supported by some manuscripts; modernized.
- **"strong rods for the sceptres of them that bare rule" → "strong stems became rulers' scepters"** (v11) — Hebrew מַטּוֹת עֹז *mattot oz* = strong rods/staffs = scepters of rulers; "rulers' scepters" clearer.
- **"her stature was exalted among the thick branches" → "it towered aloft among the thick boughs"** (v11) — Hebrew גָּבַהּ קוֹמָתוֹ בֵּין עֲבֹתִים *gavah qomato beyn avotim*; "towered aloft" for *gavah qomato*.
- **"fire is gone out of a rod of her branches" → "fire has gone out from the stem of its shoots"** (v14) — Hebrew וַתֵּצֵא אֵשׁ מִמַּטֵּה בַדֶּיהָ *vattetze esh mimatteh vaddeha*; "stem of its shoots" captures the vine imagery.

### Notes
- **First lion** = Jehoahaz (taken to Egypt, 2 Kgs 23:34).
- **Second lion** = Jehoiachin (taken to Babylon) — or Zedekiah.
- **Vine** = house of David.
