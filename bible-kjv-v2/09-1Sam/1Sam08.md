# 1 Samuel 8

[1] And it came to pass, when ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ was old, that he set his sons as judges for ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[2] And the name of his firstborn son was ⟦joel|Yoel|Joel⟧, and the name of his second, ⟦abijah|Aviyah|Abijah⟧: judges in ⟦beersheba|Beer Sheva|Beersheba⟧.

[3] And his sons did not walk in his ways, and they turned after unjust gain, and they took a bribe, and they turned aside judgment.

[4] And all the elders of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ gathered themselves and came to ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, to Ramah.

[5] And they said to him, "Behold, you have grown old, and your sons have not walked in your ways. Now set for us a king to judge us, like all the nations."

[6] And the thing was evil in the eyes of ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, when they said, "Give us a king to judge us." And ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ prayed to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[7] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, "Listen to the voice of the people in all that they say to you; for not you have they rejected, but me have they rejected from reigning over them.

[8] According to all the deeds that they have done from the day I brought them up from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ and to this day, and they have forsaken me and served other gods, so they are doing also to you.

[9] And now, listen to their voice; only, you shall surely warn them, and you shall tell them the judgment of the king who will reign over them."

[10] And ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ said all the words of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ to the people who were asking a king from him.

[11] And he said, "This will be the judgment of the king who will reign over you: your sons he will take, and he will put them for himself in his chariot and among his horsemen, and they will run before his chariot;

[12] and to set for himself commanders of thousands and commanders of fifties, and to plow his plowing and to reap his harvest, and to make his weapons of war and the gear of his chariots.

[13] And your daughters he will take, for perfumers and for cooks and for bakers.

[14] And your fields and your vineyards and your olive trees, the good ones, he will take, and he will give them to his servants.

[15] And of your seed and of your vineyards he will take a tenth, and he will give it to his eunuchs and to his servants.

[16] And your slaves and your maidservants and your young men, the good ones, and your donkeys he will take, and he will put them to his work.

[17] Of your flock he will take a tenth, and you yourselves will be slaves to him.

[18] And you will cry out on that day because of your king whom you have chosen for yourselves, and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will not answer you on that day."

[19] And the people refused to listen to the voice of ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, and they said, "No, but a king shall be over us,

[20] and we also shall be like all the nations, and our king shall judge us and go out before us and fight our wars."

[21] And ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ heard all the words of the people, and he spoke them in the ears of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[22] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, "Listen to their voice, and make a king reign for them." And ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ said to the men of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, "Go, each man to his city."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Hebrew name kept. **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew chain of וַ / וְ is kept.
- **"he made his sons judges over Israel" → "he set his sons as judges for Israel"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיָּשֶׂם "put, set", the verb of v5 ("set for us a king") and of Deut 17:14, 15; לְיִשְׂרָאֵל "for Israel".
- **"they were judges in Beersheba" → "judges in Beersheba"** (v2) — the Hebrew has no verb. מִשְׁנֵהוּ is "his second".
- **"walked not in his ways, but turned aside after lucre, and took bribes, and perverted judgment" → "did not walk in his ways, and they turned after unjust gain, and they took a bribe, and they turned aside judgment"** (v3) — "in his ways" is the reading tradition (qere); the written text (ketiv) has "in his way". One Hebrew verb, נָטָה, stands twice: וַיִּטּוּ "they turned (themselves)" and וַיַּטּוּ "they turned aside" judgment (= perverted it; Exod 23:6; Deut 16:19). בֶּצַע is gain got by wrong; שֹׁחַד "bribe" is singular.
- **"thou art old, and thy sons walk not" → "you have grown old, and your sons have not walked"** (v5) — Hebrew perfects. **"make us a king" → "set for us a king"** — Hebrew שִׂימָה־לָּנוּ; in v6 the people's word is retold with "give" (תְּנָה).
- **"the thing displeased Samuel" → "the thing was evil in the eyes of Samuel"** (v6) — Hebrew וַיֵּרַע הַדָּבָר בְּעֵינֵי; דָּבָר is "word" or "thing".
- **"Hearken unto the voice" → "Listen to the voice"** (v7, 9, 19, 22) — Hebrew שְׁמַע בְּקוֹל; "obey" is not a Hebrew word here. **"they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them" → "not you have they rejected, but me have they rejected from reigning over them"** (v7) — Hebrew כִּי לֹא אֹתְךָ מָאָסוּ כִּי־אֹתִי מָאֲסוּ מִמְּלֹךְ עֲלֵיהֶם, word for word; the objects stand first. מִמְּלֹךְ is "from reigning", "from being king".
- **"wherewith they have forsaken me, and served other gods" → "and they have forsaken me and served other gods"** (v8) — the Hebrew goes on with "and"; the sentence is loose in the Hebrew too. "Other gods" is אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים.
- **"howbeit yet protest solemnly unto them, and shew them the manner of the king" → "only, you shall surely warn them, and you shall tell them the judgment of the king"** (v9) — Hebrew הָעֵד תָּעִיד בָּהֶם, literally "testifying you shall testify against them": the infinitive with its verb; הֵעִיד בְּ is to give formal warning before witnesses (Exod 19:21). **"the manner of the king" → "the judgment of the king"** (v9, 11) — Hebrew מִשְׁפַּט הַמֶּלֶךְ. מִשְׁפָּט is "judgment": a ruling, what is right or due, and so also a fixed custom or the rights that belong to someone (Deut 18:3 "the due of the priests"). Here: what the king will hold to be his right, the way he will deal with them. The one word is kept; it answers the people's "a king to judge us" (v5, 6, 20) and "turned aside judgment" (v3).
- **"told" → "said"** (v10) — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר. **"that asked of him a king" → "who were asking a king from him"** — a participle. The verb שָׁאַל "ask" is the root of the name Saul (שָׁאוּל "asked").
- **"He will take your sons, and appoint them for himself, for his chariots, and to be his horsemen; and some shall run before his chariots" → "your sons he will take, and he will put them for himself in his chariot and among his horsemen, and they will run before his chariot"** (v11) — the object stands first, here and in v13, 14, 16; "he will take" (יִקָּח) comes four times (v11, 13, 14, 16). "Chariot" is singular both times; בְּ is "in" both times ("in his chariot and in his horsemen"). "Them" is supplied.
- **"And he will appoint him captains over thousands … and will set them to ear his ground" → "and to set for himself commanders of thousands and commanders of fifties, and to plow his plowing"** (v12) — the Hebrew goes on with infinitives: "and to set … and to plow … and to reap … and to make". שָׂרִים is "princes, chiefs", of an army "commanders". חֲרִישׁוֹ is "his plowing" (verb and noun of one root, as "reap his harvest"). **"his instruments of war, and instruments of his chariots" → "his weapons of war and the gear of his chariots"** — one Hebrew word, כְּלִי (any made article), both times. רִכְבּוֹ is a collective, "his chariots"; another word than "chariot" in v11.
- **"confectionaries" → "perfumers"** (v13) — Hebrew רַקָּחוֹת: women who blend ointments and spices (Exod 30:25). **"cooks"** — Hebrew טַבָּחוֹת, literally "slaughterers" (those who butcher and cook meat; the masculine is "the cook" in 9:23).
- **"your fields, and your vineyards, and your oliveyards, even the best of them" → "your fields and your vineyards and your olive trees, the good ones"** (v14) — Hebrew הַטּוֹבִים "the good". **"servants"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew עֲבָדָיו, of a king's men; the same word is "slaves" in v16, 17.
- **"he will take the tenth of your seed" → "of your seed and of your vineyards he will take a tenth"** (v15, 17) — Hebrew יַעְשֹׂר, one verb: "he will tithe". זַרְעֵיכֶם is "your seeds": what is sown, the grain crop. **"officers" → "eunuchs"** — Hebrew סָרִיסָיו: eunuchs, or court officers whether castrated or not.
- **"your menservants, and your maidservants, and your goodliest young men" → "your slaves and your maidservants and your young men, the good ones"** (v16) — Hebrew עֶבֶד "slave", שִׁפְחָה "maidservant", בַּחוּרֵיכֶם "your young men". The Greek has "your cattle" (בְּקַרְכֶם for בַּחוּרֵיכֶם; from memory), which many follow because donkeys come next; the Masoretic Text is kept. **"put them to his work"** — Hebrew וְעָשָׂה לִמְלַאכְתּוֹ, literally "and he will make (them) for his work"; "them" is supplied.
- **"ye shall be his servants" → "you yourselves will be slaves to him"** (v17) — Hebrew וְאַתֶּם תִּהְיוּ־לוֹ לַעֲבָדִים; the pronoun is written. "Flock" is צֹאן, sheep and goats.
- **"because of your king"** (v18) — Hebrew מִלִּפְנֵי, literally "from before". **"will not hear you" → "will not answer you"** — Hebrew יַעֲנֶה.
- **"Nay; but we will have a king over us; that we also may be like all the nations" → "No, but a king shall be over us, and we also shall be like all the nations"** (v19–20) — Hebrew לֹּא כִּי אִם־מֶלֶךְ יִהְיֶה עָלֵינוּ; v20 goes on with "and". **"fight our battles" → "fight our wars"** — Hebrew מִלְחֲמֹתֵנוּ.
- **"rehearsed them" → "spoke them"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיְדַבְּרֵם.
- **"make them a king" → "make a king reign for them"** (v22) — Hebrew וְהִמְלַכְתָּ לָהֶם מֶלֶךְ, the causative of "reign" with its own noun. **"Go ye every man unto his city" → "Go, each man to his city"**.

### Notes
- **Demand for a king** — key moment. Israel's elders request a king to be "like all the nations." Yahweh interprets: they are rejecting his own kingship.
- **Samuel's sons** — the precipitating cause. Judicial corruption. Compare Eli's sons: same failure-of-priestly-fathers pattern.
- **Deuteronomic king-law** (Deut 17) — had anticipated a king but bounded his power. Samuel's warning (v11–18) echoes those restrictions inverted: the king *will* multiply horses/wives/wealth.
- **The judgment of the king** (v11–17) — ancient Near Eastern royal administration systematically described. Taxation, conscription, confiscation. Archaeological tablets from Ugarit confirm these patterns.
- **Theological ambivalence** — Yahweh both criticizes the request (v7–8) and grants it (v22). Divine accommodation of human institutions, with warning of consequences.
- **"Like all the nations"** (v5, 20) — the fatal phrase. Israel is to be distinct; wanting to be "like all the nations" is covenantal drift.
