# 1 Samuel 8

[1] When ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ was old, he made his sons judges over ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[2] The name of his firstborn son was ⟦joel|Yoel|Joel⟧, and the name of his second, ⟦abijah|Aviyah|Abijah⟧; they were judges at ⟦beersheba|Beer Sheva|Beersheba⟧.

[3] Yet his sons did not walk in his ways; they turned aside after dishonest gain, took bribes, and perverted justice.

[4] Then all the elders of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ gathered together and came to ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ at Ramah.

[5] They said to him, "Behold, you are old, and your sons do not walk in your ways. Now appoint for us a king to judge us, like all the nations."

[6] But the thing displeased ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ when they said, "Give us a king to judge us." And ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ prayed to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[7] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ said to ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, "Obey the voice of the people in all they say to you; for it is not you they have rejected — it is I they have rejected from being king over them.

[8] According to all the deeds they have done from the day I brought them up out of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ to this day — forsaking me and serving other gods — so they are also doing to you.

[9] Now therefore obey their voice; only solemnly warn them, and declare to them the ways of the king who shall reign over them."

[10] ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ told all the words of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ to the people who were asking him for a king.

[11] He said, "These will be the ways of the king who shall reign over you: he will take your sons and appoint them for himself, for his chariots and for his horsemen, and they shall run before his chariots.

[12] He will appoint them as commanders of thousands and commanders of fifties, and some to plow his ground and reap his harvest, and to make his weapons of war and the equipment of his chariots.

[13] He will take your daughters to be perfumers, cooks, and bakers.

[14] He will take the best of your fields, your vineyards, and your olive groves, and give them to his servants.

[15] He will take a tenth of your grain and of your vineyards, and give it to his officials and servants.

[16] He will take your male servants and female servants, the best of your young men, and your donkeys, and put them to his work.

[17] He will take a tenth of your flocks, and you yourselves shall be his servants.

[18] In that day you will cry out because of your king whom you have chosen for yourselves; but ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will not answer you in that day."

[19] But the people refused to listen to the voice of ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧; they said, "No! But there shall be a king over us,

[20] that we also may be like all the nations, that our king may judge us and go out before us and fight our battles."

[21] ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ heard all the words of the people, and he recounted them in the ears of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[22] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ said to ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, "Obey their voice and make them a king." ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧ said to the men of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, "Each of you go to his city."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"lucre" → "dishonest gain"** (v3) — Hebrew בֶּצַע *betza*. Unjust profit.
- **"confectionaries" → "perfumers"** (v13) — Hebrew רַקָּחוֹת *raqqachot*. Spice / ointment makers.
- **"ear his ground" → "plow his ground"** (v12) — archaic *to ear* = to till.
- **"goodliest" → "best of"** (v16).
- **"rehearsed" → "recounted"** (v21).

### Notes
- **Demand for a king** — key moment. Israel's elders request a king to be "like all the nations." Yahweh interprets: they are rejecting his own kingship.
- **Samuel's sons** — the precipitating cause. Judicial corruption. Compare Eli's sons: same failure-of-priestly-fathers pattern.
- **Deuteronomic king-law** (Deut 17) — had anticipated a king but bounded his power. Samuel's warning (v11–18) echoes those restrictions inverted: the king *will* multiply horses/wives/wealth.
- **The ways of the king** (v11–17) — ancient Near Eastern royal administration systematically described. Taxation, conscription, confiscation. Archaeological tablets from Ugarit confirm these patterns.
- **Theological ambivalence** — Yahweh both criticizes the request (v7–8) and grants it (v22). Divine accommodation of human institutions, with warning of consequences.
- **"Like all the nations"** (v5, 20) — the fatal phrase. Israel is to be distinct; wanting to be "like all the nations" is covenantal drift.
