# 1 Samuel 20

[1] David fled from Naioth in Ramah and came and said before Jonathan, "What have I done? What is my guilt? What is my sin before your father, that he seeks my life?"

[2] He said to him, "Far from it! You shall not die. Behold, my father does nothing either great or small without disclosing it to me; why then should my father hide this matter from me? It is not so."

[3] But David swore again and said, "Your father knows well that I have found favor in your eyes; therefore he thought, 'Let not Jonathan know this, lest he be grieved.' But truly, as Yahweh lives and as your soul lives, there is but a step between me and death."

[4] Then Jonathan said to David, "Whatever your soul desires, I will do for you."

[5] David said to Jonathan, "Behold, tomorrow is the new moon, and I should not fail to sit at table with the king; but let me go, that I may hide myself in the field until the third evening.

[6] If your father misses me at all, then say, 'David earnestly asked leave of me to run to Bethlehem his city; for there is a yearly sacrifice there for the whole clan.'

[7] If he says, 'Good,' your servant shall have peace; but if he becomes furious, then know that evil is determined by him.

[8] Therefore deal kindly with your servant, for you have brought your servant into a covenant of Yahweh with you. But if there is guilt in me, kill me yourself; why should you bring me to your father?"

[9] Jonathan said, "Far be it from you! If I knew for certain that evil was determined by my father to come upon you, would I not tell you?"

[10] Then David said to Jonathan, "Who will tell me if your father answers you roughly?"

[11] Jonathan said to David, "Come, let us go out into the field." So they both went out into the field.

[12] Jonathan said to David, "Yahweh, the *Elohim* of Israel, be witness — when I have sounded out my father about this time tomorrow or on the third day, if there is good feeling toward David, shall I not send and disclose it to you?

[13] But if it pleases my father to do you harm, may Yahweh do so to Jonathan, and more also, if I do not disclose it to you and send you away, that you may go in peace. May Yahweh be with you, as he has been with my father.

[14] And while I am still alive, may you show me the kindness of Yahweh, that I may not die;

[15] and do not cut off your kindness from my house forever — not even when Yahweh cuts off every one of the enemies of David from the face of the earth."

[16] So Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David, saying, "May Yahweh require it at the hand of David's enemies."

[17] Jonathan made David swear again, for he loved him as his own soul.

[18] Then Jonathan said to him, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed, because your seat will be empty.

[19] On the third day, go down quickly to the place where you hid yourself on the day of the deed, and stay beside the stone Ezel.

[20] I will shoot three arrows to the side of it, as though I shot at a mark.

[21] And behold, I will send the young man, saying, 'Go, find the arrows.' If I expressly say to the young man, 'Behold, the arrows are on this side of you; take them,' then come, for there is peace for you and no harm, as Yahweh lives.

[22] But if I say to the young man, 'Behold, the arrows are beyond you,' then go, for Yahweh has sent you away.

[23] As for the matter of which you and I have spoken, behold, Yahweh is between you and me forever."

[24] So David hid himself in the field. When the new moon came, the king sat down to eat the meal.

[25] The king sat on his seat, as at other times, on the seat by the wall; Jonathan arose, and Abner sat by Saul's side, but David's place was empty.

[26] Yet Saul did not say anything that day, for he thought, "Something has happened to him; he is not clean; surely he is not clean."

[27] But on the second day, the day after the new moon, David's place was empty. Saul said to Jonathan his son, "Why has the son of Jesse not come to the meal, either yesterday or today?"

[28] Jonathan answered Saul, "David earnestly asked leave of me to go to Bethlehem.

[29] He said, 'Please let me go, for our clan has a sacrifice in the city, and my brother has commanded me to be there. Now, if I have found favor in your eyes, let me get away, I pray, to see my brothers.' Therefore he has not come to the king's table."

[30] Then Saul's anger was kindled against Jonathan, and he said to him, "You son of a perverse, rebellious woman! Do I not know that you have chosen the son of Jesse to your own shame and to the shame of your mother's nakedness?

[31] For as long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, neither you nor your kingdom shall be established. Now therefore send and bring him to me, for he must die."

[32] Jonathan answered his father Saul, "Why shall he be put to death? What has he done?"

[33] Saul hurled his spear at him to strike him; so Jonathan knew that his father was determined to put David to death.

[34] Jonathan rose from the table in fierce anger and ate no food the second day of the month, for he was grieved for David because his father had disgraced him.

[35] In the morning, Jonathan went out into the field at the time appointed with David, and a young lad with him.

[36] He said to his lad, "Run, find now the arrows which I shoot." As the lad ran, he shot an arrow beyond him.

[37] When the lad came to the place of the arrow that Jonathan had shot, Jonathan called after the lad and said, "Is not the arrow beyond you?"

[38] Jonathan called after the lad, "Hurry, be quick, do not stay!" So Jonathan's lad gathered up the arrows and came to his master.

[39] But the lad knew nothing; only Jonathan and David knew the matter.

[40] Jonathan gave his weapons to his lad, and said to him, "Go, carry them to the city."

[41] As soon as the lad was gone, David rose from beside the south side of the stone, fell on his face to the ground, and bowed three times. They kissed one another and wept one with another, David weeping the more.

[42] Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace, for we have sworn both of us in the name of Yahweh, saying, 'Yahweh shall be between me and you and between my offspring and your offspring, forever.'" Then he arose and departed; Jonathan went into the city.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"artillery" → "weapons"** (v40) — 1611 English for "bow and arrows / projectiles."
- **"spake not any thing" → "did not say anything"** (v26).
- **"son of the perverse rebellious woman" (v30)** — Hebrew insult of Jonathan's own mother. Sharp pre-modern invective.
- **"David exceeded" → "David weeping the more"** (v41) — Hebrew עַד דָּוִד הִגְדִּיל *ad David higdil*. David wept more / intensely.
- **"javelin" → "spear"** (v33).

### Notes
- **Jonathan's oath** (v14–17) — covenantal friendship with David includes an oath binding David to preserve Jonathan's line. Later fulfilled (2 Sam 9): David honors Mephibosheth.
- **New Moon feast** — monthly festival (Num 10:10, 28:11). David's absence requires explanation.
- **Saul's ritual cleanness thought** (v26) — assumes David may have contracted temporary ritual uncleanness. Day two forces the question.
- **Saul's spear at Jonathan** (v33) — now the king threatens his own son. Family destruction advances with kingdom disintegration.
- **Arrow signal** (v19–22, 35–39) — elaborate secret communication. Shows risk level: open communication impossible.
- **David's weeping more** (v41) — greater grief from David. His kingdom-promise is being paid in friendship-loss and homelessness.
