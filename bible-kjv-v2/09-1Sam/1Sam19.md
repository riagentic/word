# 1 Samuel 19

[1] Saul spoke to Jonathan his son and to all his servants, that they should kill David. But Jonathan Saul's son delighted greatly in David.

[2] Jonathan told David, "Saul my father is seeking to kill you. Now therefore, please be on your guard tomorrow morning; stay in a secret place and hide yourself.

[3] I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will speak with my father about you; and whatever I see, I will tell you."

[4] Jonathan spoke well of David to Saul his father and said to him, "Let not the king sin against his servant David, because he has not sinned against you; his deeds have been very good to you.

[5] He took his life in his hand and killed the Philistine, and Yahweh worked a great deliverance for all Israel. You saw it and rejoiced; why then would you sin against innocent blood by killing David without cause?"

[6] Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan, and Saul swore, "As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death."

[7] Jonathan called David and told him all these things; then Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence as before.

[8] There was war again; David went out and fought with the Philistines and struck them with a great slaughter, and they fled from him.

[9] The evil spirit from Yahweh came upon Saul as he sat in his house with his spear in his hand, while David was playing the lyre with his hand.

[10] Saul sought to pin David to the wall with the spear, but he slipped away from Saul's presence, so that he struck the spear into the wall; David fled and escaped that night.

[11] Saul sent messengers to David's house to watch him and to kill him in the morning. But Michal, David's wife, told him, "If you do not save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be killed."

[12] So Michal let David down through the window; he went, fled, and escaped.

[13] Michal took a household idol and laid it on the bed, put a pillow of goats' hair at its head, and covered it with a garment.

[14] When Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, "He is sick."

[15] Saul sent the messengers to see David, saying, "Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may kill him."

[16] When the messengers came in, behold, the household idol was on the bed, with the pillow of goats' hair at its head.

[17] Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me like this and sent my enemy away, so that he has escaped?" Michal answered Saul, "He said to me, 'Let me go; why should I kill you?'"

[18] David fled and escaped and came to Samuel at Ramah; he told him all that Saul had done to him. He and Samuel went and stayed at Naioth.

[19] It was told Saul, "Behold, David is at Naioth in Ramah."

[20] Saul sent messengers to take David; when they saw the company of prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing as their leader, the spirit of *Elohim* came upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied.

[21] When it was told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. Saul sent messengers the third time, and they also prophesied.

[22] Then he also himself went to Ramah, and came to the great well that is at Secu; he asked, "Where are Samuel and David?" Someone said, "Behold, they are at Naioth in Ramah."

[23] He went there to Naioth in Ramah; and the spirit of *Elohim* came upon him also, and he went on, prophesying, until he came to Naioth in Ramah.

[24] He also stripped off his clothes; he too prophesied before Samuel and lay naked all that day and all that night. Therefore they say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"image" → "household idol"** (v13, 16) — *teraphim*.
- **"javelin" → "spear"** (v9, 10).
- **"standing as appointed" → "standing as their leader"** (v20).
- **"pillow of goats' hair" (v13, 16)** — Hebrew כְּבִיר הָעִזִּים *kevir ha-izzim*. Likely goat-hair netting or mat.

### Notes
- **Jonathan's intercession** (v1–7) — saves David briefly. Demonstrates Saul's still-functional rationality; but only briefly.
- **Second spear-throw** (v10) — Saul's violence recurs. David no longer returns.
- **Michal's deception** (v11–17) — saves David via window-escape. The *teraphim* in her house (indicative of household idolatry) is used as a prop.
- **"Let me go; why should I kill you?"** (v17) — Michal's claim to Saul protects herself. Clever diplomacy; whether David actually threatened her is unclear.
- **Second "Is Saul also among the prophets?"** (v24) — the proverb from ch 10 recurs. Saul's prophetic ecstasy has become uncontrolled incapacitation.
- **Prophetic protection** — Samuel's prophetic community has contagious spirit-presence. Even hostile messengers are swept into prophecy.
