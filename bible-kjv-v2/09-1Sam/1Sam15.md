# 1 Samuel 15

[1] Samuel said to Saul, "Yahweh sent me to anoint you as king over his people Israel; now therefore listen to the voice of the words of Yahweh.

[2] Thus says Yahweh of Hosts: 'I will attend to what Amalek did to Israel — how he attacked him on the way when he came up from Egypt.

[3] Now go and strike Amalek, and devote to destruction all that he has. Do not spare them, but kill man and woman, infant and nursing child, ox and sheep, camel and donkey.'"

[4] Saul summoned the people and mustered them at Telaim, 200,000 foot soldiers and 10,000 men of Judah.

[5] Saul came to the city of Amalek and lay in wait in the valley.

[6] Saul said to the Kenites, "Go, depart, go down from among the Amalekites, lest I destroy you with them; for you showed kindness to all the children of Israel when they came up out of Egypt." So the Kenites departed from among the Amalekites.

[7] Saul struck the Amalekites from Havilah as far as Shur, which is east of Egypt.

[8] He took Agag king of the Amalekites alive and devoted all the people to destruction with the edge of the sword.

[9] But Saul and the people spared Agag and the best of the sheep, the oxen, the fatlings, the lambs, and all that was good, and they would not devote them to destruction; but everything despised and worthless they devoted to destruction.

[10] The word of Yahweh came to Samuel, saying,

[11] "I regret that I have made Saul king, for he has turned back from following me and has not performed my commands." Samuel was distressed and cried out to Yahweh all night.

[12] Samuel rose early to meet Saul in the morning, and it was told Samuel, "Saul came to Carmel; behold, he set up a monument for himself, then turned and passed on and went down to Gilgal."

[13] Samuel came to Saul, and Saul said to him, "Blessed be you of Yahweh! I have performed the command of Yahweh."

[14] Samuel said, "What then is this bleating of sheep in my ears, and the lowing of oxen that I hear?"

[15] Saul said, "They have brought them from the Amalekites, for the people spared the best of the sheep and oxen to sacrifice to Yahweh your *Elohim*; the rest we have devoted to destruction."

[16] Samuel said to Saul, "Stop! I will tell you what Yahweh said to me this night." He said to him, "Speak."

[17] Samuel said, "Though you were little in your own eyes, did you not become the head of the tribes of Israel? Yahweh anointed you king over Israel.

[18] Yahweh sent you on a mission and said, 'Go, devote to destruction the sinners, the Amalekites, and fight against them until they are consumed.'

[19] Why then did you not obey the voice of Yahweh? Why did you pounce on the spoil and do what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh?"

[20] Saul said to Samuel, "I have obeyed the voice of Yahweh; I went on the mission on which Yahweh sent me; I have brought Agag the king of Amalek; the Amalekites I have devoted to destruction.

[21] But the people took from the spoil — sheep and oxen, the best of the devoted things — to sacrifice to Yahweh your *Elohim* at Gilgal."

[22] Samuel said:

"Has Yahweh as much delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices
as in obeying the voice of Yahweh?
Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice,
and to listen than the fat of rams.

[23] For rebellion is like the sin of divination,
and stubbornness is like the iniquity of teraphim.
Because you have rejected the word of Yahweh,
he has also rejected you from being king."

[24] Saul said to Samuel, "I have sinned, for I have transgressed the mouth of Yahweh and your words, because I feared the people and obeyed their voice.

[25] Now therefore, please pardon my sin and return with me, that I may worship Yahweh."

[26] Samuel said to Saul, "I will not return with you; for you have rejected the word of Yahweh, and Yahweh has rejected you from being king over Israel."

[27] As Samuel turned to go away, Saul seized the skirt of his robe, and it tore.

[28] Samuel said to him, "Yahweh has torn the kingdom of Israel from you this day and has given it to a neighbor of yours who is better than you.

[29] Moreover, the Glory of Israel will not lie or change his mind, for he is not a man, that he should change his mind."

[30] Then Saul said, "I have sinned; yet please honor me now before the elders of my people and before Israel, and return with me, that I may worship Yahweh your *Elohim*."

[31] So Samuel turned back after Saul, and Saul worshiped Yahweh.

[32] Then Samuel said, "Bring here to me Agag king of the Amalekites." Agag came to him cheerfully, saying, "Surely the bitterness of death is past."

[33] But Samuel said, "As your sword has made women childless, so shall your mother be childless among women." And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before Yahweh in Gilgal.

[34] Samuel then went to Ramah, and Saul went up to his house at Gibeah of Saul.

[35] Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death; but Samuel grieved over Saul. And Yahweh regretted that he had made Saul king over Israel.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"utterly destroy" → "devote to destruction"** (throughout) — *cherem*.
- **"it repenteth me / repent" → "I regret / change his mind"** (v11, 29, 35) — *nacham*. Translating consistently is impossible here — the Hebrew uses the same verb both of Yahweh's "regretting" making Saul king, and v29's "he will not change his mind." Apparent contradiction reflects genre: v11 is narrative (human perspective of divine action); v29 is aphoristic (divine immutability principle).
- **"witchcraft / iniquity and idolatry" → "divination / iniquity of teraphim"** (v23) — Hebrew *qesem* (divination) / *teraphim* (household gods).
- **"place" → "monument"** (v12) — Hebrew יָד *yad*. Victory monument.
- **"delicately" → "cheerfully"** (v32) — Hebrew מַעֲדַנֹּת *ma'adannot*. Debated: "hopefully" / "cheerfully" / "trembling." LXX reads "trembling."
- **"Strength of Israel" → "Glory of Israel"** (v29) — Hebrew נֵצַח יִשְׂרָאֵל *Netzach Yisrael*. "Everlasting / Glory / Enduring One of Israel."

### Notes
- **The Amalekite war** — completion of Yahweh's ancient vow to destroy Amalek (Ex 17:14–16, Deut 25:17–19). Saul's test.
- **Saul's partial obedience** — kept the king alive; spared the best livestock. Substantive disobedience despite claims of compliance.
- **"To obey is better than sacrifice"** (v22) — foundational prophetic critique of ritualism. Echoes in Hos 6:6, Mic 6:8, Mt 9:13, 12:7.
- **Second rejection** — first in ch 13, now definitive. Saul's kingdom passed on to "a neighbor better than you" — David, unnamed here but introduced in ch 16.
- **Saul's torn robe** (v27) — symbolic action. Later (24:4–5) David will cut Saul's robe; and Saul himself recognizes kingdom-loss.
- **"Yahweh will not change his mind"** (v29) — the divine vs human capacity: Yahweh's judgment against Saul stands. But the narrative says Yahweh "regretted" making Saul king (v35) — showing that the text holds these together in tension.
