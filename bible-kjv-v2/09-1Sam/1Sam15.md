## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved. **"the LORD of hosts" → "Yahweh of Hosts"** (v2) — Hebrew יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v15, 21, 30) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"The LORD sent me" → "It was I whom Yahweh sent"** (v1) — Hebrew אֹתִי שָׁלַח יְהוָה: "me" stands first, with stress. **"over his people, over Israel"** — "over" twice. **"hearken thou unto the voice of the words"** → "listen to the voice of the words": Hebrew שְׁמַע לְקוֹל. "Listen to the voice" runs through the chapter (v1, 19, 20, 22, 24), and so does "voice" (v14).
- **"I remember that which Amalek did" → "I have visited what Amalek did"** (v2) — Hebrew פָּקַדְתִּי, the verb "visit": attend to, here to call to account. The perfect states the thing as decided ("I visit, I am about to punish"). **"how he laid wait for him in the way" → "how he set himself against him on the way"** — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר־שָׂם לוֹ בַּדֶּרֶךְ, literally "that he set for him on the way"; what was "set" is not said (Exod 17:8–16; Deut 25:17–19).
- **"smite Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have, and spare them not" → "strike down Amalek, and you shall devote to destruction all that he has, and you shall not have pity on him"** (v3) — Hebrew הֶחֱרִים *hecherim*, to devote to Yahweh beyond recall by destroying; the noun is חֵרֶם *cherem* (v21). The verb stands seven times in the chapter (v3, 8, 9 twice, 15, 18, 20) and is rendered the same way each time. Amalek is spoken of as one man ("he", "him"). "You shall devote" is plural, the other verbs singular. **"spare"** → "have pity on" (v3, 9, 15): Hebrew חָמַל.
- **"slay both man and woman, infant and suckling, ox and sheep, camel and ass" → "put to death from man to woman, from child to suckling, from ox to sheep, from camel to donkey"** (v3) — Hebrew הֵמִית "put to death" and מִן … עַד "from … to", four times; nothing is softened.
- **"gathered the people together" → "summoned the people"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיְשַׁמַּע, "made hear". **"numbered"** — Hebrew פָּקַד, the verb of v2 in another sense. **"two hundred thousand footmen, and ten thousand men of Judah" → "two hundred thousand men on foot, and ten thousand, the men of Judah"** — Hebrew אֶת־אִישׁ יְהוּדָה, with the object marker (or "with the men of Judah"). The numbers are given in words. "Telaim" has the article.
- **"laid wait in the valley" → "lay in ambush in the wadi"** (v5) — Hebrew וַיָּרֶב, taken as a short form of "lie in ambush" (אָרַב); as pointed it can also be "and he strove". נַחַל is a stream bed.
- **"the Kenites … the Amalekites" → "the Kenite … the Amalekite"** (v6, 15) — Hebrew הַקֵּינִי, עֲמָלֵקִי: singular for the whole people, and so "with him"; the commands are plural, "you" in "lest I gather you" and "you did" is singular. **"depart" → "turn aside"** — Hebrew סוּר. **"lest I destroy you with them" → "lest I gather you with him"** — Hebrew אֹסִפְךָ, as pointed from אָסַף "gather": gather you up with him, sweep you away together. **"ye shewed kindness to" → "you did *chesed* with"** — Hebrew עָשִׂיתָה חֶסֶד עִם. **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"**.
- **"smote the Amalekites" → "struck down Amalek"** (v7) — the Hebrew has "Amalek" here and in v2, 3, 5, 8, 18, 20, 32. **"until thou comest to Shur, that is over against Egypt" → "as you come to Shur, which faces Egypt"** — Hebrew בּוֹאֲךָ שׁוּר אֲשֶׁר עַל־פְּנֵי מִצְרָיִם (Gen 25:18).
- **"took Agag … alive" → "seized Agag … alive"** (v8) — Hebrew תָּפַשׂ. **"utterly destroyed all the people" → "all the people he devoted to destruction"**. **"with the edge of the sword"** — Hebrew לְפִי־חָרֶב, literally "by the mouth of the sword".
- **"spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, and of the oxen, and of the fatlings, and the lambs" → "had pity on Agag and on the best of the flock and the herd and the seconds, and on the lambs"** (v9) — Hebrew הַמִּשְׁנִים is literally "the seconds" (animals of the second birth, or of the second rank): uncertain; "fatlings" rests on a change of the word (הַשְּׁמֵנִים; so the Targum and the Syriac, from memory). **"would not utterly destroy them" → "were not willing to devote them to destruction"** — Hebrew וְלֹא אָבוּ. **"every thing that was vile and refuse" → "all the property that was despised and wasting"** — Hebrew הַמְּלָאכָה, "work", here what is owned, the stock (Gen 33:14; Exod 22:8); נְמִבְזָה is an irregular form of "despised"; נָמֵס is "melting", that is, wasting away, worthless.
- **"It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king" → "I am sorry that I made Saul king"** (v11); **"will not lie nor repent: for he is not a man, that he should repent" → "will not lie and will not be sorry, for he is not a man, to be sorry"** (v29); **"the LORD repented" → "Yahweh was sorry"** (v35) — Hebrew נִחַם *nicham* (nifal) in all four places: to be sorry, to regret, and so to change one's course (Gen 6:6, 7). The chapter says both that Yahweh was sorry that he had made Saul king (v11, 35) and that he will not be sorry (v29), with the same verb; the same English word is kept in all, and the two statements are left side by side as the Hebrew has them. "Made king" is literally "made reign as king" (הִמְלַכְתִּי … לְמֶלֶךְ).
- **"he is turned back from following me, and hath not performed my commandments" → "he has turned back from after me, and my words he has not established"** (v11) — Hebrew שָׁב מֵאַחֲרַי; דְּבָרַי "my words"; הֵקִים, literally "made stand" (so also v13). **"it grieved Samuel" → "Samuel burned with anger"** — Hebrew וַיִּחַר לִשְׁמוּאֵל, literally "it burned to Samuel"; at whom is not said. **"cried" → "cried out"** — Hebrew זָעַק.
- **"he set him up a place" → "he is setting up a monument for himself"** (v12) — Hebrew מַצִּיב לוֹ יָד, literally "setting up for himself a hand" (2 Sam 18:18); a participle. **"is gone about, and passed on"** → "he turned and passed on". "Carmel" is the town in Judah (Josh 15:55).
- **"Blessed be thou of the LORD" → "Blessed are you to Yahweh"** (v13) — Hebrew בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה לַיהוָה, no verb. **"I have performed the commandment" → "I have established the word"** — Hebrew הֲקִימֹתִי אֶת־דְּבַר יְהוָה, answering v11.
- **"What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen" → "And what is this voice of the flock in my ears, and the voice of the herd"** (v14) — Hebrew קוֹל "voice" twice; "bleating" and "lowing" are not in the Hebrew.
- **"to sacrifice"** (v15) — Hebrew לְמַעַן זְבֹחַ, "in order to sacrifice". "The people had pity … we have devoted": the persons as the Hebrew has them.
- **"Stay, and I will tell thee" → "Let go, and let me tell you"** (v16) — Hebrew הֶרֶף, "slacken, leave off" (11:3). "He said": the reading tradition has the singular; the written text has "and they said".
- **"When thou wast little in thine own sight, wast thou not made the head" → "Though you are small in your eyes, are you not the head"** (v17) — Hebrew הֲלוֹא אִם־קָטֹן אַתָּה בְּעֵינֶיךָ רֹאשׁ שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל אָתָּה: clauses without a verb, "is it not so — if you are small in your eyes — you are the head of the tribes of Israel?" (9:21).
- **"sent thee on a journey" → "sent you on a way"** (v18, 20) — Hebrew דֶּרֶךְ "way". **"until they be consumed" → "until they have made an end of them"** — Hebrew עַד כַּלּוֹתָם אֹתָם, literally "until their making an end of them": "they" has no clear reference, and the versions have "until you have made an end of them" (from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"didst fly upon the spoil" → "swooped on the spoil"** (v19) — Hebrew וַתַּעַט, as 14:32. **"didst evil in the sight of the LORD" → "did the evil in the eyes of Yahweh"**.
- **"Yea, I have obeyed" → "I did listen"** (v20) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר שָׁמַעְתִּי, literally "that I have listened".
- **"the chief of the things which should have been utterly destroyed" → "the first of what was devoted to destruction"** (v21) — Hebrew רֵאשִׁית הַחֵרֶם: רֵאשִׁית "the first, the first part" (the word for firstfruits), and חֵרֶם *cherem*, the devoted thing. What is *cherem* cannot be offered as a sacrifice (Lev 27:28, 29).
- **v22, 23 as verse** — the four lines of each verse are the Hebrew cola. **"Hath the LORD as great delight" → "Has Yahweh delight"** — Hebrew הַחֵפֶץ לַיהוָה, "is there delight to Yahweh"; "as great" is not in the Hebrew. **"to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken" → "to listen is better than sacrifice, to give heed"** — Hebrew שְׁמֹעַ, the "listen" of v1, 19, 20, and הַקְשִׁיב "give attention"; no "and".
- **"For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry" → "For rebellion is the sin of divination, and stubbornness is wickedness and teraphim"** (v23) — Hebrew כִּי חַטַּאת־קֶסֶם מֶרִי וְאָוֶן וּתְרָפִים הַפְצַר, word for word "for sin of divination — rebellion; and wickedness and teraphim — stubbornness". There is no "as" in the Hebrew: the one thing is said to be the other. קֶסֶם *qesem* is divination, the seeking of omens. תְּרָפִים *teraphim* are images kept in a house and used for asking (Gen 31:19; Judg 17:5; Ezek 21:21); the word is kept. הַפְצַר is found only here, from "press, push" (Gen 19:3, 9): pushing one's own way; "stubbornness" is the usual rendering, uncertain. **"he hath also rejected thee from being king"** → "he has rejected you from being king": Hebrew מִמֶּלֶךְ, "from king"; no "also".
- **"I have transgressed the commandment of the LORD" → "I have overstepped the mouth of Yahweh"** (v24) — Hebrew עָבַרְתִּי אֶת־פִּי־יְהוָה. **"obeyed their voice" → "listened to their voice"**.
- **"pardon my sin" → "forgive my sin"** (v25) — Hebrew שָׂא, literally "lift, bear". **"turn again with me, that I may worship" → "return with me, and I will bow down"** (v25, 30, 31) — Hebrew שׁוּב and הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה, to bow to the ground.
- **"he laid hold upon the skirt of his mantle, and it rent" → "he held fast to the corner of his robe, and it was torn"** (v27) — the Hebrew names no subject for "held fast": Saul takes hold of Samuel's robe, as the answer in v28 shows; a Dead Sea scroll and the Septuagint name Saul (from memory). כָּנָף is literally "wing"; מְעִיל is the robe Samuel has worn since 2:19.
- **"hath rent the kingdom of Israel from thee" → "has torn the kingdom of Israel from upon you"** (v28) — Hebrew קָרַע, the verb of v27; מֵעָלֶיךָ "from upon you"; מַמְלְכוּת, a rare form of "kingdom". **"a neighbour of thine, that is better than thou" → "your neighbor, who is better than you"** — Hebrew לְרֵעֲךָ.
- **"the Strength of Israel" → "the Eminence of Israel"** (v29) — Hebrew נֵצַח יִשְׂרָאֵל *Netzach Yisrael*, a title found only here. נֵצַח is "eminence, glory" (1 Chr 29:11) or "endurance, perpetuity" (לָנֶצַח "forever"); so also "the Glory of Israel", "the Enduring One of Israel". The meaning is uncertain. **"lie"** — Hebrew שִׁקֵּר, "deal falsely". "A man" is אָדָם.
- **"honour me now, I pray thee" → "Now honor me, please"** (v30) — Hebrew כַּבְּדֵנִי נָא.
- **"Bring ye hither to me Agag" → "Bring forward to me Agag"** (v32) — Hebrew הַגִּישׁוּ. **"Agag came unto him delicately" → "Agag went to him cheerfully"** — Hebrew מַעֲדַנֹּת *ma'adannot*: uncertain. As pointed it is a plural of "delight, dainty" used as an adverb ("with delights": at ease, cheerfully, or daintily); the same form is "bonds" in Job 38:31 ("in fetters"); the Septuagint has "trembling" (from memory). **"Surely the bitterness of death is past" → "has turned aside"** — Hebrew סָר; the Septuagint has "Is death so bitter?" (from memory).
- **"As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women" → "has bereaved … be bereaved"** (v33) — Hebrew שִׁכֵּל, to rob of children; מִנָּשִׁים is "among women" or "more than women".
- **"hewed Agag in pieces"** (v33) — Hebrew וַיְשַׁסֵּף, a verb found only here; "hew in pieces" is the traditional sense, uncertain (the Septuagint has "slaughtered", from memory). It is done "before Yahweh".
- **"came no more to see Saul … nevertheless Samuel mourned" → "did not see Saul again … for Samuel mourned"** (v35) — Hebrew לֹא־יָסַף … לִרְאוֹת, literally "did not add to see"; כִּי "for".

### Notes
- **The Amalekite war** — completion of Yahweh's ancient vow to destroy Amalek (Ex 17:14–16, Deut 25:17–19). Saul's test.
- **Saul's partial obedience** — kept the king alive; kept back the best livestock. Substantive disobedience despite claims of compliance.
- **"To listen is better than sacrifice"** (v22) — foundational prophetic critique of ritualism. Echoes in Hos 6:6, Mic 6:8, Mt 9:13, 12:7.
- **Second rejection** — first in ch 13, now definitive. Saul's kingdom passed on to "your neighbor, who is better than you" — David, unnamed here but introduced in ch 16.
- **Saul's torn robe** (v27) — symbolic action. Later (24:4–5) David will cut Saul's robe; and Saul himself recognizes kingdom-loss.
- **"Will not be sorry" / "was sorry"** (v29, 11, 35) — the same verb says that Yahweh's judgment against Saul stands and that Yahweh was sorry he had made Saul king; the text holds the two together.
