## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"was afraid at the meeting of David" → "trembled to meet David"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיֶּחֱרַד … לִקְרַאת, "trembled toward meeting": he came to meet him trembling. No verb "came" stands in the Hebrew.
- **"hath commanded me a business" → "has commanded me a matter"** (v2, 8) — Hebrew דָּבָר "word, matter"; צִוָּה "command" twice in the verse.
- **"I have appointed my servants to such and such a place" → "the young men I have directed to such and such a place"** (v2) — Hebrew יוֹדַעְתִּי, a form of יָדַע "know" found only here (literally perhaps "I have made know"); its exact sense is not certain. The Septuagint has "I have charged" and a Qumran scroll is said to read יעדתי "I have appointed" (both from memory); the Masoretic Text is translated. פְּלֹנִי אַלְמוֹנִי is "a certain one, unnamed": "the place of such and such a one". "My" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"five loaves of bread"** (v3) — Hebrew חֲמִשָּׁה־לֶחֶם, literally "five of bread"; "loaves" is supplied. **"under thine hand" kept** (v3, 4, 8) — Hebrew תַּחַת יָד.
- **"common bread … hallowed bread" → "common bread … holy bread"** (v4) — Hebrew לֶחֶם חֹל, "bread of the common" (what is not holy), and לֶחֶם קֹדֶשׁ, "bread of holiness".
- **"from women" → "from woman"** (v4, 5) — Hebrew אִשָּׁה, singular.
- **"Of a truth women have been kept from us about these three days, since I came out" → "Surely woman has been kept from us, as yesterday and the day before. When I went out …"** (v5) — Hebrew כִּתְמוֹל שִׁלְשֹׁם, literally "as yesterday, the day before" (= as before, as always); "three days" is not said. The verse is difficult. Word for word: "the vessels of the young men were holiness, and it [is] a common way, and how much more today shall it / he be holy in the vessel". כְּלִי is "vessel, implement, gear" (the same word as "gear" in v8); whether the men's bodies or their equipment are meant is not certain, nor what "it" in the last clause is (the way, the bread, or the man). The verse keeps the words and does not decide.
- **"hallowed bread" → "what was holy"** (v6) — Hebrew קֹדֶשׁ alone, "holiness, a holy thing"; "bread" is not repeated.
- **"shewbread" → "the bread of the face"** (v6) — Hebrew לֶחֶם הַפָּנִים, literally "bread of the face": the bread set before the face of Yahweh (Exod 25:30). "Showbread" and "bread of the Presence" are interpretations. "From before Yahweh" is מִלִּפְנֵי, literally "from before the face of". The participle "removed" is plural (the loaves).
- **"detained" → "kept back"** (v7) — Hebrew נֶעְצָר, from עָצַר "restrain, shut in", the verb of v5 ("kept from us"). Why he was kept there is not said.
- **"the chiefest of the herdmen" → "the mighty one of the shepherds"** (v7) — Hebrew אַבִּיר "mighty one"; רֹעִים "shepherds". "Servants" (v7, 11, 14) is עֲבָדִים, "slaves": the king's men.
- **"And is there not here"** (v8) — Hebrew וְאִין, a form found only here; it is taken as אֵין "is there not" (or as "if there is").
- **"my weapons" → "my gear"** (v8) — Hebrew כֵּלַי, the word "vessels" of v5. **"required haste" → "was urgent"** — Hebrew נָחוּץ, found only here; the sense is drawn from the context.
- **"slewest" → "struck down"** (v9, 11) — Hebrew הִכָּה "strike". **"a cloth" → "the cloak"** (v9) — Hebrew הַשִּׂמְלָה, the outer garment, with the article. **"the valley of Elah"** — אֵלָה is "terebinth": "the valley of the terebinth".
- **"for fear of Saul" → "from the face of Saul"** (v10) — Hebrew מִפְּנֵי. The same word stands in v12: "feared greatly because of Achish", literally "from the face of Achish".
- **"did they not sing one to another of him in dances" → "Is it not of this one that they answer in the dances"** (v11) — Hebrew יַעֲנוּ, from עָנָה "answer": they sing in answer to one another. **"his thousands … his ten thousands"** — Hebrew הִכָּה … בַּאֲלָפָיו, literally "struck among his thousands". The written text (ketiv) has the singular "his thousand … his ten thousand"; the reading tradition (qere) has the plural, as 18:7.
- **"laid up these words in his heart" → "put these words in his heart"** (v12) — Hebrew שִׂים "put".
- **"changed his behaviour before them" → "changed his sense in their eyes"** (v13) — Hebrew טַעַם, literally "taste": discernment, good sense (the word of 25:33 and of the heading of Psalm 34). The verb has an unusual suffix ("changed it, his sense").
- **"feigned himself mad in their hands" → "raved in their hand"** (v13) — Hebrew וַיִּתְהֹלֵל, from הָלַל "be mad, rave", in the reflexive stem: he behaved as one out of his mind. Another root than "mad" in v14–15. "In their hand": while he was in their power.
- **"scrabbled" → "made marks"** (v13) — Hebrew וַיְתָיו (qere), from תָּוָה "make a mark". The Septuagint has "drummed" (from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept. **"spittle … onto his beard"** — Hebrew רִיר; וַיּוֹרֶד "made go down".
- **"the man is mad" → "a man acting mad"; "to play the mad man in my presence" → "to act mad upon me"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew מִשְׁתַּגֵּעַ / לְהִשְׁתַּגֵּעַ, the reflexive stem of שָׁגַע "be mad" ("behave as a madman"); מְשֻׁגָּעִים "madmen". עָלַי is "upon me, against me": at my cost.
- **"Have I need of mad men" → "Am I lacking madmen"** (v15) — Hebrew חֲסַר "lacking". **"this fellow" → "this one"** — Hebrew זֶה "this".

### Notes
- **Verse numbers** — in the Hebrew Bible this chapter is 21:2–16; Hebrew 21:1 is our 20:42b.
- **David at Nob** — priestly city. Ahimelech son of Ahitub (22:9; great-grandson of Eli, 14:3) is priest. What David says about the king's mission is not true.
- **Bread of the face** (v6) — showbread; priestly food (Lev 24:5–9). Jesus cites this episode (Mk 2:25–26); there the time is named "in the days of Abiathar the high priest", while the priest in this chapter is Ahimelech, Abiathar's father (22:20).
- **Doeg the Edomite** (v7) — ominous observer. Will report to Saul (ch 22) and kill the priests.
- **Goliath's sword** — David receives the sword of the man he struck down (17:51, 54).
- **David at Gath** — fleeing to the Philistines. Recognized and feared.
- **Madness** — the headings of Psalms 34 and 56 refer to David among the Philistines of Gath; Ps 34 uses the words "changed his sense".
