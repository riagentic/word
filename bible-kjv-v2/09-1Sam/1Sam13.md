## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v13) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"Saul reigned one year; and when he had reigned two years over Israel" → "Saul was a year old when he began to reign, and two years he reigned over Israel"** (v1) — Hebrew בֶּן־שָׁנָה שָׁאוּל בְּמָלְכוֹ וּשְׁתֵּי שָׁנִים מָלַךְ עַל־יִשְׂרָאֵל, word for word "a son of a year was Saul in his reigning, and two years he reigned over Israel". This is the usual formula for a king's age and length of reign (2 Sam 2:10; 5:4), and the verse is translated as the Masoretic Text prints it. It is difficult: Saul was a grown man with a grown son (v2), and his reign fills more than two years of story. It is commonly held that the numbers were lost in copying; other old readings take the words as they stand ("like a child of one year", so the Targum; "in the second year of his reign", so the KJV). The verse is missing in the oldest Septuagint text, and later Greek manuscripts have "thirty years old"; Acts 13:21 gives Saul forty years (all cited from memory). Nothing is supplied here.
- **"three thousand men of Israel" → "three thousand out of Israel"** (v2) — the Hebrew has no word "men". **"mount Bethel" → "the hill country of Bethel"** — Hebrew הַר "mountain", of a region. **"every man to his tent" → "each man to his tents"** — Hebrew לְאֹהָלָיו, plural. "Jonathan" is here and through the chapter the short form יוֹנָתָן *Yonatan*.
- **"smote the garrison" → "struck the garrison"** (v3, 4) — Hebrew הִכָּה "strike"; נְצִיב *netziv* is uncertain: "garrison", or "prefect" (one man set over the district), or "pillar" (a standing stone, as Gen 19:26). **"heard of it" → "heard"** — no object in the Hebrew.
- **"blew the trumpet" → "blew on the shofar"** (v3) — Hebrew תָּקַע בַּשּׁוֹפָר, the ram's horn. **"Let the Hebrews hear"** — so the Masoretic Text; the Septuagint has "the slaves have revolted" (from memory).
- **"heard say that" → "heard, saying"** (v4) — Hebrew לֵאמֹר. **"was had in abomination with the Philistines" → "has made itself stink among the Philistines"** — Hebrew נִבְאַשׁ … בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים, the nifal of בָּאַשׁ "stink": has become odious to them. **"were called together" → "were called out"** — Hebrew וַיִּצָּעֲקוּ, the nifal of צָעַק "cry out": were summoned by the cry.
- **"gathered themselves together" → "were gathered"** (v5, 11) — Hebrew נֶאֶסְפוּ, nifal.
- **"thirty thousand chariots"** (v5) — Hebrew שְׁלֹשִׁים אֶלֶף רֶכֶב, as the Masoretic Text prints it. The number is far above any other chariot force in the Bible and is larger than the number of horsemen; some Greek manuscripts and the Syriac have "three thousand" (from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept. **"as the sand which is on the sea shore in multitude" → "like the sand that is on the shore of the sea for multitude"** — Hebrew שְׂפַת הַיָּם, literally "the lip of the sea". **"eastward from" → "east of"** — Hebrew קִדְמַת, literally "in front of".
- **"they were in a strait" → "they were in distress"** (v6) — Hebrew צַר־לוֹ, literally "it was narrow for him" (Israel as one man). **"distressed" → "pressed"** — Hebrew נִגַּשׂ, another root: "driven, pressed" by a taskmaster (Exod 3:7).
- **"in caves, and in thickets, and in rocks, and in high places, and in pits" → "in caves and in thickets and in crags and in vaults and in pits"** (v6) — חֲוָחִים is the plural of "thorn, bramble" ("holes" is a common correction); סְלָעִים "crags"; צְרִחִים is found only here and in Judg 9:46, 49, an underground chamber or stronghold: uncertain; בֹּרוֹת "pits, cisterns".
- **"some of the Hebrews went over Jordan" → "Hebrews passed over the Jordan"** (v7) — Hebrew וְעִבְרִים עָבְרוּ *we-ivrim averu*, a play on the two words; "some of" is not in the Hebrew. **"followed him trembling" → "trembled after him"** — Hebrew חָרְדוּ אַחֲרָיו.
- **"according to the set time that Samuel had appointed" → "to the appointed time that was Samuel's"** (v8) — Hebrew לַמּוֹעֵד אֲשֶׁר שְׁמוּאֵל, "to the appointed time that Samuel": the clause has no verb. Some Hebrew manuscripts and the old versions have "that Samuel had said / set" (from memory); "was" is supplied and nothing more. "Waited": the reading tradition has וַיּוֹחֶל, the written text another form of the same verb. **"were scattered from him" → "scattered from him"** — Hebrew וַיָּפֶץ … מֵעָלָיו, literally "from upon him".
- **"Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and peace offerings" → "Bring forward to me the burnt offering and the peace offerings"** (v9) — Hebrew הַגִּשׁוּ, with the article on both nouns. **"offered" → "offered up"** (v9, 10, 12) — Hebrew הֶעֱלָה, literally "made go up" the "going-up" (עֹלָה): one root.
- **"that he might salute him" → "to bless him"** (v10) — Hebrew לְבָרֲכוֹ; to "bless" on meeting is to greet.
- **"within the days appointed" → "at the appointed time of the days"** (v11) — Hebrew לְמוֹעֵד הַיָּמִים.
- **"I have not made supplication unto the LORD" → "the face of Yahweh I have not besought"** (v12) — Hebrew וּפְנֵי יְהוָה לֹא חִלִּיתִי, literally "the face of Yahweh I have not made soft". **"I forced myself"** — Hebrew וָאֶתְאַפַּק, "I restrained myself" (Gen 43:31; 45:1): here of mastering oneself to do what one would not.
- **"Thou hast done foolishly" → "You have acted foolishly"** (v13) — Hebrew נִסְכָּלְתָּ. **"for now would the LORD have established"** — Hebrew כִּי עַתָּה הֵכִין, literally "for now Yahweh has established": the perfect after "for now" states what would have been. "Over Israel" is אֶל "to, toward". **"kingdom"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew מַמְלָכָה.
- **"shall not continue" → "shall not stand"** (v14) — Hebrew לֹא תָקוּם.
- **"a man after his own heart" → "a man according to his heart"** (v14) — Hebrew אִישׁ כִּלְבָבוֹ, "a man like / according to his heart": a man of Yahweh's own choosing, or a man whose heart is as Yahweh's; the Hebrew says no more.
- **"hath commanded him to be captain over his people" → "has commanded him to be leader over his people"** (v14) — Hebrew וַיְצַוֵּהוּ … לְנָגִיד, literally "commanded him for *nagid*". נָגִיד *nagid* is the word used for Saul in 9:16; 10:1: the one designated to lead; it is kept apart from מֶלֶךְ "king". The verbs are past: the choice is spoken of as made.
- **"that were present with him" → "who were found with him"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew הַנִּמְצְאִים.
- **"abode in Gibeah of Benjamin" → "were sitting in Geba of Benjamin"** (v16) — Hebrew יֹשְׁבִים בְּגֶבַע; the Masoretic Text has Gibeah in v2, 15 and Geba in v3, 16, two places near each other.
- **"the spoilers" → "the destroyer"** (v17) — Hebrew הַמַּשְׁחִית, singular with the article, "the one that ruins": the raiding force. **"three companies"** — Hebrew רָאשִׁים "heads"; "turned" is an imperfect, "would turn". "The way of" stands three times in v17, 18.
- **"that looketh to the valley of Zeboim" → "that looks down on the Valley of Zeboim"** (v18) — Hebrew הַנִּשְׁקָף עַל; the name is "the valley of the hyenas".
- **"no smith" → "no craftsman"** (v19) — Hebrew חָרָשׁ, a worker in metal, wood or stone. "Said": the reading tradition has the plural, the written text the singular. **"swords or spears" → "sword or spear"** — singular.
- **"his share, and his coulter, and his axe, and his mattock" → "his plowshare and his mattock and his axe and his plowing tool"** (v20) — four tool names, three of them rare. The first (מַחֲרֶשֶׁת) and the fourth (מַחֲרֵשָׁה) are two forms from the root "plow" and cannot be told apart with certainty; אֵת is the tool of Isa 2:4 ("swords into …"), a plowshare or mattock; קַרְדֹּם is an axe. The Septuagint has "sickle" for the fourth (from memory). **"sharpen"** — Hebrew לָטַשׁ.
- **"Yet they had a file for the mattocks …" → "And the charge was a pim for the plowing tools …"** (v21) — the verse is among the hardest in the book. פְּצִירָה is found only here; "charge, price" is the best-supported sense, uncertain. פִים *pim* is found only here; stone weights marked with this word have been found, about two thirds of a shekel: it is a weight of silver, the price paid. וְלִשְׁלֹשׁ קִלְּשׁוֹן is literally "and for three of fork": a three-pronged fork, or "a third (of a shekel) for the fork"; uncertain. **"to sharpen the goads" → "for setting the goad"** — Hebrew וּלְהַצִּיב הַדָּרְבָן, "to set up / fix the goad".
- **"in the day of battle"** (v22) — Hebrew בְּיוֹם מִלְחֶמֶת, a form that expects a name after it ("the battle of …"); none follows. **"but with Saul and with Jonathan his son was there found"** — Hebrew וַתִּמָּצֵא, "it was found".
- **"the garrison of the Philistines" → "the outpost of the Philistines"** (v23) — Hebrew מַצַּב *matzav*, "standing place, post", another word than the נְצִיב of v3, 4. **"passage" → "pass"** — Hebrew מַעֲבַר, from "pass over".

### Notes
- **Chronology problem** (v1) — the framework verse for Saul's reign; its numbers do not fit the story as they stand. Acts 13:21 gives 40 years total.
- **Jonathan's initial provocation** (v3) — striking the Philistine garrison. Sets off the war.
- **Saul's impatience** (v8–14) — waits 6+ days for Samuel; on the 7th offers sacrifice himself. The act is not itself idolatrous but constitutes disobedience to Samuel's specific instruction (10:8).
- **First rejection** (v13–14) — Saul's kingdom will not stand. A "man according to his heart" (later: David) will replace him.
- **Philistine metal monopoly** (v19–22) — Philistines controlled iron smelting; Israel lacked weapon-smiths. Israel's army goes to battle nearly weaponless.
- **Transition signal** — Saul's rejection prophesied here; his replacement will appear in ch 16. His story has eight chapters of doomed reign ahead.
