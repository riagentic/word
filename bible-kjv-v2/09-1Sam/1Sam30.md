# 1 Samuel 30

[1] And it came to pass, when David and his men came to ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧ on the third day, that the ⟦amalekites|Amaleqim|Amalekites⟧ had made a raid on the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧ and on ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧, and they had struck ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧ and burned it with fire.

[2] And they had taken captive the women who were in it, from small to great; they had not put a man to death, and they drove them off and went their way.

[3] And David and his men came to the city, and behold, it was burned with fire, and their wives and their sons and their daughters had been taken captive.

[4] And David and the people who were with him lifted up their voice and wept, until there was no power in them to weep.

[5] And the two wives of David had been taken captive, ⟦ahinoam|Achinoam|Ahinoam⟧ the ⟦jezreel|Yizreelit|Jezreelitess⟧ and ⟦abigail|Avigayil|Abigail⟧ the wife of ⟦nabal|Naval|Nabal⟧ the ⟦carmel|Karmeli|Carmelite⟧.

[6] And David was in great distress, for the people spoke of stoning him, for the soul of all the people was bitter, each man for his sons and for his daughters. And David strengthened himself in ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ his ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[7] And David said to ⟦abiathar|Evyatar|Abiathar⟧ the priest, the son of ⟦ahimelech|Achimelech|Ahimelech⟧, "Bring the ephod near to me, please." And ⟦abiathar|Evyatar|Abiathar⟧ brought the ephod near to David.

[8] And David asked of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, saying, "Shall I pursue after this raiding band? Shall I overtake it?" And he said to him, "Pursue, for you shall surely overtake and shall surely deliver."

[9] And David went, he and the six hundred men who were with him, and they came to the wadi Besor, and those who remained stood still.

[10] And David pursued, he and four hundred men; and two hundred men stood still, who were too exhausted to pass over the wadi Besor.

[11] And they found ⟦egyptian|a Mitzri|an Egyptian⟧ man in the field and took him to David, and they gave him bread, and he ate, and they gave him water to drink.

[12] And they gave him a slice of a fig cake and two raisin cakes, and he ate, and his spirit returned to him, for he had not eaten bread and had not drunk water three days and three nights.

[13] And David said to him, "Whose are you, and where are you from?" And he said, "I am ⟦egyptian|a Mitzri|an Egyptian⟧ young man, a slave to an ⟦amalekites|Amaleqi|Amalekite⟧ man, and my lord forsook me, for I fell sick three days ago.

[14] We made a raid on the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧ of the ⟦cherethites|Keretim|Cherethites⟧, and on what belongs to ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, and on the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧ of ⟦caleb|Kalev|Caleb⟧, and ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧ we burned with fire."

[15] And David said to him, "Will you bring me down to this raiding band?" And he said, "Swear to me by ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ that you will not put me to death and will not hand me over into the hand of my lord, and I will bring you down to this raiding band."

[16] And he brought him down, and behold, they were spread out over the face of all the land, eating and drinking and holding a feast with all the great spoil that they had taken from the land of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ and from the land of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧.

[17] And David struck them from the twilight and until the evening of their morrow, and not a man of them escaped, except four hundred young men who rode on the camels and fled.

[18] And David delivered all that ⟦amalek|Amaleq|Amalek⟧ had taken, and his two wives David delivered.

[19] And nothing was missing to them, from the small and to the great, and to sons and daughters, and from the spoil, and to all that they had taken for themselves; David brought back the whole.

[20] And David took all the flock and the herd; they drove before that livestock, and they said, "This is the spoil of David."

[21] And David came to the two hundred men who had been too exhausted to go after David, and they had made them stay at the wadi Besor, and they went out to meet David and to meet the people who were with him. And David drew near to the people and asked them about their ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧.

[22] And every man, evil and ⟦belial|of Beliyyaal|worthless⟧, of the men who had gone with David answered, and they said, "Because they did not go with me, we will not give them of the spoil that we have delivered, except to each man his wife and his sons, and let them drive them off and go."

[23] And David said, "You shall not do so, my brothers, with what ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ has given us; and he has kept us and has given the raiding band that came against us into our hand.

[24] And who will listen to you in this matter? For as the share of him who goes down into the battle, so is the share of him who sits by the gear; together they shall share."

[25] And it came to pass from that day and onward that he made it a statute and an ordinance for ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, to this day.

[26] And David came to ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧, and he sent of the spoil to the elders of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, to his friends, saying, "Behold, a blessing for you from the spoil of the enemies of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧":

[27] to those who were in ⟦bethel|Beit El|Bethel⟧, and to those who were in ⟦ramoth|Ramot|Ramoth⟧ of the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧, and to those who were in ⟦jattir|Yattir|Jattir⟧,

[28] and to those who were in Aroer, and to those who were in ⟦siphmoth|Sifmot|Siphmoth⟧, and to those who were in Eshtemoa,

[29] and to those who were in ⟦racal|Rachal|Racal⟧, and to those who were in the cities of the ⟦jerahmeelites|Yerachmeeli|Jerahmeelites⟧, and to those who were in the cities of the ⟦kenites|Qenim|Kenites⟧,

[30] and to those who were in ⟦hormah|Chormah|Hormah⟧, and to those who were in ⟦bor-ashan|Bor-Ashan|Bor-ashan⟧, and to those who were in ⟦athach|Atach|Athach⟧,

[31] and to those who were in ⟦hebron|Chevron|Hebron⟧, and to all the places where David had walked about, he and his men.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v6, 15) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"the Amalekites had invaded the south, and Ziklag" → "the Amalekites had made a raid on the Negev and on Ziklag"** (v1) — Hebrew וַעֲמָלֵקִי פָשְׁטוּ אֶל־נֶגֶב וְאֶל־צִקְלַג: "the Amalekite", a singular collective with a plural verb; פָּשַׁט אֶל "make a raid on" (27:8); נֶגֶב, the dry south, here without the article. **"smitten" → "struck"** — Hebrew הִכָּה.
- **"had taken the women captives, that were therein: they slew not any, either great or small, but carried them away" → "had taken captive the women who were in it, from small to great; they had not put a man to death, and they drove them off"** (v2) — Hebrew אֶת־הַנָּשִׁים אֲשֶׁר־בָּהּ מִקָּטֹן וְעַד־גָּדוֹל: in the Masoretic Text only "the women who were in it" are named, and "from small to great" goes with them by the accents (KJV joins it to "they slew not"). The Septuagint is reported to add "and all" before "who were in it" (cited from memory); the earlier text followed that ("the women and all who were in it"). הֵמִית "put to death"; נָהַג "drive", as of a herd (v20, 22).
- **"lifted up their voice and wept, until they had no more power to weep"** (v4) — kept; Hebrew קוֹלָם, singular; כֹּחַ "power".
- **"Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite"** (v5) — here the adjective is masculine (הַכַּרְמְלִי) and belongs to Nabal; 27:3 has the feminine.
- **"David was greatly distressed" → "David was in great distress"** (v6) — Hebrew וַתֵּצֶר לְדָוִד מְאֹד, literally "it was very narrow for David". **"the soul of all the people was grieved" → "was bitter"** — Hebrew מָרָה נֶפֶשׁ. **"for his sons"** — the reading tradition (qere) בָּנָיו; the written text has "his son".
- **"David encouraged himself in the LORD his God" → "David strengthened himself in Yahweh his *Elohim*"** (v6) — Hebrew וַיִּתְחַזֵּק … בַּיהוָה, the reflexive of חָזַק "be strong".
- **"bring me hither the ephod" → "Bring the ephod near to me, please"** (v7) — Hebrew הַגִּישָׁה־נָּא לִי, as 23:9; נָא "please"; so also "brought … near". The ephod is the one Abiathar brought down in his hand (23:6, 9), by which Yahweh was asked.
- **"enquired at the LORD" → "asked of Yahweh"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁאַל … בַּיהוָה, the verb שָׁאַל "ask", as in 28:6: David is answered, Saul was not.
- **"Shall I pursue after this troop? shall I overtake them?" → "Shall I pursue after this raiding band? Shall I overtake it?"** (v8) — Hebrew גְּדוּד, a band of raiders (Gen 49:19); singular "it". The first question has no question particle, the second has.
- **"thou shalt surely overtake them, and without fail recover all" → "you shall surely overtake and shall surely deliver"** (v8) — Hebrew הַשֵּׂג תַּשִּׂיג וְהַצֵּל תַּצִּיל, twice the infinitive absolute with its verb: literally "overtaking you shall overtake and delivering you shall deliver". No object is named. הִצִּיל "deliver" (v18, 22).
- **"the brook Besor, where those that were left behind stayed" → "the wadi Besor, and those who remained stood still"** (v9, 10, 21) — Hebrew נַחַל הַבְּשׂוֹר, "the wadi of the Besor"; וְהַנּוֹתָרִים עָמָדוּ, "and the ones remaining stood". Who "remained" is told in v10.
- **"which were so faint that they could not go over" → "who were too exhausted to pass over"** (v10, 21) — Hebrew פִּגְּרוּ מֵעֲבֹר: the verb is found only in these two verses; it is commonly connected with פֶּגֶר "carcass" (to be dead tired). Its exact sense is uncertain.
- **"an Egyptian" → "an Egyptian man"; "brought him" → "took him"** (v11) — Hebrew אִישׁ־מִצְרִי; וַיִּקְחוּ.
- **"a piece of a cake of figs, and two clusters of raisins" → "a slice of a fig cake and two raisin cakes"** (v12) — Hebrew פֶּלַח דְּבֵלָה, a cut piece of a pressed fig cake; צִמֻּקִים, dried grapes, pressed in lumps (25:18).
- **"To whom belongest thou? and whence art thou?" → "Whose are you, and where are you from?"** (v13) — Hebrew לְמִי־אַתָּה וְאֵי מִזֶּה אָתָּה.
- **"a young man of Egypt, servant to an Amalekite; and my master left me" → "an Egyptian young man, a slave to an Amalekite man, and my lord forsook me"** (v13) — Hebrew נַעַר "boy, young man"; עֶבֶד, here a man held in bondage, "slave"; אֲדֹנִי "my lord"; עָזַב. **"three days agone I fell sick"** — Hebrew הַיּוֹם שְׁלֹשָׁה, literally "today three".
- **"We made an invasion upon the south of the Cherethites, and upon the coast which belongeth to Judah" → "We made a raid on the Negev of the Cherethites, and on what belongs to Judah"** (v14) — Hebrew נֶגֶב הַכְּרֵתִי (without a preposition; "the Cherethite", a singular collective) וְעַל־אֲשֶׁר לִיהוּדָה, with עַל "on" for the second and third. "Coast", "territory" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"Canst thou bring me down to this company?" → "Will you bring me down to this raiding band?"** (v15) — Hebrew הֲתוֹרִדֵנִי; הַגְּדוּד, the word of v8, 23. **"that thou wilt neither kill me, nor deliver me into the hands of my master" → "that you will not put me to death and will not hand me over into the hand of my lord"** — the oath form אִם … וְאִם "if you … and if you …"; הִסְגִּיר, literally "shut up into the hand".
- **"spread abroad upon all the earth, eating and drinking, and dancing, because of all the great spoil" → "spread out over the face of all the land, eating and drinking and holding a feast with all the great spoil"** (v16) — Hebrew נְטֻשִׁים, "left loose, let go" (the passive of נָטַשׁ "abandon, leave to itself"): scattered about, off their guard. חֹגְגִים is the verb of חַג "feast" (Exod 5:1); some take it here as "reeling" or "dancing". בְּכֹל is "in / with all".
- **"from the twilight even unto the evening of the next day" → "from the twilight and until the evening of their morrow"** (v17) — Hebrew מֵהַנֶּשֶׁף וְעַד־הָעֶרֶב לְמָחֳרָתָם. נֶשֶׁף is the half-light of dusk or of dawn; which is meant is not certain. לְמָחֳרָתָם, "of their morrow", with a suffix found only here, is difficult.
- **"save four hundred young men, which rode upon camels" → "except four hundred young men who rode on the camels"** (v17) — Hebrew אִישׁ־נַעַר; "the camels" has the article.
- **"David recovered all that the Amalekites had carried away: and David rescued his two wives" → "David delivered all that Amalek had taken, and his two wives David delivered"** (v18) — the one verb הִצִּיל twice; עֲמָלֵק "Amalek" (the name of the people, not "the Amalekites"); לָקַח "take".
- **"there was nothing lacking to them, neither small nor great, neither sons nor daughters, neither spoil, nor any thing that they had taken to them: David recovered all" → "nothing was missing to them, from the small and to the great, and to sons and daughters, and from the spoil, and to all that they had taken for themselves; David brought back the whole"** (v19) — word for word: מִן … וְעַד … וְעַד … וּמִ … וְעַד, "from … and to …". הֵשִׁיב "bring back, return".
- **"David took all the flocks and the herds, which they drave before those other cattle" → "David took all the flock and the herd; they drove before that livestock"** (v20) — Hebrew נָהֲגוּ לִפְנֵי הַמִּקְנֶה הַהוּא, word for word; the Hebrew is difficult. "Which" and "other" are not in it; what was driven and what "that livestock" is are not said. It is commonly understood that the flock and herd taken from Amalek were driven in front of the livestock won back; the old versions read differently (cited from memory).
- **"whom they had made also to abide" → "and they had made them stay"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיֹּשִׁיבֻם, the causative of יָשַׁב "sit, dwell", plural in the Masoretic Text; some Hebrew manuscripts and versions have the singular, "he had made them stay" (cited from memory).
- **"when David came near to the people, he saluted them" → "David drew near to the people and asked them about their *shalom*"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיִּגַּשׁ דָּוִד אֶת־הָעָם, where אֶת may be "to" or "with" ("drew near with the people"); וַיִּשְׁאַל לָהֶם לְשָׁלוֹם, literally "asked for them about peace", the greeting.
- **"all the wicked men and men of Belial" → "every man, evil and of *Beliyyaal*"** (v22) — Hebrew כָּל־אִישׁ־רָע וּבְלִיַּעַל; בְּלִיַּעַל *beliyya'al* is a common noun, "worthlessness", not a name: every evil and worthless man.
- **"Because they went not with us" → "Because they did not go with me"** (v22) — Hebrew עִמִּי "with me": each speaks for himself. Some Hebrew manuscripts and the old versions have "with us" (cited from memory), which the earlier text followed. **"recovered" → "delivered"** — Hebrew הִצַּלְנוּ. **"his wife and his children, that they may lead them away, and depart" → "his wife and his sons, and let them drive them off and go"** — Hebrew בָּנָיו "his sons"; וְיִנְהֲגוּ, the verb of v2, 20.
- **"Ye shall not do so, my brethren, with that which the LORD hath given us, who hath preserved us" → "You shall not do so, my brothers, with what Yahweh has given us; and he has kept us"** (v23) — Hebrew אֵת אֲשֶׁר־נָתַן יְהוָה לָנוּ וַיִּשְׁמֹר אֹתָנוּ: אֵת is taken as "with"; the clause is difficult (it can also be heard as an unfinished sentence, "that which Yahweh has given us —"). שָׁמַר "keep". **"the company" → "the raiding band"** — Hebrew הַגְּדוּד.
- **"but as his part is that goeth down to the battle, so shall his part be that tarrieth by the stuff: they shall part alike" → "For as the share of him who goes down into the battle, so is the share of him who sits by the gear; together they shall share"** (v24) — Hebrew חֵלֶק "share"; הַיֹּשֵׁב עַל־הַכֵּלִים, "he who sits by the vessels" (the baggage; כְּלִי is any made article); יַחְדָּו יַחֲלֹקוּ "together they shall divide".
- **"from that day forward" → "from that day and onward"** (v25) — Hebrew וָמָעְלָה, literally "and upward". **"statute and an ordinance"** — Hebrew לְחֹק וּלְמִשְׁפָּט; מִשְׁפָּט is "judgment", here a ruling that stands.
- **"to his friends" (v26)** — Hebrew לְרֵעֵהוּ, a singular, "to his friend / neighbor", said of each of them. **"a present" → "a blessing"** — Hebrew בְּרָכָה, a gift that carries a blessing (25:27).
- **"to them which were in" → "to those who were in"** (v27–31) — Hebrew לַאֲשֶׁר בְּ, thirteen times, each joined by "and"; the earlier text shortened the list. **"south Ramoth" → "Ramoth of the Negev"** (v27) — Hebrew רָמוֹת־נֶגֶב. The peoples in v29 are singular collectives ("the Jerahmeelite", "the Kenite").
- **"where David himself and his men were wont to haunt" → "where David had walked about, he and his men"** (v31) — Hebrew הִתְהַלֶּךְ, "walk about, go to and fro".

### Notes
- **Amalekite raid on Ziklag** — while David was in the Philistine camp, Amalekites raided his base. Irony: the Amalekites Samuel commanded to destroy (ch 15) return to strike the anointed king.
- **David's lowest point** (v4–6) — wept until spent; his own men consider stoning him. But "David strengthened himself in Yahweh" — pivotal moment of recovery.
- **Ephod consultation** (v7–8) — Yahweh directs: pursue and deliver.
- **Compassion for the Egyptian** (v11–12) — treats abandoned enemy-slave with kindness. Pragmatism and charity combined.
- **Equity rule** (v21–25) — rear-guard (stayed behind) and fighters get equal share. Precedent-setting for Israel.
- **Political generosity** (v26–31) — sends portions to Judahite towns. Courting Judah for his future kingship; earning loyalty.
- **Contrast with Saul** — Saul fails (ch 28); David succeeds (ch 30). The two stories placed side-by-side to contrast their trajectories.
