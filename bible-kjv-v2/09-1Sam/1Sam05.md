# 1 Samuel 5

[1] And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ had taken the ark of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and they brought it from ⟦ebenezer|Even-HaEzer|Ebenezer⟧ to Ashdod.

[2] And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ took the ark of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and brought it into the house of Dagon and set it beside Dagon.

[3] And the ⟦ashdodites|Ashdodim|Ashdodites⟧ rose early on the morrow, and behold, Dagon was fallen on his face to the earth before the ark of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧. And they took Dagon and returned him to his place.

[4] And they rose early in the morning on the morrow, and behold, Dagon was fallen on his face to the earth before the ark of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, and the head of Dagon and the two palms of his hands were cut off, upon the threshold; only Dagon was left upon him.

[5] Therefore the priests of Dagon and all who come into the house of Dagon do not tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to this day.

[6] And the hand of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ was heavy upon the ⟦ashdodites|Ashdodim|Ashdodites⟧, and he made them desolate, and he struck them with tumors, Ashdod and its borders.

[7] And the men of Ashdod saw that it was so, and they said, "The ark of the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ shall not dwell with us, for his hand is hard upon us and upon Dagon our god."

[8] And they sent and gathered all the lords of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ to them and said, "What shall we do with the ark of the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧?" And they said, "To ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧ let the ark of the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ go around." And they brought the ark of the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ around.

[9] And it came to pass, after they had brought it around, that the hand of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ was against the city, a very great confusion; and he struck the men of the city, from small and to great, and tumors broke out on them.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*. In v1, 2, 10, 11 the Hebrew has the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim". Dagon is "our god" (v7), the same word אֱלֹהִים, in plain text.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **The titles of the ark** — kept as the Hebrew varies them: "the ark of the Elohim" (v1, 2, 10), "the ark of Yahweh" (v3, 4), "the ark of the Elohim of Israel" (v7, 8 three times, 10, 11).
- **"took the ark" → "had taken the ark"** (v1) — Hebrew וּפְלִשְׁתִּים לָקְחוּ, the subject before the verb: the story looks back to 4:11. **"Eben-ezer"** — here without the article on the first word (אֶבֶן הָעֵזֶר, "the stone of the help").
- **"they of Ashdod" → "the Ashdodites"** (v3, 6) — Hebrew אַשְׁדּוֹדִים; "the men of Ashdod" (v7) is אַנְשֵׁי־אַשְׁדּוֹד.
- **"Dagon was fallen upon his face to the earth" kept** (v3, 4) — Hebrew נֹפֵל לְפָנָיו אַרְצָה, "fallen to his face, to the earth"; לְפָנָיו may also be "before it" (the ark). **"set him in his place again" → "returned him to his place"** — Hebrew וַיָּשִׁבוּ. The Septuagint has a longer text in v3 and v6 (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"only the stump of Dagon was left to him" → "only Dagon was left upon him"** (v4) — Hebrew רַק דָּגוֹן נִשְׁאַר עָלָיו, word for word. The Hebrew is difficult: with the head and the palms cut off, what is left is called "Dagon". The old versions have "the trunk of Dagon" (cited from memory); "stump", "torso" are not in the Hebrew. **"upon the threshold"** — Hebrew אֶל־הַמִּפְתָּן, "to / toward the threshold".
- **"the hand of the LORD was heavy upon them of Ashdod" → "the hand of Yahweh was heavy upon the Ashdodites"** (v6) — Hebrew וַתִּכְבַּד … אֶל, "was heavy toward"; the root of "heavy" is that of "glory" (4:21). **"he destroyed them" → "he made them desolate"** — Hebrew וַיְשִׁמֵּם.
- **"emerods" → "tumors"** (v6, 9, 12) — the qere טְחֹרִים is translated; the written text (ketiv) has עֳפָלִים. Both words name swellings; neither is certainly known. עֹפֶל is "a mound, a swelling"; טְחֹרִים is taken as swellings at the anus (so "emerods", hemorrhoids), and the tradition read it in place of the written word. Deut 28:27 has the same pair. What sickness is meant is not said.
- **"the coasts thereof" → "its borders"** (v6) — Hebrew גְּבוּלֶיהָ, the land within its borders.
- **"shall not abide with us: for his hand is sore upon us" → "shall not dwell with us, for his hand is hard upon us"** (v7) — Hebrew יֵשֵׁב "sit, dwell"; קָשְׁתָה "is hard", another word than "heavy" (v6, 11).
- **"lords of the Philistines"** (v8, 11) — Hebrew סַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים: סֶרֶן is a word used only of the five rulers of the Philistines, perhaps a word of their own tongue.
- **"Let the ark of the God of Israel be carried about unto Gath. And they carried the ark … about thither" → "To Gath let the ark of the Elohim of Israel go around. And they brought the ark … around"** (v8) — Hebrew יִסֹּב "go around" and וַיַּסֵּבּוּ "they made it go around", the same verb in v9, 10; the Hebrew has no "thither".
- **"with a very great destruction" → "a very great confusion"** (v9) — Hebrew מְהוּמָה, tumult, panic; so also מְהוּמַת־מָוֶת "a confusion of death" (v11, KJV "a deadly destruction"). **"both small and great" → "from small and to great"**.
- **"they had emerods in their secret parts" → "tumors broke out on them"** (v9) — Hebrew וַיִּשָּׂתְרוּ לָהֶם: the verb stands only here, and "break out" is its commonly accepted sense. The Hebrew has no "secret parts".
- **"sent" → "sent away"** (v10, 11) — Hebrew שִׁלַּח, the stronger form of "send".
- **"They have brought about the ark … to us, to slay us and our people" → "They have brought around to me the ark …, to put me to death, and my people"** (v10) — the Hebrew has the singular, "me … my people" (the city speaks as one); so also in v11. הֵמִית is "put to death".
- **"let it go again to his own place" → "let it return to its place"** (v11). **"the hand of God was very heavy there"** — Hebrew יַד הָאֱלֹהִים.
- **"were smitten with the emerods" → "were struck with tumors"** (v12) — Hebrew הֻכּוּ, the passive of "strike" (v6, 9). **"the cry of the city went up to heaven" → "the cry for help of the city went up to the heavens"** — Hebrew שַׁוְעָה.

### Notes
- **Yahweh and Dagon** — Dagon lies fallen before the ark, then with head and hands cut off. What Dagon was a god of (grain, or fish from *dag* "fish") is debated.
- **The threshold** (v5) — the text gives the reason for a custom at Ashdod.
- **Three cities** — Ashdod → Gath → Ekron. The ark moves, the blow follows.
- **What sickness?** — "tumors" and the mice of ch 6 have suggested a plague carried by rodents; the text does not name the sickness.
- **Zec 9:7, Am 1:6–8** — prophetic oracles against these same Philistine cities.
