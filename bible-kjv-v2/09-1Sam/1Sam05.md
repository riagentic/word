# 1 Samuel 5

[1] The Philistines took the ark of *Elohim* and brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.

[2] The Philistines took the ark of *Elohim*, brought it into the house of Dagon, and set it beside Dagon.

[3] When the men of Ashdod rose early the next day, behold, Dagon had fallen face down on the ground before the ark of Yahweh. They took Dagon and set him in his place again.

[4] When they rose early the next morning, behold, Dagon had fallen face down on the ground before the ark of Yahweh, and the head of Dagon and both palms of his hands were cut off on the threshold; only the torso of Dagon was left to him.

[5] Therefore neither the priests of Dagon nor any who enter Dagon's house tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to this day.

[6] The hand of Yahweh was heavy against the men of Ashdod; he devastated them and struck them with tumors — Ashdod and its territory.

[7] When the men of Ashdod saw what was happening, they said, "The ark of the *Elohim* of Israel shall not remain with us, for his hand is heavy against us and against Dagon our god."

[8] So they sent and gathered all the lords of the Philistines to them and said, "What shall we do with the ark of the *Elohim* of Israel?" They answered, "Let the ark of the *Elohim* of Israel be carried around to Gath." So they carried the ark of the *Elohim* of Israel around there.

[9] After they had carried it around, the hand of Yahweh was against the city with a very great panic; he struck the men of the city, both small and great, and they broke out with tumors.

[10] So they sent the ark of *Elohim* to Ekron. As the ark of *Elohim* came to Ekron, the Ekronites cried out, saying, "They have brought the ark of the *Elohim* of Israel around to us, to kill us and our people!"

[11] They sent and gathered all the lords of the Philistines and said, "Send away the ark of the *Elohim* of Israel; let it return to its own place, that it may not kill us and our people." For there was a deadly panic throughout the city; the hand of *Elohim* was very heavy there.

[12] The men who did not die were stricken with tumors, and the cry of the city went up to the heavens.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"emerods" → "tumors"** (v6, 9, 12) — Hebrew עֳפָלִים *ofalim*. Swellings / tumors, possibly bubonic-plague-like. Modernized term.
- **"lords of the Philistines"** (v8, 11) — Hebrew *sarnei Pelishtim*. The five city-governors (*seren*, possibly cognate with Greek *tyrannos*).
- **"stump of Dagon" → "torso of Dagon"** (v4) — Hebrew *dagon nish'ar alav*. Only "the Dagon" (= fish-part? body below the hands and head) remained. Fuels speculation that Dagon was part-fish.
- **"destroyed them" → "devastated them"** (v6).

### Notes
- **Yahweh vs Dagon** — mini-theomachy. Dagon prostrates before the ark; then is dismembered. Hebrew irony: *dag* = "fish"; the "fish-god" left with only his torso (etymology debated: fish-god or grain-god).
- **Threshold taboo** (v5) — etiology for a Philistine cultic custom. The text preserves it as an unintended monument to Yahweh's supremacy.
- **Plague sweeps three cities** — Ashdod → Gath → Ekron. The ark moves, the plague follows.
- **Bubonic plague?** — "tumors" and the later "mice" offering (ch 6) suggest a plague-vector connection (rodents/fleas). Pre-modern medical ignorance, but the details have been noted.
- **Zec 9:7, Am 1:6–8** — prophetic oracles against these same Philistine cities; they remained rivals until fully destroyed.
