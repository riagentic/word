# 1 Samuel 1

[1] There was a certain man of Ramathaim-zophim, of the hill country of Ephraim, whose name was Elkanah son of Jeroham son of Elihu son of Tohu son of Zuph, an Ephraimite.

[2] He had two wives; the name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.

[3] This man would go up from his city year by year to worship and to sacrifice to Yahweh of Hosts — at Shiloh. The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests of Yahweh there.

[4] On the day when Elkanah offered sacrifice, he would give portions to Peninnah his wife and to all her sons and daughters;

[5] but to Hannah he gave a double portion, because he loved Hannah, though Yahweh had closed her womb.

[6] Her rival provoked her severely, to irritate her, because Yahweh had closed her womb.

[7] So it went on year by year; whenever she went up to the house of Yahweh, she would provoke her; therefore she wept and would not eat.

[8] Then Elkanah her husband said to her, "Hannah, why do you weep? Why do you not eat? Why is your heart grieved? Am I not better to you than ten sons?"

[9] Hannah rose after they had eaten and drunk in Shiloh. Now Eli the priest was sitting on the seat by the doorpost of the temple of Yahweh.

[10] She was in bitterness of soul, and she prayed to Yahweh and wept bitterly.

[11] She vowed a vow and said, "O Yahweh of Hosts, if you will indeed look on the affliction of your maidservant and remember me, and not forget your maidservant, but will give to your maidservant a male child, then I will give him to Yahweh all the days of his life, and no razor shall come upon his head."

[12] As she continued praying before Yahweh, Eli watched her mouth.

[13] Hannah was speaking in her heart; only her lips moved, but her voice was not heard. Therefore Eli thought she was drunk.

[14] Eli said to her, "How long will you be drunk? Put away your wine from you."

[15] Hannah answered and said, "No, my lord, I am a woman troubled in spirit. I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but I have poured out my soul before Yahweh.

[16] Do not regard your maidservant as a worthless woman, for out of the abundance of my complaint and grief I have spoken until now."

[17] Then Eli answered and said, "Go in peace; and may the *Elohim* of Israel grant your petition that you have asked of him."

[18] She said, "May your maidservant find favor in your eyes." So the woman went her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad.

[19] They rose early in the morning and worshiped before Yahweh, and returned and came to their house at Ramah. Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, and Yahweh remembered her.

[20] In due time, after Hannah had conceived, she bore a son and called his name Samuel "because I have asked him of Yahweh."

[21] The man Elkanah and all his house went up to offer to Yahweh the yearly sacrifice and to fulfill his vow.

[22] But Hannah did not go up, for she said to her husband, "When the child is weaned, then I will bring him, that he may appear before Yahweh and remain there forever."

[23] Elkanah her husband said to her, "Do what seems good in your eyes. Wait until you have weaned him; only may Yahweh establish his word." So the woman stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him.

[24] When she had weaned him, she took him up with her, with a three-year-old bull, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine, and brought him to the house of Yahweh at Shiloh. And the boy was young.

[25] They slaughtered the bull and brought the boy to Eli.

[26] She said, "Oh, my lord, as your soul lives, my lord, I am the woman who stood here beside you, praying to Yahweh.

[27] For this boy I prayed, and Yahweh has granted me my petition that I asked of him.

[28] Therefore I have lent him to Yahweh; as long as he lives, he is lent to Yahweh." And he worshiped Yahweh there.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **Book name** — Hebrew *Shemuel*. English "1 Samuel" / "I Samuel." MT counts Samuel as one book; LXX and later Christian tradition divide into two.
- **"Lord of hosts" → "Yahweh of Hosts"** (v3, 11) — Hebrew יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת *YHWH Tzeva'ot*. First occurrence in the Bible. *Tzeva'ot* = "armies" — both celestial (angels, stars) and earthly (Israel).
- **"worthy portion" → "double portion"** (v5) — Hebrew מָנָה אַחַת אַפָּיִם *manah achat appayim*. Lit. "a portion of two faces" — a double / special portion.
- **"daughter of Belial" → "worthless woman"** (v16).
- **"three bullocks" → "three-year-old bull"** (v24) — MT reads *parim sheloshah* "three bulls"; DSS (4QSamᵃ) and LXX read *par meshullash* "bull of three years." DSS/LXX reading likely original — matches v25 where only one bull is slaughtered.
- **"bottle of wine" → "skin of wine"** (v24).

### Notes
- **Transition book** — 1 Samuel bridges Judges (no king) and Kings (monarchy). Samuel is the last judge, first post-Moses prophet, and kingmaker.
- **Hannah's prayer pattern** — barren woman story (like Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel). Prayer → response → child who becomes deliverer.
- **Shiloh sanctuary** (v3) — center of Israelite worship from Joshua through Samuel. Later destroyed (implied by Jer 7:12).
- **Nazirite dedication** (v11) — "no razor on his head" — Samuel is dedicated as a lifelong *nazir* like Samson.
- **Eli family** (v3) — Eli (high priest) and sons Hophni and Phinehas. The corrupt priestly family that forms the book's other major family.
- **"I have lent him to Yahweh"** (v28) — Hebrew *sha'ul* — a pun on "asked-for" (*sha'ul*) and the name of Saul. Samuel as the "asked-for" one is the first king-anointer; the first king is also named *Sha'ul* (Saul, "asked-for"). Wordplay throughout.
