# 1 Samuel 24

[1] When ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ returned from following the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧, it was told him, "Behold, David is in the wilderness of ⟦en-gedi|Ein Gedi|Engedi⟧."

[2] Then ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ took three thousand chosen men from all ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ and went to seek David and his men on the rocks of the wild goats.

[3] He came to the sheepfolds by the way, where there was a cave; and ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ went in to relieve himself. Now David and his men were sitting in the inner parts of the cave.

[6] He said to his men, "⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ forbid that I should do this thing to my lord, to the ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧ of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ — to put out my hand against him, since he is the ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧ of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"to cover his feet" → "to relieve himself"** (v3) — euphemism for defecation.
- **"skirt" → "corner"** (v4, 5, 11) — Hebrew *kanaf*. Hem / corner of the garment. Removing the corner is symbolically removing authority (cf. Ruth 3:9 "spread your wing").
- **"privily" → "stealthily"** (v4).
- **"stayed his servants" → "rebuked his men"** (v7).

### Notes
- **First sparing of Saul** — in the cave at En-gedi. David's men see opportunity as divine providence for kingship; David refuses to "strike the anointed."
- **"Anointed of Yahweh"** — David's theological commitment. Even a rejected king, still anointed, is untouchable by private violence.
- **Corner of robe cut** — symbolic rebuke. Compare 1 Sam 15:27–28 (Samuel's robe torn); the corner represents the kingdom/authority. David holds it; Saul sees the sign.
- **"Dead dog, a flea"** (v14) — David's self-deprecation. Saul's pursuit is disproportionate and absurd.
- **Saul's temporary remorse** (v16–21) — acknowledges David's righteousness and future kingship. Asks preservation of his descendants (fulfilled in David's treatment of Mephibosheth).
- **David keeps the stronghold** (v22) — does not trust Saul's contrition enough to rejoin court.
