# 1 Samuel 9

[1] There was a man of Benjamin whose name was Kish son of Abiel son of Zeror son of Becorath son of Aphiah, a Benjamite, a man of standing.

[2] He had a son whose name was Saul, a young man and handsome; there was not a man among the children of Israel more handsome than he. From his shoulders upward he was taller than any of the people.

[3] The donkeys of Kish, Saul's father, were lost. Kish said to Saul his son, "Take one of the servants with you and arise, go seek the donkeys."

[4] So he passed through the hill country of Ephraim and passed through the land of Shalishah, but they did not find them. Then they passed through the land of Shaalim, but they were not there. They passed through the land of the Benjamites, but they did not find them.

[5] When they came to the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant who was with him, "Come, let us return, lest my father stop worrying about the donkeys and begin to worry about us."

[6] But he said to him, "Behold, there is in this city a man of *Elohim*; he is a man held in honor. All that he says comes true. Let us go there; perhaps he can tell us the way we should go."

[7] Then Saul said to his servant, "But if we go, what shall we bring the man? For the bread in our sacks is gone, and there is no present to bring to the man of *Elohim*. What do we have?"

[8] The servant answered Saul again, "Behold, I have with me a quarter of a shekel of silver; I will give that to the man of *Elohim*, that he may tell us our way."

[9] (Formerly in Israel, when a man went to inquire of *Elohim*, he said, "Come, let us go to the seer"; for the one who is now called a prophet was formerly called a seer.)

[10] Saul said to his servant, "Well said; come, let us go." So they went to the city where the man of *Elohim* was.

[11] As they were going up the ascent to the city, they met some young women going out to draw water; and they said to them, "Is the seer here?"

[12] They answered, "He is; behold, he is just ahead of you. Hurry now, for he has come today to the city because there is a sacrifice for the people today on the high place.

[13] As soon as you enter the city, you will find him, before he goes up to the high place to eat; for the people will not eat until he comes, since he blesses the sacrifice, and afterward those who are invited eat. Now go up, for you will find him at about this time."

[14] So they went up to the city. As they were entering the city, behold, Samuel came out toward them, going up to the high place.

[15] Now Yahweh had revealed to Samuel a day before Saul came, saying,

[16] "Tomorrow about this time I will send to you a man from the land of Benjamin, and you shall anoint him to be prince — over my people Israel. He shall save my people from the hand of the Philistines; for I have seen my people, because their cry has come to me."

[17] When Samuel saw Saul, Yahweh said to him, "Here is the man of whom I spoke to you! This one shall rule over my people."

[18] Then Saul approached Samuel in the middle of the gate and said, "Please tell me where the house of the seer is."

[19] Samuel answered Saul, "I am the seer. Go up before me to the high place, for today you shall eat with me; in the morning I will let you go and tell you all that is on your heart.

[20] As for your donkeys that were lost three days ago, do not set your mind on them, for they have been found. And on whom is all the desire of Israel fixed? Is it not on you and on all your father's house?"

[21] Saul answered, "Am I not a Benjamite, from the smallest of the tribes of Israel? And my clan the least of all the clans of the tribe of Benjamin? Why then have you spoken to me in this way?"

[22] Then Samuel took Saul and his servant and brought them into the hall; he gave them a place at the head of those who had been invited, who were about thirty persons.

[23] Samuel said to the cook, "Bring the portion that I gave you, of which I said to you, 'Set it aside.'"

[24] So the cook took up the thigh and what was on it and set it before Saul. And Samuel said, "Behold what has been reserved! Set it before yourself and eat, for it has been kept for you until this appointed time — since I said, 'I have invited the people.'" So Saul ate with Samuel that day.

[25] When they came down from the high place into the city, they spread out a bed for Saul on the roof, and he lay down to sleep.

[26] They rose early, and as day was breaking, Samuel called to Saul on the roof, saying, "Get up, that I may send you on your way." So Saul got up, and the two of them — he and Samuel — went out into the street.

[27] As they were going down to the edge of the city, Samuel said to Saul, "Tell the servant to pass on before us" — and he passed on — "but you stand still a while, that I may proclaim to you the word of *Elohim*."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"a mighty man of power" → "a man of standing"** (v1) — *gibbor chayil*.
- **"goodly / goodlier" → "handsome"** (v2) — archaic.
- **"asses" → "donkeys"** (throughout).
- **"parlour" → "hall"** (v22) — archaic.
- **"communed" → "spread out a bed / spoke with"** (v25) — MT and LXX differ; LXX preserves: spreading out a bed on the roof for Saul. Followed here.

### Notes
- **Saul introduced** — handsome, tall, Benjamite, seeking lost donkeys. Unexpected beginning for a future king.
- **"Prophet" vs "seer"** (v9) — editorial note: terminology changed. Earlier: *ro'eh* (seer); later: *navi* (prophet). Shows the book's editorial date is well after Samuel's time.
- **Providential coincidence** — lost donkeys lead to Samuel. Prophetic foreknowledge meets personal quest. The private errand becomes a national turning-point.
- **Saul's humility** (v21) — appropriate self-deprecation. Benjamin had been nearly extinguished in Judg 20; Saul's clan smallest of the tribe.
- **Priestly portion given to Saul** (v24) — the thigh, priestly portion. Samuel transfers it to Saul: foreshadowing royal office.
- **High place** (*bamah*) — local altar-shrine. Pre-Solomonic, unproblematic; post-Temple, condemned.
