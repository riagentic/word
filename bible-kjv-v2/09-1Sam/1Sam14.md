# 1 Samuel 14

[1] One day Jonathan son of Saul said to the young man who carried his armor, "Come, let us cross over to the Philistine garrison that is on the other side." But he did not tell his father.

[2] Saul was staying in the outskirts of Gibeah, under the pomegranate tree that is at Migron; the people who were with him were about six hundred men.

[3] Ahijah son of Ahitub, Ichabod's brother, son of Phinehas son of Eli, the priest of Yahweh at Shiloh, was wearing an ephod. The people did not know that Jonathan had gone.

[4] Between the passes by which Jonathan sought to cross over to the Philistine garrison, there was a rocky crag on one side and a rocky crag on the other side; the name of the one was Bozez, and the name of the other Seneh.

[5] One crag rose on the north in front of Michmash, and the other on the south in front of Geba.

[6] Jonathan said to the young man who carried his armor, "Come, let us cross over to the garrison of these uncircumcised; perhaps Yahweh will act for us. For there is no restraint to Yahweh to save by many or by few."

[7] His armor-bearer said to him, "Do all that is in your heart; turn yourself — behold, I am with you, heart and soul."

[8] Then Jonathan said, "Behold, we will cross over to the men and reveal ourselves to them.

[9] If they say to us, 'Wait until we come to you,' then we will stand still in our place and not go up to them.

[10] But if they say, 'Come up to us,' then we will go up, for Yahweh has given them into our hand. This will be a sign to us."

[11] So both of them revealed themselves to the garrison of the Philistines, and the Philistines said, "Behold, the Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they have hidden themselves."

[12] The men of the garrison answered Jonathan and his armor-bearer, "Come up to us, and we will show you something!" Jonathan said to his armor-bearer, "Come up after me, for Yahweh has given them into the hand of Israel."

[13] Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, with his armor-bearer after him. They fell before Jonathan, and his armor-bearer killed them after him.

[14] That first slaughter that Jonathan and his armor-bearer made was about twenty men within about half a furrow's length — an acre's plowing.

[15] There was terror in the camp, in the field, and among all the people; even the garrison and the raiders trembled; the earth also quaked, and it became a terror of *Elohim*.

[16] Saul's watchmen at Gibeah of Benjamin looked; and behold, the multitude was melting away, going here and there.

[17] Then Saul said to the people who were with him, "Muster now, and see who has gone from us." When they had mustered, behold, Jonathan and his armor-bearer were not there.

[18] Saul said to Ahijah, "Bring the ephod here." (For at that time the ephod was carried with the children of Israel.)

[19] While Saul was speaking to the priest, the tumult in the camp of the Philistines continued to increase; Saul said to the priest, "Withdraw your hand."

[20] Saul and all the people who were with him rallied and went into the battle; behold, every man's sword was against his fellow, and there was very great confusion.

[21] Even the Hebrews who had been with the Philistines before, who had gone up with them into the camp from around about, turned to be with the Israelites who were with Saul and Jonathan.

[22] Likewise, all the men of Israel who had hidden themselves in the hill country of Ephraim, when they heard that the Philistines had fled, they too followed hard after them in battle.

[23] So Yahweh saved Israel that day; the battle passed on beyond Beth-aven.

[24] The men of Israel were hard-pressed that day, for Saul had made the people swear, saying, "Cursed be the man who eats any food until evening, until I am avenged on my enemies." So none of the people tasted food.

[25] All the land came to a forest; there was honey on the ground.

[26] When the people came to the forest, behold, honey was dripping; but no one put his hand to his mouth, for the people feared the oath.

[27] But Jonathan had not heard when his father had charged the people with the oath; so he extended the tip of the staff that was in his hand and dipped it in the honeycomb; he put his hand to his mouth, and his eyes brightened.

[28] Then one of the people said, "Your father strictly charged the people with an oath, saying, 'Cursed be the man who eats food today.'" And the people were faint.

[29] Jonathan said, "My father has troubled the land. See how my eyes have brightened, because I tasted a little of this honey.

[30] How much better if the people had today eaten freely of the spoil of their enemies which they found? For now the slaughter among the Philistines has not been great."

[31] They struck down the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aijalon, and the people were very faint.

[32] The people rushed on the spoil, taking sheep, oxen, and calves, and slaughtering them on the ground; and the people ate them with the blood.

[33] Then they told Saul, saying, "Behold, the people are sinning against Yahweh by eating with the blood." He said, "You have acted treacherously; roll a great stone to me here now."

[34] Saul said, "Disperse yourselves among the people and say to them, 'Let every man bring his ox and every man his sheep here to me, and slaughter them here and eat; do not sin against Yahweh by eating with the blood.'" So every one of the people brought his ox with him that night and slaughtered them there.

[35] Saul built an altar to Yahweh; this was the first altar that he built to Yahweh.

[36] Saul said, "Let us go down after the Philistines by night and plunder them until morning light; let us not leave a man of them." They said, "Do whatever seems good in your eyes." But the priest said, "Let us draw near here to *Elohim*."

[37] Saul inquired of *Elohim*: "Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you give them into the hand of Israel?" But he did not answer him that day.

[38] Saul said, "Come here, all you chiefs of the people; know and see wherein this sin has been today.

[39] For as Yahweh lives, who saves Israel — though it be in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die." But not a man of all the people answered him.

[40] Then he said to all Israel, "You be on one side, and I and Jonathan my son on the other side." And the people said to Saul, "Do what is good in your eyes."

[41] Therefore Saul said to Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel, "Give a perfect lot." And Jonathan and Saul were taken by lot, but the people escaped.

[42] Saul said, "Cast the lot between me and Jonathan my son." And Jonathan was taken.

[43] Then Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what you have done." Jonathan told him and said, "I tasted a little honey with the end of the staff that was in my hand. Here I am; I must die."

[44] Saul said, "May *Elohim* do so and more also; for you shall surely die, Jonathan."

[45] But the people said to Saul, "Shall Jonathan die, who has worked this great deliverance in Israel? Far be it from us! As Yahweh lives, not one hair of his head shall fall to the ground; for he has wrought with *Elohim* this day." So the people rescued Jonathan, and he did not die.

[46] Then Saul went up from pursuing the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their own place.

[47] When Saul had taken the kingship over Israel, he fought against all his enemies on every side — against Moab, the children of Ammon, Edom, the kings of Zobah, and the Philistines; wherever he turned, he inflicted punishment.

[48] He acted valiantly and struck down the Amalekites, and delivered Israel out of the hands of those who plundered them.

[49] Now the sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishvi, and Malchishua; the names of his two daughters were these: the name of the firstborn was Merab, and the name of the younger, Michal.

[50] The name of Saul's wife was Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz. The name of the commander of his army was Abner son of Ner, Saul's uncle.

[51] Kish, the father of Saul, and Ner, the father of Abner, were sons of Abiel.

[52] There was severe war against the Philistines all the days of Saul; and when Saul saw any strong man, or any valiant man, he attached him to himself.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"ark of God" → "ephod"** (v18) — MT reads *aron Elohim* ("ark of *Elohim*"), but LXX has "ephod." The LXX reading fits better: the ark was at Kiriath-jearim, not with Saul. Ephod is the priestly oracle-consultation device. LXX followed here.
- **"discovered themselves" → "revealed themselves"** (v8, 11).
- **"discomfiture" → "confusion"** (v20).
- **"adjured" → "made swear"** (v24).
- **"straitly charged" → "strictly charged"** (v28).
- **"haply" → "" (removed, idiomatic modernization)** (v30).

### Notes
- **Jonathan's faith adventure** (v6) — "there is no restraint to Yahweh to save by many or by few." Archetypal faith-statement. Compare Gideon's 300.
- **Bozez and Seneh** (v4–5) — the two crags. "Shining" and "Thorny." Archaeological identification disputed but real geography.
- **Earthquake** (v15) — a natural event coincident with Philistine panic. Yahweh uses cosmic and psychological forces.
- **Deserters and hiders return** (v21–22) — Hebrews who had been with the Philistines switch sides (v21); the men hiding in the hill country of Ephraim join the pursuit (v22).
- **Saul's foolish oath** (v24) — hasty vow creates crisis. Exhausted army cannot eat; Jonathan, ignorant of the oath, tastes honey and is strengthened.
- **Blood-eating** (v32–34) — famished troops slaughter and eat without draining blood (Lev 17). Saul improvises with a slaughtering stone.
- **Jonathan's near-execution** — Saul's oath would require his son's death; the people refuse. First open rift between Saul's will and his people's.
- **Royal summary** (v47–52) — Saul's successes listed. Not a complete failure; but tragic trajectory is set.
