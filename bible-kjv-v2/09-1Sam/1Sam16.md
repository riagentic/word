## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him" → "How long are you mourning for Saul, and I have rejected him"** (v1) — Hebrew מִתְאַבֵּל, a participle ("are mourning"), and וַאֲנִי "and I", not "seeing / since".
- **"I have provided me a king" → "I have seen … a king for myself"** (v1) — Hebrew רָאִיתִי … לִי, the verb רָאָה "see": to see for oneself is to look out, choose (Gen 22:8). The same verb stands in v7 (three times), v17 ("See for me a man") and v18 ("I have seen a son of Jesse").
- **"If Saul hear it, he will kill me" → "And Saul will hear, and he will kill me"** (v2) — the Hebrew has two plain clauses with "and", no "if" and no "it".
- **"Take an heifer with thee" → "A heifer of the herd you shall take in your hand"** (v2) — Hebrew עֶגְלַת בָּקָר … בְּיָדֶךָ.
- **"I will shew thee" → "I myself will make known to you"** (v3) — Hebrew וְאָנֹכִי אוֹדִיעֲךָ, with the pronoun expressed. **"whom I name unto thee" → "whom I say to you"** — Hebrew אָמַר "say".
- **"call Jesse to the sacrifice" kept** (v3, 5) — Hebrew קָרָא "call"; "invite" is the sense. In v3 the Hebrew has בַּזָּבַח, literally "in / at the sacrifice".
- **"town" → "city"; "trembled at his coming" → "trembled to meet him"** (v4) — Hebrew עִיר; וַיֶּחֶרְדוּ … לִקְרָאתוֹ.
- **"and said, Comest thou peaceably?" → "and he said, 'Is your coming *shalom*?'"** (v4, 5) — Hebrew שָׁלֹם בּוֹאֶךָ, literally "peace your coming?", a noun, with no question word; the answer is the one word שָׁלוֹם. The verb "said" is singular in the Masoretic Text ("he said"), though the elders are many; the old versions have the plural (from memory).
- **"sanctify" kept** (v5) — Hebrew הִתְקַדְּשׁוּ, וַיְקַדֵּשׁ; one root.
- **"he looked on Eliab" → "he saw Eliab"** (v6) — Hebrew רָאָה.
- **"Surely the LORD's anointed is before him" → "Surely before Yahweh is his anointed"** (v6) — Hebrew אַךְ נֶגֶד יְהוָה מְשִׁיחוֹ, in the Hebrew order; מָשִׁיחַ *mashiach* "anointed one".
- **"Look not on his countenance" → "Do not look at his appearance"** (v7) — Hebrew הִבִּיט "look at" and מַרְאֶה "appearance".
- **"for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart" → "For not what man sees — for man looks to the eyes, and Yahweh looks to the heart"** (v7) — Hebrew כִּי לֹא אֲשֶׁר יִרְאֶה הָאָדָם כִּי הָאָדָם יִרְאֶה לַעֵינַיִם וַיהוָה יִרְאֶה לַלֵּבָב, word for word. The first clause is unfinished in the Masoretic Text: "the LORD seeth" is not there (the Septuagint has "not as man sees does God see": from memory). The verb is רָאָה "see" all three times, another verb than "look at" at the head of the verse; with לְ it is "look to". לַעֵינַיִם is "to the eyes": "outward appearance" is an explanation. It may be the eyes of the one seen (what shows) or the eyes of the one who sees ("according to the eyes"); the Hebrew does not say. "The man" has the article (mankind).
- **"seven of his sons" → "his seven sons"** (v10) — Hebrew שִׁבְעַת בָּנָיו.
- **"Are here all thy children?" → "Have the boys come to an end?"** (v11) — Hebrew הֲתַמּוּ הַנְּעָרִים: תַּם "come to an end", נַעַר "boy / young man" (the word of v18, "young men").
- **"he keepeth the sheep" → "he is shepherding the flock"** (v11, 19) — Hebrew רֹעֶה בַּצֹּאן; צֹאן is sheep and goats together. **"the youngest"** — literally "the small one".
- **"fetch him" → "take him"; "we will not sit down" → "we will not gather around"** (v11) — Hebrew לֹא־נָסֹב, from סָבַב "go around, surround". It is commonly taken of sitting around the meal; the word itself says only "go around", and the sense here is not certain.
- **"of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to" → "with beautiful eyes, and good in appearance"** (v12) — Hebrew עִם־יְפֵה עֵינַיִם וְטוֹב רֹאִי, literally "with beautiful of eyes and good of sight". The grammar of "with" before the adjective is difficult. **"ruddy"** — Hebrew אַדְמוֹנִי "reddish" (of Esau, Gen 25:25): of skin or of hair is not said.
- **"came upon David" → "rushed upon David"** (v13) — Hebrew וַתִּצְלַח … אֶל, the verb of Judg 14:6 and 1 Sam 10:10; here with אֶל "to".
- **"But the Spirit of the LORD departed … troubled him" → "And the spirit of Yahweh departed … terrified him"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew בִּעֵת "terrify, fall upon with terror". In v14 the subject stands first and the verb is a perfect: also "had departed". The second verb, וּבִעֲתַתּוּ, has the form of a repeated act ("and would terrify him").
- **"an evil spirit from the LORD" / "an evil spirit from God" → "an evil spirit from Yahweh" / "an evil spirit of Elohim" / "the spirit of Elohim"** (v14, 15, 16, 23) — the Hebrew varies and is kept as it varies: v14 רוּחַ־רָעָה מֵאֵת יְהוָה "an evil spirit from Yahweh"; v15, 16 רוּחַ־אֱלֹהִים רָעָה "a spirit of Elohim, an evil one"; v23 first רוּחַ־אֱלֹהִים "a spirit of Elohim", without "evil", and then רוּחַ הָרָעָה "the evil spirit" (literally "the spirit of evil"). רוּחַ is "spirit", "wind" or "breath"; רָעָה is "evil / bad", of harm as well as of moral evil.
- **"Let our lord now command thy servants, which are before thee, to seek out" → "Let our lord please say; your servants are before you, let them seek"** (v16) — Hebrew יֹאמַר־נָא אֲדֹנֵנוּ עֲבָדֶיךָ לְפָנֶיךָ יְבַקְשׁוּ: "say", not "command"; the words are set side by side without a link. **"servants"** (v15–17) — Hebrew עֶבֶד "slave", of a king's men.
- **"a cunning player on an harp" → "who knows how to play on the lyre"** (v16, 18) — Hebrew יֹדֵעַ מְנַגֵּן בַּכִּנּוֹר, literally "knowing, playing on the lyre"; *kinnor* is a lyre, not the modern harp.
- **"and thou shalt be well" → "and it will be good for you"** (v16, 23) — Hebrew וְטוֹב לָךְ.
- **"Provide me now a man that can play well" → "See for me, please, a man who plays well"** (v17) — Hebrew רְאוּ־נָא לִי, the verb of v1.
- **"a mighty valiant man" → "a mighty one of ability"** (v18) — Hebrew גִּבּוֹר חַיִל: חַיִל is ability, force, wealth. **"prudent in matters" → "understanding in word"** — Hebrew נְבוֹן דָּבָר: דָּבָר is "word" or "matter". **"a comely person" → "a man of form"** — Hebrew אִישׁ תֹּאַר.
- **"an ass laden with bread" → "a donkey with bread"** (v20) — Hebrew חֲמוֹר לֶחֶם, literally "a donkey of bread"; "laden" is not in the Hebrew. The expression is difficult (the Septuagint has a measure, "a gomor of bread": from memory). **"a bottle" → "a skin"**; **"a kid" → "one kid of the goats"** — Hebrew גְּדִי־עִזִּים אֶחָד.
- **"and he loved him greatly"** (v21) — the Hebrew names no subject; the first use of אָהֵב "love" with David as its object (18:1, 3, 16, 20, 22, 28; 20:17).
- **"armourbearer" → "weapon-bearer"** (v21) — Hebrew נֹשֵׂא כֵלִים, literally "bearer of gear": כְּלִי is any made article, here weapons.
- **"Let David … stand before me" kept** (v22) — Hebrew יַעֲמָד־נָא … לְפָנַי, the "stood before him" of v21: to stand before a king is to serve him.
- **"when the evil spirit from God was upon Saul" → "when the spirit of Elohim was upon Saul"** (v23) — the Masoretic Text has no "evil" here; the preposition is אֶל "to". **"so Saul was refreshed" → "Saul would have relief"** — Hebrew וְרָוַח לְשָׁאוּל, literally "and it was wide for Saul"; a play of sound with רוּחַ "spirit". The verbs of the verse have the form of repeated acts.

### Notes
- **David's anointing** — private and hidden. Compared with Saul's public anointing, David's is familial, unexpected, with divine criterion ("Yahweh looks to the heart").
- **"Yahweh looks to the heart"** (v7) — famous verse. Divine criterion of choice opposed to human standards.
- **Seven rejected brothers** — eight total sons of Jesse (17:12). David (youngest, eighth) is the chosen one. Compare the younger-brother motif throughout Genesis (Jacob over Esau, Joseph over his brothers).
- **"Evil spirit from Yahweh"** (v14) — theologically challenging. Ancient Near Eastern worldview: no dualism — all spirits under Yahweh's sovereignty. Whether the spirit is personified or a depression-like affliction remains debated.
- **David's initial entry to Saul's court** — as musician and weapon-bearer. Set-up for the Goliath narrative (ch 17) which complicates this narrative (two traditions of David's introduction).
- **Music as spiritual therapy** (v23) — the first biblical therapeutic music. David's psalm-tradition begins here.
