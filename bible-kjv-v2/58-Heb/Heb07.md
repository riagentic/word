# Hebrews 7

[1] For this ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧, king of ⟦salem|Shalem|Salem⟧, priest of the Most High ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, met ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,

[2] and to him ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ apportioned a tenth part of everything. He is first, by translation of his name, king of righteousness, and then he is also king of ⟦salem|Shalem|Salem⟧, that is, king of peace.

[3] He is without father or mother or genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but resembling the Son of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ he continues a priest forever.

[4] See how great this man was to whom ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ the patriarch gave a tenth of the spoils!

[5] And those descendants of Levi who receive the priestly office have a commandment in the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ to take tithes from the people, that is, from their brothers, though these also are descended from ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧.

[6] But this man who does not have his descent from them received tithes from ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ and blessed him who had the promises.

[7] It is beyond dispute that the inferior is blessed by the superior.

[8] In the one case tithes are received by mortal men, but in the other case, by one of whom it is testified that he lives.

[9] One might even say that Levi himself, who receives tithes, paid tithes through ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧,

[10] for he was still in the loins of his ancestor when ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧ met him.

[11] Now if perfection had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood (for under it the people received the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧), what further need would there have been for another priest to arise after the order of ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧, rather than one named after the order of ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧?

[12] For when there is a change in the priesthood, there is necessarily a change in the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ as well.

[13] For the one of whom these things are spoken belonged to another tribe, from which no one has ever served at the altar.

[14] For it is evident that our Lord was descended from ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, and in connection with that tribe ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said nothing about priests.

[15] This becomes even more evident when another priest arises in the likeness of ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧,

[16] who has become a priest, not on the basis of a legal requirement concerning bodily descent, but by the power of an indestructible life.

[17] For it is witnessed of him,

"**You are a priest forever, after the order of ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧**."

[18] On the one hand, a former commandment is set aside because of its weakness and uselessness

[19] (for the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ made nothing perfect); but on the other hand, a better hope is introduced, through which we draw near to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[20] And it was not without an oath. For those who formerly became priests were made such without an oath,

[21] but this one was made a priest with an oath by the one who said to him:

"**⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The Lord⟧ has sworn and will not change his mind,
'You are a priest forever.'**"

[22] This makes ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ the guarantor of a better covenant.

[23] The former priests were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office,

[24] but he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever.

[25] Consequently, **he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them**.

[26] For it was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens.

[27] He has no need, like those high priests, to offer sacrifices daily, first for his own sins and then for those of the people, since he did this once for all when he offered up himself.

[28] For the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ appoints men in their weakness as high priests, but the word of the oath, which came later than the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧, appoints a Son who has been made perfect forever.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v22) — systematic.
- **"the most high God" → "the Most High *Elohim*"** (v1) — Greek τοῦ Θεοῦ τοῦ ὑψίστου *tou Theou tou hypsistou* = Hebrew אֵל עֶלְיוֹן *El Elyon*; "God" → "*Elohim*."
- **"without father, without mother, without descent" → "without father or mother or genealogy"** (v3) — Greek ἀπάτωρ ἀμήτωρ ἀγενεαλόγητος *apatōr amētōr agenealogētos*; "without descent" is correct but "genealogy" (the specific word used) is more precise — no priestly genealogy is recorded.
- **"a commandment to take tithes of the people according to the law" → "a commandment in the Torah to take tithes from the people"** (v5) — "law" → "Torah" in the context of Mosaic law/legislation; Greek κατὰ τὸν νόμον *kata ton nomon* = "according to the Torah."
- **"Now consider how great this man was" → "See how great this man was"** (v4) — minor modernization.
- **"he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him" → "he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to *Elohim* through him"** (v25) — "to the uttermost" = "completely / forever" (Greek εἰς τὸ παντελές *eis to panteles*); "come unto God" → "draw near to *Elohim*."
- **"The law maketh men high priests which have infirmity" → "the Torah appoints men in their weakness as high priests"** (v28) — "law" → "Torah" (Mosaic context); "infirmity" → "weakness."

### Notes
- **v1-2** echoes Gen 14:17-20.
- **v17, 21** quotes Ps 110:4.
