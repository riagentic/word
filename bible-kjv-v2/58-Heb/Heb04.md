# Hebrews 4

[1] Let us fear, then, lest, a promise being left of entering into his rest, any one of you should seem to have come short;

[2] for indeed we have had good news brought to us, just as they also; but the word of hearing did not profit them, not having been mixed together by faith with those who heard.

[3] For we who believed enter into the rest, as he has said,

"**As I swore in my wrath,
'They shall not enter into my rest,'**"

although the works had come to be from the foundation of the world.

[4] For he has said somewhere about the seventh thus: "**And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ rested on the seventh day from all his works**,"

[5] and in this again,

"**They shall not enter into my rest**."

[6] Since, then, it remains for some to enter into it, and those who formerly had good news brought to them did not enter because of disobedience,

[7] again he marks out a certain day, "Today," saying in David after so long a time, as it has been said before,

"**Today, if you hear his voice,
do not harden your hearts**."

[8] For if ⟦joshua|Yehoshua|Joshua⟧ had given them rest, he would not after these things be speaking about another day.

[9] So then there remains a keeping of ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ for the people of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;

[10] for he who entered into his rest also himself rested from his works, as ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ did from his own.

[11] Let us be diligent, then, to enter into that rest, that no one fall in the same example of disobedience.

[12] **For living is the word of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and effective, and sharper than every two-edged sword, and piercing as far as the division of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow, and able to judge the reflections and intentions of the heart;**

[13] and there is no creature not manifest before him, but all things are naked and laid bare to the eyes of him to whom is our account.

[14] Having, then, a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ the Son of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, let us hold the confession.

[15] **For we do not have a high priest who is not able to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who has been tempted in all things according to likeness, apart from sin.**

[16] Let us approach, then, with boldness the throne of grace, that we may receive mercy and find grace for timely help.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v4, 9, 10, 12, 14) — systematic. In v8 and at the start of v10 the Greek has no "God" ("he would not", "his rest").
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v14) — systematic.
- **"lest, a promise being left us … any of you should seem to come short of it" → "lest, a promise being left … any one of you should seem to have come short"** (v1) — Greek καταλειπομένης ἐπαγγελίας, without "us"; ὑστερηκέναι, a perfect, without "of it". δοκῇ is "seem" or "think".
- **"unto us was the gospel preached, as well as unto them" → "we have had good news brought to us, just as they also"** (v2) — Greek ἐσμεν εὐηγγελισμένοι, the verb "bring good news" in the passive; so also v6.
- **"the word preached" → "the word of hearing"** (v2) — Greek ὁ λόγος τῆς ἀκοῆς, "the word of the hearing", the word that was heard; ἀκοή is of the root of "those who heard".
- **"not being mixed with faith in them that heard it" → "not having been mixed together by faith with those who heard"** (v2) — the printed text reads συγκεκερασμένους, which agrees with "them": they were not joined with those who heard. τῇ πίστει is "by faith" or "in the faith". So SBLGNT, WH, Treg and NA28; the KJV follows the reading in the singular (συγκεκερασμένος / συγκεκραμένος), which agrees with "the word".
- **"we which have believed do enter into rest" → "we who believed enter into the rest"** (v3) — Greek οἱ πιστεύσαντες, an aorist. The verse follows εἰς τὴν κατάπαυσιν, "the rest" (WH, Treg, NA28); the SBLGNT (Holmes, with the WH margin) has no article, "into rest".
- **"if they shall enter into my rest" → "They shall not enter into my rest"** (v3, 5) — Greek εἰ εἰσελεύσονται εἰς τὴν κατάπαυσίν μου, literally "if they shall enter into my rest": the Hebrew oath formula of Ps 95:11, "if" with the consequence left unsaid. It is a sworn "they shall not enter" (so the author himself, 3:18–19), and the verse gives that meaning, because a literal "if" would tell the reader the opposite of what is said; see 3:11.
- **"although the works were finished" → "although the works had come to be"** (v3) — Greek τῶν ἔργων … γενηθέντων, "having come to be, having been done"; the Greek has no "his" and no "finished".
- **"of the seventh day" → "about the seventh"** (v4) — Greek περὶ τῆς ἑβδόμης, without "day"; the word "day" comes in the quotation.
- **"in this place again" → "in this again"** (v5) — Greek ἐν τούτῳ πάλιν; no "place" and no "he said" in the Greek.
- **"they to whom it was first preached entered not in because of unbelief" → "those who formerly had good news brought to them did not enter because of disobedience"** (v6) — Greek διʼ ἀπείθειαν, "refusal to be persuaded, disobedience" (also v11; the verb in 3:18); "unbelief" is ἀπιστία (3:12, 19).
- **"he limiteth a certain day" → "he marks out a certain day"** (v7) — Greek ὁρίζει, "set a boundary, determine". **"saying in David" kept** — Greek ἐν Δαυίδ, as "in the prophets" (1:1). **"as it is said" → "as it has been said before"** — the printed text reads προείρηται.
- **"For if Jesus had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day" → "For if *Yehoshua* had given them rest, he would not after these things be speaking about another day"** (v8) — the Greek has Ἰησοῦς, the same name as in v14: in Greek "Joshua" (Hebrew *Yehoshua*) and "*Yeshua*" are one name. Here it is the son of Nun, who brought Israel into the land. ἐλάλει is an imperfect; the Greek does not name the speaker.
- **"There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God" → "So then there remains a keeping of Shabbat for the people of *Elohim*"** (v9) — Greek σαββατισμός, "a keeping of Sabbath", a different word from "rest" (κατάπαυσις) in the rest of the chapter; the verse does not add "rest".
- **"he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works" → "he who entered into his rest also himself rested from his works"** (v10) — Greek κατέπαυσεν, the verb of v4 and of "rest". "His rest" is *Elohim*'s rest (v1); the Greek has "his", and "his own" (ἰδίων) only of *Elohim*'s works.
- **"Let us labour therefore" → "Let us be diligent, then"** (v11) — Greek σπουδάσωμεν.
- **"after the same example of unbelief" → "in the same example of disobedience"** (v11) — Greek ἐν τῷ αὐτῷ … ὑποδείγματι τῆς ἀπειθείας; ὑπόδειγμα is the word rendered "copy" in 8:5; 9:23.
- **"quick, and powerful" → "living … and effective"** (v12) — Greek ζῶν, which stands first in the sentence; ἐνεργής, "at work, effective" (as Phlm 6).
- **"piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow" → "piercing as far as the division of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow"** (v12) — Greek ἄχρι μερισμοῦ (the word of 2:4); ἁρμῶν τε καὶ μυελῶν, both plural.
- **"a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart" → "able to judge the reflections and intentions of the heart"** (v12) — Greek κριτικός, "fit to judge" (the root of "judgment"); ἐνθυμήσεων καὶ ἐννοιῶν.
- **"Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight" → "and there is no creature not manifest before him"** (v13) — Greek ἀφανής, "unseen"; ἐνώπιον αὐτοῦ. "Him" / "his" can be *Elohim* or the word.
- **"opened" → "laid bare"** (v13) — Greek τετραχηλισμένα, from τράχηλος "neck": with the neck bent back and exposed.
- **"with whom we have to do" → "to whom is our account"** (v13) — Greek πρὸς ὃν ἡμῖν ὁ λόγος, literally "toward whom is the word for us". λόγος is the "word" of v12 (and v2); here it has the sense "account, reckoning" (as 13:17), so the fixed rendering "word" is not used; the phrase can also mean "with whom our matter is".
- **"that is passed into the heavens" → "who has passed through the heavens"** (v14) — Greek διεληλυθότα τοὺς οὐρανούς.
- **"let us hold fast our profession" → "let us hold the confession"** (v14) — Greek κρατῶμεν τῆς ὁμολογίας; a different verb from "hold fast" (κατέχω, 3:6, 14); the Greek has no "our".
- **"which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities" → "who is not able to sympathize with our weaknesses"** (v15) — Greek συμπαθῆσαι, "suffer with, feel with"; ἀσθενείαις.
- **"but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" → "but one who has been tempted in all things according to likeness, apart from sin"** (v15) — Greek πεπειρασμένον, a perfect; καθʼ ὁμοιότητα, "according to likeness": the Greek does not say whose (his likeness to us, 2:17, or "in like manner as we"). χωρὶς ἁμαρτίας, "apart from sin, without sin"; the Greek has no "yet".
- **"Let us therefore come boldly" → "Let us approach, then, with boldness"** (v16) — Greek προσερχώμεθα … μετὰ παρρησίας, as 3:6.
- **"that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need" → "that we may receive mercy and find grace for timely help"** (v16) — Greek λάβωμεν; εἰς εὔκαιρον βοήθειαν, "for help at the right time"; the Greek has no "need".

### Notes
- **v3, 5** quotes Ps 95:11.
- **v4** quotes Gen 2:2.
- **v7** quotes Ps 95:7-8.
- **v8** — "Joshua" and "*Yeshua*" are the same name in Greek.
- **v12–13** — one sentence in Greek.
