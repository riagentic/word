# Hebrews 1

[1] In many parts and in many ways long ago ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, having spoken to the fathers in the prophets,

[2] at the last of these days spoke to us in a Son, whom he appointed heir of all things, through whom also he made the ages;

[3] who, being the radiance of the glory and the imprint of his substance, and bearing all things by the word of his power, having made purification of sins, sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high,

[4] having become so much better than the angels as he has inherited a name more excellent than they.

[5] For to which of the angels did he ever say,

"**You are my Son,
I today have begotten you**"?

And again,

"**I will be to him for a father,
and he will be to me for a son**"?

[6] But when again he brings the firstborn into the inhabited earth, he says,

"**And let all angels of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ bow down to him**."

[7] And as to the angels he says,

"**He who makes his angels spirits,
and his ministers a flame of fire**."

[8] But as to the Son,

"**Your throne, O ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, is unto the age of the age,
and the rod of uprightness is the rod of your kingdom.
[9] You loved righteousness and hated lawlessness;
because of this ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, anointed you
with oil of exultation beyond your companions**."

[10] And,

"**You, Lord, at the beginning founded the earth,
and the heavens are works of your hands;
[11] they will perish, but you remain;
and all will grow old like a garment,
[12] and like a cloak you will roll them up,
like a garment, and they will be changed;
but you are the same,
and your years will not fail**."

[13] But to which of the angels has he ever said,

"**Sit at my right hand
until I make your enemies a footstool of your feet**"?

[14] Are they not all ministering spirits, sent out for service on account of those who are about to inherit salvation?

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 6, 8, 9) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*. In v3 and v5 the Greek has no "God" ("the glory", "did he ever say"), so the verse has none.
- **"at sundry times and in divers manners … in time past unto the fathers by the prophets" → "In many parts and in many ways long ago … to the fathers in the prophets"** (v1) — Greek πολυμερῶς ("in many parts, portions"), πολυτρόπως ("in many ways"), πάλαι; ἐν τοῖς προφήταις is "in the prophets". The Greek has "the fathers", not "our fathers". "Having spoken" is a participle (λαλήσας); the main verb comes in v2.
- **"in these last days spoken unto us by his Son" → "at the last of these days spoke to us in a Son"** (v2) — Greek ἐπʼ ἐσχάτου τῶν ἡμερῶν τούτων, "at the end of these days"; ἐν υἱῷ has no article and no "his": "in a Son", in one who is Son.
- **"made the worlds" → "made the ages"** (v2) — Greek ἐποίησεν τοὺς αἰῶνας; αἰών is "age" (so also v8); "created" would be a different verb.
- **"the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person" → "the radiance of the glory and the imprint of his substance"** (v3) — Greek ἀπαύγασμα τῆς δόξης καὶ χαρακτὴρ τῆς ὑποστάσεως αὐτοῦ. ἀπαύγασμα is light shining out (or light reflected); χαρακτήρ is the mark a stamp or seal leaves (the Greek has no "exact"); ὑπόστασις is "what stands under", the real being — the same word in 3:14 and 11:1 ("assurance"). The one "his" (αὐτοῦ) stands at the end and can cover both "glory" and "substance".
- **"upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins" → "bearing all things by the word of his power, having made purification of sins"** (v3) — the verse follows αὐτοῦ (WH, Treg, NA28): τῷ ῥήματι τῆς δυνάμεως αὐτοῦ, "by the word of his power". The SBLGNT (Holmes) alone reads διʼ αὑτοῦ, "by the word of the power, having through himself made purification"; the Byzantine text has both (αὐτοῦ διʼ ἑαυτοῦ), whence the KJV's "by himself". "Our" (ἡμῶν) is only in the Byzantine text. "Word" here is ῥῆμα (what is spoken), not λόγος. φέρων is "bearing, carrying"; τὰ πάντα "all things".
- **"Being made so much better than the angels" → "having become so much better than the angels"** (v4) — Greek κρείττων γενόμενος; κρείττων is "better" throughout the letter. "Has inherited" is κεκληρονόμηκεν, the verb of "heir" (v2) and of v14.
- **"today have I begotten thee" → "I today have begotten you"** (v5) — Greek ἐγὼ σήμερον γεγέννηκά σε, with the "I" stated. **"I will be to him a Father" → "for a father … for a son"** — Greek εἰς πατέρα, εἰς υἱόν.
- **"And again, when he bringeth in the firstbegotten into the world" → "But when again he brings the firstborn into the inhabited earth"** (v6) — Greek ὅταν δὲ πάλιν εἰσαγάγῃ: "again" can introduce one more quotation (as in v5) or go with "brings in" (a second bringing in); the word order of the Greek is kept. οἰκουμένη is the inhabited earth (also 2:5), a different word from "world" (κόσμος).
- **"let all the angels of God worship him" → "let all angels of *Elohim* bow down to him"** (v6) — Greek προσκυνησάτωσαν, "bow down, do homage"; "worship" is kept for λατρεύω. The quotation begins with "And" (καί).
- **"of the angels" / "unto the Son" → "as to the angels" / "as to the Son"** (v7, 8) — Greek πρὸς μὲν τοὺς ἀγγέλους … πρὸς δὲ τὸν υἱόν, the same preposition twice: "with regard to" or "to". v8 has no second "he says".
- **"Who maketh his angels spirits" kept as "He who makes his angels spirits"** (v7) — Greek ὁ ποιῶν … πνεύματα. πνεῦμα is "spirit" or "wind"; in the Psalm (Ps 104:4) it is "winds". "Spirits" is kept because v14 takes up both words of the quotation: "ministering spirits".
- **"Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever" → "Your throne, O *Elohim*, is unto the age of the age"** (v8) — Greek ὁ θρόνος σου ὁ θεὸς εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα τοῦ αἰῶνος. ὁ θεός is a nominative: an address ("O *Elohim*"), as rendered, or the predicate ("*Elohim* is your throne"). The Greek has no verb.
- **"a sceptre of righteousness is the sceptre of thy kingdom" → "and the rod of uprightness is the rod of your kingdom"** (v8) — Greek ῥάβδος, "rod, staff" (9:4; 11:21); εὐθύτης is "straightness". The verse has "and" (WH, Treg, NA28) and "your kingdom" (σου: SBLGNT, Treg, NA28); WH reads "his kingdom" (αὐτοῦ).
- **"hated iniquity" → "hated lawlessness"** (v9) — Greek ἀνομίαν. The verbs are past tenses (ἠγάπησας, ἐμίσησας, ἔχρισεν).
- **"God, even thy God" → "*Elohim*, your *Elohim*"** (v9) — Greek ὁ θεός, ὁ θεός σου; the first can also be an address, "O *Elohim*, your *Elohim* anointed you", as in v8.
- **"the oil of gladness above thy fellows" → "oil of exultation beyond your companions"** (v9) — Greek ἔλαιον ἀγαλλιάσεως (as Jude 24); μετόχους is the word rendered "partakers" in 3:1, 14.
- **"in the beginning hast laid the foundation of the earth" → "at the beginning founded the earth"** (v10) — Greek κατʼ ἀρχάς … ἐθεμελίωσας, one verb. "Works" is plural (ἔργα).
- **"they all shall wax old" → "all will grow old"** (v11) — Greek παλαιωθήσονται, the verb of 8:13.
- **"as a vesture shalt thou fold them up, and they shall be changed" → "like a cloak you will roll them up, like a garment, and they will be changed"** (v12) — Greek περιβόλαιον, a wrap thrown around; ἑλίξεις "roll up". The printed text has the second "like a garment" (ὡς ἱμάτιον), which the KJV's text lacks.
- **"thy years shall not fail" kept** (v12) — Greek οὐκ ἐκλείψουσιν, "will not give out".
- **"Sit on my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool" → "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool of your feet"** (v13) — Greek ὑποπόδιον τῶν ποδῶν σου.
- **"sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation" → "sent out for service on account of those who are about to inherit salvation"** (v14) — Greek εἰς διακονίαν ("service"), a different root from "ministering" (λειτουργικά, as "ministers" in v7); διὰ τοὺς μέλλοντας κληρονομεῖν.

### Notes
- **v1–4** — one sentence in Greek.
- **v5** quotes Ps 2:7 + 2 Sam 7:14.
- **v6** quotes Deut 32:43 (LXX) / Ps 97:7.
- **v7** quotes Ps 104:4.
- **v8-9** quotes Ps 45:6-7.
- **v10-12** quotes Ps 102:25-27.
- **v13** quotes Ps 110:1.
- The quotations are translated from the Greek of this letter, which follows the Septuagint; where that differs from the Hebrew (v6, 7, 10–12), the Greek is kept.
