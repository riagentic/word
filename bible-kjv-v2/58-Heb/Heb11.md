# Hebrews 11

[1] **Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen.**

[2] For by it the people of old received their commendation.

[3] By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of *Elohim*, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible.

[4] By faith Abel offered to *Elohim* a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain, through which he was commended as righteous, *Elohim* commending him by accepting his gifts. And through his faith, though he died, he still speaks.

[5] By faith Enoch was taken up so that he should not see death, and he was not found, because *Elohim* had taken him. Now before he was taken he was commended as having pleased *Elohim*.

[6] And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to *Elohim* must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.

[7] By faith Noah, being warned by *Elohim* concerning events as yet unseen, in reverent fear constructed an ark for the saving of his household. By this he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith.

[8] By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place that he was to receive as an inheritance. And he went out, not knowing where he was going.

[9] By faith he went to live in the land of promise, as in a foreign land, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, heirs with him of the same promise.

[10] For he was looking forward to the city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is *Elohim*.

[11] By faith Sarah herself received power to conceive, even when she was past the age, since she considered him faithful who had promised.

[12] Therefore from one man, and him as good as dead, were born descendants as many as the stars of heaven and as many as the innumerable grains of sand by the seashore.

[13] These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar, and having acknowledged that they were strangers and exiles on the earth.

[14] For people who speak thus make it clear that they are seeking a homeland.

[15] If they had been thinking of that land from which they had gone out, they would have had opportunity to return.

[16] But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore *Elohim* is not ashamed to be called their *Elohim*, for he has prepared for them a city.

[17] By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac, and he who had received the promises was in the act of offering up his only son,

[18] of whom it was said, "**Through Isaac shall your offspring be named**."

[19] He considered that *Elohim* was able even to raise him from the dead, from which, figuratively speaking, he did receive him back.

[20] By faith Isaac invoked future blessings on Jacob and Esau.

[21] By faith Jacob, when dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, bowing in worship over the head of his staff.

[22] By faith Joseph, at the end of his life, made mention of the exodus of the Israelites and gave directions concerning his bones.

[23] By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful, and they were not afraid of the king's edict.

[24] By faith Moses, when he was grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,

[25] choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of *Elohim* than to enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin.

[26] He considered the reproach of the *Mashiach* greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking to the reward.

[27] By faith he left Egypt, not being afraid of the anger of the king, for he endured as seeing him who is invisible.

[28] By faith he kept the Passover and sprinkled the blood, so that the Destroyer of the firstborn might not touch them.

[29] By faith the people crossed the Red Sea as on dry land, but the Egyptians, when they attempted to do the same, were drowned.

[30] By faith the walls of Jericho fell down after they had been encircled for seven days.

[31] By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had given a friendly welcome to the spies.

[32] And what more shall I say? For time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets —

[33] who through faith conquered kingdoms, enforced justice, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions,

[34] quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight.

[35] Women received back their dead by resurrection. Some were tortured, refusing to accept release, so that they might rise again to a better life.

[36] Others suffered mocking and flogging, and even chains and imprisonment.

[37] They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were killed with the sword. They went about in skins of sheep and goats, destitute, afflicted, mistreated —

[38] of whom the world was not worthy — wandering about in deserts and mountains, and in dens and caves of the earth.

[39] And all these, though commended through their faith, did not receive what was promised,

[40] since *Elohim* had provided something better for us, that apart from us they should not be made perfect.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v26) — systematic.
- **"Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen" → "Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen"** (v1) — Greek ὑπόστασις *hypostasis* = "substance / confidence / assurance / underlying reality"; ἔλεγχος *elenchos* = "proof / conviction / evidence." "Assurance" and "conviction" better capture the experiential and rational dimensions; "substance" implies materiality which is odd for faith.
- **"obtained a good report" → "received their commendation"** (v2) — Greek ἐμαρτυρήθησαν *emartyrtēthēsan* = "were witnessed / testified of / commended"; "obtained a good report" is idiomatic in KJV but unclear.
- **"the worlds were framed by the word of God" → "the universe was created by the word of *Elohim*"** (v3) — Greek τοὺς αἰῶνας *tous aiōnas* = "the ages / the world-ages"; "universe" captures the scope in modern English; "framed" → "created."
- **"By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice" → "By faith Abel offered to *Elohim* a more acceptable sacrifice"** (v4) — "more excellent" → "more acceptable" (Greek πλείονα θυσίαν *pleiona thysian* = "greater / more abundant / more acceptable sacrifice").
- **"in reverent fear … moved with fear" → "in reverent fear"** (v7) — Greek εὐλαβηθείς *eulabētheis* = "being cautious / acting with reverent fear / godly circumspection."
- **"By faith Sara herself received strength to conceive seed" → "By faith Sarah herself received power to conceive"** (v11) — "Sara" → "Sarah"; "received strength to conceive seed" → "received power to conceive"; modernized.
- **"in a parable" → "figuratively speaking"** (v19) — Greek ἐν παραβολῇ *en parabolē* = "as in a parable / figuratively."
- **"esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt" → "He considered the reproach of the *Mashiach* greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt"** (v26) — "Christ" → "*Mashiach*"; "riches" → "wealth"; the identification of Moses' suffering with the pre-existent *Mashiach* is a significant theological move preserved in the translation.
- **"wrought righteousness" → "enforced justice"** (v33) — Greek εἰργάσαντο δικαιοσύνην *eirgasanto dikaiosynēn* = "worked / achieved righteousness / justice"; "enforced justice" conveys the active, governance sense in context.
- **"were tortured, not accepting deliverance" → "were tortured, refusing to accept release"** (v35) — Greek ἐτυμπανίσθησαν *etympanisthēsan* = "were beaten on the drum" (a form of torture on a wheel/rack); "tortured" is an adequate rendering.
- **"being destitute, afflicted, tormented" → "destitute, afflicted, mistreated"** (v37) — Greek κακουχούμενοι *kakoüchoumenoi* = "mistreated / suffering ill treatment"; "tormented" in modern English implies psychological anguish; "mistreated" more accurate.

### Notes
- **v18** quotes Gen 21:12.
