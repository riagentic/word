# Hebrews 5

[1] For every high priest, being taken from among men, is appointed on behalf of men in the things pertaining to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins,

[2] being able to deal gently with those who are ignorant and going astray, since he himself also is encompassed with weakness,

[3] and because of it he is obliged, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins.

[4] And no one takes the honor to himself, but being called by ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, just as ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧ also.

[5] So also ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ did not glorify himself to become a high priest, but the one who spoke to him:

"**You are my Son,
today I have begotten you**";

[6] as he says also in another place:

"**You are a priest forever,
according to the order of ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧**,"

[7] who, in the days of his flesh, having offered both supplications and entreaties to the one able to save him out of death, with strong crying and tears, and having been heard because of his reverence,

[8] though being a son, learned obedience from the things which he suffered,

[9] and having been perfected, he became to all those who obey him the cause of eternal salvation,

[10] having been addressed by ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ as high priest according to the order of ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧.

[11] Concerning whom the word we have is much, and hard to explain in saying, since you have become sluggish in hearing.

[12] For indeed, though you ought to be teachers because of the time, you again have need that someone teach you the elements of the beginning of the oracles of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and you have become ones having need of milk, and not of solid food.

[13] For everyone who partakes of milk is without experience of the word of righteousness, for he is an infant;

[14] but solid food is for the perfect, those who because of habit have their senses trained for the distinguishing of both good and evil.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v5) — systematic.
- **"ordained for men in things pertaining to God" → "appointed on behalf of men in the things pertaining to *Elohim*"** (v1) — Greek καθίσταται τὰ πρὸς τὸν θεόν, as 2:17; λαμβανόμενος is "being taken", not "chosen". The Greek has no verb "to act".
- **"Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way" → "being able to deal gently with those who are ignorant and going astray"** (v2) — Greek μετριοπαθεῖν, "to be moderate in one's feelings toward"; v1–3 are one sentence. πλανωμένοις is middle or passive: "going astray" or "being led astray" (πλανάω, elsewhere "deceive").
- **"compassed with infirmity" → "encompassed with weakness"** (v2) — Greek περίκειται ἀσθένειαν, "has weakness lying around him"; ἀσθένεια is "weakness" as 4:15; 7:28.
- **"by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins" → "because of it he is obliged, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins"** (v3) — Greek διʼ αὐτήν, "because of it" (the weakness); the Greek has no noun "sacrifice" and no "his own sins".
- **"this honour" → "the honor"; "but he that is called of God, as was Aaron" → "but being called by *Elohim*, just as *Aharon* also"** (v4) — Greek τὴν τιμήν; καλούμενος is a participle; the Greek has no "only" and no second verb.
- **"glorified not himself" kept** (v5) — Greek οὐχ ἑαυτὸν ἐδόξασεν; "exalt" is a different verb. The clause "but the one who spoke to him" has no verb in Greek; none is supplied.
- **"Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee" → "You are my Son, today I have begotten you"** (v5) — Ps 2:7 as the Greek prints it; ἐγώ, "I", is emphatic.
- **"after the order of" → "according to the order of"** (v6, 10) — Greek κατὰ τὴν τάξιν; so throughout Hebrews.
- **"Who in the days of his flesh, when he had offered up prayers and supplications" → "who, in the days of his flesh, having offered both supplications and entreaties"** (v7) — the Greek has the relative ὅς, "who", not the name Jesus; v7–10 continue the sentence of v5–6. δεήσεις is "supplications" (δέησις); ἱκετηρίας, "entreaties", occurs only here in the New Testament. προσενέγκας is the verb "offer" of v1, 3.
- **"from death" → "out of death"** (v7) — Greek ἐκ θανάτου.
- **"strong crying" kept** (v7) — Greek κραυγῆς ἰσχυρᾶς, singular.
- **"was heard in that he feared" → "having been heard because of his reverence"** (v7) — Greek εἰσακουσθεὶς ἀπὸ τῆς εὐλαβείας. εὐλάβεια is reverent caution, godly fear (12:28); ἀπό is literally "from", so the words can also be read "heard [and delivered] from his fear".
- **"Though he were a Son" → "though being a son"** (v8) — Greek καίπερ ὢν υἱός, without the article.
- **"by the things which he suffered" → "from the things which he suffered"** (v8) — Greek ἀφʼ ὧν ἔπαθεν; a play on ἔμαθεν … ἔπαθεν, "learned … suffered".
- **"being made perfect" → "having been perfected"** (v9) — Greek τελειωθείς; τελειόω is "perfect" throughout Hebrews (2:10; 7:19, 28; 9:9).
- **"the author of eternal salvation" → "the cause of eternal salvation"** (v9) — Greek αἴτιος, "the one who is the cause"; "author" stands for ἀρχηγός (2:10).
- **"Called of God an high priest" → "having been addressed by *Elohim* as high priest"** (v10) — Greek προσαγορευθείς, "addressed, greeted by a name"; not the "called" of v4 (καλούμενος).
- **"Of whom we have many things to say, and hard to be uttered" → "Concerning whom the word we have is much, and hard to explain in saying"** (v11) — Greek περὶ οὗ πολὺς ἡμῖν ὁ λόγος καὶ δυσερμήνευτος λέγειν. οὗ can be masculine ("whom": *Malki-Tzedeq*, or the *Mashiach*) or neuter ("which").
- **"dull of hearing" → "sluggish in hearing"** (v11) — Greek νωθροὶ … ταῖς ἀκοαῖς; νωθρός again in 6:12.
- **"for the time" → "because of the time"** (v12) — Greek διὰ τὸν χρόνον.
- **"ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles" → "you again have need that someone teach you the elements of the beginning"** (v12) — the verse reads τινά, "someone" (SBLGNT, WH, NA28); with the other accent, τίνα (Treg, RP), it is "what the elements are". The manuscripts have no accents. στοιχεῖα is "elements" (Gal 4:3; Col 2:8); τῆς ἀρχῆς, "of the beginning", as 6:1.
- **"are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat" → "you have become ones having need of milk, and not of solid food"** (v12) — Greek γεγόνατε χρείαν ἔχοντες; στερεὰ τροφή is "solid food". The verse follows καὶ οὐ, "and not" (Treg, NA28, RP); οὐ alone, "not" (SBLGNT, WH).
- **"every one that useth milk is unskilful in the word of righteousness: for he is a babe" → "everyone who partakes of milk is without experience of the word of righteousness, for he is an infant"** (v13) — Greek μετέχων, "partaking of" (2:14; 7:13); ἄπειρος, "without experience"; νήπιος, "infant".
- **"them that are of full age" → "the perfect"** (v14) — Greek τελείων; τέλειος is "perfect" throughout, here of the full-grown as against the infant; the same root as "perfected" (v9) and "perfection" (6:1).
- **"by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil" → "because of habit have their senses trained for the distinguishing of both good and evil"** (v14) — Greek διὰ τὴν ἕξιν, "because of the habit" or "because of their state"; αἰσθητήρια are the organs of perception; πρὸς διάκρισιν is a noun.

### Notes
- **v5** quotes Ps 2:7.
- **v6** quotes Ps 110:4 (Ps 109:4 in the Septuagint). *Malki-Tzedeq* = Melchizedek, "my king is righteousness" / "king of righteousness" (7:2).
- **v5–10** — one sentence in Greek; "who" (v7) refers to the *Mashiach* of v5.
