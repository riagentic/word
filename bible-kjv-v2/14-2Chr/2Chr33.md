# 2 Chronicles 33

[1] Manasseh was twelve years old when he began to reign, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem.

[2] He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, like the abominations of the nations whom Yahweh had cast out before the children of Israel.

[3] He rebuilt the high places that Hezekiah his father had broken down; he raised up altars for the Baals, made Asherah poles, worshiped all the host of heaven, and served them.

[4] He also built altars in the house of Yahweh, of which Yahweh had said, "In Jerusalem shall my name be forever."

[5] He built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the house of Yahweh.

[6] He made his children pass through the fire in the Valley of the Son of Hinnom; he practiced soothsaying, used omens, practiced sorcery, and dealt with mediums and spiritist — spirit-knowers. He did much evil in the eyes of Yahweh, provoking him to anger.

[7] He set a carved image, the idol he had made, in the house of *Elohim*, of which *Elohim* had said to David and to Solomon his son, "In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel, I will put my name forever.

[8] I will not again remove the foot of Israel from the land that I appointed for your fathers — if only they will take care to do all that I have commanded them, according to the whole Law, the statutes, and the ordinances given through the hand of Moses."

[9] Manasseh led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem astray, to do worse than the nations whom Yahweh had destroyed before the children of Israel.

[10] Yahweh spoke to Manasseh and to his people, but they would not listen.

[11] Therefore Yahweh brought upon them the commanders of the army of the king of Assyria, who captured Manasseh with hooks, bound him with bronze fetters, and brought him to Babylon.

[12] When he was in distress, he entreated Yahweh his *Elohim* and humbled himself greatly before the *Elohim* of his fathers.

[13] He prayed to him, and *Elohim* was moved by his entreaty, heard his supplication, and brought him back to Jerusalem, to his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that Yahweh was *Elohim*.

[14] After this he built an outer wall to the city of David, on the west side of Gihon in the valley, to the entrance of the Fish Gate; it encircled Ophel and raised it to a very great height. He put military commanders in all the fortified cities in Judah.

[15] He took away the foreign gods and the idol from the house of Yahweh, and all the altars that he had built on the mount of the house of Yahweh and in Jerusalem, and cast them out of the city.

[16] He restored the altar of Yahweh and sacrificed on it peace offerings and thanksgiving offerings, and commanded Judah to serve Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel.

[17] Nevertheless, the people still sacrificed on the high places, but only to Yahweh their *Elohim*.

[18] The rest of the acts of Manasseh, his prayer to his *Elohim*, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel, behold, they are in the Book of the Kings of Israel.

[19] His prayer also, and how *Elohim* was moved by his entreaty, and all his sin and his unfaithfulness, and the places where he built high places and set up Asherah poles and idols before he humbled himself, behold, they are written in the Records of the Seers.

[20] Manasseh slept with his fathers, and they buried him in his own house; Amon his son reigned in his place.

[21] Amon was twenty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem.

[22] He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, as Manasseh his father had done; Amon sacrificed to all the carved images that Manasseh his father had made, and served them.

[23] He did not humble himself before Yahweh, as Manasseh his father had humbled himself, but Amon incurred guilt more and more.

[24] His servants conspired against him and killed him in his own house.

[25] But the people of the land struck down all those who had conspired against King Amon; the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his place.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"heathen" → "nations"** (v2, 9).
- **"observed times, used enchantments, used witchcraft" → "practiced soothsaying, used omens, practiced sorcery"** (v6).
- **"thorns" → "hooks"** (v11) — Hebrew *chochim*; disputed, but "hooks" (nose-hook or chin-hook for captives) attested in Assyrian reliefs. May also read "among thorns" (Aramaic brush-captivity).
- **"strange gods" → "foreign gods"** (v15).
- **"Sayings of the Seers" → "Records of the Seers"** (v19).
- **"trespass" → "unfaithfulness / guilt"** (v19, 23) — *ma'al*.

### Notes
- **Parallel to 2 Kgs 21:1-18** — Chronicler uniquely adds Manasseh's Babylonian captivity, repentance, restoration, and reform (v11-17). Kings paints him unrelievedly black.
- **Manasseh's exile to Babylon** — no direct external attestation; some scholars propose he was among vassals summoned by Ashurbanipal after Shamash-shum-ukin's Babylonian revolt (ca. 652 BC), or earlier by Esarhaddon. Manasseh's name appears in Assyrian tribute lists.
- **"Prayer of Manasseh"** — separate apocryphal text in LXX/Vulgate tradition, inspired by v18-19; not canonical.
- **People still on high places but to Yahweh** (v17) — incomplete reform; later requires Josiah.
- **Chronicler's theology** — repentance always possible; even the worst king can turn. Reframes 55 years of reign as concluded in genuine conversion.
- **Amon unchanged** — compared unfavorably: does not humble himself. Quick regicide follows.
