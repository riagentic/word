## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; in v8 the Hebrew has the article (הָאֱלֹהִים), rendered the same.
- **"And" kept** (throughout) — the Hebrew וַ / וְ at the head of each clause is translated; KJV's "Moreover", "So", "Now", "therefore", "Nevertheless", "But" are all the same "and" (v22 כִּי after a negative is "but").
- **"kept a passover" → "made a Pesach"** (v1, 16, 17, 18, 19) — Hebrew עָשָׂה פֶסַח "make a Pesach", as Exod 12:48; Num 9; Deut 16:1; 2 Kgs 23:21–23. פֶּסַח names the animal, the meal and the feast alike: no "lamb", "offering" or "feast" is added (v1, 6, 11, 13 "slaughtered / boiled the Pesach").
- **"killed the passover" → "slaughtered the Pesach"** (v1, 6, 11) — שָׁחַט "slaughter" (cut the throat).
- **"on the fourteenth day" → "on the fourteenth"** (v1) — the Hebrew has no word "day" here.
- **"set the priests in their charges, and encouraged them" → "made the priests stand at their charges and strengthened them"** (v2) — הֶעֱמִיד "make stand"; חִזֵּק piel "make strong" ("encourage" is the sense).
- **"that taught all Israel" → "who gave understanding to all Israel"** (v3) — the qere הַמְּבִינִים, hifil of בִּין "understand"; the written form (ketiv) המבונים is of the same root and its pointing is not certain.
- **"Put the holy ark" → "Place the holy ark"** (v3) — תְּנוּ, lit. "give"; "the holy ark" is lit. "the ark of holiness".
- **"it shall not be a burden upon your shoulders" → "you have no burden on the shoulder"** (v3) — אֵין־לָכֶם מַשָּׂא בַּכָּתֵף, a statement without a verb: "there is not for you a burden on the shoulder".
- **"prepare yourselves" → "prepare"** (v4) — the reading tradition (qere) is the hifil וְהָכִינוּ "prepare", with no object; the written consonants והכונו are the nifal "be prepared / prepare yourselves". The same verb הֵכִין stands in v6, 14, 15, 20 and its nifal in v10, 16 ("was prepared"); the root also says "establish, set firm".
- **"after your courses" → "according to your divisions"** (v4, 10) — מַחְלְקוֹת "divisions". **"according to the writing … according to the writing" → "by the writing … by the writing"** (v4) — the preposition is בְּ "in, by"; two nouns of one root, כְּתָב (David) and מִכְתָּב (Solomon), both "writing".
- **"stand in the holy place according to the divisions of the families of the fathers" → "stand in the Holy, by the sections of the fathers' houses"** (v5) — בַּקֹּדֶשׁ "in the Holy": "place" is not in the Hebrew. פְּלֻגּוֹת (v5) and מִפְלַגּוֹת (v12), from פָּלַג "divide", are found only here and are rendered "sections" to keep them apart from מַחְלְקוֹת "divisions". **"the division of the families of the Levites" → "a portion of a father's house for the Levites"** (v5) — חֲלֻקָּה, found only here (root חָלַק "divide, share"). **"the people" → "the sons of the people"** (v5, 7, 12, 13) — בְּנֵי הָעָם, the common people (as 2 Kgs 23:6).
- **"prepare your brethren, that they may do" → "prepare for your brothers, to do"** (v6) — לַאֲחֵיכֶם "for your brothers"; לַעֲשׂוֹת "to do": the Hebrew does not say who does.
- **"gave" → "lifted up"** (v7, 8, 9) — הֵרִים "lift up", the verb of תְּרוּמָה "contribution": what is lifted off and set apart (as Num 18:26; 31:28). In v8 נָתְנוּ "gave" is another verb.
- **"of the flock, lambs and kids" → "flock … lambs and sons of goats"** (v7) — צֹאן "flock" (sheep and goats); בְּנֵי־עִזִּים lit. "sons of goats" = young goats. **"passover offerings" → "Pesachim"** (v7, 8, 9) — the plural of פֶּסַח: the animals for the Pesach; "offerings" is not in the Hebrew. **"three thousand bullocks" → "of the herd three thousand"** (v7, 8, 9) — בָּקָר "herd" (cattle). **"small cattle" dropped** (v8, 9) — the Hebrew gives only the number. **"that were present" → "who were found"** (v7, 17, 18) — הַנִּמְצָא, lit. "that was found" (= that was there). **"the king's substance" → "the goods of the king"** (v7) — רְכוּשׁ.
- **"gave willingly" → "lifted up for a freewill offering"** (v8) — לִנְדָבָה. **"rulers" → "leaders"** (v8) — נְגִידִים. **"princes" / "chief" → "princes" / "chiefs"** (v8, 9) — one word, שַׂר: the king's "princes" (v8), the "chiefs" of the Levites (v9, as 1 Chr 15:16).
- **"Conaniah"** (v9) — the qere כָנַנְיָהוּ; the written form is כונניהו (Conaniah, 2 Chr 31:12).
- **"stood in their place" → "stood at their place"** (v10) — עָמְדָם, lit. "their standing"; in v15 מַעֲמָד "station" is another noun of the same root.
- **"sprinkled the blood from their hands" → "dashed from their hand"** (v11) — זָרַק "dash" (throw from a bowl), not "sprinkle"; "the blood" is not in the Hebrew, which names no object; מִיָּדָם "from their hand", that is, from the hand of those who slaughtered. **"flayed them" → "were flaying"** (v11) — a participle without an object.
- **"removed the burnt offerings, that they might give" → "removed the burnt offering, to give them"** (v12) — הָעֹלָה is singular, the suffix of לְתִתָּם "to give them" plural. **"to offer" → "to bring near"** (v12) — הִקְרִיב. **"And so did they with the oxen" → "and so for the herd"** (v12) — no verb in the Hebrew.
- **"roasted the passover with fire" → "boiled the Pesach with fire"** (v13) — the verb is בִּשֵּׁל "boil", the same that stands in the next clause of the holy things "in the pots"; beside it stands בָּאֵשׁ "with fire". Exod 12:8–9 commands the Pesach "roasted with fire" (צְלִי־אֵשׁ) and forbids it "boiled at all in water" (בָּשֵׁל מְבֻשָּׁל בַּמָּיִם); Deut 16:7 has the same verb as here ("you shall boil and eat"). The Hebrew says "boiled … with fire", and the verse says the same; "roast" would be another word.
- **"the other holy offerings sod they in pots, and in caldrons, and in pans" → "the holy things they boiled in the pots and in the kettles and in the pans"** (v13) — הַקֳּדָשִׁים "the holy things"; "other" and "offerings" are not in the Hebrew. צֵלָחוֹת "pans": the kind of vessel is not certain. **"divided them speedily" → "rushed them"** (v13) — וַיָּרִיצוּ, lit. "made run" (as Gen 41:14); "them" is supplied.
- **"were busied in offering of burnt offerings and the fat" → "were at the offering up of the burnt offering and the fat parts"** (v14) — בְּהַעֲלוֹת "in offering up" (lit. "making go up"; one root with עֹלָה "burnt offering"); "busied" is not in the Hebrew; הָעוֹלָה singular; חֲלָבִים "fats".
- **"the king's seer" → "the seer of the king"** (v15) — חוֹזֶה (the "gazer"). **"the porters waited at every gate" → "the gatekeepers at each gate"** (v15) — lit. "at gate and gate"; no verb. **"they might not depart" → "it was not for them to turn aside"** (v15) — אֵין לָהֶם לָסוּר: either "they did not need to" or "they were not to"; the Hebrew does not decide.
- **"the children of Israel" → "the sons of Israel"** (v17) — בְּנֵי.
- **"there was no passover like to that kept" → "there had not been made a Pesach like it"** (v18) — נַעֲשָׂה "was made", as 2 Kgs 23:22, which reads "the like of this Pesach from the days of the judges who judged Israel, and all the days of the kings of Israel and the kings of Judah"; Chronicles has "from the days of Samuel the prophet" and names the kings of Israel only. **"neither did all the kings of Israel keep such a passover" → "of all the kings of Israel none had made the like of the Pesach"** (v18) — lit. "and all the kings of Israel did not make like the Pesach".
- **"was this passover kept" → "this Pesach was made"** (v19) — as 2 Kgs 23:23, which has "of King Josiah" (לַמֶּלֶךְ) where Chronicles has "of the reign of Josiah" (לְמַלְכוּת), and adds "to Yahweh in Jerusalem".
- **"had prepared the temple" → "had prepared the house"** (v20) — הַבַּיִת "the house". **"came up to fight against Charchemish by Euphrates" → "went up to fight at Carchemish by the Euphrates"** (v20) — עָלָה "go up"; בְּכַרְכְּמִישׁ "at" (or "against") Carchemish. 2 Kgs 23:29 reads "Pharaoh Neco … went up against the king of Assyria, by the river Euphrates; and King Josiah went to meet him": Chronicles has no "Pharaoh", no "king of Assyria", no "river", and "went out" (וַיֵּצֵא) for "went". **"went out against him" → "went out to meet him"** (v20) — לִקְרָאתוֹ, as Kings.
- **"ambassadors" → "messengers"** (v21) — מַלְאָכִים. **"What have I to do with thee"** (v21) — kept as in Judg 11:12; 1 Kgs 17:18; lit. "What is there to me and to you?"
- **"I come not against thee this day, but against the house wherewith I have war" → "Not against you, you, today, but to the house of my war"** (v21) — word for word: לֹא־עָלֶיךָ אַתָּה הַיּוֹם כִּי אֶל־בֵּית מִלְחַמְתִּי. The Hebrew has no verb; the pronoun אַתָּה "you" stands a second time and is hard ("I come" is commonly read in its place: a conjecture, not in the text). "The house of my war" = the house with which I am at war.
- **"for God commanded me to make haste" → "and Elohim has said to hasten me"** (v21) — וֵאלֹהִים אָמַר לְבַהֲלֵנִי: "and", not "for"; אָמַר "said"; בִּהֵל piel with the suffix "me", in late Hebrew "hasten" (elsewhere "terrify"). Whether Neco means the God of Israel or a god of his own the word does not say; v22 says "from the mouth of Elohim".
- **"forbear thee from meddling with God … that he destroy thee not" → "Cease yourself from Elohim … and let him not destroy you"** (v21) — חֲדַל־לְךָ מֵאֱלֹהִים, lit. "cease for you from Elohim"; "meddling" is not in the Hebrew. וְאַל־יַשְׁחִיתֶךָ is a wish with "and": "and let him not destroy you" (הִשְׁחִית "ruin", of a man "destroy").
- **"would not turn his face" → "did not turn his face"** (v22) — a plain past. **"disguised himself"** (v22) — הִתְחַפֵּשׂ as printed (as 1 Kgs 22:30). The Septuagint and 1 Esdras 1:28 are commonly cited with another reading here ("strengthened himself" / "undertook"): from memory, not seen with the tools; the Masoretic text is kept. **"hearkened not unto" → "did not listen to"** (v22) — שָׁמַע אֶל. **"the valley of Megiddo" → "the plain of Megiddo"** (v22) — בִּקְעָה, a broad valley floor.
- **"Have me away; for I am sore wounded" → "Take me away, for I am greatly wounded"** (v23) — הַעֲבִירוּנִי, lit. "make me pass over" (so also v24 "took him away"); הָחֳלֵיתִי lit. "I have been made sick" (as 1 Kgs 22:34). **"the archers"** (v23) — lit. "the shooters".
- **"put him in the second chariot" → "made him ride on the second chariot"** (v24) — וַיַּרְכִּיבֻהוּ, the verb of 2 Kgs 23:30 ("his servants made him ride, dead, from Megiddo"). In Kings he is put to death at Megiddo and brought dead to Jerusalem and buried "in his burial place"; in Chronicles he is taken (וַיּוֹלִיכֻהוּ, lit. "they made him go") to Jerusalem, dies, and is buried "in the graves of his fathers". Each is translated as it stands. **"mourned" → "were mourning"** (v24) — a participle.
- **"lamented for Josiah … in their lamentations … an ordinance in Israel" → "chanted a dirge over Josiah … in their dirges … a statute over Israel"** (v25) — קוֹנֵן / קִינָה "chant a dirge / dirge"; חֹק "statute"; עַל "over, upon"; וַיִּתְּנוּם lit. "they gave them for a statute". The book name "Qinot" is the same word, "the dirges". **"to this day" → "until today"** (v25) — עַד־הַיּוֹם.
- **"his goodness" → "his chasadim"** (v26) — חֲסָדָיו, the plural of חֶסֶד: his acts of steadfast love (as 32:32). **"according to that which was written" → "as it is written"** (v26) — כַּכָּתוּב, as v12.
- **"his deeds, first and last" → "his acts, the first and the last"** (v27) — דְּבָרָיו, the same word as "the acts of" in v26 (lit. "words / matters"); the Hebrew has the article twice. "In the book" is lit. "upon the book". 2 Kgs 23:28 has the formula of Kings: "and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?"

### Notes
- **Parallel to 2 Kgs 23:21-30** — Chronicler greatly expands Passover details (v1-19 vs 2 Kgs 23:21-23), compresses reform notice (already covered ch 34).
- **"No Passover like it since Samuel"** — slightly different from 2 Kgs 23:22 ("since the judges"). Chronicler's Samuel-to-Josiah framing.
- **Levitical prominence** (v3-15) — carry out Passover with unprecedented organization. Priest-Levite coordination model.
- **Ark to rest in temple** (v3) — ark had been returned to temple? Or carried out during Manasseh's defilement? Unclear; Chronicler's note implies a period when ark was not in place.
- **Josiah's death at Megiddo (609 BC)** — Pharaoh Neco marching north to support failing Assyria against Babylon. Josiah intercepts, dies. Tragic end.
- **Neco's "word from *Elohim*"** — remarkable: Chronicler accepts a pagan pharaoh's claim to divine message. Josiah's fatal error is rejecting it.
- **Jeremiah's lament** (v25) — likely not canonical Lamentations (which mourns Jerusalem); may be a separate tradition. Shows Jeremiah's political stance linked to Josiah's reforms.
