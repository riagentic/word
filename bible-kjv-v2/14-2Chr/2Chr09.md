# 2 Chronicles 9

[1] When the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon, she came to test Solomon with hard questions at Jerusalem, with a very great retinue — camels bearing spices, gold in abundance, and precious stones. When she came to Solomon, she spoke with him of all that was in her heart.

[2] Solomon answered all her questions; there was nothing hidden from Solomon that he did not explain to her.

[3] When the queen of Sheba saw Solomon's wisdom, the house that he had built,

[4] the food of his table, the seating of his servants, the attendance of his ministers and their apparel, his cupbearers and their apparel, and his ascent by which he went up to the house of Yahweh, there was no more breath in her.

[5] She said to the king, "The report was true that I heard in my own land of your deeds and of your wisdom;

[6] but I did not believe their words until I came and my own eyes had seen — behold, half of the greatness of your wisdom had not been told me; you exceed the report I heard.

[7] Happy are your men and happy are these your servants, who stand continually before you and hear your wisdom.

[8] Blessed be Yahweh your *Elohim*, who has delighted in you, setting you on his throne as king for Yahweh your *Elohim*! Because your *Elohim* loved Israel, to establish them forever, therefore he made you king over them, to execute justice and righteousness."

[9] She gave the king one hundred twenty talents of gold, spices in great abundance, and precious stones; there was no such spice as that which the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.

[10] The servants of Huram and the servants of Solomon who brought gold from Ophir also brought algum wood and precious stones.

[11] From the algum wood the king made steps for the house of Yahweh and for the king's palace, and lyres and harps for the singers; no such wood had ever been seen in the land of Judah.

[12] King Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all that she desired, whatever she asked, beyond what she had brought to the king. Then she turned and went away to her own land, she and her servants.

[13] The weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred sixty-six talents of gold,

[14] besides what traders and merchants brought. All the kings of Arabia and the governors of the country brought gold and silver to Solomon.

[15] King Solomon made two hundred large shields of hammered gold; six hundred shekels of hammered gold went into each large shield.

[16] He made three hundred small shields of hammered gold; three hundred shekels of gold went into each shield. The king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.

[17] The king made a great ivory throne and overlaid it with pure gold.

[18] The throne had six steps with a footstool of gold, fastened to the throne; there were armrests on each side of the seat, and two lions standing beside the armrests.

[19] Twelve lions stood there on the six steps, on one side and on the other. Nothing like it was made for any kingdom.

[20] All King Solomon's drinking vessels were of gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were of pure gold; none were of silver, for it was not considered anything in the days of Solomon.

[21] For the king had ships that went to Tarshish with the servants of Huram; once every three years the ships of Tarshish came, bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes, and baboons.

[22] King Solomon surpassed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom.

[23] All the kings of the earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which *Elohim* had put in his heart.

[24] Each brought his gift — articles of silver and gold, garments, weapons, spices, horses, and mules — a fixed amount year by year.

[25] Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he stationed in the chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem.

[26] He reigned over all the kings from the Euphrates River to the land of the Philistines, and to the border of Egypt.

[27] The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedars he made as plentiful as the sycamores in the Shephelah.

[28] They imported horses for Solomon from Egypt and from all lands.

[29] The rest of the acts of Solomon, first and last — are they not written in the Records of Nathan the Prophet, in the Prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the Visions of Iddo the Seer concerning Jeroboam son of Nebat?

[30] Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years.

[31] Solomon slept with his fathers; he was buried in the city of David his father, and Rehoboam his son reigned in his place.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"prove" → "test"** (v1).
- **"peacocks" → "baboons"** (v21) — *tukkiyim* disputed; same debate as 1 Kgs 10:22.
- **"targets" → "large shields"** (v15); **"shields" → "small shields"** (v16) — *tzinnot / maginnim*.
- **"harness" → "weapons"** (v24) — *nesheq*.
- **"the river" → "the Euphrates River"** (v26) — *ha-nahar*.
- **"low plains" → "Shephelah"** (v27).
- **"book of Nathan the prophet... visions of Iddo" → "Records of Nathan the Prophet... Visions of Iddo the Seer"** (v29).

### Notes
- **Parallel to 1 Kgs 10** — Chronicler reproduces, tightening. Omits 1 Kgs 10:28-29 on horse-import pricing.
- **Queen of Sheba** — Saba; modern Yemen. Jesus references her wisdom-seeking (Matt 12:42).
- **"Throne of Yahweh"** (v8) — Chronicler's theology: Solomon sits on Yahweh's throne. Davidic king is Yahweh's viceroy.
- **666 talents** (v13) — famously referenced Rev 13:18. Likely original wealth-marker of excessive power.
- **Deut 17 violations** — Chronicler simply reports Solomon's horses, gold, international alliances without condemnation. 1 Kgs 11 (not in Chronicles) records the resulting apostasy.
- **Chronicler omits Solomon's decline** — entire 1 Kgs 11 (foreign wives, idolatry, adversaries) is absent. Solomon's kingdom passes to Rehoboam without explanation. Chronicler presents an idealized Solomon.
- **Three sources cited** — Nathan, Ahijah, Iddo. Prophet-historians; several of Chronicles' source-works are otherwise lost.
