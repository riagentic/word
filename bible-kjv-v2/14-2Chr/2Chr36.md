## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; in v16, 18, 19 with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. The chapter runs beside 2 Kgs 23:30–25:21; where the Hebrew of the two books is the same the wording is the same, and nothing is taken over from Kings.
- **"Jehoahaz"** (v2, 4) — here the Hebrew has the shorter form יוֹאָחָז, "Yoachaz"; v1 has יְהוֹאָחָז. The same man; the name keeps one form.
- **"Jehoahaz was twenty and three years old when he began to reign" → "was twenty-three years old when he began to reign"** (v2, 5, 9, 11) — Hebrew בֶּן־… שָׁנָה … בְּמָלְכוֹ, literally "a son of … years … at his reigning"; v2 orders the number "three and twenty".
- **"put him down at Jerusalem, and condemned the land in" → "removed him in Jerusalem, and he fined the land"** (v3) — Hebrew הֵסִיר "remove" (= deposed); עָנַשׁ "fine", the verb (2 Kgs 23:33 has the noun, "put a fine on", and says that Neco bound him at Riblah).
- **"and Necho took Jehoahaz his brother, and carried him to Egypt" → "and Jehoahaz his brother Neco took, and he brought him to Egypt"** (v4) — the Hebrew order; הֵבִיא "bring". Kings has "and he came to Egypt and died there".
- **"that which was evil in the sight of the LORD" → "the evil in the eyes of Yahweh"** (v5, 9, 12) — Hebrew הָרַע בְּעֵינֵי, with the article. "His Elohim" (v5, 12) is not in Kings.
- **"bound him in fetters, to carry him to Babylon" → "bound him in bronze fetters to lead him to Babylon"** (v6) — Hebrew נְחֻשְׁתַּיִם, a dual of "bronze"; הוֹלִיךְ "lead". The verse says what was meant, not that he came there. "Against him … came up": Kings has "In his days".
- **"carried of the vessels … and put them in his temple" → "of the utensils … brought … and he placed them in his palace"** (v7) — Hebrew כֵּלִים, any made article; מִן "of" = some of; נָתַן, literally "gave". הֵיכָל is a great house, of a god ("temple") or of a king ("palace"): of the king's own הֵיכָל it is rendered "palace", but "his temple" may be meant; the Hebrew does not decide.
- **"his abominations which he did, and that which was found in him" → "his abominations that he did, and what was found against him"** (v8) — Hebrew עָלָיו, literally "upon him": against him, or on him. "Acts": Hebrew דִּבְרֵי, literally "words / matters". הִנָּם "behold, they"; Kings has "are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah".
- **"Jehoiachin was eight years old"** (v9) — so the Hebrew here, בֶּן־שְׁמוֹנֶה שָׁנִים; 2 Kgs 24:8 has "eighteen years" and "three months" without the ten days. Each book is translated as printed.
- **"when the year was expired" → "at the return of the year"** (v10) — Hebrew לִתְשׁוּבַת הַשָּׁנָה. **"goodly vessels" → "desirable utensils"** — Hebrew כְּלֵי חֶמְדַּת בֵּית־יְהוָה, literally "the utensils of the desire of the house of Yahweh". **"Zedekiah his brother"** — so the Hebrew, אָחִיו; 2 Kgs 24:17 has "Mattaniah his uncle" (דֹּדוֹ). אָח is also said of a kinsman.
- **"humbled not himself before Jeremiah the prophet speaking from the mouth of the LORD" → "he did not bring himself low before Jeremiah the prophet, from the mouth of Yahweh"** (v12) — the Hebrew has no "speaking" and no "and" before the clause; מִפִּי יְהוָה stands bare. נִכְנַע, a nifal: "be brought low" or "bring oneself low" (= humble himself), as 2 Kgs 22:19.
- **"hardened his heart from turning" → "strengthened his heart against returning"** (v13) — Hebrew אִמֵּץ "strengthen, make firm"; מִשּׁוּב, literally "from returning": so as not to return. "Stiffened his neck": הִקְשָׁה "make hard".
- **"all the chief of the priests, and the people, transgressed very much after all the abominations of the heathen" → "all the chiefs of the priests and the people committed much unfaithfulness, according to all the abominations of the nations"** (v14) — Hebrew שָׂרֵי, the same word as "princes" in v18; הִרְבּוּ לִמְעָל־מַעַל, literally "made much to act unfaithfully an unfaithfulness", verb and noun of one root: a breach of trust; גּוֹיִם "nations". **"polluted … hallowed" → "defiled … sanctified"** — Hebrew טִמֵּא, literally "make unclean"; הִקְדִּישׁ.
- **"sent to them by his messengers, rising up betimes, and sending" → "sent to them by the hand of his messengers, rising early and sending"** (v15) — Hebrew עֲלֵיהֶם, literally "upon them"; בְּיַד; הַשְׁכֵּם וְשָׁלוֹחַ, two infinitives: he sent early and again and again. No "them" follows. מַלְאָךְ is the one word for "messenger" and "angel". **"had compassion … dwelling place" → "had pity … abode"** — Hebrew חָמַל "have pity, spare"; מָעוֹן.
- **"they mocked the messengers of God, and despised his words, and misused his prophets" → "they were jesting at the messengers of Elohim and despising his words and mocking his prophets"** (v16) — three participles with "were": a lasting way of acting. מַלְעִבִים is found only here; "jest, make sport" is its likely sense, not certain. מִתַּעְתְּעִים "mock", as Gen 27:12. **"the wrath of the LORD arose … till there was no remedy" → "the fury of Yahweh came up … until there was no healing"** — Hebrew חֵמָה, literally "heat"; עָלָה "come up"; מַרְפֵּא "healing".
- **"Therefore he brought upon them the king of the Chaldees, who slew" → "And he brought up against them the king of the Chaldeans, and he killed"** (v17) — the Hebrew has no "therefore" and no "who": וַיַּהֲרֹג "and he killed", and who is meant is not said (Yahweh, the subject before and after, or the king). The qere is כַּשְׂדִּים; the ketiv writes the name with a double yod. **"had no compassion upon young man or maiden, old man, or him that stooped for age" → "had no pity on young man or virgin, old man or aged"** — Hebrew בְּתוּלָה "virgin"; יָשֵׁשׁ in this form is found only here: "aged"; "stooped" is not said. **"he gave them all" → "all he gave"** — Hebrew הַכֹּל "the whole".
- **"vessels … his princes"** (v18) — "utensils" as v7; שָׂרָיו "his princes".
- **"brake down the wall … burnt all the palaces thereof … destroyed all the goodly vessels thereof" → "broke down the wall … all its citadels they burned … all its desirable utensils they ruined"** (v19) — Hebrew נִתֵּץ; אַרְמוֹן, a fortified great house; וְכָל־כְּלֵי מַחֲמַדֶּיהָ לְהַשְׁחִית, literally "and all the utensils of its desirable things, to ruin": an infinitive, not a finite verb.
- **"them that had escaped from the sword carried he away to Babylon … until the reign of the kingdom of Persia" → "he carried into exile to Babylon the remnant from the sword … until the kingdom of Persia reigned"** (v20) — Hebrew הֶגְלָה; שְׁאֵרִית "remnant", what the sword left; עַד־מְלֹךְ, literally "until the reigning of".
- **"until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths: for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath" → "until the land had paid off its Shabbatot; all the days of its being made desolate it ceased"** (v21) — the words of Lev 26:34, 35, 43, rendered as there. רָצָה with "Shabbats" is uncertain: "pay off", or "be pleased with, enjoy". שָׁבָתָה is the verb "cease", the root of "Shabbat"; the noun does not stand here a second time. **"by the mouth of Jeremiah"** (v21, 22) — Hebrew בְּפִי, literally "in the mouth of". "Fulfill": Hebrew מִלֵּא, literally "fill", twice.
- **"that the word of the LORD … might be accomplished" → "that the word of Yahweh … might be finished"** (v22) — Hebrew לִכְלוֹת, of כָּלָה "be finished, come to its end"; another verb than "fulfill" in v21. "In the first year": literally "in year one". **"he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and put it also in writing" → "he made a voice pass through all his kingdom, and also in writing"** — Hebrew וַיַּעֲבֶר־קוֹל, as Exod 36:6; the second half has no verb.
- **"the LORD God of heaven … hath charged me" → "Yahweh the Elohim of the heavens … has appointed me"** (v23) — Hebrew שָׁמַיִם is plural; פָּקַד עָלַי, literally "appointed upon me": laid it on me as a charge. **"Who is there among you of all his people? The LORD his God be with him, and let him go up" → "Whoever among you is of all his people, Yahweh his Elohim be with him, and let him go up"** — Hebrew מִי "who", a question or "whoever"; "Yahweh his Elohim with him" has no verb: "be" is supplied, and "is with him" is as possible. The book ends in the middle of the decree; Ezra 1:1–3 has it again and goes on, with small differences ("from the mouth of Jeremiah"; "may his Elohim be with him").

### Notes
- **Parallel to 2 Kgs 24-25** — Chronicler compresses greatly. Four kings in 23 verses.
- **Three deportations** implied: 605 (Jehoiakim-era temple vessels + Daniel), 597 (Jehoiachin), 586 (Zedekiah/full destruction).
- **"Seventy years"** (v21) — Jer 25:11, 29:10. Interpretation: 586 to 516 BC (destruction to rebuilt temple) fits ~70; or from 605 to 539 BC (first deportation to Cyrus's decree). Sabbath-year interpretation: land repays 490 years of neglected Sabbath-years (Lev 26:34-35).
- **Cyrus's edict (539 BC)** — Chronicler ends with this hopeful pivot. Ezra 1 picks up the text and continues. Hebrew Bible order places Chronicles last — this is the final word: return, rebuild, hope.
- **Jeremiah's prophecy** cited twice (v12, 21-22); Chronicler emphasizes prophetic fulfillment. Even exile fits Yahweh's plan.
- **Cyrus Cylinder** — Persian imperial document supports this decree-style; Cyrus granted restoration to many displaced peoples. Historically corroborated.
- **Chronicler's theology** — destruction is covenantal judgment, but not final. Davidic line preserved (Jeconiah released, 2 Kgs 25:27); Sabbath debt paid; Yahweh stirs Gentile king; return begins. Hope, not despair, closes the Hebrew canon.
