# 2 Chronicles 12

[1] When Rehoboam had established his kingdom and had strengthened himself, he forsook the Law of Yahweh, and all Israel with him.

[2] In the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem — because they had acted unfaithfully toward Yahweh —

[3] with 1,200 chariots and 60,000 horsemen; the people who came with him out of Egypt were without number — Libyans, Sukkites, and Cushites.

[4] He took the fortified cities that belonged to Judah, and came to Jerusalem.

[5] Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and to the leaders of Judah, who were gathered to Jerusalem because of Shishak, and said to them, "Thus says Yahweh: 'You have forsaken me; therefore I have left you in the hand of Shishak.'"

[6] Then the leaders of Israel and the king humbled themselves; they said, "Yahweh is righteous."

[7] When Yahweh saw that they had humbled themselves, the word of Yahweh came to Shemaiah, saying, "They have humbled themselves; I will not destroy them, but I will grant them some deliverance; my wrath shall not be poured out on Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak.

[8] Nevertheless they shall become his servants, that they may know the difference between serving me and serving the kingdoms of the lands."

[9] So Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem; he carried away the treasures of the house of Yahweh and the treasures of the king's house — he took everything. He also carried away the shields of gold that Solomon had made.

[10] King Rehoboam made in their place shields of bronze, and committed them to the hands of the commanders of the guard who kept the entrance of the king's house.

[11] Whenever the king entered the house of Yahweh, the guards came and carried them, and returned them to the guardroom.

[12] Because he humbled himself, the wrath of Yahweh turned from him, so as not to destroy him utterly; also, in Judah conditions were good.

[13] King Rehoboam strengthened himself in Jerusalem and reigned. Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city that Yahweh had chosen out of all the tribes of Israel to put his name there. His mother's name was Naamah the Ammonitess.

[14] He did evil, because he did not set his heart to seek Yahweh.

[15] The acts of Rehoboam, first and last — are they not written in the Records of Shemaiah the Prophet and of Iddo the Seer, as in the enrollment by genealogies? There were wars between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually.

[16] Rehoboam slept with his fathers and was buried in the city of David; Abijah his son reigned in his place.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"Lubims / Sukkiims / Ethiopians" → "Libyans / Sukkites / Cushites"** (v3) — standardized ethnonyms.
- **"service of the kingdoms" → "difference between serving me and serving the kingdoms"** (v8) — spelled out the implicit contrast.
- **"chief of the guard" → "commanders of the guard"** (v10) — *sarei ha-ratzim*.
- **"brass" → "bronze"** (v10).
- **"concerning genealogies" → "as in the enrollment by genealogies"** (v15) — Hebrew *le-hityacheis*; genealogical records.

### Notes
- **Parallel to 1 Kgs 14:21-31** — Chronicler expands on Shishak with theological framing.
- **Shishak = Sheshonq I** — first Libyan pharaoh, 22nd Dynasty. Campaign ca. 925 BC. Karnak temple reliefs list conquered Levantine cities — historical confirmation.
- **Troop composition** — Libyans, Sukkites (Egyptian mercenaries), Cushites (Nubians). Matches Sheshonq's mixed Egyptian force.
- **Humbling → partial reprieve** — Chronicler's repeated theological pattern (cf. 7:14). Judgment averted but consequences retained (vassalage, tribute).
- **"Know difference between my service and kingdoms'"** (v8) — educational judgment: Judah learns by contrast. Proto-exile lesson.
- **Bronze for gold** — diminished splendor; symbolic of Rehoboam's compromised reign.
- **Genealogical records** cited — Chronicler's pronounced interest in lineage and census documentation.
