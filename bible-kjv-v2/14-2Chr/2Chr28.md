# 2 Chronicles 28

[1] Ahaz was twenty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was right in the eyes of Yahweh, as David his father had done.

[2] He walked in the ways of the kings of Israel; he made cast images for the Baals.

[3] He burned incense in the Valley of the Son of Hinnom, and burned his children in the fire, according to the abominations of the nations whom Yahweh had cast out before the children of Israel.

[4] He sacrificed and burned incense on the high places, on the hills, and under every green tree.

[5] Therefore Yahweh his *Elohim* gave him into the hand of the king of Aram; they struck him, took captive a great multitude from his people, and brought them to Damascus. He was also given into the hand of the king of Israel, who struck him with a great slaughter.

[6] Pekah son of Remaliah killed in Judah one hundred twenty thousand in one day — all valiant men — because they had forsaken Yahweh the *Elohim* of their fathers.

[7] Zichri, a mighty warrior of Ephraim, killed Maaseiah the king's son, Azrikam the governor of the palace, and Elkanah who was next to the king.

[8] The children of Israel took captive of their brothers two hundred thousand women, sons, and daughters; they also took much spoil from them and brought the spoil to Samaria.

[9] But a prophet of Yahweh was there, whose name was Oded; he went out before the army that came to Samaria, and said to them, "Behold, because Yahweh the *Elohim* of your fathers was angry with Judah, he gave them into your hand; but you have killed them in a rage that has reached to heaven.

[10] Now you propose to subjugate for yourselves the children of Judah and Jerusalem as male and female slaves. But are you not yourselves guilty of sins against Yahweh your *Elohim*?

[11] Now hear me, and send back the captives whom you have taken from your brothers, for the fierce wrath of Yahweh is upon you."

[12] Then certain heads of the children of Ephraim — Azariah son of Johanan, Berechiah son of Meshillemoth, Jehizkiah son of Shallum, and Amasa son of Hadlai — stood up against those who were coming from the war.

[13] They said to them, "You shall not bring the captives in here; for you intend to add to our sins and our guilt a trespass against Yahweh; our guilt is already great, and there is fierce wrath against Israel."

[14] So the armed men left the captives and the spoil before the officials and all the assembly.

[15] The men who were designated by name rose up, took the captives, and with the spoil clothed all who were naked among them; they clothed them, gave them sandals, gave them food and drink, anointed them, carried all the feeble on donkeys, and brought them to Jericho, the City of Palms, to their brothers. Then they returned to Samaria.

[16] At that time King Ahaz sent to the kings of Assyria for help.

[17] The Edomites had again come and struck Judah, carrying away captives.

[18] The Philistines also had invaded the cities of the Shephelah and of the Negev of Judah, and had taken Beth-shemesh, Aijalon, Gederoth, Soco with its villages, Timnah with its villages, and Gimzo with its villages, and settled there.

[19] For Yahweh had brought Judah low because of Ahaz king of Israel, for he had led Judah to cast off restraint and had acted unfaithfully against Yahweh.

[20] Tiglath-pilneser king of Assyria came to him and distressed him instead of strengthening him.

[21] For Ahaz had plundered a portion from the house of Yahweh, from the house of the king, and from the officials, and given it to the king of Assyria; but it did not help him.

[22] In the time of his distress he acted yet more unfaithfully against Yahweh — this King Ahaz.

[23] For he sacrificed to the gods of Damascus, who had struck him, and said, "Because the gods of the kings of Aram help them, I will sacrifice to them, that they may help me." But they were the ruin of him and of all Israel.

[24] Ahaz gathered together the vessels of the house of *Elohim*, cut in pieces the vessels of the house of *Elohim*, and shut up the doors of the house of Yahweh; he made for himself altars in every corner of Jerusalem.

[25] In every city of Judah he made high places to burn incense to other gods, and provoked Yahweh the *Elohim* of his fathers to anger.

[26] The rest of his acts and all his ways, first and last, behold, they are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.

[27] Ahaz slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the city — in Jerusalem — but they did not bring him into the tombs of the kings of Israel; Hezekiah his son reigned in his place.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"heathen" → "nations"** (v3).
- **"Syria / Syrians" → "Aram / Arameans"** (v5, 23).
- **"low country" → "Shephelah"** (v18).
- **"south" → "Negev"** (v18).
- **"Tilgath-pilneser" → "Tiglath-pilneser"** (v20).
- **"made Judah naked" → "led Judah to cast off restraint"** (v19) — *hiphria be-yehudah*; let them go wild.
- **"bondmen and bondwomen" → "male and female slaves"** (v10).
- **"city of palm trees" → "City of Palms"** (v15) — Jericho.

### Notes
- **Parallel to 2 Kgs 16** — Chronicler adds substantial unique material: the massive Israelite capture and northern repentance (v5-15), additional Edomite/Philistine raids (v17-18).
- **"King of Israel" (v19)** — Chronicler uses "Israel" for Judahite Ahaz pejoratively, since he walks "in the ways of the kings of Israel" (northern). Ironic misnomer.
- **Valley of Hinnom / Molech** — child sacrifice; Gehenna's origin.
- **Prophet Oded's intervention** — unique Chronicler material. Northerners display moral reasoning: they heed the prophet, release captives, clothe and feed them. Chronicler's unexpected positive view of the North at this moment.
- **The merciful northern response** (v12-15) — inspiration for Luke 10's Good Samaritan (parallels: clothed, anointed, carried on beast to hostelry).
- **Assyrian alliance** — Chronicler reports Ahaz's appeal to Tiglath-pileser, but distinctively says Assyrian "distressed him, not strengthened him." Kings is more ambiguous.
- **Sacrificing to Damascene gods** — climactic irony. Worships gods of those who defeated him.
- **Shut temple doors** (v24) — unique to Chronicler. Worship-suspension; Hezekiah will reopen (29:3).
- **Dishonorable burial** — not in royal tombs.
