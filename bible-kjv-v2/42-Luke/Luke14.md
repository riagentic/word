# Luke 14

[1] And it came to pass, when he went into a house of one of the rulers of the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ on a ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ to eat bread, that they were watching him closely.

[2] And behold, there was a certain man before him who had dropsy.

[3] And answering, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ spoke to the lawyers and ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧, saying, "Is it permitted to heal on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧, or not?"

[4] But they became quiet. And laying hold of him, he healed him and sent him away.

[5] And to them he said, "Whose son or ox, among you, will fall into a well, and he will not immediately draw him up on the day of the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧?"

[6] And they did not have strength to answer back to these things.

[7] Now he was saying a parable to those who had been called, marking how they were choosing out the first couches, saying to them,

[8] "When you are called by someone to a wedding, do not recline on the first couch, lest one more honored than you has been called by him,

[9] and the one who called you and him will come and say to you, 'Give place to this one,' and then you will begin with shame to hold the last place.

[10] But when you are called, go and recline in the last place, so that when the one who has called you comes, he will say to you, 'Friend, go up higher.' Then you will have glory before all those reclining with you.

[11] **Because everyone who lifts himself up will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be lifted up.**"

[12] And he was saying also to the one who had called him, "When you make a dinner or a supper, do not call your friends nor your brothers nor your kinsmen nor rich neighbors, lest they also call you in return and a repayment come to you.

[13] But when you make a reception, call the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind;

[14] and you will be blessed, because they do not have the means to repay you; for it will be repaid to you in the resurrection of the righteous."

[15] And one of those reclining with him, hearing these things, said to him, "Blessed is whoever will eat bread in the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧."

[16] But he said to him, "A certain man was making a great supper, and he called many,

[17] and he sent his slave at the hour of the supper to say to those who had been called, 'Come, because things are now ready.'

[18] And they all, as one, began to excuse themselves. The first said to him, 'I bought a field, and I have necessity to go out and see it; I request you, have me excused.'

[19] And another said, 'I bought five yoke of oxen, and I am going to test them; I request you, have me excused.'

[20] And another said, 'I married a wife, and because of this I am not able to come.'

[21] And the slave, arriving, reported these things to his lord. Then the master of the house, becoming wrathful, said to his slave, 'Go out quickly into the broad streets and streets of the city, and bring in here the poor and crippled and blind and lame.'

[22] And the slave said, 'Lord, what you ordered has been done, and still there is place.'

[23] And the lord said to the slave, 'Go out into the ways and fences and compel them to enter, so that my house may be filled;

[24] for I say to you that none of those men who had been called will taste my supper.'"

[25] Now many crowds were going along with him, and turning, he said to them,

[26] "If anyone comes to me and does not hate his own father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, and furthermore his own soul also, he is not able to be my disciple.

[27] Whoever does not bear his own cross and come after me is not able to be my disciple.

[28] For which of you, wanting to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the expense, whether he has enough for completion?

[29] Lest, when he has laid a foundation and does not have strength to finish, all who behold it begin to mock him,

[30] saying, 'This man began to build and did not have strength to finish.'

[31] Or what king, going to engage another king in war, will not sit down first and take counsel whether he is able with ten thousand to meet the one coming against him with twenty thousand?

[32] But if not, while he is still far off, sending an embassy, he requests the things that make for peace.

[33] So therefore every one of you who does not take leave of all that belongs to himself is not able to be my disciple.

[34] "Salt therefore is good; but if even the salt becomes tasteless, with what will it be seasoned?

[35] It is fit neither for the earth nor for the dung heap; they cast it out. He who has ears to hear, let him hear."

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v15) — divine name retained.
- **"one of the chief Pharisees" → "one of the rulers of the Pharisees"** (v1) — Greek τινος τῶν ἀρχόντων τῶν Φαρισαίων. "To eat bread" (φαγεῖν ἄρτον) is the Hebrew way of saying "to take a meal"; παρατηρούμενοι, "watching closely" (Mark 3:2).
- **"Is it lawful" → "Is it permitted"** (v3) — Greek ἔξεστιν. "Answering" (ἀποκριθείς): no one has spoken; he answers their watching.
- **"held their peace" → "became quiet"; "took him" → "laying hold of him"; "let him go" → "sent him away"** (v4) — Greek ἡσύχασαν; ἐπιλαβόμενος; ἀπέλυσεν.
- **"Which of you shall have an ass or an ox fallen into a pit" → "Whose son or ox, among you, will fall into a well"** (v5) — Greek τίνος ὑμῶν υἱὸς ἢ βοῦς εἰς φρέαρ πεσεῖται, a future; the printed Greek has υἱός, "son" (the tool lists no other reading in its editions; the KJV's "an ass" follows a different text); φρέαρ, "well".
- **"could not answer him again" → "did not have strength to answer back"** (v6) — Greek οὐκ ἴσχυσαν ἀνταποκριθῆναι.
- **"bidden" → "called"** (v7–10, 12, 13, 16, 17, 24) — Greek καλέω, "call": the word for an invitation and for God's call; "invite" would hide that it is one word. In v12 "do not call your friends" is φωνέω, a different verb; ἀντικαλέσωσιν, "call in return".
- **"chief rooms" / "highest room" → "first couches" / "first couch"** (v7, 8) — Greek πρωτοκλισία, as Mark 12:39; Matt 23:6. ἐπέχων, "fixing his attention on": "marking".
- **"sit down" → "recline"** (v8, 10) — Greek κατακλιθῇς, ἀνάπεσε ("fall back"); v10, 15 συνανακειμένων, "reclining with".
- **"more honourable man" → "one more honored"; "lowest room" → "last place"** (v8–10) — Greek ἐντιμότερος; τὸν ἔσχατον τόπον; κατέχειν, "hold".
- **"then shalt thou have worship" → "Then you will have glory"** (v10) — Greek δόξα. "He will say" is a future indicative after ἵνα (WH, NA28).
- **"exalteth … abased … exalted" → "lifts up … humbled … lifted up"** (v11) — Greek ὑψόω, ταπεινόω, as Matt 23:12; Luke has present participles (ὁ ὑψῶν, ὁ ταπεινῶν).
- **"a recompence be made thee" → "a repayment come to you"** (v12; v14 "repay", "be repaid") — Greek ἀνταπόδομα, ἀνταποδίδωμι.
- **"a feast" → "a reception"** (v13) — Greek δοχή (from δέχομαι "receive"), a banquet for guests; a different word from "dinner" (ἄριστον) and "supper" (δεῖπνον) in v12.
- **"the maimed" → "the crippled"** (v13, 21) — Greek ἀνάπειρος.
- **"they cannot recompense thee" → "they do not have the means to repay you"** (v14) — Greek οὐκ ἔχουσιν ἀνταποδοῦναί σοι, literally "they do not have to repay you".
- **"the just" → "the righteous"** (v14) — Greek τῶν δικαίων.
- **"servant" → "slave"; "supper time" → "the hour of the supper"** (v17, 21–23) — Greek δοῦλος; τῇ ὥρᾳ τοῦ δείπνου. "Things are now ready": ἤδη ἕτοιμά ἐστιν, a neuter plural without a noun; "all" (πάντα: Tregelles, Byzantine) is not in WH, NA28.
- **"with one consent began to make excuse" → "as one, began to excuse themselves"** (v18) — Greek ἀπὸ μιᾶς, "from one" (a feminine noun is left unsaid: voice, mind); παραιτεῖσθαι, "ask off, beg off"; ἔχε με παρῃτημένον is the same verb.
- **"I must needs go" → "I have necessity to go out"; "I pray thee" → "I request you"; "prove" → "test"** (v18, 19) — Greek ἔχω ἀνάγκην; ἐρωτῶ σε; δοκιμάσαι.
- **"lord" / "Lord" kept; "being angry" → "becoming wrathful"** (v21–23) — Greek κύριος, κύριε (no "Sir"); ὀργισθείς.
- **"streets and lanes" → "broad streets and streets"; "the halt" → "lame"** (v21) — Greek πλατείας καὶ ῥύμας.
- **"yet there is room" → "still there is place"** (v22) — Greek τόπος, the word of v9, 10.
- **"highways and hedges" → "ways and fences"; "compel them to come in" → "compel them to enter"** (v23) — Greek ὁδούς, φραγμούς; ἀνάγκασον εἰσελθεῖν has no object: "them" is supplied.
- **"you"** (v24) — plural (ὑμῖν), though the lord speaks to one slave: the word passes over to the hearers at the table.
- **"hate" kept** (v26) — Greek μισεῖ, the word as it stands. In Hebrew usage "hate" beside "love" marks the one set second (Gen 29:30–31; Deut 21:15; Mal 1:2–3); Matt 10:37 has "loves … above me". The Greek puts the article before each member: "his own father and the mother and the wife …".
- **"yea, and his own life also" → "and furthermore his own soul also"** (v26) — Greek ἔτι τε καὶ τὴν ψυχὴν ἑαυτοῦ; ψυχή, "soul", is the self and its life.
- **"cannot be" → "is not able to be"** (v26, 27, 33; v20 "am not able") — Greek οὐ δύναται.
- **"counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it" → "calculate the expense, whether he has enough for completion"** (v28) — Greek ψηφίζει τὴν δαπάνην, εἰ ἔχει εἰς ἀπαρτισμόν; "enough" is supplied.
- **"is not able" → "does not have strength"** (v29, 30) — Greek ἰσχύω, as v6; θεωροῦντες, "beholding".
- **"going to make war against another king" → "going to engage another king in war"; "consulteth" → "take counsel"** (v31) — Greek συμβαλεῖν εἰς πόλεμον; βουλεύσεται (WH, NA28, a future; Tregelles and the Byzantine text βουλεύεται, a present).
- **"an ambassage, and desireth conditions of peace" → "an embassy, he requests the things that make for peace"** (v32) — Greek ἐρωτᾷ τὰ πρὸς εἰρήνην, literally "requests the things toward peace".
- **"forsaketh not all that he hath" → "does not take leave of all that belongs to himself"** (v33) — Greek ἀποτάσσεται, "say farewell to, take leave of" (Mark 6:46; Acts 18:18), commonly rendered "renounce"; τοῖς ἑαυτοῦ ὑπάρχουσιν.
- **"Salt is good: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be seasoned?" → "Salt therefore is good; but if even the salt becomes tasteless, with what will it be seasoned?"** (v34) — Greek οὖν (WH, Tregelles, NA28; not in the Byzantine text) and καί "even"; μωρανθῇ, literally "is made foolish", as Matt 5:13; ἀρτυθήσεται, "seasoned", the verb of Mark 9:50.
- **"neither fit for the land, nor yet for the dunghill; but men cast it out" → "fit neither for the earth nor for the dung heap; they cast it out"** (v35) — Greek γῆ; ἔξω βάλλουσιν αὐτό, "they cast it outside".

### Notes
- **v16–24** — compare Matt 22:1–10, a different parable in different Greek (a king, a wedding for his son, slaves in the plural).
- **v34–35** — parallels: Matt 5:13; Mark 9:50.
