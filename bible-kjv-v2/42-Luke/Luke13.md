# Luke 13

[1] Now some were present at that very time, reporting to him about the Galileans whose blood ⟦pilate|Pilatus|Pilate⟧ mixed with their sacrifices.

[2] And answering, he said to them, "Do you think that these Galileans were sinners beyond all the Galileans, because they have suffered these things?

[3] No, I say to you; but unless you repent, you will all perish in like manner.

[4] Or those eighteen on whom the tower in ⟦siloam|Shiloach|Siloam⟧ fell and killed them — do you think that they themselves were debtors beyond all the men who dwell in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧?

[5] No, I say to you; but unless you repent, you will all perish likewise."

[6] And he was saying this parable: "Someone had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, and he came seeking fruit on it and did not find any.

[7] And he said to the vinedresser, 'Behold, it is three years since I have been coming seeking fruit on this fig tree, and I do not find any. Cut it down! Why does it even make the earth idle?'

[8] But he, answering, says to him, 'Lord, leave it this year also, until I dig around it and cast dung,

[9] and if indeed it makes fruit for the time to come — but if not, you will cut it down.'"

[10] Now he was teaching in one of the synagogues on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧.

[11] And behold, a woman having a spirit of weakness eighteen years, and she was bent together and not able to straighten up completely.

[12] And seeing her, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ addressed her and said to her, "Woman, you have been released from your weakness,"

[13] and he laid his hands on her; and instantly she was made straight again, and she was glorifying ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[14] But the synagogue ruler, answering, being indignant that ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ had healed on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧, was saying to the crowd, "There are six days in which it is necessary to work; on them therefore come and be healed, and not on the day of the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧."

[15] But the Lord answered him and said, "Hypocrites, does not each of you on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ loose his ox or his donkey from the manger and, leading it away, give it to drink?

[16] And this woman, being a daughter of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, whom Satan bound — behold, ten and eight years — was it not necessary that she be loosed from this bond on the day of the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧?"

[17] And as he was saying these things, all those opposing him were being put to shame, and all the crowd was rejoicing over all the glorious things that were being done by him.

[18] He was saying therefore, "What is the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ like, and to what shall I liken it?

[19] It is like a grain of mustard, which a man took and cast into his own garden, and it grew and became a tree, and the birds of the heaven dwelt in its branches."

[20] And again he said, "To what shall I liken the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧?

[21] It is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three seahs of flour, until the whole was leavened."

[22] And he was going through cities and villages, teaching and making a journey to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[23] And someone said to him, "Lord, are those who are being saved few?" But he said to them,

[24] "Struggle to enter through the narrow door, because many, I say to you, will seek to enter and will not have strength,

[25] from the time when the master of the house has arisen and shut the door, and you begin to stand outside and to knock at the door, saying, 'Lord, open to us'; and answering, he will say to you, 'I do not know you, where you are from.'

[26] Then you will begin to say, 'We ate before you and drank, and in our broad streets you taught.'

[27] And he will say, speaking to you, 'I do not know where you are from; depart from me, all workers of unrighteousness.'

[28] There will be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth, when you see ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ and ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ and ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ and all the prophets in the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, but yourselves being cast out outside.

[29] And they will come from east and west and from north and south, and will recline in the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[30] And behold, there are last ones who will be first, and there are first ones who will be last."

[31] In that very hour some ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ approached, saying to him, "Go out and go from here, because ⟦herod|Herodes|Herod⟧ wants to kill you."

[32] And he said to them, "Go and say to this fox, 'Behold, I cast out demons and accomplish healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I am perfected.

[33] Nevertheless it is necessary for me to go today and tomorrow and on the day after, because it is not possible that a prophet perish outside ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.'

[34] "**⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, the one killing the prophets and stoning those sent to her — how often I wanted to gather your children together, the way a hen her own brood under the wings, and you did not want it!**

**[35] Behold, your house is left to you.** And I say to you, you shall by no means see me until the time comes when you say, '**Blessed is the one coming in the name of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧!**'"

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv13, 18, 20, 28, 29) — divine name retained.
- **"told him" → "reporting to him"; "had mingled" → "mixed"** (v1) — Greek ἀπαγγέλλοντες, ἔμιξεν (a plain past).
- **"were sinners above all the Galilaeans" → "were sinners beyond all the Galileans"** (v2; v4 "debtors beyond all the men") — Greek ἁμαρτωλοὶ παρὰ πάντας, "sinners beyond all": no comparative ("worse") and no "other" in the Greek. ἐγένοντο is literally "became / proved to be".
- **"sinners" → "debtors"** (v4) — Greek ὀφειλέται, a different word from ἁμαρτωλοί in v2: those who owe.
- **"I tell you" → "I say to you"** (v3, 5, 24, 35) — Greek λέγω ὑμῖν.
- **"likewise" → "in like manner" / "likewise"** (v3, 5) — two Greek words, ὁμοίως (v3) and ὡσαύτως (v5).
- **"A certain man" → "Someone"** (v6) — Greek τις; no "man".
- **"these three years I come" → "it is three years since I have been coming"** (v7) — Greek τρία ἔτη ἀφʼ οὗ ἔρχομαι. NA28 alone adds οὖν, "Cut it down therefore"; WH, Tregelles and the Byzantine text do not.
- **"why cumbereth it the ground?" → "Why does it even make the earth idle?"** (v7) — Greek ἱνατί καὶ τὴν γῆν καταργεῖ; καταργέω, elsewhere "abolish", is here in its first sense, "make idle, unproductive" (ἀργός "idle"); καί, "even / also": besides bearing nothing itself.
- **"Lord" kept** (v8) — Greek Κύριε, the address of the vinedresser to the owner; the same word as in v23, 25.
- **"dung it" → "cast dung"** (v8) — Greek βάλω κόπρια.
- **"if it bear fruit, well" → "if indeed it makes fruit for the time to come —"** (v9) — Greek κἂν μὲν ποιήσῃ καρπὸν εἰς τὸ μέλλον· the sentence breaks off; "well" is not in the Greek. εἰς τὸ μέλλον, "for the coming [time]", is commonly taken as "next year". "You will cut it down" is a future, not "you can".
- **"a spirit of infirmity" → "a spirit of weakness"** (v11, 12) — Greek ἀσθένεια. The verse has no verb "there was".
- **"could in no wise lift up herself" → "not able to straighten up completely"** (v11) — Greek μὴ δυναμένη ἀνακύψαι εἰς τὸ παντελές. The last words can go with "straighten up" ("completely") or with "not able" ("not able at all").
- **"thou art loosed" → "you have been released"** (v12) — Greek ἀπολέλυσαι, perfect of ἀπολύω ("send away, release"); v15–16 have the simple verb λύω, "loose".
- **"immediately" → "instantly"** (v13) — Greek παραχρῆμα.
- **"ought" → "it is necessary"** (v14, 16, 33) — Greek δεῖ / ἔδει, the same word three times.
- **"lead him away to watering" → "leading it away, give it to drink"** (v15) — Greek ἀπαγαγὼν ποτίζει.
- **"eighteen years" → "ten and eight years"** (v16) — Greek δέκα καὶ ὀκτὼ ἔτη, written out; v4, 11 have δεκαοκτώ.
- **"What is … like" kept; "whereunto shall I resemble it" → "to what shall I liken it"** (v18, 20) — Greek ὁμοία, ὁμοιώσω.
- **"cast into his garden … waxed a great tree … lodged" → "cast into his own garden … became a tree … dwelt"** (v19) — Greek ἔβαλεν εἰς κῆπον ἑαυτοῦ; ἐγένετο εἰς δένδρον (literally "became into a tree"); κατεσκήνωσεν, "tented, dwelt" (Matt 13:32; Mark 4:32). "Great" (μέγα: Tregelles, Byzantine) is not in WH, NA28.
- **"three measures of meal" → "three seahs of flour"** (v21) — Greek σάτα, the Hebrew measure *seah*, as Matt 13:33. Luke has ἔκρυψεν εἰς (WH, Tregelles; NA28 ἐνέκρυψεν as Matthew); no difference in translation.
- **"are there few that be saved?" → "are those who are being saved few?"** (v23) — Greek εἰ ὀλίγοι οἱ σῳζόμενοι; a present participle.
- **"Strive … strait gate … shall not be able" → "Struggle … narrow door … will not have strength"** (v24) — Greek ἀγωνίζεσθε; θύρας "door" (Matt 7:13 πύλης "gate", so the Byzantine text here); οὐκ ἰσχύσουσιν.
- **"When once" → "from the time when"** (v25) — Greek ἀφʼ οὗ ἄν. The printed text (SBLGNT, WH) joins v25 to v24 with a comma; other editions (NA28) begin a new sentence at v25, whose main clause then comes at "and answering, he will say" or at v26.
- **"I know you not whence ye are" → "I do not know you, where you are from"** (v25, 27) — Greek οὐκ οἶδα ὑμᾶς πόθεν ἐστέ. In v27 WH and Tregelles have no ὑμᾶς ("I do not know where you are from"); NA28 and the Byzantine text have it.
- **"he shall say, I tell you" → "he will say, speaking to you"** (v27) — Greek ἐρεῖ λέγων ὑμῖν (WH, NA28); Tregelles and the Byzantine text read ἐρεῖ· Λέγω ὑμῖν, "he will say, 'I say to you'".
- **"workers of iniquity" → "workers of unrighteousness"** (v27) — Greek ἐργάται ἀδικίας (Matt 7:23 has ἀνομία, "lawlessness").
- **"weeping and gnashing of teeth" → "the weeping and the gnashing of teeth"** (v28) — Greek has both articles; ἐκεῖ "there". ἐκβαλλομένους is a present participle, "being cast out".
- **"sit down" → "recline"** (v29) — Greek ἀνακλιθήσονται. The verb has no stated subject ("people" is not in the Greek).
- **"The same day … Get thee out, and depart hence" → "In that very hour … Go out and go from here"** (v31) — Greek ἐν αὐτῇ τῇ ὥρᾳ (WH, NA28; Tregelles and the Byzantine text ἡμέρᾳ, "day"); ἔξελθε καὶ πορεύου.
- **"that fox" → "this fox"; "I do cures" → "I accomplish healings"; "I shall be perfected" → "I am perfected"** (v32) — Greek τῇ ἀλώπεκι ταύτῃ; ἰάσεις ἀποτελῶ; τελειοῦμαι, a present, passive or middle: "I am perfected / brought to the end" or "I finish".
- **"I must walk" → "it is necessary for me to go"; "the day following" → "the day after"; "out of Jerusalem" → "outside Jerusalem"** (v33) — Greek δεῖ με … πορεύεσθαι, the verb of v31 ("go from here") and v32 ("Go and say"); τῇ ἐχομένῃ; ἔξω Ἰερουσαλήμ.
- **"which killest … how often would I have gathered thy children together, as a hen doth gather her brood" → "the one killing … how often I wanted to gather your children together, the way a hen her own brood"** (v34) — Greek ἡ ἀποκτείνουσα … πρὸς αὐτήν, third person ("to her"), then "your children"; no "O" and no "city". Luke has no second verb ("gathers") and τὴν ἑαυτῆς νοσσιάν, "her own brood"; Matt 23:37 has "gathers together her chicks". ἠθέλησα … οὐκ ἠθελήσατε is one verb: "I wanted … you did not want".
- **"your house is left unto you desolate" → "your house is left to you"** (v35) — Greek ἀφίεται ὑμῖν ὁ οἶκος ὑμῶν; "desolate" (ἔρημος, Byzantine text) is not in WH, Tregelles, NA28.
- **"Ye shall not see me, until the time come when ye shall say" → "you shall by no means see me until the time comes when you say"** (v35) — Greek οὐ μή, the strong negation; ἕως ἥξει ὅτε εἴπητε (Tregelles, NA28), literally "until it will come when you say": "the time" is supplied. WH omits ἥξει ὅτε: "until you say".
- **"cities"** (v22) — Greek πόλεις; "making a journey", πορείαν ποιούμενος.

### Notes
- **v35** quotes Ps 118:26.
- **v18–21** — parallels: Matt 13:31–33; Mark 4:30–32. Luke has "cast into his own garden" where Matthew has "sowed in his field".
