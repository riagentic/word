# Luke 21

[1] And looking up, he saw the rich ones casting their gifts into the treasury.

[2] And he saw a certain needy widow casting in there two lepta,

[3] and he said, "Truly I say to you that this poor widow cast in more than all;

[4] for all these out of what abounds to them cast in to the gifts, but she out of her lack cast in all the livelihood that she had."

[5] And as some were saying about the temple that it was adorned with fine stones and votive offerings, he said,

[6] "These things which you behold — days will come in which there will not be left stone upon stone which will not be torn down."

[7] And they asked him, saying, "Teacher, when therefore will these things be, and what is the sign when these things are about to come to pass?"

[8] But he said, "Look, lest you be deceived; for many will come upon my name, saying, 'I am,' and, 'The time has drawn near.' Do not go after them.

[9] But when you hear of wars and disorders, do not be terrified; for these things must come to pass first, but the end is not immediately."

[10] Then he was saying to them, "Nation will arise against nation and kingdom against kingdom,

[11] and there will be great earthquakes and in places famines and pestilences, and there will be terrors and great signs from heaven.

[12] But before all these things they will lay their hands on you and will persecute you, delivering you up to the synagogues and prisons, you being led away before kings and governors for the sake of my name;

[13] it will turn out for you unto a testimony.

[14] Put it therefore in your hearts not to rehearse beforehand how to defend yourselves,

[15] for I will give you a mouth and wisdom which all those opposing you will not be able to resist or contradict.

[16] But you will be delivered up even by parents and brothers and kinsmen and friends, and they will put some of you to death,

[17] and you will be hated by all because of my name.

[18] And a hair of your head shall by no means perish.

[19] **In your endurance acquire your souls.**

[20] "But when you see ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ being encircled by armies, then know that her desolation has drawn near.

[21] Then let those in ⟦judea|Yehudah|Judea⟧ flee into the mountains, and let those in the midst of her go out, and let those in the country places not enter into her,

[22] for these are days of vengeance, that all the things written may be fulfilled.

[23] Woe to those who are with child and to those who are nursing in those days! For there will be great necessity upon the land and wrath to this people,

[24] and they will fall by the mouth of the sword and will be taken captive into all the nations, and ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ will be trampled by nations, until times of nations are fulfilled.

[25] "And there will be signs in sun and moon and stars, and upon the earth anguish of nations in perplexity at the sound of sea and surge,

[26] men fainting from fear and expectation of the things coming upon the inhabited earth; for the powers of the heavens will be shaken.

[27] And then they will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud with power and much glory.

[28] But when these things begin to come to pass, straighten up and lift up your heads, because your redemption is drawing near."

[29] And he spoke a parable to them: "See the fig tree and all the trees;

[30] when they already put forth, seeing it you know of yourselves that the summer is already near;

[31] so also you, when you see these things coming to pass, know that the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is near.

[32] Amen, I say to you that this generation shall by no means pass away until all things come to pass.

[33] **The heaven and the earth will pass away, but my words shall by no means pass away.**

[34] "But give heed to yourselves, lest your hearts be weighed down in dissipation and drunkenness and anxieties pertaining to this life, and that day come upon you sudden

[35] as a snare; for it will come in upon all those sitting upon the face of all the earth.

[36] But keep watch at every time, beseeching that you may prevail to escape all these things that are about to come to pass, and to stand before the Son of Man."

[37] And during the days he was teaching in the temple, and during the nights, going out, he was spending the night on the mount called ⟦mount-of-olives|HaZeitim|Olivet⟧;

[38] and all the people were coming early in the morning to him in the temple to hear him.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v31) — divine name retained.
- **"And he looked up, and saw the rich men casting their gifts into the treasury" → "And looking up, he saw the rich ones casting their gifts into the treasury"** (v1) — Greek ἀναβλέψας δὲ εἶδεν: no name stands in the verse (the earlier "Yeshua" was supplied and is removed); βάλλω "cast" and γαζοφυλάκιον "treasury" as Mark 12:41–44.
- **"a certain poor widow casting in thither two mites" → "a certain needy widow casting in there two lepta"** (v2) — Greek πενιχράν, "needy" (the root of πένης), a different word from πτωχή "poor" in v3; λεπτὰ δύο, two lepta, the smallest copper coin (Mark 12:42).
- **"Of a truth I say unto you, that this poor widow hath cast in more than they all" → "Truly I say to you that this poor widow cast in more than all"** (v3) — Greek ἀληθῶς, "truly" (Mark 12:43 has ἀμήν); ἔβαλεν, a past tense. Editions: αὕτη ἡ πτωχή WH Treg NA28 ] ἡ πτωχὴ αὕτη RP (and the printed SBLGNT): the order only.
- **"For all these have of their abundance cast in unto the offerings of God: but she of her penury hath cast in all the living that she had" → "for all these out of what abounds to them cast in to the gifts, but she out of her lack cast in all the livelihood that she had"** (v4) — Greek ἐκ τοῦ περισσεύοντος αὐτοῖς (as Mark 12:44); εἰς τὰ δῶρα, "into the gifts" ("of God", Treg RP, is not in WH NA28); ὑστερήματος, "lack" (Mark has ὑστερήσεως, of the same root); βίος, "livelihood".
- **"And as some spake of the temple, how it was adorned with goodly stones and gifts" → "And as some were saying about the temple that it was adorned with fine stones and votive offerings"** (v5) — Greek ἱερόν, the whole precinct; καλοῖς, "good, fine"; ἀναθήμασιν, things set up in a temple as dedicated offerings, a different word from δῶρα "gifts" (v1, 4).
- **"As for these things which ye behold, the days will come, in the which there shall not be left one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down" → "These things which you behold — days will come in which there will not be left stone upon stone which will not be torn down"** (v6) — Greek ταῦτα ἃ θεωρεῖτε stands first without a construction, and the sentence starts again: left so, with a dash. λίθος ἐπὶ λίθῳ, "stone upon stone"; καταλυθήσεται, "be torn down" (Mark 13:2; Matt 24:2). Here the negations are the plain οὐκ … οὐ (Mark has οὐ μή twice). "Here" (ὧδε, WH alone) is not in Treg NA28 RP.
- **"Master, but when shall these things be? and what sign will there be when these things shall come to pass?" → "Teacher, when therefore will these things be, and what is the sign when these things are about to come to pass?"** (v7) — Greek διδάσκαλε; οὖν, "therefore"; τί τὸ σημεῖον, without a verb; ὅταν μέλλῃ ταῦτα γίνεσθαι (Mark 13:4 has συντελεῖσθαι "be consummated").
- **"Take heed that ye be not deceived: for many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ; and the time draweth near: go ye not therefore after them" → "Look, lest you be deceived; for many will come upon my name, saying, 'I am,' and, 'The time has drawn near.' Do not go after them"** (v8) — Greek βλέπετε μὴ πλανηθῆτε, a passive (Mark 13:5: "lest anyone deceive you"); ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματί μου, "upon my name" (as Mark 13:6); Ἐγώ εἰμι, "I am" ("Christ" is the KJV's addition from Matt 24:5); ἤγγικεν, a perfect, "has drawn near" (as v20); "therefore" (RP οὖν) is not in the critical text.
- **"wars and commotions, be not terrified: for these things must first come to pass; but the end is not by and by" → "wars and disorders, do not be terrified; for these things must come to pass first, but the end is not immediately"** (v9) — Greek ἀκαταστασίας, "disorders, upheavals" (as 2 Cor 6:5); πτοηθῆτε, "be terrified" (again 24:37; Mark 13:7 has θροεῖσθε "be alarmed"); οὐκ εὐθέως τὸ τέλος has no verb.
- **"Then said he unto them, Nation shall rise against nation" → "Then he was saying to them, 'Nation will arise against nation'"** (v10) — Greek ἔλεγεν, an imperfect; ἐγερθήσεται, as Mark 13:8.
- **"And great earthquakes shall be in divers places, and famines, and pestilences; and fearful sights and great signs shall there be from heaven" → "and there will be great earthquakes and in places famines and pestilences, and there will be terrors and great signs from heaven"** (v11) — Greek κατὰ τόπους, "in places" (Mark 13:8); the critical text puts καί before it (WH Treg NA28; RP after it), so that it goes more naturally with the famines, but the Greek has no comma and "great earthquakes in places, and famines" is possible. λιμοὶ καὶ λοιμοί, two words of nearly one sound (NA28 RP; WH Treg in the opposite order). φόβητρα, "terrors, things that frighten". Editions: ἀπ' οὐρανοῦ σημεῖα μεγάλα WH NA28 ] σημεῖα ἀπ' οὐρανοῦ μεγάλα Treg RP (and the printed SBLGNT): the order only.
- **"they shall lay their hands on you, and persecute you, delivering you up to the synagogues, and into prisons, being brought before kings and rulers for my name's sake" → "they will lay their hands on you and will persecute you, delivering you up to the synagogues and prisons, you being led away before kings and governors for the sake of my name"** (v12) — Greek παραδιδόντες, "delivering up"; ἀπαγομένους (WH Treg NA28), "being led away", a participle that agrees with "you"; ἡγεμόνας, "governors" (Mark 13:9); ἕνεκεν τοῦ ὀνόματός μου.
- **"And it shall turn to you for a testimony" → "it will turn out for you unto a testimony"** (v13) — Greek ἀποβήσεται ὑμῖν εἰς μαρτύριον (the verb of Phil 1:19): it will end in your bearing testimony, or in a testimony for you. "And" (Treg RP δέ) is not in WH NA28.
- **"Settle it therefore in your hearts, not to meditate before what ye shall answer" → "Put it therefore in your hearts not to rehearse beforehand how to defend yourselves"** (v14) — Greek θέτε, "put"; μὴ προμελετᾶν ἀπολογηθῆναι, literally "not to practice beforehand to make a defense" (προμελετάω, the word for rehearsing a speech; ἀπολογέομαι as Acts 26:1): "how" is supplied because English cannot join the two infinitives.
- **"which all your adversaries shall not be able to gainsay nor resist" → "which all those opposing you will not be able to resist or contradict"** (v15) — Greek ἀντιστῆναι ἢ ἀντειπεῖν in this order (WH Treg NA28); οἱ ἀντικείμενοι ὑμῖν, "those opposing you" (as Phil 1:28).
- **"ye shall be betrayed both by parents, and brethren, and kinsfolks, and friends; and some of you shall they cause to be put to death" → "you will be delivered up even by parents and brothers and kinsmen and friends, and they will put some of you to death"** (v16) — Greek παραδοθήσεσθε, "be delivered up"; θανατώσουσιν ἐξ ὑμῶν, literally "they will put to death from among you".
- **"for my name's sake" → "because of my name"** (v17) — Greek διὰ τὸ ὄνομά μου, word for word Mark 13:13.
- **"But there shall not an hair of your head perish" → "And a hair of your head shall by no means perish"** (v18) — Greek καί, "and"; οὐ μὴ ἀπόληται, the strong negation (also v32, 33).
- **"In your patience possess ye your souls" → "In your endurance acquire your souls"** (v19) — Greek ὑπομονή, "endurance"; κτάομαι is "acquire, gain" (Acts 1:18), not "possess"; ψυχάς, "souls". Editions: κτήσασθε NA28 RP, a command, "acquire" ] κτήσεσθε WH Treg, a future, "you will acquire" — 2–2 among the four editions, NA28 decides.
- **"compassed with armies, then know that the desolation thereof is nigh" → "being encircled by armies, then know that her desolation has drawn near"** (v20) — Greek κυκλουμένην, a present participle: the encircling is going on; στρατοπέδων, "encamped armies, camps"; ἤγγικεν, a perfect (as v8); ἐρήμωσις, the word of Mark 13:14.
- **"flee to the mountains; and let them which are in the midst of it depart out; and let not them that are in the countries enter thereinto" → "flee into the mountains, and let those in the midst of her go out, and let those in the country places not enter into her"** (v21) — the first clause is word for word Mark 13:14. αὐτῆς, αὐτήν, "her": most naturally Jerusalem (v20), though by grammar Judea could be meant. ἐν ταῖς χώραις, "in the country districts".
- **"these be the days of vengeance, that all things which are written may be fulfilled" → "these are days of vengeance, that all the things written may be fulfilled"** (v22) — Greek ἡμέραι ἐκδικήσεως, without the article; τοῦ πλησθῆναι, from πίμπλημι "fill" (πληρόω stands in v24).
- **"But woe unto them … for there shall be great distress in the land, and wrath upon this people" → "Woe to those … For there will be great necessity upon the land and wrath to this people"** (v23) — "But" (RP δέ) is not in the critical text. Greek ἀνάγκη, "necessity, constraint", here the hard pressure of distress (as 1 Cor 7:26); ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς, "upon the land" or "upon the earth": γῆ means both, and "this people" speaks for the land (in v25 and v35 the wider sense stands); ὀργὴ τῷ λαῷ τούτῳ, a plain dative ("in", RP, is not in the critical text).
- **"by the edge of the sword, and shall be led away captive into all nations: and Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled" → "by the mouth of the sword and will be taken captive into all the nations, and Jerusalem will be trampled by nations, until times of nations are fulfilled"** (v24) — Greek στόματι μαχαίρης, "mouth of the sword", the Hebrew idiom (as Heb 11:34); αἰχμαλωτισθήσονται; τὰ ἔθνη, ἐθνῶν, ἐθνῶν: one word three times, "nations" (the peoples other than Israel); the last two have no article. καιροί, "times, seasons" (Acts 1:7). WH alone adds καὶ ἔσονται, "and they will be".
- **"signs in the sun, and in the moon, and in the stars; and upon the earth distress of nations, with perplexity; the sea and the waves roaring" → "signs in sun and moon and stars, and upon the earth anguish of nations in perplexity at the sound of sea and surge"** (v25) — the Greek has no articles; συνοχή, "anguish, a being hemmed in" (as 2 Cor 2:4); ἐν ἀπορίᾳ, "at a loss" (the noun of ἀπορέω); ἤχους θαλάσσης καὶ σάλου (WH Treg NA28), "of the sound of sea and tossing"; RP has ἠχούσης, "the sea and surge roaring", which the KJV follows.
- **"Men's hearts failing them for fear, and for looking after those things which are coming on the earth" → "men fainting from fear and expectation of the things coming upon the inhabited earth"** (v26) — Greek ἀποψυχόντων ἀνθρώπων, "breathing out", that is, fainting or expiring ("hearts" is not in the Greek); προσδοκίας; τῇ οἰκουμένῃ, "the inhabited earth".
- **"with power and great glory" → "with power and much glory"** (v27) — Greek μετὰ δυνάμεως καὶ δόξης πολλῆς, as Matt 24:30 (Mark 13:26: "with much power and glory"); ἐν νεφέλῃ, "in a cloud", singular (Mark: "in clouds").
- **"And when these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh" → "But when these things begin to come to pass, straighten up and lift up your heads, because your redemption is drawing near"** (v28) — Greek ἀνακύψατε, "straighten up, unbend" (as John 8:7); ἐγγίζει.
- **"Behold the fig tree" → "See the fig tree"** (v29) — Greek ἴδετε.
- **"When they now shoot forth, ye see and know of your own selves that summer is now nigh at hand" → "when they already put forth, seeing it you know of yourselves that the summer is already near"** (v30) — Greek προβάλωσιν, "put forth", without an object; ἤδη, "already", twice; βλέποντες ἀφ' ἑαυτῶν γινώσκετε: "it" is supplied for English.
- **"So likewise ye, when ye see these things come to pass, know ye that the kingdom of God is nigh at hand" → "so also you, when you see these things coming to pass, know that the kingdom of Elohim is near"** (v31) — word for word Mark 13:29 as far as "near"; γινώσκετε is "you know" or "know!".
- **"Verily I say unto you, This generation shall not pass away, till all be fulfilled" → "Amen, I say to you that this generation shall by no means pass away until all things come to pass"** (v32) — Greek ἀμήν, left untranslated; οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ; ἕως ἂν πάντα γένηται, "come to pass" (not "be fulfilled").
- **"Heaven and earth shall pass away: but my words shall not pass away" → "The heaven and the earth will pass away, but my words shall by no means pass away"** (v33) — word for word Mark 13:31: both nouns with the article, οὐ μή.
- **"And take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, and drunkenness, and cares of this life, and so that day come upon you unawares. For as a snare shall it come on all them that dwell on the face of the whole earth" → "But give heed to yourselves, lest your hearts be weighed down in dissipation and drunkenness and anxieties pertaining to this life, and that day come upon you sudden as a snare; for it will come in upon all those sitting upon the face of all the earth"** (v34–35) — Greek προσέχετε ἑαυτοῖς (as Acts 5:35; 20:28); βαρηθῶσιν, "be weighed down"; κραιπάλη, the giddiness and headache of a drinking bout; μερίμναις βιωτικαῖς, "anxieties of this life" (βιωτικός as 1 Cor 6:3); αἰφνίδιος, an adjective, "sudden" (1 Thess 5:3). The critical text (WH Treg NA28) reads ὡς παγίς· ἐπεισελεύσεται γάρ, so "as a snare" ends the sentence of v34, though the words are counted to v35; RP has ὡς παγὶς γὰρ ἐπελεύσεται, "for as a snare it will come", which the KJV follows. καθημένους, "sitting", that is, dwelling; πάσης τῆς γῆς, "all the earth" or "all the land".
- **"Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass" → "But keep watch at every time, beseeching that you may prevail to escape all these things that are about to come to pass"** (v36) — Greek ἀγρυπνεῖτε, "keep watch" (Mark 13:33); δέ, "but" (WH Treg NA28; RP οὖν); ἐν παντὶ καιρῷ (as Eph 6:18), which may go with "keep watch" or with "beseeching"; δεόμενοι, "beseeching"; κατισχύσητε (WH Treg NA28), "have the strength, prevail"; RP has καταξιωθῆτε, "be accounted worthy". μέλλοντα γίνεσθαι, as v7.
- **"And in the day time he was teaching in the temple; and at night he went out, and abode in the mount that is called the mount of Olives" → "And during the days he was teaching in the temple, and during the nights, going out, he was spending the night on the mount called Olivet"** (v37) — Greek τὰς ἡμέρας … τὰς δὲ νύκτας, plurals; ηὐλίζετο, an imperfect, "was spending the night" (Matt 21:17); εἰς τὸ ὄρος, literally "unto the mount"; Ἐλαιῶν as printed is "of Olives"; with another accent (Ἐλαιών) it is the name "Olivet" (so Acts 1:12).
- **"came early in the morning" → "were coming early in the morning"** (v38) — Greek ὤρθριζεν, an imperfect: "rose at dawn to come".

### Notes
- **v1–4** has its parallel in Mark 12:41–44; **v5–33** in Mark 13 and Matt 24.
- **v27** echoes Dan 7:13.
