# Luke 18

[1] And he was telling them a parable, to the end that they must always pray and not grow weary,

[2] saying, "There was a certain judge in a certain city, not fearing ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and not respecting man.

[3] And there was a widow in that city, and she was coming to him, saying, 'Avenge me of my adversary.'

[4] And he was not willing for a time; but after these things he said within himself, 'Even if I do not fear ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ nor respect man,

[5] yet because this widow gives me trouble, I will avenge her, lest coming to the end she bruise me.'"

[6] And the Lord said, "Hear what the judge of unrighteousness says;

[7] and will ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ not indeed work the vengeance of his elect, who cry to him day and night, and is he patient over them?

[8] I say to you that he will work their vengeance shortly. Yet the Son of Man, having come, will he indeed find the faith on the earth?"

[9] And he said also to some who were confident in themselves that they are righteous, and who despised the rest, this parable:

[10] "Two men went up into the temple to pray, the one a ⟦pharisee|Parush|Pharisee⟧ and the other a toll collector.

[11] The ⟦pharisee|Parush|Pharisee⟧, standing, was praying these things to himself: '⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, I give thanks to you that I am not just as the rest of men, extortioners, unrighteous, adulterers, or even as this toll collector.

[12] I fast twice in the week; I tithe all things, whatever I acquire.'

[13] But the toll collector, standing from afar, was not willing even to lift up his eyes to heaven, but was striking his breast, saying, '⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, be propitiated toward me, the sinner!'

[14] I say to you, this one went down to his house justified, rather than that one. **For everyone who lifts himself up will be humbled, but the one who humbles himself will be lifted up.**"

[15] And they were bringing to him also the babies, that he might touch them; but the disciples, seeing it, were rebuking them.

[16] But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ called them to himself, saying, "Let the young children come to me, and do not hinder them, for of such is the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[17] Amen, I say to you, whoever does not accept the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ as a young child will by no means enter into it."

[18] And a certain ruler asked him, saying, "Good Teacher, by doing what shall I inherit eternal life?"

[19] But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to him, "Why do you call me good? No one is good except one, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[20] You know the commandments: '**Do not commit adultery, Do not murder, Do not steal, Do not bear false witness, Honor your father and mother**.'"

[21] But he said, "All these things I guarded from youth."

[22] And hearing it, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to him, "Yet one thing is wanting to you: sell all things, whatever you have, and distribute to the poor, and you will have treasure in the heavens; and come, follow me."

[23] But he, hearing these things, became deeply grieved, for he was exceedingly rich.

[24] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, seeing him become deeply grieved, said, "With what difficulty do those who have wealth go into the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧!

[25] For it is easier for a camel to enter through the hole of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧."

[26] And those who heard said, "And who is able to be saved?"

[27] But he said, "The things impossible with men are possible with ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧."

[28] And ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ said, "Behold, we, having left our own things, followed you."

[29] And he said to them, "Amen, I say to you that there is no one who left house or wife or brothers or parents or children for the sake of the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[30] who will not assuredly receive back many times as much in this time, and in the age that is coming eternal life."

[31] And taking along the twelve, he said to them, "Behold, we are going up to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, and all the things written through the prophets for the Son of Man will be finished;

[32] for he will be delivered up to the nations and will be mocked and treated shamefully and spit on,

[33] and having scourged him they will kill him, and on the third day he will rise."

[34] And they understood none of these things, and this word was hidden from them, and they were not knowing the things being said.

[35] And it came to pass, as he was drawing near to ⟦jericho|Yericho|Jericho⟧, that a certain blind man was sitting beside the way, begging.

[36] And hearing a crowd going through, he was inquiring what this might be.

[37] And they reported to him, "⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ the ⟦nazarenes|Notzri|Nazorean⟧ is passing by."

[38] And he cried, saying, "⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, Son of David, have mercy on me!"

[39] And those going before were rebuking him, that he should be silent; but he was crying out much more, "Son of David, have mercy on me!"

[40] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, standing still, commanded him to be brought to him. And when he had drawn near, he asked him,

[41] "What do you want me to do for you?" And he said, "Lord, that I may receive sight."

[42] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to him, "Receive sight; your faith has saved you."

[43] And instantly he received sight, and he was following him, glorifying ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧. And all the people, seeing it, gave praise to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv2, 4, 7, 11, 13, 16, 17, 19, 24, 25, 27, 29, 43) — divine name retained throughout.
- **"Peter" → "*Kefa*"** (v28) — Aramaic name preserved.
- **"he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint" → "he was telling them a parable, to the end that they must always pray and not grow weary"** (v1) — Greek ἔλεγεν, an imperfect; πρὸς τὸ δεῖν πάντοτε προσεύχεσθαι αὐτούς, "with a view to its being necessary that they always pray": "they" (αὐτούς, WH Treg NA28), the disciples of 17:22, not "men"; ἐγκακεῖν, "grow weary, lose heart" (as 2 Thess 3:13).
- **"a judge, which feared not God, neither regarded man" → "a certain judge … not fearing *Elohim* and not respecting man"** (v2, 4) — Greek κριτής τις … ἔν τινι πόλει, "a certain … in a certain"; ἐντρεπόμενος, "respecting" (ἐντρέπομαι, as Mark 12:6).
- **"she came unto him, saying, Avenge me of mine adversary" kept as "she was coming to him, saying, 'Avenge me of my adversary'"** (v3) — Greek ἤρχετο, an imperfect: she kept coming. ἐκδίκησόν με ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀντιδίκου μου: ἐκδικέω is "avenge" (so v5), and ἐκδίκησις in v7, 8 is "vengeance" (as Acts 7:24); here the sense is that of a court: "do me justice, get me my right from my opponent at law" (ἀντίδικος, as Matt 5:25).
- **"he would not for a while: but afterward" → "he was not willing for a time; but after these things"** (v4) — Greek οὐκ ἤθελεν, an imperfect; ἐπὶ χρόνον; μετὰ ταῦτα.
- **"because this widow troubleth me, I will avenge her, lest by her continual coming she weary me" → "because this widow gives me trouble, I will avenge her, lest coming to the end she bruise me"** (v5) — Greek παρέχειν μοι κόπον, "give me trouble" (as Mark 14:6). εἰς τέλος ἐρχομένη, "coming to the end": either "coming without end, continually" or "in the end, at last" (with "bruise"); the Greek allows both. ὑπωπιάζῃ, literally "strike under the eye, give a black eye" (the verb of 1 Cor 9:27, "bruise"): here of wearing a man down, or of blackening his name.
- **"the unjust judge" → "the judge of unrighteousness"** (v6) — Greek ὁ κριτὴς τῆς ἀδικίας, a genitive (as 16:8, 9).
- **"shall not God avenge his own elect, which cry day and night unto him, though he bear long with them?" → "will *Elohim* not indeed work the vengeance of his elect, who cry to him day and night, and is he patient over them?"** (v7) — Greek οὐ μὴ ποιήσῃ τὴν ἐκδίκησιν, the strong negation οὐ μή in a question that expects "yes, he will"; ποιέω ἐκδίκησιν as Acts 7:24. βοώντων, "crying" (βοάω). καὶ μακροθυμεῖ ἐπ' αὐτοῖς (WH Treg NA28, a present indicative; RP has the participle μακροθυμῶν): μακροθυμέω is "be patient". The clause is printed as part of the question and its sense is disputed: "and is he patient (slow to act) over them?", or "and he is patient with them", or "though he is patient over them" (so the KJV, from the RP reading). The verse keeps the plain words.
- **"he will avenge them speedily. Nevertheless when the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?" → "he will work their vengeance shortly. Yet the Son of Man, having come, will he indeed find the faith on the earth?"** (v8) — Greek ἐν τάχει, "shortly, with speed" (as Rom 16:20); πλήν, "yet"; ἐλθών; ἆρα, a particle that marks a question asked in doubt; τὴν πίστιν, with the article: "the faith" or simply "faith".
- **"unto certain which trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others" → "to some who were confident in themselves that they are righteous, and who despised the rest"** (v9) — Greek τοὺς πεποιθότας ἐφ' ἑαυτοῖς (πέποιθα, "be confident"); εἰσίν, a present; ἐξουθενοῦντας τοὺς λοιπούς, "despising the rest" (so "the rest of men", v11). καί, "also" (WH Treg NA28).
- **"publican" → "toll collector"** (v10, 11, 13) — Greek τελώνης, from τέλος "toll": a collector of tolls and customs.
- **"The Pharisee stood and prayed thus with himself, God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are … unjust" → "The *Parush*, standing, was praying these things to himself: '*Elohim*, I give thanks to you that I am not just as the rest of men … unrighteous'"** (v11) — Greek σταθεὶς πρὸς ἑαυτὸν ταῦτα προσηύχετο (NA28 RP; WH Treg ταῦτα πρὸς ἑαυτόν — 2–2 among the four editions, NA28 decides). In the order of the verse πρὸς ἑαυτόν can go with "standing" ("standing by himself") or with "was praying" ("to himself, with himself"); the other order allows only the second. εὐχαριστῶ, "I give thanks"; ὥσπερ, "just as", then ὡς, "as"; ἄδικοι, "unrighteous"; ἅρπαγες, "extortioners" (from ἁρπάζω "seize").
- **"I give tithes of all that I possess" → "I tithe all things, whatever I acquire"** (v12) — Greek ἀποδεκατῶ πάντα ὅσα κτῶμαι: κτάομαι is "acquire, get" (as Matt 10:9), not "possess". δὶς τοῦ σαββάτου, "twice in the week": σάββατον here means the week.
- **"standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner" → "standing from afar, was not willing even to lift up his eyes to heaven, but was striking his breast, saying, '*Elohim*, be propitiated toward me, the sinner!'"** (v13) — Greek μακρόθεν ἑστώς; οὐκ ἤθελεν, ἔτυπτε, imperfects (τύπτω "strike"). ἱλάσθητί μοι: the passive of ἱλάσκομαι, "make propitiation for" (Heb 2:17; the root of ἱλασμός "propitiation" and ἱλαστήριον, the mercy seat): "be propitiated, be made propitious toward me". It is not ἐλέησον, "have mercy" (v38, 39). τῷ ἁμαρτωλῷ, with the article: "the sinner".
- **"this man went down to his house justified rather than the other: for every one that exalteth himself shall be abased … shall be exalted" → "this one went down to his house justified, rather than that one. For everyone who lifts himself up will be humbled … will be lifted up"** (v14) — Greek δεδικαιωμένος, a perfect participle of δικαιόω "justify": "having been justified"; παρ' ἐκεῖνον (WH Treg NA28), "beside, beyond that one": rather than he. ὑψόω, "lift up", ταπεινόω, "humble" (the same words as 14:11; Matt 23:12).
- **"they brought unto him also infants, that he would touch them: but when his disciples saw it, they rebuked them" → "they were bringing to him also the babies, that he might touch them; but the disciples, seeing it, were rebuking them"** (v15) — Greek προσέφερον, ἐπετίμων, imperfects; τὰ βρέφη, "the babies" (as Acts 7:19), where Mark 10:13 has παιδία; in v16, 17 Luke too has παιδία, "young children".
- **"called them unto him, and said, Suffer little children to come unto me, and forbid them not" → "called them to himself, saying, 'Let the young children come to me, and do not hinder them'"** (v16) — Greek προσεκαλέσατο αὐτά: "them" is neuter, the babies. ἄφετε, "let"; κωλύετε, "hinder". The words of Yeshua are those of Mark 10:14, with "and".
- **"Verily I say unto you, Whosoever shall not receive … as a little child shall in no wise enter therein" → "Amen, I say to you, whoever does not accept … as a young child will by no means enter into it"** (v17) — Greek ἀμήν, left untranslated; δέξηται, "accept" (δέχομαι); οὐ μὴ εἰσέλθῃ. Word for word Mark 10:15.
- **"Good Master, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?" → "Good Teacher, by doing what shall I inherit eternal life?"** (v18) — Greek διδάσκαλε, "Teacher"; τί ποιήσας … κληρονομήσω, literally "having done what shall I inherit" (as 10:25; Mark 10:17 has τί ποιήσω ἵνα, "what shall I do that"). ἄρχων, "ruler".
- **"none is good, save one, that is, God" → "No one is good except one, *Elohim*"** (v19) — Greek οὐδεὶς ἀγαθὸς εἰ μὴ εἷς ὁ θεός, as Mark 10:18: "except one, God" or "except the one God".
- **"Do not kill" → "Do not murder"; "thy father and thy mother" → "your father and mother"** (v20) — Greek φονεύσῃς; the second "thy" (RP σου) is not in the critical text. Luke names adultery first, then murder (Exod 20:13–14 has the other order in the Hebrew).
- **"All these have I kept from my youth up" → "All these things I guarded from youth"** (v21) — Greek ἐφύλαξα, "guarded" (φυλάσσω). Editions: μου Treg RP (and the printed SBLGNT), "from my youth" ] – WH NA28 — 2–2 among the four editions, NA28 decides: "from youth".
- **"when Jesus heard these things, he said unto him, Yet lackest thou one thing: sell all that thou hast … treasure in heaven" → "hearing it, *Yeshua* said to him, 'Yet one thing is wanting to you: sell all things, whatever you have … treasure in the heavens'"** (v22) — "these things" (RP) is not in the critical text; "it" is supplied. Greek ἔτι ἕν σοι λείπει, "still one thing is wanting to you" (Mark 10:21 has ἕν σε ὑστερεῖ); πάντα ὅσα ἔχεις; διάδος, "distribute" (as Acts 4:35; Mark has δός, "give"). Editions: οὐρανοῖς SBLGNT (WH margin) ] τοῖς οὐρανοῖς WH Treg NA28; οὐρανῷ RP — "in the heavens", plural with the article.
- **"he was very sorrowful: for he was very rich" → "became deeply grieved, for he was exceedingly rich"** (v23) — Greek περίλυπος ἐγενήθη (περίλυπος as Mark 6:26; 14:34); πλούσιος σφόδρα.
- **"when Jesus saw that he was very sorrowful, he said, How hardly shall they that have riches enter" → "*Yeshua*, seeing him become deeply grieved, said, 'With what difficulty do those who have wealth go into'"** (v24) — Editions: Ἰησοῦς WH (and the printed SBLGNT), "seeing him, Yeshua said" ] + περίλυπον γενόμενον Treg NA28 RP — the verse follows the majority. Greek πῶς δυσκόλως, as Mark 10:23; εἰσπορεύονται, "go in", a present (WH Treg NA28; Mark and RP have the future εἰσελεύσονται, "will enter"); χρήματα, "wealth".
- **"to go through a needle's eye" → "to enter through the hole of a needle"** (v25) — Greek διὰ τρήματος βελόνης εἰσελθεῖν (WH Treg NA28): τρῆμα, "hole, something bored through", and βελόνη, "needle", are Luke's own words (Mark 10:25 τρυμαλιά, ῥαφίς; Matt 19:24 τρύπημα, ῥαφίς); εἰσελθεῖν, "enter", stands twice. γάρ, "for".
- **"Who then can be saved?" → "And who is able to be saved?"** (v26) — Greek καὶ τίς δύναται σωθῆναι, as Mark 10:26.
- **"The things which are impossible with men" kept as "The things impossible with men"** (v27) — Greek τὰ ἀδύνατα παρὰ ἀνθρώποις, plural: "with men", not "with man".
- **"Lo, we have left all, and followed thee" → "Behold, we, having left our own things, followed you"** (v28) — Greek ἀφέντες τὰ ἴδια (WH Treg NA28), "having left the things that are our own" — one's own home and goods (as Acts 21:6); RP has "we left all", from Mark 10:28. ἠκολουθήσαμεν, a simple past.
- **"that hath left house, or parents, or brethren, or wife, or children" → "who left house or wife or brothers or parents or children"** (v29) — Greek ἀφῆκεν; the order of WH NA28 (Treg RP: parents, brothers, wife).
- **"Who shall not receive manifold more in this present time, and in the world to come life everlasting" → "who will not assuredly receive back many times as much in this time, and in the age that is coming eternal life"** (v30) — Greek ὃς οὐχὶ μὴ ἀπολάβῃ: οὐχί with the strong negation μή, literally "who shall by no means not receive back"; ἀπολαμβάνω, "receive back" (Treg NA28 RP; WH λάβῃ, "receive"); πολλαπλασίονα, "many times as much" (Mark: "a hundredfold"); ἐν τῷ αἰῶνι τῷ ἐρχομένῳ, "in the age that is coming"; αἰώνιον, "eternal".
- **"he took unto him the twelve … all things that are written by the prophets concerning the Son of man shall be accomplished" → "taking along the twelve … all the things written through the prophets for the Son of Man will be finished"** (v31) — Greek παραλαβών (as Mark 10:32); τελεσθήσεται, "will be finished, brought to their end" (τελέω, as John 19:30); διὰ τῶν προφητῶν, "through the prophets"; τῷ υἱῷ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου, a plain dative, "for / to the Son of Man": it can go with "written" (written for him, about him) or with "will be finished" (will be carried out on him). The verse keeps the Greek order.
- **"delivered unto the Gentiles … spitefully entreated, and spitted on" → "delivered up to the nations … treated shamefully and spit on"** (v32) — Greek τοῖς ἔθνεσιν, "the nations"; ὑβρισθήσεται (ὑβρίζω, as 1 Thess 2:2), a word Mark and Matthew do not have here. All three verbs are passives; Mark 10:34 has them as actives.
- **"they shall scourge him, and put him to death: and the third day he shall rise again" → "having scourged him they will kill him, and on the third day he will rise"** (v33) — Greek μαστιγώσαντες ἀποκτενοῦσιν αὐτόν; τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ ἀναστήσεται (Mark 10:34: "after three days").
- **"this saying was hid from them, neither knew they the things which were spoken" → "this word was hidden from them, and they were not knowing the things being said"** (v34) — Greek τὸ ῥῆμα τοῦτο, "this word" (ῥῆμα, the thing said; not λόγος); οὐκ ἐγίνωσκον, an imperfect; τὰ λεγόμενα, a present participle.
- **"as he was come nigh unto Jericho, a certain blind man sat by the way side begging" → "as he was drawing near to *Yericho*, that a certain blind man was sitting beside the way, begging"** (v35) — Greek ἐν τῷ ἐγγίζειν αὐτόν; ἐκάθητο, an imperfect; παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν; ἐπαιτῶν (WH Treg NA28). Mark 10:46 sets the healing at the going out from Jericho.
- **"hearing the multitude pass by, he asked what it meant" → "hearing a crowd going through, he was inquiring what this might be"** (v36) — Greek ὄχλου διαπορευομένου, without the article; ἐπυνθάνετο (πυνθάνομαι, "inquire"); τί εἴη τοῦτο.
- **"they told him, that Jesus of Nazareth passeth by" → "they reported to him, '*Yeshua* the Nazorean is passing by'"** (v37) — Greek ἀπήγγειλαν, "reported"; Ἰησοῦς ὁ Ναζωραῖος, "Yeshua the Nazorean" (the form of Matt 2:23; Acts 2:22; Mark 10:47 has Ναζαρηνός, "Nazarene"); the place name is not in the verse.
- **"he cried, saying, Jesus, thou Son of David" kept as "he cried, saying, '*Yeshua*, Son of David'"** (v38) — Greek ἐβόησεν (βοάω, "cry", the verb of v7); in v39 ἔκραζεν (κράζω, "cry out"), an imperfect.
- **"they which went before rebuked him, that he should hold his peace: but he cried so much the more" → "those going before were rebuking him, that he should be silent; but he was crying out much more"** (v39) — Greek οἱ προάγοντες; ἐπετίμων, an imperfect; σιγήσῃ (σιγάω, WH Treg NA28; Mark 10:48 has σιωπήσῃ; both are "be silent"); πολλῷ μᾶλλον ἔκραζεν, as Mark.
- **"Jesus stood, and commanded him to be brought unto him: and when he was come near" → "*Yeshua*, standing still, commanded him to be brought to him. And when he had drawn near"** (v40) — Greek σταθείς (the form of v11); ἐκέλευσεν, "commanded"; ἀχθῆναι, "to be led, brought"; ἐγγίσαντος, "having drawn near" (as v35).
- **"Saying, What wilt thou that I shall do unto thee? … that I may receive my sight" → "'What do you want me to do for you?' … 'that I may receive sight'"** (v41) — "saying" (Treg RP) is not in WH NA28. Greek τί σοι θέλεις ποιήσω, as Mark 10:51; κύριε, "Lord" (Mark: "Rabbouni"); ἵνα ἀναβλέψω: ἀναβλέπω, "look up", of a blind man "see again, receive sight"; "my" is not in the Greek (so v42, 43).
- **"thy faith hath saved thee" kept as "your faith has saved you"** (v42) — Greek ἡ πίστις σου σέσωκέν σε, as 17:19 and Mark 10:52: σῴζω, "save", said here to a man who receives his sight; the Greek has one word for "save" and "make well".
- **"immediately he received his sight, and followed him" → "instantly he received sight, and he was following him"** (v43) — Greek παραχρῆμα, "instantly" (Mark: εὐθύς, "immediately"); ἠκολούθει, an imperfect; ἔδωκεν αἶνον, "gave praise".
