# Luke 6

[1] Now it came to pass on a ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ that he was going through grainfields, and his disciples were plucking and eating the ears, rubbing them with their hands.

[2] But some of the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ said, "Why are you doing what is not permitted on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧?"

[3] And answering them, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said, "Did you not read even this, what David did when he was hungry, he and those who were with him:

[4] how he entered into the house of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and taking the loaves of the setting forth he ate, and gave to those with him, which it is not permitted to eat except for the priests alone?"

[5] And he was saying to them, "**The Son of Man is lord of the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧.**"

[6] And it came to pass on another ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ that he entered into the synagogue and was teaching; and there was a man there, and his right hand was withered.

[7] And the scribes and the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ were watching him closely, whether he heals on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧, that they might find how to accuse him.

[8] But he knew their reasonings, and he said to the man having the withered hand, "Arise and stand in the midst." And rising, he stood.

[9] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to them, "I ask you whether it is permitted on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ to do good or to do evil, to save a soul or to destroy?"

[10] And looking around at them all, he said to him, "Stretch out your hand." And he did, and his hand was restored.

[11] But they were filled with folly, and were talking over with one another what they might do to ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[12] And it came to pass in these days that he went out into the mountain to pray, and he was passing the whole night in the prayer of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[13] And when day came, he called to his disciples, and choosing from them twelve, whom he also named apostles:

[14] ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧, whom he also named ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, and ⟦andrew|Andreas|Andrew⟧ his brother, and ⟦james|Yaaqov|James⟧ and ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧, and ⟦philip|Philippos|Philip⟧ and ⟦bartholomew|Bar-Talmai|Bartholomew⟧,

[15] and ⟦matthew|Mattai|Matthew⟧ and ⟦thomas|Toma|Thomas⟧, and ⟦james|Yaaqov|James⟧ the son of ⟦alphaeus|Alphaios|Alphaeus⟧, and ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ the one called the Zealot,

[16] and ⟦jude|Yehudah|Judas⟧ the son of ⟦james|Yaaqov|James⟧, and ⟦judas|Yehudah|Judas⟧ Iscariot, who became a traitor —

[17] and coming down with them, he stood on a level place, and a great crowd of his disciples, and a great multitude of the people from all ⟦judea|Yehudah|Judea⟧ and ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ and the seacoast of ⟦tyre|Tzor|Tyre⟧ and ⟦sidon|Tzidon|Sidon⟧, who came to hear him and to be healed from their diseases;

[18] and those harassed by unclean spirits were being healed.

[19] And all the crowd were seeking to touch him, for power was coming out from him and healing all.

[20] And he, lifting up his eyes upon his disciples, was saying:

"Blessed are the poor, for yours is the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.
[21] Blessed are those who hunger now, for you shall be filled.
Blessed are those who weep now, for you shall laugh.
[22] Blessed are you when men hate you, and when they separate you and reproach you and cast out your name as evil, on account of the Son of Man.

[23] Rejoice in that day and leap, for behold, your wage is great in heaven; for in the same way their fathers were doing to the prophets.

[24] Nevertheless woe to you, the rich, for you have your comfort in full.
[25] Woe to you, those who have been filled up now, for you shall hunger.
Woe, those who laugh now, for you shall mourn and weep.
[26] Woe, when all men speak well of you, for in the same way their fathers were doing to the false prophets."

[27] "But to you who hear I say: **Love your enemies, do well to those who hate you,**

[28] bless those who curse you, pray for those who abuse you.

[29] To the one who strikes you upon the cheek, offer the other also; and from the one who takes away your garment, do not withhold the tunic also.

[30] To everyone who asks you, give; and from the one who takes away what is yours, do not ask it back.

**[31] And just as you want that men should do to you, do to them likewise.**

[32] "And if you love those who love you, what grace is it to you? For even the sinners love those who love them.

[33] For even if you do good to those who do good to you, what grace is it to you? Even the sinners do the same.

[34] And if you lend to those from whom you hope to receive, what grace is it to you? Even sinners lend to sinners, that they may receive back the equal amount.

[35] Nevertheless love your enemies, and do good, and lend, hoping for nothing back; and your wage will be great, and you will be sons of the Most High, for he himself is kind toward the ungrateful and evil.

[36] Become compassionate, just as your Father also is compassionate.

[37] "**And do not judge, and you will by no means be judged; and do not condemn, and you will by no means be condemned. Release, and you will be released;**

**[38] give, and it will be given to you: a good measure, pressed down, shaken, running over, they will give into your bosom. For with what measure you measure, it will be measured back to you.**"

[39] And he also spoke a parable to them: "Is a blind man able to guide a blind man? Will they not both fall into a pit?

[40] A disciple is not above the teacher; but everyone, having been made complete, will be as his teacher.

[41] And why do you see the speck that is in your brother's eye, but the beam that is in your own eye you do not observe?

[42] How are you able to say to your brother, 'Brother, let me cast out the speck that is in your eye,' yourself not seeing the beam in your eye? Hypocrite, first cast the beam out of your eye, and then you will see clearly to cast out the speck that is in your brother's eye.

[43] "For there is no good tree making rotten fruit, nor again a rotten tree making good fruit;

[44] for each tree is known from its own fruit. For they do not collect figs from thorns, nor do they gather grapes from a bramble bush.

[45] The good man out of the good treasure of the heart brings forth the good, and the evil one out of the evil brings forth the evil; for out of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaks.

[46] "And why do you call me 'Lord, Lord,' and do not do the things which I say?

[47] Everyone who comes to me and hears my words and does them — I will show you whom he is like:

[48] he is like a man building a house, who dug and went deep and laid a foundation upon the rock; and when a flood came, the river broke against that house, and did not have strength to shake it, because it had been built well.

[49] But the one who heard and did not do is like a man who built a house upon the earth without a foundation, against which the river broke, and immediately it collapsed, and the breaking of that house became great."

## Changes

- **"James" → "Jacob"** (v14, 15, 16) — Greek Ἰάκωβος *Iakōbos* = Hebrew יַעֲקֹב *Yaaqov*, the same name as the patriarch; "James" is a later English form.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv4, 12, 20) — divine name retained.
- **"Simon, whom he also named Peter" → "Simon, whom he also named *Kefa*"** (v14) — Aramaic name *Kefa* preserved.
- **"on the second sabbath after the first" → "on a *Shabbat*"** (v1) — the text is ἐν σαββάτῳ (WH Treg NA28); only RP adds δευτεροπρώτῳ "second-first".
- **"corn" → "grainfields … ears"** (v1) — Greek σπόριμα; στάχυας; see Mark 2:23.
- **"not lawful" → "not permitted"** (v2, 4, 9) — Greek ἔξεστιν. "to do" (KJV, v2) is RP only.
- **"Have ye not read so much as this" → "Did you not read even this"** (v3) — Greek οὐδὲ τοῦτο ἀνέγνωτε, aorist. The verse follows ὅτε (WH Treg NA28; RP ὁπότε), with no difference in meaning.
- **"shewbread" → "loaves of the setting forth"** (v4) — Greek οἱ ἄρτοι τῆς προθέσεως, as Mark 2:26; Heb 9:2.
- **"watched him" → "were watching him closely"** (v7) — Greek παρετηροῦντο αὐτόν (WH NA28; Treg RP without "him"), as Mark 3:2. "whether he heals" is present in Greek.
- **"find an accusation against him" → "find how to accuse him"** (v7) — Greek ἵνα εὕρωσιν κατηγορεῖν αὐτοῦ, literally "that they might find to accuse him"; "how" is supplied for English.
- **"thoughts" → "reasonings"** (v8) — Greek διαλογισμοί, as 5:22.
- **"Rise up, and stand forth in the midst" → "Arise and stand in the midst"** (v8) — Greek ἔγειρε καὶ στῆθι εἰς τὸ μέσον; Mark 3:3 has only "Arise into the midst".
- **"to save life, or to destroy it" → "to save a soul or to destroy"** (v9) — Greek ψυχὴν σῶσαι ἢ ἀπολέσαι (WH Treg NA28; RP ἀποκτεῖναι "to kill", as Mark 3:4). The second verb has no object.
- **"whole as the other" not in the text** (v10) — WH NA28; Treg adds "as the other", RP "healthy as the other".
- **"madness" → "folly"** (v11) — Greek ἄνοια, "want of understanding" (2 Tim 3:9), not "fury".
- **"communed" → "were talking over"** (v11) — Greek διελάλουν.
- **"in prayer to God" → "in the prayer of *Elohim*"** (v12) — Greek ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ τοῦ θεοῦ, a plain genitive; the sense is prayer to *Elohim*.
- **"he chose twelve"** (v13–17) — in Greek "choosing" is a participle and the sentence has no main verb until "he stood" in v17; the dash after v16 keeps that.
- **"the son of Alphaeus" / "the son of *Yaaqov*"** (v15, 16) — Greek has only the genitive (Ἰάκωβον Ἁλφαίου, Ἰούδαν Ἰακώβου); "the son" is supplied, as in Acts 1:13. For the second some understand "brother of".
- **"called Zelotes" → "the one called the Zealot"** (v15) — Greek τὸν καλούμενον Ζηλωτήν; Mark 3:18 and Matt 10:4 have "the Cananaean".
- **"which also was the traitor" → "who became a traitor"** (v16) — Greek ὃς ἐγένετο προδότης (WH NA28; Treg RP + καί "also"). Mark 3:19: "who also delivered him up".
- **"the company of his disciples" → "a great crowd of his disciples"** (v17) — Greek ὄχλος πολύς (WH NA28; Treg RP without "great").
- **"vexed with" → "harassed by"; "they were healed" → "were being healed"** (v18) — Greek ἐνοχλούμενοι ἀπό; ἐθεραπεύοντο, imperfect. In v17 and v19 "heal" is ἰάομαι.
- **"virtue" → "power"** (v19) — Greek δύναμις.
- **"Blessed be ye poor" → "Blessed are the poor"** (v20) — Greek μακάριοι οἱ πτωχοί: third person, then "yours". "in spirit" (Matt 5:3) is not here. So also v21: "those who hunger … you shall be filled".
- **"ye shall be filled"** (v21) — Greek χορτασθήσεσθε; "filled up" in v25 is another verb, ἐμπεπλησμένοι.
- **"separate you from their company" → "separate you"** (v22) — Greek ἀφορίσωσιν ὑμᾶς, "mark you off"; elsewhere the verb is "set apart" (Rom 1:1), here in a bad sense.
- **"reward" → "wage"** (v23, 35) — Greek μισθός.
- **"in the like manner" → "in the same way"** (v23, 26) — Greek κατὰ τὰ αὐτά (WH Treg NA28; RP ταῦτα).
- **"But woe unto you that are rich" → "Nevertheless woe to you, the rich"** (v24) — Greek πλὴν οὐαὶ ὑμῖν τοῖς πλουσίοις.
- **"ye have received your consolation" → "you have your comfort in full"** (v24) — Greek ἀπέχετε τὴν παράκλησιν ὑμῶν: ἀπέχω is the word of a receipt (Matt 6:2); παράκλησις here answers to "be comforted" in Matt 5:4.
- **"Woe unto you that laugh" → "Woe, those who laugh"; "Woe unto you, when" → "Woe, when"** (v25, 26) — the Greek has "to you" (ὑμῖν) only in v24 and in the first woe of v25 (WH Treg NA28; RP adds it in the third).
- **"do good" → "do well"** (v27) — Greek καλῶς ποιεῖτε; "do good" (v9, 33, 35) is ἀγαθοποιέω.
- **"despitefully use" → "abuse"** (v28) — Greek ἐπηρεάζω (1 Pet 3:16).
- **"smiteth" → "strikes"; "cloke … coat" → "garment … tunic"; "forbid not to take" → "do not withhold"** (v29) — Greek τύπτω; ἱμάτιον, χιτών; μὴ κωλύσῃς ἀπό, literally "do not hinder from".
- **"thy goods" → "what is yours"** (v30) — Greek τὰ σά.
- **"what thank have ye" → "what grace is it to you"** (v32–34) — Greek ποία ὑμῖν χάρις ἐστίν: χάρις "grace, favor", as 1 Pet 2:19–20. Matt 5:46 has "what wage".
- **"And if ye do good" → "For even if you do good"** (v33) — the verse follows καὶ γὰρ ἐάν (WH NA28; Treg RP καὶ ἐάν "And if").
- **"as much again" → "the equal amount"** (v34) — Greek τὰ ἴσα.
- **"hoping for nothing again" → "hoping for nothing back"** (v35) — Greek μηδὲν ἀπελπίζοντες. Everywhere else the verb means "to despair": "despairing of nothing". The sense "hoping for nothing back" rests on v34 ("from whom you hope to receive") and the old versions; both are possible.
- **"unthankful" → "ungrateful"** (v35) — Greek ἀχάριστος (2 Tim 3:2).
- **"Be ye therefore merciful" → "Become compassionate"** (v36) — Greek γίνεσθε οἰκτίρμονες (root οἰκτιρμός "compassion"); "therefore" is RP only. The verse follows καθὼς καί "just as … also" (Treg NA28; WH without καί).
- **"ye shall not be judged" → "you will by no means be judged"** (v37) — Greek οὐ μή, the strong negation.
- **"forgive, and ye shall be forgiven" → "Release, and you will be released"** (v37) — Greek ἀπολύετε, καὶ ἀπολυθήσεσθε: ἀπολύω is "send away, release" (of a debtor or prisoner), not ἀφίημι "forgive".
- **"shall men give" → "they will give"** (v38) — Greek δώσουσιν; no subject is named.
- **"shaken together" → "shaken"; "measured to you again" → "measured back to you"** (v38) — Greek σεσαλευμένον; ἀντιμετρηθήσεται.
- **"Can the blind lead the blind?" → "Is a blind man able to guide a blind man?"** (v39) — Greek ὁδηγεῖν, as Matt 15:14. The first question begins with μήτι and expects "no", the second with οὐχί and expects "yes".
- **"every one that is perfect" → "everyone, having been made complete"** (v40) — Greek κατηρτισμένος, "fitted out, put in order" (1 Thess 3:10), not τέλειος "perfect".
- **"mote … beam" → "speck … beam"; "pull out" → "cast out"** (v41–42) — Greek κάρφος, δοκός; ἐκβάλλω, as Matt 7:3–5.
- **"bringeth not forth corrupt fruit" → "making rotten fruit"** (v43) — Greek ποιοῦν καρπὸν σαπρόν; "good" is καλός, of the tree and of the fruit.
- **"gather" → "collect … gather"** (v44) — Greek συλλέγουσιν; τρυγῶσιν ("harvest", of grapes). "grapes" is singular in Greek (σταφυλήν).
- **"of his heart" → "of the heart"; "an evil man out of the evil treasure of his heart" → "the evil one out of the evil"** (v45) — the verse follows the text without αὐτοῦ (WH NA28; Treg RP "his"); "man" and "treasure of his heart" are not repeated in the Greek (RP adds them). "brings forth" is προφέρει; Matt 12:35 has ἐκβάλλει "puts forth".
- **"digged deep" → "dug and went deep"** (v48) — Greek ἔσκαψεν καὶ ἐβάθυνεν, two verbs.
- **"the stream beat vehemently" → "the river broke against"** (v48, 49) — Greek προσέρηξεν ὁ ποταμός; "the breaking" (ῥῆγμα) in v49 is of the same root.
- **"for it was founded upon a rock" → "because it had been built well"** (v48) — Greek διὰ τὸ καλῶς οἰκοδομῆσθαι αὐτήν (WH Treg NA28); the KJV reading is RP.
- **"could not shake" → "did not have strength to shake"** (v48) — Greek οὐκ ἴσχυσεν.
- **"heareth, and doeth not" → "heard and did not do"** (v49) — Greek aorist participles; "fell" → "collapsed" (συνέπεσεν, WH Treg NA28).
