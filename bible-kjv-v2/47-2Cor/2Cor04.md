# 2 Corinthians 4

[1] Therefore, having this service, even as we received mercy, we do not grow weary,

[2] but we have renounced the hidden things of shame, not walking in craftiness nor handling the word of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ with guile, but by the manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every conscience of men in the sight of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[3] But even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled among those who are perishing,

[4] among whom the god of this age blinded the thoughts of the unbelievers, so that they should not see the enlightenment of the gospel of the glory of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, who is the image of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[5] For we do not proclaim ourselves, but ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ as Lord, and ourselves as your slaves on account of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[6] For it is the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ who said, "**Out of darkness light shall shine**," who shone in our hearts for the enlightenment of the knowledge of the glory of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in the face of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[7] But we have this treasure in vessels of clay, so that the excess of the power may be of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and not from us;

[8] in everything afflicted but not straitened, at a loss but not utterly at a loss,

[9] persecuted but not forsaken, cast down but not perishing,

[10] always carrying about in the body the putting to death of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, so that the life of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ also may be manifested in our body.

[11] For we who live are ever being delivered up unto death on account of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, so that the life of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ also may be manifested in our mortal flesh.

[12] So then death works in us, but life in you.

[13] But having the same spirit of faith, according to what is written, "**I believed, therefore I spoke**," we also believe, therefore we also speak,

[14] knowing that he who raised the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ will raise us also with ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ and will present us with you.

[15] For all things are for your sake, so that the grace, having increased through the greater number, may make the thanksgiving abound unto the glory of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[16] Therefore we do not grow weary; but even if our outer man is being corrupted, yet our inner man is being renewed day by day.

[17] For **the momentary lightness of our affliction works out for us, exceedingly unto excess, an eternal weight of glory**,

[18] while we look not at the things that are seen but at the things that are not seen; for the things that are seen are temporary, but the things that are not seen are eternal.

## Changes

- **"Jesus Christ" / "Christ Jesus" → "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*" / "*Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v5) — systematic.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v5, 10, 11, 14) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v2, 4, 6, 7, 15) — systematic.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v4, 5, 6) — systematic.
- **"seeing we have this ministry, as we have received mercy, we faint not" → "having this service, even as we received mercy, we do not grow weary"** (v1, 16) — Greek διακονίαν, "service", as 3:7–9; καθὼς ἠλεήθημεν, "as we were shown mercy", without "of God"; ἐγκακοῦμεν, "grow weary, give up" (2 Thess 3:13).
- **"the hidden things of dishonesty" → "the hidden things of shame"** (v2) — Greek τὰ κρυπτὰ τῆς αἰσχύνης, a plain genitive.
- **"not walking in craftiness" kept; "nor handling the word of God deceitfully" → "nor handling the word of *Elohim* with guile"** (v2) — Greek περιπατοῦντες ἐν πανουργίᾳ; δολοῦντες, from δόλος "guile": to falsify, to adulterate. The Greek has participles, not "we refuse".
- **"by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience" → "by the manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every conscience of men"** (v2) — Greek τῇ φανερώσει τῆς ἀληθείας, the noun of φανερόω "manifest" (v10, 11); πρὸς πᾶσαν συνείδησιν ἀνθρώπων.
- **"if our gospel be hid, it is hid to them that are lost" → "even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled among those who are perishing"** (v3) — Greek κεκαλυμμένον, the verb of κάλυμμα "veil" (3:13–16); ἐν τοῖς ἀπολλυμένοις, as 2:15. The Greek has no "only".
- **"In whom the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not" → "among whom the god of this age blinded the thoughts of the unbelievers"** (v4) — Greek ἐν οἷς; αἰών is "age", not "world"; ἐτύφλωσεν, past tense; νοήματα "thoughts" (2:11; 3:14).
- **"lest the light of the glorious gospel of Christ … should shine unto them" → "so that they should not see the enlightenment of the gospel of the glory of the *Mashiach*"** (v4) — Greek εἰς τὸ μὴ αὐγάσαι τὸν φωτισμὸν τοῦ εὐαγγελίου τῆς δόξης τοῦ Χριστοῦ. αὐγάζω is "see clearly" or "shine": also possible is "so that the enlightenment … should not shine forth". The printed text has no "unto them" (the KJV follows manuscripts that add it). φωτισμός, "enlightenment", is a different word from φῶς, "light" (v6); "of the glory" is a noun.
- **"Christ Jesus the Lord; and ourselves your servants for Jesus' sake" → "the *Mashiach* *Yeshua* as Lord, and ourselves as your slaves on account of *Yeshua*"** (v5) — Greek Χριστὸν Ἰησοῦν κύριον, which can also be read "the Lord *Mashiach* *Yeshua*"; δούλους, "slaves"; διὰ Ἰησοῦν. The verse follows Χριστὸν Ἰησοῦν (WH, Treg, RP); NA28: Ἰησοῦν Χριστόν, "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*".
- **"For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts" → "For it is the *Elohim* who said, 'Out of darkness light shall shine,' who shone in our hearts"** (v6) — Greek ὁ θεὸς ὁ εἰπών· Ἐκ σκότους φῶς λάμψει, ὃς ἔλαμψεν. The printed text has the future λάμψει, "shall shine"; the KJV follows the infinitive λάμψαι. The Greek has no main verb ("it is" is supplied).
- **"to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" → "for the enlightenment of the knowledge of the glory of *Elohim* in the face of the *Mashiach*"** (v6) — Greek πρὸς φωτισμὸν, as v4, without "to give". The verse follows ἐν προσώπῳ Χριστοῦ (WH, Treg, NIV); NA28 and RP: Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ, "of *Yeshua* the *Mashiach*".
- **"in earthen vessels" → "in vessels of clay"** (v7) — Greek ἐν ὀστρακίνοις σκεύεσιν (as 2 Tim 2:20).
- **"that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us" → "so that the excess of the power may be of *Elohim* and not from us"** (v7) — Greek ἡ ὑπερβολὴ τῆς δυνάμεως, "the excess, the surpassing measure" (1:8; v17); τοῦ θεοῦ, a genitive, then ἐξ ἡμῶν, "from us". The Greek has no "to show".
- **"We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed" → "in everything afflicted but not straitened"** (v8) — Greek ἐν παντὶ θλιβόμενοι ἀλλʼ οὐ στενοχωρούμενοι; θλίβω as 1:6; στενοχωρέω is "press into a narrow place" (6:12). v8–10 are participles that continue the sentence of v7; the Greek has no "we are".
- **"perplexed, but not in despair" → "at a loss but not utterly at a loss"** (v8) — Greek ἀπορούμενοι ἀλλʼ οὐκ ἐξαπορούμενοι, a play on one verb and its stronger form; the second is the verb of 1:8.
- **"cast down, but not destroyed" → "cast down but not perishing"** (v9) — Greek ἀπολλύμενοι, the word of v3 and 2:15.
- **"bearing about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus" → "carrying about in the body the putting to death of *Yeshua*"** (v10) — Greek τὴν νέκρωσιν τοῦ Ἰησοῦ; νέκρωσις is the act of putting to death, or the state of being dead (Rom 4:19), from νεκρόω (Col 3:5). The printed text has no "the Lord". "In the body" has no "our" the first time; "our body" is singular.
- **"are alway delivered unto death for Jesus' sake" → "are ever being delivered up unto death on account of *Yeshua*"** (v11) — Greek ἀεί, a different word from "always" (πάντοτε, v10); παραδιδόμεθα, present tense; διὰ Ἰησοῦν.
- **"made manifest" → "manifested"** (v10, 11) — Greek φανερωθῇ.
- **"death worketh in us" → "death works in us"** (v12) — Greek ἐνεργεῖται, as 1:6.
- **"We having the same spirit of faith" → "But having the same spirit of faith"** (v13) — Greek τὸ αὐτὸ πνεῦμα τῆς πίστεως; "spirit" can also be "Spirit".
- **"I believed, and therefore have I spoken" → "I believed, therefore I spoke"** (v13) — Greek ἐπίστευσα, διὸ ἐλάλησα, the wording of Ps 116:10 in the Septuagint (there Ps 115:1).
- **"he which raised up the Lord Jesus shall raise up us also by Jesus, and shall present us with you" → "he who raised the Lord *Yeshua* will raise us also with *Yeshua* and will present us with you"** (v14) — the verse follows τὸν κύριον Ἰησοῦν, "the Lord *Yeshua*" (WH, Treg, NA28, RP); the printed SBLGNT reads τὸν Ἰησοῦν, without "Lord" (Holmes, WH margin). σὺν Ἰησοῦ is "with" (WH, Treg, NA28); the KJV follows διά (RP). The Greek has no "into his presence".
- **"that the abundant grace might through the thanksgiving of many redound to the glory of God" → "so that the grace, having increased through the greater number, may make the thanksgiving abound unto the glory of *Elohim*"** (v15) — Greek ἵνα ἡ χάρις πλεονάσασα διὰ τῶν πλειόνων τὴν εὐχαριστίαν περισσεύσῃ. "Through the greater number" (as 2:6) can go with "having increased" or with "the thanksgiving": also possible is "the grace, having increased, may through the thanksgiving of the greater number abound".
- **"though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day" → "even if our outer man is being corrupted, yet our inner man is being renewed day by day"** (v16) — Greek διαφθείρεται, present tense, a stronger form of φθείρω "corrupt": to decay, to be destroyed; ἀνακαινοῦται, present. "Man" stands once in the Greek (ὁ ἔσω ἡμῶν, "our inner"); the second is supplied. ἡμέρᾳ καὶ ἡμέρᾳ is literally "day and day".
- **"our light affliction, which is but for a moment" → "the momentary lightness of our affliction"** (v17) — Greek τὸ … παραυτίκα ἐλαφρὸν τῆς θλίψεως ἡμῶν, "the momentary light thing of our affliction".
- **"worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory" → "works out for us, exceedingly unto excess, an eternal weight of glory"** (v17) — Greek καθʼ ὑπερβολὴν εἰς ὑπερβολὴν … κατεργάζεται; ὑπερβολή twice, as 1:8 and v7; κατεργάζομαι is "work out" (Phil 2:12), not "prepare". βάρος, "weight", is of the root of "weighed down" in 1:8.
- **"While we look not at the things which are seen" kept in sense** (v18) — Greek μὴ σκοπούντων ἡμῶν, "we not looking at". πρόσκαιρα, "temporary", is "for a season".
- **"Let there be light" (v6)** — the words echo Gen 1:3 but are not its wording; cf. Isa 9:2.

### Notes
- **v6** echoes Gen 1:3.
- **v13** quotes Ps 116:10.
- **v7–10** — one sentence in Greek.
