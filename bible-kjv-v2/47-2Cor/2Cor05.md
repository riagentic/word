# 2 Corinthians 5

[1] For we know that if our earthly house of the tent is torn down, we have a building from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, a house not made with hands, eternal, in the heavens.

[2] For indeed in this we groan, longing to clothe ourselves over with our dwelling which is from heaven,

[3] if indeed, having also clothed ourselves, we shall not be found naked.

[4] For indeed we who are in the tent groan, being burdened, because we do not wish to unclothe ourselves but to clothe ourselves over, so that the mortal may be swallowed up by life.

[5] Now he who prepared us for this very thing is ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, who gave us the pledge of the Spirit.

[6] Being therefore always of good courage, and knowing that while we are at home in the body we are away from home, from the Lord —

[7] for **we walk by faith, not by sight** —

[8] but we are of good courage and take pleasure rather in being away from home, out of the body, and at home with the Lord;

[9] therefore we also aspire, whether at home or away from home, to be well-pleasing to him.

[10] For we must all be manifested before the judgment seat of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, so that each one may carry off the things done through the body, according to what he did, whether good or bad.

[11] Knowing therefore the fear of the Lord, we persuade men, but to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ we have been manifested; and I hope to have been manifested also in your consciences.

[12] We are not again commending ourselves to you, but giving you an occasion for a boast on our behalf, so that you may have it toward those who boast in face and not in heart.

[13] For if we were beside ourselves, it was for ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; if we are sober-minded, it is for you.

[14] For the love of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ constrains us, having judged this: that one died for all; therefore all died;

[15] and he died for all, so that those who live might no longer live to themselves but to him who for them died and was raised.

[16] So then we from now on know no one according to the flesh; even if we have known ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ according to the flesh, yet now we know him no longer.

[17] So then, **if anyone is in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ — a new creation; the old things passed away; behold, new things have come to be;**

[18] and all things are from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, who reconciled us to himself through ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and gave us the service of reconciliation,

[19] namely, that ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ was in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ reconciling the world to himself, not reckoning to them their trespasses, and having placed in us the word of reconciliation.

[20] On behalf of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ therefore we are ambassadors, as though ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ were appealing through us; we beseech on behalf of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧: **be reconciled to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧**.

[21] Him who did not know sin he made sin for us, so that we might become the righteousness of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in him.

## Changes

- **"Christ" / "Christ Jesus" → "*Mashiach*" / "*Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v10, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"our earthly house of this tabernacle" → "our earthly house of the tent"** (v1) — Greek ἡ ἐπίγειος ἡμῶν οἰκία τοῦ σκήνους: a genitive ("the house that is the tent"); there is no "this". "Torn down" is καταλυθῇ, the word for taking down a tent or a building.
- **"in this"** (v2) — Greek ἐν τούτῳ: "in this (tent)" or "for this reason"; the Greek does not say which.
- **"clothed upon" → "clothe ourselves over"** (v2, 4) — Greek ἐπενδύσασθαι, to put a garment on over another; "clothe ourselves" (v3) is ἐνδυσάμενοι and "unclothe ourselves" (v4) ἐκδύσασθαι: one root, all three in the middle voice.
- **"being clothed" → "having also clothed ourselves"** (v3) — Greek εἴ γε καὶ ἐνδυσάμενοι (SBLGNT, WH, Treg). NA28: ἐκδυσάμενοι, "having unclothed ourselves".
- **"not for that we would be unclothed" → "because we do not wish to unclothe ourselves"** (v4) — Greek ἐφʼ ᾧ οὐ θέλομεν: "because" or "in that".
- **"wrought" → "prepared"** (v5) — Greek κατεργασάμενος, elsewhere "work out" (7:10); with a person as object it means to make ready, to fashion for something.
- **"earnest" → "pledge"** (v5) — Greek ἀρραβών, a first payment that binds the giver to the rest; "the pledge of the Spirit" is a genitive in Greek (the pledge that is the Spirit, or that the Spirit gives). "Gave" is aorist (δούς).
- **"we are always confident, knowing" → "Being therefore always of good courage, and knowing … —"** (v6–8) — Greek has two participles and no main verb; the sentence breaks off, v7 is an aside, and v8 starts again with "but we are of good courage". The break is kept.
- **"present" / "absent" → "at home" / "away from home"** (v6, 8, 9) — Greek ἐνδημέω / ἐκδημέω, from δῆμος: to be in one's own country, to be abroad.
- **"by sight" kept** (v7) — Greek διὰ εἴδους; εἶδος is the form that is seen, "appearance".
- **"we labour" → "we aspire"** (v9) — Greek φιλοτιμούμεθα, to hold something as one's honour. "Take pleasure" (v8) is εὐδοκοῦμεν; "well-pleasing" (v9) is εὐάρεστοι.
- **"appear" → "be manifested"** (v10, 11) — Greek φανερωθῆναι, πεφανερώμεθα, πεφανερῶσθαι: one verb three times, passive.
- **"judgment seat of Christ" → "judgment seat of the *Mashiach*"** (v10) — βήματος τοῦ Χριστοῦ; some MSS read "God" here.
- **"the things done in his body, according to that he hath done" → "the things done through the body, according to what he did"** (v10) — Greek τὰ διὰ τοῦ σώματος πρὸς ἃ ἔπραξεν; "done" is supplied, the Greek has only "the things through the body". "Bad" is φαῦλον, worthless, base (not κακόν, "evil", which other manuscripts read).
- **"terror of the Lord" → "fear of the Lord"** (v11) — Greek τὸν φόβον τοῦ κυρίου.
- **"occasion to glory on our behalf" → "an occasion for a boast on our behalf"** (v12) — Greek ἀφορμὴν καυχήματος ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν. "That you may have it": the Greek has no object ("that you may have toward those …"). "In face" is ἐν προσώπῳ: in what is seen from outside, the outward appearance.
- **"whether we be beside ourselves" → "if we were beside ourselves"** (v13) — Greek ἐξέστημεν is aorist (past); σωφρονοῦμεν, "we are sober-minded", is present. The Greek has no verb in the second half of each clause: "to *Elohim*", "to you".
- **"constraineth" → "constrains"** (v14) — Greek συνέχει, to hold together, hold fast, press hard. "The love of the *Mashiach*": his love for us or our love for him; the genitive is kept.
- **"because we thus judge" → "having judged this"** (v14) — Greek κρίναντας τοῦτο, an aorist participle. "Died" (ἀπέθανεν, ἀπέθανον) is aorist both times: "one died … all died".
- **"know"** (v16) — two Greek verbs: οἴδαμεν ("we know no one") and ἐγνώκαμεν, γινώσκομεν ("have known", "know him"). "Him" is supplied; "once" and "thus" are not in the Greek.
- **"he is a new creature" → "— a new creation"** (v17) — Greek εἴ τις ἐν Χριστῷ, καινὴ κτίσις has no verb: "he is a new creation" or "there is a new creation". "New things have come to be": γέγονεν καινά. The sentence runs on into v18.
- **"ministry" → "service"** (v18) — Greek διακονία.
- **"reconciliation" (v18, 19, 20)** — καταλλαγή *katallagē* = restoration of broken relationship between parties.
- **"God was in Christ, reconciling" → "*Elohim* was in the *Mashiach* reconciling"** (v19) — Greek θεὸς ἦν ἐν Χριστῷ κόσμον καταλλάσσων: either "*Elohim* was in the *Mashiach*, reconciling" or "in the *Mashiach* *Elohim* was reconciling"; the Greek order is kept without a comma.
- **"imputing" → "reckoning"** (v19) — Greek λογιζόμενος, the accounting sense (elsewhere "consider").
- **"hath committed unto us the word" → "having placed in us the word"** (v19) — Greek θέμενος ἐν ἡμῖν τὸν λόγον; ἐν ἡμῖν is "in us" or "among us".
- **"as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ's stead" → "as though *Elohim* were appealing through us; we beseech on behalf of the *Mashiach*"** (v20) — Greek παρακαλοῦντος ("appeal", as in 6:1) and δεόμεθα ("beseech"); neither verb has "you". ὑπὲρ Χριστοῦ is the same phrase twice.
- **"For he hath made him to be sin" → "Him who did not know sin he made sin"** (v21) — Greek ἁμαρτίαν ἐποίησεν, without "to be"; the printed text has no "for".
- **"righteousness of God" → "righteousness of *Elohim*"** (v21) — δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ.
