# 2 Corinthians 10

[1] Now I myself, ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, appeal to you through the meekness and gentleness of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ — I who to the face am humble among you, but being absent am of good courage toward you;

[2] but I beseech that I may not, being present, be of good courage with the confidence with which I consider to dare against some who consider us as walking according to the flesh.

[3] For walking in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh —

[4] for the weapons of our warfare are not fleshly but powerful for ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ unto the pulling down of strongholds — pulling down reasonings

[5] and every high thing that lifts itself up against the knowledge of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and taking captive every thought unto the obedience of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧,

[6] and holding in readiness to avenge every disobedience, when your obedience is fulfilled.

[7] You look at the things before the face. If anyone is confident in himself that he is ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach's|Christ's⟧, let him consider this again with himself, that even as he is ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach's|Christ's⟧, so also are we.

[8] For even if I boast somewhat more abundantly about our authority, which the Lord gave for building up and not for pulling you down, I will not be put to shame,

[9] that I may not seem as if to terrify you through the letters;

[10] because "The letters," he says, "are weighty and strong, but the presence of the body is weak, and the word despised."

[11] Let such a one consider this, that such as we are in word through letters, being absent, such also being present in deed.

[12] For we do not dare to class ourselves among or compare ourselves with some of those who commend themselves; but they, measuring themselves by themselves and comparing themselves with themselves, do not understand.

[13] But we will not boast unto the things without measure, but according to the measure of the rule which ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ apportioned to us as a measure, to reach even as far as you —

[14] for we do not overstretch ourselves, as though not reaching to you, for even as far as you we came in the gospel of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ —

[15] not boasting unto the things without measure in the labors of others, but having hope, as your faith grows, to be magnified among you according to our rule unto abundance,

[16] to bring good news unto the parts beyond you, not to boast in another's rule unto the things made ready.

[17] But "**let the one who boasts boast in ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧**";

[18] for not the one who commends himself, that one is approved, but he whom the Lord commends.

## Changes

- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (throughout) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv4, 5, 13) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"beseech" → "appeal"; "I beseech you" → "I beseech"** (v1, 2) — two Greek verbs: παρακαλῶ (v1; in this letter "appeal" for a request, "encourage" in affliction, "exhort" for urging) and δέομαι (v2), which has no "you" here.
- **"by the meekness and gentleness" → "through the meekness and gentleness"** (v1) — Greek διὰ τῆς πραΰτητος καὶ ἐπιεικείας.
- **"who in presence am base among you, but being absent am bold toward you" → "I who to the face am humble among you, but being absent am of good courage toward you"** (v1) — Greek κατὰ πρόσωπον, "according to face" (the same words in v7); ταπεινός, "low, lowly, humble"; θαρρῶ as 5:6, 8; 7:16. The Greek has no verb "am" with "humble".
- **"be bold … wherewith I think to be bold against some, which think of us" → "be of good courage … with which I consider to dare against some who consider us"** (v2) — Greek θαρρῆσαι (as v1), then τολμῆσαι, "dare" (v12; 11:21), a different verb; λογίζομαι twice, "consider" (also v7, 11).
- **"For though we walk in the flesh" → "For walking in the flesh"** (v3) — Greek περιπατοῦντες, a participle; there is no "though".
- **"not carnal, but mighty through God" → "not fleshly but powerful for *Elohim*"** (v4) — Greek σαρκικά, from σάρξ "flesh" (v2–3); δυνατὰ τῷ θεῷ, a plain dative: "powerful for God", "powerful before God" or "powerful by God". The Greek does not say "divine power".
- **"to the pulling down of strong holds" kept; "casting down imaginations" → "pulling down reasonings"** (v4) — Greek καθαίρεσιν … καθαιροῦντες, one root (also v8; 13:10); λογισμούς, from λογίζομαι "consider". In the printed text v3b–4a is a parenthesis and "pulling down reasonings" stands in v4; the KJV begins v5 with it. The participles of v4–6 continue "we do not war" (v3).
- **"every high thing that exalteth itself" → "every high thing that lifts itself up"** (v5) — Greek πᾶν ὕψωμα ἐπαιρόμενον; the participle is middle or passive ("lifting itself up" or "being lifted up"). The same verb in 11:20.
- **"bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ" → "taking captive every thought unto the obedience of the *Mashiach*"** (v5) — Greek αἰχμαλωτίζοντες πᾶν νόημα εἰς τὴν ὑπακοὴν τοῦ Χριστοῦ. The genitive is plain: obedience to the *Mashiach*, or the obedience the *Mashiach* showed. νόημα "thought" as 2:11; 3:14; 4:4; 11:3.
- **"having in a readiness to revenge all disobedience" → "holding in readiness to avenge every disobedience"** (v6) — Greek ἐκδικῆσαι, "avenge, punish"; παρακοή, "disobedience", the opposite of ὑπακοή "obedience" in the same verse.
- **"Do ye look on things after the outward appearance?" → "You look at the things before the face."** (v7) — Greek τὰ κατὰ πρόσωπον βλέπετε. The verb can be a statement, a question or a command ("Look at the things before the face"); the printed text has a full stop.
- **"If any man trust to himself … let him of himself think this again" → "If anyone is confident in himself … let him consider this again with himself"** (v7) — Greek πέποιθεν ἑαυτῷ; λογιζέσθω … ἐφʼ ἑαυτοῦ.
- **"which the Lord hath given us for edification, and not for your destruction" → "which the Lord gave for building up and not for pulling you down"** (v8) — Greek ἔδωκεν, with no "us"; οἰκοδομή "building up", καθαίρεσις "pulling down" as v4. ὑμῶν "of you" stands last and can belong to both nouns.
- **"somewhat more" → "somewhat more abundantly"** (v8) — Greek περισσότερόν τι.
- **"That I may not seem as if I would terrify you by letters" → "that I may not seem as if to terrify you through the letters"** (v9) — Greek διὰ τῶν ἐπιστολῶν, with the article and no "my". The clause hangs on v8; the Greek has no "I do not want".
- **"For his letters, say they … his bodily presence is weak, and his speech contemptible" → "because "The letters," he says, "… the presence of the body is weak, and the word despised""** (v10) — Greek φησίν, singular: "he says" or "one says". The Greek has no "his". παρουσία is "presence" here (elsewhere "coming"); λόγος "word"; ἐξουθενημένος, "counted as nothing, despised".
- **"Let such an one think this … such will we be also in deed when we are present" → "Let such a one consider this … such also being present in deed"** (v11) — Greek λογιζέσθω; the second half has no verb (neither "we do" nor "we will be").
- **"make ourselves of the number, or compare ourselves" → "class ourselves among or compare ourselves with"** (v12) — Greek ἐγκρῖναι ἢ συγκρῖναι, a play on two compounds of one verb.
- **"measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise" → "measuring themselves by themselves and comparing themselves with themselves, do not understand"** (v12) — Greek οὐ συνιᾶσιν, a verb: "do not understand".
- **"of things without our measure" → "unto the things without measure"** (v13, 15) — Greek εἰς τὰ ἄμετρα; there is no "our".
- **"the measure of the rule which God hath distributed to us, a measure to reach even unto you" → "the measure of the rule which *Elohim* apportioned to us as a measure, to reach even as far as you"** (v13) — Greek κατὰ τὸ μέτρον τοῦ κανόνος οὗ ἐμέρισεν ἡμῖν ὁ θεὸς μέτρου. κανών is a measuring rod, a rule, and so the limit or field measured out; the Greek does not say "area of influence".
- **"we stretch not ourselves beyond our measure" → "we do not overstretch ourselves"** (v14) — Greek ὑπερεκτείνομεν.
- **"we are come as far as to you also in preaching the gospel" → "even as far as you we came in the gospel"** (v14) — Greek ἐφθάσαμεν ἐν τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ; there is no "preaching". φθάνω is "arrive, come", and also "come first" (1 Thess 4:15).
- **"when your faith is increased, that we shall be enlarged by you according to our rule abundantly" → "as your faith grows, to be magnified among you according to our rule unto abundance"** (v15) — Greek αὐξανομένης τῆς πίστεως ὑμῶν ἐν ὑμῖν μεγαλυνθῆναι … εἰς περισσείαν. ἐν ὑμῖν is "among you" or "in you", and can also go with "grows". It is "we" who are to be magnified, not an "area".
- **"To preach the gospel in the regions beyond you, and not to boast in another man's line of things made ready to our hand" → "to bring good news unto the parts beyond you, not to boast in another's rule unto the things made ready"** (v16) — Greek εὐαγγελίσασθαι, the verb of "gospel"; εἰς τὰ ὑπερέκεινα ὑμῶν; ἐν ἀλλοτρίῳ κανόνι (κανών as v13, 15); εἰς τὰ ἕτοιμα, "with regard to the things ready".
- **"But he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord" → "But "let the one who boasts boast in *Yahweh*""** (v17) — Greek καυχάομαι, "boast" throughout (v8, 13, 15, 16). The words are those of Jer 9:24, where the Hebrew has no name in this clause; the Greek here is ἐν κυρίῳ, "in the Lord".
- **"For not he that commendeth himself is approved" → "for not the one who commends himself, that one is approved"** (v18) — Greek ἐκεῖνός ἐστιν δόκιμος; ἐκεῖνος "that one" takes up the subject again.

### Notes
- **v17** echoes Jer 9:24 (also 1 Cor 1:31).
