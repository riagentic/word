# 2 Corinthians 1

[1] ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, an apostle of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ through the will of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧ the brother,

to the church of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ that is in ⟦corinth|Korinthos|Corinth⟧, with all the saints who are in the whole of Achaia:

[2] Grace to you and peace from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ our Father and the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[3] Blessed be the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and Father of our Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, the Father of compassions and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of all encouragement,

[4] who encourages us in all our affliction, so that we may be able to encourage those who are in any affliction through the encouragement with which we ourselves are encouraged by ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[5] For just as the sufferings of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ abound unto us, so through ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ our encouragement also abounds.

[6] But whether we are afflicted, it is for your encouragement and salvation; whether we are encouraged, it is for your encouragement, which works in the endurance of the same sufferings that we also suffer,

[7] and our hope for you is firm, knowing that as you are sharers of the sufferings, so also of the encouragement.

[8] For we do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, concerning our affliction that came to pass in Asia, that exceedingly, beyond power, we were weighed down, so that we were utterly at a loss even as to living;

[9] but we ourselves have had within ourselves the answer of death, so that we should not be confident in ourselves but in ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ who raises the dead,

[10] who rescued us out of so great a death and will rescue, on whom we have hoped that he will also yet rescue,

[11] you also working together on our behalf by supplication, so that from many faces thanks may be given on our behalf for the gift unto us through many.

[12] For our boasting is this, the testimony of our conscience, that in holiness and sincerity of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and not in fleshly wisdom but in the grace of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, we conducted ourselves in the world, and more abundantly toward you.

[13] For we write to you no other things than what you read or also know fully, and I hope that you will know fully unto the end,

[14] just as you also came to know us fully in part, that we are your boast, even as you also are ours, in the day of our Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[15] And in this confidence I wished to come to you first, so that you might have a second grace,

[16] and through you to pass on into ⟦macedonia|Makedonia|Macedonia⟧, and again from ⟦macedonia|Makedonia|Macedonia⟧ to come to you, and by you to be sent forward into ⟦judea|Yehudah|Judea⟧.

[17] Wishing this, then, did I use lightness? Or the things that I plan, do I plan according to the flesh, so that with me there should be the Yes, yes and the No, no?

[18] But ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is faithful, that our word to you is not Yes and No.

[19] For the Son of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, who was proclaimed among you through us — through me and Silvanus and ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧ — did not become Yes and No, but in him it has become Yes.

[20] For as many as are the promises of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, in him is the Yes; therefore also through him is the Amen to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, unto glory, through us.

[21] Now he who confirms us with you unto ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and anointed us is ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[22] who also sealed us and gave the pledge of the Spirit in our hearts.

[23] But I call upon ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ as witness upon my soul, that sparing you I came no more to ⟦corinth|Korinthos|Corinth⟧.

[24] Not that we lord it over your faith, but we are fellow workers of your joy; for by faith you stand.

## Changes

- **"Jesus Christ" / "Christ Jesus" → "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*" / "*Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v1, 2, 3, 5, 14, 19, 21) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"church"** (v1) — systematic.
- **"by the will of God" → "through the will of *Elohim*"; "our brother" → "the brother"** (v1) — Greek διὰ θελήματος θεοῦ; ὁ ἀδελφός, without "our".
- **"Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ" → "Blessed be the *Elohim* and Father of our Lord *Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (v3) — Greek εὐλογητὸς ὁ θεὸς καὶ πατήρ, one article for both nouns; the Greek has no verb ("be" is supplied).
- **"mercies" → "compassions"** (v3) — Greek οἰκτιρμῶν, as Phil 2:1; "mercy" is ἔλεος.
- **"comfort" / "comforteth" / "consolation" → "encouragement" / "encourages"** (v3–7) — Greek παράκλησις, παρακαλέω, ten times in five verses, one root; the KJV changes between "comfort" and "consolation". The word means a calling to one's side: encouragement, exhortation, appeal. In this letter it is "encourage / encouragement" wherever it answers affliction, "appeal" where it is a request (2:8), "exhort" where it is urging; "comfort" stays for παραμυθέομαι (as Phil 2:1).
- **"tribulation" / "trouble" → "affliction"** (v4, 8) — Greek θλῖψις, three times; the verb θλίβω in v6 is "afflicted". In v4 the first is "all our affliction" (with the article), the second "any affliction" (πάσῃ θλίψει, without it).
- **"the sufferings of Christ abound in us" → "the sufferings of the *Mashiach* abound unto us"; "our consolation also aboundeth by Christ" → "through the *Mashiach* our encouragement also abounds"** (v5) — Greek περισσεύει … εἰς ἡμᾶς; διὰ τοῦ Χριστοῦ. The sufferings and the encouragement are the subjects; the Greek has no "we share".
- **"which is effectual in the enduring" → "which works in the endurance"** (v6) — Greek τῆς ἐνεργουμένης ἐν ὑπομονῇ; ἐνεργέω is "work" (also 4:12). The Greek has no "you experience" and no "patiently". "It is" is supplied twice; the Greek has only "for your encouragement".
- **Order of v6–7** — the printed text reads "whether we are afflicted … salvation; whether we are encouraged … which we also suffer, and our hope for you is firm". The KJV follows manuscripts with another order ("and our hope of you is stedfast" in the middle of v6, and "salvation" twice).
- **"stedfast" → "firm"** (v7) — Greek βεβαία; the verb of the same root in v21 is "confirms".
- **"partakers" → "sharers"** (v7) — Greek κοινωνοί, the root of κοινωνία "fellowship". "The sufferings", "the encouragement": the Greek has no "our"; "you are" is present tense, and the second half has no verb.
- **"we were pressed out of measure, above strength" → "exceedingly, beyond power, we were weighed down"** (v8) — Greek καθʼ ὑπερβολὴν ὑπὲρ δύναμιν ἐβαρήθημεν; ὑπερβολή is "excess" (4:7, 17), δύναμις "power" (4:7), and the verb is of the root of βάρος "weight" (4:17).
- **"we despaired even of life" → "we were utterly at a loss even as to living"** (v8) — Greek ἐξαπορηθῆναι … καὶ τοῦ ζῆν; the same verb as 4:8, a stronger form of ἀπορέω "be at a loss": to see no way out, hence "despair". τοῦ ζῆν is a verb, "living".
- **"we had the sentence of death in ourselves" → "we ourselves have had within ourselves the answer of death"** (v9) — Greek τὸ ἀπόκριμα τοῦ θανάτου ἐσχήκαμεν, perfect tense. ἀπόκριμα, only here in the New Testament, is an official answer or decision; "sentence" is κρίμα. The Greek has no "we felt" and no "that was to".
- **"that we should not trust in ourselves" → "so that we should not be confident in ourselves"** (v9) — Greek πεποιθότες ὦμεν, the root of πεποίθησις "confidence" (v15; 3:4).
- **"delivered" / "doth deliver" / "will yet deliver" → "rescued" / "will rescue" / "will also yet rescue"** (v10) — Greek ῥύομαι, three times. The printed text has the future twice (καὶ ῥύσεται); the KJV follows the reading ῥύεται, "does deliver". ἐκ τηλικούτου θανάτου is "out of so great a death", not "a deadly peril". The Greek has no object the second and third time.
- **"in whom we trust" → "on whom we have hoped"** (v10) — Greek εἰς ὃν ἠλπίκαμεν, perfect of ἐλπίζω "hope".
- **"Ye also helping together by prayer for us" → "you also working together on our behalf by supplication"** (v11) — Greek συνυπουργούντων, a participle that continues v10, not a command; δέησις is "supplication".
- **"by the means of many persons" → "from many faces"** (v11) — Greek ἐκ πολλῶν προσώπων; πρόσωπον is "face" everywhere in this letter (2:10; 3:7, 13, 18; 4:6); here the faces of those who pray, that is, many persons.
- **"the gift bestowed upon us by the means of many persons thanks may be given by many on our behalf" → "thanks may be given on our behalf for the gift unto us through many"** (v11) — Greek τὸ εἰς ἡμᾶς χάρισμα διὰ πολλῶν εὐχαριστηθῇ ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν. χάρισμα is a gift of grace (χάρις). "Through many" can go with "the gift" or with "thanks may be given"; the Greek has no "prayers".
- **"our rejoicing" → "our boasting"** (v12) — Greek καύχησις, the act of boasting; καύχημα in v14 is "boast".
- **"in simplicity and godly sincerity" → "in holiness and sincerity of *Elohim*"** (v12) — the printed text reads ἁγιότητι, "holiness" (WH, Treg, NIV). NA28 and RP: ἁπλότητι, "simplicity", which the KJV also follows. τοῦ θεοῦ is a genitive, "of God", and can go with both nouns.
- **"not with fleshly wisdom" → "and not in fleshly wisdom"** (v12) — the verse follows καὶ οὐκ, "and not" (WH, NA28); the printed SBLGNT reads οὐκ, "not" (Treg, NIV, RP).
- **"fleshly wisdom" kept; "by the grace of God" → "in the grace of *Elohim*"** (v12) — Greek ἐν σοφίᾳ σαρκικῇ, ἐν χάριτι θεοῦ.
- **"we have had our conversation" → "we conducted ourselves"; "more abundantly to you-ward" → "more abundantly toward you"** (v12) — Greek ἀνεστράφημεν; περισσοτέρως.
- **"than what ye read or acknowledge … ye shall acknowledge even to the end" → "than what you read or also know fully … you will know fully unto the end"** (v13) — Greek ἀναγινώσκετε … ἐπιγινώσκετε … ἐπιγνώσεσθε, a play on two verbs of one root; ἐπιγινώσκω is "know fully" (ἐπίγνωσις "full knowledge"). ἕως τέλους is "unto the end"; it can also mean "completely".
- **"ye have acknowledged us in part" → "you also came to know us fully in part"** (v14) — Greek ἐπέγνωτε ἡμᾶς ἀπὸ μέρους; the same verb as v13.
- **"we are your rejoicing, even as ye also are ours" → "we are your boast, even as you also are ours"** (v14) — Greek καύχημα ὑμῶν ἐσμεν, present tense: "we are", not "you will boast".
- **"I was minded" → "I wished"; "before" → "first"; "a second benefit" → "a second grace"** (v15) — Greek ἐβουλόμην; πρότερον; δευτέραν χάριν, the word "grace" of v2, 12. The Greek has no "experience".
- **"to pass by you" → "through you to pass on"; "of you to be brought on my way" → "by you to be sent forward"** (v16) — Greek διʼ ὑμῶν διελθεῖν; προπεμφθῆναι (as 3 John 6).
- **"When I therefore was thus minded, did I use lightness?" → "Wishing this, then, did I use lightness?"** (v17) — Greek τοῦτο οὖν βουλόμενος μήτι ἄρα τῇ ἐλαφρίᾳ ἐχρησάμην; the question expects the answer no. ἐλαφρία is of the root of ἐλαφρόν "light" (4:17).
- **"the things that I purpose, do I purpose according to the flesh" → "the things that I plan, do I plan according to the flesh"** (v17) — Greek βουλεύομαι, twice.
- **"that with me there should be yea yea, and nay nay" kept as "the Yes, yes and the No, no"** (v17) — Greek τὸ Ναὶ ναὶ καὶ τὸ Οὒ οὔ; the Greek has no "at the same time".
- **"But as God is true" → "But *Elohim* is faithful, that"** (v18) — Greek πιστὸς δὲ ὁ θεὸς ὅτι; the words work as an oath. "Is not Yes and No": present tense (ἔστιν) in the printed text; the KJV follows ἐγένετο, "was".
- **"who was preached among you by us" → "who was proclaimed among you through us"** (v19) — Greek ὁ … διʼ ἡμῶν κηρυχθείς, passive; κηρύσσω is "proclaim".
- **"was not yea and nay, but in him was yea" → "did not become Yes and No, but in him it has become Yes"** (v19) — Greek οὐκ ἐγένετο … ἀλλὰ Ναὶ ἐν αὐτῷ γέγονεν, a past and a perfect of γίνομαι "become". It can also be read "Yes has come to be in him". The Greek has no "always".
- **"For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us" → "For as many as are the promises of *Elohim*, in him is the Yes; therefore also through him is the Amen to *Elohim*, unto glory, through us"** (v20) — Greek ὅσαι γὰρ ἐπαγγελίαι θεοῦ, ἐν αὐτῷ τὸ Ναί· διὸ καὶ διʼ αὐτοῦ τὸ Ἀμὴν τῷ θεῷ πρὸς δόξαν διʼ ἡμῶν. The Greek has no verb in either half ("is" is supplied twice), no "we utter" and no "his".
- **"he which stablisheth us with you in Christ" → "he who confirms us with you unto the *Mashiach*"** (v21) — Greek ὁ βεβαιῶν … εἰς Χριστόν; βεβαιόω "confirm" (v7 "firm"), εἰς "unto". "Anointed" (χρίσας) is of the same root as Χριστός, "Anointed One".
- **"the earnest of the Spirit" → "the pledge of the Spirit"** (v22) — Greek τὸν ἀρραβῶνα τοῦ πνεύματος, a plain genitive. ἀρραβών is the first payment that binds the giver to pay the rest (also 5:5). The Greek has no "his".
- **"I call God for a record upon my soul" → "I call upon *Elohim* as witness upon my soul"** (v23) — Greek μάρτυρα τὸν θεὸν ἐπικαλοῦμαι ἐπὶ τὴν ἐμὴν ψυχήν; ψυχή is "soul". The Greek has no "against me".
- **"to spare you I came not as yet unto Corinth" → "sparing you I came no more to *Korinthos*"** (v23) — Greek φειδόμενος ὑμῶν οὐκέτι ἦλθον; οὐκέτι is "no more, not again".
- **"Not for that we have dominion over your faith, but are helpers of your joy" → "Not that we lord it over your faith, but we are fellow workers of your joy"** (v24) — Greek κυριεύομεν, from κύριος "lord"; συνεργοί … τῆς χαρᾶς ὑμῶν, a plain genitive. τῇ πίστει is "by faith" or "in the faith".

### Notes
- **v1** — a new paragraph begins inside the verse, at the address.
- **v8–11** — one sentence in Greek.
- **v21–22** — one sentence in Greek: the four participles (confirms, anointed, sealed, gave) all describe *Elohim*.
