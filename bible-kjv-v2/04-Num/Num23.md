## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"parable" → "oracle"** (v7, 18) — Hebrew מָשָׁל *mashal*. Proverb / riddle / oracular saying, not NT parable.
- **"unicorn" → "wild ox"** (v22) — Hebrew רְאֵם *re'em*. The aurochs, a large wild ox (now extinct). KJV "unicorn" came from LXX/Vulgate mistranslation; no single-horned animal meant.
- **"iniquity in Jacob / perverseness in Israel" → "misfortune in Jacob / trouble in Israel"** (v21) — Hebrew אָוֶן *aven* / עָמָל *amal*. Means primarily trouble / harm / calamity, not moral iniquity. Balaam's oracle sees divine favor preventing disaster on Israel, not denying their sin.
- **"repent" → "change his mind"** (v19) — Hebrew נָחַם *nacham*. Regret / relent, not morally repent.
- **"enchantment / divination" → "sorcery / divination"** (v23) — Hebrew נַחַשׁ *nachash* / קֶסֶם *qesem*.
- **"El" in oracles (v8, 19, 22, 23) kept as Hebrew.** Balaam as a non-Israelite diviner uses the generic divine name in his poetry.
- **Poetic formatting** — Balaam's oracles are poetry in the MT; set as line-broken verse.

### Notes
- **Balaam's oracles** — four major oracles spanning chs 23–24. Instead of cursing, Balaam blesses Israel in increasingly elaborate poetry.
- **First oracle** (v7–10) — Israel as a people set apart, innumerable; Balaam wishes his death to be like theirs.
- **Second oracle** (v18–24) — divine faithfulness; Yahweh does not change his mind; Israel as a lion.
- **"El is not a man, that he should lie"** (v19) — foundational theological statement; quoted 1 Sam 15:29, echoed in Mal 3:6. Divine immutability.
- **Seven altars, seven bulls, seven rams** (v1, 14, 29) — non-Israelite ritual pattern. Similar sevenfold sacrifices in Mesopotamian and Canaanite texts. Balaam uses his native ritual expertise.
- **Balaam genuinely hears Yahweh** — astonishing theological position: the text grants that a Gentile pagan diviner authentically receives Yahweh's word. Universal scope of Yahweh's sovereignty.
