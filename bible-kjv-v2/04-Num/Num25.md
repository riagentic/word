## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"commit whoredom" → "whore"** (v1) — Hebrew זָנָה *zanah*. Sexual / covenantal infidelity.
- **"joined himself" → "yoked itself"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew נִצְמַד *nitzmad* = attached, coupled, joined.
- **"Baal-peor"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew בַּעַל פְּעוֹר *Ba'al Pe'or*. Local manifestation of Baal on Mount Peor.
- **"hang them up ... against the sun" → "impale them before Yahweh in the sun"** (v4) — Hebrew הוֹקַע *hoqa*. Execution by impalement / public display.
- **"into the tent" → "into the inner chamber"** (v8) — Hebrew קֻבָּה *qubbah*. Specifically the inner alcove / bedchamber — here probably the woman's tent.
- **"covenant of peace" (v12)** — Hebrew בְּרִית שָׁלוֹם *berit shalom*. Permanent well-being / priestly tenure.
- **"zealous / jealousy" (v11, 13)** — Hebrew קָנָא *qana*. Same root underlies both "zeal" and "jealousy"; Phinehas' zeal mirrors Yahweh's own qana'. Retained both senses.
- **"vex" → "hostile to"** (v17, 18) — Hebrew צָרַר *tzarar* = be an enemy / adversary.

### Notes
- **Shittim's seduction** — the wilderness generation's last and worst apostasy. Sexual + idolatrous infidelity. Balak failed to curse Israel; the seduction at Peor succeeds where imprecation failed. Rev 2:14 blames Balaam's counsel (cf. Num 31:16).
- **Phinehas' zeal** — radical priestly action. Intervenes unilaterally with lethal force when leaders weep without acting. Celebrated in Ps 106:30–31 ("it was credited to him as righteousness, for all generations forever"). Sometimes compared to Abraham's righteousness by faith (Gen 15:6).
- **Covenant of perpetual priesthood** (v13) — Phinehas' line inherits the high priesthood. 1 Chr 6:4–15 traces the Zadokite line from Phinehas.
- **Plague statistic** (v9) — 24,000 dead. Paul references this (1 Cor 10:8) but gives 23,000 (possibly exclusive of leaders executed).
- **Hinge chapter** — the end of the first wilderness generation. Ch 26 begins the new census of the generation that will enter the land.
- **Moral ambiguity** — the text celebrates Phinehas' violence as atoning. Subsequent Jewish and Christian readings have struggled with the ethics: zeal tempered vs zeal channeled; Jesus refuses to call fire down on Samaritans (Lk 9:54–55) but zealots named for Phinehas remained a political force in the Second Temple era.
