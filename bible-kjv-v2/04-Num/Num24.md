## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"God" → "El"** (v4, 8, 16, 23) — Hebrew אֵל *El*, as in the oracles of ch. 23.
- **"enchantments" → "omens"** (v1) — Hebrew נְחָשִׁים *nechashim*. Divinatory practice — seeking signs.
- **"spirit of God" → "spirit of *Elohim*"** (v2) — kept.
- **"the most High" → "Elyon"** (v16) — Hebrew עֶלְיוֹן *Elyon*.
- **"parable" → "oracle"** (throughout) — *mashal*.
- **"falling into a trance" → "falling down"** (v4, 16) — Hebrew נֹפֵל *nofel*. The diviner's prostrate receptive posture; "trance" interpretive.
- **"lign aloes" → "aloes"** (v6) — archaic term for aromatic aloe-wood.
- **"unicorn" → "wild ox"** (v8) — as in ch 23.
- **"smite the corners of Moab" → "crush the brow of Moab"** (v17) — Hebrew פַּאֲתֵי מוֹאָב *pa'atei Moav*. Temples / sides of the head; crushing victor's blow.
- **"children of Sheth" → "sons of Seth"** (v17) — obscure. Possibly name for a nomadic people, or Seth (Gen 4:25) as a figure for humanity, or parallelism with "Moab."
- **"Eber" (v24)** — ancestor of Hebrews (cf. Gen 10:21), here possibly a broader designation for trans-Euphrates peoples.
- **"Kenites" → "Kenite"** (v21) — Hebrew קֵינִי *Qeini*. Wordplay on *qen* "nest" and *Qayin* "Cain" (v22 "Kain").

### Notes
- **Fourth oracle's messianic reading** (v17) — "a star shall come forth from Jacob, and a scepter shall rise from Israel." Traditionally read messianically: Targums, DSS (*1QM*, *4QTestimonia*), Bar Kokhba (Aramaic for "son of the star"), the Matthean star (Mt 2) all depend on this oracle.
- **Balaam shifts from paid oracle to ecstatic vision** (v1–2) — drops his divinatory apparatus once he realizes Yahweh's position. The subsequent oracles are progressively more exalted.
- **Four oracles for Israel, brief postscripts** — Balaam's scope expands from Israel to Amalek, Kenites, Asshur, Kittim — a panoramic eschatology.
- **Kittim afflict Asshur** (v24) — Mediterranean powers defeating the eastern empires. Fulfilled partly in Alexander the Great's conquests; the DSS sect later applied it to Roman judgment on Hellenistic kingdoms.
- **Balaam's "open eye"** — v3, 15 "the man whose eye is *shetum*" — meaning debated: "open" or "closed." Ancient versions differ. The paradox of the prophet who sees more clearly than he can see.
- **Despite this honorable showing**, Balaam's later scheme corrupts Israel at Peor (ch 31:16 credits him with the counsel for Midianite seduction, ch 25). OT and NT treat him as ambiguous.
