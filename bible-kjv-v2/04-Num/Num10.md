## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Raguel" → "Reuel"** (v29) — Hebrew spelling רְעוּאֵל *Re'u'el*. Same person as "Jethro" / "Reuel" in Exodus. Variant-naming patterns are common for Moses' father-in-law.
- **"be to us instead of eyes" → "be eyes for us"** (v31) — Hebrew וְהָיִיתָ לָּנוּ לְעֵינָיִם *ve-hayita lanu le-einayim*. Serve as our guide.
- **"many thousands" → "myriads"** (v36) — Hebrew רִבְבוֹת *ribevot* = ten-thousands / myriads.
- **"rereward" → "rearguard"** (v25) — modernized.

### Notes
- **Two silver trumpets** (v1–10) — sacred signaling instruments, distinct from the *shofar* (ram's horn). Functions: assembly summons, march orders, war alarm, festival announcement. Titus' arch (Rome, AD 81) depicts these Temple trumpets seized at Jerusalem's fall.
- **Departure from Sinai** (v11–12) — after roughly a year at Sinai (from Ex 19 to here), Israel finally sets out. Beginning of the wilderness trek proper.
- **Hobab / Jethro's family** — Moses' father-in-law-family tradition varies: Jethro (Ex 3:1, 18:1), Reuel (Ex 2:18), Hobab (here, Judg 4:11). Several proposals — same person with multiple names, different family members, or textual stratification.
- **Wilderness guide request** (v29–32) — Moses asks a Midianite for help navigating. The cloud is Yahweh's guide; local knowledge remains useful. Combines divine providence with human expertise.
- **Ark-invocation songs** (v35–36) — two of the oldest poetic fragments in the Torah. In the MT they are bracketed by inverted *nun* letters, suggesting this section is a unit in itself. Ps 68:1 quotes v35.
