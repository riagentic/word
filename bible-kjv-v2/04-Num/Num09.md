# Numbers 9

[1] Yahweh spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the first month of the second year after they had come out of the land of Egypt, saying,

[2] "Let the children of Israel keep the Passover at its appointed time.

[3] On the fourteenth day of this month, at twilight, you shall keep it at its appointed time; according to all its statutes and all its ordinances you shall keep it."

[4] So Moses spoke to the children of Israel, that they should keep the Passover.

[5] And they kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month at twilight in the wilderness of Sinai; according to all that Yahweh had commanded Moses, so the children of Israel did.

[6] But there were some men who were unclean through contact with a corpse, so that they could not keep the Passover on that day; and they came before Moses and Aaron on that day.

[7] Those men said to him, "We are unclean through contact with a corpse; why are we kept from presenting the offering of Yahweh at its appointed time among the children of Israel?"

[8] Moses said to them, "Stand by, and I will hear what Yahweh will command concerning you."

[9] Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

[10] "Speak to the children of Israel, saying: 'If any one of you or of your descendants is unclean because of a corpse, or is on a distant journey, he may still keep the Passover to Yahweh.

[11] On the fourteenth day of the second month, at twilight, they shall keep it; they shall eat it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.

[12] They shall leave none of it until morning, nor break a bone of it; according to all the ordinances of the Passover they shall keep it.

[13] But the person who is clean and is not on a journey, yet fails to keep the Passover — that person shall be cut off from his people, because he did not bring the offering of Yahweh at its appointed time; that man shall bear his sin.

[14] If a foreigner sojourning among you wishes to keep the Passover to Yahweh, he shall keep it according to the statute of the Passover and its ordinance. You shall have one statute, for the foreigner and for the native of the land.'"

[15] On the day the Dwelling was set up, the cloud covered the Dwelling, the Tent of the Testimony; and in the evening it was over the Dwelling like the appearance of fire, until morning.

[16] So it was continually: the cloud covered it by day, and the appearance of fire by night.

[17] Whenever the cloud lifted from over the tent, after that the children of Israel would set out; and in the place where the cloud settled, there the children of Israel would encamp.

[18] At the command of Yahweh the children of Israel would set out, and at the command of Yahweh they would encamp; as long as the cloud rested on the Dwelling, they remained encamped.

[19] Even when the cloud continued over the Dwelling many days, the children of Israel kept the charge of Yahweh and did not set out.

[20] Sometimes the cloud was a few days over the Dwelling; at the command of Yahweh they remained encamped, and at the command of Yahweh they set out.

[21] Sometimes the cloud remained from evening until morning, and when the cloud lifted in the morning, they set out; whether by day or by night, when the cloud lifted, they set out.

[22] Whether two days, a month, or a year — as long as the cloud lingered over the Dwelling, resting upon it — the children of Israel remained encamped and did not set out; but when it lifted, they set out.

[23] At the command of Yahweh they encamped, and at the command of Yahweh they set out; they kept the charge of Yahweh, according to the command of Yahweh by the hand of Moses.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"even" → "twilight"** (v3, 5, 11) — Hebrew בֵּין הָעַרְבַּיִם *bein ha-arbayim* = "between the two evenings" — dusk / twilight.
- **"bitter herbs" (v11)** — Hebrew מְרֹרִים *merorim*. Traditional Passover accompaniment; horseradish, lettuce, chicory in various traditions.
- **"the tent of the testimony" → "the Tent of the Testimony"** (v15) — Hebrew אֹהֶל הָעֵדֻת *ohel ha-edut*.
- **"abode / tarried" → "rested / lingered"** (v17–22) — modernized.

### Notes
- **Second Passover** (v1–5) — one year after the first, celebrated in the wilderness. Confirms ongoing practice.
- **Pesach Sheni** (v6–14) — "Second Passover": a make-up observance on the 14th of the second month for those unclean or distant at the first. Unique Torah provision — a deferred second chance for a one-time-annual observance.
- **Exclusion from inclusion** — the case opens not legally but narratively: men with *ritual disability* seek access. Moses' inquiry and Yahweh's gracious ruling becomes a pattern (cf. daughters of Zelophehad, Num 27).
- **Stranger included** (v14) — same Passover rite for resident foreigners. Ruth's inclusion in the covenant community presumes such provisions.
- **Cloud-pillar guidance** (v15–23) — the tabernacle is mobile; the community moves only when the cloud moves. Unique model of divine-led journey — Yahweh determines duration and direction, not human planning.
- **Foreshadowing the dependent life** — the wilderness cloud-fire becomes the prototype of ongoing divine leading (Isa 4:5–6 — future restoration imagery).
