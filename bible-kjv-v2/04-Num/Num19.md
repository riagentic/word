# Numbers 19

[1] Yahweh spoke to Moses and Aaron, saying,

[2] "This is the statute of the law that Yahweh has commanded: Speak to the children of Israel, that they bring you a red heifer without blemish, in which there is no defect, upon which no yoke has ever come.

[3] You shall give her to Eleazar the priest; and he shall bring her outside the camp, and she shall be slaughtered in his presence.

[4] Eleazar the priest shall take some of her blood with his finger and sprinkle it seven times toward the front of the Tent of Meeting.

[5] The heifer shall be burned in his sight — her hide, her flesh, her blood, with her dung — shall be burned.

[6] The priest shall take cedarwood, hyssop, and scarlet yarn, and cast them into the midst of the burning of the heifer.

[7] Then the priest shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and afterward he may come into the camp; and the priest shall be unclean until evening.

[8] The one who burns her shall wash his clothes in water and bathe his body in water, and he shall be unclean until evening.

[9] A man who is clean shall gather up the ashes of the heifer and deposit them outside the camp in a clean place; they shall be kept for the congregation of the children of Israel for the water of impurity-cleansing; it is for purification from sin.

[10] The one who gathers the ashes of the heifer shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening. It shall be for the children of Israel and for the foreigner who sojourns among them a perpetual statute.

[11] Whoever touches the corpse of any human being shall be unclean seven days.

[12] He shall purify himself with the water on the third day and on the seventh day, and then he shall be clean; but if he does not purify himself on the third day, then on the seventh day he shall not be clean.

[13] Whoever touches a corpse — the body of a person who has died — and does not purify himself, defiles the Dwelling of Yahweh; that person shall be cut off from Israel. Because the water of impurity-cleansing was not sprinkled on him, he shall be unclean; his uncleanness is still on him.

[14] This is the law when a person dies in a tent: everyone who enters the tent and everything that is in the tent shall be unclean seven days.

[15] Every open vessel, with no cover fastened on it, is unclean.

[16] Whoever in the open field touches someone killed by the sword, or a corpse, or a human bone, or a grave, shall be unclean seven days.

[17] For the unclean person they shall take some of the ashes of the burnt purification offering, and fresh water shall be added to them in a vessel.

[18] A clean person shall take hyssop, dip it in the water, and sprinkle it upon the tent, upon all the utensils, upon the persons who were there, and upon the one who touched a bone, or one slain, or one dead, or a grave.

[19] The clean person shall sprinkle upon the unclean on the third day and on the seventh day; and on the seventh day he shall purify him, and he shall wash his clothes and bathe himself in water, and shall be clean at evening.

[20] But the person who has become unclean and does not purify himself — that person shall be cut off from the midst of the assembly, because he has defiled the sanctuary of Yahweh. The water of impurity-cleansing has not been sprinkled on him; he is unclean.

[21] It shall be a perpetual statute for them. The one who sprinkles the water of impurity-cleansing shall wash his clothes, and the one who touches the water of impurity-cleansing shall be unclean until evening.

[22] Whatever the unclean person touches shall be unclean, and the person who touches it shall be unclean until evening."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"water of separation" → "water of impurity-cleansing"** (v9, 13, 20, 21) — Hebrew מֵי נִדָּה *mei niddah*. Lit. "water of *niddah*" — where *niddah* here means impurity / removal / defilement. Not menstrual separation.
- **"purification for sin" → "purification from sin"** (v9) — Hebrew חַטָּאת *chatta't*. Here functioning as the ash-source; technically a purification offering.
- **"running water" → "fresh water"** (v17) — Hebrew *mayim chayyim*. Living / flowing water.
- **"a bone of a man / in the open fields" → "a human bone / in the open field"** — modernized.

### Notes
- **Red heifer** — Hebrew פָּרָה אֲדֻמָּה *parah adummah*. Rare and costly: female, totally red, unblemished, un-yoked. Rabbinic tradition records only nine were ever prepared in biblical/second-temple history. Modern Orthodox preparations debated.
- **Paradox of purity** — the heifer's ashes purify the unclean, yet defile those who handle them (v7, 8, 10, 21). The most potent purification agent is itself dangerous — mystery central to Rabbinic midrash (Bamidbar Rabbah 19).
- **Corpse-uncleanness** — the supreme human impurity, lasting seven days. The sprinkling with red-heifer water on days 3 and 7 reverses it.
- **Cedar, hyssop, scarlet** — same triad as *tsara'at* cleansing (Lev 14). Also at the cross: hyssop raised to Jesus (Jn 19:29).
- **Heb 9:13–14** — "If the blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkling the ceremonially unclean..." — explicit NT application: the red-heifer ash typifies Christ's purifying blood.
- **"Open vessel" rule** (v15) — corpse-defilement diffuses through uncovered air-space. Basis for later Jewish *ohel* ("tent") purity calculations.
