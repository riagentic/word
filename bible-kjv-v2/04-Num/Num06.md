## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"liquor of grapes" → "grape juice"** (v3) — Hebrew מִשְׁרַת עֲנָבִים *mishrat anavim* = grape infusion.
- **"moist grapes, or dried" → "grapes, whether fresh or dried"** (v3) — modernized.
- **"kernels / husk" → "seeds / skins"** (v4) — Hebrew חַרְצַנִּים *chartzannim* = grape seeds / pits; זָג *zag* = skin / husk.
- **"trespass offering" → "guilt offering"** (v12) — *asham*.
- **"sodden" → "boiled"** (v19) — archaic.

### Notes
- **Nazirite vow** — voluntary lay consecration: three prohibitions (grapes/wine, haircutting, corpse-contact). Temporary unless lifelong (Samson, Samuel, John the Baptist).
- **Re-starting after defilement** (v9–12) — unavoidable corpse-contact resets the count to zero; requires sacrifice and restart.
- **Burning the hair** (v18) — the grown hair is itself a physical offering at completion; thrown onto the peace-offering fire. The consecrated body-part (hair) returns to Yahweh.
- **Paul and Nazirites** (Acts 21:23–26) — Paul purifies himself with four men completing a Nazirite vow in Jerusalem. Indicates the vow was still practiced in the 1st century.
- **Priestly Blessing** (v24–26) — the *Aaronic Benediction*, oldest surviving biblical text inscribed (Ketef Hinnom silver amulets, 7th century BC). Three-line structure, each line longer than the last (Hebrew: 3, 5, 7 words). Still recited in Jewish and Christian liturgy.
- **"Put my name upon"** (v27) — blessing imparts the divine name to Israel. The repeated *YHWH* (three times) over the congregation constitutes the blessing itself.
