## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **Name of book** — Hebrew בְּמִדְבַּר *Ba-midbar* = "In the wilderness," taken from v1. English "Numbers" (LXX *Arithmoi*) after the census chapters.
- **"take the sum / number" → "take a census / number"** (v2, 3) — Hebrew נָשָׂא אֶת־רֹאשׁ *nasa' et-rosh* = "lift up the head" — census idiom.
- **"by their polls" → "one by one" / "head by head"** (v2, 18, 20) — Hebrew לְגֻלְגְּלוֹת *le-gulgeloth* = by skulls/heads.
- **"by their armies" → "company by company"** (v3) — Hebrew לְצִבְאֹתָם *le-tzivotam*. Military units.
- **"tabernacle of testimony" → "Dwelling of the Testimony"** (v50, 53) — Hebrew מִשְׁכַּן הָעֵדֻת *mishkan ha-edut*. *Mishkan* = dwelling-place; *edut* = the tablets of the covenant.
- **"princes" → "chiefs"** (v16, 44) — Hebrew נְשִׂיאִים *nesi'im* = chieftains / tribal leaders.
- **"pedigrees" → "lineages"** (v18) — Hebrew יַחַס *yachas*.
- **"stranger that cometh nigh" → "outsider who comes near"** (v51) — Hebrew *zar* = non-Levite (not foreigner).
- **Traditional number-forms** — KJV "threescore and fourteen thousand and six hundred" (v27) modernized to "74,600," etc.

### Notes
- **First census, pre-wilderness** — conducted in the second year after exodus, second month. The generation counted here dies in the wilderness; Num 26 will re-census the new generation.
- **Total 603,550 fighting men** (v46) — implies a total population around 2 million. Numbers much debated: some scholars read *eleph* ("thousand") as a clan / military unit rather than literal thousand, yielding smaller totals. Text itself presents the large number.
- **Twelve tribes, Levi excluded** — Levi set apart for sanctuary service; Joseph split into Ephraim and Manasseh to maintain twelve fighting tribes.
- **Levite role** (v50–53) — to transport, assemble/disassemble the Dwelling; to camp around it as a protective buffer between the sacred and the common.
- **Frame of Numbers** — opens with census (of the first wilderness generation), narrates journey and rebellion, leading to their death; then re-census (ch 26) of the new generation that will enter the land.
