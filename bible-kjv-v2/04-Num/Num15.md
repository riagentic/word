## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"meat offering" → "grain offering"** (throughout) — as elsewhere.
- **"heave offering" → "contribution"** (v19, 20, 21) — as in Lev 7.
- **"through ignorance / by ignorance" → "unintentionally"** (v22, 24, 25, 27, 28) — Hebrew *bi-shegagah*, as in Lev 4.
- **"presumptuously" → "with a high hand"** (v30) — Hebrew בְּיָד רָמָה *be-yad ramah*. Defiant, intentional rebellion. The contrast with *shegagah* is deliberate: no sacrifice atones for high-handed sin.
- **"reproacheth" → "blasphemes"** (v30) — Hebrew מְגַדֵּף *megaddef*.
- **"ward" → "custody"** (v34) — modernized.
- **"use to go a whoring" → "tend to whore"** (v39) — Hebrew זֹנִים אַחֲרֵיהֶם *zonim achareihem*. Covenantal infidelity metaphor.

### Notes
- **Grain and drink offering supplements** (v1–16) — specifies exact quantities of flour, oil, and wine accompanying each burnt or peace offering, depending on animal size.
- **Firstfruits of dough** (v17–21) — the *challah* portion. Still observed in traditional Judaism: a small piece of dough separated before baking.
- **Intentional vs unintentional** (v22–31) — a sharp boundary. *Shegagah* (accidental) has sacrificial remedy; *yad ramah* (deliberate defiance) has none. Hebrews 10:26 picks up this distinction theologically.
- **Sabbath-breaker narrative** (v32–36) — case study for "high-handed" sin. The man is put in custody pending divine ruling, then executed. Parallel to the blasphemer in Lev 24.
- **Tsitsit** (v37–41) — visible tactile reminder of the commandments. Each tassel had a thread of blue (*tekhelet*) — expensive purple-blue dye from murex snail. Jesus wore tsitsit: Mt 9:20, woman touches the "fringe of his garment."
- **Blue color** — same color as the High Priest's robe (Ex 28:31). The tsitsit transfer a priestly visual marker to every Israelite.
