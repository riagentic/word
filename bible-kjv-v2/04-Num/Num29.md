[2] And you shall offer a burnt offering for a soothing smell to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧: one bull of the herd, one ram, seven lambs a year old, without blemish.

[3] And their grain offering: fine flour mixed with oil; three tenths for the bull, two tenths for the ram,

[4] and one tenth for the one lamb, for the seven lambs.

[5] And one male goat as a sin offering, to make atonement for you,

[6] besides the burnt offering of the month and its grain offering, and the continual burnt offering and its grain offering, and their drink offerings, according to their ordinance, for a soothing smell, an offering by fire to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew וַ / וְ "and" at the head of a clause is kept; where a verse has none (v11, 24, 35, 37) none is written. **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (v40) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"an holy convocation"** kept (v1, 7, 12) — Hebrew מִקְרָא־קֹדֶשׁ, literally "a calling of holiness": a day called out as holy, or a calling together of the people (Lev 23:2).
- **"ye shall do no servile work" → "no work of service shall you do"** (v1, 12, 35) — Hebrew כָּל־מְלֶאכֶת עֲבֹדָה לֹא תַעֲשׂוּ, literally so: מְלָאכָה "work" and עֲבֹדָה "service, labor" (Lev 23:7). On the tenth day (v7) it is כָּל־מְלָאכָה, "no work" at all.
- **"it is a day of blowing the trumpets unto you" → "a day of shouting it shall be for you"** (v1) — Hebrew יוֹם תְּרוּעָה. תְּרוּעָה is a loud cry raised: a shout, or the blast of a horn (Lev 23:24; 25:9; Num 10:5). The Hebrew names no trumpets; that horns were blown on this day is the old understanding.
- **"ye shall offer a burnt offering for a sweet savour" → "you shall offer a burnt offering for a soothing smell"** (v2, 6, 8, 13, 36) — Hebrew רֵיחַ נִיחֹחַ, from נוּחַ "rest": a smell that brings rest. The verb in v2 and v39 is עָשָׂה "do, make"; in v8, 13, 36 it is הִקְרִיב "bring near". עֹלָה "burnt offering" is literally "what goes up".
- **"one young bullock" → "one bull of the herd"** (v2, 8, 13, 17) — Hebrew פַּר בֶּן־בָּקָר, literally "a bull, a son of the herd"; the phrase says nothing of age. From the third day on (v20–36) the Hebrew has only פָּרִים / פַּר, "bulls", "bull". **"lambs of the first year" → "lambs a year old"** (throughout) — Hebrew בְּנֵי־שָׁנָה, literally "sons of a year". **"without blemish"** — Hebrew תָּמִים, "whole, complete": the word that is "blameless" of a man.
- **"their meat offering shall be of flour mingled with oil, three tenth deals for a bullock" → "their grain offering: fine flour mixed with oil; three tenths for the bull"** (v3, 9, 14) — Hebrew מִנְחָה; סֹלֶת "fine flour"; עֶשְׂרֹנִים "tenths" (of an ephah: the measure is not named). The Hebrew has no verb.
- **"one tenth deal for one lamb, throughout the seven lambs" → "one tenth for the one lamb, for the seven lambs"** (v4) — Hebrew לַכֶּבֶשׂ הָאֶחָד לְשִׁבְעַת הַכְּבָשִׂים, literally so: for each single lamb, of the seven. **"A several tenth deal" → "a tenth each"** (v10, 15) — Hebrew עִשָּׂרוֹן עִשָּׂרוֹן, literally "a tenth, a tenth": the doubling means "one each". In v14 and v15 the Hebrew has "for thirteen bulls", "for fourteen lambs" without the article, and "for the two rams" with it.
- **"one kid of the goats for a sin offering" → "one male goat as a sin offering"** (v5, 11, 16, 19, 25) — Hebrew שְׂעִיר־עִזִּים אֶחָד חַטָּאת, literally "one hairy one of the goats, a sin"; חַטָּאת is the one word "sin". **"one goat for a sin offering" → "one male goat of sin offering"** (v22, 28, 31, 34, 38) — here the Hebrew is שְׂעִיר חַטָּאת אֶחָד, "one goat of sin offering", without "of the goats": the two forms are kept apart. **"to make an atonement for you"** — Hebrew כִּפֶּר, literally "cover".
- **"the burnt offering of the month"** kept (v6) — Hebrew עֹלַת הַחֹדֶשׁ. חֹדֶשׁ is the word "month" of v1, 7, 12 (literally "the new one": the new moon and the month it opens); the offering of the heads of the months, 28:11–15, is meant ("the burnt offering of a month in its month", 28:14), which falls on this first day of the month.
- **"the daily burnt offering" / "the continual burnt offering" → "the continual burnt offering"** (v6, 11, 16–38) — Hebrew עֹלַת הַתָּמִיד, "the burnt offering of continuance" (28:3–8; Exod 29:42): never left off. "Daily" is not the word.
- **"according unto their manner" → "according to their ordinance"** (v6, 33) and **"after the manner" → "according to the ordinance"** (v18, 21, 24, 27, 30, 37) — Hebrew מִשְׁפָּט, "judgment": here the ruling laid down for them. The seventh day (v33) has כְּמִשְׁפָּטָם "according to their ordinance", the other days כַּמִּשְׁפָּט.
- **"a sacrifice made by fire" → "an offering by fire"** (v6, 13, 36) — Hebrew אִשֶּׁה (commonly taken from אֵשׁ "fire"; the derivation is not certain). In v13 and v36 it is the construct אִשֵּׁה רֵיחַ נִיחֹחַ, "an offering by fire of a soothing smell".
- **"ye shall afflict your souls"** kept (v7) — Hebrew וְעִנִּיתֶם אֶת־נַפְשֹׁתֵיכֶם, word for word (Lev 16:29, 31; 23:27, 32). עִנָּה is "afflict, humble"; fasting is commonly understood (Isa 58:3, 5), but the Hebrew does not say "fast".
- **"they shall be unto you without blemish" → "without blemish they shall be for you"** (v8; so v13) — the Hebrew order.
- **"beside the sin offering of atonement"** kept (v11) — Hebrew חַטַּאת הַכִּפֻּרִים, literally "the sin offering of the atonements" (Lev 16; Exod 30:10).
- **"ye shall keep a feast unto the LORD" → "you shall hold a feast to Yahweh"** (v12) — Hebrew וְחַגֹּתֶם חַג, verb and noun of one root (Lev 23:41). The chapter does not name the feast; it is the Feast of Sukkot of Lev 23:34.
- **"his meat offering, and his drink offering"** — the small differences between the days are kept as the Hebrew has them: "its grain offering and its drink offering" without "and" before the first (v16, 25, 34) or with it (v22, 28, 38); "and their drink offerings" (v19, where the Hebrew has the plural suffix וְנִסְכֵּיהֶם); "its drink offerings" (v31, Hebrew וּנְסָכֶיהָ, a plural noun); "according to their ordinance" (v33). In v33 "their drink offerings" is written וְנִסְכֵּהֶם, without the י: the meaning is the same.
- **"a solemn assembly"** kept (v35) — Hebrew עֲצֶרֶת, from עָצַר "hold back, close up": a day of holding back (from work), or the closing gathering of a feast (Lev 23:36; Deut 16:8). The exact sense is not certain; "solemn" is the customary English and is not a word of its own in the Hebrew.
- **"These things ye shall do unto the LORD in your set feasts, beside your vows" → "These you shall offer to Yahweh at your appointed times, apart from your vows"** (v39) — Hebrew תַּעֲשׂוּ, the verb "do, make" of v2; מוֹעֲדִים, from יָעַד "appoint": fixed times, not the word "feast" (חַג, v12); לְבַד מִן "apart from" beside מִלְּבַד "besides" of the other verses. **"for your burnt offerings, and for your meat offerings …"** — the "for" (לְ) is repeated four times in the Hebrew.
- **"And Moses told the children of Israel according to all" → "And Moshe said to the sons of Israel according to all"** (v40) — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר "said".

### Notes
- **Verse numbers** — v40 is 30:1 in the Hebrew Bible; our numbers are kept.
- **A dotted word** (v15) — the first עִשָּׂרוֹן of v15 carries a dot above it in the Masoretic Text (the scribes' extraordinary point); the wording is not touched.
- **Seventh-month cluster** — the three autumn holy days: (1) first day = the day of shouting, later Rosh Hashanah (v1–6); (2) tenth day = the day of atonement / Yom Kippur (v7–11); (3) fifteenth to twenty-first day = the Feast of Sukkot + the eighth-day Atzeret (v12–38).
- **Sukkot bull count diminishes** (v13, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29, 32) — 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7 bulls across the seven days. Total: 70 bulls. Rabbinic tradition: 70 bulls for 70 nations of the world (Gen 10); Sukkot sacrifices benefit all humanity.
- **Eighth day assembly** (v35) — *Shemini Atzeret*. Not part of Sukkot's seven days but its closing assembly. Jn 7:37 ("last day, the great day of the feast") may refer to this day.
- **Scale** — the Sukkot week's sacrifices are the largest of any festival: 70 bulls, 14 rams, 98 lambs and 7 goats in seven days, plus grain and wine.
- **Ch 28–29 summary** — the offerings of the sanctuary through the year: daily (two lambs), Shabbat (two more), the heads of the months, and the offerings of the appointed times. A substantial economic and pastoral operation.
