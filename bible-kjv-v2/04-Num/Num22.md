# Numbers 22

[1] The children of Israel set out and encamped in the plains of Moab beyond the Jordan at Jericho.

[2] Balak son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites.

[3] Moab was greatly afraid of the people because they were many; Moab was in dread of the children of Israel.

[4] Moab said to the elders of Midian, "This horde will now lick up all that surrounds us, as an ox licks up the grass of the field." Now Balak son of Zippor was king of Moab at that time.

[5] He sent messengers to Balaam son of Beor at Pethor, which is by the River — in the land of the children of his people — to summon him, saying, "Behold, a people has come out of Egypt; they cover the face of the earth, and they are settled opposite me.

[6] Now come, please, curse this people for me, for they are too mighty for me. Perhaps I shall prevail; we may strike them down, and I may drive them out of the land. For I know that whomever you bless is blessed, and whomever you curse is cursed."

[7] So the elders of Moab and the elders of Midian departed with the fees of divination in their hand; and they came to Balaam and spoke to him the words of Balak.

[8] He said to them, "Lodge here tonight, and I will bring you word as Yahweh speaks to me." So the chiefs of Moab stayed with Balaam.

[9] *Elohim* came to Balaam and said, "Who are these men with you?"

[10] Balaam said to *Elohim*, "Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, has sent to me, saying,

[11] 'Behold, the people that has come out of Egypt cover the face of the earth. Now come, curse them for me; perhaps I shall be able to fight against them and drive them out.'"

[12] *Elohim* said to Balaam, "You shall not go with them; you shall not curse the people, for they are blessed."

[13] So Balaam rose in the morning and said to the chiefs of Balak, "Go back to your own land, for Yahweh has refused to let me go with you."

[14] The chiefs of Moab rose and went to Balak and said, "Balaam refuses to come with us."

[15] Once again Balak sent chiefs, more numerous and more honored than they.

[16] They came to Balaam and said to him, "Thus says Balak son of Zippor: 'Please let nothing hinder you from coming to me,

[17] for I will honor you very greatly, and whatever you say to me I will do. Please come, curse this people for me.'"

[18] But Balaam answered the servants of Balak, "If Balak should give me his house full of silver and gold, I could not go beyond the command of Yahweh my *Elohim*, to do less or more.

[19] So please, stay here tonight as well, that I may learn what more Yahweh may say to me."

[20] *Elohim* came to Balaam that night and said to him, "If the men have come to summon you, rise up, go with them; but only do what I tell you."

[21] So Balaam rose in the morning and saddled his donkey and went with the chiefs of Moab.

[22] But the anger of *Elohim* was kindled because he was going; and the Messenger of Yahweh stood in the way as an adversary against him. Now he was riding on his donkey, and his two servants were with him.

[23] The donkey saw the Messenger of Yahweh standing in the way, with his drawn sword in his hand; the donkey turned aside out of the way and went into the field. Balaam struck the donkey to turn her back into the way.

[24] Then the Messenger of Yahweh stood in a narrow path between the vineyards, with a wall on this side and a wall on that side.

[25] When the donkey saw the Messenger of Yahweh, she pressed against the wall and crushed Balaam's foot against the wall; so he struck her again.

[26] The Messenger of Yahweh went on farther and stood in a narrow place where there was no way to turn to the right or to the left.

[27] When the donkey saw the Messenger of Yahweh, she lay down under Balaam; and Balaam's anger was kindled, and he struck the donkey with his staff.

[28] Then Yahweh opened the mouth of the donkey, and she said to Balaam, "What have I done to you, that you have struck me these three times?"

[29] Balaam said to the donkey, "Because you have mocked me. If only I had a sword in my hand — I would kill you now."

[30] The donkey said to Balaam, "Am I not your donkey, on which you have ridden all your life to this day? Have I ever been in the habit of doing this to you?" And he said, "No."

[31] Then Yahweh opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the Messenger of Yahweh standing in the way with his drawn sword in his hand; and he bowed his head and fell on his face.

[32] The Messenger of Yahweh said to him, "Why have you struck your donkey these three times? Behold, I myself have come out as an adversary because your way is reckless before me.

[33] The donkey saw me and turned aside from me these three times; had she not turned aside from me, surely I would have killed you by now and let her live."

[34] Balaam said to the Messenger of Yahweh, "I have sinned; I did not know that you were standing in the way against me. Now therefore, if it is evil in your eyes, I will turn back."

[35] The Messenger of Yahweh said to Balaam, "Go with the men, but only the word that I tell you shall you speak." So Balaam went on with the chiefs of Balak.

[36] When Balak heard that Balaam had come, he went out to meet him at Ir-Moab, on the border of the Arnon, at the edge of the territory.

[37] Balak said to Balaam, "Did I not earnestly send to you to call you? Why did you not come to me? Am I not able indeed to honor you?"

[38] Balaam said to Balak, "Behold, I have come to you. But now have I any power at all to say anything? The word that *Elohim* puts in my mouth — that I must speak."

[39] So Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kiryat-chutsot.

[40] Balak sacrificed oxen and sheep, and sent portions to Balaam and to the chiefs who were with him.

[41] In the morning Balak took Balaam and brought him up to Bamoth-baal, the high places of Baal, and from there he saw the outskirts of the people.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"angel of the Lord" → "Messenger of Yahweh"** (v22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 31, 32, 34, 35) — Hebrew מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה *mal'akh YHWH*. Kept capitalized due to the figure's theological weight (cf. Gen 16).
- **"ass" → "donkey"** (throughout) — modernized.
- **"perverse" → "reckless"** (v32) — Hebrew יָרַט *yarat*. Obscure — "reckless / headlong / contrary"; translations vary.
- **"rewards of divination" → "fees of divination"** (v7) — Hebrew קְסָמִים *qesamim*. Fee for a diviner's service.
- **"princes" → "chiefs"** (throughout) — Hebrew *sarim*.
- **"would I" → "if only I" / "I would"** — modernized archaic optatives.
- **"Pethor" (v5)** — city in northern Mesopotamia, near the Euphrates (the "River" of v5).

### Notes
- **Balaam introduced** — a non-Israelite diviner from Mesopotamia. Unusual: a Gentile prophet to whom Yahweh actually speaks. Ambiguous figure throughout the narrative — obedient in what he says but greedy for Balak's reward.
- **Talking donkey** (v28–30) — one of the Bible's most peculiar episodes. Parodies Balaam's pretension to supernatural sight: his donkey sees the Messenger, but Balaam does not.
- **"Messenger of Yahweh"** (v22) — this figure stands both *as* Yahweh and *separate from* Yahweh throughout OT. Later Christian interpretation often reads christological; Jewish interpretation: high-ranking intermediary or Yahweh's presence in angelic form.
- **Three refusals, three blockings** — the narrative's pattern: Balak sends three rounds of embassy (v5–7, 15–17, later); the donkey is struck three times (v23, 25, 27); Balaam will later pronounce three major oracles (chs 23–24).
- **2 Pet 2:15–16, Jude 1:11, Rev 2:14** — NT uses Balaam as archetype of the prophet-for-hire: outwardly faithful, inwardly mercenary. Judaism preserved a tradition of Balaam as wicked sorcerer despite his biblical oracles.
