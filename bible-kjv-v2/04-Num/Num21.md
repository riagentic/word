## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v5) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים *Elohim*.
- **"came by the way of the spies" → "coming by the way of Atharim"** (v1) — Hebrew הָאֲתָרִים *ha-atharim*. Likely a place name, not "spies." KJV interpreted as derived from *tur* "to scout."
- **"brass" → "bronze"** (v9) — Hebrew נְחֹשֶׁת *nechoshet*. Bronze, not modern brass.
- **"light bread" → "worthless food"** (v5) — Hebrew לֶחֶם הַקְּלֹקֵל *lechem ha-qeloqel*. Contemptuous term; the manna scorned as trashy / insubstantial.
- **"much discouraged" → "impatient"** (v4) — Hebrew וַתִּקְצַר נֶפֶשׁ *va-tiqtzar nefesh* = "soul became short" — impatience.
- **"lawgiver" → "scepter"** (v18) — Hebrew מְחֹקֵק *mechoqeq* = lawgiver / ruler's staff / scepter. Poetic parallel with *mish'aneh* (staff).
- **"Red sea" → "Sea of Reeds"** (v4).
- **"Book of the Wars of the Lord" → "Book of the Wars of Yahweh"** (v14) — a lost ancient Israelite war-poetry collection.
- **"Chemosh" (v29)** — the national deity of Moab (cf. 1 Kgs 11:7, 33; 2 Kgs 3:27).

### Notes
- **Bronze serpent / *Nehushtan*** — later destroyed by Hezekiah because it had become an idol (2 Kgs 18:4). Jesus invokes it: "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (Jn 3:14–15).
- **Mercy in the bite** — the remedy is to look at the very thing afflicting them. Symbolism widely developed in Christian and Jewish readings: focus on the source of judgment to find salvation.
- **Quoted poetry** (v14–15, 17–18, 27–30) — excerpts from lost songbooks (*Book of the Wars of Yahweh*, an unnamed drinking-song at Beer, Amorite victory-song). Numbers preserves these ancient poetic fragments.
- **Sihon and Og** — Israel's first major military victories, in the Transjordan. Later commemorated extensively (Ps 135:10–12, 136:17–22). These kings' lands become the inheritance of Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh (ch 32).
- **"Book of the Wars of Yahweh"** (v14) — one of several lost books mentioned in OT (also the Book of Jashar, Josh 10:13; the Annals of Solomon, 1 Kgs 11:41). Ancient pre-biblical sources that contributed to the canon.
