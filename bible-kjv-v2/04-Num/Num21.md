## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v5) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"king Arad the Canaanite, which dwelt in the south" → "the Canaanite, the king of Arad, who dwelt in the Negev"** (v1) — Hebrew הַכְּנַעֲנִי מֶלֶךְ־עֲרָד יֹשֵׁב הַנֶּגֶב. "The Canaanite" is a singular for the people (also v3); so "the Amorite" (v13, 21–34).
- **"came by the way of the spies" → "had come by the way of the Atharim"** (v1) — Hebrew דֶּרֶךְ הָאֲתָרִים, with the article. The word is found only here and its meaning is not known: a place name, or "the tracks"; KJV "spies" takes it as תָּרִים (Num 14:6).
- **"took some of them prisoners" → "took captives from him"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁבְּ מִמֶּנּוּ שֶׁבִי, literally "took captive from him a captivity"; "him" is Israel.
- **"If thou wilt indeed deliver" → "If you will surely give"** (v2) — Hebrew infinitive absolute with its verb: נָתֹן תִּתֵּן, literally "giving you give".
- **"utterly destroy" → "devote to destruction"** (v2, 3) — Hebrew הֶחֱרִים, to give over to Yahweh beyond recall. The name *Hormah* (חָרְמָה, v3) is from this root (חֵרֶם "devoted thing").
- **"hearkened to the voice" → "listened to the voice"** (v3) — Hebrew שָׁמַע בְּקוֹל. **"delivered up the Canaanites; and they utterly destroyed" → "gave up the Canaanite, and he devoted"** — Hebrew וַיִּתֵּן אֶת־הַכְּנַעֲנִי "gave the Canaanite"; "into his hand" is not in the Masoretic Text (the Septuagint and the Samaritan text have it; cited from memory). The verbs "devoted" and "called" are singular: Israel, or with "called" an unnamed "one".
- **"journeyed" → "set out"** (v4, 10–13), **"pitched" → "camped"** (v10–13) — Hebrew נָסַע, חָנָה; the wording of the stages is that of Num 33:43–45.
- **"Red sea" → "Sea of Reeds"** (v4) — Hebrew יַם־סוּף. **"to compass" → "to go around"**.
- **"the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the way" → "the soul of the people grew short on the way"** (v4) — Hebrew וַתִּקְצַר נֶפֶשׁ־הָעָם בַּדָּרֶךְ, literally "the soul of the people was short": they lost patience (Judg 16:16; Zech 11:8). בַּדָּרֶךְ is "on the way" or "because of the way".
- **"our soul loatheth this light bread" → "our soul loathes this worthless bread"** (v5) — Hebrew קָצָה בְּ "loathe"; לֶחֶם "bread"; הַקְּלֹקֵל is found only here, from the root קלל "be light, be of no account": "light, worthless, contemptible"; the exact sense is uncertain. "Brought us up" is plural: Elohim and Moses are addressed.
- **"fiery serpents" → "the saraf serpents"** (v6), **"a fiery serpent" → "a saraf"** (v8) — Hebrew הַנְּחָשִׁים הַשְּׂרָפִים, "the serpents, the *serafim*", with the article; in v8 שָׂרָף alone. שָׂרָף is from שָׂרַף "burn": "burning one"; whether the burning is the look of the snake or the pain of its bite the word does not say (Deut 8:15; Isa 14:29; 30:6; the same word names the beings of Isa 6:2, 6). The word is kept.
- **"sent … among the people" kept** (v6) — Hebrew וַיְשַׁלַּח … בָּעָם, the intensive stem of "send": "sent out, let loose"; בְּ is "among" or "against".
- **"take away the serpents" → "remove the serpent"** (v7) — Hebrew הַנָּחָשׁ, singular with the article, for the kind. **"prayed for" → "prayed on behalf of"** — Hebrew בְּעַד.
- **"set it upon a pole" → "put it on a pole"** (v8, 9) — Hebrew נֵס, a standard set up to be seen from far (Exod 17:15 "banner"; Isa 5:26; 11:10); here the pole that carries the image.
- **"every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live" → "everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live"** (v8) — Hebrew וְרָאָה אֹתוֹ "and sees it"; "looked at" in v9 is another verb, וְהִבִּיט אֶל.
- **"a serpent of brass" → "a serpent of bronze"** (v9) — Hebrew נְחַשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת *nechash nechoshet*: the two words sound alike, "serpent" (נָחָשׁ) and "bronze / copper" (נְחֹשֶׁת); the command of v8 said שָׂרָף. The play of sound cannot be shown in English. 2 Kgs 18:4 gives the image the name *Nechushtan*. **"he lived"** — the Hebrew verb forms tell of what happened each time. John 3:14 refers to these verses.
- **"which is before Moab, toward the sunrising" → "that faces Moab, toward the rising of the sun"** (v11) — Hebrew עַל־פְּנֵי מוֹאָב, literally "on the face of Moab"; מִמִּזְרַח הַשָּׁמֶשׁ "from the rising of the sun": on the east.
- **"the valley of Zared" → "the wadi Zered"** (v12) — Hebrew נַחַל, a stream bed, dry or running (also v14, 15).
- **"that cometh out of the coasts of the Amorites" → "which comes out from the border of the Amorite"** (v13) — Hebrew הַיֹּצֵא מִגְּבוּל הָאֱמֹרִי; what "comes out" (the Arnon, or the wilderness) the Hebrew does not say.
- **"What he did in the Red sea, and in the brooks of Arnon, and at the stream of the brooks that goeth down to the dwelling of Ar, and lieth upon the border of Moab" → "Waheb in Suphah, and the wadis, the Arnon, and the slope of the wadis that turned toward the seat of Ar and leans on the border of Moab"** (v14–15) — Hebrew אֶת־וָהֵב בְּסוּפָה וְאֶת־הַנְּחָלִים אַרְנוֹן וְאֶשֶׁד הַנְּחָלִים אֲשֶׁר נָטָה לְשֶׁבֶת עָר וְנִשְׁעַן לִגְבוּל מוֹאָב. A fragment of an old song, translated as the Masoretic Text stands; it is uncertain at several points:
  1. The lines begin with the object marker אֶת, and no verb governs them: the sentence is broken off at its start.
  2. וָהֵב is found only here; it is taken as a place name, *Waheb*. סוּפָה is taken as a place name, *Suphah*; the same letters mean "storm-wind". KJV "What he did in the Red sea" reads the words differently (as from יהב "give" and as סוּף); the Septuagint has "he set Zoob on fire" (cited from memory).
  3. הַנְּחָלִים אַרְנוֹן is "the wadis, Arnon": the first word has the article, so it is not "the wadis of".
  4. אֶשֶׁד is found only here in the singular (the plural in Deut 3:17; Josh 10:40): "slope" or "outpouring"; uncertain.
  5. שֶׁבֶת is the infinitive of יָשַׁב "dwell, sit": "the seat", the place where Ar lies. נָטָה "turned, stretched"; נִשְׁעַן "leans".
- **"And from thence they went to Beer" → "And from there to Beer"** (v16) — Hebrew וּמִשָּׁם בְּאֵרָה: there is no verb. *Beer* (בְּאֵר) means "well"; the same word follows as "the well". **"and I will give them water" → "that I may give them water"** — Hebrew וְאֶתְּנָה, a form of will or purpose.
- **"Spring up, O well; sing ye unto it" → "Come up, O well! Sing to it!"** (v17) — Hebrew עֲלִי בְאֵר "go up, come up"; עֱנוּ־לָהּ "sing to it", from ענה "sing in answer". "Then Israel sang this song" is word for word as Exod 15:1.
- **"The princes digged the well, the nobles of the people digged it, by the direction of the lawgiver, with their staves" → "The well that princes dug, that the nobles of the people dug, with the ruler's staff, with their staffs"** (v18) — Hebrew בְּאֵר חֲפָרוּהָ שָׂרִים כָּרוּהָ נְדִיבֵי הָעָם, literally "a well, princes dug it; the nobles of the people dug it": two verbs for "dig" (חָפַר, כָּרָה), for which English has one plain word. נְדִיבִים are "the willing, the noble". בִּמְחֹקֵק is "with the ruler's staff" (Gen 49:10) or "by the commander / lawgiver" (so KJV): מְחֹקֵק names the one who decrees or his staff; the parallel "with their staffs" favours the thing; uncertain. מִשְׁעֶנֶת is a staff to lean on, another word than מַטֶּה.
- **"And from the wilderness they went to Mattanah" → "And from the wilderness to Mattanah"** (v18) — Hebrew וּמִמִּדְבָּר מַתָּנָה, without a verb. מַתָּנָה means "gift"; the words can also be read as the last line of the song, "and from the wilderness, a gift".
- **"to the valley, that is in the country of Moab, to the top of Pisgah, which looketh toward Jeshimon" → "to the valley that is in the field of Moab, the top of the Pisgah, which looks down over the face of the Jeshimon"** (v20) — Hebrew הַגַּיְא "the valley" (a narrow valley); שְׂדֵה מוֹאָב "the field of Moab"; רֹאשׁ הַפִּסְגָּה stands beside it without a preposition; וְנִשְׁקָפָה עַל־פְּנֵי הַיְשִׁימֹן "and it looks down over the face of the *yeshimon*", "the waste".
- **"we will not turn into the fields, or into the vineyards … the waters of the well … the king's high way" → "We will not turn into field or into vineyard … the water of a well. By the way of the king"** (v22) — Hebrew singulars; דֶּרֶךְ הַמֶּלֶךְ as 20:17. The message changes from "let me" to "we".
- **"would not suffer Israel to pass" → "did not allow Israel to pass"** (v23) — Hebrew וְלֹא־נָתַן … עֲבֹר, literally "did not give Israel to pass". **"went out against Israel" → "went out to meet Israel"** — Hebrew לִקְרַאת (also v33).
- **"smote" → "struck"** (v24), **"smote" → "struck down"** (v35) — Hebrew הִכָּה. "The edge of the sword" is literally "the mouth of the sword". **"possessed" → "took possession of"** (v24, 35) — Hebrew יָרַשׁ.
- **"children of Ammon" → "sons of Ammon"** (v24) — Hebrew בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן. **"was strong" kept** — Hebrew עַז; the Septuagint reads the name "Jazer" here (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"in all the villages thereof" → "in all its daughters"** (v25, 32) — Hebrew בְּנֹתֶיהָ, "her daughters": the settlements that belong to a city, not women; the Hebrew way of speaking is kept (as Num 32:42).
- **"who had fought" → "and he had fought"** (v26) — Hebrew וְהוּא נִלְחַם, a clause of its own.
- **"they that speak in proverbs" → "those who speak in parables"** (v27) — Hebrew הַמֹּשְׁלִים, those who make a מָשָׁל: a saying built on a likeness, a proverb, a figurative poem, a taunt song (the word of 23:7, 18; 24:3–23). Whose song it is, Amorite or Israelite, the verse does not say.
- **"Come into Heshbon, let the city of Sihon be built and prepared" → "Come to Heshbon! Let the city of Sihon be built and established"** (v27) — Hebrew תִּבָּנֶה וְתִכּוֹנֵן, two wishes.
- **"a flame from the city of Sihon" → "flame from the town of Sihon"** (v28) — Hebrew קִרְיָה, another word than עִיר "city" (v27). **"it hath consumed" → "it devoured"** — Hebrew אָכְלָה "ate".
- **"the lords of the high places of Arnon" kept** (v28) — Hebrew בַּעֲלֵי בָּמוֹת אַרְנֹן: "the lords / owners / citizens of the high places of the Arnon"; uncertain. The Septuagint has a verb here, "and swallowed" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"thou art undone" → "You have perished"** (v29) — Hebrew אָבַדְתָּ. **"his sons that escaped" → "his sons as fugitives"** — Hebrew פְּלֵיטִם. "He" is Chemosh, the god of Moab (1 Kgs 11:7), or Moab. **"unto Sihon king of the Amorites" → "to the king of the Amorite, Sihon"** — Hebrew לְמֶלֶךְ אֱמֹרִי סִיחוֹן, "to an Amorite king, Sihon".
- **"We have shot at them; Heshbon is perished even unto Dibon, and we have laid them waste even unto Nophah, which reacheth unto Medeba" → "And we shot them; Heshbon has perished as far as Dibon, and we laid waste as far as Nophah, which is as far as Medeba"** (v30) — Hebrew וַנִּירָם אָבַד חֶשְׁבּוֹן עַד־דִּיבוֹן וַנַּשִּׁים עַד־נֹפַח אֲשֶׁר עַד־מֵידְבָא. The Masoretic Text is translated as it stands; the verse is among the hardest of the book:
  1. וַנִּירָם is taken as "and we shot them" (from יָרָה "shoot"); others read a noun, "their lamp" or "their tilled land" (נִיר), with "has perished": uncertain.
  2. וַנַּשִּׁים "and we laid waste" (from שָׁמֵם); the object is not named.
  3. In אֲשֶׁר "which" the last letter carries a scribal dot in the Masoretic Text, a mark of doubt about the word; the Septuagint and the Samaritan text read אֵשׁ "fire" (cited from memory). The first colon is set as one line, as the Hebrew accents have it.
- **"sent to spy out Jaazer" kept without "men"** (v32) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁלַח מֹשֶׁה לְרַגֵּל: no object is named. **"took" → "captured"** — Hebrew לָכַד; "took" in v25, 26 is לָקַח.
- **"drove out the Amorites that were there" → "he dispossessed the Amorite who was there"** (v32) — the reading tradition (qere) is וַיּוֹרֶשׁ "and he dispossessed", singular; the written form (ketiv) ויירש is "and he took possession of".
- **"Bashan" → "the Bashan"** (v33) — Hebrew הַבָּשָׁן, with the article. **"to the battle at Edrei" → "to battle at Edrei"** — Hebrew לַמִּלְחָמָה אֶדְרֶעִי; מִלְחָמָה is "war", of a single fight "battle".
- **"I have delivered him into thy hand" → "into your hand I have given him"** (v34) — Hebrew word order. **"which dwelt at Heshbon"** — Hebrew יוֹשֵׁב, a participle.
- **"until there was none left him alive" → "until no survivor was left to him"** (v35) — Hebrew עַד־בִּלְתִּי הִשְׁאִיר־לוֹ שָׂרִיד, literally "until not leaving to him a survivor".

### Notes
- **Bronze serpent / *Nehushtan*** — later destroyed by Hezekiah, because the sons of Israel were burning incense to it (2 Kgs 18:4). Jesus refers to it: "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (John 3:14–15).
- **The remedy** — those bitten are told to look at an image of the very thing that bit them. The verse gives no explanation; Wisdom 16:5–7 and John 3:14 are early readings of it.
- **Quoted poetry** (v14–15, 17–18, 27–30) — excerpts from older songs (the *Book of the Wars of Yahweh*, the song at the well, the song about Heshbon). Their text is old and in places no longer understood.
- **Sihon and Og** — Israel's first major military victories, in the Transjordan. Later commemorated extensively (Ps 135:10–12; 136:17–22). These kings' lands become the inheritance of Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh (ch 32).
- **"Book of the Wars of Yahweh"** (v14) — one of several lost books mentioned in the OT (also the Book of Jashar, Josh 10:13; the Book of the Acts of Solomon, 1 Kgs 11:41).
