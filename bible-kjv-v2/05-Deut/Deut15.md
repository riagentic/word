# Deuteronomy 15

[1] "At the end of every seven years you shall grant a remission of debts.

[2] This is the manner of the remission: every creditor shall release what he has lent to his neighbor. He shall not exact it of his neighbor, his brother, because Yahweh's remission has been proclaimed.

[3] From a foreigner you may exact it, but whatever of yours is with your brother your hand shall release.

[4] There shall be no poor among you — for Yahweh will bless you abundantly in the land that Yahweh your *Elohim* is giving you for an inheritance to possess —

[5] if only you will carefully listen to the voice of Yahweh your *Elohim*, to be careful to do all this commandment that I am commanding you today.

[6] For Yahweh your *Elohim* will bless you, as he promised you; and you shall lend to many nations, but you shall not borrow; you shall rule over many nations, but they shall not rule over you.

[7] If there is a poor man among you, one of your brothers, within any of your gates in your land that Yahweh your *Elohim* is giving you, you shall not harden your heart or shut your hand against your poor brother.

[8] But you shall surely open your hand to him and surely lend him whatever he needs, enough for his lack.

[9] Take care, lest there be a wicked thought in your heart, saying, 'The seventh year, the year of remission, is near,' and your eye be evil against your poor brother, so that you give him nothing, and he cry out to Yahweh against you, and it be a sin in you.

[10] You shall surely give to him, and your heart shall not be grudging when you give to him, because for this Yahweh your *Elohim* will bless you in all your work and in everything you undertake.

[11] For the poor will never cease out of the land; therefore I command you, saying, 'You shall surely open your hand to your brother, to your needy, and to your poor in your land.'

[12] If your brother — a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman — is sold to you, he shall serve you six years, and in the seventh year you shall let him go free from you.

[13] When you let him go free from you, you shall not send him away empty-handed.

[14] You shall furnish him liberally out of your flock, your threshing floor, and your winepress; as Yahweh your *Elohim* has blessed you, so you shall give to him.

[15] You shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and Yahweh your *Elohim* redeemed you; therefore I command you this thing today.

[16] But if he says to you, 'I will not go away from you,' because he loves you and your household, since it is well with him with you,

[17] then you shall take an awl and thrust it through his ear to the door, and he shall be your servant forever. Also to your female servant you shall do likewise.

[18] It shall not seem hard to you when you send him free from you, for in serving you six years he has been worth twice the wage of a hired worker; and Yahweh your *Elohim* will bless you in all that you do.

[19] All the firstborn males that are born of your herd and of your flock you shall consecrate to Yahweh your *Elohim*. You shall do no work with the firstborn of your ox, nor shear the firstborn of your flock.

[20] You shall eat it before Yahweh your *Elohim* year by year at the place that Yahweh will choose, you and your household.

[21] But if it has any blemish — if it is lame or blind or has any serious defect — you shall not sacrifice it to Yahweh your *Elohim*.

[22] You shall eat it within your gates; the unclean and the clean alike shall eat it, as if it were a gazelle or a deer.

[23] Only you shall not eat its blood; you shall pour it out on the ground like water.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"release" → "remission"** (v1, 2, 9) — Hebrew שְׁמִטָּה *shemittah*. Legally: the sabbatical cancellation of debts.
- **"bondman" → "slave"** (v15) — modernized.
- **"maidservant" → "female servant"** (v17).
- **"aul" → "awl"** (v17) — archaic spelling.
- **"roebuck / hart" → "gazelle / deer"** (v22).
- **"threshingfloor / winepress" → "threshing floor / winepress"** — modernized spelling.
- **"I command thee this thing today" → "I command you this thing today"** — modernized pronouns.

### Notes
- **Sabbatical debt remission** (v1–11) — every seventh year, debts cancelled for fellow Israelites. Foreigners exempt (v3) because they are outside the covenant's mutual solidarity obligations.
- **"No poor among you" vs "the poor never cease"** (v4, 11) — tension: ideal (v4) and reality (v11). Jesus references v11 ("the poor you always have with you," Mk 14:7), not as cynicism but as mandate for continual generosity.
- **Six-year servitude** (v12–18) — parallels Ex 21:1–6. Deut's expansion: (1) gender-inclusive (female servants also freed); (2) "furnish liberally" command — not empty-handed release but economic reintegration.
- **Ear-piercing for voluntary servant** (v17) — physical sign of permanent, voluntary servitude. The door-frame may signify household-identification.
- **Connection to exodus** (v15) — "you were slaves in Egypt" — ethical motivation for compassionate slave-release.
- **First-born animals** (v19–23) — continues the theme. Firstborn belong to Yahweh; consumed at the central sanctuary or, if blemished, eaten at home.
