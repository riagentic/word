## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"shewing mercy unto thousands" → "showing lovingkindness to the thousandth generation"** (v10) — Hebrew חֶסֶד לַאֲלָפִים *chesed la-alafim*. *Chesed* = steadfast love; *la-alafim* likely "to the thousandth generation" (parallel to "third and fourth generation" of v9) rather than "to thousands of people." The contrast is generational-reach: punishment to 4 generations, kindness to 1000.
- **"take the name ... in vain" → "lift up the name ... for an empty purpose"** (v11) — Hebrew לַשָּׁוְא תִּשָּׂא *la-shav tissa*. *Shav* = emptiness, worthlessness, falsity. Broader than "vain oaths"; includes invoking Yahweh's name falsely or pointlessly.
- **"Thou shalt not kill" → "You shall not murder"** (v17) — Hebrew רָצַח *ratzach*. Unjustified killing; murder, manslaughter, unlawful homicide (distinct from *harag* = kill, *zavach* = slay/sacrifice, and military killing). "Kill" in modern English too broad.
- **"graven image" → "carved image"** (v8) — as before.
- **"jealous God" → "jealous El"** (v9).
- **"stranger" → "foreigner"** (v14).
- **"manservant / maidservant" → "male servant / female servant"** — modernized.

### Notes
- **Second giving of the Decalogue** — Deut 5 reproduces Ex 20 with a few notable differences:
  - Sabbath rationale: here, exodus-liberation (v15); in Ex 20, creation-rest.
  - Order of coveted items: here, wife separated out first; in Ex 20, house listed first.
  - Additions of "and" between commandments in Deut; "and" of "field" added here.
- **"With us here, alive today"** (v3) — the new generation is addressed as if the Sinai theophany were their own. Collective memory, collective covenant — critical to Deuteronomy's "today" rhetoric.
- **Ten Words** — numbering varies by tradition: Catholic/Lutheran count v7 as part of v8–10 (no prologue, wife/house combined); Reformed / Jewish / Orthodox count v6 as first ("I am Yahweh") or make v7 the first.
- **Mediator role** (v5, 27) — Moses stands between Yahweh and people. The fear-response to direct theophany establishes prophecy and later mediatorial offices.
- **"If only"** (v29) — Yahweh's wistful utterance about Israel's heart. Sets up the key Deuteronomic theme: heart-circumcision (Deut 10:16, 30:6).
