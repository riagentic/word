# Deuteronomy 10

[1] "At that time ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to me, 'Cut two tablets of stone like the first, and come up to me on the mountain, and make yourself an ark of wood.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"a great God" → "the great El"** (v17) — Hebrew הָאֵל הַגָּדֹל *ha-El ha-gadol*.
- **"shittim" → "acacia"** (v3).
- **"ten commandments" → "ten words"** (v4) — as in ch 5.
- **"circumcise the foreskin of your heart"** (v16) — Hebrew וּמַלְתֶּם אֵת עָרְלַת לְבַבְכֶם *u-maltem et orlat levavkhem*. Metaphor for inner covenant transformation. Paul develops: Rom 2:29, Col 2:11.
- **"God of gods, and Lord of lords" → "*Elohim* of gods and the Lord of lords"** (v17) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱלֹהִים וַאֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים *Elohei ha-*Elohim* va-Adonei ha-Adonim*. Superlative construction.
- **"regardeth not persons, nor taketh reward" → "shows no partiality and takes no bribe"** (v17).
- **"stranger" → "foreigner"** (v18, 19).
- **"raiment" → "clothing"** (v18).
- **"threescore and ten" → "seventy"** (v22) — modernized.

### Notes
- **New tablets, ark of wood** (v1–5) — the ark here is made by Moses of simple wood. Ex 37 tells a different story (Bezalel makes an elaborate gold-overlaid ark). Harmonizers: this is the provisional ark; Ex 37 the permanent one. Source-critics: variant tradition.
- **Parenthesis on wilderness stations** (v6–9) — appears to interrupt the narrative; preserves traditions about Aaron's death and Levite selection in narrative summary form.
- **"What does Yahweh ask of you?"** (v12) — central Deuteronomic statement. Quoted by Micah 6:8 (reframed as justice, mercy, humble walk).
- **"Circumcise the heart"** (v16) — Deut's key theological metaphor. Later: Deut 30:6 promises Yahweh himself will do this. NT: internalization of covenant.
- **Love of foreigners** (v19) — repeated grounding in exodus-experience: "you were foreigners." Unique in ancient law — the immigrant as love-command.
- **"Seventy persons"** (v22) — the family of Jacob entering Egypt (Gen 46:27). From seventy to stars-of-heaven: the promise fulfilled.
