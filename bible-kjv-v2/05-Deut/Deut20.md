# Deuteronomy 20

[1] "When you go out to battle against your enemies and see horses and chariots and an army larger than your own, you shall not be afraid of them; for Yahweh your *Elohim* is with you, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt.

[2] When you come near to the battle, the priest shall approach and speak to the people,

[3] and shall say to them, 'Hear, O Israel: today you are drawing near to battle against your enemies. Do not let your heart grow faint; do not be afraid, do not tremble, and do not be in dread of them,

[4] for Yahweh your *Elohim* is he who goes with you, to fight for you against your enemies, to save you.'

[5] The officers shall speak to the people, saying, 'Is there any man who has built a new house and has not dedicated it? Let him go back to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man dedicate it.

[6] Is there any man who has planted a vineyard and has not begun to enjoy its fruit? Let him go back to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man enjoy its fruit.

[7] Is there any man who has betrothed a wife and has not taken her? Let him go back to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man take her.'

[8] The officers shall speak further to the people, and shall say, 'Is there any man who is afraid and fainthearted? Let him go back to his house, lest his brothers' hearts melt as his own heart.'

[9] When the officers have finished speaking to the people, they shall appoint commanders of hosts at the head of the people.

[10] When you draw near to a city to fight against it, offer terms of peace to it.

[11] If it accepts your terms of peace and opens to you, then all the people found in it shall be your forced laborers and shall serve you.

[12] But if it does not make peace with you but makes war against you, then you shall besiege it.

[13] When Yahweh your *Elohim* gives it into your hand, you shall strike every male in it with the edge of the sword,

[14] but the women and the little ones, the livestock, and everything in the city — all its spoil — you shall take as plunder for yourselves. You shall enjoy the spoil of your enemies, which Yahweh your *Elohim* has given you.

[15] Thus you shall do to all the cities that are very far from you, which are not of the cities of these nations.

[16] But of the cities of these peoples that Yahweh your *Elohim* is giving you as an inheritance, you shall leave alive nothing that breathes,

[17] but you shall devote them to utter destruction — the Hittite, the Amorite, the Canaanite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite — as Yahweh your *Elohim* has commanded you,

[18] so that they may not teach you to do according to all their abominations which they have done for their gods, and so you sin against Yahweh your *Elohim*.

[19] When you besiege a city for many days in making war against it to take it, you shall not destroy its trees by wielding an axe against them; for you may eat from them. You shall not cut them down, for is the tree of the field a man, that it should come under siege from you?

[20] Only the trees that you know are not trees for food, them you may destroy and cut down; and you may build siegeworks against the city that makes war with you, until it falls.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"fetcheth a stroke with the axe" → "wielding an axe"** (v19) — modernized.
- **"man's life" rendered to reflect Hebrew rhetorical question** (v19) — Hebrew כִּי הָאָדָם עֵץ הַשָּׂדֶה *ki ha-adam etz ha-sadeh*. Debated: "for the tree is man's life" (KJV) or rhetorical question "for is the tree a man?" Hebrew syntax inverted; most modern translations read as rhetorical question.
- **"utterly destroy" → "devote to utter destruction"** (v17) — *cherem*.
- **"captains of armies" → "commanders of hosts"** (v9).
- **"bulwarks" → "siegeworks"** (v20).
- **"tributaries" → "forced laborers"** (v11) — Hebrew לָמַס *la-mas*. Corvée / tribute-labor.
- **"hath not eaten of it / another man eat of it" → "has not begun to enjoy its fruit / another man enjoy its fruit"** (v6) — Hebrew חִלְּלוֹ *chillelo*. Lit. "to treat as common" — make first use of. Common agricultural practice: fruit-trees' fruit is forbidden for three years (Lev 19:23); fourth year it's Yahweh's; fifth year is first lay use.

### Notes
- **Three exemptions from military draft** (v5–8) — (1) new-house owner who hasn't dedicated it; (2) vineyard-planter who hasn't eaten its fruit; (3) betrothed man who hasn't married. Plus: (4) the fearful, dismissed to protect the morale of others. Unique in ancient war-law.
- **Distant cities vs Canaanite cities** (v10–18) — two categories:
  - *Far cities*: offer peace; if accepted, subjugation and tribute; if rejected, kill males, keep women/children/property.
  - *Canaanite cities* (7 nations): *cherem*, total destruction.
- **The limits of warfare** — this is one of the ancient world's earliest attempts at circumscribing war. Even distant cities must be offered peace first; the utter destruction is limited to the Canaanite seven.
- **Tree preservation** (v19–20) — an ecological protection during siege. Fruit-bearing trees are preserved; non-fruit trees may be used for siegeworks. Jewish tradition (*bal tashchit*, "do not destroy") derives environmental ethics from this verse.
- **Warrior-priest** (v2–4) — priestly encouragement before battle. The priest's presence before combat preserves the religious character of Israelite war — it is not merely military.
