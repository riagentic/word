# Deuteronomy 21

[1] "When a slain one is found on the ground that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is giving to you to possess it, fallen in the field, and it is not known who struck him down,

[2] then your elders and your judges shall go out and measure to the cities that are around the slain one.

[3] And it shall be, the city nearest to the slain one: the elders of that city shall take a heifer of the herd that has not been worked with, that has not pulled in a yoke,

[4] and the elders of that city shall bring the heifer down to an ever-flowing wadi, which is not worked and not sown, and they shall break the neck of the heifer there in the wadi.

[5] And the priests, the sons of Levi, shall draw near, for them ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has chosen to minister to him and to bless in the name of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, and at their mouth shall be every case and every stroke.

[6] And all the elders of that city, who are nearest to the slain one, shall wash their hands over the heifer whose neck was broken in the wadi,

[7] and they shall answer and say, 'Our hands have not shed this blood, and our eyes have not seen.

[8] Make atonement for your people ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, whom you have ransomed, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, and do not put innocent blood in the midst of your people ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.' And the blood shall be atoned for them.

[9] And you, you shall purge the innocent blood from your midst, when you do what is right in the eyes of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[10] When you go out to battle against your enemies, and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ gives him into your hand, and you take his captives captive,

[11] and you see among the captives a woman beautiful in form, and you long for her and take her to yourself as a wife,

[12] then you shall bring her into the midst of your house, and she shall shave her head and do her nails,

[13] and she shall remove the cloak of her captivity from upon her and dwell in your house and weep for her father and her mother a month of days; and after that you may go in to her and become her husband, and she shall be your wife.

[14] And it shall be, if you do not delight in her, then you shall send her away to her soul, and you shall surely not sell her for money; you shall not deal tyrannically with her, because you have humbled her.

[15] When a man has two wives, the one loved and the one hated, and they bear him sons, the loved and the hated, and the firstborn son is the hated one's,

[16] then it shall be, on the day when he makes his sons inherit what he has, he cannot make the son of the loved one the firstborn before the face of the son of the hated one, the firstborn;

[17] but the firstborn, the son of the hated one, he shall recognize, to give him a double portion of all that is found with him, for he is the beginning of his vigor; his is the right of the birthright.

[18] When a man has a stubborn and rebellious son, who does not listen to the voice of his father and to the voice of his mother, and they discipline him and he does not listen to them,

[19] then his father and his mother shall seize him and bring him out to the elders of his city and to the gate of his place,

[20] and they shall say to the elders of his city, 'This son of ours is stubborn and rebellious; he does not listen to our voice; a glutton and a drunkard.'

[21] And all the men of his city shall stone him with stones, and he shall die; and you shall purge the evil from your midst, and all ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ shall hear and fear.

[22] And when there is in a man a sin of a judgment of death, and he is put to death, and you hang him on a tree,

[23] his dead body shall not lodge on the tree, but you shall surely bury him on that day, for a curse of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is the hanged one; and you shall not defile your ground, which ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is giving to you as an inheritance.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וְ "and"; the chain is kept. "You" is one person (singular) throughout.
- **"If one be found slain in the land … lying in the field" → "When a slain one is found on the ground … fallen in the field"** (v1) — Hebrew חָלָל, "one pierced, slain"; אֲדָמָה "ground"; נֹפֵל "fallen". **"who hath slain him" → "who struck him down"** — Hebrew הִכָּהוּ, "strike".
- **"measure unto the cities"** (v2) — the Hebrew has no object: "measure to the cities".
- **"an heifer, which hath not been wrought with, and which hath not drawn in the yoke" → "a heifer of the herd that has not been worked with, that has not pulled in a yoke"** (v3) — Hebrew עֶגְלַת בָּקָר, "a heifer of the herd"; עֻבַּד is the passive of עָבַד "serve, work".
- **"a rough valley, which is neither eared nor sown" → "an ever-flowing wadi, which is not worked and not sown"** (v4) — Hebrew נַחַל אֵיתָן. נַחַל is a wadi, a stream bed; אֵיתָן is "lasting, never failing" (Amos 5:24), so a wadi whose water does not dry up; the sense "hard, rough" is also defended (so the KJV). יֵעָבֵד is again the verb "serve, work" (v3). **"strike off the heifer's neck" → "break the neck of the heifer"** — Hebrew עָרַף (Exod 13:13).
- **"shall come near" → "shall draw near"** (v5) — Hebrew נָגַשׁ. **"by their word shall every controversy and every stroke be tried" → "at their mouth shall be every case and every stroke"** — Hebrew וְעַל־פִּיהֶם יִהְיֶה כָּל־רִיב וְכָל־נָגַע, literally so; "be tried" is not in the Hebrew. רִיב is a contention, in law a case. נֶגַע is "a touch, a stroke": either a blow, an injury done to someone (17:8), or the "plague" that the priests judge in Lev 13–14 (the word of 24:8). The verse does not decide, so "stroke" stands here and not "plague".
- **"shall wash their hands over the heifer that is beheaded in the valley" → "over the heifer whose neck was broken in the wadi"** (v6) — Hebrew הָעֲרוּפָה.
- **"neither have our eyes seen it" → "and our eyes have not seen"** (v7) — the Hebrew has no object. "Have shed" follows the reading tradition (qere שָׁפְכוּ, plural); the written form (ketiv שפכה) is singular, with the same meaning.
- **"Be merciful, O LORD, unto thy people" → "Make atonement for your people …, Yahweh"** (v8) — Hebrew כַּפֵּר, "make atonement" (literally "cover"); "O" is not in the Hebrew. **"redeemed" → "ransomed"** — Hebrew פָּדָה. **"lay not innocent blood unto thy people of Israel's charge" → "do not put innocent blood in the midst of your people Israel"** — Hebrew וְאַל־תִּתֵּן דָּם נָקִי בְּקֶרֶב, literally "do not give". **"the blood shall be forgiven them" → "the blood shall be atoned for them"** — Hebrew וְנִכַּפֵּר, the same root; "the blood" is the subject.
- **"So shalt thou put away the guilt of innocent blood from among you" → "And you, you shall purge the innocent blood from your midst"** (v9) — Hebrew וְאַתָּה תְּבַעֵר הַדָּם הַנָּקִי מִקִּרְבֶּךָ: the "you" is stressed; "the guilt of" is not in the Hebrew. On "purge" see v21. **"right in the sight of" → "right in the eyes of"** — Hebrew הַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינֵי.
- **"hath delivered them into thine hands, and thou hast taken them captive" → "gives him into your hand, and you take his captives captive"** (v10) — Hebrew וּנְתָנוֹ … וְשָׁבִיתָ שִׁבְיוֹ: after the plural "enemies" the Hebrew goes on with the singular "him … his", the enemy as one. "Captives" (שְׁבִי, v11 שִׁבְיָה) is a collective. "Battle" as in 20:1.
- **"a beautiful woman, and hast a desire unto her, that thou wouldest have her to thy wife" → "a woman beautiful in form, and you long for her and take her to yourself as a wife"** (v11) — Hebrew אֵשֶׁת יְפַת־תֹּאַר (Gen 29:17); חָשַׁק בְּ "long for" (Gen 34:8); וְלָקַחְתָּ לְךָ לְאִשָּׁה is a plain "and you take", not a wish; the Hebrew has no "her" here. Where the conclusion of the long sentence begins is not marked in the Hebrew; it is set at v12.
- **"home to thine house" → "into the midst of your house"** (v12) — Hebrew אֶל־תּוֹךְ בֵּיתֶךָ. **"pare her nails" → "do her nails"** — Hebrew וְעָשְׂתָה אֶת־צִפָּרְנֶיהָ, the plain verb "do, make": to dress them, commonly taken as cutting them (as 2 Sam 19:24 of a beard); "let them grow" is an old Jewish reading (cited from memory).
- **"put the raiment of her captivity from off her" → "remove the cloak of her captivity from upon her"** (v13) — Hebrew שִׂמְלָה, the outer wrap. **"remain" → "dwell"** — Hebrew יָשַׁב. **"bewail … a full month" → "weep for … a month of days"** — Hebrew בָּכָה; יֶרַח יָמִים. **"be her husband" → "become her husband"** — Hebrew וּבְעַלְתָּהּ, the verb of בַּעַל "owner, husband": literally "become owner of her", the word for marrying (24:1).
- **"let her go whither she will" → "send her away to her soul"** (v14) — Hebrew וְשִׁלַּחְתָּהּ לְנַפְשָׁהּ, word for word: to herself, free to go as she wishes (Jer 34:16). **"thou shalt not sell her at all for money" → "you shall surely not sell her for money"** — Hebrew וּמָכֹר לֹא־תִמְכְּרֶנָּה, literally "selling you shall not sell her"; "money" is כֶּסֶף "silver".
- **"thou shalt not make merchandise of her" → "you shall not deal tyrannically with her"** (v14) — Hebrew הִתְעַמֵּר, found only here and in 24:7; its meaning is not certain: "deal as a master with, treat as a slave", or "trade in". One rendering stands in both verses.
- **"because thou hast humbled her" → "because you have humbled her"** (v14) — Hebrew עִנִּיתָהּ, literally "afflicted, humbled her": the word used of a woman taken by a man (Gen 34:2; Deut 22:24, 29). The word alone does not say that force was used.
- **"one beloved, and another hated" → "the one loved and the one hated"** (v15–17) — Hebrew אֲהוּבָה and שְׂנוּאָה, the plain words "loved" and "hated" (Gen 29:31): the wife who is set back behind the other. "Unloved" would soften the word. **"children" → "sons"** — Hebrew בָנִים.
- **"he may not make the son of the beloved firstborn before the son of the hated, which is indeed the firstborn" → "he cannot make the son of the loved one the firstborn before the face of the son of the hated one, the firstborn"** (v16) — Hebrew לֹא יוּכַל "he is not able": he is not free to; לְבַכֵּר "make firstborn"; עַל־פְּנֵי "upon the face of": in his presence, to his loss, in preference to him. "Indeed" is not in the Hebrew. **"maketh his sons to inherit"** — Hebrew הַנְחִילוֹ.
- **"acknowledge" → "recognize"** (v17) — Hebrew הִכִּיר (Gen 37:32). **"a double portion"** — Hebrew פִּי שְׁנַיִם, literally "a mouth of two", two parts: twice the share of another son, or two thirds of the whole (as the phrase means in Zech 13:8). **"of all that he hath" → "of all that is found with him"** — Hebrew בְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר־יִמָּצֵא לוֹ. **"the beginning of his strength" → "the beginning of his vigor"** — Hebrew רֵאשִׁית אֹנוֹ, as Gen 49:3. **"the right of the firstborn" → "the right of the birthright"** — Hebrew מִשְׁפַּט הַבְּכֹרָה: מִשְׁפָּט is "judgment", here what is due by law; בְּכֹרָה is "birthright".
- **"will not obey the voice" → "does not listen to the voice"** (v18, 20) — Hebrew שֹׁמֵעַ בְּקוֹל; "obey" is not a Hebrew word here. **"when they have chastened him" → "and they discipline him"** — Hebrew וְיִסְּרוּ אֹתוֹ. **"stubborn and rebellious"** — Hebrew סוֹרֵר, "turning aside, wayward", and מוֹרֶה, from מָרָה "rebel".
- **"lay hold on him" → "seize him"** (v19) — Hebrew תָּפַשׂ בְּ (20:19; 22:28). **"unto the gate" → "and to the gate"** — Hebrew וְאֶל.
- **"he is a glutton, and a drunkard"** (v20) — Hebrew זוֹלֵל וְסֹבֵא, without "he is": זוֹלֵל is one who squanders, a glutton (Prov 23:20, 21); סֹבֵא a heavy drinker.
- **"stone him with stones, that he die" → "stone him with stones, and he shall die"** (v21) — Hebrew וָמֵת; the verb is רָגַם. **"so shalt thou put evil away from among you" → "and you shall purge the evil from your midst"** — Hebrew וּבִעַרְתָּ הָרָע מִקִּרְבֶּךָ: בִּעֵר is "burn away, clear out utterly"; one wording through the book (13:5; 17:7, 12; 19:19; 22:21, 22, 24; 24:7). הָרָע is "the evil": the evil deed, or the evil man.
- **"if a man have committed a sin worthy of death" → "when there is in a man a sin of a judgment of death"** (v22) — Hebrew וְכִי־יִהְיֶה בְאִישׁ חֵטְא מִשְׁפַּט־מָוֶת, word for word: a sin for which the judgment is death. **"and he be to be put to death" → "and he is put to death"** — Hebrew וְהוּמָת. **"hang him on a tree"** — Hebrew וְתָלִיתָ אֹתוֹ עַל־עֵץ: עֵץ is "tree" and "wood"; a stake may be meant. The man is hanged up after he has been put to death. The clause can also be read as the conclusion: "then you shall hang him on a tree".
- **"His body shall not remain all night upon the tree" → "his dead body shall not lodge on the tree"** (v23) — Hebrew לֹא־תָלִין נִבְלָתוֹ: לוּן is "lodge, spend the night"; נְבֵלָה is the word for a carcass (Lev 11), here of a man. **"thou shalt in any wise bury him" → "you shall surely bury him"** — Hebrew קָבוֹר תִּקְבְּרֶנּוּ, literally "burying you shall bury him".
- **"(for he that is hanged is accursed of God)" → "for a curse of Elohim is the hanged one"** (v23) — Hebrew כִּי־קִלְלַת אֱלֹהִים תָּלוּי, word for word. קִלְלַת אֱלֹהִים is a genitive, "a curse of Elohim", and the Hebrew does not say which way it runs: (1) the hanged one is under a curse from Elohim, "cursed by God" — so the Septuagint, cited from memory, and Gal 3:13, which follows the Greek ("Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree"); or (2) the hanged one is a curse, an insult, to Elohim — so old Jewish readings (cited from memory). The verse keeps the genitive and does not decide. קְלָלָה is the noun of קִלֵּל "make light of, curse".
- **"that thy land be not defiled" → "and you shall not defile your ground"** (v23) — Hebrew וְלֹא תְטַמֵּא אֶת־אַדְמָתְךָ, active, "and … not"; אֲדָמָה "ground".

### Notes
- **Unsolved killing** (v1–9) — unique provision. When the one who struck is unknown, the elders of the nearest city break a heifer's neck in a wadi, wash their hands over it and declare that they neither shed the blood nor saw; the blood is then "atoned for them".
- **The captive wife** (v10–14) — the Torah regulates rather than endorses. She has a month to weep for her father and mother and becomes a wife. If the man no longer delights in her, he must let her go free; she may not be sold.
- **The firstborn's double portion** (v15–17) — the father's preference cannot set aside the order of birth. It protects the sons of the "hated" wife (compare Leah, Gen 29:31).
- **Rebellious son** (v18–21) — deeply disturbing text. Rabbinic tradition hedged it so severely that the Talmud (Sanhedrin 71a) claims it never actually occurred. Required: both parents together, a hearing before the elders at the gate, a settled pattern (a glutton and a drunkard).
- **The hanged one** (v22–23) — burial on the same day is required. Paul applies the verse to the crucifixion: the Mashiach "became a curse for us, for it is written …" (Gal 3:13, in the wording of the Greek).
