# Deuteronomy 21

[1] "If someone is found slain in the land that Yahweh your *Elohim* is giving you to possess, lying in the open field, and it is not known who has struck him down,

[2] then your elders and your judges shall come out and measure the distance to the cities around the slain one.

[3] The city that is nearest to the slain man — the elders of that city shall take a heifer that has never been worked and has not drawn in the yoke,

[4] and the elders of that city shall bring the heifer down to a valley with running water, which is neither plowed nor sown, and shall break the heifer's neck there in the valley.

[5] The priests, the sons of Levi, shall come near, for Yahweh your *Elohim* has chosen them to minister to him and to bless in the name of Yahweh, and by their word every dispute and every assault shall be decided.

[6] All the elders of that city who are nearest to the slain man shall wash their hands over the heifer whose neck was broken in the valley;

[7] and they shall declare, 'Our hands did not shed this blood, nor did our eyes see it.

[8] Atone, O Yahweh, for your people Israel whom you have redeemed, and do not impute innocent blood to your people Israel.' And the bloodguilt shall be atoned for them.

[9] So you shall purge the guilt of innocent blood from your midst, when you do what is right in the eyes of Yahweh.

[10] When you go out to war against your enemies, and Yahweh your *Elohim* gives them into your hand and you take them captive,

[11] and you see among the captives a beautiful woman, and you desire her and would take her as your wife,

[12] you shall bring her into your house, and she shall shave her head and pare her nails.

[13] She shall take off the clothing of her captivity and shall remain in your house and mourn her father and mother a full month; and after that you may go in to her and be her husband, and she shall be your wife.

[14] If you have no delight in her, then you shall let her go wherever she wishes. You shall not sell her for money, nor shall you treat her as merchandise, since you have violated her.

[15] If a man has two wives, one loved and the other unloved, and both the loved and the unloved have borne him children, and if the firstborn son is the unloved one's,

[16] then on the day when he bequeaths what he has to his sons, he may not treat the son of the loved as firstborn in preference to the son of the unloved, who is indeed the firstborn.

[17] But he shall acknowledge the firstborn, the son of the unloved, by giving him a double portion of all that he has; for he is the firstfruits of his vigor; the right of the firstborn is his.

[18] If a man has a stubborn and rebellious son who will not obey his father's voice or his mother's voice, and though they discipline him he will not listen to them,

[19] then his father and his mother shall lay hold of him and bring him out to the elders of his city at the gate of his place,

[20] and they shall say to the elders of his city, 'This son of ours is stubborn and rebellious; he will not obey our voice; he is a glutton and a drunkard.'

[21] Then all the men of his city shall stone him to death with stones. So you shall purge the evil from your midst; all Israel shall hear, and be afraid.

[22] If a man has committed a crime punishable by death, and he is put to death, and you hang him on a tree,

[23] his corpse shall not remain on the tree overnight; you shall bury him the same day — for a hanged man is under the curse of *Elohim* — so that you do not defile your land that Yahweh your *Elohim* is giving you for an inheritance.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"rough valley" → "valley with running water"** (v4) — Hebrew נַחַל אֵיתָן *nachal etan*. Perpetually flowing stream; ritually significant setting.
- **"eared" → "plowed"** (v4) — archaic *eared* = tilled.
- **"priests the sons of Levi" → "priests, the sons of Levi"** (v5).
- **"hated" → "unloved"** (v15, 16, 17) — Hebrew שְׂנוּאָה *senu'ah*. Legal idiom for less-loved co-wife; not morally hated (cf. Gen 29:31, Leah and Rachel).
- **"beginning of his strength" → "firstfruits of his vigor"** (v17) — Hebrew רֵאשִׁית אוֹנוֹ *reshit ono*. Firstborn son.
- **"chastened" → "discipline"** (v18).
- **"humbled" → "violated"** (v14) — Hebrew עִנָּה *innah*. Sexually used / violated. Specifically reflects the context of forced wartime marriage.
- **"he that is hanged is accursed of God" → "a hanged man is under the curse of *Elohim*"** (v23) — Hebrew קִלְלַת אֱלֹהִים תָּלוּי *qilelat *Elohim* taluy*. Quoted by Paul (Gal 3:13) applying to Christ's crucifixion.

### Notes
- **Unsolved murder ritual** (v1–9) — unique provision. When a murderer is unknown, the nearest city's elders perform a purification ritual that absolves the community of bloodguilt. The heifer is killed like the victim, and the elders declare their innocence.
- **The captive wife** (v10–14) — the Torah regulates rather than endorses. She receives a month of mourning, haircut/nail-paring (mourning signs), becomes a wife (not concubine). If divorced, cannot be sold or enslaved. Modest protections against the worst of ancient-world war-captivity.
- **Double portion for firstborn** (v15–17) — favoritism cannot override birth-order in inheritance. Protects sons of less-loved wives (like Leah's Reuben vs Rachel's Joseph; note Reuben's disqualification comes from his own sin, Gen 35:22).
- **Rebellious son** (v18–21) — deeply disturbing text. Rabbinic tradition hedged it so severely that Talmud (Sanhedrin 71a) claims it never actually occurred. Required: both parents united, full due-process, specifically a confirmed pattern (glutton + drunkard).
- **Hanged tree curse** (v22–23) — same day burial required. Paul's application: Christ "became a curse for us, for it is written..." (Gal 3:13). Earliest Christian proof-text for crucifixion-theology.
