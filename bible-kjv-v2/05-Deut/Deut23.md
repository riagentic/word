## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"wounded in the stones" → "crushed testicles"** (v1) — Hebrew פְּצוּעַ דַּכָּה *petzua dakka*. Castration-injury.
- **"bastard" → "one born of a forbidden union"** (v2) — Hebrew מַמְזֵר *mamzer*. Specifically a child of a forbidden union (incest, adultery), not merely "illegitimate." Later rabbinic definitions varied.
- **"Mesopotamia" → "Aram-naharaim"** (v4) — Hebrew אֲרַם נַהֲרַיִם *Aram naharaim*. Lit. "Aram of the two rivers" — upper Mesopotamia.
- **"paddle" → "trowel"** (v13) — Hebrew יָתֵד *yated* = peg / stake. The soldier's excavating tool for hygiene.
- **"sodomite" → "cult-prostitute"** (v17) — Hebrew קָדֵשׁ *qadesh* / קְדֵשָׁה *qedeshah*. Lit. "holy one" — temple-prostitute associated with Canaanite fertility rites. Not "sodomite" in modern usage.
- **"the price of a dog" (v18)** — probably metaphorical for male cult-prostitute earnings; *keleb* "dog" likely a cultic term for male prostitute.
- **"usury" → "interest"** (v19, 20) — Hebrew נֶשֶׁךְ *neshekh*. Any interest on loans, not merely excessive rates.
- **"corn" → "grain"** (v25).

### Notes
- **Exclusions from assembly** (v1–8) — five categories regarding entry into Israelite worship-assembly:
  - Physically mutilated males
  - Forbidden-union offspring
  - Ammonites and Moabites (perpetually, due to hostility at exodus)
  - Edomites and Egyptians (after 3 generations)
- **Historical tension with Ruth** — Ruth the Moabite seemingly contradicts v3. Jewish tradition: the prohibition applied only to males; the Book of Ruth itself is likely a theological contestation of rigid readings.
- **Camp holiness** (v9–14) — military camp must maintain ritual purity; includes bodily functions. Yahweh's presence walks with the camp; indecent elements drive his presence away. "Keep your camp holy" — ritually and ethically.
- **Refugee slave protection** (v15–16) — escaped slaves are not returned to their masters. Radical departure from ancient Near Eastern norms (Code of Hammurabi §16 required return). Applied likely to foreign slaves fleeing to Israel.
- **Cult-prostitution prohibition** (v17–18) — Canaanite temple-prostitutes banned in Israelite worship; their earnings cannot be offered.
- **No interest to fellow Israelites** (v19–20) — shared covenantal solidarity. Foreigners may be lent at interest (commercial-partnership context).
- **Gleaning-on-the-go** (v24–25) — neighbor's field can be grazed for immediate hunger but not harvested. The balance: hospitality, no theft.
