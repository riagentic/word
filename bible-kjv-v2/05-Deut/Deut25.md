# Deuteronomy 25

[1] "If there is a dispute between men, and they come to court, and the judges decide between them, they shall acquit the innocent and condemn the guilty.

[2] If the guilty man deserves to be beaten, the judge shall have him lie down, and he shall be beaten in his presence with a number of stripes proportionate to his offense.

[3] Forty stripes he may give him but no more — lest, if he should go further and beat him with more stripes than these, your brother should be degraded in your eyes.

[4] You shall not muzzle an ox while it is treading out grain.

[5] If brothers dwell together, and one of them dies and has no son, the wife of the dead man shall not be married outside the family to a stranger; her husband's brother shall go in to her and take her as his wife and perform the duty of a brother-in-law to her.

[6] The firstborn whom she bears shall carry on the name of the dead brother, so that his name may not be blotted out of Israel.

[7] But if the man does not wish to take his brother's wife, then his brother's wife shall go up to the gate to the elders and say, 'My husband's brother refuses to perpetuate his brother's name in Israel; he will not perform the duty of a brother-in-law to me.'

[8] Then the elders of his city shall summon him and speak to him. If he persists and says, 'I do not wish to take her,'

[9] then his brother's wife shall come up to him in the presence of the elders, remove his sandal from his foot, spit in his face, and declare, 'So shall it be done to the man who will not build up his brother's house.'

[10] His name shall be called in Israel: 'The house of the one whose sandal was removed.'

[11] When men fight with one another, and the wife of one draws near to deliver her husband from the hand of the one striking him, and puts out her hand and seizes his genitals,

[12] then you shall cut off her hand; your eye shall not pity her.

[13] You shall not have in your bag two different weights, a large and a small.

[14] You shall not have in your house two different measures, a large and a small.

[15] You shall have a full and honest weight; a full and honest measure you shall have, that your days may be long in the land that Yahweh your *Elohim* is giving you.

[16] For everyone who does these things, anyone who acts dishonestly, is an abomination to Yahweh your *Elohim*.

[17] Remember what Amalek did to you on the way, when you came out of Egypt —

[18] how he met you on the way and attacked your rear, all who were lagging behind you, when you were faint and weary; he did not fear *Elohim*.

[19] Therefore when Yahweh your *Elohim* has given you rest from all your surrounding enemies, in the land that Yahweh your *Elohim* is giving you for an inheritance to possess, you shall blot out the memory of Amalek from under the heavens; do not forget.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"corn" → "grain"** (v4).
- **"brother's wife / duty of a husband's brother" → "brother-in-law / duty of a brother-in-law"** (v5, 7) — Hebrew יָבָם *yavam* (brother-in-law) / יְבָמָה *yevamah* (sister-in-law). The *yibbum* / levirate-marriage institution.
- **"shoe" → "sandal"** (v9, 10) — Hebrew נַעַל *na'al*. Footwear of the period.
- **"divers weights / divers measures" → "two different weights / two different measures"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew אֶבֶן וָאֶבֶן *even va'even* / אֵיפָה וְאֵיפָה *eifah ve-eifah*. Cheating devices: one weight for buying (heavy), another for selling (light).
- **"perfect and just" → "full and honest"** (v15) — Hebrew *shelemah va-tsedeq*.
- **"secrets" → "genitals"** (v11) — euphemism modernized; Hebrew *mevushav* = private parts.

### Notes
- **Judicial flogging** (v1–3) — maximum 40 lashes. Later rabbinic tradition cautiously limited to 39 (Paul received "forty minus one" five times, 2 Cor 11:24) to avoid accidentally exceeding 40. Protection of dignity even in punishment.
- **Muzzling ox** (v4) — the ox that threshes may eat from the grain. Paul applies: 1 Cor 9:9, 1 Tim 5:18 — workers deserve support.
- **Levirate marriage** (v5–10) — if a man dies childless, his brother marries the widow; their son continues the dead brother's line and inherits his portion. Basis of Ruth's story (Boaz as *go'el*) and the Sadducees' question to Jesus (Mt 22:24–28).
- **Shame of refusing levirate duty** (v9–10) — shoe-removal + spitting in face; lifelong family-name disgrace. Taken up in Ruth 4:7–8 (shoe-exchange formalized the land-transaction).
- **Wife's-grabbing law** (v11–12) — ethically difficult; the wife who seizes a man's genitals in a fight has her hand cut off. Among the harshest physical penalties; debated heavily in rabbinic tradition (often mitigated to monetary fine).
- **Weights and measures** (v13–16) — integrity in commerce. Basic anti-fraud law; Prov 11:1, 20:10 echo. Ancient discoveries show many carved weight-sets in Iron Age Israel — matching pairs, standardizing the shekel.
- **Blot out Amalek** (v17–19) — perpetual enmity. Saul's failure to fulfill this leads to his rejection (1 Sam 15). Esther 3:1: Haman is "the Agagite" — descendant of Amalekite king Agag.
