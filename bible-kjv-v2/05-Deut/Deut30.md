# Deuteronomy 30

[1] "And it shall be, when all these things come upon you, the blessing and the curse that I have set before you, and you bring back to your heart among all the nations where ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has driven you away,

[2] and you return to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and listen to his voice, according to all that I am commanding you today, you and your sons, with all your heart and with all your soul,

[3] and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will turn back your captivity and have compassion on you, and he will return and gather you from all the peoples where ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has scattered you.

[4] If your driven-away one is at the end of the heavens, from there ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will gather you, and from there he will take you.

[5] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will bring you to the land that your fathers took possession of, and you shall take possession of it; and he will do good to you and multiply you more than your fathers.

[6] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will circumcise your heart and the heart of your seed, to love ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ with all your heart and with all your soul, for the sake of your life.

[7] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will set all these curses on your enemies and on those who hate you, who pursued you.

[9] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will make you abound in all the work of your hand, in the fruit of your belly and in the fruit of your beasts and in the fruit of your ground, for good; for ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will return to exult over you for good, as he exulted over your fathers,

[10] when you listen to the voice of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, to keep his commandments and his statutes, what is written in this book of the ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧, when you return to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ with all your heart and with all your soul.

[11] For this commandment that I am commanding you today, it is not too wonderful for you, and it is not far off.

[12] It is not in the heavens, to say, 'Who will go up for us to the heavens and take it for us and make us hear it, and we will do it?'

[13] And it is not across the sea, to say, 'Who will pass over for us to the other side of the sea and take it for us and make us hear it, and we will do it?'

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וְ "and"; the chain is kept. In v1–3 the Hebrew does not mark where the condition ends and the promise begins: every clause after "when all these things come upon you" has the same form (וְ with the perfect), so "and you bring back … and you return … and Yahweh will turn back" may also be read "then you shall bring back …".
- **"And it shall come to pass" → "And it shall be"** (v1) — Hebrew וְהָיָה. **"things"** — Hebrew הַדְּבָרִים, the word for "words" and "things". **"set before thee"** (v1, 15, 19) — Hebrew נָתַתִּי לְפָנֶיךָ, literally "given before your face".
- **"thou shalt call them to mind" → "you bring back to your heart"** (v1) — Hebrew וַהֲשֵׁבֹתָ אֶל־לְבָבֶךָ, the causative of שׁוּב "return"; the Hebrew names no object ("them" is supplied by KJV). The verb שׁוּב runs through v1–10 seven times: bring back (v1), return (v2), turn back … return (v3), return (v8, 9, 10).
- **"hath driven thee" → "has driven you away"; "If any of thine be driven out" → "If your driven-away one"; "shalt be drawn away" kept** (v1, 4, 17) — one Hebrew verb, נָדַח "thrust, drive" (הִדִּיחֲךָ, נִדַּחֲךָ, וְנִדַּחְתָּ): driven away among the nations (v1, 4), and "drawn away", literally "driven away", to other gods (v17; 4:19). In v4 the word is a singular: "the one of yours who is driven away". In v17 the passive form may also mean "let yourself be drawn away".
- **"shalt return unto the LORD" → "return to Yahweh"** (v2) — Hebrew עַד "as far as, right up to", not the usual אֶל "to" (v10). **"obey his voice" → "listen to his voice"** (v2, 8, 10, 20) — Hebrew שָׁמַע בְּקוֹל; "obey" is not a Hebrew word here. **"thy children" → "your sons"** — Hebrew בָּנֶיךָ.
- **"will turn thy captivity" → "will turn back your captivity"** (v3) — Hebrew וְשָׁב … אֶת־שְׁבוּתְךָ. The noun שְׁבוּת is uncertain: it is taken either from שָׁבָה "take captive" ("captivity", so the old versions) or from שׁוּב "turn" itself ("turn your turning" = restore your fortunes, as the phrase must mean in Job 42:10). The verse keeps "captivity", which the scattering among the peoples supports; the other sense stands beside it.
- **"will return and gather thee" kept** (v3) — Hebrew וְשָׁב וְקִבֶּצְךָ; "return and gather" is the Hebrew way of saying "gather again" (so "return and listen", v8; "return to rejoice", v9). **"nations" → "peoples"** — Hebrew הָעַמִּים (v1 has הַגּוֹיִם "the nations").
- **"unto the outmost parts of heaven" → "at the end of the heavens"** (v4) — Hebrew בִּקְצֵה הַשָּׁמָיִם. **"will he fetch thee" → "he will take you"** — Hebrew יִקָּחֶךָ.
- **"which thy fathers possessed, and thou shalt possess it" → "that your fathers took possession of, and you shall take possession of it"** (v5) — Hebrew יָרַשׁ, the verb of "to possess it" (v16, 18).
- **"thy seed"** (v6, 19) — Hebrew זֶרַע. **"that thou mayest live" → "for the sake of your life"** (v6) — Hebrew לְמַעַן חַיֶּיךָ, a noun: "on account of your life".
- **"circumcise thine heart" kept** (v6) — Hebrew וּמָל … אֶת־לְבָבְךָ. In 10:16 Israel is told to circumcise the foreskin of its heart; here Yahweh does it. "To love" is the Hebrew infinitive לְאַהֲבָה: whether it gives the aim or the result is not said.
- **"will put all these curses upon" → "will set all these curses on"** (v7) — Hebrew וְנָתַן … עַל, literally "give … upon" (11:29); הָאָלוֹת, the oath-curses of 29:20, 21; "the curse" of v1, 19 is another word, הַקְּלָלָה. **"which persecuted thee" → "who pursued you"** — Hebrew רָדַף.
- **"will make thee plenteous" → "will make you abound"** (v9) — Hebrew וְהוֹתִירְךָ, literally "will make you have over and above" (28:11). **"the fruit of thy body, … of thy cattle" → "the fruit of your belly … of your beasts"** — Hebrew בֶּטֶן, בְּהֵמָה. **"will again rejoice" → "will return to exult"** — Hebrew יָשׁוּב … לָשׂוּשׂ (28:63).
- **"If thou shalt hearken … and if thou turn" → "when you listen … when you return"** (v10) — Hebrew כִּי twice: "when", "if" or "for"; the word does not decide. **"which are written" → "what is written"** — Hebrew הַכְּתוּבָה, a feminine singular that does not agree with the plural "commandments and statutes": "that which is written". "This" belongs to "book" (הַזֶּה).
- **"it is not hidden from thee" → "it is not too wonderful for you"** (v11) — Hebrew לֹא־נִפְלֵאת הִוא מִמְּךָ, from the root of פֶּלֶא "wonder": beyond one's power or grasp (Gen 18:14).
- **"that thou shouldest say" → "to say"; "bring it unto us, that we may hear it, and do it" → "take it for us and make us hear it, and we will do it"** (v12, 13) — Hebrew לֵאמֹר "to say, saying"; וְיִקָּחֶהָ "and take it"; וְיַשְׁמִעֵנוּ, the causative "make us hear"; וְנַעֲשֶׂנָּה "and we will do it" (or "that we may do it"). Rom 10:6–8 uses these verses in another wording ("Who will descend into the abyss?"); the Hebrew is translated as it stands.
- **"beyond the sea … go over the sea for us" → "across the sea … pass over for us to the other side of the sea"** (v13) — Hebrew מֵעֵבֶר לַיָּם … אֶל־עֵבֶר הַיָּם, the noun עֵבֶר "the side beyond" both times, with the verb of the same root, עָבַר.
- **"that thou mayest do it" → "to do it"** (v14) — Hebrew לַעֲשֹׂתוֹ.
- **"life and good, and death and evil" kept** (v15) — each noun has the article and the object marker in the Hebrew: "the life and the good, and the death and the evil". טוֹב "good", רַע "evil": also "well-being" and "harm".
- **"In that I command thee" kept** (v16) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוְּךָ, "which / in that I am commanding you": the clause hangs loosely on v15. The Greek has a longer beginning, "If you listen to the commandments of the Lord your God" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept. **"his judgments" → "his ordinances"** — Hebrew מִשְׁפָּטָיו, "judgments, rulings". **"that thou mayest live" → "and you shall live"** — Hebrew וְחָיִיתָ. **"whither thou goest to possess it" → "to which you are going in to possess it"** — Hebrew בָא־שָׁמָּה לְרִשְׁתָּהּ (so "to go in", v18).
- **"turn away, so that thou wilt not hear" → "turns and you do not listen"** (v17) — Hebrew יִפְנֶה "turns (its face)"; וְלֹא תִשְׁמָע. **"worship" → "bow down to"** — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה.
- **"I denounce unto you this day" → "I tell you today"** (v18) — Hebrew הִגַּדְתִּי, a perfect: "I have told", said as the act is done. "You" is plural in "I tell you … you shall surely perish; you shall not prolong", singular in the rest of the chapter. **"ye shall surely perish"** — Hebrew אָבֹד תֹּאבֵדוּן, literally "perishing you shall perish". **"the land" → "the ground"** (v18, 20) — Hebrew הָאֲדָמָה; "the land" of v5, 16 is הָאָרֶץ.
- **"I call heaven and earth to record this day against you" → "I call the heavens and the earth to witness against you today"** (v19) — Hebrew הַעִידֹתִי בָכֶם, a perfect: "I have called to witness against you"; "you" is plural here, singular in "before you" and "you shall choose". **"therefore choose life" → "and you shall choose life"** — Hebrew וּבָחַרְתָּ בַּחַיִּים, also read as a command, "so choose life". **"that both thou and thy seed may live" → "so that you may live, you and your seed"** — Hebrew לְמַעַן תִּחְיֶה "so that you may live" (as 4:1; 16:20).
- **"That thou mayest love … obey … cleave unto him" → "to love … to listen … to cling to him"** (v20) — three Hebrew infinitives; דָּבַק "cling" (Gen 2:24). **"for he is thy life"** — Hebrew כִּי הוּא חַיֶּיךָ; הוּא may be "he" (Yahweh) or "that" (to love, to listen and to cling). **"that thou mayest dwell" → "to dwell"** — Hebrew לָשֶׁבֶת.

### Notes
- **Exile-and-return prophecy** (v1–10) — assumes exile has happened; points beyond it to restoration. Source-critical view: composed in or shortly before the exile. Traditional view: anticipatory prophecy.
- **Circumcision of the heart** (v6) — taken up in Rom 2:29 and Col 2:11. Promises internal transformation as divine gift. Jer 31:33 (new covenant) develops this.
- **"It is not in the heavens"** (v11–14) — Paul uses the passage in Rom 10:6–8 in a freer wording and applies it to "the word of faith". Famous rabbinic use: Bab. Talmud, Bava Metzia 59b — "it is not in heaven" rules that even a divine voice cannot override the majority decision of the sages. Torah belongs to the earthly community.
- **Two ways** (v15, 19) — "life / good" vs "death / evil." Classic two-ways theology (Ps 1, later Qumran "Two Spirits"; early Christian *Didache*).
- **"Choose life"** (v19) — the covenantal decision. Not a fatalistic doctrine; Israel's choice determines the outcome. Moses' final summons.
- **Final chapter of Moses' Great Sermon** — ch 30 closes the long hortatory address (chs 5–30). Chs 31–34 will narrate Moses' final acts and death.
