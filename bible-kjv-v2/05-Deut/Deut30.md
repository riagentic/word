## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"turn thy captivity" → "restore your fortunes"** (v3) — Hebrew וְשָׁב ... אֶת־שְׁבוּתְךָ *ve-shav ... et-shevutkha*. Hebrew double entendre: "turn your turning" — restoration both of captives and of general fortunes.
- **"circumcise thine heart"** (v6) — Hebrew *u-mal YHWH Eloheikha et levavkha*. Internal transformation promised by Yahweh himself. Compare 10:16 (Israel commanded to circumcise their own hearts). Here, Yahweh does it.
- **"not hidden / not far off" → "not too wondrous for you, nor is it far off"** (v11) — Hebrew לֹא־נִפְלֵאת *lo nifle't*. Not beyond understanding / reach.
- **"to record" → "to witness"** (v19) — Hebrew *ha-idoti bakhem*. Invocation of heaven and earth as witnesses.
- **"cleave unto him" → "holding fast to him"** (v20) — *davaq*.
- **"denounce" → "declare"** (v18) — modernized.

### Notes
- **Exile-and-return prophecy** (v1–10) — assumes exile has happened; points beyond it to restoration. Source-critical view: composed in or shortly before the exile. Traditional view: anticipatory prophecy.
- **Circumcision of the heart** (v6) — Paul quotes in Rom 2:29, Col 2:11. Promises internal transformation as divine gift. Jer 31:33 (new covenant) develops this.
- **"It is not in heaven"** (v11–14) — Paul quotes in Rom 10:6–8, reapplying messianically: the word is now the gospel "in the mouth and heart." Famous rabbinic use: Bab. Talmud, Bava Metzia 59b — "it is not in heaven" rules that even a divine voice cannot override the majority decision of the sages. Torah belongs to the earthly community.
- **Two ways** (v15, 19) — "life / good" vs "death / evil." Classic two-ways theology (Ps 1, later Qumran "Two Spirits"; early Christian *Didache*).
- **"Choose life"** (v19) — the covenantal decision. Not a fatalistic doctrine; Israel's choice determines the outcome. Moses' final summons.
- **Final chapter of Moses' Great Sermon** — ch 30 closes the long hortatory address (chs 5–30). Chs 31–34 will narrate Moses' final acts and death.
