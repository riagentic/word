# Mark 9

[1] And he was saying to them, "Amen, I say to you that there are some here of those standing who will by no means taste death until they see the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ having come in power."

[2] And after six days ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ takes along ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ and ⟦james|Yaaqov|James⟧ and ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧, and brings them up into a high mountain privately, alone. And he was transformed before them,

[3] and his garments became glistening, exceedingly white, such as a fuller on the earth is not able thus to whiten.

[4] And there appeared to them ⟦elijah|Eliyahu|Elijah⟧ with ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, and they were talking with ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[5] And answering, ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ says to ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, "Rabbi, it is good for us to be here, and let us make three tents, one for you and one for ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and one for ⟦elijah|Eliyahu|Elijah⟧."

[6] For he did not know what he should answer, for they became terrified.

[7] And there came a cloud overshadowing them, and there came a voice out of the cloud, "**This is my Son, the beloved; hear him.**"

[8] And suddenly, looking around, they no longer saw anyone, but ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ alone with themselves.

[9] And as they were coming down from the mountain, he enjoined them that they should relate to no one the things which they saw, except when the Son of Man should rise from the dead.

[10] And they held the word to themselves, disputing what the rising from the dead is.

[11] And they were asking him, saying, "Why do the scribes say that ⟦elijah|Eliyahu|Elijah⟧ must come first?"

[12] But he said to them, "⟦elijah|Eliyahu|Elijah⟧ indeed, coming first, restores all things; and how is it written concerning the Son of Man, that he should suffer many things and be despised?

[13] But I say to you that ⟦elijah|Eliyahu|Elijah⟧ also has come, and they did to him whatever they wanted, even as it is written concerning him."

[14] And coming to the disciples, they saw a great crowd around them, and scribes disputing with them.

[15] And immediately all the crowd, seeing him, were utterly astounded, and running up they were greeting him.

[16] And he asked them, "What are you disputing with them?"

[17] And one out of the crowd answered him, "Teacher, I brought my son to you, having a speechless spirit;

[18] and wherever it lays hold of him, it dashes him down, and he foams and grinds his teeth and withers; and I told your disciples that they should cast it out, and they did not have the strength."

[19] But he, answering them, says, "O unbelieving generation, how long shall I be with you? How long shall I bear with you? Bring him to me."

[20] And they brought him to him. And seeing him, the spirit immediately convulsed him, and falling on the ground he was rolling about, foaming.

[21] And he asked his father, "How long a time is it since this has happened to him?" And he said, "From childhood;

[22] and often it cast him both into fire and into waters, that it might destroy him; but if you are able in anything, help us, being moved with compassion for us."

[23] But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to him, "'If you are able' — all things are possible to the one who believes."

[24] Immediately crying out, the father of the young child was saying, "**I believe; help my unbelief!**"

[25] But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, seeing that a crowd is running together, rebuked the unclean spirit, saying to it, "Speechless and deaf spirit, I order you, come out of him and enter into him no more."

[26] And crying out and convulsing him much, it came out; and he became as one dead, so that the many were saying that he had died.

[27] But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, taking hold of his hand, raised him, and he rose.

[28] And when he entered into a house, his disciples were asking him privately, "Why were we not able to cast it out?"

[29] And he said to them, "This kind is able to come out by nothing except by prayer."

[30] And going out from there, they were going along through ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧, and he did not want anyone to know;

[31] for he was teaching his disciples and was saying to them, "The Son of Man is delivered up into the hands of men, and they will kill him, and having been killed, after three days he will rise."

[32] But they did not understand the word, and were afraid to ask him.

[33] And they came into ⟦capernaum|Kfar Nachum|Capernaum⟧. And having come to be in the house, he was asking them, "What were you reasoning on the way?"

[34] But they were silent, for with one another they had discussed on the way who is greater.

[35] And sitting down, he called the twelve and says to them, "**If anyone wants to be first, he shall be last of all and servant of all.**"

[36] And taking a young child, he stood it in the midst of them, and taking it in his arms he said to them,

[37] "Whoever accepts one of such young children upon my name accepts me; and whoever accepts me, accepts not me but the one who sent me."

[38] ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧ said to him, "Teacher, we saw someone casting out demons in your name, and we were hindering him, because he was not following us."

[39] But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said, "Do not hinder him, for there is no one who will do a work of power upon my name and will be able quickly to speak evil of me;

[40] **for he who is not against us is for us.**

[41] For whoever gives you a cup of water to drink in the name that you are ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach's|Christ's⟧ — Amen, I say to you that he will by no means lose his wage.

[42] And whoever makes one of these little ones who believe in me stumble, it is better for him if a donkey's millstone lies around his neck and he has been cast into the sea.

[43] And if your hand makes you stumble, cut it off; it is better for you to enter into life maimed than, having the two hands, to go away into ⟦gehenna|Gehenna|hell⟧, into the unquenchable fire.

[45] And if your foot makes you stumble, cut it off; it is better for you to enter into life lame than, having the two feet, to be cast into ⟦gehenna|Gehenna|hell⟧.

[47] And if your eye makes you stumble, cast it out; it is better for you to enter one-eyed into the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ than, having two eyes, to be cast into ⟦gehenna|Gehenna|hell⟧,

[48] '**where their worm does not die and the fire is not quenched**.'

[49] For everyone will be salted with fire.

[50] Salt is good; but if the salt becomes saltless, with what will you season it? Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another."

## Changes

- **"James" → "Jacob"** (v2) — Greek Ἰάκωβος *Iakōbos* = Hebrew יַעֲקֹב *Yaaqov*, the same name as the patriarch; "James" is a later English form.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v2, 4, 5, 8, 23, 25, 27, 39) — Hebrew name preserved. The Greek does not name him in v21; there it has "he", and so does the verse.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv1, 47) — divine name retained.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v41) — Hebrew Anointed.
- **"Peter" → "*Kefa*"** (vv2, 5) — Aramaic name preserved.
- **"And" / "immediately" / present tenses kept** (throughout) — Mark's καί at the start of sentences, εὐθύς "immediately" (v15, 20, 24) and his present tenses ("takes along … brings up", v2; "says", v5, 19, 35) are kept as they are.
- **"Verily" → "Amen"** (v1, 41) — Greek ἀμήν, left untranslated as Mark leaves it.
- **"some of them that stand here" → "some here of those standing"** (v1) — the verse follows ὧδε τῶν ἑστηκότων (WH Treg NA28); RP and the SBLGNT read τῶν ὧδε ἑστηκότων, "of those standing here". "By no means": the strong negation οὐ μή (also v41).
- **"till they have seen the kingdom of God come with power" → "until they see the kingdom of *Elohim* having come in power"** (v1) — Greek ἐληλυθυῖαν, a perfect participle; ἐν δυνάμει, "in power".
- **"leadeth them up … apart by themselves" → "brings them up … privately, alone"** (v2) — Greek ἀναφέρει, κατ' ἰδίαν μόνους.
- **"transfigured" → "transformed"** (v2) — Greek μετεμορφώθη, the verb of Rom 12:2; 2 Cor 3:18: a change of form (μορφή).
- **"his raiment became shining, exceeding white as snow; so as no fuller on earth can white them" → "his garments became glistening, exceedingly white, such as a fuller on the earth is not able thus to whiten"** (v3) — Greek στίλβοντα λευκὰ λίαν οἷα γναφεὺς … οὐ δύναται οὕτως λευκᾶναι; "as snow" (RP ὡς χιών) is not in the critical text. A fuller cleaned and whitened cloth.
- **"answered and said … Master" → "answering … says … Rabbi"** (v5) — Greek ἀποκριθεὶς … λέγει, Ῥαββί.
- **"tabernacles" → "tents"** (v5) — Greek σκηνάς.
- **"wist not what to say; for they were sore afraid" → "did not know what he should answer, for they became terrified"** (v6) — Greek τί ἀποκριθῇ (RP λαλήσει, "say"); ἔκφοβοι … ἐγένοντο.
- **"there was a cloud that overshadowed them: and a voice came" → "there came a cloud overshadowing them, and there came a voice"** (v7) — Greek ἐγένετο twice (WH NA28; Treg RP ἦλθεν, "came", the second time).
- **"my beloved Son" → "my Son, the beloved"** (v7) — Greek ὁ υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός, as 1:11.
- **"save Jesus only with themselves" → "but Yeshua alone with themselves"** (v8) — Greek ἀλλὰ τὸν Ἰησοῦν μόνον μεθ' ἑαυτῶν (Treg NA28 RP; WH: "no longer saw anyone with themselves except Yeshua alone").
- **"charged" → "enjoined"; "tell" → "relate"** (v9) — Greek διεστείλατο (as 5:43; 7:36; 8:15), διηγήσωνται (as 5:16). "From the mountain": ἐκ τοῦ ὄρους, "out of" (WH NA28; Treg RP ἀπό).
- **"till the Son of man were risen" → "except when the Son of Man should rise"** (v9) — Greek εἰ μὴ ὅταν … ἀναστῇ.
- **"they kept that saying with themselves, questioning one with another what the rising from the dead should mean" → "they held the word to themselves, disputing what the rising from the dead is"** (v10) — Greek τὸν λόγον ἐκράτησαν πρὸς ἑαυτοὺς συζητοῦντες τί ἐστιν τὸ ἐκ νεκρῶν ἀναστῆναι. πρὸς ἑαυτούς can go with "held" ("to themselves") or with "disputing" ("among themselves"). κρατέω is "hold" as in 7:3, 8; συζητέω "dispute" as in 1:27; 8:11 (v14, 16).
- **"Why say the scribes"** (v11) — Greek Ὅτι λέγουσιν: ὅτι as a question word, "why" (as 2:16; v28). It can also be read as a statement: "The scribes say that Eliyahu must come first."
- **"Elias verily cometh first, and restoreth all things" → "Eliyahu indeed, coming first, restores all things"** (v12) — Greek Ἠλίας μὲν ἐλθὼν πρῶτον ἀποκαθιστάνει πάντα, a participle and a present tense.
- **"and how it is written … be set at nought" → "and how is it written … be despised?"** (v12) — Greek ἐξουδενηθῇ, "be counted as nothing". The question mark is the editors'; without it the words are a statement: "and how it is written concerning the Son of Man, that he should suffer …". "Concerning": ἐπί with the accusative, as in v13.
- **"Elias is indeed come" → "Eliyahu also has come"** (v13) — Greek καὶ Ἠλίας ἐλήλυθεν; καί is "also" or "even".
- **"whatsoever they listed" → "whatever they wanted"** (v13) — Greek ὅσα ἤθελον.
- **"when he came to his disciples, he saw" → "coming to the disciples, they saw"** (v14) — Greek ἐλθόντες … εἶδον, plural (WH Treg NA28; RP singular).
- **"greatly amazed" → "utterly astounded"** (v15) — Greek ἐξεθαμβήθησαν, a strengthened form of the verb of 1:27 ("astounded").
- **"he asked the scribes, What question ye with them?" → "he asked them, What are you disputing with them?"** (v16) — "the scribes" (RP) is not in the critical text. Editions: πρὸς αὐτούς, "with them", WH Treg NA28 RP ] πρὸς αὑτούς, "among yourselves", the printed SBLGNT (Holmes). The two differ only in the breathing mark, which the old manuscripts do not have.
- **"which hath a dumb spirit" → "having a speechless spirit"** (v17, 25) — Greek ἄλαλον, "not speaking", as 7:37.
- **"wheresoever he taketh him, he teareth him … and pineth away" → "wherever it lays hold of him, it dashes him down … and withers"** (v18) — Greek καταλάβῃ; ῥήσσει, "throws down" (or "tears, convulses"); ξηραίνεται, "dries up, withers" (the verb of 3:1; 4:6; 5:29): he becomes stiff or wastes away.
- **"they could not" → "they did not have the strength"** (v18) — Greek οὐκ ἴσχυσαν, as 5:4.
- **"faithless" → "unbelieving"; "suffer" → "bear with"** (v19) — Greek ἄπιστος (v24 ἀπιστία, "unbelief"); ἀνέξομαι.
- **"when he saw him, straightway the spirit tare him" → "seeing him, the spirit immediately convulsed him"** (v20) — Greek ἰδὼν αὐτὸν τὸ πνεῦμα: the participle is masculine, the noun "spirit" neuter. Either the spirit is spoken of as a person, or the boy is the one who sees ("and when he saw him, the spirit immediately …"). συνεσπάραξεν (WH NA28; Treg RP ἐσπάραξεν) is a strengthened form of the verb of 1:26; v26.
- **"wallowed" → "was rolling about"** (v20) — Greek ἐκυλίετο, imperfect.
- **"Of a child" → "From childhood"** (v21) — Greek Ἐκ παιδιόθεν.
- **"into the fire, and into the waters" → "both into fire and into waters"** (v22) — Greek καὶ εἰς πῦρ … καὶ εἰς ὕδατα, without articles; "waters" is plural.
- **"if thou canst do any thing, have compassion on us, and help us" → "if you are able in anything, help us, being moved with compassion for us"** (v22) — Greek εἴ τι δύνῃ, literally "if you are able anything" (no verb "do"); βοήθησον ἡμῖν σπλαγχνισθεὶς ἐφ' ἡμᾶς: σπλαγχνίζομαι, from σπλάγχνα "inward parts", as 1:41; 6:34; 8:2.
- **"If thou canst believe, all things are possible" → "'If you are able' — all things are possible"** (v23) — Greek Τὸ Εἰ δύνῃ: the article τό quotes the father's own words back to him. "Believe" (RP πιστεῦσαι) is not in the critical text. "Possible" (δυνατά) is of one root with "are able" (δύνῃ); the same word is "powerful" in the letters.
- **"cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe" → "crying out … was saying, I believe"** (v24) — "with tears" and "Lord" (RP) are not in the critical text; nor is "And" at the start (WH NA28; Treg RP καί). "Help" here is a present imperative (βοήθει), in v22 an aorist (βοήθησον).
- **"the people came running together" → "a crowd is running together"** (v25) — Greek ἐπισυντρέχει ὄχλος, a present tense, without the article.
- **"I charge thee" → "I order you"** (v25) — Greek ἐγὼ ἐπιτάσσω σοι, with an emphatic "I"; ἐπιτάσσω as 1:27.
- **"the spirit cried, and rent him sore … insomuch that many said, He is dead" → "crying out and convulsing him much, it came out … so that the many were saying that he had died"** (v26) — Greek κράξας καὶ πολλὰ σπαράξας, again masculine participles; the Greek has no object ("him" is supplied; RP adds αὐτόν). τοὺς πολλούς, "the many", is "most of them". ἀπέθανεν, "he died".
- **"took him by the hand, and lifted him up; and he arose" → "taking hold of his hand, raised him, and he rose"** (v27) — Greek κρατήσας τῆς χειρὸς αὐτοῦ ἤγειρεν αὐτόν, καὶ ἀνέστη, as 1:31; 5:41–42.
- **"into the house" → "into a house"** (v28) — Greek εἰς οἶκον, without the article; it can also mean "home", as in 2:1; 3:20.
- **"Why could not we cast him out?" → "Why were we not able to cast it out?"** (v28) — Greek Ὅτι ἡμεῖς οὐκ ἠδυνήθημεν: "we" is emphatic; ὅτι as in v11. "It" (αὐτό) is neuter, the spirit.
- **"can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting" → "is able to come out by nothing except by prayer"** (v29) — Greek ἐν οὐδενὶ … εἰ μὴ ἐν προσευχῇ (WH NA28); Treg RP add καὶ νηστείᾳ, "and fasting".
- **"passed through" → "were going along through"** (v30) — Greek παρεπορεύοντο (NA28 RP and the SBLGNT), as 2:23; WH Treg ἐπορεύοντο, "were going".
- **"is delivered" → "is delivered up"** (v31) — Greek παραδίδοται, a present tense: "is (being) handed over"; the verb of 1:14; 3:19.
- **"after that he is killed, he shall rise the third day" → "having been killed, after three days he will rise"** (v31) — Greek μετὰ τρεῖς ἡμέρας (WH Treg NA28), as 8:31; RP "on the third day".
- **"they understood not that saying" → "they did not understand the word"** (v32) — Greek ἠγνόουν τὸ ῥῆμα: ἀγνοέω is "not know, be ignorant of" (a different verb from "understand", συνίημι, 8:17); ῥῆμα is "a thing said", a different word from λόγος (v10).
- **"he came to Capernaum … What was it that ye disputed among yourselves by the way?" → "they came into Capernaum … What were you reasoning on the way?"** (v33) — Greek ἦλθον, plural (WH Treg NA28); διελογίζεσθε, as 2:6, 8; 8:16. "Among yourselves" (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"held their peace … had disputed among themselves, who should be the greatest" → "were silent … had discussed … who is greater"** (v34) — Greek ἐσιώπων; διελέχθησαν; τίς μείζων, a comparative ("greater"), which in this Greek also serves for "greatest".
- **"If any man desire to be first, the same shall be last" → "If anyone wants to be first, he shall be last"** (v35) — Greek ἔσται, a future: a command or a statement of what will happen.
- **"a child … set him" → "a young child … stood it"** (v36) — Greek παιδίον, a neuter diminutive (5:39–41; 7:28, 30); the Greek pronoun is neuter and does not say boy or girl.
- **"receive … in my name" → "accepts … upon my name"** (v37, 39) — Greek δέχομαι (as 6:11); ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματί μου, "on the ground of my name". In v38 the preposition is ἐν, "in your name"; in v41 ἐν ὀνόματι.
- **"and he followeth not us: and we forbad him" → "and we were hindering him"** (v38) — Greek καὶ ἐκωλύομεν αὐτόν, ὅτι οὐκ ἠκολούθει ἡμῖν (WH NA28); Treg RP add "who does not follow us" before it. ἐκωλύομεν is an imperfect: "we were hindering" or "we tried to hinder".
- **"a miracle … lightly" → "a work of power … quickly"** (v39) — Greek δύναμιν (as 6:5), ταχύ.
- **"on our part" → "for us"** (v40) — Greek ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν (WH Treg NA28; RP "against you … for you").
- **"in my name, because ye belong to Christ" → "in the name that you are the Mashiach's"** (v41) — Greek ἐν ὀνόματι ὅτι χριστοῦ ἐστε, literally "in name that you are of Mashiach": on the ground that you are his. "My" (RP) is not in the critical text; χριστοῦ has no article.
- **"reward" → "wage"** (v41) — Greek μισθόν.
- **"offend" → "makes … stumble"** (v42, 43, 45, 47) — Greek σκανδαλίζω, "cause to stumble", as 4:17; 6:3; the Greek has no word "sin".
- **"it is better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and he were cast" → "it is better for him if a donkey's millstone lies around his neck and he has been cast"** (v42) — Greek καλόν ἐστιν αὐτῷ μᾶλλον εἰ περίκειται μύλος ὀνικὸς … καὶ βέβληται: literally "it is good for him rather, if …"; a present and a perfect tense. μύλος ὀνικός is the large millstone turned by a donkey (RP "a mill stone").
- **"it is better for thee"** (v43, 45, 47) — Greek καλόν ἐστίν σε … ἤ, literally "it is good for you … than": the Semitic way of making a comparison.
- **"maimed" kept; "having two hands to go into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched" → "having the two hands, to go away into Gehenna, into the unquenchable fire"** (v43) — Greek γέεννα *geenna*, from Hebrew *Ge-Hinnom*, the valley of Hinnom; τὸ πῦρ τὸ ἄσβεστον.
- **"into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched" → "into Gehenna"** (v45) — the second phrase (Treg RP) is not in WH NA28.
- **"pluck it out … with one eye … into hell fire" → "cast it out … one-eyed … into Gehenna"** (v47) — Greek ἔκβαλε, the verb of "cast out" demons (v18, 28, 38); μονόφθαλμον; "of fire" (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"dieth not" kept** (v48) — Greek τελευτᾷ, "comes to its end, dies" (as 7:10).
- **"and every sacrifice shall be salted with salt" dropped** (v49) — WH NA28 and the SBLGNT read only "For everyone will be salted with fire"; Treg RP add καὶ πᾶσα θυσία ἁλὶ ἁλισθήσεται (Lev 2:13).
- **"have lost his saltness" → "becomes saltless"** (v50) — Greek ἄναλον γένηται.
- **"have peace one with another" → "be at peace with one another"** (v50) — Greek εἰρηνεύετε ἐν ἀλλήλοις, "among one another".

### Notes
- **v44, 46** — not in the text: WH and NA28 do not have them; Treg and RP repeat the words of v48 after v43 and v45.
- **v48** quotes Isa 66:24.
- **v7** — "hear him": compare Deut 18:15.
