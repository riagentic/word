# Mark 4

[1] And again he began to teach beside the sea. And there gathers to him a very great crowd, so that he, stepping into a boat, sat in the sea, and all the crowd were by the sea on the land.

[2] And he was teaching them many things in parables, and he was saying to them in his teaching:

[3] "Hear! Behold, the sower went out to sow.

[4] And it came to pass in the sowing that some fell beside the way, and the birds came and devoured it.

[5] And other fell upon the rocky place, where it did not have much earth, and immediately it sprang up, because of not having depth of earth;

[6] and when the sun rose, it was scorched, and because of not having root it withered.

[7] And other fell into the thorns, and the thorns came up and choked it, and it did not give fruit.

[8] And others fell into the good earth and were giving fruit, coming up and growing, and were bearing, one thirty and one sixty and one a hundred."

[9] And he was saying, "**He who has ears to hear, let him hear.**"

[10] And when he came to be alone, those around him with the twelve were asking him about the parables.

[11] And he was saying to them, "To you the mystery of the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has been given; but to those who are outside all things come to be in parables,

[12] so that

'looking they may look and not see,
and hearing they may hear and not understand,
lest they should turn and it be forgiven them.'"

[13] And he says to them, "Do you not know this parable? And how will you come to know all the parables?

[14] The sower sows the word.

[15] And these are the ones beside the way, where the word is sown; and whenever they hear, immediately Satan comes and takes away the word that has been sown into them.

[16] And these likewise are the ones being sown upon the rocky places, who, whenever they hear the word, immediately receive it with joy,

[17] and they have no root in themselves, but are for a time; then, when affliction or persecution comes on account of the word, immediately they are made to stumble.

[18] And others are the ones being sown into the thorns; these are the ones who heard the word,

[19] and the anxieties of the age and the deceit of riches and the desires concerning the remaining things, entering in, choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

[20] And those are the ones sown upon the good earth, who hear the word and accept it and bear fruit, one thirty and one sixty and one a hundred."

[21] And he was saying to them, "Does the lamp come that it may be put under the bushel measure or under the bed? Is it not that it may be put upon the lampstand?

[22] For there is nothing hidden except that it may be manifested, nor did anything become concealed but that it may come into manifest view.

[23] If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear."

[24] And he was saying to them, "Look to what you hear. **In what measure you measure, it will be measured to you**, and it will be added to you.

[25] For he who has, to him it will be given; and he who does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him."

[26] And he was saying, "Thus is the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧: as if a man should cast the seed upon the earth

[27] and should sleep and arise, night and day, and the seed should sprout and lengthen, how, he himself does not know.

[28] Of itself the earth bears fruit, first a blade, then an ear, then full grain in the ear.

[29] But when the fruit allows, immediately he sends the sickle, because the harvest has come."

[30] And he was saying, "How shall we liken the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, or in what parable shall we set it?

[31] As to a grain of mustard, which, when it is sown upon the earth, being smaller than all the seeds that are upon the earth —

[32] and when it is sown, it comes up and becomes greater than all the garden herbs and makes great branches, so that the birds of the heaven are able to dwell under its shade."

[33] And with many such parables he was speaking the word to them, even as they were able to hear;

[34] and without a parable he was not speaking to them, but privately to his own disciples he was explaining all things.

[35] And he says to them on that day, when evening had come, "Let us go through to the other side."

[36] And leaving the crowd, they take him along, as he was, in the boat; and other boats were with him.

[37] And there comes a great storm of wind, and the waves were beating into the boat, so that the boat was already being filled.

[38] And he himself was in the stern, sleeping upon the cushion; and they rouse him and say to him, "Teacher, is it no concern to you that we are perishing?"

[39] And being roused, he rebuked the wind and said to the sea, "**Be silent! Be muzzled!**" And the wind abated, and there came a great calm.

[40] And he said to them, "Why are you cowardly? Do you not yet have faith?"

[41] And they feared a great fear, and were saying to one another, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv11, 26, 30) — divine name retained.
- **"kingdom of God" → "kingdom of *Elohim*"** (vv11, 26, 30) — consistent rendering.
- **"immediately" kept, also for "anon"** (v5, 15, 16, 17, 29) — Greek *euthys*, one rendering throughout.
- **"there was gathered … he said … they took him … there arose" → presents kept** (v1, 13, 35, 36, 37, 38) — Greek συνάγεται, λέγει, παραλαμβάνουσιν, γίνεται, ἐγείρουσιν, λέγουσιν: Mark's present tense in narrative. ἔλεγεν, "he was saying" (v2, 9, 11, 21, 24, 26, 30), is an imperfect.
- **"a great multitude … he entered into a ship, and sat in the sea" → "a very great crowd … stepping into a boat, sat in the sea"** (v1) — Greek ὄχλος πλεῖστος (WH Treg NA28), a superlative; καθῆσθαι ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ, "sat in the sea", that is, in the boat on the water.
- **"in his doctrine" → "in his teaching"** (v2) — Greek διδαχή.
- **"Hearken; Behold, there went out a sower" → "Hear! Behold, the sower went out"** (v3) — Greek ἀκούετε, the verb "hear" of v9, 12, 23, 24, 33; ὁ σπείρων, "the one sowing", with the article.
- **"by the way side" → "beside the way"** (v4, 15) — Greek παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν, "beside the road"; "of the air" (KJV v4) is not in the Greek here.
- **"stony ground … earth" → "the rocky place … earth"** (v5, 16) — Greek τὸ πετρῶδες, rock under thin soil; γῆ is "earth" throughout the chapter (v5, 8, 20, 26, 28, 31), "land" in v1.
- **"sprang up … was up" → "sprang up … rose"** (v5, 6) — Greek ἐξανέτειλεν, ἀνέτειλεν, one root.
- **"it yielded no fruit" → "it did not give fruit"; "did yield fruit that sprang up and increased; and brought forth, some thirty" → "were giving fruit, coming up and growing, and were bearing, one thirty"** (v7, 8) — Greek καρπὸν οὐκ ἔδωκεν; ἐδίδου, ἔφερεν, imperfects; ἀναβαίνοντα καὶ αὐξανόμενα. Greek ἓν τριάκοντα καὶ ἓν ἑξήκοντα καὶ ἓν ἑκατόν, "one thirty …" (NA28, SBLGNT); the same letters are read as εἰς / ἐν, "unto thirty, in sixty, in a hundred" (WH; Treg εἰς three times), or ἐν, "in" (RP; so WH Treg RP in v20).
- **"asked of him the parable" → "were asking him about the parables"** (v10) — Greek ἠρώτων, imperfect; τὰς παραβολάς, plural (WH Treg NA28).
- **"Unto you it is given to know the mystery" → "To you the mystery … has been given"** (v11) — Greek τὸ μυστήριον δέδοται; "to know" (RP γνῶναι) is not in the critical text. μυστήριον is "mystery" throughout.
- **"all these things are done in parables" → "all things come to be in parables"** (v11) — Greek τὰ πάντα γίνεται.
- **"That seeing they may see, and not perceive" → "looking they may look and not see"** (v12) — two Greek verbs: βλέποντες βλέπωσι ("look, see with the eyes") and ἴδωσιν ("see"). "understand" is συνιῶσιν.
- **"lest at any time they should be converted, and their sins should be forgiven them" → "lest they should turn and it be forgiven them"** (v12) — Greek μήποτε ἐπιστρέψωσιν καὶ ἀφεθῇ αὐτοῖς; "their sins" (RP) is not in the critical text. μήποτε is "lest" (or "unless perhaps"). The wording follows Isa 6:9–10 in a form close to the Aramaic Targum ("be forgiven", where the Hebrew and the Septuagint have "be healed").
- **"Know ye not … how then will ye know" → "Do you not know … how will you come to know"** (v13) — two Greek verbs, οἴδατε and γνώσεσθε.
- **"taketh away the word that was sown in their hearts" → "takes away the word that has been sown into them"** (v15) — Greek εἰς αὐτούς (WH Treg NA28); "in their hearts" is RP.
- **"which are sown on stony ground" → "the ones being sown upon the rocky places"** (v16, 18) — Greek σπειρόμενοι, present participles; in v20 σπαρέντες, "sown", a past. NA28 has no "likewise" (ὁμοίως) in v16; WH Treg SBLGNT RP have it.
- **"endure but for a time" → "are for a time"** (v17) — Greek πρόσκαιροί εἰσιν, "are temporary".
- **"affliction" kept; "they are offended" → "they are made to stumble"** (v17) — Greek θλῖψις; σκανδαλίζονται, from σκάνδαλον, a trap or stumbling block.
- **"such as hear the word" → "the ones who heard the word"** (v18) — Greek ἀκούσαντες, a past (WH Treg NA28).
- **"the cares of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, and the lusts of other things" → "the anxieties of the age and the deceit of riches and the desires concerning the remaining things"** (v19) — Greek αἱ μέριμναι τοῦ αἰῶνος ("this", RP, is not in the critical text); ἡ ἀπάτη τοῦ πλούτου; αἱ περὶ τὰ λοιπὰ ἐπιθυμίαι.
- **"receive it" → "accept it"** (v20) — Greek παραδέχονται; a different verb from "receive" (λαμβάνουσιν, v16).
- **"Is a candle brought to be put under a bushel" → "Does the lamp come that it may be put under the bushel measure"** (v21) — Greek ἔρχεται ὁ λύχνος, "does the lamp come"; *lychnos* = oil lamp, not a wax candle; μόδιος is a vessel for measuring grain (about 8.7 litres). λυχνία is a lampstand.
- **"which shall not be manifested; neither was any thing kept secret, but that it should come abroad" → "except that it may be manifested, nor did anything become concealed but that it may come into manifest view"** (v22) — Greek ἐὰν μὴ ἵνα φανερωθῇ … ἀλλ' ἵνα ἔλθῃ εἰς φανερόν: twice a purpose ("in order that"); φανερόω and φανερός are one root.
- **"Take heed what ye hear" → "Look to what you hear"** (v24) — Greek βλέπετε, the verb of v12.
- **"with what measure ye mete … and unto you that hear shall more be given" → "In what measure you measure … and it will be added to you"** (v24) — Greek ἐν ᾧ μέτρῳ μετρεῖτε; προστεθήσεται ὑμῖν; "that hear" (RP) is not in the critical text, and the Greek has no "more".
- **"So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground" → "Thus is the kingdom of *Elohim*: as if a man should cast the seed upon the earth"** (v26) — Greek οὕτως … ὡς ἄνθρωπος βάλῃ τὸν σπόρον ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς.
- **"rise night and day … spring and grow up, he knoweth not how" → "arise, night and day … sprout and lengthen, how, he himself does not know"** (v27) — Greek ἐγείρηται; βλαστᾷ καὶ μηκύνηται; ὡς οὐκ οἶδεν αὐτός.
- **"For the earth bringeth forth fruit of herself … the full corn in the ear" → "Of itself the earth bears fruit … full grain in the ear"** (v28) — Greek αὐτομάτη, "self-moved"; καρποφορεῖ as v20; "corn" in 1611 = any grain. "For" (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"when the fruit is brought forth, immediately he putteth in the sickle" → "when the fruit allows, immediately he sends the sickle"** (v29) — Greek ὅταν δὲ παραδοῖ ὁ καρπός: παραδίδωμι, "hand over" (1:14; 3:19), here "permits" or "yields itself"; ἀποστέλλει τὸ δρέπανον, "sends (out) the sickle" (Joel 3:13); παρέστηκεν, "stands ready".
- **"Whereunto shall we liken … or with what comparison shall we compare it?" → "How shall we liken … or in what parable shall we set it?"** (v30) — Greek πῶς ὁμοιώσωμεν … ἐν τίνι αὐτὴν παραβολῇ θῶμεν (WH Treg NA28).
- **"It is like a grain of mustard seed, which … is less than all the seeds" → "As to a grain of mustard, which … being smaller than all the seeds that are upon the earth —"** (v31) — Greek ὡς κόκκῳ σινάπεως … μικρότερον ὄν: the sentence has no main verb and breaks off; it starts again in v32. μικρότερον is a comparative.
- **"groweth up, and becometh greater than all herbs, and shooteth out great branches; so that the fowls of the air may lodge" → "comes up and becomes greater than all the garden herbs and makes great branches, so that the birds of the heaven are able to dwell"** (v32) — Greek ἀναβαίνει (as v7, 8); λάχανα, garden plants; ποιεῖ; τὰ πετεινὰ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ; κατασκηνοῦν, "pitch a tent, settle" (Dan 4:12, 21; Ezek 17:23).
- **"spake he … he expounded all things to his disciples" → "he was speaking … to his own disciples he was explaining all things"** (v33, 34) — Greek imperfects; τοῖς ἰδίοις μαθηταῖς (WH NA28); ἐπέλυεν, "untie, resolve".
- **"Let us pass over unto the other side" → "Let us go through to the other side"** (v35) — Greek διέλθωμεν.
- **"when they had sent away the multitude, they took him even as he was in the ship" → "leaving the crowd, they take him along, as he was, in the boat"** (v36) — Greek ἀφέντες τὸν ὄχλον, the verb of 1:18, 20; παραλαμβάνουσιν, "take along" (elsewhere "receive"); ὡς ἦν ἐν τῷ πλοίῳ can also be read "as he was in the boat".
- **"the waves beat into the ship, so that it was now full" → "the waves were beating into the boat, so that the boat was already being filled"** (v37) — Greek ἐπέβαλλεν, γεμίζεσθαι, action in progress.
- **"asleep on a pillow: and they awake him … Master, carest thou not that we perish?" → "sleeping upon the cushion; and they rouse him … Teacher, is it no concern to you that we are perishing?"** (v38) — Greek τὸ προσκεφάλαιον, with the article; ἐγείρουσιν; διδάσκαλε, "Teacher"; οὐ μέλει σοι.
- **"he arose … Peace, be still … ceased" → "being roused … Be silent! Be muzzled! … abated"** (v39) — Greek διεγερθείς; σιώπα, πεφίμωσο: two verbs, "be silent" and "be muzzled" (as 1:25; a perfect: "be muzzled and stay so"); ἐκόπασεν, "grew tired, abated".
- **"Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?" → "Why are you cowardly? Do you not yet have faith?"** (v40) — Greek δειλοί, "cowardly, timid", not the word "fear" of v41; οὔπω, "not yet" (WH Treg NA28). The Greek has no "so".
- **"they feared exceedingly" → "they feared a great fear"** (v41) — Greek ἐφοβήθησαν φόβον μέγαν.
- **"fowls of the air" → "birds"** (v4) — Greek *peteina*; "fowls" is archaic.

### Notes
- **v12** quotes Isa 6:9-10.
