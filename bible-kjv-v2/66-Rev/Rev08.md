# Revelation 8

[1] And when he opened the seventh seal, there came to be silence in heaven about half an hour.

[2] And I saw the seven angels who stand before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and seven trumpets were given to them.

[3] And another angel came and stood at the altar, having a golden censer; and much incense was given to him, that he shall give it to the prayers of all the saints upon the golden altar which is before the throne.

[4] And the smoke of the incense went up with the prayers of the saints out of the hand of the angel before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[5] And the angel took the censer, and he filled it from the fire of the altar and cast it to the earth; and there came thunders and voices and lightnings and an earthquake.

[6] And the seven angels who had the seven trumpets made themselves ready to sound the trumpets.

[7] And the first sounded the trumpet; and there came hail and fire mixed with blood, and it was cast to the earth; and the third of the earth was burned up, and the third of the trees was burned up, and all green grass was burned up.

[8] And the second angel sounded the trumpet; and as it were a great mountain burning with fire was cast into the sea; and the third of the sea became blood,

[9] and the third of the creatures which are in the sea died, those having souls, and the third of the ships were ruined.

[10] And the third angel sounded the trumpet; and there fell out of heaven a great star burning as a torch, and it fell upon the third of the rivers and upon the springs of the waters.

[11] And the name of the star is called Wormwood. And the third of the waters became wormwood, and many of the men died from the waters, because they were made bitter.

[12] And the fourth angel sounded the trumpet; and the third of the sun was struck, and the third of the moon, and the third of the stars, so that the third of them might be darkened, and the day might not shine for the third of it, and the night likewise.

[13] And I saw, and I heard one eagle flying in mid-heaven, saying with a loud voice, "Woe, woe, woe to those who dwell on the earth, from the remaining voices of the trumpet of the three angels who are about to sound the trumpet!"

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v2, 4) — systematic.
- **"And" kept at the head of the sentences** (throughout) — Greek καί.
- **"when he had opened" → "when he opened"** (v1) — Greek ὅταν ἤνοιξεν: ὅταν "whenever" with a past indicative, against normal grammar (RP ὅτε); the sense is plain "when". The subject is not named ("the Lamb" is from 6:1).
- **"about the space of half an hour" → "about half an hour"** (v1) — Greek ὡς ἡμιώριον *hōs hēmiōrion*, "as it were / about a half-hour"; the measure is kept as the Greek has it.
- **"stood" → "stand"** (v2) — Greek ἑστήκασιν, perfect with present sense; "the seven angels" has the article, as a known group.
- **"that he should offer it with the prayers of all saints" → "that he shall give it to the prayers of all the saints"** (v3) — Greek ἵνα δώσει ταῖς προσευχαῖς: δίδωμι "give" with a plain dative, "give to the prayers" (that is, add it to them; or "give it for the prayers"); "offer … with" is an interpretation. δώσει is a future indicative after ἵνα (RP δώσῃ). θυμιάματα is a plural ("incenses"). ἐπὶ τοῦ θυσιαστηρίου "at / over the altar".
- **"which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up" → "went up with the prayers of the saints"** (v4) — Greek ταῖς προσευχαῖς, the same plain dative as in v3: "with the prayers" or "for the prayers"; the Greek leaves it open.
- **"took" kept** (v5) — Greek εἴληφεν, a perfect ("has taken") among aorists, as 5:7.
- **"filled it with fire of the altar, and cast it into the earth" → "filled it from the fire of the altar and cast it to the earth"** (v5) — Greek ἐκ τοῦ πυρός "from the fire"; ἔβαλεν εἰς τὴν γῆν.
- **"voices, and thunderings, and lightnings" → "thunders and voices and lightnings"** (v5) — Greek order βρονταὶ καὶ φωναὶ καὶ ἀστραπαί; φωναί is "voices" (not "rumblings").
- **"prepared themselves to sound" → "made themselves ready to sound the trumpets"** (v6) — Greek ἡτοίμασαν αὐτοὺς ἵνα σαλπίσωσιν; σαλπίζω is one verb, "sound the trumpet". The pronoun is αὐτούς in Treg and NA28, αὑτούς in WH and SBLGNT (ἑαυτούς RP): the difference is a breathing mark only, and all mean "themselves".
- **"The first angel sounded" → "the first sounded the trumpet"** (v7) — Greek ὁ πρῶτος, without "angel".
- **"there followed hail and fire mingled with blood, and they were cast" → "there came hail and fire mixed with blood, and it was cast"** (v7) — Greek ἐγένετο; μεμιγμένα ἐν αἵματι, literally "mixed in blood"; ἐβλήθη is singular.
- **"the third part" → "the third"** (throughout) — Greek τὸ τρίτον *to triton*, with the article: "the third (part)". The first clause of v7, "the third of the earth was burned up", is not in the KJV; all the editions consulted have it.
- **"as it were a great mountain burning with fire"** (v8) — Greek ὡς ὄρος μέγα πυρὶ καιόμενον: "as it were" (ὡς) marks a likeness, not a mountain; no "something".
- **"the creatures which were in the sea, and had life" → "the creatures which are in the sea, … those having souls"** (v9) — Greek τῶν κτισμάτων τῶν ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ, τὰ ἔχοντα ψυχάς: ψυχή is "soul"; the participle stands in the nominative after a genitive (the grammar is broken in the Greek), kept as a loose apposition. The verb "died" stands before its subject in Greek.
- **"destroyed" → "ruined"** (v9) — Greek διεφθάρησαν (διαφθείρω, as 11:18); plural verb with the singular "the third".
- **"burning as it were a lamp" → "burning as a torch"** (v10) — Greek λαμπάς *lampas*, a torch.
- **"fountains of waters" → "springs of the waters"** (v10) — Greek τὰς πηγὰς τῶν ὑδάτων, as 7:17.
- **"Wormwood" kept** (v11) — Greek ὁ Ἄψινθος *ho Apsinthos* (the article is absent in Treg), the bitter plant *Artemisia absinthium*; ἐγένετο … εἰς ἄψινθον "turned into wormwood".
- **"many men died of the waters" → "many of the men died from the waters"** (v11) — Greek πολλοὶ τῶν ἀνθρώπων … ἐκ τῶν ὑδάτων; ἐπικράνθησαν "they were made bitter" (plural, the waters).
- **"was smitten" → "was struck"** (v12) — Greek ἐπλήγη (πλήσσω), the verb from which πληγή "plague, stroke" (9:18, 20) comes.
- **"the day shone not for a third part of it" → "the day might not shine for the third of it"** (v12) — Greek ἡ ἡμέρα μὴ φάνῃ τὸ τρίτον αὐτῆς; "their light" is not in the Greek ("the third of them").
- **"an angel flying through the midst of heaven" → "one eagle flying in mid-heaven"** (v13) — Greek ἑνὸς ἀετοῦ "one eagle" (all the editions consulted, RP included); the KJV has "angel". εἷς "one" stands here much as "a(n)". μεσουράνημα *mesouranēma*, the zenith, the middle of the sky; "crying" is not in the Greek (λέγοντος "saying").
- **"Woe, woe, woe, to the inhabiters of the earth" → "Woe, woe, woe to those who dwell on the earth"** (v13) — Greek τοὺς κατοικοῦντας, an accusative after οὐαί (against normal grammar; RP has the dative).
- **"by reason of the other voices of the trumpet" → "from the remaining voices of the trumpet"** (v13) — Greek ἐκ τῶν λοιπῶν φωνῶν τῆς σάλπιγγος: ἐκ "from, because of"; φωνή "voice" (the sound of the trumpet); "trumpet" is singular.
