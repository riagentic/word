# Revelation 9

[1] And the fifth angel sounded the trumpet; and I saw a star out of heaven fallen to the earth, and the key of the well of the abyss was given to him.

[2] And he opened the well of the abyss, and smoke went up out of the well as the smoke of a great furnace, and the sun was darkened, and the air, from the smoke of the well.

[3] And out of the smoke locusts came out to the earth, and authority was given to them as the scorpions of the earth have authority.

[4] And it was said to them that they shall not harm the grass of the earth nor any green thing nor any tree, but only the men who do not have the seal of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ on their foreheads.

[5] And it was given to them that they should not kill them, but that they shall be tormented five months; and their torment was as the torment of a scorpion when it strikes a man.

[6] And in those days men will seek death and will by no means find it, and they will desire to die, and death flees from them.

[7] And the likenesses of the locusts were like horses made ready for war; and on their heads as it were crowns like gold, and their faces as faces of men,

[8] and they had hair as the hair of women, and their teeth were as those of lions,

[9] and they had breastplates as breastplates of iron, and the voice of their wings was as the voice of chariots of many horses running to war;

[10] and they have tails like scorpions, and stings, and in their tails is their authority to harm men five months.

[11] They have over them a king, the angel of the abyss; his name in ⟦hebrew|Ivrit|Hebrew⟧ is **⟦abaddon|Avaddon|Abaddon⟧**, and in the Greek he has the name **Apollyon**.

[12] The one woe has gone away; behold, there come yet two woes after these things.

[13] And the sixth angel sounded the trumpet; and I heard one voice out of the horns of the golden altar which is before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[14] saying to the sixth angel, the one having the trumpet, "Loose the four angels who are bound at the great river ⟦euphrates|Perat|Euphrates⟧."

[15] And the four angels were loosed, who had been made ready for the hour and day and month and year, that they should kill the third of men.

[16] And the number of the armies of the cavalry was two myriads of myriads; I heard their number.

[17] And thus I saw the horses in the vision, and those sitting on them, having breastplates of fire and of hyacinth and of sulfur; and the heads of the horses were as heads of lions, and out of their mouths proceeds fire and smoke and sulfur.

[18] From these three plagues the third of men were killed, from the fire and the smoke and the sulfur proceeding out of their mouths.

[19] For the authority of the horses is in their mouth and in their tails; for their tails are like serpents, having heads, and with them they harm.

[20] And the rest of men, who were not killed in these plagues, did not even repent of the works of their hands, that they shall not bow down to the demons and the idols of gold and of silver and of copper and of stone and of wood, which can neither see nor hear nor walk;

[21] and they did not repent of their murders nor of their potions nor of their sexual immorality nor of their thefts.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v4, 13) — systematic.
- **"And" kept at the head of the sentences** (throughout) — Greek καί.
- **"a star fall from heaven unto the earth" → "a star out of heaven fallen to the earth"** (v1) — Greek πεπτωκότα, perfect participle: the star had already fallen when it was seen. "To him … he opened": Greek αὐτῷ agrees with ἀστήρ "star" (masculine), so "to it … it opened" is equally possible; the gender is grammatical.
- **"the bottomless pit" → "the well of the abyss"** (v1, 2) — Greek τὸ φρέαρ τῆς ἀβύσσου *to phrear tēs abyssou*: φρέαρ is a "well" (John 4:11), here the shaft that goes down to the abyss; "bottomless pit" is a paraphrase. "The key of" is a plain genitive.
- **"by reason of the smoke" → "from the smoke"** (v2) — Greek ἐκ τοῦ καπνοῦ. "Was darkened" is ἐσκοτώθη (SBLGNT, WH, NA28; Treg and RP ἐσκοτίσθη, the same sense); it is singular and stands before "the sun".
- **"power" → "authority"** (v3, 10, 19) — Greek ἐξουσία, one word throughout.
- **"it was commanded them that they should not hurt" → "it was said to them that they shall not harm"** (v4) — Greek ἐρρέθη "it was said"; ἵνα μὴ ἀδικήσουσιν, a future indicative after ἵνα (SBLGNT, WH, NA28; Treg and RP have the subjunctive). ἀδικέω "harm", as 6:6; 7:2–3.
- **"but only those men"** (v4) — Greek εἰ μὴ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους, literally "except the men", though men are not part of what was named before. "On their foreheads": the Greek has the article only (ἐπὶ τῶν μετώπων; RP adds αὐτῶν "their").
- **"it was given that they should not kill them, but that they should be tormented" → "it was given to them that they should not kill them, but that they shall be tormented"** (v5) — Greek ἐδόθη αὐτοῖς ("to them", masculine: Treg, NA28, SBLGNT; WH and RP αὐταῖς, feminine, agreeing with "locusts"); ἵνα βασανισθήσονται is a future passive after ἵνα. No "allowed".
- **"five months"** (v5, 10) — Greek μῆνας πέντε, kept as it stands.
- **"their torment was as the torment of a scorpion, when he striketh a man"** (v5) — the Greek has no verb ("was" is supplied, as in v7–9, 17); παίω "strike", not the special word "sting".
- **"shall not find it … death shall flee from them" → "will by no means find it … death flees from them"** (v6) — Greek οὐ μὴ εὑρήσουσιν, the strong negation; φεύγει is a present tense (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28; RP has the future φεύξεται). ἐπιθυμήσουσιν "will desire".
- **"the shapes of the locusts were like unto horses prepared unto battle" → "the likenesses of the locusts were like horses made ready for war"** (v7) — Greek τὰ ὁμοιώματα … ὅμοια; πόλεμος is "war" (also v9).
- **"as it were crowns like gold"** (v7) — Greek ὡς στέφανοι ὅμοιοι χρυσῷ (RP: χρυσοῖ "golden"): twice removed from real crowns of gold. ὡς is "as", ὅμοιος "like" throughout.
- **"the sound of their wings was as the sound of chariots of many horses" → "the voice of their wings was as the voice of chariots of many horses"** (v9) — Greek φωνή "voice", one word throughout the book; ἁρμάτων ἵππων πολλῶν, two genitives side by side.
- **"they had tails like unto scorpions, and there were stings in their tails: and their power was to hurt" → "they have tails like scorpions, and stings, and in their tails is their authority to harm"** (v10) — Greek ἔχουσιν, present; ἐν ταῖς οὐραῖς αὐτῶν ἡ ἐξουσία αὐτῶν (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"And they had a king over them" → "They have over them a king"** (v11) — Greek ἔχουσιν, present, with no "and".
- **"in the Hebrew tongue" → "in Hebrew"** (v11) — Greek Ἑβραϊστί *Hebraisti*, "in the language of the Hebrews".
- **"Abaddon … Apollyon"** (v11) — Greek Ἀβαδδών, the Hebrew אֲבַדּוֹן *avaddon* "destruction" (Job 26:6; Prov 15:11); Ἀπολλύων *Apollyōn*, a Greek participle, "the one destroying". The explanation "the Destroyer" is not in the text.
- **"One woe is past; and, behold, there come two woes more hereafter" → "The one woe has gone away; behold, there come yet two woes after these things"** (v12) — Greek Ἡ οὐαὶ ἡ μία ἀπῆλθεν "the one woe" (Semitic "one" for "first"); ἔρχεται is singular with "two woes"; "woe" is treated as a feminine noun.
- **"a voice from the four horns of the golden altar" → "one voice out of the horns of the golden altar"** (v13) — Greek φωνὴν μίαν "one voice" (εἷς as 8:13); ἐκ τῶν κεράτων without "four" (SBLGNT, WH, Treg); RP and NA28 add τεσσάρων "four".
- **"Saying to the sixth angel which had the trumpet"** (v14) — Greek λέγοντα (masculine, though "voice" is feminine; RP λέγουσαν) and ὁ ἔχων (nominative after a dative): the grammar is broken in the Greek.
- **"Loose" kept** (v14, 15) — Greek λῦσον, ἐλύθησαν, the opposite of "bound" (δεδεμένους).
- **"prepared for an hour, and a day, and a month, and a year" → "made ready for the hour and day and month and year"** (v15) — Greek εἰς τὴν ὥραν καὶ ἡμέραν καὶ μῆνα καὶ ἐνιαυτόν: one article for the four words, so one appointed point of time, not a length of time.
- **"the third part of men" → "the third of men"** (v15, 18) — Greek τὸ τρίτον τῶν ἀνθρώπων, as chapter 8.
- **"the army of the horsemen were two hundred thousand thousand" → "the armies of the cavalry was two myriads of myriads"** (v16) — Greek τῶν στρατευμάτων τοῦ ἱππικοῦ, plural "armies"; δισμυριάδες μυριάδων *dismyriades myriadōn* (SBLGNT, WH, NA28; Treg δύο μυριάδες; RP μυριάδες μυριάδων "myriads of myriads"): a myriad is ten thousand, so twice ten thousand times ten thousand, 200,000,000. The Greek number is kept as it stands.
- **"breastplates of fire, and of jacinth, and brimstone" → "breastplates of fire and of hyacinth and of sulfur"** (v17) — Greek πυρίνους καὶ ὑακινθίνους καὶ θειώδεις, three adjectives: fiery, hyacinth-coloured (a dark blue), sulfurous; they answer to the fire, smoke and sulfur of the mouths. θεῖον *theion* is "sulfur" ("brimstone").
- **"issued" → "proceeds"** (v17) — Greek ἐκπορεύεται, present tense, singular.
- **"By these three was the third part of men killed" → "From these three plagues the third of men were killed"** (v18) — Greek ἀπὸ τῶν τριῶν πληγῶν τούτων; πληγή *plēgē* "plague", literally "blow, stroke".
- **"their power is in their mouth"** (v19) — Greek ἡ ἐξουσία τῶν ἵππων "the authority of the horses"; ἐν τῷ στόματι singular.
- **"with them they do hurt" → "with them they harm"** (v19) — Greek ἀδικοῦσιν, as v4, 10; "wound" is not the word.
- **"yet repented not of the works of their hands, that they should not worship devils" → "did not even repent of the works of their hands, that they shall not bow down to the demons"** (v20) — Greek οὐδὲ μετενόησαν "did not even repent" (SBLGNT, NA28; WH and RP οὐ "did not"; Treg οὔτε "neither"); μετανοέω ἐκ "repent out of"; ἵνα μὴ προσκυνήσουσιν, future indicative after ἵνα; προσκυνέω "bow down to" (commonly "worship"); δαιμόνια "demons", with the article. "Give up" is not in the Greek.
- **"idols of gold, and silver, and brass, and stone, and of wood" → "the idols of gold and of silver and of copper and of stone and of wood"** (v20) — five Greek adjectives, each with the article; χαλκᾶ "of copper" (copper or its alloy bronze).
- **"nor of their sorceries" → "nor of their potions"** (v21) — Greek φαρμάκων *pharmakōn* (SBLGNT, WH, NA28), the plural of φάρμακον: a drug, a poison, a potion or charm used in sorcery; Treg and RP read φαρμακειῶν "sorceries" (the practice; Gal 5:20). "Fornication" → "sexual immorality" (πορνεία).

### Notes
- **v11** — a play on names: both mean destruction (ἀπώλεια, ἀπόλλυμι).
- **v14** — the Euphrates token gives the Hebrew name of the river; the Greek has Εὐφράτης.
