# Revelation 11

[1] And a reed like a rod was given to me, saying, "Arise and measure the sanctuary of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and the altar and those who bow down in it,

[2] and the court which is outside the sanctuary cast out outside, and do not measure it, because it was given to the nations, and they will trample the holy city forty and two months.

[3] And I will give to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy a thousand two hundred and sixty days, clothed in sackcloth."

[4] These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands which stand before the Lord of the earth.

[5] And if anyone wants to harm them, fire proceeds out of their mouth and devours their enemies; and if anyone should want to harm them, thus he must be killed.

[6] These have the authority to shut the heaven, so that no rain may fall during the days of their prophecy; and they have authority over the waters to turn them into blood, and to strike the earth with every plague, as often as they want.

[7] And when they finish their testimony, the beast that comes up out of the abyss will make war with them and overcome them and kill them.

[8] And their corpse is on the broad street of the great city, which is called spiritually ⟦sodom|Sedom|Sodom⟧ and ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, where also their Lord was crucified.

[9] And some out of the peoples and tribes and tongues and nations see their corpse three days and a half, and they do not let their corpses be put into a grave.

[10] And those who dwell on the earth rejoice over them and make merry, and they will send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who dwell on the earth.

[11] And after the three days and a half a spirit of life from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ entered into them, and they stood upon their feet, and great fear fell upon those beholding them;

[12] and they heard a loud voice out of heaven saying to them, "Come up here." And they went up into heaven in the cloud, and their enemies beheld them.

[13] And in that hour there came a great earthquake, and the tenth of the city fell, and there were killed in the earthquake seven thousand names of men, and the rest became afraid and gave glory to the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of heaven.

[14] The second woe has gone away; behold, the third woe comes quickly.

[15] And the seventh angel sounded the trumpet; and there came loud voices in heaven, saying, "**The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his ⟦mashiach|Mashiach|Christ⟧, and he will reign unto the ages of the ages.**"

[16] And the twenty-four elders, who sit before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ on their thrones, fell upon their faces and bowed down to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[17] saying,

"**We give thanks to you, Lord ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, the Almighty,
the one who is and the one who was,
because you have taken your great power
and reigned.
[18] And the nations were wrathful,
and your wrath came,
and the time of the dead to be judged,
and to give the wage to your slaves the prophets and to the saints
and to those who fear your name,
the small and the great,
and to ruin those who ruin the earth**."

[19] And the sanctuary of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ which is in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant appeared in his sanctuary; and there came lightnings and voices and thunders and an earthquake and great hail.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v15) — systematic.
- **"And" kept at the head of the sentences** (throughout) — Greek καί.
- **"there was given me a reed like unto a rod: and the angel stood, saying" → "a reed like a rod was given to me, saying"** (v1) — Greek κάλαμος ὅμοιος ῥάβδῳ, λέγων *kalamos homoios rhabdō, legōn*: "saying" is a masculine nominative with nothing to agree with (the grammar is broken in the Greek); who speaks is not said ("the angel stood" is not in the Greek, nor "I was told"). κάλαμος is a "reed" (here used for measuring, as 21:15), ῥάβδος a "rod".
- **"Rise" → "Arise"** (v1) — Greek Ἔγειρε.
- **"the temple of God" → "the sanctuary of *Elohim*"** (v1, 2, 19) — Greek ναός *naos*, the sanctuary itself (the temple building), not the whole precinct (ἱερόν, which this book does not use).
- **"them that worship therein" → "those who bow down in it"** (v1), **"worshipped God" → "bowed down to *Elohim*"** (v16) — Greek προσκυνέω "bow down, do obeisance" (commonly "worship").
- **"the court which is without the temple leave out" → "the court which is outside the sanctuary cast out outside"** (v2) — Greek ἔκβαλε ἔξωθεν, literally "cast out outside"; ἐκβάλλω is "cast out", stronger than "leave out".
- **"it is given unto the Gentiles" → "it was given to the nations"** (v2) — Greek ἐδόθη, aorist; ἔθνη "nations".
- **"forty and two months" kept** (v2) — the verse follows τεσσεράκοντα καὶ δύο (WH, NA28, RP: "+ καὶ"); the printed SBLGNT text and Treg have τεσσεράκοντα δύο "forty-two". The number is kept as the Greek has it: forty-two months are three and a half years (compare the 1,260 days of v3 and 12:6, and 13:5).
- **"I will give power unto my two witnesses" → "I will give to my two witnesses"** (v3) — Greek δώσω … καὶ προφητεύσουσιν: "give" has no object ("power", "authority" are not in the Greek); a Semitic turn, "I will grant my two witnesses to prophesy".
- **"a thousand two hundred and threescore days" → "a thousand two hundred and sixty days"** (v3) — Greek ἡμέρας χιλίας διακοσίας ἑξήκοντα, written out as in the Greek (1,260 days, forty-two months of thirty days).
- **"clothed in sackcloth"** (v3) — Greek σάκκους, plural. "Clothed" is περιβεβλημένοι (nominative: NA28, RP, SBLGNT; WH and Treg have the accusative περιβεβλημένους, against the grammar).
- **"candlesticks" → "lampstands"** (v4) — Greek λυχνίαι *lychniai*, stands for oil lamps (as 1:12). "Which stand" is a masculine participle (ἑστῶτες) after feminine nouns: the persons are in view (RP has the feminine).
- **"the God of the earth" → "the Lord of the earth"** (v4) — Greek τοῦ κυρίου τῆς γῆς.
- **"if any man will hurt them" → "if anyone wants to harm them … if anyone should want to harm them"** (v5) — Greek εἴ τις … θέλει, then εἴ τις θελήσῃ (subjunctive after εἰ: WH, NA28, SBLGNT; RP θέλει again); θέλω "want"; ἀδικέω "harm", as 6:6; 9:4.
- **"devoureth" kept** (v5) — Greek κατεσθίει, as 10:9–10. "In this manner" → "thus" (οὕτως); "doomed" is not in the Greek (δεῖ "must").
- **"These have power to shut heaven, that it rain not" → "These have the authority to shut the heaven, so that no rain may fall"** (v6) — Greek τὴν ἐξουσίαν … ἐξουσίαν, "authority" twice; οὐρανός is "heaven" throughout (here the sky); ἵνα μὴ ὑετὸς βρέχῃ, literally "that rain may not rain".
- **"in the days of their prophecy" → "during the days of their prophecy"** (v6) — Greek τὰς ἡμέρας τῆς προφητείας αὐτῶν, an accusative of duration; προφητεία "prophecy".
- **"to smite the earth with all plagues, as often as they will" → "to strike the earth with every plague, as often as they want"** (v6) — Greek πατάξαι … ἐν πάσῃ πληγῇ, singular.
- **"when they shall have finished" → "when they finish"** (v7) — Greek ὅταν τελέσωσιν; τελέω "finish", as 10:7.
- **"the beast that ascendeth out of the bottomless pit shall make war against them, and shall overcome them" → "the beast that comes up out of the abyss will make war with them and overcome them"** (v7) — Greek τὸ θηρίον *to thērion* "the beast" (with the article, though not named before; a different word from the "living creatures", ζῷα); ἐκ τῆς ἀβύσσου "out of the abyss"; ποιήσει μετʼ αὐτῶν πόλεμον "will make war with them"; νικήσει "will overcome", as 6:2.
- **"their dead bodies shall lie in the street" → "their corpse is on the broad street"** (v8) — Greek τὸ πτῶμα αὐτῶν, singular (also in v9a; plural τὰ πτώματα in v9b), a "corpse"; the Greek has no verb ("is" is supplied; "shall lie" is not there); πλατεῖα *plateia*, the broad street or open square of a city.
- **"spiritually" kept** (v8) — Greek πνευματικῶς *pneumatikōs*, "spiritually, in a spiritual way"; "symbolically" or "figuratively" is an interpretation of it.
- **"where also our Lord was crucified" → "where also their Lord was crucified"** (v8) — Greek ὁ κύριος αὐτῶν "their Lord".
- **"they of the people … shall see their dead bodies … and shall not suffer" → "some out of the peoples … see their corpse … and they do not let"** (v9) — Greek βλέπουσιν, ἀφίουσιν, present tenses (RP has the future ἀφήσουσιν); ἐκ τῶν λαῶν "(some) out of the peoples", a partitive without a noun. "Kindreds" → "tribes"; γλῶσσαι "tongues".
- **"three days and an half"** (v9, 11) — Greek ἡμέρας τρεῖς καὶ ἥμισυ, kept as it stands.
- **"in graves" → "into a grave"** (v9) — Greek εἰς μνῆμα, singular.
- **"shall rejoice over them, and make merry, and shall send gifts" → "rejoice over them and make merry, and they will send gifts"** (v10) — Greek χαίρουσιν … εὐφραίνονται, present tenses (RP future), then πέμψουσιν, future (RP δώσουσιν "will give"): the change of tense is in the Greek.
- **"tormented them that dwelt on the earth"** (v10) — Greek ἐβασάνισαν, a verb, "tormented" ("had been a torment" turns it into a noun).
- **"the Spirit of life from God entered into them" → "a spirit of life from *Elohim* entered into them"** (v11) — Greek πνεῦμα ζωῆς without the article: "a spirit of life" or "a breath of life" (the word is one; compare Ezek 37:5, 10); εἰσῆλθεν ἐν αὐτοῖς, literally "entered in them" (RP εἰς αὐτούς). The verbs of v11–13 are past tenses.
- **"them which saw them" → "those beholding them"** (v11, 12) — Greek θεωροῦντας, ἐθεώρησαν (θεωρέω "behold"); "see" in v9 is βλέπω.
- **"they ascended up to heaven in a cloud" → "they went up into heaven in the cloud"** (v12) — Greek ἐν τῇ νεφέλῃ, with the article. "They heard" is ἤκουσαν (WH, Treg, NA28, SBLGNT); RP reads ἤκουσα "I heard".
- **"the same hour was there a great earthquake, and the tenth part of the city fell, and in the earthquake were slain of men seven thousand" → "in that hour there came a great earthquake, and the tenth of the city fell, and there were killed in the earthquake seven thousand names of men"** (v13) — Greek ὀνόματα ἀνθρώπων χιλιάδες ἑπτά, literally "names of men, seven thousands": "names" for persons counted one by one (as 3:4; Acts 1:15). ἀπεκτάνθησαν is "were killed" (ἀποκτείνω; "slain" is kept for σφάζω). RP reads "in that day".
- **"were affrighted" → "became afraid"** (v13) — Greek ἔμφοβοι ἐγένοντο.
- **"The second woe is past; and, behold, the third woe cometh quickly" → "The second woe has gone away; behold, the third woe comes quickly"** (v14) — Greek ἀπῆλθεν, as 9:12; ταχύ "quickly" (also "soon").
- **"The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ" → "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his *Mashiach*"** (v15) — Greek Ἐγένετο ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ κόσμου τοῦ κυρίου ἡμῶν καὶ τοῦ χριστοῦ αὐτοῦ: "kingdom" is singular and stands once; literally "The kingdom of the world has become our Lord's and his Anointed's" (the second "the kingdom" is supplied for English). "Saying" is a masculine participle (λέγοντες: WH, NA28, SBLGNT) after the feminine "voices"; Treg and RP have the feminine.
- **"for ever and ever" → "unto the ages of the ages"** (v15) — Greek εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων.
- **"four and twenty elders, which sat before God on their seats" → "the twenty-four elders, who sit before *Elohim* on their thrones"** (v16) — Greek εἴκοσι τέσσαρες; καθήμενοι, present participle; θρόνους "thrones".
- **"O Lord God Almighty, which art, and wast, and art to come" → "Lord *Elohim*, the Almighty, the one who is and the one who was"** (v17) — Greek κύριε, ὁ θεός, ὁ παντοκράτωρ, ὁ ὢν καὶ ὁ ἦν: each title has its own article; "and art to come" is not in the Greek here (it stands in 1:4, 8; 4:8), and none of the editions consulted has it.
- **"hast reigned" → "reigned"** (v17) — Greek εἴληφας, perfect, "you have taken"; ἐβασίλευσας, aorist, "you reigned" (often understood as "you began to reign"; "begun" is not a separate word).
- **"the nations were angry" → "the nations were wrathful"** (v18) — Greek ὠργίσθησαν, the verb of ὀργή "wrath" in the next line: "the nations were wrathful, and your wrath came" (the wordplay is lost with "raged"). The lines are joined by καί "and", not "but". Echo of Ps 2:1, 5.
- **"that thou shouldest give reward unto thy servants the prophets" → "to give the wage to your slaves the prophets"** (v18) — Greek τὸν μισθόν "the wage"; δούλοις "slaves".
- **"small and great" → "the small and the great"** (v18) — Greek τοὺς μικροὺς καὶ τοὺς μεγάλους, in the accusative after a row of datives (the grammar is broken in the Greek; RP has datives).
- **"shouldest destroy them which destroy the earth" → "to ruin those who ruin the earth"** (v18) — Greek διαφθεῖραι τοὺς διαφθείροντας, one verb twice (as 8:9); it means both "destroy" and "corrupt".
- **"the temple of God was opened in heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament" → "the sanctuary of *Elohim* which is in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant appeared in his sanctuary"** (v19) — Greek ὁ ναὸς τοῦ θεοῦ ὁ ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ; ὤφθη "was seen, appeared" (as 12:1); διαθήκη "covenant" (RP: "the covenant of the Lord").
- **"lightnings, and voices, and thunderings, and an earthquake, and great hail"** (v19) — Greek ἀστραπαὶ καὶ φωναὶ καὶ βρονταὶ καὶ σεισμὸς καὶ χάλαζα μεγάλη; φωναί "voices" (not "rumblings"); RP lacks "and an earthquake".

### Notes
- **v4** — the two olive trees and the lampstand echo Zech 4:2–3, 11–14.
- **v2–3, 9, 11** — the numbers (forty and two months, a thousand two hundred and sixty days, three days and a half) are left as written; the text does not explain them.
