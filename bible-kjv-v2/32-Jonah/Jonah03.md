# Jonah 3

[1] Then the word of Yahweh came to Jonah the second time, saying,

[2] "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you."

[3] So Jonah arose and went to Nineveh, according to the word of Yahweh. Now Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, three days' journey in breadth.

[4] Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's journey. And he called out, "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!"

[5] And the people of Nineveh believed *Elohim*. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them.

[6] The word reached the king of Nineveh, and he arose from his throne, removed his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in ashes.

[7] And he issued a proclamation and published through Nineveh, "By the decree of the king and his nobles: Let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste anything. Let them not feed or drink water,

[8] but let man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and let them call out mightily to *Elohim*. Let everyone turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands.

[9] Who knows? *Elohim* may turn and relent and turn from his fierce anger, so that we may not perish."

[10] When *Elohim* saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, *Elohim* relented of the disaster that he had said he would do to them, and he did not do it.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv5, 8, 9, 10) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים; the Ninevites and narrator use *Elohim* (the general divine name) rather than Yahweh (Israel's covenant name); distinction preserved.
- **"preach unto it the preaching that I bid thee" → "call out against it the message that I tell you"** (v2) — Hebrew קְרָא אֵלֶיהָ אֶת הַקְּרִיאָה אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי דֹּבֵר אֵלֶיךָ; "the message I tell you" more precise than "the preaching"; Jonah's role is to deliver a message.
- **"Nineveh was an exceeding great city of three days' journey" → "Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, three days' journey in breadth"** (v3) — Hebrew עִיר גְּדוֹלָה לֵאלֹהִים מַהֲלַךְ שְׁלֹשֶׁת יָמִים; "in breadth" clarifies the measurement; "exceedingly great" for *gadol le'Elohim* (great to God = extremely great).
- **"Jonah began to enter into the city a day's journey" → "Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's journey"** (v4) — modernized.
- **"the people of Nineveh believed God" → "the people of Nineveh believed *Elohim*"** (v5) — *Elohim* retained; the pagan city responds to the general divine name.
- **"proclaimed a fast" → "called for a fast"** (v5) — Hebrew וַיִּקְרְאוּ צוֹם *wayyiqre'u tzom*; "called for" more natural.
- **"he arose from his throne, and he laid his robe from him" → "he arose from his throne, removed his robe"** (v6) — modernized.
- **"And he caused it to be proclaimed and published through Nineveh" → "And he issued a proclamation and published through Nineveh"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיַּזְעֵק וַיֹּאמֶר; "issued a proclamation" more natural.
- **"Let them not feed, nor drink water" → "Let them not feed or drink water"** (v7) — modernized.
- **"cry mightily unto God" → "call out mightily to *Elohim*"** (v8) — *Elohim* consistent.
- **"Who can tell if God will turn and repent" → "Who knows? *Elohim* may turn and relent"** (v9) — Hebrew מִי יוֹדֵעַ יָשׁוּב וְנִחַם הָאֱלֹהִים *mi yodea' yashuv venicham ha'Elohim*; "Who knows?" more vivid; *nicham* = relented (not "repented"); *Elohim* retained.
- **"God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way" → "When *Elohim* saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way"** (v10) — modernized.
- **"God repented of the evil" → "*Elohim* relented of the disaster"** (v10) — Hebrew וַיִּנָּחֶם הָאֱלֹהִים עַל הָרָעָה *wayyinnachem ha'*Elohim* al hara'ah*; *nicham* = relented; *ra'ah* = evil/disaster; "disaster" more precise in context of divine judgment.
