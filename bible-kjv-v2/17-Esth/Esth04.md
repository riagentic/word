# Esther 4

[1] When Mordecai learned all that had been done, he tore his clothes, put on sackcloth and ashes, and went out into the midst of the city, crying with a loud and bitter cry;

[2] he went as far as the front of the king's gate, for no one was allowed to enter the king's gate clothed with sackcloth.

[3] In every province, wherever the king's command and his decree came, there was great mourning among the Jews, with fasting, weeping, and wailing; and many lay in sackcloth and ashes.

[4] Esther's maidservants and her eunuchs came and told her. The queen was deeply distressed; she sent clothes to dress Mordecai, that he might take off his sackcloth, but he would not accept them.

[5] Then Esther summoned Hathach, one of the king's eunuchs whom he had appointed to attend her; she commanded him to go to Mordecai to learn what this was and why it was.

[6] Hathach went out to Mordecai, to the open square of the city in front of the king's gate.

[7] Mordecai told him all that had happened to him, and the exact amount of silver that Haman had promised to pay into the king's treasuries for the destruction of the Jews.

[8] He also gave him a copy of the written decree that had been issued in Susa for their destruction, to show it to Esther, to explain it to her, and to charge her to go in to the king to make supplication and plead with him for her people.

[9] Hathach came and told Esther the words of Mordecai.

[10] Then Esther spoke to Hathach and commanded him to go back to Mordecai:

[11] "All the king's servants and the people of the king's provinces know that any man or woman who enters the king's inner court without being summoned, there is one law: they are to be put to death — except the one to whom the king extends the golden scepter, that he may live. As for me, I have not been summoned to come in to the king these thirty days."

[12] When Mordecai was told Esther's words,

[13] Mordecai told them to reply to Esther, "Do not think that you alone of all the Jews in the king's house will escape.

[14] For if you remain silent at this time, relief and deliverance will arise for the Jews from another place, but you and your father's house shall perish. And who knows — perhaps you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

[15] Then Esther told them to reply to Mordecai:

[16] "Go, gather all the Jews present in Susa, and fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day. I and my maidens will also fast in the same way. Then I will go in to the king, which is not according to the law; and if I perish, I perish."

[17] So Mordecai went his way and did everything as Esther had commanded him.

## Changes

- **"chamberlains" → "eunuchs"** (v4, 5).
- **"money" → "silver"** (v7).

### Notes
- **Sackcloth and ashes** — public mourning; Persian court protocol excludes such near the king.
- **Approaching the king unsummoned** — capital offense; historical Persian practice confirms (Herodotus III.118, Xenophon Cyropaedia).
- **"Thirty days"** — Esther not recently called; her position uncertain.
- **"Another place"** (v14) — oblique reference to divine providence? Mordecai avoids naming *Elohim*; the theological reality is present sub-textually throughout Esther.
- **"For such a time as this"** — signature verse; providence-and-agency tension. The narrator offers the suggestion without theological certainty.
- **Three-day fast** — solidarity, resolve. Communal pre-action preparation.
- **"If I perish, I perish"** — Esther's heroic pivot. Role-reversal from submissive cousin and ward (2:7) to decisive queen.
