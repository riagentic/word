# Esther 2

[1] After these things, when the wrath of King Ahasuerus had subsided, he remembered Vashti, what she had done, and what had been decreed against her.

[2] Then the king's young men who attended him said, "Let beautiful young virgins be sought for the king.

[3] Let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom to gather every beautiful young virgin to Susa the citadel, to the house of the women, under the custody of Hegai the king's eunuch, keeper of the women; let their cosmetic treatments be given them.

[4] Let the young woman who pleases the king be queen in place of Vashti." The thing pleased the king, and he did so.

[5] There was in Susa the citadel a certain Jew, whose name was Mordecai son of Jair son of Shimei son of Kish, a Benjaminite,

[6] who had been carried away from Jerusalem with the captivity that was carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried away.

[7] He was raising Hadassah — that is, Esther — his uncle's daughter, for she had neither father nor mother. The young woman was shapely and beautiful; when her father and mother died, Mordecai took her as his own daughter.

[8] When the king's command and decree were heard, and many young women were gathered to Susa the citadel, into the custody of Hegai, Esther also was taken to the king's palace, into the custody of Hegai, keeper of the women.

[9] The young woman pleased him and won his favor; he speedily provided her cosmetic treatments and her portion of food, with seven chosen maidservants from the king's palace; he moved her and her maidservants to the best place in the house of the women.

[10] Esther had not disclosed her people or her kindred, for Mordecai had charged her not to disclose it.

[11] Every day Mordecai walked in front of the court of the women's house to learn how Esther was and what would happen to her.

[12] When the turn came for each young woman to go in to King Ahasuerus — after she had completed twelve months of treatment prescribed for the women (for so were the days of their beautification fulfilled: six months with oil of myrrh, six months with spices and cosmetics for women) —

[13] the young woman went in to the king in this way: whatever she asked was given her to take with her from the house of the women to the king's palace.

[14] In the evening she went in, and in the morning she returned to the second house of the women under the custody of Shaashgaz the king's eunuch, keeper of the concubines; she did not go in to the king again unless the king delighted in her and she was summoned by name.

[15] When the turn came for Esther daughter of Abihail, the uncle of Mordecai (who had taken her as his daughter), to go in to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai the king's eunuch, keeper of the women, advised. Esther was winning favor in the eyes of all who saw her.

[16] Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus in his royal palace in the tenth month, which is the month Tebet, in the seventh year of his reign.

[17] The king loved Esther more than all the women; she won grace and favor in his sight more than all the virgins. So he set the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti.

[18] The king held a great feast for all his officials and his servants — "Esther's Feast" — and granted a release to the provinces and gave gifts with royal liberality.

[19] When virgins were gathered together a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate.

[20] Esther had not yet made known her kindred or her people, as Mordecai had charged her; Esther continued to obey Mordecai's command, just as when she was raised by him.

[21] In those days, while Mordecai sat at the king's gate, two of the king's eunuchs, Bigthan and Teresh, of those who guarded the threshold, were angry and sought to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.

[22] The thing became known to Mordecai, who told it to Queen Esther; Esther reported it to the king in Mordecai's name.

[23] When the matter was investigated and found to be true, the two were hanged on the gallows; it was recorded in the Book of the Chronicles in the king's presence.

## Changes

- **"things for purification" → "cosmetic treatments"** (v3, 9) — *tamruqim*.
- **"fair and beautiful" → "shapely and beautiful"** (v7) — *yefat-to'ar we-tovat mar'eh*.
- **"princes" → "officials"** (v18).
- **"release to the provinces" — retained** (v18) — *hanachah*; probably tax remission or amnesty.

### Notes
- **Mordecai** — possibly namesake Marduk-inflected Persian-court name. Kish-Shimei-Saul Benjaminite line, setting up Haman/Agag confrontation.
- **"Carried away with Jeconiah"** — 597 BC; Mordecai would be elderly by Xerxes's time (ca. 115+ years); "carried away" may refer to his family, not himself personally.
- **Esther** = *hadassah* (myrtle) in Hebrew; *Esther* possibly from Persian *stāra* "star" or Babylonian Ishtar. Dual name suggests Persian-court assimilation.
- **Year 7** = 479 BC, after Xerxes's failed Greek campaign (480-479). Historical synchronism: king returns humiliated, seeks distraction.
- **"Esther's Feast"** — civic holiday named after the new queen.
- **Bigthan and Teresh plot** — assassination attempt. Mordecai's discovery sets up later reward.
- **Persian impalement** — attested execution method; not modern "hanging."
