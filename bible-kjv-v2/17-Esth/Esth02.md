# Esther 2

[1] After these things, when the wrath of King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧ had subsided, he remembered Vashti, what she had done, and what had been decreed against her.

[2] Then the king's young men who attended him said, "Let beautiful young virgins be sought for the king.

[3] Let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom to gather every beautiful young virgin to ⟦susa|Shushan|Susa⟧ the citadel, to the house of the women, under the custody of Hegai the king's eunuch, keeper of the women; let their cosmetic treatments be given them.

[4] Let the young woman who pleases the king be queen in place of Vashti." The thing pleased the king, and he did so.

[5] There was in ⟦susa|Shushan|Susa⟧ the citadel a certain ⟦jew|Yehudi|Jew⟧, whose name was ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ son of ⟦jair|Yair|Jair⟧ son of ⟦shimei|Shimi|Shimei⟧ son of ⟦kish|Qish|Kish⟧, a ⟦benjaminite|Yemini|Benjaminite⟧,

[6] who had been carried away from ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ with the captivity that was carried away with ⟦jeconiah|Yechonyah|Jeconiah⟧ king of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, whom ⟦nebuchadnezzar|Nevuchadnetzar|Nebuchadnezzar⟧ king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ had carried away.

[7] He was raising Hadassah — that is, ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ — his uncle's daughter, for she had neither father nor mother. The young woman was shapely and beautiful; when her father and mother died, ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ took her as his own daughter.

[8] When the king's command and decree were heard, and many young women were gathered to ⟦susa|Shushan|Susa⟧ the citadel, into the custody of Hegai, ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ also was taken to the king's palace, into the custody of Hegai, keeper of the women.

[9] The young woman pleased him and won his favor; he speedily provided her cosmetic treatments and her portion of food, with seven chosen maidservants from the king's palace; he moved her and her maidservants to the best place in the house of the women.

[10] ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ had not disclosed her people or her kindred, for ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ had charged her not to disclose it.

[11] Every day ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ walked in front of the court of the women's house to learn how ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ was and what would happen to her.

[12] When the turn came for each young woman to go in to King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧ — after she had completed twelve months of treatment prescribed for the women (for so were the days of their beautification fulfilled: six months with oil of myrrh, six months with spices and cosmetics for women) —

[13] the young woman went in to the king in this way: whatever she asked was given her to take with her from the house of the women to the king's palace.

[14] In the evening she went in, and in the morning she returned to the second house of the women under the custody of Shaashgaz the king's eunuch, keeper of the concubines; she did not go in to the king again unless the king delighted in her and she was summoned by name.

[15] When the turn came for ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ daughter of ⟦abihail|Avichayil|Abihail⟧, the uncle of ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ (who had taken her as his daughter), to go in to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai the king's eunuch, keeper of the women, advised. ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ was winning favor in the eyes of all who saw her.

[16] ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ was taken to King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧ in his royal palace in the tenth month, which is the month ⟦tebet|Tevet|Tebeth⟧, in the seventh year of his reign.

[17] The king loved ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ more than all the women; she won grace and favor in his sight more than all the virgins. So he set the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti.

[18] The king held a great feast for all his officials and his servants — "⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧'s Feast" — and granted a release to the provinces and gave gifts with royal liberality.

[19] When virgins were gathered together a second time, ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ was sitting at the king's gate.

[20] ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ had not yet made known her kindred or her people, as ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ had charged her; ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ continued to obey ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧'s command, just as when she was raised by him.

[21] In those days, while ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ sat at the king's gate, two of the king's eunuchs, ⟦bigthan|Bigtan|Bigthan⟧ and Teresh, of those who guarded the threshold, were angry and sought to lay hands on King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧.

[22] The thing became known to ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧, who told it to Queen ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧; ⟦esther|Ester|Esther⟧ reported it to the king in ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧'s name.

[23] When the matter was investigated and found to be true, the two were hanged on a pole; it was recorded in the Book of the Chronicles in the king's presence.

## Changes

- **"things for purification" → "cosmetic treatments"** (v3, 9) — *tamruqim*.
- **"fair and beautiful" → "shapely and beautiful"** (v7) — *yefat-to'ar we-tovat mar'eh*.
- **"princes" → "officials"** (v18).
- **"release to the provinces" — retained** (v18) — *hanachah*; probably tax remission or amnesty.
- **"hanged on a tree" → "hanged on a pole"** (v23) — *talah al-etz*; *etz* = wood, tree, pole. Not "gallows": the text names suspension on a pole, not a noose.

### Notes
- **Mordecai** — possibly namesake Marduk-inflected Persian-court name. Kish-Shimei-Saul Benjaminite line, setting up Haman/Agag confrontation.
- **"Carried away with Jeconiah"** — 597 BC; Mordecai would be elderly by Xerxes's time (ca. 115+ years); "carried away" may refer to his family, not himself personally.
- **Esther** = *hadassah* (myrtle) in Hebrew; *Esther* possibly from Persian *stāra* "star" or Babylonian Ishtar. Dual name suggests Persian-court assimilation.
- **Year 7** = 479 BC, after Xerxes's failed Greek campaign (480-479). Historical synchronism: king returns humiliated, seeks distraction.
- **"Esther's Feast"** — civic holiday named after the new queen.
- **Bigthan and Teresh plot** — assassination attempt. Mordecai's discovery sets up later reward.
- **Hanged on a pole** — Persians impaled or displayed the condemned on stakes (Herodotus 3.159); *talah* names only the suspension, not modern "hanging" by a noose.
