# Esther 10

[1] King Ahasuerus imposed tribute on the land and on the coasts of the sea.

[2] All the acts of his power and might, and the full account of the greatness of Mordecai to which the king advanced him — are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Media and Persia?

[3] For Mordecai the Jew was second to King Ahasuerus, and was great among the Jews and popular with the multitude of his brothers; he sought the welfare of his people and spoke peace for all his seed.

## Changes

- **"tribute upon the land, and upon the isles of the sea" → "tribute on the land and on the coasts of the sea"** (v1) — *ha-aretz we-iyyei ha-yam*; coastal regions / maritime provinces.

### Notes
- **Brief closing** — three-verse epilogue formalizes Mordecai's status and legacy.
- **Tribute restored** — Xerxes imposes taxes after his Greek campaign losses. Historical: heavy Persian taxation documented.
- **Mordecai second to king** — Joseph-parallel for Jewish diaspora leadership. Both rise from exile to become key advisor to pagan monarch.
- **"Sought the welfare of his people, spoke *shalom* for his seed"** — ideal diaspora-leadership formula. Sets template for later Jewish court-advocacy (Daniel, later rabbis in Persian and Roman courts).
- **"Book of Chronicles of Media and Persia"** — source citation; historical framing.
- **Book ends without explicit reference to Yahweh** — throughout. Providence in hiddenness; Jewish survival is the theological point.
