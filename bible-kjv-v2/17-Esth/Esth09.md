# Esther 9

[1] In the twelfth month, the month of Adar, on its thirteenth day, when the king's command and his decree were about to be executed — on the day the enemies of the Jews had hoped to overpower them, though it was reversed so that the Jews themselves overpowered those who hated them —

[2] the Jews gathered in their cities throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay hands on those who sought their harm; no one could stand against them, for the fear of them had fallen on all the peoples.

[3] All the officials of the provinces, the satraps, the governors, and those who did the king's work were supporting the Jews, because the fear of Mordecai had fallen on them.

[4] For Mordecai was great in the king's house; his fame went out through all the provinces; the man Mordecai grew greater and greater.

[5] The Jews struck all their enemies with the sword, with slaughter and destruction, and did as they pleased with those who hated them.

[6] In Susa the citadel the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men.

[7] They killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,

[8] Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,

[9] Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha —

[10] the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews; but on the plunder they did not lay their hand.

[11] On that day the number of those slain in Susa the citadel was reported to the king.

[12] The king said to Queen Esther, "The Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men in Susa the citadel, and the ten sons of Haman. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces! Now what is your petition? It shall be granted to you. What is your further request? It shall be done."

[13] Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let tomorrow also be granted to the Jews who are in Susa to act according to this day's decree; and let Haman's ten sons be hanged on the gallows."

[14] The king commanded it to be done; a decree was issued in Susa, and they hanged Haman's ten sons.

[15] The Jews who were in Susa gathered together also on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar, and killed three hundred men in Susa; but on the plunder they did not lay their hand.

[16] The rest of the Jews who were in the king's provinces gathered to defend themselves and gained relief from their enemies, killing seventy-five thousand of those who hated them; but on the plunder they did not lay their hand.

[17] This was on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar; on the fourteenth day they rested, and made it a day of feasting and gladness.

[18] But the Jews who were in Susa gathered on the thirteenth and the fourteenth; on the fifteenth they rested and made it a day of feasting and gladness.

[19] Therefore the Jews of the villages who live in the unwalled towns make the fourteenth day of the month of Adar a day of gladness and feasting, a holiday, and an occasion for sending portions to one another.

[20] Mordecai wrote these things and sent letters to all the Jews in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, both near and far,

[21] to establish among them the observance of the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and the fifteenth day of the same, year by year,

[22] as the days on which the Jews gained relief from their enemies, the month that was turned for them from sorrow to joy and from mourning to a holiday — that they should make them days of feasting and joy, and of sending portions to one another and gifts to the poor.

[23] The Jews took it upon themselves to continue what they had begun, and what Mordecai had written to them.

[24] For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast lot (that is, the lot) to crush and to destroy them.

[25] But when it came before the king, he commanded in writing that the wicked plan which Haman had devised against the Jews should return on his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the gallows.

[26] Therefore they called these days Purim, from the word lot. Therefore, because of all the words of this letter, and of what they had seen concerning this matter, and what had come upon them,

[27] the Jews ordained and took upon themselves and upon their seed and upon all who joined them — without fail — to keep these two days according to their writing and according to their appointed time every year.

[28] These days were to be remembered and kept throughout every generation, every family, every province, and every city; these days of Purim were not to fall into neglect among the Jews, nor their memorial perish among their seed.

[29] Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, along with Mordecai the Jew, wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter of Purim.

[30] Letters were sent to all the Jews in the 127 provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus — words of peace and truth —

[31] to confirm these days of Purim in their appointed times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had enjoined them, and as they had established upon themselves and their seed, with matters of the fastings and their lamentations.

[32] The decree of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim; and it was recorded in a book.

## Changes

- **"lieutenants" → "satraps"** (v3).
- **"mischief" → "harm"** (v2).
- **"prey / spoil" → "plunder"** (v10, 15, 16) — *bizah*.
- **"Purim" — retained** (v26, 28, 29, 31, 32).
- **"sending portions... gifts to the poor" — retained** (v22) — foundational Purim customs.

### Notes
- **Two days of combat**: provinces on 13 Adar, Susa adding 14 Adar. This becomes the basis for Purim's two-day observance split between walled and unwalled cities.
- **Ten sons of Haman** — Amalekite extermination echo. Notably, when chanted in Hebrew synagogues, these names are read in one breath (tradition from Megillah).
- **"Did not lay hand on the plunder"** (three times repeated) — moral restraint. Unlike Saul with Amalek (1 Sam 15:9), the Jews obey *cherem*-type purity, not taking spoil. Theological rehabilitation of tribal heritage.
- **75,000 killed** — large, but defensive: "stood for their lives."
- **Hanging ten sons posthumously** — public exposure; political symbolism of complete Amalekite extinction.
- **"Purim"** — etymological note from *pur* (lot, v24). Festival institutionalized.
- **Four customs of Purim**: hearing megillah-reading, feast, gifts to friends (*mishloach manot*), gifts to poor (*matanot la-evyonim*) — all from v22.
- **"Second letter"** (v29) — supplementary decree formalizing the festival.
