# Esther 7

[1] So the king and Haman came to banquet with Queen Esther.

[2] On the second day, as they were drinking wine, the king again said to Esther, "What is your petition, Queen Esther? It shall be granted you. And what is your request? Even to half of the kingdom, it shall be done."

[3] Queen Esther answered, "If I have found favor in your sight, O king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request.

[4] For we are sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish. If we had been merely sold as male and female slaves, I would have kept silent; but our distress would not be worth the king's loss."

[5] Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he, who has dared to presume in his heart to do this?"

[6] Esther said, "The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman!" Then Haman was terrified before the king and queen.

[7] The king rose in his wrath from the banquet of wine and went out into the palace garden; Haman remained to plead for his life with Queen Esther, for he saw that evil had been determined against him by the king.

[8] When the king returned from the palace garden to the house of the banquet of wine, Haman had fallen on the couch on which Esther was reclining. The king said, "Will he even violate the queen in my presence in my own house?" As the word went out of the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

[9] Then Harbonah, one of the eunuchs in attendance on the king, said, "Behold also, the pole fifty cubits high, which Haman had prepared for Mordecai — who had spoken good concerning the king — stands in Haman's house." The king said, "Hang him on it."

[10] So they hanged Haman on the pole that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king's wrath subsided.

## Changes

- **"although the enemy could not countervail the king's damage" → "but our distress would not be worth the king's loss"** (v4) — Hebrew obscure: *ki eyn ha-tzar shoveh be-nezeq ha-melek*; possibly "for the distress does not equal the damage to the king" — Esther argues that even slavery would not be worth bringing this loss upon the king.
- **"chamberlains" → "eunuchs"** (v9).
- **"bondmen and bondwomen" → "male and female slaves"** (v4).
- **"gallows" → "pole"** (v9, 10) — *etz*, wood/pole; see 2:23.

### Notes
- **"We are sold"** (v4) — echoes Haman's 10,000 talents offered (3:9); Esther picks up the term.
- **Esther names Haman** — moment of revelation. Haman realizes his peril.
- **King's strategic exit** — steps into garden to control temper and assess situation.
- **Haman on couch** — probably clinging to Esther's feet for mercy (ANE posture); king mistakes for assault.
- **Covered face** — condemnation sign; death-sentence signaled.
- **Harbonah** — court eunuch delivers decisive information. Providential small character.
- **Hanged on his own pole** — measure-for-measure justice.
