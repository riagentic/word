# Psalm 56

To the overseer, upon ⟦jonath-elem-rehokim|Yonat elem rechoqim|The Dove on Far-off Terebinths⟧. Of David. A ⟦miktam|Michtam|Miktam⟧, when the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ seized him in ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧.

[1] Show me favor, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, for mortal man has panted after me;
all the day, fighting, he oppresses me.

[2] Those who watch for me have panted all the day,
for many are fighting against me, loftily.

[3] The day I fear,
I — in you I will trust.

[4] In ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ I will praise his word;
in ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ I have trusted; I will not fear.
What can flesh do to me?

[5] All the day they hurt my words;
all their thoughts are against me for evil.

[6] They stir up strife, they lie hidden;
they watch my heels,
as they have waited for my soul.

[7] Because of wickedness, rescue them?
In anger bring down peoples, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧!

[8] You have numbered my wandering, you yourself;
put my tears in your skin;
are they not in your account?

[9] Then my enemies will turn back
in the day I call;
this I know, that ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is for me.

[10] In ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ I will praise the word;
in ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ I will praise the word.

[11] In ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ I have trusted; I will not fear.
What can man do to me?

[12] Upon me, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, are your vows;
I will repay thanksgivings to you.

[13] For you have delivered my soul from death —
have you not my feet from stumbling? —
to walk before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in the light of life.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 4, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v10) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"To the chief Musician upon Jonathelemrechokim, Michtam of David, when the Philistines took him in Gath" → "To the overseer, upon Yonat elem rechoqim. Of David. A Miktam, when the Philistines seized him in Gath."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ עַל־יוֹנַת אֵלֶם רְחֹקִים לְדָוִד מִכְתָּם בֶּאֱחֹז אֹתוֹ פְלִשְׁתִּים בְּגַת, the first verse of the Hebrew. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer" (the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21; traditional "to the chief musician"). עַל + the name of a tune, kept as a name: word for word "the dove of silence of the far ones" (אֵלֶם as printed is "silence", the word of Ps 58:1; read as אֵלִים it is "terebinths", the traditional form); the meaning is unknown. לְדָוִד "of David": the לְ may be "by", "for" or "belonging to" David. *Miktam* is retained: its meaning is not known. אָחַז "seize, take hold of". Hebrew order: "Of David" before "A Miktam".
- **"Be merciful unto me … for man would swallow me up; he fighting daily oppresseth me" → "Show me favor … for mortal man has panted after me; all the day, fighting, he oppresses me"** (v1) — Hebrew חָנֵּנִי, from חָנַן "show favor"; אֱנוֹשׁ "mortal man"; שְׁאָפַנִי, perfect of שָׁאַף "pant, pant after" (also v2; 57:3). Some take a second root "trample, crush" for these verses; the meaning is uncertain. לֹחֵם "fighting" (participle); לָחַץ "oppress"; כָּל־הַיּוֹם "all the day".
- **"Mine enemies would daily swallow me up: for they be many that fight against me, O thou most High" → "Those who watch for me have panted all the day, for many are fighting against me, loftily"** (v2) — Hebrew שׁוֹרְרַי, from שׁוּר "watch, lie in wait" (as Ps 5:8), not אֹיֵב "enemy"; the verb has no object ("after me" is not written). מָרוֹם "height" stands alone at the end: an adverb "loftily, from on high" (= proudly), or a name of God addressed, "O Height" (KJV "O thou most High"); not decided.
- **"What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee" → "The day I fear, I — in you I will trust"** (v3) — Hebrew יוֹם אִירָא אֲנִי אֵלֶיךָ אֶבְטָח, "a day I fear — I, toward you I will trust"; the pronoun אֲנִי stands out.
- **"In God I will praise his word, in God I have put my trust; I will not fear what flesh can do unto me" → "In Elohim I will praise his word; in Elohim I have trusted; I will not fear. What can flesh do to me?"** (v4) — Hebrew בֵּאלֹהִים אֲהַלֵּל דְּבָרוֹ, "in Elohim I will praise his word" (not "whose word"); בָּטַחְתִּי perfect "I have trusted"; מַה־יַּעֲשֶׂה בָשָׂר לִי is a question of its own. The refrain returns in v10–11 with differences (see there).
- **"Every day they wrest my words" → "All the day they hurt my words"** (v5) — Hebrew דְּבָרַי יְעַצֵּבוּ, piel of עָצַב "hurt, grieve": what is meant is uncertain — "twist my words" (KJV "wrest"; a second root עָצַב "shape" is also proposed), or, with דָּבָר "matter", "they trouble my affairs".
- **"They gather themselves together, they hide themselves, they mark my steps, when they wait for my soul" → "They stir up strife, they lie hidden; they watch my heels, as they have waited for my soul"** (v6) — Hebrew יָגוּרוּ, another root than גּוּר "sojourn": "stir up strife, attack" (as 59:3; Isa 54:15), or "gather together" (KJV); uncertain. יִצְפּוֹנוּ, the qere, qal "they lie hidden"; the written form יצפינו (hifil) is "they hide (it)": the sense is close. הֵמָּה עֲקֵבַי יִשְׁמֹרוּ, "they — my heels they watch" (עָקֵב "heel"); כַּאֲשֶׁר קִוּוּ נַפְשִׁי, קִוָּה "wait for", perfect.
- **"Shall they escape by iniquity? in thine anger cast down the people" → "Because of wickedness, rescue them? In anger bring down peoples"** (v7) — Hebrew עַל־אָוֶן פַּלֶּט־לָמוֹ, "upon wickedness — rescue for them!": פַּלֶּט is the imperative of פִּלֵּט "rescue"; the line has no question word and reads as an indignant question (KJV "shall they escape"); a change to פַּלֵּס "weigh out" is often proposed (from memory) and is not followed. בְּאַף "in anger" (אַף, the word for "nose"); there is no "your". עַמִּים "peoples", without the article; הוֹרֵד "bring down".
- **"Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?" → "You have numbered my wandering, you yourself; put my tears in your skin; are they not in your account?"** (v8) — Hebrew נֹדִי סָפַרְתָּה אָתָּה, "my wandering you have numbered, you" (נֹד, root of נוּד "wander"; perfect of סָפַר "number"; אַתָּה stands out); דִמְעָתִי singular, collective; נֹאד "skin" (a skin bottle, Josh 9:4); סִפְרָה "account" (only here), from סָפַר: it answers "numbered" (not סֵפֶר "book").
- **"When I cry unto thee, then shall mine enemies turn back: this I know; for God is for me" → "Then my enemies will turn back in the day I call; this I know, that Elohim is for me"** (v9) — Hebrew אָז "then" first; בְּיוֹם אֶקְרָא "in the day I call"; זֶה־יָדַעְתִּי כִּי, "this I know, that".
- **"In God will I praise his word: in the LORD will I praise his word" → "In Elohim I will praise the word; in Yahweh I will praise the word"** (v10) — Hebrew דָּבָר, without "his" (v4 has דְּבָרוֹ "his word") and without the article: "the" is supplied for English. Elohistic psalm: the second line has יהוה.
- **"In God have I put my trust: I will not be afraid what man can do unto me" → "In Elohim I have trusted; I will not fear. What can man do to me?"** (v11) — as v4, with אָדָם "man" where v4 has בָּשָׂר "flesh".
- **"Thy vows are upon me, O God: I will render praises unto thee" → "Upon me, Elohim, are your vows; I will repay thanksgivings to you"** (v12) — Hebrew עָלַי … נְדָרֶיךָ, "upon me … your vows" (vows made to you); אֲשַׁלֵּם, piel of שָׁלֵם "repay" (as "repay a vow"); תּוֹדֹת "thanksgivings" (also of thank offerings).
- **"wilt not thou deliver my feet from falling, that I may walk before God in the light of the living?" → "have you not my feet from stumbling? — to walk before Elohim in the light of life"** (v13) — Hebrew הֲלֹא רַגְלַי מִדֶּחִי, "is it not my feet from stumbling?": the verb "delivered" carries over from the first line and is not repeated; the line is a question (הֲ). דֶּחִי "stumbling" (from דָּחָה "push down"). לְהִתְהַלֵּךְ לִפְנֵי, "to walk before" (hitpael: walk about); בְּאוֹר הַחַיִּים "in the light of the life" or "of the living" (חַיִּים, plural, both senses).

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1–13 = Heb v2–14.
- **Gath capture** — 1 Sam 21.
- **"Tears in your skin"** — *nod* "wandering" and *nod* "skin" sound alike; the tears are kept and numbered.
- **Refrain** — v4 and v10–11: trust in *Elohim*, "I will not fear".
