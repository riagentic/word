# Psalm 45

To the overseer. Upon ⟦shoshannim|Shoshannim|Lilies⟧. Of the sons of ⟦korah|Qorach|Korah⟧. A Maskil. A song of loves.

[1] My heart stirs with a good word;
I am speaking my works to the king;
my tongue is the pen of a skilled scribe.

[2] You are more beautiful than the sons of man;
favor has been poured out on your lips;
therefore ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has blessed you forever.

[3] Gird your sword upon the thigh, ⟦gibbor|gibbor|mighty one⟧,
your majesty and your splendor.

[4] And your splendor — prosper, ride,
for the sake of truth and humility of righteousness;
and your right hand will teach you fearsome things.

[5] Your arrows are sharp —
peoples fall under you —
in the heart of the enemies of the king.

[6] Your throne, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, is forever and ever;
a scepter of uprightness is the scepter of your kingdom.

[7] You have loved righteousness and hated wickedness;
therefore ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, has anointed you
with the oil of exultation above your companions.

[8] Myrrh and aloes, cassia, are all your garments;
from palaces of ivory strings have gladdened you.

[9] Daughters of kings are among your precious ones;
the consort stands at your right hand in gold of ⟦ophir|Ofir|Ophir⟧.

[10] Hear, daughter, and see, and incline your ear;
and forget your people and the house of your father,

[11] and let the king desire your beauty;
for he is your lord, and bow down to him.

[12] And the daughter of ⟦tyre|Tzor|Tyre⟧, with an offering —
your face they will beseech, the rich of the people.

[13] All glorious is the daughter of the king within;
of settings of gold is her clothing.

[14] In embroidered work she is led to the king;
virgins after her, her companions,
are brought to you.

[15] They are led with gladness and joy;
they enter into the palace of the king.

[16] In place of your fathers shall be your sons;
you will set them as princes in all the earth.

[17] Let me bring your name to remembrance in every generation and generation;
therefore peoples will give thanks to you forever and ever.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v2, 6, 7) — Hebrew *Elohim*; an Elohistic psalm: the divine names as printed.
- **"To the chief Musician upon Shoshannim, for the sons of Korah, Maschil, A Song of loves" → "To the overseer. Upon Shoshannim. Of the sons of Korah. A Maskil. A song of loves."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ עַל־שֹׁשַׁנִּים לִבְנֵי־קֹרַח מַשְׂכִּיל שִׁיר יְדִידֹת, in this order. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer" (the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21; traditional "to the chief musician"); שֹׁשַׁנִּים "lilies", taken as the name of a tune: its meaning in a heading is uncertain; לִבְנֵי: לְ "by", "for" or "belonging to", "of" leaves it open; מַשְׂכִּיל: the kind of psalm is not known; שִׁיר יְדִידֹת "a song of loves" (יְדִידֹת plural, from the root of יָדִיד "beloved": "of beloved ones" or "a love song").
- **"My heart is inditing a good matter: I speak of the things which I have made touching the king: my tongue is the pen of a ready writer" → "My heart stirs with a good word; I am speaking my works to the king; my tongue is the pen of a skilled scribe"** (v1) — Hebrew רָחַשׁ לִבִּי דָּבָר טוֹב: רָחַשׁ only here, "stir, bubble up" (uncertain); דָּבָר "word"; אֹמֵר אָנִי מַעֲשַׂי לְמֶלֶךְ, participle with "I": "I am speaking my works" (= the poem I have made); לְמֶלֶךְ without the article (supplied); עֵט סוֹפֵר מָהִיר, no "like"; מָהִיר "quick", of a scribe "skilled" (Ezra 7:6).
- **"Thou art fairer than the children of men: grace is poured into thy lips" → "You are more beautiful than the sons of man; favor has been poured out on your lips"** (v2) — Hebrew יָפְיָפִיתָ, a doubled form of יָפָה "be beautiful" (= "you are very beautiful"); מִבְּנֵי אָדָם "than the sons of man"; חֵן "favor" (not "grace"); הוּצַק, passive of יָצַק "pour".
- **"Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O most mighty, with thy glory and thy majesty" → "Gird your sword upon the thigh, mighty one, your majesty and your splendor"** (v3) — Hebrew עַל־יָרֵךְ "upon the thigh" ("thy" is not written); גִּבּוֹר "mighty one" (the token kept; no "most"); הוֹדְךָ וַהֲדָרֶךָ "your majesty and your splendor" stand in apposition, with no "with" or "in": what they stand beside (the sword, or the one addressed) is left open.
- **"And in thy majesty ride prosperously because of truth and meekness and righteousness; and thy right hand shall teach thee terrible things" → "And your splendor — prosper, ride, for the sake of truth and humility of righteousness; and your right hand will teach you fearsome things"** (v4) — Hebrew וַהֲדָרְךָ צְלַח רְכַב: "your splendor" repeats the last word of v3 (often taken as a copying slip; the Masoretic Text is kept), with no "in"; two imperatives "prosper, ride"; עַל־דְּבַר־אֱמֶת "for the sake of truth" (lit. "upon the matter of truth"); וְעַנְוָה־צֶדֶק, joined by a maqqef with no "and": "humility of righteousness" (or "humility, righteousness"); עֲנָוָה "humility" (the עָנָו row; not "meekness"); וְתוֹרְךָ "and it will teach you" (or a wish, "let it teach you"); נוֹרָאוֹת "fearsome things".
- **"Thine arrows are sharp in the heart of the king's enemies; whereby the people fall under thee" → "Your arrows are sharp — peoples fall under you — in the heart of the enemies of the king"** (v5) — Hebrew חִצֶּיךָ שְׁנוּנִים עַמִּים תַּחְתֶּיךָ יִפְּלוּ בְּלֵב אוֹיְבֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ, in this order: the middle clause stands between "sharp" and "in the heart"; "whereby" is not written; עַמִּים "peoples".
- **"Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever: the sceptre of thy kingdom is a right sceptre" → "Your throne, Elohim, is forever and ever; a scepter of uprightness is the scepter of your kingdom"** (v6) — Hebrew כִּסְאֲךָ אֱלֹהִים עוֹלָם וָעֶד, word for word "your throne Elohim age and ever": the verse takes אֱלֹהִים as addressed to the king (as the accents allow); other readings: "your throne is Elohim forever", "your throne (is the throne) of Elohim", "your divine throne". The quotation in Heb 1:8 has "Your throne, O God, is unto the age of the age" (ὁ θεός, a nominative used for address). עוֹלָם וָעֶד without לְ; שֵׁבֶט מִישֹׁר שֵׁבֶט מַלְכוּתֶךָ, "a scepter of uprightness" first, as in the Hebrew; Heb 1:8 adds "and" and the article ("and the rod of uprightness"; Westcott-Hort "of his kingdom", the other editions "of your kingdom").
- **"Thou lovest righteousness, and hatest wickedness: therefore God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows" → "You have loved righteousness and hated wickedness; therefore Elohim, your Elohim, has anointed you with the oil of exultation above your companions"** (v7) — Hebrew אָהַבְתָּ … וַתִּשְׂנָא, perfect and narrative form ("have loved … hated"); עַל־כֵּן מְשָׁחֲךָ אֱלֹהִים אֱלֹהֶיךָ: either "Elohim, your Elohim, has anointed you" or "therefore, Elohim, your Elohim has anointed you" (the first *Elohim* addressed, as v6); in the Elohistic psalms אֱלֹהִים often stands where other psalms have יהוה (from memory), so "Elohim your Elohim" may answer to "Yahweh your Elohim" — as printed here. שֶׁמֶן שָׂשׂוֹן "oil of exultation" ("with" supplied; שָׂשׂוֹן, kept apart from שִׂמְחָה "gladness" and גִּיל "joy" of v15; Heb 1:9 ἀγαλλιάσεως "exultation"); מֵחֲבֵרֶיךָ "above (more than) your companions".
- **"All thy garments smell of myrrh, and aloes, and cassia, out of the ivory palaces, whereby they have made thee glad" → "Myrrh and aloes, cassia, are all your garments; from palaces of ivory strings have gladdened you"** (v8) — Hebrew מֹר־וַאֲהָלוֹת קְצִיעוֹת כָּל־בִּגְדֹתֶיךָ, a verbless clause ("smell of" is not written: the garments are so perfumed that they "are" myrrh); מִן־הֵיכְלֵי שֵׁן "from palaces of ivory" (שֵׁן lit. "tooth"); מִנִּי שִׂמְּחוּךָ: מִנִּי taken as "strings" (as מִנִּים Ps 150:4); it can also be the poetic form of מִן "from" ("from there they have gladdened you"): uncertain. שִׂמַּח "gladden".
- **"Kings' daughters were among thy honourable women: upon thy right hand did stand the queen in gold of Ophir" → "Daughters of kings are among your precious ones; the consort stands at your right hand in gold of Ophir"** (v9) — Hebrew בְּיִקְּרוֹתֶיךָ "among your precious ones" (יָקָר "precious"; "women" is not written, the form is feminine); נִצְּבָה, נִצָּב "stand"; שֵׁגַל "consort" (the king's wife, as Neh 2:6; not מַלְכָּה "queen"); כֶּתֶם "gold" (the gold words row: another word than זָהָב "gold" of v13).
- **"Hearken, O daughter, and consider, and incline thine ear; forget also thine own people, and thy father's house" → "Hear, daughter, and see, and incline your ear; and forget your people and the house of your father"** (v10) — Hebrew שִׁמְעִי … וּרְאִי, "hear … see"; וְשִׁכְחִי "and forget"; בֵּית אָבִיךְ "the house of your father".
- **"So shall the king greatly desire thy beauty: for he is thy Lord; and worship thou him" → "and let the king desire your beauty; for he is your lord, and bow down to him"** (v11) — Hebrew וְיִתְאָו, the short (jussive) form: "and let the king desire" (also read "so the king will desire"); "greatly" is not written; אֲדֹנַיִךְ "your lord", the plain word for a husband or master (no capital); וְהִשְׁתַּחֲוִי "and bow down".
- **"And the daughter of Tyre shall be there with a gift; even the rich among the people shall intreat thy favour" → "And the daughter of Tyre, with an offering — your face they will beseech, the rich of the people"** (v12) — Hebrew וּבַת־צֹר בְּמִנְחָה פָּנַיִךְ יְחַלּוּ עֲשִׁירֵי עָם: "shall be there" is not written; מִנְחָה "offering" (outside the cult); חִלָּה פְּנֵי "beseech the face of"; whether "the daughter of Tyre" is addressed or is one of those who come is left open.
- **"The king's daughter is all glorious within: her clothing is of wrought gold" → "All glorious is the daughter of the king within; of settings of gold is her clothing"** (v13) — Hebrew כָּל־כְּבוּדָּה בַת־מֶלֶךְ פְּנִימָה, "all glory" first; פְּנִימָה "within" (inside the house; "chamber" is not written); מִמִּשְׁבְּצוֹת זָהָב, מִשְׁבְּצוֹת "settings" (of gold thread or filigree: uncertain, as Exod 28).
- **"She shall be brought unto the king in raiment of needlework: the virgins her companions that follow her shall be brought unto thee" → "In embroidered work she is led to the king; virgins after her, her companions, are brought to you"** (v14) — Hebrew לִרְקָמוֹת "in embroideries" ("raiment" not written); תּוּבַל "she is led", מוּבָאוֹת "they are brought"; three lines, as the accents divide them; לָךְ "to you" (the king).
- **"With gladness and rejoicing shall they be brought: they shall enter into the king's palace" → "They are led with gladness and joy; they enter into the palace of the king"** (v15) — Hebrew תּוּבַלְנָה בִּשְׂמָחֹת וָגִיל, the verb first; שְׂמָחֹת plural ("gladnesses"); גִּיל "joy"; הֵיכַל מֶלֶךְ "the palace of the king".
- **"Instead of thy fathers shall be thy children, whom thou mayest make princes in all the earth" → "In place of your fathers shall be your sons; you will set them as princes in all the earth"** (v16) — Hebrew תַּחַת "in place of"; בָּנֶיךָ "your sons"; תְּשִׁיתֵמוֹ, שִׁית "set", a main verb ("whom" is not written); שַׂר "prince"; בְּכָל־הָאָרֶץ "in all the earth" (or "in all the land").
- **"I will make thy name to be remembered in all generations: therefore shall the people praise thee for ever and ever" → "Let me bring your name to remembrance in every generation and generation; therefore peoples will give thanks to you forever and ever"** (v17) — Hebrew אַזְכִּירָה, cohortative of הִזְכִּיר ("let me / I will bring to remembrance"); בְּכָל־דֹּר וָדֹר "in every generation and generation" (as Ps 10:6); עַמִּים "peoples"; הוֹדָה "give thanks to".

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1–17 = Heb v2–18; v6–7 = Heb v7–8.
- **Poetic structure** — praise of king (v1-9), address to the bride (v10-15), promise of descendants and praise (v16-17).
- **Royal wedding song** — king and foreign queen.
- **v6-7 cited Heb 1:8-9** — applied to the Son. The Hebrew is kept as it stands; the Greek of Hebrews is the Greek translation of the psalm (from memory: the Septuagint has the same wording), not levelled into the verse.
