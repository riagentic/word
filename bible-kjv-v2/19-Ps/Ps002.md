# Psalm 2

[1] Why have nations raged,
and do folks utter an empty thing?

[2] The kings of the earth take their stand,
and dignitaries have taken counsel together,
against ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ and against his ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧:

[3] "Let us tear apart their bonds,
and let us cast from us their cords."

[4] He who sits in the heavens laughs;
⟦adonai|Adonai|the Lord⟧ derides them.

[5] Then he will speak to them in his anger,
and in his burning anger he will dismay them:

[6] "And as for me, I have set my king
on ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧, my holy mountain."

[7] Let me recount concerning the statute:
⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to me, "You are my son;
I today have begotten you.

[8] Ask of me, and I will give nations as your inheritance,
and as your possession the ends of the earth.

[9] You shall break them with a rod of iron,
like a potter's vessel you shall dash them in pieces."

[10] And now, kings, act with insight;
be disciplined, judges of the earth.

[11] Serve ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ with fear,
and be glad with trembling.

[12] Kiss the son, lest he be angry,
and you perish in the way,
for his anger burns quickly.
Happy are all who take refuge in him.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved. **"the Lord" → "Adonai"** (v4) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי.
- **"Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?" → "Why have nations raged, and do folks utter an empty thing?"** (v1) — Hebrew לָמָּה רָגְשׁוּ גוֹיִם וּלְאֻמִּים יֶהְגּוּ־רִיק: a perfect, then an imperfect; neither noun has the article. רָגַשׁ is found as a verb only here in Hebrew (an Aramaic word of the same letters stands in Dan 6:6, 11, 15, from memory); "rage, be in tumult" is the likely sense, uncertain. לְאֻמִּים "folks" (beside גּוֹיִם "nations"). יֶהְגּוּ is the הָגָה of 1:2 ("softly recites"); of plotting, "utter". רִיק "emptiness, an empty thing". Acts 4:25–26 cites v1–2 in its Greek form; the verse here follows the Hebrew.
- **"set themselves" → "take their stand"** (v2) — Hebrew יִתְיַצְּבוּ. **"the rulers" → "dignitaries"** — Hebrew רוֹזְנִים (also Judg 5:3; Prov 8:15). **"take counsel together" → "have taken counsel together"** — Hebrew נוֹסְדוּ־יָחַד, a perfect; the verb is taken as נוֹסַד, literally "be founded, set close together" (of the root of סוֹד "council"); uncertain. "Saying" (KJV) is not in the Hebrew.
- **"his anointed"** (v2) — Hebrew מְשִׁיחוֹ, "his anointed" (the token gives *mashiach*); "Anointed One", with a capital, said more than the Hebrew and is withdrawn.
- **"break their bands asunder" → "tear apart their bonds"** (v3) — Hebrew נְנַתְּקָה; both verbs are cohortative, "let us … let us". מוֹסְרוֹת "bonds"; עֲבֹתִים "cords".
- **"He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh" → "He who sits in the heavens laughs"** (v4) — Hebrew יוֹשֵׁב, "sits" (of one enthroned; also "dwells"). **"shall have them in derision" → "derides them"** — Hebrew יִלְעַג־לָמוֹ.
- **"in his wrath, and vex them in his sore displeasure" → "in his anger, and in his burning anger he will dismay them"** (v5) — Hebrew בְאַפּוֹ (אַף, literally "nose"), וּבַחֲרוֹנוֹ (חָרוֹן "burning"); יְבַהֲלֵמוֹ "dismay them". The Hebrew order of the second line is kept.
- **"Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion" → "And as for me, I have set my king on Zion, my holy mountain"** (v6) — Hebrew וַאֲנִי נָסַכְתִּי, with the pronoun stressed. נָסַךְ is "pour out" (a drink offering) and also "set, install" (as Prov 8:23); "set" is taken; uncertain. הַר־קָדְשִׁי, literally "the mountain of my holiness".
- **"I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me" → "Let me recount concerning the statute: Yahweh said to me"** (v7) — Hebrew אֲסַפְּרָה אֶל חֹק, a cohortative; אֶל "to, concerning"; חֹק "statute" (a fixed decree). The accents end the first half at חֹק, so "Yahweh" begins the saying ("Yahweh said to me"); others read "the statute of Yahweh". **"Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee" → "You are my son; I today have begotten you"** — Hebrew בְּנִי אַתָּה אֲנִי הַיּוֹם יְלִדְתִּיךָ, the "I" written out and stressed; "son" is not a title here. Acts 13:33; Heb 1:5; 5:5 cite the verse; their Greek says the same.
- **"I shall give thee the heathen" → "I will give nations"** (v8) — Hebrew וְאֶתְּנָה גוֹיִם, no article. **"the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession" → "as your possession the ends of the earth"** — Hebrew order; אֲחֻזָּה "possession, holding".
- **"Thou shalt break them" → "You shall break them"** (v9) — Hebrew תְּרֹעֵם, from רָעַע "break, shatter". The Septuagint read the same letters as "you shall shepherd them" (from רָעָה), and Rev 2:27; 12:5; 19:15 follow that wording (from memory); the verse here follows the Masoretic Text. **"like a potter's vessel"** — Hebrew order kept: "like a potter's vessel you shall dash them in pieces".
- **"Be wise now therefore, O ye kings: be instructed" → "And now, kings, act with insight; be disciplined"** (v10) — Hebrew וְעַתָּה; הַשְׂכִּילוּ "act with insight"; הִוָּסְרוּ, nifal of יָסַר "discipline". **"ye judges of the earth"** — Hebrew שֹׁפְטֵי אָרֶץ, "judges" (not "rulers").
- **"rejoice with trembling" → "be glad with trembling"** (v11) — Hebrew גִּילוּ.
- **"Kiss the Son" → "Kiss the son"** (v12) — Hebrew נַשְּׁקוּ־בַר. The plainest sense: בַּר is "son" (the Aramaic word, used in Hebrew poetry in Prov 31:2; v7 has the Hebrew בֵּן); the Hebrew has no article, and there is no capital in Hebrew. Doubt: בַּר is also Hebrew "pure" ("kiss purely, in sincerity"; so Jerome, from memory) and "grain"; the Septuagint has "lay hold of discipline" (from memory); some emend v11–12 to "kiss his feet" (conjecture, not followed). The verse keeps the Hebrew as printed.
- **"lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way" → "lest he be angry, and you perish in the way"** (v12) — Hebrew וְתֹאבְדוּ דֶרֶךְ, "you perish — way", with no preposition: "in / on the way", or "as to the way".
- **"when his wrath is kindled but a little" → "for his anger burns quickly"** (v12) — Hebrew כִּי־יִבְעַר כִּמְעַט אַפּוֹ: כִּי "for" (or "when"); כִּמְעַט, literally "as a little", is "soon, quickly" or "a little"; אַף "anger".
- **"Blessed are all they that put their trust in him" → "Happy are all who take refuge in him"** (v12) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵי (as 1:1); חוֹסֵי בוֹ, from חָסָה בְּ "take refuge in" (בָּטַח is "trust"). "Him" may be the son or Yahweh; the Hebrew does not say.

### Notes
- **No heading** — Psalm 2 has no heading; Acts 4:25 names David as the speaker.
- **Royal psalm** — the king installed on Zion; 2 Sam 7:14 "I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son".
- **Psalm 1 and 2** — "happy" opens 1:1 and closes 2:12; הָגָה stands in 1:2 and 2:1.
- **NT** — v1–2 in Acts 4:25–26; v7 in Acts 13:33; Heb 1:5; 5:5; v9 in Rev 2:27; 12:5; 19:15.
