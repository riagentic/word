# Psalm 4

To the overseer, with stringed instruments. A psalm of David.

[1] When I call, answer me, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of my righteousness!
In distress you have made room for me;
show me favor and hear my prayer.

[2] Sons of man, how long shall my glory be for shame,
will you love an empty thing, will you seek a lie? Selah.

[3] And know that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ has distinguished a faithful one for himself;
⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will hear when I call to him.

[4] Be agitated, and do not sin;
say in your heart on your bed, and be silent. Selah.

[5] Sacrifice sacrifices of righteousness,
and trust in ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[6] Many are saying, "Who will show us good?"
Lift up upon us the light of your face, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧!

[7] You have put gladness in my heart,
more than at the time their grain and their new wine are many.

[8] In ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧ together I will lie down and sleep,
for you, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, alone
make me dwell in security.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי *Elohim*.
- **"To the chief Musician on Neginoth" → "To the overseer, with stringed instruments"** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ, participle of נִצַּח "oversee" (1 Chr 15:21; 2 Chr 2:2): the one who leads the performance; traditional "To the chief musician". בִּנְגִינוֹת, "with stringed instruments" (נְגִינָה, from נִגֵּן "play a stringed instrument"). **"A Psalm of David"** — מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד; לְ "of": by, for or belonging to David (as Ps 3).
- **Layout** (v8) — the Hebrew verse has three lines: the first ends at "sleep", the second at "alone".
- **No "O"** (v1, 6, 8) — the Hebrew vocatives ("Elohim of my righteousness", "Yahweh") have none.
- **"Hear me when I call" → "When I call, answer me"** (v1) — Hebrew בְּקָרְאִי עֲנֵנִי, in this order; עָנָה "answer".
- **"thou hast enlarged me when I was in distress" → "In distress you have made room for me"** (v1) — Hebrew בַּצָּר הִרְחַבְתָּ לִּי, literally "in the narrow (place) you have made wide for me" (הִרְחִיב, the "enlarge" of 2 Sam 22:37); a perfect. **"have mercy upon me" → "show me favor"** — Hebrew חָנֵּנִי, from חָנַן (not "mercy", which is רַחֲמִים).
- **"O ye sons of men" → "Sons of man"** (v2) — Hebrew בְּנֵי אִישׁ, with אִישׁ "man" (not אָדָם, as in "son of man"); some take it as "men of standing" (as Ps 49:2; 62:9).
- **"how long will ye turn my glory into shame?" → "how long shall my glory be for shame"** (v2) — Hebrew עַד־מֶה כְבוֹדִי לִכְלִמָּה, with no verb: "how long — my glory for shame"; כָּבוֹד "glory". **"how long will ye love vanity, and seek after leasing?" → "will you love an empty thing, will you seek a lie?"** — Hebrew תֶּאֱהָבוּן רִיק תְּבַקְשׁוּ כָזָב; "how long" is written once and governs the verse; רִיק as 2:1, כָּזָב "a lie" (singular).
- **"the LORD hath set apart him that is godly for himself" → "Yahweh has distinguished a faithful one for himself"** (v3) — Hebrew הִפְלָה, "set apart as distinct" (as Exod 8:22; 9:4; 11:7); חָסִיד "faithful one", one who keeps *chesed*. Some Hebrew manuscripts have הִפְלִיא ("has made wonderful", as Ps 31:21, from memory); the verse follows the printed text. **"will hear"** — Hebrew יִשְׁמַע, an imperfect.
- **"Stand in awe, and sin not" → "Be agitated, and do not sin"** (v4) — Hebrew רִגְזוּ, "be agitated, tremble": in anger or in fear; the verse does not decide. Eph 4:26 has "Be angry and do not sin" in the Greek of the Septuagint; the verse here follows the Hebrew.
- **"commune with your own heart upon your bed, and be still" → "say in your heart on your bed, and be silent"** (v4) — Hebrew אִמְרוּ בִלְבַבְכֶם עַל־מִשְׁכַּבְכֶם וְדֹמּוּ: "say" (אָמַר); "heart" and "bed" are singular with "your" plural; דֹּמּוּ from דָּמַם "be silent, be still".
- **"Offer the sacrifices of righteousness" → "Sacrifice sacrifices of righteousness"** (v5) — Hebrew זִבְחוּ זִבְחֵי־צֶדֶק, verb and noun of one root; no article. **"put your trust in" → "trust in"** — Hebrew בִטְחוּ אֶל.
- **"There be many that say, Who will shew us any good?" → "Many are saying, 'Who will show us good?'"** (v6) — Hebrew רַבִּים אֹמְרִים, as 3:2; "any" is not in the Hebrew. **"LORD, lift thou up the light of thy countenance upon us" → "Lift up upon us the light of your face, Yahweh"** — Hebrew נְסָה־עָלֵינוּ אוֹר פָּנֶיךָ, in this order. נְסָה is an unusual spelling, taken as the imperative of נָשָׂא "lift up" (as Num 6:26); uncertain.
- **"Thou hast put gladness in my heart, more than in the time that their corn and their wine increased" → "You have put gladness in my heart, more than at the time their grain and their new wine are many"** (v7) — Hebrew מֵעֵת דְּגָנָם וְתִירוֹשָׁם רָבּוּ, "from the time": a comparison; דָּגָן "grain", תִּירוֹשׁ "new wine"; רָבּוּ "they are many", the verb of 3:1. "Their" has no named antecedent (the "many" of v6).
- **"I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep" → "In shalom together I will lie down and sleep"** (v8) — Hebrew בְּשָׁלוֹם יַחְדָּו אֶשְׁכְּבָה וְאִישָׁן; יַחְדָּו "together" (= at once: the lying down and the sleep); the verbs of 3:5.
- **"for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety" → "for you, Yahweh, alone make me dwell in security"** (v8) — Hebrew לְבָדָד, "alone, apart": the accents join it to "you, Yahweh" ("you alone"); it may also go with what follows ("make me dwell alone, in security", as Deut 33:28 בֶּטַח בָּדָד). לָבֶטַח "in security".

### Notes
- **Numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1 = Heb v2, and so on to v8 = Heb v9.
- **Evening psalm** — pair to Ps 3 ("I will lie down and sleep", 3:5; 4:8).
