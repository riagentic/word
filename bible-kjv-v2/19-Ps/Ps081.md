# Psalm 81

To the overseer, upon the ⟦gittith|Gittit|Gittith⟧. Of ⟦asaph|Asaf|Asaph⟧.

[1] Shout for joy to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ our strength;
shout to the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧!

[2] Lift up a melody, and sound the timbrel,
the pleasant lyre with the harp.

[3] Blow a ⟦shofar|shofar|trumpet⟧ at the new moon,
at the full moon, for the day of our feast.

[4] For it is a statute for ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧,
an ordinance of the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧.

[5] As a testimony in ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ he set it,
when he went out over the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.
I hear a lip that I did not know:

[6] "I removed his shoulder from the burden;
his palms passed from the basket.

[7] In distress you called, and I pulled you out;
I answered you in the hiding place of thunder;
I tried you at the waters of ⟦meribah|Merivah|Meribah⟧. Selah.

[8] Hear, my people, and I will testify against you;
⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, if you would listen to me!

[9] There shall not be among you a strange ⟦el|el|god⟧,
and you shall not bow down to a foreign ⟦el|el|god⟧.

[10] I am ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,
who brought you up from the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.
Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it.

[11] And my people did not listen to my voice,
and ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ was not willing toward me.

[12] And I let him go in the stubbornness of their heart;
they walked in their counsels.

[13] If only my people were listening to me,
⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ would walk in my ways!

[14] Soon would I bring low their enemies,
and against their adversaries I would turn my hand.

[15] Those who hate ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ would cringe before him,
and their time would be forever.

[16] And he fed him with the fat of the wheat,
and with honey from the rock I would satisfy you."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 4, 10) — Hebrew *Elohim*. Ps 81 is one of the Elohistic psalms (42–83): the divine names stand as printed.
- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v10, 15) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"To the chief Musician upon Gittith, A Psalm of Asaph" → "To the overseer, upon the Gittit. Of Asaph."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ עַל־הַגִּתִּית לְאָסָף: לַמְנַצֵּחַ from the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21 (traditional "to the chief musician"); עַל־הַגִּתִּית "upon the Gittit", meaning unknown (as Ps 8); the Hebrew has no "psalm" here; לְ: by, for or belonging to (not decided).
- **"Sing aloud unto God our strength: make a joyful noise unto the God of Jacob" → "Shout for joy to Elohim our strength; shout to the Elohim of Jacob"** (v1) — Hebrew הַרְנִינוּ "shout for joy" (רָנַן); הָרִיעוּ "shout" (הֵרִיעַ, of voices); עוּזֵּנוּ "our strength".
- **"Take a psalm, and bring hither the timbrel, the pleasant harp with the psaltery" → "Lift up a melody, and sound the timbrel, the pleasant lyre with the harp"** (v2) — Hebrew שְׂאוּ־זִמְרָה "lift up a melody" (זִמְרָה, music sung or played; not מִזְמוֹר "psalm"); וּתְנוּ־תֹף, literally "and give a timbrel"; כִּנּוֹר "lyre", נֵבֶל "harp" (the kinds are uncertain).
- **"Blow up the trumpet in the new moon, in the time appointed, on our solemn feast day" → "Blow a shofar at the new moon, at the full moon, for the day of our feast"** (v3) — Hebrew שׁוֹפָר without the article; בַּחֹדֶשׁ "at the new moon" (also "month"); בַּכֶּסֶה "at the full moon" (uncertain; as Prov 7:20; "the time appointed" is a guess); לְיוֹם חַגֵּנוּ "for the day of our feast".
- **"For this was a statute for Israel, and a law of the God of Jacob" → "For it is a statute for Israel, an ordinance of the Elohim of Jacob"** (v4) — Hebrew כִּי חֹק לְיִשְׂרָאֵל הוּא "for a statute for Israel it is"; מִשְׁפָּט "ordinance" (literally "judgment", of a ruling); לֵאלֹהֵי "of (belonging to) the Elohim"; "and" is not written.
- **"This he ordained in Joseph for a testimony, when he went out through the land of Egypt: where I heard a language that I understood not" → "As a testimony in Joseph he set it, when he went out over the land of Egypt. I hear a lip that I did not know"** (v5) — Hebrew עֵדוּת "testimony", first; בִּיהוֹסֵף, the long spelling Yehosef (only here); שָׂמוֹ "he set it". בְּצֵאתוֹ עַל־אֶרֶץ מִצְרָיִם "when he went out over the land of Egypt": Elohim going out against Egypt (Exod 11:4), or Joseph going out over the land (Gen 41:45, the same words); not decided. שְׂפַת "lip": a language is meant (as Gen 11:1); אֶשְׁמָע "I hear", an imperfect. Who says "I" is not named: the speaker hears the words that follow.
- **"I removed his shoulder from the burden: his hands were delivered from the pots" → "I removed his shoulder from the burden; his palms passed from the basket"** (v6) — Hebrew שִׁכְמוֹ "his shoulder", כַּפָּיו "his palms" (third person; the former "your" was not in the Hebrew); סֵבֶל "burden" (forced carrying, 1 Kgs 11:28); דּוּד "basket" (or "pot"); תַּעֲבֹרְנָה "passed", an imperfect in a past setting.
- **"I delivered thee; I answered thee in the secret place of thunder: I proved thee" → "I pulled you out; I answered you in the hiding place of thunder; I tried you"** (v7) — Hebrew וָאֲחַלְּצֶךָּ "and I pulled you out" (חִלֵּץ); סֵתֶר "hiding place"; אֶבְחָנְךָ "I tried you" (בָּחַן, as Ps 7:9); אֶעֶנְךָ and אֶבְחָנְךָ are imperfects in a past setting.
- **"Hear, O my people, and I will testify unto thee: O Israel, if thou wilt hearken unto me" → "Hear, my people, and I will testify against you; Israel, if you would listen to me"** (v8) — Hebrew וְאָעִידָה בָּךְ "and I will testify against you" (הֵעִיד בְּ, as Ps 50:7); אִם here is a wish ("if only you would"); no "O".
- **"There shall no strange god be in thee; neither shalt thou worship any strange god" → "There shall not be among you a strange el, and you shall not bow down to a foreign el"** (v9) — Hebrew אֵל זָר "a strange El", אֵל נֵכָר "a foreign El" (token `el`, "god" in small letters, as Deut 32:12): two adjectives, kept apart; תִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה "bow down" (not "worship").
- **"which brought thee out of the land of Egypt" → "who brought you up from the land of Egypt"** (v10) — Hebrew הַמַּעַלְךָ, hifil of עָלָה "go up"; אָנֹכִי "I".
- **"But my people would not hearken to my voice; and Israel would none of me" → "And my people did not listen to my voice, and Israel was not willing toward me"** (v11) — Hebrew וְלֹא־שָׁמַע … לְקוֹלִי "did not listen to my voice"; לֹא־אָבָה לִי, literally "was not willing to me".
- **"So I gave them up unto their own hearts' lust: and they walked in their own counsels" → "And I let him go in the stubbornness of their heart; they walked in their counsels"** (v12) — Hebrew וָאֲשַׁלְּחֵהוּ "and I let him go" (שִׁלַּח; the people as "him", then "their"); שְׁרִירוּת "stubbornness" (as Deut 29:19; Jer 7:24); יֵלְכוּ, an imperfect after the past ("they walked", or "that they might walk"); "own" is not written.
- **"Oh that my people had hearkened unto me, and Israel had walked in my ways!" → "If only my people were listening to me, Israel would walk in my ways!"** (v13) — Hebrew לוּ with the participle שֹׁמֵעַ "if only … were listening"; יְהַלֵּכוּ "would walk" (the second line may also go on with the wish).
- **"I should soon have subdued their enemies" → "Soon would I bring low their enemies"** (v14) — Hebrew כִּמְעַט "soon" (literally "as a little"); אַכְנִיעַ "bring low" (hifil of כָּנַע); צָרֵיהֶם "their adversaries".
- **"The haters of the LORD should have submitted themselves unto him: but their time should have endured for ever" → "Those who hate Yahweh would cringe before him, and their time would be forever"** (v15) — Hebrew יְכַחֲשׁוּ־לוֹ, literally "would lie to him": a feigned submission (as Deut 33:29; 2 Sam 22:45); וִיהִי עִתָּם לְעוֹלָם "and their time would be forever": whose time, and of what, is not said ("but" is not written).
- **"He should have fed them also with the finest of the wheat: and with honey out of the rock should I have satisfied thee" → "And he fed him with the fat of the wheat, and with honey from the rock I would satisfy you"** (v16) — Hebrew וַיַּאֲכִילֵהוּ, a narrative past form, "and he fed him": as printed (Deut 32:13–14 tells the same in the past); many read "and I would feed him" (versions, from memory). מֵחֵלֶב חִטָּה "with the fat of the wheat" (the best of it); the change of persons (he / I, him / you) is the Hebrew's.
- **Gittith** — musical term; token kept.
- **Poetic structure** — festival call to worship (v1-5), divine oracle recalling Exodus (v6-16).

### Notes
- **Numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1 = Heb v2, and so on to v16 = Heb v17.
