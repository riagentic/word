# Psalm 131

A song of the ascents. Of David.

[1] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, my heart has not been high,
and my eyes have not been raised;
and I have not walked about in great things
and in things too wonderful for me.

[2] Surely I have smoothed and quieted my soul,
like a weaned child upon his mother;
like the weaned child upon me is my soul.

[3] Hope, ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, toward ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧,
from now and forever.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 3) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"A Song of degrees of David" → "A song of the ascents. Of David"** (heading) — Hebrew שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת לְדָוִד, the first words of v1; מַעֲלָה "a going up, a step", with the article. לְדָוִד may be "by", "for" or "belonging to" David; "of" leaves it open.
- **"LORD, my heart is not haughty, nor mine eyes lofty" → "Yahweh, my heart has not been high, and my eyes have not been raised"** (v1) — Hebrew לֹא־גָבַהּ לִבִּי וְלֹא־רָמוּ עֵינַי, two perfects: גָּבַהּ "be high" (as 2 Chr 26:16 "his heart was high"), רוּם "be high, be raised"; two verbs, so "raised" for the second. Each line keeps its וְ "and". **"neither do I exercise myself in great matters, or in things too high for me" → "and I have not walked about in great things and in things too wonderful for me"** — Hebrew הִלַּכְתִּי, piel of הָלַךְ "walk about"; גְּדֹלוֹת "great things"; נִפְלָאוֹת מִמֶּנִּי "things too wonderful for me" (נִפְלָא מִן, as Gen 18:14).
- **"Surely I have behaved and quieted myself" → "Surely I have smoothed and quieted my soul"** (v2) — Hebrew אִם־לֹא, the form of an oath, literally "if I have not …": it means "surely I have". שִׁוִּיתִי, piel of שָׁוָה "make level, smooth" (as Isa 28:25); דּוֹמַמְתִּי "I have made silent" (from דָּמַם "be silent"); the object נַפְשִׁי "my soul" belongs to both. **"as a child that is weaned of his mother: my soul is even as a weaned child" → "like a weaned child upon his mother; like the weaned child upon me is my soul"** — Hebrew כְּגָמֻל עֲלֵי אִמּוֹ כַּגָּמֻל עָלַי נַפְשִׁי: גָּמֻל "a weaned one" (from גָּמַל "wean"); עֲלֵי אִמּוֹ "upon his mother" and עָלַי "upon me", one preposition twice. "within me" (as עָלַי of the soul is rendered in Ps 42:4–6) would cut the second line from the first, so "upon me" is kept here.
- **"Let Israel hope in the LORD from henceforth and for ever" → "Hope, Israel, toward Yahweh, from now and forever"** (v3) — Hebrew יַחֵל יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶל־יְהוָה, the imperative, Israel addressed, אֶל "toward" (as Ps 130:7); מֵעַתָּה וְעַד־עוֹלָם, literally "from now and until the age" (as Ps 125:2).

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — the heading is part of v1 in the Hebrew too; the numbers agree.
- **Poetic structure** — a lowly heart (v1), the soul quieted like a weaned child (v2), Israel called to hope (v3); v3 joins the words of Ps 130:7 and Ps 125:2.
