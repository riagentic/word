# Psalm 44

To the overseer. Of the sons of ⟦korah|Qorach|Korah⟧. A Maskil.

[1] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, with our ears we have heard,
our fathers have recounted to us,
a work you worked in their days,
in the days of old.

[2] You, with your hand, dispossessed nations,
and you planted them;
you did evil to folks, and you sent them out.

[3] For not by their sword did they possess the land,
and their arm did not save them;
but your right hand and your arm
and the light of your face,
for you were pleased with them.

[4] You are he, my King, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;
command the salvations of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧.

[5] Through you we gore our adversaries;
through your name we trample those who rise against us.

[6] For not in my bow do I trust,
and my sword will not save me.

[7] For you have saved us from our adversaries,
and those who hate us you have put to shame.

[8] In ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ we have praised all the day,
and to your name we will give thanks forever. Selah.

[9] Yet you have rejected and disgraced us,
and you do not go out with our hosts.

[10] You make us turn back from the adversary,
and those who hate us have pillaged for themselves.

[11] You give us like a flock for food,
and among the nations you have strewn us.

[12] You sell your people for no wealth,
and you have made no increase by their price.

[13] You make us a reproach to our neighbors,
a derision and a scorn to those around us.

[14] You make us a proverb among the nations,
a shaking of the head among the folks.

[15] All the day my disgrace is before me,
and the shame of my face has covered me,

[16] from the voice of the one who reproaches and reviles,
because of the enemy and the avenger.

[17] All this has come upon us,
and we have not forgotten you,
and we have not dealt falsely in your covenant.

[18] Our heart has not drawn back,
nor have our steps turned aside from your path,

[19] for you have crushed us in the place of jackals
and covered us over with the shadow of death.

[20] If we have forgotten the name of our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧
and spread out our palms to a strange ⟦el|el|god⟧,

[21] would not ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ search this out?
For he knows the hidden things of the heart.

[22] For on account of you we have been killed all the day;
we have been reckoned as a flock for slaughter.

[23] Rouse yourself! Why do you sleep, ⟦adonai|Adonai|Lord⟧?
Awake! Do not reject forever!

[24] Why do you hide your face,
forget our affliction and our oppression?

[25] For our soul has bowed down to the dust;
our belly has clung to the earth.

[26] Rise up as a help for us,
and ransom us on account of your ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 4, 8, 20, 21) — Hebrew *Elohim*; an Elohistic psalm: the divine names as printed. **"O Lord" → "Adonai"** (v23) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי.
- **"To the chief Musician for the sons of Korah, Maschil" → "To the overseer. Of the sons of Korah. A Maskil."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ לִבְנֵי־קֹרַח מַשְׂכִּיל, in this order. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer", from the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21 (traditional "to the chief musician"). לִבְנֵי: the לְ may mean "by", "for" or "belonging to"; "of" leaves it open. מַשְׂכִּיל (from שָׂכַל "have insight, make wise"): the kind of psalm is not known ("instructive" or "skilful song").
- **"We have heard with our ears, O God … what work thou didst in their days, in the times of old" → "Elohim, with our ears we have heard … a work you worked in their days, in the days of old"** (v1) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים בְּאָזְנֵינוּ שָׁמַעְנוּ, *Elohim* first; סִפֵּר "recount"; פֹּעַל פָּעַלְתָּ, noun and verb of one root ("work … worked"); בִּימֵי קֶדֶם "in the days of old".
- **"How thou didst drive out the heathen with thy hand, and plantedst them; how thou didst afflict the people, and cast them out" → "You, with your hand, dispossessed nations, and you planted them; you did evil to folks, and you sent them out"** (v2) — Hebrew אַתָּה יָדְךָ גּוֹיִם הוֹרַשְׁתָּ, literally "you — your hand — nations you dispossessed" ("with" supplied); הוֹרִישׁ "dispossess"; תָּרַע לְאֻמִּים, הֵרַע לְ "do evil to"; לְאֻמִּים "folks". וַתְּשַׁלְּחֵם "and you sent them out": "them" may be Israel sent out to spread (as a vine its shoots) or the folks cast out; the Hebrew leaves it open. No "how" in the Hebrew.
- **"they got not the land in possession by their own sword … because thou hadst a favour unto them" → "not by their sword did they possess the land … for you were pleased with them"** (v3) — Hebrew לֹא בְחַרְבָּם יָרְשׁוּ אָרֶץ; "own" is not written; כִּי־יְמִינְךָ after the negative "but your right hand"; רְצִיתָם, רָצָה "be pleased with". פָּנִים "face" (KJV "countenance").
- **"Thou art my King, O God: command deliverances for Jacob" → "You are he, my King, Elohim; command the salvations of Jacob"** (v4) — Hebrew אַתָּה־הוּא מַלְכִּי אֱלֹהִים, "you (are) he": the pronoun הוּא stresses "you"; צַוֵּה imperative; יְשׁוּעוֹת יַעֲקֹב "salvations of Jacob", plural, construct. The Greek reads a participle, "who commands" (from memory); the Masoretic imperative is kept.
- **"Through thee will we push down our enemies: through thy name will we tread them under" → "Through you we gore our adversaries; through your name we trample"** (v5) — Hebrew נְנַגֵּחַ, נָגַח "gore" (of an ox with its horns, as Deut 33:17); צָרֵינוּ "our adversaries" (צַר, also v7, 10); בּוּס "trample".
- **"For I will not trust in my bow, neither shall my sword save me" → "For not in my bow do I trust, and my sword will not save me"** (v6) — Hebrew כִּי לֹא בְקַשְׁתִּי אֶבְטָח וְחַרְבִּי, "not in my bow" first; וְ "and".
- **"But thou hast saved us" → "For you have saved us"** (v7) — Hebrew כִּי, "for" (or, after the negative of v6, "but").
- **"In God we boast all the day long, and praise thy name for ever" → "In Elohim we have praised all the day, and to your name we will give thanks forever"** (v8) — Hebrew בֵּאלֹהִים הִלַּלְנוּ, piel perfect "we have praised" ("boast" is the hitpael of this verb); וְשִׁמְךָ לְעוֹלָם נוֹדֶה, הוֹדָה "give thanks to".
- **"But thou hast cast off, and put us to shame; and goest not forth with our armies" → "Yet you have rejected and disgraced us, and you do not go out with our hosts"** (v9) — Hebrew אַף־זָנַחְתָּ וַתַּכְלִימֵנוּ: זָנַח "reject" (as v23); הִכְלִים "disgrace" here and כְּלִמָּה "disgrace" v15, because בּוֹשׁ "put to shame / shame" stands beside it in v7 and v15 (the הִכְלִים row has "put to shame"); תֵצֵא imperfect "you do not go out"; צְבָאוֹת "hosts" (as "Yahweh of Hosts").
- **"Thou makest us to turn back from the enemy: and they which hate us spoil for themselves" → "You make us turn back from the adversary, and those who hate us have pillaged for themselves"** (v10) — Hebrew מִנִּי־צָר "from the adversary"; שָׁסוּ לָמוֹ, שָׁסָה "pillage", perfect. In v10–14 the Hebrew uses the imperfect for what God does (here an English present) and the perfect in v11b, 12b.
- **"Thou hast given us like sheep appointed for meat; and hast scattered us among the heathen" → "You give us like a flock for food, and among the nations you have strewn us"** (v11) — Hebrew תִּתְּנֵנוּ כְּצֹאן מַאֲכָל: "appointed" is not written; צֹאן "flock"; זָרָה "strew" (as Lev 26:33).
- **"Thou sellest thy people for nought, and dost not increase thy wealth by their price" → "You sell your people for no wealth, and you have made no increase by their price"** (v12) — Hebrew בְלֹא־הוֹן "for no wealth" (= for nothing); וְלֹא־רִבִּיתָ "you have not made much", with no object ("thy wealth" is not written); מְחִירֵיהֶם is plural, "their prices".
- **"a scorn and a derision" → "a derision and a scorn"** (v13) — Hebrew לַעַג וָקֶלֶס, in this order; חֶרְפָּה "reproach"; סְבִיבוֹתֵינוּ "those around us" (lit. "our surroundings").
- **"a byword among the heathen, a shaking of the head among the people" → "a proverb among the nations, a shaking of the head among the folks"** (v14) — Hebrew מָשָׁל "proverb" (as Deut 28:37); the Hebrew writes בַּל־אֻמִּים, commonly taken as בַּלְאֻמִּים "among the folks" (as לְאֻמִּים v2): the written division would give בַּל "not", which makes no sense here.
- **"My confusion is continually before me" → "All the day my disgrace is before me"** (v15) — Hebrew כָּל־הַיּוֹם (as v8, 22), כְּלִמָּתִי "my disgrace"; בֹּשֶׁת פָּנַי "the shame of my face".
- **"For the voice of him that reproacheth and blasphemeth; by reason of the enemy and avenger" → "from the voice of the one who reproaches and reviles, because of the enemy and the avenger"** (v16) — Hebrew מִקּוֹל מְחָרֵף וּמְגַדֵּף מִפְּנֵי אוֹיֵב וּמִתְנַקֵּם: חֵרֵף "reproach", גִּדֵּף "revile" (not "blaspheme"); מִפְּנֵי of a cause "because of" (lit. "from the face of"); מִתְנַקֵּם hitpael, one who takes vengeance for himself.
- **"yet have we not forgotten thee, neither have we dealt falsely in thy covenant" → "and we have not forgotten you, and we have not dealt falsely in your covenant"** (v17) — Hebrew וְלֹא … וְלֹא־, "and … and"; "yet" is not written.
- **"Our heart is not turned back, neither have our steps declined from thy way" → "Our heart has not drawn back, nor have our steps turned aside from your path"** (v18) — Hebrew לֹא־נָסוֹג אָחוֹר לִבֵּנוּ וַתֵּט אֲשֻׁרֵינוּ: the second verb has no negative of its own; the לֹא of the first line carries over ("nor"), else the line would say the opposite of v17; אֹרַח "path" (דֶּרֶךְ is "way").
- **"Though thou hast sore broken us in the place of dragons" → "for you have crushed us in the place of jackals"** (v19) — Hebrew כִּי דִכִּיתָנוּ: כִּי may be "for", "though" or "that" (we have not turned away, that you should crush us): "for" leaves it open; "sore" is not written; תַּנִּים "jackals" (as Job 30:29); some read תַּנִּין "sea monster" (from memory). צַלְמָוֶת "the shadow of death" as pointed (the reading "deep darkness" as the Job 3 row); וַתְּכַס עָלֵינוּ "and covered over us".
- **"or stretched out our hands to a strange god" → "and spread out our palms to a strange el"** (v20) — Hebrew וַנִּפְרֹשׂ כַּפֵּינוּ לְאֵל זָר: וַ "and"; כַּף "palm"; אֵל with small letters, as "another el" (Exod 34:14).
- **"Shall not God search this out? for he knoweth the secrets of the heart" → "would not Elohim search this out? For he knows the hidden things of the heart"** (v21) — Hebrew חָקַר "search out"; תַּעֲלֻמוֹת "hidden things".
- **"Yea, for thy sake are we killed all the day long; we are counted as sheep for the slaughter" → "For on account of you we have been killed all the day; we have been reckoned as a flock for slaughter"** (v22) — Hebrew כִּי־עָלֶיךָ הֹרַגְנוּ, "for upon you (= because of you)"; both verbs perfect (pual, nifal); חָשַׁב "reckon"; צֹאן "flock". Quoted in Rom 8:36 in the Greek's present ("we are being put to death").
- **"Awake, why sleepest thou, O Lord? arise, cast us not off for ever" → "Rouse yourself! Why do you sleep, Adonai? Awake! Do not reject forever!"** (v23) — Hebrew עוּרָה … הָקִיצָה: עוּר "rouse oneself", הֵקִיץ "awake" (two verbs); אַל־תִּזְנַח לָנֶצַח has no object ("us" is not written); זָנַח "reject" as v9; לָנֶצַח "forever" (as Ps 13:1).
- **"Wherefore hidest thou thy face, and forgettest our affliction" → "Why do you hide your face, forget our affliction"** (v24) — Hebrew תִּשְׁכַּח without "and": the one "why" governs both verbs; עֳנִי "affliction", לַחַץ "oppression".
- **"For our soul is bowed down to the dust: our belly cleaveth unto the earth" → "For our soul has bowed down to the dust; our belly has clung to the earth"** (v25) — Hebrew perfects שָׁחָה, דָּבְקָה; דָּבַק "cling"; בֶּטֶן "belly".
- **"Arise for our help, and redeem us for thy mercies' sake" → "Rise up as a help for us, and ransom us on account of your chesed"** (v26) — Hebrew קוּמָה עֶזְרָתָה לָּנוּ, "rise, (as) a help for us" ("as" supplied); פָּדָה "ransom" (גָּאַל is "redeem"); לְמַעַן חַסְדֶּךָ, singular *chesed*.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1–26 = Heb v2–27.
- **Poetic structure** — past-victory recital (v1-8), lament of present defeat (v9-22), plea for help (v23-26).
- **National lament** despite covenant-fidelity — suffers unjustly.
- **v22 cited Rom 8:36** — Paul on Christian suffering.
