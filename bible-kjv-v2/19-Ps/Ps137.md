# Psalm 137

[1] By the rivers of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ —
there we sat, also we wept,
when we remembered ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧.

[2] On the willows in its midst
we hung up our lyres.

[3] For there our captors
asked of us words of song,
and our tormentors gladness:
"Sing to us from the song of ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧!"

[4] How shall we sing the song of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧
on foreign ground?

[5] If I forget you, ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧,
let my right hand forget!

[6] Let my tongue cling to my palate,
if I do not remember you,
if I do not bring up ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧
above the head of my gladness!

[7] Remember, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, against the sons of Edom
the day of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧,
those who said, "Lay bare, lay bare,
down to the foundation in it!"

[8] Daughter of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧, the destroyed one,
happy is he who repays you
your dealing that you dealt to us!

[9] Happy is he who seizes and dashes in pieces
your children against the crag!

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v4, 7) — Tetragrammaton; the Hebrew vocatives have no "O" (v5, 7, 8).
- **"there we sat down, yea, we wept" → "there we sat, also we wept"** (v1) — Hebrew שָׁם יָשַׁבְנוּ גַּם־בָּכִינוּ: יָשַׁב "sit"; גַּם "also".
- **"We hanged our harps upon the willows in the midst thereof" → "On the willows in its midst we hung up our lyres"** (v2) — Hebrew עַל־עֲרָבִים בְּתוֹכָהּ: עֲרָבִים "willows" (as Lev 23:40; perhaps the Euphrates poplar: the kind is uncertain); בְּתוֹכָהּ "in its midst" (of Babylon); כִּנּוֹר "lyre".
- **"they that carried us away captive required of us a song; and they that wasted us required of us mirth, saying, Sing us one of the songs of Zion" → "our captors asked of us words of song, and our tormentors gladness: "Sing to us from the song of Zion!""** (v3) — Hebrew שְׁאֵלוּנוּ "asked of us" (שָׁאַל, once for both lines); שׁוֹבֵינוּ "our captors" (שָׁבָה "take captive"); דִּבְרֵי־שִׁיר "words of song"; תּוֹלָלֵינוּ is found only here: the meaning is uncertain ("our tormentors" by the sense; "our plunderers", from שָׁלַל, is a guess); שִׂמְחָה "gladness" (also v6); "saying" is not written; מִשִּׁיר צִיּוֹן "from the song of Zion", singular.
- **"in a strange land" → "on foreign ground"** (v4) — Hebrew עַל אַדְמַת נֵכָר, literally "on the ground of foreignness" (אֲדָמָה "ground", as בֶּן־נֵכָר "foreigner").
- **"let my right hand forget her cunning" → "let my right hand forget"** (v5) — Hebrew תִּשְׁכַּח יְמִינִי has no object: what the hand forgets is not said ("her cunning", "its skill" are supplied). A self-curse in the form "If I …, let …": the curse is spoken, so it stands as written (not the bare oath formula). The common emendation תִּכְחַשׁ "wither" is not taken (from memory).
- **"let my tongue cleave to the roof of my mouth; if I prefer not Jerusalem above my chief joy" → "Let my tongue cling to my palate, if I do not remember you, if I do not bring up Jerusalem above the head of my gladness"** (v6) — Hebrew תִּדְבַּק "cling" (דָּבַק); חֵךְ "palate"; אַעֲלֶה "bring up" (hifil of עָלָה; "prefer" is the sense); עַל רֹאשׁ שִׂמְחָתִי "above the head of my gladness" = above my chief gladness.
- **"Remember, O LORD, the children of Edom in the day of Jerusalem; who said, Rase it, rase it, even to the foundation thereof" → "Remember, Yahweh, against the sons of Edom the day of Jerusalem, those who said, "Lay bare, lay bare, down to the foundation in it!""** (v7) — Hebrew זְכֹר … לִבְנֵי אֱדוֹם אֵת יוֹם: "the day" is the object of "remember", לְ "against" (literally "for, to" the sons of Edom: count it to them); בְּנֵי "sons"; הָאֹמְרִים "those who said" (participle); עָרוּ עָרוּ "lay bare" (עָרָה), twice; עַד הַיְסוֹד בָּהּ "to the foundation in it".
- **"O daughter of Babylon, who art to be destroyed" → "Daughter of Babylon, the destroyed one"** (v8) — Hebrew הַשְּׁדוּדָה, passive participle of שָׁדַד with the article, "the destroyed (one)" (as Judg 5:27 "destroyed"); the time is not marked ("to be destroyed" says a future the form does not; "the devastated one" by sense). An active reading "the destroyer" (שׁוֹדֵדָה) is proposed by some (from memory) and is not taken.
- **"happy shall he be, that rewardeth thee as thou hast served us" → "happy is he who repays you your dealing that you dealt to us"** (v8) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵי שֶׁיְשַׁלֶּם־לָךְ "happy is he who repays you" (אַשְׁרֵי "happy", also v9; שִׁלֵּם "repay"); אֶת־גְּמוּלֵךְ שֶׁגָּמַלְתְּ לָנוּ "your dealing that you dealt to us" (גְּמוּל and its verb גָּמַל, as Ps 94:2).
- **"Happy shall he be, that taketh and dasheth thy little ones against the stones" → "Happy is he who seizes and dashes in pieces your children against the crag"** (v9) — Hebrew שֶׁיֹּאחֵז וְנִפֵּץ "who seizes and dashes in pieces" (אָחַז "seize"; נִפֵּץ "dash in pieces", as Ps 2:9); עֹלָלַיִךְ "your children" (עוֹלֵל, a young child, as 1 Sam 15:3); אֶל־הַסָּלַע "against the crag" (סֶלַע, singular with the article). The words stand as written; nothing is softened.

### Notes
- **Exile lament** — Babylon (v1–4), the vow to Jerusalem (v5–6), the plea against Edom and Babylon (v7–9).
- **v7–9** — the Hebrew asks Yahweh to remember the day of Jerusalem's fall against Edom and calls happy the one who repays Babylon in kind (compare Obad 10–14; Isa 13:16; Lam 4:21–22). The translation gives the words as they stand.
