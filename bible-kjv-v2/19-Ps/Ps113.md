[2] Blessed be the name of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧

[3] From the rising of the sun to its setting,

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 2, 3, 4, 5) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v5) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Praise ye the LORD" → "Halleluyah"** (v1, 9) — Hebrew הַלְלוּ יָהּ, "praise Yah"; in v1 the WLC writes it as two words (with a dividing stroke), in v9 joined by maqqef: the same cry both times.
- **"Praise, O ye servants of the LORD" → "Praise, servants of Yahweh"** (v1) — Hebrew הַלְלוּ עַבְדֵי יְהוָה; no "O" (the Hebrew has no word for it). עֶבֶד "servant" (the word is "slave": of Yahweh's men "servant", as its row).
- **"from this time forth and for evermore" → "from now and forever"** (v2) — Hebrew מֵעַתָּה וְעַד־עוֹלָם, "from now and unto the age"; יְהִי … מְבֹרָךְ is a wish ("let … be blessed").
- **"unto the going down of the same the LORD's name is to be praised" → "to its setting, praised is the name of Yahweh"** (v3) — Hebrew מְבוֹאוֹ "its setting", literally "its coming in" (as Ps 50:1). מְהֻלָּל is a participle, "praised" (as Ps 48:1); "is to be" is not written: it may also be read "worthy of praise".
- **"The LORD is high above all nations, and his glory above the heavens" → "High above all nations is Yahweh, above the heavens his glory"** (v4) — Hebrew רָם עַל־כָּל־גּוֹיִם יְהוָה עַל הַשָּׁמַיִם כְּבוֹדוֹ, in the Hebrew order; the second line has no verb and no "and".
- **"who dwelleth on high, who humbleth himself to behold the things that are in heaven, and in the earth" → "who sits on high, who stoops to see in the heavens and in the earth"** (v5–6) — Hebrew הַמַּגְבִּיהִי לָשָׁבֶת הַמַּשְׁפִּילִי לִרְאוֹת, literally "who makes high to sit … who makes low to see": the hifil with the infinitive says how he does it, "sits high … looks low". יָשַׁב "sit" (enthroned; the row's "dwell" is used of v9). "the things that are" is not written: בַּשָּׁמַיִם וּבָאָרֶץ "in the heavens and in the earth" may say where he looks or where he sits and stoops (the high and low of v5–6). The ‑ִי endings on the participles (also v7–9) are an old poetic form, not "my".
- **"He raiseth up the poor out of the dust, and lifteth the needy out of the dunghill" → "He raises up the poor from the dust; from the ash heap he raises the needy"** (v7) — Hebrew מְקִימִי … יָרִים, the wording of 1 Sam 2:8 (Hannah's prayer, the same Hebrew here): הֵקִים "raise up", הֵרִים "raise". אַשְׁפֹּת "ash heap" (the refuse heap outside a town; KJV "dunghill").
- **"That he may set him with princes" → "to make them sit with nobles"** (v8) — Hebrew לְהוֹשִׁיבִי, hifil of יָשַׁב, "to make sit"; the Hebrew has no object: "them" is supplied, as 1 Sam 2:8. נְדִיבִים "nobles" (literally "willing ones"; the 1 Sam 2 row).
- **"He maketh the barren woman to keep house, and to be a joyful mother of children" → "He makes the barren woman of the house dwell, the mother of the sons, glad"** (v9) — Hebrew מוֹשִׁיבִי עֲקֶרֶת הַבַּיִת אֵם־הַבָּנִים שְׂמֵחָה: מוֹשִׁיבִי, the verb of v8 (יָשַׁב hifil), "makes dwell" (settles her); עֲקֶרֶת הַבַּיִת "the barren woman of the house" ("woman" is in the feminine form). אֵם־הַבָּנִים "the mother of the sons" (בָּנִים "sons", with the article, not "children"), then שְׂמֵחָה "glad"; "and to be" is not written.

### Notes
- **Opens the Egyptian Hallel** (Pss 113–118) — sung at Passover.
- **Hebrew numbering** — the same as ours (the Hebrew has no heading; the opening Halleluyah is part of v1).
- **v7–8 = 1 Sam 2:8a** — the same words in Hannah's prayer; Mary's song (Luke 1:46–55) takes up the reversal of the low and the high.
