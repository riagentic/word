# Psalm 120

A song of the ascents.

[1] To ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, in my distress,
I called, and he answered me.

[2] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, deliver my soul from a lip of falsehood,
from a tongue of treachery.

[3] What will he give to you,
and what will he add to you, tongue of treachery?

[4] Arrows of a mighty one, sharpened,
with coals of broom trees.

[5] Woe to me, that I have sojourned in Meshech,
that I have abided with the tents of ⟦kedar|Qedar|Kedar⟧.

[6] Long has my soul abided
with one who hates ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧.

[7] I am ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧, and when I speak,
they are for war.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 2) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"A Song of degrees" → "A song of the ascents"** (heading) — Hebrew שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת, the first words of v1, with the article: "a song of the ascents" (מַעֲלָה "going up, step"). The same heading stands over Ps 120–134 (Ps 121 has לַמַּעֲלוֹת "for the ascents"). What "the ascents" are is not said: the going up to Jerusalem (Ezra 7:9 has the word of the return), or the steps of the temple; "degrees" (KJV) is the old word for "steps".
- **"In my distress I cried unto the LORD, and he heard me" → "To Yahweh, in my distress, I called, and he answered me"** (v1) — Hebrew אֶל־יְהוָה בַּצָּרָתָה לִּי קָרָאתִי וַיַּעֲנֵנִי: "to Yahweh" stands first; בַּצָּרָתָה לִּי, literally "in the distress to me" (צָרָה "distress"); קָרָא "call", עָנָה "answer". The perfect and the narrative form are past; some take them as what he has always found (from memory).
- **"Deliver my soul, O LORD, from lying lips, and from a deceitful tongue" → "Yahweh, deliver my soul from a lip of falsehood, from a tongue of treachery"** (v2) — Hebrew הַצִּילָה נַפְשִׁי "deliver my soul" (נֶפֶשׁ "soul"; dropped in the former wording); מִשְּׂפַת־שֶׁקֶר "from a lip of falsehood" (שָׂפָה "lip", singular; שֶׁקֶר "falsehood"); מִלָּשׁוֹן רְמִיָּה "from a tongue of treachery" (רְמִיָּה "treachery", as Ps 52:2). No "O": the Hebrew vocative has none.
- **"What shall be given unto thee? or what shall be done unto thee, thou false tongue?" → "What will he give to you, and what will he add to you, tongue of treachery?"** (v3) — Hebrew מַה־יִּתֵּן לְךָ וּמַה־יֹּסִיף לָךְ, two active verbs: "he will give", "he will add" (יָסַף "add"); the subject is not named (Yahweh, or "one"; the curse formula of 1 Sam 3:17, כֹּה יַעֲשֶׂה־לְּךָ אֱלֹהִים וְכֹה יוֹסִיף, has the same "add"). The tongue is addressed. Layout: the Hebrew verse has two lines, not three.
- **"Sharp arrows of the mighty, with coals of juniper" → "Arrows of a mighty one, sharpened, with coals of broom trees"** (v4) — Hebrew חִצֵּי גִבּוֹר שְׁנוּנִים: גִּבּוֹר "mighty one" (singular, no article), שָׁנוּן "sharpened"; גַּחֲלֵי רְתָמִים "coals of broom trees" (רֹתֶם, the broom of 1 Kgs 19:4, whose coals burn long; not "juniper"). Whether this is the answer to v3 (what the tongue will receive) or a picture of the tongue itself is not said.
- **"Woe is me, that I sojourn in Mesech, that I dwell in the tents of Kedar!" → "Woe to me, that I have sojourned in Meshech, that I have abided with the tents of Kedar"** (v5) — Hebrew אוֹיָה־לִי, a longer form of אוֹי "woe"; כִּי־גַרְתִּי מֶשֶׁךְ "that I have sojourned in Meshech": גּוּר "sojourn" (live as a stranger), a perfect; "in" is supplied (the Hebrew has the place as a bare object). שָׁכַנְתִּי עִם־אָהֳלֵי קֵדָר: שָׁכַן "abide", עִם "with". "that" stands only once in the Hebrew. Meshech lies far to the north (Gen 10:2; Ezek 38:2), Kedar in the Arabian desert (Gen 25:13; Isa 21:16): two far-apart peoples, so the names are taken as pictures of a hostile neighborhood (from memory).
- **"My soul hath long dwelt with him that hateth peace" → "Long has my soul abided with one who hates shalom"** (v6) — Hebrew רַבַּת שָׁכְנָה־לָּהּ נַפְשִׁי: רַבַּת "much, long" stands first; שָׁכַן "abide" as v5; לָּהּ "for itself" (of the soul) cannot be carried into English. נֶפֶשׁ "soul" (dropped in the former wording "I had my dwelling"). שׂוֹנֵא is singular, "one who hates" (the former "those who" made it plural). שָׁלוֹם *shalom*.
- **"I am for peace: but when I speak, they are for war" → "I am shalom, and when I speak, they are for war"** (v7) — Hebrew אֲנִי־שָׁלוֹם "I am *shalom*": no "for" is written (a man made all of peace); הֵמָּה לַמִּלְחָמָה "they are for war" (לְ). וְכִי "and when"; "but" is not marked.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — the same as ours: the heading is the first words of v1.
- **First of the fifteen songs of the ascents** (Ps 120–134).
