# Psalm 49

To the overseer. Of the sons of ⟦korah|Qorach|Korah⟧. A psalm.

[1] Hear this, all the peoples!
Give ear, all dwellers of the world,

[2] both sons of man and sons of man,
together, rich and needy!

[3] My mouth shall speak wisdom,
and the musing of my heart is understanding.

[4] I will incline my ear to a parable;
I will open my riddle with the lyre.

[5] Why should I fear in days of evil,
when the iniquity of my heels surrounds me,

[6] those who trust in their wealth
and glory in the multitude of their riches?

[7] No man can surely ransom a brother;
he cannot give to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ his ransom,

[8] and the ransom price of their soul is costly,
and it ceases forever,

[9] that he should live on forever
and not see the pit.

[10] For he sees: the wise die;
together the fool and the brute perish
and leave their wealth to others.

[11] Their inward part: their houses are forever,
their dwellings to generation and generation;
they have called by their names upon lands.

[12] And man in honor does not lodge;
he is likened to the beasts; they are cut off.

[13] This is their way: folly is theirs;
and after them they are pleased with their mouth. Selah.

[14] Like the flock, to Sheol they are set;
death shepherds them,
and the upright have dominion over them in the morning;
and their form is for Sheol to wear away,
far from a lofty dwelling for him.

[15] But ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will ransom my soul from the hand of Sheol,
for he will take me. Selah.

[16] Do not fear when a man grows rich,
when the glory of his house increases.

[17] For in his death he will take nothing at all;
his glory will not go down after him.

[18] For his soul he blesses in his life —
and they give thanks to you when you do good to yourself —

[19] it shall come to the generation of his fathers;
forever they will not see light.

[20] Man in honor, and he does not understand,
is likened to the beasts; they are cut off.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v7, 15) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"grave" → "Sheol"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew שְׁאוֹל, the plain word.
- **"To the chief Musician, A Psalm for the sons of Korah" → "To the overseer. Of the sons of Korah. A psalm."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ לִבְנֵי־קֹרַח מִזְמוֹר, in this order. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer", from the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21 (traditional "to the chief musician"). לִבְנֵי־קֹרַח: the לְ may mean "by", "for" or "belonging to" the sons of Korah; "of" leaves it open.
- **"all ye inhabitants of the world" → "all dwellers of the world"** (v1) — Hebrew כָּל־יֹשְׁבֵי חָלֶד; הַאֲזִינוּ "give ear"; חָלֶד "world", literally "duration, lifetime": the world of passing time (תֵּבֵל is the other "world", 50:12).
- **"Both low and high, rich and poor, together" → "both sons of man and sons of man, together, rich and needy"** (v2) — Hebrew גַּם־בְּנֵי אָדָם גַּם־בְּנֵי־אִישׁ: English has one word for both (reviewer row); the first is בְּנֵי אָדָם "sons of mankind", the second בְּנֵי־אִישׁ "sons of a man". "Low and high" is the sense commonly drawn from the pair (the common sort and men of standing); יַחַד "together" stands before "rich and needy"; אֶבְיוֹן "needy".
- **"shall speak of wisdom; and the meditation of my heart shall be of understanding" → "shall speak wisdom, and the musing of my heart is understanding"** (v3) — Hebrew חָכְמוֹת and תְבוּנוֹת are plurals (of fullness); no "of". הָגוּת only here, the noun of הָגָה "softly recite" (Ps 1:2): what the heart says over to itself. The second line has no verb.
- **"I will open my dark saying upon the harp" → "I will open my riddle with the lyre"** (v4) — Hebrew אֶפְתַּח בְּכִנּוֹר חִידָתִי; מָשָׁל "parable". **חִידָה is translated as printed, "riddle"**: whether a riddle to be solved, an obscure saying, or a hard question (the question of v5) is meant is not certain. כִּנּוֹר "lyre".
- **"when the iniquity of my heels shall compass me about" → "when the iniquity of my heels surrounds me"** (v5) — Hebrew עֲוֺן עֲקֵבַי יְסוּבֵּנִי, as pointed "my heels" (the iniquity that dogs my steps, or my own missteps); pointed עֹקְבַי it would be "those who supplant me" (the earlier wording "those who cheat me"): the MT is kept. "When" is supplied: the line has no conjunction. בִּימֵי רָע "in days of evil", no article.
- **"boast themselves in the multitude of their riches" → "glory in the multitude of their riches"** (v6) — Hebrew וּבְרֹב עָשְׁרָם יִתְהַלָּלוּ; הִתְהַלֵּל "glory"; חַיִל "wealth".
- **"None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him" → "No man can surely ransom a brother; he cannot give to Elohim his ransom"** (v7) — Hebrew אָח לֹא־פָדֹה יִפְדֶּה אִישׁ, literally "a brother, ransoming he will not ransom, a man": the infinitive absolute gives "surely"; "a brother" stands first, and the line may also be read "a brother cannot ransom, nor a man". Many read אַךְ "surely" for אָח (from memory): the MT is kept. כָּפְרוֹ "his ransom" (כֹּפֶר, the price that covers a life); פָּדָה "ransom" is a verb of another root (one English word: note).
- **"For the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceaseth for ever" → "and the ransom price of their soul is costly, and it ceases forever"** (v8) — Hebrew וְיֵקַר פִּדְיוֹן נַפְשָׁם וְחָדַל לְעוֹלָם: both clauses have "and" (KJV's parenthesis is not in the Hebrew); פִּדְיוֹן is the noun of פָּדָה. Who or what "ceases" is not said: the price fails, or one gives it up, or he ceases to live.
- **"That he should still live for ever, and not see corruption" → "that he should live on forever and not see the pit"** (v9) — Hebrew וִיחִי־עוֹד לָנֶצַח לֹא יִרְאֶה הַשָּׁחַת: וִיחִי is a short form with "and", read as purpose ("that he should live"); עוֹד "still, on"; לָנֶצַח "forever" (another word than לְעוֹלָם of v8); שַׁחַת "pit".
- **"For he seeth that wise men die, likewise the fool and the brutish person perish" → "For he sees: the wise die; together the fool and the brute perish"** (v10) — Hebrew כִּי יִרְאֶה חֲכָמִים יָמוּתוּ יַחַד כְּסִיל וָבַעַר יֹאבֵדוּ: no "that", יַחַד "together"; the subject of "sees" is not named (the man of v9, or anyone).
- **"Their inward thought is, that their houses shall continue for ever … they call their lands after their own names" → "Their inward part: their houses are forever, their dwellings to generation and generation; they have called by their names upon lands"** (v11) — Hebrew קִרְבָּם בָּתֵּימוֹ לְעוֹלָם, word for word "their inward part, their houses forever" (MT). The Septuagint and Syriac read קִבְרָם "their grave: their houses forever" (from memory), which the earlier wording "Their graves are their homes" followed: the MT is restored. לְדֹר וָדֹר "to generation and generation"; קָרְאוּ בִשְׁמוֹתָם עֲלֵי אֲדָמוֹת, perfect, "they have called with their names upon lands" (אֲדָמָה "ground", plural): their names were called over estates.
- **"Nevertheless man being in honour abideth not: he is like the beasts that perish" → "And man in honor does not lodge; he is likened to the beasts; they are cut off"** (v12) — Hebrew וְאָדָם בִּיקָר בַּל־יָלִין: "And", not "nevertheless"; יְקָר "honor"; לִין "lodge" (spend the night: he does not stay even a night). נִמְשַׁל, nifal of מָשַׁל (the root of מָשָׁל "parable", v4), "is likened"; נִדְמוּ is a second verb, "they are cut off" (from דָּמָה "be silenced, destroyed"; the same letters give "be like": a play on the words, uncertain). No "that perish": אָבַד stands in v10.
- **"This their way is their folly: yet their posterity approve their sayings" → "This is their way: folly is theirs; and after them they are pleased with their mouth"** (v13) — Hebrew זֶה דַרְכָּם כֵּסֶל לָמוֹ. **כֶּסֶל "folly" is kept against the word list ("confidence", Job 8:14; 31:24)**: the word has both senses, and "folly to them" fits the line; "the way of those who have confidence" (the trust of v6) is the other reading. וְאַחֲרֵיהֶם בְּפִיהֶם יִרְצוּ, literally "and after them, in their mouth they take pleasure": those who come after approve what they said; no "people" in the Hebrew.
- **"Like sheep they are laid in the grave; death shall feed on them … and their beauty shall consume in the grave from their dwelling" → "Like the flock, to Sheol they are set; death shepherds them … and their form is for Sheol to wear away, far from a lofty dwelling for him"** (v14) — Hebrew כַּצֹּאן לִשְׁאוֹל שַׁתּוּ: צֹאן "flock"; שַׁתּוּ "they are set" (as from שִׁית "set"; the form is hard). מָוֶת יִרְעֵם, רָעָה "shepherd" (KJV "feed on"). וַיִּרְדּוּ בָם יְשָׁרִים לַבֹּקֶר, רָדָה "have dominion" (a wayyiqtol: the tense is open; "go down" from יָרַד is another reading, from memory). Qere וְצוּרָם "and their form" (ketiv וצירם, the same sense or "their image"); לְבַלּוֹת שְׁאוֹל, "for Sheol to wear (it) away"; מִזְּבֻל לוֹ, "from a lofty dwelling for him" (זְבֻל "lofty dwelling", as 1 Kgs 8:13): far from any dwelling of his own. The verse is among the hardest of the Psalter.
- **"But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me" → "But Elohim will ransom my soul from the hand of Sheol, for he will take me"** (v15) — Hebrew אַךְ "but, only" (also "surely"); פָּדָה "ransom" as v7; מִיַּד "from the hand of"; יִקָּחֵנִי, לָקַח "take" (the verb of Gen 5:24 of Enoch and 2 Kgs 2:3 of Elijah).
- **"Be not thou afraid when one is made rich" → "Do not fear when a man grows rich"** (v16) — Hebrew אַל־תִּירָא כִּי־יַעֲשִׁר אִישׁ, qal "grow rich"; אִישׁ "a man".
- **"he shall carry nothing away" → "he will take nothing at all"** (v17) — Hebrew לֹא … יִקַּח הַכֹּל, literally "he will not take the whole": not anything; לָקַח "take" as v15.
- **"Though while he lived he blessed his soul: and men will praise thee, when thou doest well to thyself" → "For his soul he blesses in his life — and they give thanks to you when you do good to yourself —"** (v18) — Hebrew כִּי־נַפְשׁוֹ בְּחַיָּיו יְבָרֵךְ: כִּי "for" (or "though"); imperfect. וְיוֹדֻךָ, הוֹדָה "give thanks to" (= praise); no "men"; the address turns to "you", as in the Hebrew; הֵיטִיב לְ "do good to".
- **"He shall go to the generation of his fathers; they shall never see light" → "it shall come to the generation of his fathers; forever they will not see light"** (v19) — Hebrew תָּבוֹא, feminine "it shall come" (the soul of v18) or "you shall come" (the address of v18): open; the earlier wording supplied "his soul". עַד־נֵצַח לֹא יִרְאוּ־אוֹר, "to perpetuity they will not see light"; "again" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"Man that is in honour, and understandeth not" → "Man in honor, and he does not understand"** (v20) — Hebrew אָדָם בִּיקָר וְלֹא יָבִין: the refrain of v12 with וְלֹא יָבִין "and he does not understand" for בַּל־יָלִין "does not lodge", and no "and" at the head.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1–20 = Heb v2–21.
- **Wisdom psalm** — meditation on wealth and death.
- **"*Elohim* will ransom my soul … for he will take me"** (v15) — hope beyond death; seed of resurrection faith.
