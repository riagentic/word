# Psalm 20

To the overseer. A psalm of David.

[1] May ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ answer you in the day of distress!
May the name of the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ set you on high!

[2] May he send your help from the holy place,
and from ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧ may he sustain you!

[3] May he remember all your grain offerings,
and your burnt offering may he regard as fat! Selah.

[4] May he give you according to your heart,
and all your counsel may he fulfill!

[5] Let us shout for joy in your salvation,
and in the name of our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ let us raise a standard!
May ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ fulfill all your requests!

[6] Now I know that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ has saved his ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧;
he will answer him from his holy heavens
with the mighty deeds of the salvation of his right hand.

[7] Some of chariots and some of horses,
but we make mention of the name of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[8] They have bowed and fallen,
but we have risen and stood upright.

[9] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, save!
May the king answer us in the day we call.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 5, 6, 7, 9) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 5, 7) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David" → "To the overseer. A psalm of David."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד, as Ps 19; לְ "of" (by, for or belonging to: not decided).
- **Wishes** (v1–5) — the verbs are jussives ("may he …") and, in v5a, cohortatives ("let us …"); they stay wishes.
- **"in the day of trouble" → "in the day of distress"** (v1) — Hebrew צָרָה. **"defend thee" → "set you on high"** — Hebrew יְשַׂגֶּבְךָ, the verb of מִשְׂגָּב "secure height": lift out of reach.
- **"Send thee help from the sanctuary, and strengthen thee out of Zion" → "send your help from the holy place … sustain you"** (v2) — Hebrew עֶזְרְךָ "your help"; מִקֹּדֶשׁ, literally "from holiness" (the holy place; מִקְדָּשׁ "sanctuary" is another word); יִסְעָדֶךָּ "sustain".
- **"all thy offerings … accept thy burnt sacrifice" → "all your grain offerings … your burnt offering may he regard as fat"** (v3) — Hebrew מִנְחֹתֶיךָ (מִנְחָה beside עוֹלָה: the grain offering); יְדַשְּׁנֶה, piel of דָּשֵׁן "be fat": "find fat, accept as rich".
- **"Grant thee according to thine own heart, and fulfil all thy counsel" → "give you according to your heart, and all your counsel may he fulfill"** (v4) — Hebrew יִתֶּן; כִלְבָבֶךָ; עֲצָתְךָ "your counsel".
- **"We will rejoice in thy salvation, and … we will set up our banners" → "Let us shout for joy in your salvation … let us raise a standard"** (v5) — Hebrew נְרַנְּנָה, נִדְגֹּל, cohortatives. נִדְגֹּל is from דֶּגֶל "standard"; the verb is rare and its sense uncertain ("our" is not written; the Septuagint has "we shall be magnified", from memory). **"petitions" → "requests"** — Hebrew מִשְׁאֲלוֹתֶיךָ.
- **"saveth his anointed" → "has saved his mashiach (anointed)"** (v6) — Hebrew הוֹשִׁיעַ, a perfect: the rescue is spoken of as done. **"from his holy heaven" → "from his holy heavens"** — Hebrew מִשְּׁמֵי קָדְשׁוֹ, literally "from the heavens of his holiness". **"with the saving strength of his right hand" → "with the mighty deeds of the salvation of his right hand"** — Hebrew בִּגְבֻרוֹת יֵשַׁע יְמִינוֹ, plural "mights"; "deeds" is supplied.
- **"Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name" → "Some of chariots and some of horses, but we make mention of the name"** (v7) — Hebrew אֵלֶּה בָרֶכֶב וְאֵלֶּה בַסּוּסִים, literally "these in chariots and these in horses": the first line has no verb ("trust" is supplied in KJV); the one verb, נַזְכִּיר (הִזְכִּיר בְּשֵׁם "make mention of the name", as Josh 23:7), stands at the end.
- **"They are brought down and fallen: but we are risen, and stand upright" → "They have bowed and fallen, but we have risen and stood upright"** (v8) — Hebrew כָּרְעוּ "they bowed (down)"; קַמְנוּ וַנִּתְעוֹדָד, perfect and narrative form; הִתְעוֹדֵד "stand upright, be restored". **"but" for וְ** (v7, 8) — the וְ comes before the pronoun "we", set against "they".
- **"Save, LORD: let the king hear us when we call" → "Yahweh, save! May the king answer us in the day we call"** (v9) — the MT accents divide after הוֹשִׁיעָה: "Yahweh, save; the king — may he answer us". The Septuagint joins "the king" to "save" ("save the king, and answer us", from memory), and so did the earlier wording here; the MT division is kept. "The king" may be the earthly king or Yahweh. יַעֲנֵנוּ "answer" (v1, 6); בְיוֹם־קָרְאֵנוּ "in the day of our calling".

### Notes
- **Numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1 = Heb v2, and so on to v9 = Heb v10.
- **Royal blessing before battle** — congregation prays for the king.
- **"Chariots and horses vs name of Yahweh"** — famous contrast.
