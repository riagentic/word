# Psalm 110

Of David. A psalm.

[1] Declaration of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ to my lord: "Sit at my right hand,
until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet."

[2] The staff of your strength ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will send out from ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧:
have dominion in the midst of your enemies.

[3] Your people are freewill offerings in the day of your force;
in the splendors of holiness, from the womb of the dawn,
to you is the dew of your youth.

[4] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ has sworn and will not be sorry:
"You are a priest forever
according to the manner of ⟦melchizedek|Malki-Tzedeq|Melchizedek⟧."

[5] ⟦adonai|Adonai|The Lord⟧ at your right hand
has struck through kings in the day of his anger.

[6] He will judge among the nations, full of corpses;
he has struck through the head over a great land.

[7] From a wadi on the way he will drink;
therefore he will lift up the head.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 2, 4) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"Lord" → "Adonai"** (v5) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי with the divine pointing (unlike לַאדֹנִי in v1).
- **Heading "A Psalm of David" → "Of David. A psalm."** — Hebrew לְדָוִד מִזְמוֹר, in this order. לְדָוִד: the לְ may mean "by", "for" or "belonging to" David; "Of David" leaves it open.
- **"The LORD said unto my Lord" → "Declaration of Yahweh to my lord"** (v1) — Hebrew נְאֻם יְהוָה לַאדֹנִי: נְאֻם is the noun "declaration" (the formula of the prophets, "declares Yahweh"), not the verb "said". לַאדֹנִי is pointed אֲדֹנִי "my lord", the address to a human lord (a king), not אֲדֹנָי, the title of God (v5): hence "lord" with a small letter. The Greek, quoted in Matt 22:44; Mark 12:36; Luke 20:42; Acts 2:34–35; Heb 1:13, has Εἶπεν ὁ κύριος τῷ κυρίῳ μου, "The Lord said to my lord", where one Greek word stands for both Hebrew words; the verse keeps the Hebrew. **"Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool" → "Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool for your feet"** — Hebrew שֵׁב "sit"; אָשִׁית, שִׁית "set" ("as" is supplied); הֲדֹם לְרַגְלֶיךָ "a footstool for your feet". Two lines, as the accents divide the verse (after the heading).
- **"The LORD shall send the rod of thy strength out of Zion: rule thou in the midst of thine enemies" → "The staff of your strength Yahweh will send out from Zion: have dominion in the midst of your enemies"** (v2) — Hebrew מַטֵּה־עֻזְּךָ first; מַטֶּה "staff" (שֵׁבֶט is "rod"); עֹז "strength"; יִשְׁלַח "will send (out)". רְדֵה, imperative of רָדָה "have dominion" (as Gen 1:26, 28). Two lines, as the accents (the earlier text had three).
- **"Thy people shall be willing in the day of thy power, in the beauties of holiness from the womb of the morning: thou hast the dew of thy youth" → "Your people are freewill offerings in the day of your force; in the splendors of holiness, from the womb of the dawn, to you is the dew of your youth"** (v3) — a hard verse; the MT translated as it stands, and its meaning is uncertain throughout. עַמְּךָ נְדָבֹת, without a verb: "your people (are) freewill offerings" (נְדָבָה "freewill offering", plural; = they offer themselves willingly). בְּיוֹם חֵילֶךָ, חַיִל "force" (of an army; also "ability, wealth"): "the day of your force" may be the day the king musters his army. בְּהַדְרֵי־קֹדֶשׁ "in the splendors of holiness" (הָדָר "splendor"); many Hebrew manuscripts and Jerome read בְּהַרְרֵי־קֹדֶשׁ "on the mountains of holiness" (one letter, ד / ר; from memory). מֵרֶחֶם מִשְׁחָר "from the womb of the dawn": מִשְׁחָר is found only here (from שַׁחַר "dawn"). לְךָ טַל יַלְדֻתֶיךָ "to you (is) the dew of your youth": יַלְדוּת "youth, the time of being a child". The Greek reads "from the womb before the morning star I have begotten you" (יְלִדְתִּיךָ, the vowels of another word, as Ps 2:7; from memory); the verse keeps the MT. Which words go with which line is not certain: the lines follow the accents (three lines; the earlier text had five).
- **"The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek" → "Yahweh has sworn and will not be sorry: You are a priest forever according to the manner of Malki-Tzedeq"** (v4) — Hebrew נִשְׁבַּע "has sworn"; וְלֹא יִנָּחֵם, נִחַם nifal "be sorry" (as 1 Sam 15:29; not "repent", not "change his mind"). כֹּהֵן "priest"; לְעוֹלָם "forever". עַל־דִּבְרָתִי: דִּבְרָה "matter, cause, manner" with an old connecting vowel -ִי (it can also be read "my": "on my account, Malki-Tzedeq", or "on account of"); "according to the manner of" is the best-supported sense. מַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק is the name of Gen 14:18; its letters also say "my king of righteousness" ("king of righteousness", Heb 7:2). Heb 5:6, 10; 6:20; 7:17, 21 quote the Greek, κατὰ τὴν τάξιν Μελχισέδεκ, "according to the order of Melchizedek"; the verse keeps the Hebrew.
- **"The Lord at thy right hand shall strike through kings in the day of his wrath" → "Adonai at your right hand has struck through kings in the day of his anger"** (v5) — Hebrew מָחַץ, a perfect, "has struck through" (מָחַץ "strike through", as Num 24:8, 17); a perfect of certainty in prophecy is possible, the past form is kept. אַפּוֹ "his anger" (אַף, literally "nose").
- **"He shall judge among the heathen, he shall fill the places with the dead bodies; he shall wound the heads over many countries" → "He will judge among the nations, full of corpses; he has struck through the head over a great land"** (v6) — Hebrew יָדִין "he will judge" (דִּין, beside שָׁפַט: English has one word); בַּגּוֹיִם "among the nations". מָלֵא גְוִיּוֹת "full of corpses": מָלֵא is the adjective or the qal perfect ("it is full", "he has filled"); "the places" is not in the Hebrew, and what is full is not said. מָחַץ, a perfect, "has struck through" (as v5); רֹאשׁ singular, without the article ("head": a ruler, or heads; "the" is supplied for English, as v7); עַל־אֶרֶץ רַבָּה "over a great (wide) land" or "over the wide earth". Two lines, as the accents (the earlier text had three).
- **"He shall drink of the brook in the way: therefore shall he lift up the head" → "From a wadi on the way he will drink; therefore he will lift up the head"** (v7) — Hebrew מִנַּחַל בַּדֶּרֶךְ, נַחַל "wadi" (a stream bed, here with water), no article; יָרִים רֹאשׁ "he will lift up (the) head": רֹאשׁ as in v6, without the article or "his". Who drinks (the king, or Adonai of v5) is not named.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — the same as ours: the heading is the first words of v1.
- **Poetic structure** — two sayings of Yahweh (v1, v4), each followed by what is promised (v2–3, v5–7).
- **Most-quoted OT verse in NT** — v1 cited Matt 22:44, Mark 12:36, Luke 20:42, Acts 2:34, Heb 1:13, etc.
- **Priest-king** — the Malki-Tzedeq theme is central to Hebrews 5–7.
