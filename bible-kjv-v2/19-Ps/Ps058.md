# Psalm 58

To the chief musician. Al-tashcheth. A Miktam of David.

[1] Do you indeed speak righteousness, O elim — mighty ones?
Do you judge the children of men uprightly?

[2] No, in your hearts you devise wrongs;
your hands deal out violence on the earth.

[3] The wicked are estranged from the womb;
they go astray from birth, speaking lies.

[4] They have venom like the venom of a serpent,
like the deaf adder that stops its ear,

[5] so that it does not hear the voice of charmers
or of the cunning enchanter.

[6] O *Elohim*, break the teeth in their mouths;
tear out the fangs of the young lions, O Yahweh!

[7] Let them vanish like water that runs away;
when he aims his arrows, let them be blunted.

[8] Let them be like the snail that dissolves into slime,
like the stillborn child who never sees the sun.

[9] Sooner than your pots can feel the heat of thorns —
whether green or ablaze — may he sweep them away!

[10] The righteous will rejoice when he sees the vengeance;
he will bathe his feet in the blood of the wicked.

[11] Mankind will say, "Surely there is a reward for the righteous;
surely there is an *Elohim* who judges on earth."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v6, 11) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v6) — Tetragrammaton.
- **Miktam** — retained in superscription.
- **Al-tashcheth** — retained in superscription.
- **Poetic structure** — denunciation of corrupt rulers (v1-5), imprecatory curses (v6-9), vindication conclusion (v10-11).

### Notes
- **Imprecatory psalm** against corrupt judges ("elim" = gods? or earthly rulers?).
- **Vivid curses** — snails melting, stillborn.
