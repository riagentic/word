# Psalm 65

To the overseer. A psalm of David, a song.

[1] For you silence is praise, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, in ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧,
and to you a vow shall be repaid.

[2] You who hear prayer,
to you all flesh shall come.

[3] Matters of iniquities have prevailed over me;
our transgressions — you atone for them.

[4] Happy is the one you choose and bring near:
he shall abide in your courts.
We shall be sated with the goodness of your house,
the holy place of your temple.

[5] With fearsome things in righteousness you answer us,
⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of our salvation,
the trust of all the ends of the earth
and of the sea, of those far off;

[6] who establishes the mountains by his power,
girded with might;

[7] who stills the roar of the seas,
the roar of their waves,
and the tumult of the folks.

[8] And those who dwell at the ends fear because of your signs;
the goings out of morning and evening you make to shout for joy.

[9] You have visited the earth and made it overflow;
greatly you enrich it;
the channel of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is full of water;
you prepare their grain,
for so you prepare it.

[10] Drenching its furrows,
settling its ridges,
with showers you soften it;
its sprouting you bless.

[11] You have crowned the year of your goodness,
and your courses drip fatness.

[12] The pastures of the wilderness drip,
and the hills gird themselves with gladness.

[13] The meadows have put on the flock,
and the valleys wrap themselves in grain;
they shout in triumph; yes, they sing.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 5, 9) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"To the chief Musician, A Psalm and Song of David" → "To the overseer. A psalm of David, a song."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד שִׁיר, in this order: שִׁיר "a song" comes after "of David". לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer", from the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21 (traditional "to the chief musician"). לְדָוִד: the לְ may mean "by", "for" or "belonging to" David; "of" leaves it open.
- **No "O"** (v1, 2, 5) — the Hebrew vocatives have none.
- **"Praise waiteth for thee, O God, in Sion" → "For you silence is praise, Elohim, in Zion"** (v1) — Hebrew לְךָ דֻמִיָּה תְהִלָּה, as printed: דֻּמִיָּה is the noun "silence" (as Ps 22:2; 39:2; 62:1), set beside תְּהִלָּה "praise" without a verb; the sense is open ("to you silence is praise", "to you belong silence and praise", "silence before you, praise"). Uncertain: the Greek ("praise is fitting for you", so the earlier text "Praise is due to you") reads another word, דֹּמִיָּה from דָּמָה "be like" (from memory); the verse keeps the printed text. **"unto thee shall the vow be performed" → "to you a vow shall be repaid"** — Hebrew יְשֻׁלַּם־נֶדֶר, pual of שִׁלֵּם "repay" (as 61:8; Ps 22:25); נֶדֶר singular, no article.
- **"O thou that hearest prayer, unto thee shall all flesh come" → "You who hear prayer, to you all flesh shall come"** (v2) — Hebrew שֹׁמֵעַ תְּפִלָּה, a participle ("hearer of prayer"); עָדֶיךָ "to you, up to you".
- **"Iniquities prevail against me: as for our transgressions, thou shalt purge them away" → "Matters of iniquities have prevailed over me; our transgressions — you atone for them"** (v3) — Hebrew דִּבְרֵי עֲוֺנֹת "matters of iniquities" (דָּבָר, literally "words": no speech is meant); there is no "when" (so the earlier text). גָּבְרוּ מֶנִּי, גָּבַר "prevail", "they are stronger than I", a perfect. פְּשָׁעֵינוּ אַתָּה תְכַפְּרֵם, "our transgressions" first, "you" stressed; כִּפֶּר "atone, cover" (the "make atonement" of the cult; here God is the one who does it).
- **"Blessed is the man whom thou choosest, and causest to approach unto thee, that he may dwell in thy courts" → "Happy is the one you choose and bring near: he shall abide in your courts"** (v4) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵי "Happy" (not "Blessed": that is בָּרוּךְ; as Ps 1:1); "the one" is supplied for the relative without a noun. תְקָרֵב "bring near". יִשְׁכֹּן, שָׁכַן "abide"; חָצֵר "court". **"we shall be satisfied with the goodness of thy house, even of thy holy temple" → "We shall be sated with the goodness of your house, the holy place of your temple"** — Hebrew נִשְׂבְּעָה, a cohortative ("we shall", or "let us be sated"); שָׂבַע "be sated". קְדֹשׁ הֵיכָלֶךָ, "the holy (thing) of your temple": קָדוֹשׁ, an adjective, in the construct; "place" is supplied; it may also be read "holy is your temple".
- **"By terrible things in righteousness wilt thou answer us" → "With fearsome things in righteousness you answer us"** (v5) — Hebrew נוֹרָאוֹת "fearsome things" (נוֹרָא, not "awesome"); "deeds" is not in the Hebrew. **"who art the confidence of all the ends of the earth, and of them that are afar off upon the sea" → "the trust of all the ends of the earth and of the sea, of those far off"** — Hebrew מִבְטָח "trust" (what one trusts in; from בָּטַח), not "hope" (the earlier text); וְיָם רְחֹקִים, "and of the sea, far ones": רְחֹקִים is plural and יָם singular; it may mean "the far ones of the sea" or "the far-off seas".
- **"Which by his strength setteth fast the mountains; being girded with power" → "who establishes the mountains by his power, girded with might"** (v6) — Hebrew מֵכִין, participle of הֵכִין "establish" (present, not past); כֹּחַ "power"; נֶאְזָר "girded"; גְּבוּרָה "might" (as Ps 20:6). הֵכִין is "prepare" in v9: one verb, two senses.
- **"Which stilleth the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the people" → "who stills the roar of the seas, the roar of their waves, and the tumult of the folks"** (v7) — Hebrew מַשְׁבִּיחַ "stills"; שְׁאוֹן "roar", twice; הֲמוֹן "tumult" (the word is "multitude" elsewhere: here the noise of a multitude); לְאֻמִּים "folks" (לְאֹם, beside עַם "people").
- **"They also that dwell in the uttermost parts are afraid at thy tokens: thou makest the outgoings of the morning and evening to rejoice" → "And those who dwell at the ends fear because of your signs; the goings out of morning and evening you make to shout for joy"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיִּירְאוּ יֹשְׁבֵי קְצָוֺת: a narrative form (wayyiqtol) after the participles of v6–7, describing what is always so: given as a present. קְצָוֺת "ends" ("of the earth" is not in the Hebrew); מֵאוֹתֹתֶיךָ "from (because of) your signs"; מוֹצָא "going out" (of sunrise and sunset, or of the east and the west); תַּרְנִין, hifil of רָנַן "shout for joy".
- **"Thou visitest the earth, and waterest it: thou greatly enrichest it with the river of God, which is full of water" → "You have visited the earth and made it overflow; greatly you enrich it; the channel of Elohim is full of water"** (v9) — Hebrew פָּקַדְתָּ הָאָרֶץ, a perfect; פָּקַד "visit" (attend to). וַתְּשֹׁקְקֶהָ, polel of שׁוּק "make overflow". רַבַּת תַּעְשְׁרֶנָּה "greatly you enrich it"; "with" is not in the Hebrew: the channel is a clause of its own. פֶּלֶג "channel" (as Ps 1:3; not נָהָר "river"). **"thou preparest them corn, when thou hast so provided for it" → "you prepare their grain, for so you prepare it"** — Hebrew תָּכִין דְּגָנָם כִּי־כֵן תְּכִינֶהָ: "their grain" (the people's); דָּגָן "grain"; the same verb twice ("it" = the earth).
- **"Thou waterest the ridges thereof abundantly: thou settlest the furrows thereof" → "Drenching its furrows, settling its ridges"** (v10) — Hebrew תְּלָמֶיהָ רַוֵּה נַחֵת גְּדוּדֶיהָ: two infinitives absolute; תֶּלֶם "furrow"; גְּדוּד here "ridge" (another word of the same letters as the "band" of men). **"thou makest it soft with showers: thou blessest the springing thereof" → "with showers you soften it; its sprouting you bless"** — Hebrew רְבִיבִים "showers" (as Deut 32:2); צִמְחָהּ "its sprouting" (צֶמַח, from צָמַח "spring up").
- **"Thou crownest the year with thy goodness; and thy paths drop fatness" → "You have crowned the year of your goodness, and your courses drip fatness"** (v11) — Hebrew עִטַּרְתָּ שְׁנַת טוֹבָתֶךָ, a perfect, "the year of your goodness" (a construct; no "with"); מַעְגָּל "track" (the rut of a wheel); רָעַף "drip"; דֶּשֶׁן "fatness" (as 63:5).
- **"They drop upon the pastures of the wilderness: and the little hills rejoice on every side" → "The pastures of the wilderness drip, and the hills gird themselves with gladness"** (v12) — Hebrew יִרְעֲפוּ נְאוֹת מִדְבָּר, "the pastures of the wilderness drip" (the pastures are the subject); וְגִיל גְּבָעוֹת תַּחְגֹּרְנָה, "and gladness the hills gird on" (גִּיל "gladness"; חָגַר "gird").
- **"The pastures are clothed with flocks; the valleys also are covered over with corn; they shout for joy, they also sing" → "The meadows have put on the flock, and the valleys wrap themselves in grain; they shout in triumph; yes, they sing"** (v13) — Hebrew לָבְשׁוּ כָרִים הַצֹּאן, a perfect, "the meadows have put on the flock" (כַּר here "meadow", kept apart from נְאוֹת "pastures" of v12; the "lamb" of Deut 32:14 is the same letters); צֹאן "flock", with the article. יַעַטְפוּ־בָר, עָטַף "wrap", בָּר "grain" (another word than דָּגָן of v9). יִתְרוֹעֲעוּ, hitpolel of רוּעַ "shout (in triumph)"; אַף "yes, also"; "together" is not in the Hebrew.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1 = Heb v2, and so on to v13 = Heb v14.
- **Poetic structure** — praise in Zion (v1–4), the creator of mountains and seas (v5–8), the rain and the harvest (v9–13).
- **Creation/harvest psalm** — *Elohim*'s care for the land.
- **Classic thanksgiving.**
