# Song of Songs 6

[1] Where has your beloved gone,
most beautiful among women?
Where has your beloved turned,
that we may seek him with you?

[2] My beloved has gone down to his garden,
to the beds of spice,
to pasture in the gardens
and to gather lilies.

[3] I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine,
who pastures among the lilies.

[4] Beautiful are you, my companion, as ⟦tirzah|Tirtzah|Tirzah⟧,
lovely as ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧,
dreadful as those under standards.

[5] Turn your eyes away from before me,
for they have overwhelmed me.
Your hair is like the drove of goats
that have streamed down from ⟦gilead|Gilad|Gilead⟧.

[6] Your teeth are like the drove of ewes
that have come up from the washing,
that all of them bear twins,
and a bereaved one there is not among them.

[7] Like a slice of the pomegranate is your temple
behind your veil.

[8] Sixty are they, queens, and eighty concubines,
and young women without number.

[9] One is she, my dove, my flawless one;
one is she to her mother;
pure is she to the one who bore her.
Daughters saw her and called her happy,
queens and concubines, and they praised her.

[10] Who is this who looks down like the dawn,
beautiful as the moon,
pure as the sun,
dreadful as those under standards?

[11] To the garden of nuts I went down,
to look at the green shoots of the wadi,
to see whether the vine had budded,
whether the pomegranates had blossomed.

[12] I did not know;
my soul set me
among the chariots of my noble people.

[13] Return, return, ⟦shulammite|Shulammit|Shulammite⟧;
return, return, and let us behold you!

What will you behold in the ⟦shulammite|Shulammit|Shulammite⟧?
Like the dance of the two camps.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; v10 now has the four lines of the accents.
- **"O thou fairest among women" → "most beautiful among women"** (v1) — Hebrew הַיָּפָה בַּנָּשִׁים, lit. "the beautiful one among women" (as 5:9); no "O". **"whither is thy beloved turned aside" → "Where has your beloved turned"** — פָּנָה "turn". "that we may seek him with you": וְ + imperfect after the question.
- **"the beds of spices" → "the beds of spice"** (v2) — Hebrew הַבֹּשֶׂם, singular (as 5:13). **"to feed in the gardens" → "to pasture in the gardens"** (v2, 3) — Hebrew רָעָה, "shepherd, pasture"; whether he pastures a flock or grazes himself is not said (row רָעָה). לָקַט "gather".
- **"he feedeth among the lilies" → "who pastures among the lilies"** (v3) — Hebrew הָרֹעֶה, participle with the article. The order of 2:16 is reversed here ("I am my beloved's" first).
- **"Thou art beautiful, O my love, as Tirzah" → "Beautiful are you, my companion, as Tirzah"** (v4) — Hebrew order; רַעְיָתִי "my companion". Tirzah: a city, later the first royal city of the northern kingdom (1 Kgs 14:17; 16:23).
- **"terrible as an army with banners" → "dreadful as those under standards"** (v4, 10) — Hebrew אֲיֻמָּה כַּנִּדְגָּלוֹת: אָיֹם "dreadful" (Hab 1:7; root of אֵימָה "dread"); נִדְגָּלוֹת, a feminine plural participle from the root of דֶּגֶל "standard" (2:4; row): "those set under standards". "army" is not in the Hebrew; whether hosts, or sights in the sky (v10), are meant is not certain.
- **"for they have overcome me" → "for they have overwhelmed me"** (v5) — Hebrew הִרְהִיבֻנִי, hifil of רָהַב (perfect). The row for this verb (Ps 138:3; Song 6:5) has "make bold" (uncertain); here, after "Turn your eyes away", "make bold" would say the opposite of the plea, so "overwhelm" (lit. "storm at, press on") is kept against the row. מִנֶּגְדִּי "from before me" (מִנֶּגֶד "opposite").
- **"as a flock of goats that appear from Gilead" → "like the drove of goats that have streamed down from Gilead"** (v5–6) — עֵדֶר "drove" (row), with the article; גָּלַשׁ only here and 4:1: uncertain ("come down", "leap", "sit"); "slopes" is not in the Hebrew; הַגִּלְעָד has the article ("the Gilead"). v5b–7 repeat 4:1b–3 with small differences: 6:6 has הָרְחֵלִים "the ewes" for הַקְּצוּבוֹת "the shorn ones" of 4:2.
- **"whereof every one beareth twins, and there is not one barren among them" → "that all of them bear twins, and a bereaved one there is not among them"** (v6) — Hebrew מַתְאִימוֹת "bearing twins" (or "matching, paired": the teeth in pairs); שַׁכֻּלָה "a bereaved one" (row שָׁכֹל: one who has lost her young), in the Hebrew order.
- **"As a piece of a pomegranate are thy temples" → "Like a slice of the pomegranate is your temple"** (v7) — Hebrew רַקָּתֵךְ "your temple" (singular; the side of the head, as Judg 4:21; the previous "cheeks" interprets); פֶּלַח "slice" (row); "behind your veil": צַמָּה, a veil (or "locks": uncertain, Isa 47:2).
- **"There are threescore queens, and fourscore concubines, and virgins without number" → "Sixty are they, queens, and eighty concubines, and young women without number"** (v8) — Hebrew שִׁשִּׁים הֵמָּה מְלָכוֹת, with the pronoun הֵמָּה; עֲלָמוֹת "young women" (row עַלְמָה; "virgin" is בְּתוּלָה); אֵין מִסְפָּר, lit. "there is no number".
- **"My dove, my undefiled is but one" → "One is she, my dove, my flawless one"** (v9) — Hebrew order; תַּמָּתִי as 5:2. **"she is the choice one of her that bare her" → "pure is she to the one who bore her"** — בָּרָה "pure, clear" (the same word as v10 "pure as the sun"). **"The daughters saw her, and blessed her" → "Daughters saw her and called her happy"** — בָּנוֹת without the article; אִשֵּׁר "call happy" (row; "bless" is בֵּרַךְ).
- **"Who is she that looketh forth as the morning" → "Who is this who looks down like the dawn"** (v10) — Hebrew נִשְׁקָף "look down" (row), שַׁחַר "dawn". "the moon", "the sun": לְבָנָה and חַמָּה, the poetic words ("the white one", "the hot one"). **"clear as the sun" → "pure as the sun"** — בָּרָה, as v9.
- **"I went down into the garden of nuts" → "To the garden of nuts I went down"** (v11) — Hebrew order; the speaker's gender is not marked. **"to see the fruits of the valley" → "to look at the green shoots of the wadi"** — אֵב "fresh green" (Job 8:12), נַחַל "wadi" (row). **"whether the vine flourished" → "whether the vine had budded"** — פָּרַח of a plant "bud" (row); the vine is singular. **"the pomegranates budded" → "had blossomed"** — הֵנֵצוּ (נֵץ "blossom").
- **"Or ever I was aware, my soul made me like the chariots of Amminadib" → "I did not know; my soul set me among the chariots of my noble people"** (v12) — one of the most obscure verses of the book. Hebrew לֹא יָדַעְתִּי "I did not know"; נַפְשִׁי שָׂמַתְנִי "my soul set (put) me"; מַרְכְּבוֹת עַמִּי־נָדִיב has no preposition: "among" is supplied. עַמִּי־נָדִיב, joined by maqqef, is read "my people, a noble one" / "my noble people", or as a name, Amminadib (KJV); the verse is translated as the words stand, the sense open.
- **"O Shulamite" → "Shulammite"** (v13; Heb 7:1) — Hebrew הַשּׁוּלַמִּית with the article, a vocative: no "O". **"that we may look upon thee" → "and let us behold you"** — וְנֶחֱזֶה, cohortative; חָזָה "behold" (row), also in the next line.
- **"What will ye see in the Shulamite? As it were the company of two armies" → "What will you behold in the Shulammite? Like the dance of the two camps"** (v13) — מְחֹלָה "dance" (not "company"); הַמַּחֲנָיִם, dual with the article, "the two camps" (מַחֲנֶה "camp"); it is also the name of the place Mahanaim (Gen 32:2), and "the dance of Mahanaim" is possible: said here, the verse keeps the common noun. Who asks and who answers is not marked; תֶּחֱזוּ is masculine plural.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — v13 is Heb 7:1; Song 7:1–13 is Heb 7:2–14. Our numbers are kept.
- **Who speaks** — v1 a group to a woman (דּוֹדֵךְ "your", feminine); v2–3 the woman; v4–10 a man to the woman (feminine "you"); v11–12 unmarked; v13 a group ("let us behold you", feminine "you").
- **Shulammite** — הַשּׁוּלַמִּית is formed like a gentilic ("the woman of Shulam"); whether it names a place (Shunem has been suggested) or is a feminine of the name שְׁלֹמֹה (Solomon) is not certain.
