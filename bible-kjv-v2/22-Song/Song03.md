# Song of Songs 3

[1] On my bed by night
I sought him whom my soul loves;
I sought him, but found him not.

[2] I will rise now and go about the city,
in the streets and in the squares;
I will seek him whom my soul loves.
I sought him, but found him not.

[3] The watchmen found me
as they went about in the city.
"Have you seen him whom my soul loves?"

[4] Scarcely had I passed them
when I found him whom my soul loves.
I held him, and would not let him go
until I had brought him into my mother's house,
and into the chamber of her who conceived me.

[5] I adjure you, O daughters of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧,
by the gazelles or the does of the field,
that you not stir up or awaken love
until it pleases.

[6] What is that coming up from the wilderness
like columns of smoke,
perfumed with myrrh and frankincense,
with all the fragrant powders of a merchant?

[7] Behold, it is the litter of ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧!
Around it are sixty warriors,
of the warriors of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧,

[8] all of them armed with swords,
expert in war,
each with his sword at his thigh,
against terrors of the night.

[9] King ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧ made himself a carriage
from the wood of ⟦lebanon|Levanon|Lebanon⟧.

[10] He made its posts of silver,
its back of gold,
its seat of purple;
its interior was inlaid with love
by the daughters of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[11] Go out, O daughters of ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧,
and look upon King ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧,
with the crown with which his mother crowned him
on the day of his wedding,
on the day of the gladness of his heart.

## Changes

- **"bed" → "litter"** (v7) — Hebrew מִטָּתוֹ *mitato* in v7, but context (v7 "litter of Solomon" with sixty warriors) indicates a royal palanquin; "litter" used.
- **"chariot" → "carriage"** (v9) — Hebrew אַפִּרְיוֹן *apiryon*; a litter or sedan-chair (probably a loanword from Greek or Sanskrit *paryanka*); rendered "carriage" for readability.
- **"daughters of Jerusalem" / "daughters of Zion"** — both phrases kept as they appear (different Hebrew phrases, same referent: the chorus/chorus-women).
