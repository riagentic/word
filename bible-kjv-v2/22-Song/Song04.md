# Song of Songs 4

[1] Behold, you are beautiful, my companion,
behold, you are beautiful;
your eyes are doves behind your veil.
Your hair is like the drove of goats
that have streamed down from the mountain of ⟦gilead|Gilad|Gilead⟧.

[2] Your teeth are like the drove of shorn ones
that have come up from the washing,
that all of them bear twins,
and a bereaved one there is not among them.

[3] Like a thread of scarlet are your lips,
and your mouth is lovely.
Like a slice of the pomegranate is your temple
behind your veil.

[4] Like the tower of David is your neck,
built in courses;
a thousand shields hang upon it,
all the bucklers of the mighty ones.

[5] Your two breasts are like two fawns,
twins of a gazelle,
that pasture among the lilies.

[6] Until the day breathes
and the shadows flee,
I will get myself to the mountain of myrrh
and to the hill of frankincense.

[7] All of you is beautiful, my companion,
and there is no blemish in you.

[8] With me from ⟦lebanon|Levanon|Lebanon⟧, bride,
with me from ⟦lebanon|Levanon|Lebanon⟧ you will come.
You will watch from the top of ⟦amana|Amanah|Amana⟧,
from the top of Senir and ⟦hermon|Chermon|Hermon⟧,
from the dens of lions,
from the mountains of leopards.

[9] You have taken my heart, my sister, bride;
you have taken my heart with one of your eyes,
with one chain of your neck.

[10] How beautiful is your love, my sister, bride!
How much better is your love than wine,
and the smell of your oils than all spices!

[11] Your lips drip honey, bride;
honey and milk are under your tongue,
and the smell of your cloaks is like the smell of ⟦lebanon|Levanon|Lebanon⟧.

[12] A garden locked, my sister, bride,
a fountain locked, a spring sealed.

[13] Your shoots are a park of pomegranates
with fruit of choice things,
henna with nard,

[14] nard and saffron, cane and cinnamon,
with all trees of frankincense,
myrrh and aloes,
with all the chief spices;

[15] a spring of gardens, a well of living water,
and streams from ⟦lebanon|Levanon|Lebanon⟧.

[16] Awake, north,
and come, south!
Make my garden breathe,
let its spices flow.
Let my beloved come to his garden,
and eat the fruit of his choice things.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; v1, 16 keep the break inside the line where the accents mark a pause.
- **"Behold, thou art fair, my love" → "Behold, you are beautiful, my companion"** (v1, 7) — Hebrew הִנָּךְ יָפָה רַעְיָתִי (as 1:15). **"thou hast doves' eyes within thy locks" → "your eyes are doves behind your veil"** — Hebrew עֵינַיִךְ יוֹנִים מִבַּעַד לְצַמָּתֵךְ; מִבַּעַד "from behind"; צַמָּה (also v3; 6:7; Isa 47:2) is "veil" or "locks of hair": uncertain. English has one word "veil" also for צָעִיף (Gen 24:65), another Hebrew word.
- **"a flock of goats, that appear from mount Gilead" → "a drove of goats that have streamed down from the mountain of Gilead"** (v1) — Hebrew כְּעֵדֶר הָעִזִּים; עֵדֶר "drove" (also v2); the article on "the goats" is the kind (English "of goats"); גָּלְשׁוּ, only here and 6:5: "stream down, flow down" (the look of a flock coming down a slope), uncertain; מֵהַר "from the mountain".
- **"a flock of sheep that are even shorn" → "a drove of shorn ones"** (v2) — Hebrew הַקְּצוּבוֹת, feminine passive participle "the shorn (ones)": "sheep" is not written (6:6 has רְחֵלִים "ewes"). **"whereof every one bear twins, and none is barren among them" → "all of which bear twins, and none among them is bereaved"** — Hebrew מַתְאִימוֹת "bearing twins"; שַׁכֻּלָה "bereaved" (of young; שָׁכֹל "be bereaved"), not "barren".
- **"Thy lips are like a thread of scarlet" → "Like a thread of scarlet are your lips"** (v3) — Hebrew כְּחוּט הַשָּׁנִי שִׂפְתֹתַיִךְ, Hebrew order; חוּט "thread", שָׁנִי "scarlet". **"thy speech is comely" → "your mouth is lovely"** — Hebrew מִדְבָּרֵיךְ, only here in this sense, "your instrument of speaking" (from דִּבֶּר "speak"; the same letters as מִדְבָּר "wilderness"): "mouth" or "speech", uncertain.
- **"thy temples are like a piece of a pomegranate" → "Like a slice of the pomegranate is your temple"** (v3) — Hebrew כְּפֶלַח הָרִמּוֹן רַקָּתֵךְ; פֶּלַח "slice" (as 1 Sam 30:12); רַקָּה "temple" (the side of the head, as Judg 4:21), singular; not "cheeks".
- **"builded for an armoury" → "built in courses"** (v4) — Hebrew בָּנוּי לְתַלְפִּיּוֹת; תַּלְפִּיּוֹת is found only here: "in courses (of stone)", "for weapons", or a place name; uncertain. "of stone" is not written.
- **"whereon there hang a thousand bucklers, all shields of mighty men" → "a thousand shields hang upon it, all the bucklers of the mighty ones"** (v4) — Hebrew אֶלֶף הַמָּגֵן "a thousand of the shield" (singular with the article, as a collective); שִׁלְטֵי הַגִּבּוֹרִים: שֶׁלֶט beside מָגֵן is "buckler" (as 2 Chr 23:9); גִּבּוֹרִים "mighty ones".
- **"like two young roes that are twins, which feed among the lilies" → "like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, that pasture among the lilies"** (v5) — Hebrew עֳפָרִים "fawns" (as 2:9), תְּאוֹמֵי צְבִיָּה "twins of a gazelle"; הָרוֹעִים "that pasture" (of the animals themselves, as 2:16).
- **"Until the day break" → "Until the day breathes"** (v6) — as 2:17. **"I will get me to the mountain of myrrh" → "I will get myself to the mountain of myrrh"** — Hebrew אֵלֶךְ לִי, with לִי ("for myself"): the reflexive of the "Get yourself" row; הַמּוֹר and הַלְּבוֹנָה have the article (the kind). לְבוֹנָה "frankincense" (also v14).
- **"Thou art all fair, my love; there is no spot in thee" → "All of you is beautiful, my companion, and there is no blemish in you"** (v7) — Hebrew כֻּלָּךְ יָפָה "all of you is beautiful"; וּמוּם אֵין בָּךְ, with "and"; מוּם "blemish" (as Lev 21:17).
- **"Come with me from Lebanon, my spouse" → "With me from Lebanon, bride"** (v8) — Hebrew אִתִּי מִלְּבָנוֹן כַּלָּה: no "come", no "my". כַּלָּה "bride" (the wedding is meant; also v9–12). **"with me from Lebanon" → "with me from Lebanon you will come"** — Hebrew תָּבוֹאִי, imperfect: "you will come" (or a wish, "may you come").
- **"look from the top of Amana" → "You will watch from the top of Amana"** (v8) — Hebrew תָּשׁוּרִי, imperfect of שׁוּר "watch" (as Num 23:9); a second verb שׁוּר "journey, go down" is also proposed: uncertain. רֹאשׁ "top" (lit. "head"). מְעֹנוֹת "dens" (מָעוֹן "abode", of animals), הַרְרֵי "mountains" (as Deut 33:15).
- **"Thou hast ravished my heart, my sister, my spouse" → "You have taken my heart, my sister, bride"** (v9) — Hebrew לִבַּבְתִּנִי, a verb made from לֵב "heart": "you have taken my heart" or "you have made my heart bold", uncertain. אֲחֹתִי "my sister"; כַּלָּה without "my" (also v10, 11, 12).
- **"with one of thine eyes, with one chain of thy neck" → "with one of your eyes, with one chain of your neck"** (v9) — **Qere** בְּאַחַת (feminine, with "eye", which is feminine); the written form באחד is masculine: the meaning is the same. עֲנָק "chain" (a neck ornament, as Prov 1:9); מִצַּוְּרֹנָיִךְ, only here, from צַוָּאר "neck" (the plural of the necklace or of the neck).
- **"How fair is thy love … how much better is thy love than wine! and the smell of thine ointments than all spices!" → "How beautiful is your love … How much better is your love than wine, and the smell of your oils than all spices!"** (v10) — Hebrew מַה־יָּפוּ דֹדַיִךְ … מַה־טֹּבוּ דֹדַיִךְ מִיַּיִן, plural דֹּדִים "loves" (as 1:2), English singular; רֵיחַ "smell"; שֶׁמֶן "oil"; בְּשָׂמִים "spices" (also v14, 16).
- **"Thy lips, O my spouse, drop as the honeycomb" → "Your lips drip honey, bride"** (v11) — Hebrew נֹפֶת תִּטֹּפְנָה שִׂפְתוֹתַיִךְ, as Prov 5:3: נֹפֶת is honey that drips from the comb. The "honey" of the next line is דְּבַשׁ, another word (English has one). **"the smell of thy garments" → "the smell of your cloaks"** — Hebrew שַׂלְמֹתַיִךְ, plural of שַׂלְמָה (= שִׂמְלָה "cloak").
- **"A garden inclosed is my sister, my spouse; a spring shut up, a fountain sealed" → "A garden locked, my sister, bride, a fountain locked, a spring sealed"** (v12) — Hebrew גַּן נָעוּל … גַּל נָעוּל מַעְיָן חָתוּם, no verb: "is" is not written (it may be "a garden locked is my sister", or "you are" said to her). גַּל, elsewhere a "heap" of stones (Gen 31:46) and "waves", is here a welling water: "fountain", uncertain; some Hebrew manuscripts and the versions have גַּן "garden" (from memory); MT kept. מַעְיָן "spring".
- **"Thy plants are an orchard of pomegranates, with pleasant fruits; camphire, with spikenard" → "Your shoots are a park of pomegranates with fruit of choice things, henna with nard"** (v13) — Hebrew שְׁלָחַיִךְ, only here: "your shoots" (from שָׁלַח "send out") or "your channels", uncertain; פַּרְדֵּס "park" (a Persian loan word, as Neh 2:8); פְּרִי מְגָדִים "fruit of choice things" (מֶגֶד, as Deut 33:13); כְּפָרִים עִם־נְרָדִים, both plural ("hennas with nards"); כֹּפֶר "henna" (as 1:14).
- **"Spikenard and saffron; calamus and cinnamon … myrrh and aloes, with all the chief spices" → "nard and saffron, cane and cinnamon … myrrh and aloes, with all the chief spices"** (v14) — Hebrew נֵרְדְּ "nard"; כַּרְכֹּם, only here, "saffron" (the crocus), uncertain; קָנֶה "cane" (an aromatic reed: "fragrant cane", Exod 30:23; "calamus" is the Latin name); קִנָּמוֹן "cinnamon"; עֲצֵי לְבוֹנָה "trees of frankincense"; מֹר "myrrh"; אֲהָלוֹת "aloes" (an aromatic wood, perhaps eaglewood; not the aloe plant: uncertain); רָאשֵׁי בְשָׂמִים "the chief spices", lit. "heads of spices".
- **"A fountain of gardens, a well of living waters, and streams from Lebanon" → "a spring of gardens, a well of living water, and streams from Lebanon"** (v15) — Hebrew מַעְיַן גַּנִּים "a spring of gardens" (מַעְיָן "spring", as v12); בְּאֵר מַיִם חַיִּים "a well of living water" (running water from a spring); נֹזְלִים "streams" (lit. "flowing ones").
- **"Awake, O north wind; and come, thou south" → "Awake, north, and come, south!"** (v16) — Hebrew עוּרִי צָפוֹן וּבוֹאִי תֵימָן: "wind" is not written (the quarters of the sky are addressed, with feminine verbs); תֵּימָן is lit. "the right side" (south). **"blow upon my garden, that the spices thereof may flow out" → "Make my garden breathe, let its spices flow"** — Hebrew הָפִיחִי גַנִּי, hifil of פּוּחַ "breathe" (the verb of v6; 2:17), with "my garden" as object; יִזְּלוּ, a wish. **"his pleasant fruits" → "the fruit of his choice things"** — Hebrew פְּרִי מְגָדָיו (as v13); וְיֹאכַל "and eat" (a wish, with "Let").

### Notes
- **Speakers** — the Hebrew has no speaker names: v1–15 are a man's words to a woman (feminine "you", "my companion", "my sister, bride"); v16 begins with the winds addressed and ends in a woman's voice ("my beloved … his garden"), or the man speaks "my garden" and she answers: not marked.
