# 1 Chronicles 12

[1] These are those who came to David at Ziklag, while he was still kept from the presence of Saul son of Kish; they were among the warriors who helped him in war.

[2] They were armed with bows, able to sling stones with the right or the left and to shoot arrows from a bow; they were Saul's kinsmen, of Benjamin.

[3] The chief was Ahiezer, then Joash, the sons of Shemaah the Gibeathite; Jeziel and Pelet, the sons of Azmaveth; Beracah; and Jehu the Anathothite;

[4] Ishmaiah the Gibeonite, a warrior among the Thirty and over the Thirty; Jeremiah, Jahaziel, Johanan, Jozabad the Gederathite;

[5] Eluzai, Jerimoth, Bealiah, Shemariah, Shephatiah the Haruphite;

[6] Elkanah, Isshiah, Azarel, Joezer, and Jashobeam, the Korahites;

[7] Joelah and Zebadiah, the sons of Jeroham of Gedor.

[8] Of the Gadites there separated themselves to David in the stronghold in the wilderness men of might — men trained for battle, who could handle shield and spear, whose faces were like the faces of lions, and who were swift as gazelles on the mountains.

[9] Ezer the first, Obadiah the second, Eliab the third,

[10] Mishmannah the fourth, Jeremiah the fifth,

[11] Attai the sixth, Eliel the seventh,

[12] Johanan the eighth, Elzabad the ninth,

[13] Jeremiah the tenth, Machbannai the eleventh.

[14] These Gadites were chiefs of the army; the least was equal to a hundred, and the greatest to a thousand.

[15] These are those who crossed the Jordan in the first month, when it had overflowed all its banks, and put to flight all those in the valleys, both east and west.

[16] Some of the children of Benjamin and Judah came to the stronghold to David.

[17] David went out to meet them, and said to them, "If you have come peaceably to me to help me, my heart shall be knit to you; but if to betray me to my enemies — though there is no wrong in my hands — may the *Elohim* of our fathers see it and rebuke you."

[18] Then the spirit clothed Amasai, chief of the Thirty:

"Yours are we, David,
and with you, O son of Jesse!
Peace, peace to you,
and peace to your helpers,
for your *Elohim* helps you."

Then David received them and made them officers of his raiding band.

[19] Some from Manasseh also defected to David when he came with the Philistines against Saul to battle — though they did not help them; for after consultation the lords of the Philistines sent him away, saying, "He will defect to his master Saul at the cost of our heads."

[20] As he went to Ziklag, there defected to him from Manasseh: Adnah, Jozabad, Jediael, Michael, Jozabad, Elihu, and Zillethai — chiefs of thousands belonging to Manasseh.

[21] They helped David against the raiding band, for they were all valiant warriors and became commanders in the army.

[22] Day by day men came to David to help him, until it was a great army, like the army of *Elohim*.

[23] These are the numbers of the divisions of armed troops who came to David at Hebron, to turn the kingdom of Saul over to him, according to the word of Yahweh.

[24] The children of Judah who bore shield and spear: six thousand eight hundred, armed for war.

[25] Of the children of Simeon, valiant warriors for the war: seven thousand one hundred.

[26] Of the children of Levi: four thousand six hundred.

[27] Jehoiada was the leader of the Aaronides; with him were three thousand seven hundred.

[28] Zadok was a young man, a valiant warrior; with his father's house were twenty-two officers.

[29] Of the children of Benjamin, the kinsmen of Saul: three thousand — until then the majority of them had maintained allegiance to the house of Saul.

[30] Of the children of Ephraim: twenty thousand eight hundred valiant warriors, famous men of their fathers' houses.

[31] Of the half-tribe of Manasseh: eighteen thousand, who were designated by name to come and make David king.

[32] Of the children of Issachar, who had understanding of the times — knowing what Israel should do — their chiefs were two hundred; all their brothers were under their command.

[33] Of Zebulun, those ready to go out for battle, drawn up with all the weapons of war: fifty thousand who could keep rank — not of double heart.

[34] Of Naphtali: one thousand officers; with them, with shield and spear, thirty-seven thousand.

[35] Of the Danites, trained for war: twenty-eight thousand six hundred.

[36] Of Asher, those ready to go out for battle, trained for war: forty thousand.

[37] From beyond the Jordan, of the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh, with all manner of weapons of war for the battle: one hundred twenty thousand.

[38] All these men of war, who could keep rank, came with a whole heart to Hebron to make David king over all Israel; all the rest of Israel also were of one heart to make David king.

[39] They were there with David three days, eating and drinking, for their kinsmen had made preparation for them.

[40] Those nearby, as far as Issachar and Zebulun and Naphtali, were bringing food on donkeys, camels, mules, and oxen — abundant provisions: flour, cakes of figs, clusters of raisins, wine, oil, oxen, and sheep — for there was joy in Israel.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Antothite" → "Anathothite"** (v3).
- **"roes" → "gazelles"** (v8) — *tzevayim*.
- **"upon advisement" → "after consultation"** (v19).
- **"rovers" → "raiding band"** (v21) — *gedud*.
- **"Aaronites" → "Aaronides"** (v27).
- **"perfect heart" → "whole heart"** (v38) — *levav shalem*.
- **"meat, meal" → "flour"** (v40) — Hebrew *qemach*; "meat" in KJV = archaic for food-staple.

### Notes
- **Chronicler's emphasis** — progressive all-Israel support for David, from fugitive days (Ziklag) through stronghold to Hebron.
- **Benjaminite support** — unusual: Saul's own tribe defects to David. Chronicler signals theological legitimation.
- **Gadites' heroism** — crossing flooded Jordan in first month = high-water season. Feats of reconnaissance and valor.
- **Amasai's oracle** (v18) — poetic; "your *Elohim* helps you." Spirit-inspired loyalty pledge.
- **Issachar "understood the times"** (v32) — classic phrase; contextual political wisdom. 200 tribal heads as counselors.
- **"Not of double heart"** (v33) — Hebrew *be-lo-lev-va-lev*; unwavering commitment.
- **Total count** — ~340,000 men. Symbolic magnitude, showing comprehensive all-Israel backing.
- **Post-coronation feast** — three-day celebration. Unity theme.
