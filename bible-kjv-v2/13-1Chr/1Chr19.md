# 1 Chronicles 19

[1] After this, Nahash king of the children of Ammon died, and his son reigned in his place.

[2] David said, "I will show steadfast love to Hanun son of Nahash, because his father showed steadfast love to me." So David sent messengers to console him concerning his father. The servants of David came into the land of the children of Ammon to Hanun, to console him.

[3] But the princes of the children of Ammon said to Hanun, "Do you think that David is honoring your father, because he has sent consolers to you? Is it not to search out, overthrow, and spy out the land that his servants have come to you?"

[4] So Hanun took David's servants, shaved them, cut off their garments in the middle at the hips, and sent them away.

[5] Some went and told David how the men had been treated; he sent to meet them, for the men were greatly ashamed. The king said, "Remain at Jericho until your beards have grown, and then return."

[6] When the children of Ammon saw that they had made themselves odious to David, Hanun and the children of Ammon sent a thousand talents of silver to hire chariots and horsemen from Mesopotamia, from Aram-maacah, and from Zobah.

[7] They hired thirty-two thousand chariots, and the king of Maacah with his people, who came and encamped before Medeba. The children of Ammon gathered from their cities and came to battle.

[8] When David heard of it, he sent Joab and all the army of the warriors.

[9] The children of Ammon came out and drew up in battle array at the entrance of the city; the kings who had come were by themselves in the open field.

[10] When Joab saw that the battle was set against him both in front and behind, he chose from all the choice men of Israel and arrayed them against the Arameans.

[11] The rest of the people he placed under the command of Abishai his brother; they arrayed themselves against the children of Ammon.

[12] He said, "If the Arameans are too strong for me, then you shall help me; but if the children of Ammon are too strong for you, then I will help you.

[13] Be strong, and let us show courage for our people and for the cities of our *Elohim*; and may Yahweh do what is good in his eyes."

[14] So Joab and the people who were with him drew near before the Arameans for battle; they fled before him.

[15] When the children of Ammon saw that the Arameans had fled, they likewise fled before Abishai his brother and entered the city. Then Joab came to Jerusalem.

[16] When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they sent messengers and called out the Arameans who were beyond the River; Shophach the commander of Hadadezer's army went before them.

[17] It was told David; he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, came upon them, and drew up battle array against them. When David had drawn up in battle array against the Arameans, they fought with him.

[18] The Arameans fled before Israel; David killed of the Arameans seven thousand who fought in chariots and forty thousand foot soldiers, and killed Shophach the commander of the army.

[19] When the servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with David and became his servants. The Arameans would no longer help the children of Ammon.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Syria / Syrians" → "Aram / Arameans"** (throughout).
- **"captain" → "commander"** (v16, 18) — *sar*.
- **"in the midst hard by their buttocks" → "in the middle at the hips"** (v4) — Hebrew *ba-chatzi ad ha-seta*; cut to expose; a calculated public humiliation.
- **"behave ourselves valiantly" → "show courage"** (v13) — *nitchazzaq*.

### Notes
- **Parallel to 2 Sam 10** — Chronicler omits the Bathsheba-Uriah scandal that 2 Sam 11-12 embeds within this campaign. Telling silence: focuses on covenant-dynasty, suppresses David's darker deeds.
- **Nahash** — same who besieged Jabesh-Gilead (1 Sam 11); "*chesed*" from him to David unexplained (perhaps shelter during Saul's persecution?).
- **Shaving and garment-cutting** — deliberate diplomatic insult combining two dishonor symbols. Levitical priests are not to trim beard (Lev 21:5); exposing lower body is deep shame.
- **Hired coalition** — Aram-maacah, Zobah, Mesopotamia (*Aram-naharaim*). Ammon buys a massive chariot force.
- **Two-front battle** — Joab's tactical excellence. Outnumbered, splits force; Abishai backs him.
- **Chronicler simplifies** — omits 2 Sam 10:18's different casualty numbers (700 chariot horses vs 7,000 chariot-men; Chronicler counts men, Sam counts horses — likely scribal variants of same event).
- **Final pax** (v19) — Aramean vassalage; closes northern frontier at least until Hadadezer's successor state emerges.
