## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"familiar spirit" → "medium"** (v13) — *ov*.
- **"transgression" → "unfaithfulness"** (v13) — *ma'al*; Chronicler's key theological term for covenant breach.
- **"all his house died together"** (v6) — Chronicler's summary, simpler than 1 Sam 31:6 ("his armor-bearer and all his men the same day"); focuses on dynastic end.
- **"slew him" → "put him to death"** (v14) — *hemit*.

### Notes
- **Parallel to 1 Sam 31** — Chronicler's abridged, theologically sharpened version. 1 Sam sets Saul's end in broader narrative (Ish-bosheth follows); Chronicles compresses to focus on dynastic transfer.
- **"All his house died together" (v6)** — overstatement (Ish-bosheth survives in 1 Sam); rhetorical closure of Saul's dynasty for Chronicler's purpose.
- **Theological gloss (v13-14)** — Chronicler adds the interpretation. Two reasons for Saul's fall: covenant unfaithfulness, necromantic consultation (En-dor episode).
- **"Turned the kingdom" (v14)** — Yahweh actively transfers. David's kingship is Yahweh's doing.
- **Chronicler begins narrative at Saul's death** — no account of Saul's reign, no Samuel-Saul relationship. For Chronicler, history begins with David's rise.
