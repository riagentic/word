# 1 Chronicles 10

[1] Now the Philistines fought against Israel; the men of Israel fled before the Philistines and fell slain on Mount Gilboa.

[2] The Philistines pressed hard after Saul and after his sons; the Philistines killed Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchi-shua, the sons of Saul.

[3] The battle pressed heavily against Saul; the archers found him, and he was wounded by the archers.

[4] Then Saul said to his armor-bearer, "Draw your sword and thrust me through with it, lest these uncircumcised come and abuse me." But his armor-bearer would not, for he was very afraid. So Saul took the sword and fell on it.

[5] When his armor-bearer saw that Saul was dead, he likewise fell on the sword and died.

[6] So Saul died, with his three sons; all his house died together.

[7] When all the men of Israel who were in the valley saw that they had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they abandoned their cities and fled; the Philistines came and lived in them.

[8] The next day, when the Philistines came to strip the slain, they found Saul and his sons fallen on Mount Gilboa.

[9] They stripped him, took his head and his armor, and sent into the land of the Philistines on every side, to carry the good news to their idols and to the people.

[10] They put his armor in the house of their gods, and fastened his head in the temple of Dagon.

[11] When all Jabesh-gilead heard all that the Philistines had done to Saul,

[12] all the valiant men arose and took away the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons, and brought them to Jabesh; they buried their bones under the oak in Jabesh, and fasted seven days.

[13] So Saul died for his unfaithfulness that he committed against Yahweh — against the word of Yahweh that he did not keep — and also for consulting a medium for guidance,

[14] not inquiring of Yahweh; therefore Yahweh put him to death and turned the kingdom over to David son of Jesse.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"familiar spirit" → "medium"** (v13) — *ov*.
- **"transgression" → "unfaithfulness"** (v13) — *ma'al*; Chronicler's key theological term for covenant breach.
- **"all his house died together"** (v6) — Chronicler's summary, simpler than 1 Sam 31:6 ("his armor-bearer and all his men the same day"); focuses on dynastic end.
- **"slew him" → "put him to death"** (v14) — *hemit*.

### Notes
- **Parallel to 1 Sam 31** — Chronicler's abridged, theologically sharpened version. 1 Sam sets Saul's end in broader narrative (Ish-bosheth follows); Chronicles compresses to focus on dynastic transfer.
- **"All his house died together" (v6)** — overstatement (Ish-bosheth survives in 1 Sam); rhetorical closure of Saul's dynasty for Chronicler's purpose.
- **Theological gloss (v13-14)** — Chronicler adds the interpretation. Two reasons for Saul's fall: covenant unfaithfulness, necromantic consultation (En-dor episode).
- **"Turned the kingdom" (v14)** — Yahweh actively transfers. David's kingship is Yahweh's doing.
- **Chronicler begins narrative at Saul's death** — no account of Saul's reign, no Samuel-Saul relationship. For Chronicler, history begins with David's rise.
