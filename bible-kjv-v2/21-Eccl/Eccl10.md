# Ecclesiastes 10

[1] Flies of death make stink, make bubble the oil of a perfumer;
weightier than wisdom, than glory, is a little foolishness.

[2] The heart of a wise man is to his right,
and the heart of a fool to his left.

[3] And also on the way, when a foolish one walks, his heart is lacking,
and he says to all, he is a foolish one.

[4] If the spirit of the ruler rises up against you, do not leave your place, for calmness lays great sins to rest.

[5] There is an evil I have seen under the sun, like an error that goes out from before the ruler:

[6] foolishness is set in many heights, and rich ones sit in low estate.

[7] I have seen slaves on horses, and princes walking like slaves on the earth.

[8] He who digs a pit will fall into it,
and he who breaks through a fence, a serpent will bite him.

[9] He who quarries stones will be pained by them;
he who splits wood will be endangered by them.

[10] If the iron is blunt, and he has not whetted the face,
then he must make strengths mighty;
and the advantage of giving success is wisdom.

[11] If the serpent bites without charming,
then there is no advantage for the master of the tongue.

[12] The words of the mouth of a wise man are favor,
and the lips of a fool swallow him.

[13] The beginning of the words of his mouth is foolishness,
and the end of his mouth is evil madness.

[14] And the foolish one multiplies words;
man does not know what will be,
and what will be after him, who will tell him?

[15] The toil of the fools wearies him,
who does not know to go to a city.

[16] Woe to you, land, whose king is a boy,
and your princes eat in the morning!

[17] Happy are you, land, whose king is a son of nobles,
and your princes eat at the time,
in might and not in drinking!

[18] Through sloth the rafters sink low,
and through lowness of hands the house leaks.

[19] For laughter they make bread,
and wine makes life glad,
and the silver answers everything.

[20] Even in your knowledge do not curse a king,
and in the chambers of your bed do not curse a rich man;
for the birds of the heavens will carry the voice,
and a winged one will tell a word.

## Changes

- **"Dead flies cause the ointment of the apothecary to send forth a stinking savour" → "Flies of death make stink, make bubble the oil of a perfumer"** (v1) — Hebrew זְבוּבֵי מָוֶת "flies of death" (dead flies, or flies that bring death: kept literal); two verbs without "and", both singular: יַבְאִישׁ "makes stink", יַבִּיעַ "makes bubble" (hifil of נָבַע "bubble, pour forth": of oil going bad, uncertain); שֶׁמֶן "oil"; רוֹקֵחַ "perfumer", without the article.
- **"so doth a little folly him that is in reputation for wisdom and honour" → "weightier than wisdom, than glory, is a little foolishness"** (v1) — Hebrew יָקָר מֵחָכְמָה מִכָּבוֹד סִכְלוּת מְעָט: יָקָר "precious, costly", with מִן "weightier than" (a little outweighs both); כָּבוֹד "glory" (WORDS-HE). סִכְלוּת "foolishness" (v13; root of סָכָל "foolish one", v3, 14), kept apart from אִוֶּלֶת "folly" (WISDOM.md).
- **"A wise man's heart is at his right hand; but a fool's heart at his left" → "The heart of a wise man is to his right, and the heart of a fool to his left"** (v2) — Hebrew לִימִינוֹ … לִשְׂמֹאלוֹ, "to his right … to his left" (the right is the side of strength and favor); no verb "inclines"; "and", not "but". כְּסִיל "fool" (WISDOM.md).
- **"his wisdom faileth him, and he saith to every one that he is a fool" → "his heart is lacking, and he says to all, he is a foolish one"** (v3) — qere כְּשֶׁסָּכָל "when a foolish one" (the written form has the article, "the foolish one"); לִבּוֹ חָסֵר "his heart is lacking" (heart = mind: WISDOM.md, חֲסַר־לֵב). וְאָמַר לַכֹּל סָכָל הוּא: he shows everyone that he is foolish, or he calls everyone foolish; the verse keeps both open.
- **"for yielding pacifieth great offences" → "for calmness lays great sins to rest"** (v4) — Hebrew רוּחַ "spirit" (the ruler's temper; WORDS-HE). אַל־תַּנַּח "do not leave" and יַנִּיחַ "lays to rest" are one verb (hifil of נוּחַ), a play the English cannot keep. מַרְפֵּא "calmness" (from רָפָא "heal" or רָפָה "relax"; uncertain). חֲטָאִים "sins" (WORDS-HE), not "offences".
- **"as an error which proceedeth from the ruler" → "like an error that goes out from before the ruler"** (v5) — Hebrew שְׁגָגָה "error" (WORDS-HE); מִלִּפְנֵי "from before". הַשַּׁלִּיט "the ruler" (as Gen 42:6; v4 has מוֹשֵׁל: English has one word for both).
- **"Folly is set in great dignity, and the rich sit in low place" → "foolishness is set in many heights, and rich ones sit in low estate"** (v6) — Hebrew הַסֶּכֶל, only here, of the root of סִכְלוּת (v1, 13): "foolishness" for both; בַּמְּרוֹמִים רַבִּים "in many heights" (רַבִּים has no article and may also be "great" or "the many": uncertain); שֵׁפֶל "low estate" (WORDS-HE); יֵשֵׁבוּ "sit" (יָשַׁב of the body).
- **"servants" → "slaves"** (v7) — Hebrew עֲבָדִים (WORDS-HE); שָׂרִים "princes"; עַל־הָאָרֶץ "on the earth" (= on foot).
- **"whoso breaketh an hedge" → "he who breaks through a fence"** (v8) — Hebrew גּוּמָּץ "pit" (only here; an Aramaic word, beside בּוֹר); פֹּרֵץ "break through" (פָּרַץ); גָּדֵר "fence" (WORDS-HE: a stone wall round a field); נָחָשׁ "serpent".
- **"shall be hurt therewith … shall be endangered thereby" → "will be pained by them … will be endangered by them"** (v9) — Hebrew מַסִּיעַ "quarries" (lit. "pulls out", as 1 Kgs 5:17); יֵעָצֵב, nifal of עָצַב, elsewhere "be grieved" (WORDS-HE); here the pain of the body (as עֶצֶב "pain"); יִסָּכֶן "be endangered" (only here in this sense); בָּם "by them" both times.
- **"and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength: but wisdom is profitable to direct" → "and he has not whetted the face, then he must make strengths mighty; and the advantage of giving success is wisdom"** (v10) — Hebrew פָּנִים "face" (= the edge: kept); קִלְקַל "whet". וַחֲיָלִים יְגַבֵּר, literally "and strengths he makes mighty" (חַיִל plural; גִּבֵּר piel of גָּבַר): he must put in more force. qere הַכְשֵׁר, hifil infinitive of כָּשֵׁר "succeed" (11:6; the written form הכשיר has the same root); יִתְרוֹן "advantage" (WISDOM.md); "and", not "but".
- **"Surely the serpent will bite without enchantment; and a babbler is no better" → "If the serpent bites without charming, then there is no advantage for the master of the tongue"** (v11) — Hebrew אִם "if"; בְּלוֹא־לָחַשׁ "without a charm" (לַחַשׁ, a whispered spell); בַּעַל הַלָּשׁוֹן "the master of the tongue", literally "owner of the tongue" (= the charmer; בַּעַל + noun: one marked by the thing, WORDS-HE). יִתְרוֹן "advantage", as v10.
- **"are gracious; but the lips of a fool will swallow up himself" → "are favor, and the lips of a fool swallow him"** (v12) — Hebrew חֵן "favor" (WORDS-HE), a noun, no verb ("win him favor" was supplied); תְּבַלְּעֶנּוּ "swallow him" (בָּלַע; whom is not said: the fool himself, or the wise man).
- **"and the end of his talk is mischievous madness" → "and the end of his mouth is evil madness"** (v13) — Hebrew אַחֲרִית פִּיהוּ "the end of his mouth"; הוֹלֵלוּת "madness" (9:3); רָעָה "evil".
- **"A fool also is full of words: a man cannot tell what shall be" → "And the foolish one multiplies words; man does not know what will be"** (v14) — Hebrew וְהַסָּכָל יַרְבֶּה דְבָרִים "and the foolish one multiplies words"; לֹא־יֵדַע "does not know" ("though" is not written); מִי יַגִּיד לוֹ "who will tell him?"
- **"The labour of the foolish wearieth every one of them, because he knoweth not how to go to the city" → "The toil of the fools wearies him, who does not know to go to a city"** (v15) — Hebrew עֲמַל הַכְּסִילִים, plural, with the singular "him"; עָמָל "toil" (WORDS-HE); תְּיַגְּעֶנּוּ, piel of יָגַע "labor": "make weary"; אֲשֶׁר "who", not "because"; אֶל־עִיר "to a city", without the article.
- **"Woe to thee, O land, when thy king is a child, and thy princes eat in the morning" → "Woe to you, land, whose king is a boy …"** (v16) — no "O" (WORDS-HE reviewer row); אִי־לָךְ "woe to you" (a rare form of אוֹי); שֶׁ "whose"; נַעַר "boy" (also "young man", "servant": WORDS-HE); יֹאכֵלוּ "eat" (not "feast").
- **"Blessed art thou, O land … in due season, for strength, and not for drunkenness" → "Happy are you, land … at the time, in might and not in drinking"** (v17) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵיךְ "happy are you" (WORDS-HE: "Happy", never "Blessed"); בֶּן־חוֹרִים "a son of nobles" (free-born men); בָּעֵת "at the time" (the proper time); בִּגְבוּרָה "in might" (גְּבוּרָה; "for strength" is the sense commonly given); בַשְּׁתִי "in drinking" (only here; "drunkenness" is the sense).
- **"By much slothfulness the building decayeth; and through idleness of the hands the house droppeth through" → "Through sloth the rafters sink low, and through lowness of hands the house leaks"** (v18) — Hebrew עֲצַלְתַּיִם, a dual form of "sloth" (double sloth, much sloth); יִמַּךְ "sink low" (מָכַךְ, WORDS-HE); הַמְּקָרֶה "the rafters" (only here; from קָרָה "lay beams": the roof-work); שִׁפְלוּת יָדַיִם "lowness of hands" (hands let hang); יִדְלֹף "leaks, drips".
- **"A feast is made for laughter, and wine maketh merry: but money answereth all things" → "For laughter they make bread, and wine makes life glad, and the silver answers everything"** (v19) — Hebrew עֹשִׂים, an active participle with no subject ("they make"), not "is made"; לֶחֶם "bread" (WORDS-HE: "food" only with a note; here a meal); יְשַׂמַּח חַיִּים "makes life glad" (piel of שָׂמַח); וְ "and", not "but". הַכֶּסֶף "the silver" (money is meant: WORDS-HE); יַעֲנֶה "answers" (עָנָה; or "keeps busy", from the root of עִנְיָן "occupation", 1:13: uncertain).
- **"Curse not the king, no not in thy thought; and curse not the rich in thy bedchamber" → "Even in your knowledge do not curse a king, and in the chambers of your bed do not curse a rich man"** (v20) — Hebrew גַּם בְּמַדָּעֲךָ "even in your knowledge" (מַדָּע, WORDS-HE; = in your own mind); קִלֵּל "curse" (literally "make light of": WORDS-HE); מֶלֶךְ and עָשִׁיר without the article; חַדְרֵי מִשְׁכָּבְךָ "the chambers of your bed" (מִשְׁכָּב "bed").
- **"for a bird of the air shall carry the voice, and that which hath wings shall tell the matter" → "for the birds of the heavens will carry the voice, and a winged one will tell a word"** (v20) — Hebrew עוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם "the birds of the heavens" (collective, with a singular verb; WORDS-HE); יוֹלִיךְ "carry" (hifil of הָלַךְ "make go"); הַקּוֹל "the voice" ("your" is not written). qere בַּעַל כְּנָפַיִם "a winged one", literally "owner of wings" (the written form has the article); דָּבָר "word".
- **"under the sun"** (v5) — kept.
