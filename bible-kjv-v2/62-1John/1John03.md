# 1 John 3

[1] See what manner of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; and we are. Because of this the world does not know us, because it did not know him.

[2] Beloved, now we are children of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and it has not yet been manifested what we shall be. We know that if he is manifested we shall be like him, because we shall see him just as he is.

[3] And everyone who has this hope set on him purifies himself, just as that one is pure.

[4] Everyone who does sin also does lawlessness, and sin is lawlessness.

[5] And you know that that one was manifested so that he might take away sins, and in him there is no sin.

[6] Everyone who abides in him does not sin; everyone who sins has not seen him, nor has he come to know him.

[7] Little children, let no one deceive you: the one who does righteousness is righteous, just as that one is righteous;

[8] the one who does sin is of the devil, because the devil sins from the beginning. For this the Son of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ was manifested, that he might undo the works of the devil.

[9] Everyone who has been begotten of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ does not do sin, because his seed abides in him, and he is not able to sin, because he has been begotten of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[10] By this the children of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and the children of the devil are manifest: everyone who does not do righteousness is not of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, nor the one who does not love his brother.

[11] Because this is the message which you heard from the beginning, that we love one another;

[12] not as ⟦cain|Qayin|Cain⟧ was of the evil one and slaughtered his brother. And for what cause did he slaughter him? Because his works were evil, but those of his brother righteous.

[13] Do not marvel, brothers, if the world hates you.

[14] **We know that we have passed over out of death into life, because we love the brothers. The one who does not love abides in death.**

[15] Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in him.

[16] By this we have come to know love, that that one laid down his soul for us; and we ought to lay down our souls for the brothers.

[17] But whoever has the livelihood of the world and beholds his brother having need and shuts his inward parts from him, how does the love of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ abide in him?

[18] Little children, let us not love in word nor with the tongue, but in work and truth.

[19] And by this we shall know that we are of the truth, and before him we shall persuade our heart

[20] that, if our heart condemns us, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is greater than our heart and knows all things.

[21] Beloved, if the heart does not condemn us, we have boldness toward ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[22] and whatever we ask we receive from him, because we keep his commandments and do the things that are pleasing before him.

[23] And this is his commandment, that we believe the name of his Son ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and love one another, just as he gave us a commandment.

[24] And the one who keeps his commandments abides in him, and he in him; and by this we know that he abides in us, from the Spirit which he gave us.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"Jesus Christ" → "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (v23) — systematic.
- **"Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us" → "See what manner of love the Father has given to us"** (v1) — Greek ἴδετε ποταπὴν ἀγάπην δέδωκεν; δίδωμι is "give".
- **"that we should be called the sons of God" → "that we should be called children of *Elohim*; and we are"** (v1, 2, 10) — Greek τέκνα θεοῦ, "children"; καὶ ἐσμέν, "and we are", is in the critical text and not in the KJV's.
- **"therefore the world knoweth us not" → "Because of this the world does not know us"** (v1) — Greek διὰ τοῦτο … ὅτι, "because of this … because".
- **"it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear" → "it has not yet been manifested what we shall be. We know that if he is manifested"** (v2) — Greek ἐφανερώθη … ἐὰν φανερωθῇ, the passive of φανερόω both times, with ἐάν "if" as in 2:28; no "but" in the critical text. The subject of the second verb is not expressed: "if he is manifested" or "if it is manifested" (what we shall be).
- **"every man that hath this hope in him" → "everyone who has this hope set on him"** (v3) — Greek ἐπ' αὐτῷ, "upon him": the hope rests on him ("set" is supplied for English).
- **"even as he is pure" → "just as that one is pure"** (v3, 5, 7, 16) — Greek ἐκεῖνος, as in 2:6.
- **"Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law" → "Everyone who does sin also does lawlessness, and sin is lawlessness"** (v4) — Greek ὁ ποιῶν τὴν ἁμαρτίαν καὶ τὴν ἀνομίαν ποιεῖ, καὶ ἡ ἁμαρτία ἐστὶν ἡ ἀνομία: the verb ποιέω "do" twice, the noun ἀνομία "lawlessness" twice, καί "and".
- **"to take away our sins" → "that he might take away sins"** (v5) — NA28 τὰς ἁμαρτίας, without "our".
- **"sinneth not"** (v6, 8, 9) — Greek ἁμαρτάνει, ἁμαρτάνειν, plain present forms: "does not sin", "sins", "to sin". The Greek present often describes what goes on or is usual; the words "keeps on" or "makes a practice of" are not in the text.
- **"neither known him" → "nor has he come to know him"** (v6) — Greek ἔγνωκεν, a perfect.
- **"Little children" kept** (v7) — Greek τεκνία, as in v18 (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA27, RP). NA28 and the WH margin: παιδία, "Young children", as in 2:14, 18.
- **"that he might destroy the works of the devil" → "that he might undo the works of the devil"** (v8) — Greek λύσῃ, "loose, untie, dissolve".
- **"Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him" → "Everyone who has been begotten of *Elohim* does not do sin, because his seed abides in him"** (v9) — Greek γεγεννημένος, γεγέννηται (see 2:29); σπέρμα αὐτοῦ, "his seed" — the Greek does not repeat the name; μένει "abides"; οὐ δύναται, "is not able".
- **"In this the children of God are manifest" → "By this … are manifest"** (v10) — Greek φανερά, the same root as "manifested".
- **"that we should love one another" → "that we love one another"** (v11) — Greek ἵνα ἀγαπῶμεν ἀλλήλους, as in 2 John 5.
- **"Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother" → "not as *Qayin* was of the evil one and slaughtered his brother"** (v12) — Greek οὐ καθὼς Κάϊν ἐκ τοῦ πονηροῦ ἦν: no "we should be" and no "who"; ἔσφαξεν is "slaughter, cut the throat", the word for killing an animal; τοῦ πονηροῦ as in 2:13–14.
- **"Marvel not, my brethren, if the world hate you" → "Do not marvel, brothers, if the world hates you"** (v13) — Greek εἰ, "if"; no "my" in the critical text. NA28 begins καὶ μή, "And do not marvel"; SBLGNT, WH, Treg and RP have μή alone.
- **"He that loveth not his brother abideth in death" → "The one who does not love abides in death"** (v14) — NA28 ὁ μὴ ἀγαπῶν, with no object. The "we" (ἡμεῖς) at the start is emphatic.
- **"murderer" kept** (v15) — Greek ἀνθρωποκτόνος, "man-killer" (John 8:44), a different word from "slaughtered" in v12.
- **"Hereby perceive we the love of God, because he laid down his life for us" → "By this we have come to know love, that that one laid down his soul for us"** (v16) — Greek ἐγνώκαμεν τὴν ἀγάπην, a perfect, and "of God" is not in the text; τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτοῦ ἔθηκεν: ψυχή is "soul", the living self, a different word from "life" (ζωή) in v14–15.
- **"whoso hath this world's good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him" → "whoever has the livelihood of the world and beholds his brother having need and shuts his inward parts from him"** (v17) — Greek τὸν βίον τοῦ κόσμου, βίος as in 2:16; θεωρῇ "look at, behold"; τὰ σπλάγχνα, the inward parts as the seat of deep feeling (Phlm 7, 12, 20) — "heart" is a different Greek word (v19–21); "of compassion" is not in the Greek.
- **"in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth" → "in word nor with the tongue, but in work and truth"** (v18) — Greek ἐν ἔργῳ καὶ ἀληθείᾳ, one preposition; ἔργον "work" as in v8, 12.
- **"shall assure our hearts before him" → "before him we shall persuade our heart"** (v19) — Greek πείσομεν, "persuade"; τὴν καρδίαν, singular (SBLGNT, WH, NA28; Treg and RP have the plural). The verse begins καὶ ἐν τούτῳ, "And by this" (Treg, NA28, RP); SBLGNT and WH have ἐν τούτῳ, without "And".
- **"For if our heart condemn us, God is greater than our heart" → "that, if our heart condemns us, *Elohim* is greater than our heart"** (v20) — Greek ὅτι ἐὰν καταγινώσκῃ ἡμῶν ἡ καρδία, ὅτι μείζων ἐστὶν ὁ θεός: ὅτι stands twice. The verse reads the first as "that" (what the heart is persuaded of) and the second as taking it up again. Other readings: "because if our heart condemns us, [it is] because *Elohim* is greater", or ὅ τι ἐάν, "in whatever our heart condemns us, because *Elohim* is greater". καταγινώσκω "condemn" is literally "know against"; "knows" (γινώσκει) in the same verse is the same root.
- **"then have we confidence toward God" → "we have boldness toward *Elohim*"** (v21) — Greek παρρησίαν … πρὸς τὸν θεόν, as in 2:28; no "then". Greek ἡ καρδία μὴ καταγινώσκῃ ἡμῶν, "the heart does not condemn us" (SBLGNT, Treg). NA28: ἡ καρδία ἡμῶν μὴ καταγινώσκῃ, "our heart does not condemn"; WH: μὴ καταγινώσκῃ without ἡμῶν; RP has ἡμῶν twice.
- **"those things that are pleasing in his sight" → "the things that are pleasing before him"** (v22) — Greek τὰ ἀρεστὰ ἐνώπιον αὐτοῦ.
- **"That we should believe on the name of his Son" → "that we believe the name of his Son"** (v23) — Greek πιστεύσωμεν τῷ ὀνόματι, a dative without a preposition: "believe the name", not "believe in / on" (εἰς, as in 5:10, 13).
- **"as he gave us commandment" → "just as he gave us a commandment"** (v23) — Greek ἔδωκεν ἐντολήν.
- **"dwelleth in him, and he in him" kept as "abides in him, and he in him"** (v24) — Greek ἐν αὐτῷ μένει καὶ αὐτὸς ἐν αὐτῷ; the Greek has pronouns only and does not name *Elohim* here.
- **"by the Spirit which he hath given us" → "from the Spirit which he gave us"** (v24) — Greek ἐκ τοῦ πνεύματος οὗ ἡμῖν ἔδωκεν: ἐκ "from, out of", an aorist; "which" follows the neuter πνεῦμα; the gender is grammatical (as in Titus 3:6).
