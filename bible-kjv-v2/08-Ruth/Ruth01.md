# Ruth 1

[1] In the days when the judges ruled, there was a famine in the land. A certain man of Bethlehem in Judah went to sojourn in the country of Moab — he, his wife, and his two sons.

[2] The name of the man was Elimelech, and the name of his wife Naomi, and the names of his two sons Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites of Bethlehem in Judah. They came to the country of Moab and remained there.

[3] Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left with her two sons.

[4] They took wives from the Moabite women: the name of one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth; and they dwelt there about ten years.

[5] Mahlon and Chilion also died — both of them. The woman was left bereft of her two sons and her husband.

[6] Then she arose with her daughters-in-law to return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the country of Moab that Yahweh had visited his people in giving them bread.

[7] So she went out from the place where she had been, with her two daughters-in-law with her, and they went on the way to return to the land of Judah.

[8] Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May Yahweh deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with the dead and with me.

[9] May Yahweh grant that each of you may find rest in the house of her husband." Then she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices and wept.

[10] They said to her, "Surely we will return with you to your people."

[11] Naomi said, "Turn back, my daughters; why will you go with me? Are there yet sons in my womb that they may be your husbands?

[12] Turn back, my daughters, go your way, for I am too old to have a husband. Even if I should say I have hope — if I should take a husband this night and also bear sons —

[13] would you wait for them until they were grown? Would you shut yourselves off from having husbands? No, my daughters; for it is bitterer for me than for you, because the hand of Yahweh has gone out against me."

[14] They lifted up their voices and wept again; Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her.

[15] She said, "Behold, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods; return after your sister-in-law."

[16] But Ruth said, "Do not urge me to leave you, to turn back from following you. For where you go, I will go; and where you lodge, I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your *Elohim* my *Elohim*.

[17] Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May Yahweh do so to me, and more also, if anything but death parts me from you."

[18] When she saw that she was determined to go with her, she stopped speaking to her.

[19] So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. When they came to Bethlehem, the whole city was stirred because of them; the women said, "Is this Naomi?"

[20] She said to them, "Do not call me Naomi ("Pleasant"); call me Mara ("Bitter"), for Shaddai has dealt very bitterly with me.

[21] I went out full, and Yahweh has brought me back empty. Why do you call me Naomi, when Yahweh has testified against me, and Shaddai has brought calamity upon me?"

[22] So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess her daughter-in-law with her, who returned out of the country of Moab; they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"the Almighty" → "Shaddai"** (v20, 21) — Hebrew *Shaddai* kept.
- **"Mara / Naomi"** (v20) — Hebrew נָעֳמִי *No'omi* = "pleasant"; מָרָא *Mara* = "bitter." Etymology added.
- **"intreat me not" → "do not urge me"** (v16).
- **"stedfastly minded" → "determined"** (v18).
- **"steadfast love" Hebrew chesed** (v8) — translated as "deal kindly."
- **"hap" (KJV archaism — no longer used; elsewhere).

### Notes
- **Setting in Judges era** — Ruth takes place "when the judges ruled," providing bright contrast to the moral chaos of the Judges-appendix. A story of chesed (covenant faithfulness) amid faithlessness.
- **Names meaningful** — Elimelech "my El is king"; Mahlon "sickness"; Chilion "destruction"; Naomi "pleasant"; Ruth possibly "friend / companion"; Orpah "back of the neck / turning away."
- **Moab connection** — the Israelites' ancient enemy (Num 22–25) becomes the place of refuge and the origin of David's great-grandmother. Striking theological point: Moab incorporated into Davidic line.
- **Ruth's pledge** (v16–17) — one of the great loyalty-statements in world literature. Marriage-vow language repurposed for daughter-in-law loyalty.
- **Barley harvest** (v22) — March-April. Provides the agricultural setting for the gleaning narrative of ch 2.
