# Ruth 3

[1] Then ⟦naomi|Noomi|Naomi⟧ her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, should I not seek a resting place for you, that it may be well with you?

[2] And now, is not Boaz our kinsman, with whose young women you were? Behold, he is winnowing barley tonight at the threshing floor.

[3] Wash yourself, therefore, anoint yourself, put on your cloak, and go down to the threshing floor; but do not make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking.

[4] When he lies down, take note of the place where he lies; then go in, uncover his feet, and lie down. He will tell you what to do."

[5] She said to her, "All that you say to me I will do."

[6] So she went down to the threshing floor and did according to all that her mother-in-law had commanded her.

[7] When Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. She came softly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.

[8] At midnight the man was startled and turned himself; and behold, a woman was lying at his feet.

[9] He said, "Who are you?" And she answered, "I am ⟦ruth|Rut|Ruth⟧ your maidservant. Spread your wing over your maidservant, for you are a kinsman-redeemer."

[10] He said, "May you be blessed by ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, my daughter. You have made this latter kindness better than the first — in that you did not go after young men, whether poor or rich.

[11] And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you ask, for all the men of my people know that you are a woman of noble character.

[12] And now, it is true that I am a kinsman-redeemer; but there is another kinsman-redeemer nearer than I.

[13] Stay tonight; and in the morning, if he will redeem you, very well, let him redeem. But if he is not willing to redeem, I will redeem you, as ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ lives. Lie down until the morning."

[14] So she lay at his feet until the morning, and she rose up before one could recognize another. He said, "Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."

[15] He said, "Bring the cloak you are wearing and hold it out." When she held it out, he measured out six measures of barley and put it on her; then he went into the city.

[16] When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, "How did you fare, my daughter?" And she told her all that the man had done for her.

[17] She said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, for he said to me, 'You shall not go empty-handed to your mother-in-law.'"

[18] Then she said, "Sit still, my daughter, until you know how the matter falls out, for the man will not rest but will settle the matter today."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"raiment" → "cloak"** (v3).
- **"near kinsman" → "kinsman-redeemer"** (v9, 12) — *go'el*.
- **"spread therefore thy skirt" → "spread your wing"** (v9) — Hebrew וּפָרַשְׂתָּ כְנָפֶךָ *u-farasta khenafekha*. Lit. "spread your wing" — *kanaf* means wing / skirt / corner of garment. Double meaning: (1) marital-protection gesture (spreading the cloak over a woman = claiming her as wife; cf. Ezek 16:8); (2) echoes 2:12 "under whose wings you have come for refuge" — Boaz is invited to be the physical embodiment of Yahweh's protective wing.
- **"virtuous woman" → "woman of noble character"** (v11) — Hebrew אֵשֶׁת חַיִל *eshet chayil*. Same phrase as Prov 31:10.
- **"as Yahweh lives"** (v13) — oath formula.
- **"how did you fare" → "How did you fare"** (v16) — Hebrew literally "Who are you, my daughter?" — a slightly odd phrase suggesting Naomi asking both identity and status: "How did it go?"

### Notes
- **The threshing floor scene** — culturally charged. Threshing floors were sites of celebration, and sometimes illicit behavior (cf. Hos 9:1). Naomi's plan is bold but risk-conscious (after his meal, at night, discreet).
- **"Uncover his feet"** (v4, 7) — Hebrew *margelotav*. The foot-area. Not explicitly sexual, though the euphemism possibility exists. The narrative is deliberately ambiguous; Ruth acts, Boaz responds honorably.
- **"Spread your wing"** (v9) — Ruth's proposition. A formal request for covering / marriage.
- **The nearer kinsman** (v12) — plot complication. Boaz cannot simply act on Ruth's request; legal priority must first be addressed.
- **Six measures of barley** (v15) — a gift to Naomi, also a discreet signal that negotiations are in progress. "Not empty-handed" echoes 1:21 ("he brought me back empty").
- **Boaz's character** — exemplary throughout: covers for Ruth's reputation; acts promptly; respects the legal order (v12–13). The opposite of the moral chaos of Judges.
