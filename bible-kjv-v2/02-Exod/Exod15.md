# Exodus 15

[1] Then Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to Yahweh, saying:

"I will sing to Yahweh, for he has triumphed gloriously;
the horse and its rider he has hurled into the sea.

[2] Yah is my strength and my song;
he has become my salvation.
This is my *Elohim*, and I will praise him;
my father's *Elohim*, and I will exalt him.

[3] Yahweh is a warrior;
Yahweh is his name.

[4] Pharaoh's chariots and his army he has cast into the sea,
and his chosen officers are sunk in the Sea of Reeds.

[5] The deeps covered them;
they went down into the depths like a stone.

[6] Your right hand, O Yahweh, is majestic in power;
your right hand, O Yahweh, shatters the enemy.

[7] In the greatness of your majesty you threw down your adversaries;
you sent forth your burning wrath — it consumed them like stubble.

[8] By the blast of your nostrils the waters were piled up;
the floods stood up like a heap;
the deeps congealed in the heart of the sea.

[9] The enemy said,
'I will pursue, I will overtake,
I will divide the spoil,
my throat shall be filled with them;
I will draw my sword,
my hand shall dispossess them.'

[10] You blew with your wind — the sea covered them;
they sank like lead in the mighty waters.

[11] Who is like you among the gods, O Yahweh?
Who is like you — majestic in holiness,
fearful in praises, doing wonders?

[12] You stretched out your right hand —
the earth swallowed them.

[13] In your lovingkindness you led the people whom you redeemed;
in your strength you guided them to your holy dwelling.

[14] Peoples have heard; they tremble;
anguish has seized the inhabitants of Philistia.

[15] Then were the chiefs of Edom dismayed;
trembling seized the leaders of Moab;
all the inhabitants of Canaan melted away.

[16] Terror and dread fall upon them;
by the greatness of your arm they are still as a stone —
until your people pass by, O Yahweh,
until the people whom you have acquired pass by.

[17] You will bring them in and plant them
on the mountain of your inheritance,
the place, O Yahweh, which you made for your dwelling,
the sanctuary, O Lord, which your hands established.

[18] Yahweh shall reign forever and ever."

[19] For the horse of Pharaoh with his chariots and his cavalry went into the sea, and Yahweh brought back the waters of the sea upon them; but the children of Israel walked on dry ground in the midst of the sea.

[20] Then Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel in her hand; and all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances.

[21] And Miriam answered them:

"Sing to Yahweh, for he has triumphed gloriously;
the horse and its rider he has hurled into the sea."

[22] Moses led Israel onward from the Sea of Reeds, and they went out into the wilderness of Shur; they went three days in the wilderness and found no water.

[23] When they came to Marah, they could not drink the waters of Marah because they were bitter; therefore its name was called Marah.

[24] The people grumbled against Moses, saying, "What shall we drink?"

[25] He cried out to Yahweh, and Yahweh showed him a tree, and he threw it into the water, and the water became sweet.

There he made for them a statute and an ordinance, and there he tested them,

[26] and said, "If you will diligently listen to the voice of Yahweh your *Elohim*, and do what is right in his eyes, and give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, then I will put none of the diseases upon you that I put upon the Egyptians; for I am Yahweh, your healer."

[27] Then they came to Elim, where there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees; and they encamped there beside the waters.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Red sea" → "Sea of Reeds"** (v4, 22) — Hebrew יַם־סוּף *Yam Suph*, as in 10:19.
- **"the LORD is my strength and song" → "Yah is my strength and my song"** (v2) — Hebrew עָזִּי וְזִמְרָת יָהּ *ozzi wezimrat Yah*. *Yah* is the short form of YHWH (used in names like Hallelu-Yah "praise Yah"). Preserved as distinct form.
- **"habitation" → "praise"** (v2) — Hebrew וְאַנְוֵהוּ *we-anwehu*. Debated:
  1. From נָוָה *nawah* II = adorn / beautify / praise (traditional for this context).
  2. From נָוָה *nawah* I = dwell / make a dwelling (KJV).
  Most modern commentators favor "praise / glorify" — the immediate parallelism ("I will exalt him") fits praise-verbs. KJV's "prepare him a habitation" is a minority reading.
- **"man of war" → "warrior"** (v3) — Hebrew אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה *ish milchamah*. Literal "man of battle" = warrior.
- **"depths" (v5)** — Hebrew תְּהֹמֹת *tehomot* = primal deeps (plural of *tehom*, Gen 1:2). The cosmic waters of chaos swallow the pursuing army — a creation-reversal for Egypt.
- **"my lust shall be satisfied" → "my throat shall be filled"** (v9) — Hebrew תִּמְלָאֵמוֹ נַפְשִׁי *timla'emo naphshi*. *Nephesh* here = throat / gullet (the physical appetite-seat). Not "lust" in the sexual sense; the enemy's insatiable appetite for plunder.
- **"destroy them" → "dispossess them"** (v9) — Hebrew תּוֹרִישֵׁמוֹ יָדִי *torishemo yadi* from *yarash* = dispossess / drive out. Conquest vocabulary.
- **"gods" (v11)** — Hebrew אֵלִים *elim*. Divine beings / rival deities. Yahweh incomparable among the divine council.
- **"mercy" → "lovingkindness"** (v13) — Hebrew חֶסֶד *chesed*, as in Gen 24.
- **"Palestina" → "Philistia"** (v14) — Hebrew פְּלָשֶׁת *Pelesheth*. The coastal region. "Palestina" in KJV reflects the Latinized form.
- **"dukes of Edom" → "chiefs of Edom"** (v15) — Hebrew אַלּוּפֵי אֱדוֹם *allufe Edom*. As in Gen 36.
- **"holy habitation" → "holy dwelling"** (v13) — Hebrew נְוֵה קָדְשֶׁךָ *neweh qodshekha*. The goal of the exodus: dwelling with Yahweh in a holy place. Ultimately the temple.
- **"murmured" → "grumbled"** (v24) — Hebrew וַיִּלֹּנוּ *wa-yillonu* from *lun* = complain / grumble. The first of many wilderness complaints.
- **"Marah — *bitter* gloss preserved** (v23) — Hebrew מָרָה *Marah* = "bitter" (same as the place name Naomi takes for herself in Ruth 1:20).
- **"healeth" → "healer"** (v26) — Hebrew אֲנִי יְהוָה רֹפְאֶךָ *ani Yahweh rofe'ekha*. Divine self-revelation as healer. First use of the "Yahweh Rofe" title.

### Notes

- **The Song of the Sea** — one of the oldest poetic passages in the Bible (scholarly consensus: possibly 12th–11th century BCE composition), preserved in archaic Hebrew. It celebrates Yahweh's victory over Pharaoh as a cosmic-scale triumph — Yahweh the divine warrior defeating chaos waters as at creation, and the nations who will oppose Israel trembling in anticipation.
- **Miriam's song** (v20–21) — a brief antiphonal response. Miriam is called "the prophetess" here — one of the few women in the OT with that title. She leads the women in timbrel-and-dance celebration.
- **Bitter waters and healing** (v22–26) — immediate post-exodus test: no water, then bitter water. The "tree" that sweetens the water has been read typologically (Christian tradition: the cross). The theological point: Yahweh who delivered from Egypt is also the healer of his people, contingent on their listening.
- **Twelve springs / seventy palms** (v27) — numerological echo: twelve tribes, seventy elders (and seventy descendants of Jacob, Gen 46:27). Elim as oasis of fullness after the bitter waters.
