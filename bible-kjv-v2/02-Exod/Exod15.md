# Exodus 15

[1] Then ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ sang this song to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, and they said, saying:

"I will sing to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, for he is surely exalted;
the horse and its rider he has thrown into the sea.

[2] My strength and song is ⟦yah|Yah|the LORD⟧,
and he has become my salvation.
This is my ⟦el|El|God⟧, and I will praise him,
the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of my father, and I will extol him.

[3] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ is a man of war;
⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ is his name.

[4] The chariots of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ and his force he has cast into the sea,
and the choice of his officers are sunk in the ⟦red-sea|Sea of Reeds|Red Sea⟧.

[5] Deeps cover them;
they went down into the depths like a stone.

[6] Your right hand, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, is majestic in power;
your right hand, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, shatters the enemy.

[7] And in the abundance of your exaltation you throw down those who rise against you;
you send out your burning anger; it consumes them like stubble.

[8] And by the breath of your nostrils waters were piled up;
streams stood like a heap;
deeps congealed in the heart of the sea.

[9] The enemy said,
'I will pursue, I will overtake,
I will divide spoil;
my soul shall be filled with them;
I will draw my sword;
my hand shall dispossess them.'

[10] You blew with your wind; the sea covered them;
they sank like lead in mighty waters.

[11] Who is like you among the gods, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧?
Who is like you, majestic in holiness,
fearsome in praises, doing wonders?

[12] You stretched out your right hand;
the earth swallows them.

[13] You led in your ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ the people that you redeemed;
you guided them in your strength to your holy habitation.

[14] Peoples heard; they are agitated;
anguish seized the dwellers of ⟦philistia|Peleshet|Philistia⟧.

[15] Then the chiefs of Edom were dismayed;
the leaders of ⟦moab|Moav|Moab⟧, trembling seizes them;
all the dwellers of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧ melted away.

[16] Dread and fear fall upon them;
by the greatness of your arm they are still as a stone,
until your people pass over, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧,
until the people that you got pass over.

[17] You will bring them in and plant them
on the mountain of your inheritance,
the established place for your dwelling that you have wrought, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧,
the sanctuary, ⟦adonai|Adonai|Lord⟧, that your hands have established.

[18] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ shall reign forever and ever."

[19] For the horse of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ went with his chariots and with his horsemen into the sea, and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ brought back upon them the waters of the sea; and the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ walked on the dry land in the midst of the sea.

[20] And ⟦miriam|Miryam|Miriam⟧ the prophetess, the sister of ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧, took the timbrel in her hand, and all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances.

[21] And ⟦miriam|Miryam|Miriam⟧ answered them:

"Sing to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, for he is surely exalted;

[22] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ made ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ journey from the ⟦red-sea|Sea of Reeds|Red Sea⟧, and they went out to the wilderness of Shur; and they went three days in the wilderness and did not find water.

[23] And they came to Marah, and they were not able to drink the waters of Marah, for they were bitter; therefore its name was called Marah.

[24] And the people grumbled against ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, saying, "What shall we drink?"

[26] And he said, "If you will surely listen to the voice of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and do what is right in his eyes, and give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, all the sickness that I put on ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ I will not put on you, for I am ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, your healer."

[27] And they came to Elim, and there were twelve springs of water there and seventy palm trees; and they camped there by the waters.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved. In the song the name is spoken to directly (v6, 11, 16, 17); the Hebrew has no "O".
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v2, 26) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים. **"my God" → "my El"** (v2) — Hebrew אֵלִי *Eli*; "the *Elohim* of my father" in the next line is אֱלֹהֵי אָבִי.
- **The song (v1–18)** — poetry: one Hebrew line (colon) to a line. The tenses follow the Hebrew forms: perfects are past or perfect ("he has thrown", "they went down", "you led"), imperfects are present ("cover", "shatters", "throw down", "swallows", "seizes", "fall") and in v17–18 future. In old Hebrew poetry the imperfect often tells of past events, so the imperfects of v5–7, 12, 14–16 may also be read as past ("covered", "shattered", "swallowed"). The Hebrew of the song has almost no articles; "the" is supplied where English needs it.
- **"Then sang Moses … and spake, saying" → "Then … sang … and they said, saying"** (v1) — Hebrew אָז יָשִׁיר (an imperfect after אָז "then") and וַיֹּאמְרוּ לֵאמֹר.
- **"he hath triumphed gloriously" → "he is surely exalted"** (v1, 21) — Hebrew גָאֹה גָּאָה, an infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "rising he has risen": גָּאָה is "rise up, be high, be exalted". "Triumph" is not in the word. The noun of the same root is "exaltation" in v7.
- **"the horse and his rider" → "the horse and its rider"** (v1, 21) — Hebrew סוּס וְרֹכְבוֹ; רֹכֵב is one who rides, on a horse or in a chariot. **"thrown"** — Hebrew רָמָה; "cast" in v4 is another verb (יָרָה).
- **"The LORD is my strength and song" → "My strength and song is Yah"** (v2) — Hebrew עָזִּי וְזִמְרָת יָהּ. "Strength" (v2, 13) is עֹז; "power" (v6) is כֹּחַ. יָהּ *Yah* is the short form of the name Yahweh, here for the first time in the Bible. זִמְרָת is uncertain: (1) "song" (from זָמַר "make music"), so the tradition; (2) "might, protection" (a second root זמר), which gives "my strength and might". The word has no "my" in the Masoretic Text (the Samaritan text reads "my song", cited from memory); in English "my" covers both nouns. The line recurs in Isa 12:2; Ps 118:14.
- **"he is become my salvation" → "he has become my salvation"** (v2) — Hebrew וַיְהִי־לִי לִישׁוּעָה, literally "and he became to me for salvation".
- **"I will prepare him an habitation" → "I will praise him"** (v2) — Hebrew וְאַנְוֵהוּ, found only here; the meaning is uncertain: (1) "praise, adorn" (related to נָאוֶה "beautiful"), the best-supported sense and that of the Greek ("glorify", cited from memory); (2) "give him a dwelling" (from נָוֶה "habitation", v13), so KJV. "Praises" in v11 is another word (תְּהִלֹּת). **"exalt" → "extol"** — Hebrew רוֹמֵם, "lift high"; "be exalted" is kept for the verb of v1.
- **"a man of war" kept** (v3) — Hebrew אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה, literally so.
- **"his host" → "his force"** (v4) — Hebrew חֵיל, as in 14:4. **"his chosen captains also are drowned" → "the choice of his officers are sunk"** — Hebrew וּמִבְחַר שָׁלִשָׁיו טֻבְּעוּ; שָׁלִישׁ is "third man", an officer (14:7); טֻבְּעוּ is a passive, "have been sunk".
- **"Red sea" → "Sea of Reeds"** (v4, 22) — Hebrew יַם־סוּף *Yam Suf*.
- **"The depths have covered them: they sank into the bottom" → "Deeps cover them; they went down into the depths"** (v5) — Hebrew תְּהֹמֹת, the plural of תְּהוֹם "the deep" (Gen 1:2), and מְצוֹלֹת "depths". "Cover" is an imperfect, "went down" a perfect. "Sank" in v10 is another verb.
- **"is become glorious in power" → "is majestic in power"** (v6) — Hebrew נֶאְדָּרִי בַּכֹּחַ, a participle with an old ending; "is" is supplied. The same word is "majestic in holiness" in v11, and the adjective of its root is "mighty" of the waters in v10 (אַדִּירִים). **"hath dashed in pieces" → "shatters"** — an imperfect.
- **"in the greatness of thine excellency thou hast overthrown them that rose up against thee" → "in the abundance of your exaltation you throw down those who rise against you"** (v7) — Hebrew רֹב "abundance" and גְּאוֹנְךָ, from the verb "be exalted" of v1. **"thy wrath" → "your burning anger"** — Hebrew חֲרֹנְךָ, literally "your burning".
- **"with the blast of thy nostrils" → "by the breath of your nostrils"** (v8) — Hebrew בְּרוּחַ אַפֶּיךָ. רוּחַ is "breath", "wind" and "spirit" (it is "wind" in v10); אַפַּיִם is "nostrils" and also "anger": "the wind of your anger" is the other reading.
- **"were gathered together" → "were piled up"** (v8) — Hebrew נֶעֶרְמוּ, found only here. **"the floods stood upright as an heap" → "streams stood like a heap"** — Hebrew נֹזְלִים, "flowing ones", and נֵד, a heap or dam of water (Josh 3:13, 16; Ps 78:13).
- **"my lust shall be satisfied upon them" → "my soul shall be filled with them"** (v9) — Hebrew תִּמְלָאֵמוֹ נַפְשִׁי. נֶפֶשׁ is "soul", here as the seat of appetite and desire (the word is also used of the throat). **"I will draw my sword"** — literally "I will empty out my sword". **"shall destroy them" → "shall dispossess them"** — Hebrew תּוֹרִישֵׁמוֹ, the causative of יָרַשׁ "possess": drive out of what they hold.
- **"Thou didst blow with thy wind"** (v10) — Hebrew בְּרוּחֲךָ, the word rendered "breath" in v8.
- **"among the gods"** (v11) — Hebrew בָּאֵלִם, the plural of אֵל *El*: divine beings. **"glorious in holiness" → "majestic in holiness"** — Hebrew נֶאְדָּר בַּקֹּדֶשׁ; בַּקֹּדֶשׁ can also be "among the holy ones" or "in the sanctuary". **"fearful in praises" → "fearsome in praises"** — Hebrew נוֹרָא תְהִלֹּת, "to be feared", with "praises": feared for his praiseworthy deeds, or feared even while he is praised. **"doing wonders"** — Hebrew עֹשֵׂה פֶלֶא, a singular: "doing a wonder".
- **"the earth swallowed them" → "the earth swallows them"** (v12) — an imperfect.
- **"Thou in thy mercy hast led forth" → "You led in your *chesed*"** (v13) — Hebrew חֶסֶד: faithful, loyal love. **"the people which thou hast redeemed"** — Hebrew גָּאָלְתָּ, to redeem as a kinsman does. עַם־זוּ has the old relative זוּ, "the people that" (so also v16).
- **"thou hast guided them … unto thy holy habitation"** (v13) — Hebrew נֵהַלְתָּ, to lead gently, as to water; "them" is supplied. נְוֵה קָדְשֶׁךָ is literally "the habitation of your holiness": נָוֶה is a pasture, an abode of shepherds and flocks.
- **"The people shall hear, and be afraid" → "Peoples heard; they are agitated"** (v14) — Hebrew שָׁמְעוּ עַמִּים יִרְגָּזוּן, a perfect and an imperfect. רָגַז is to be shaken, to tremble, in fear or in anger. **"sorrow shall take hold on" → "anguish seized"** — Hebrew חִיל, the writhing of a woman in labor. **"the inhabitants of Palestina" → "the dwellers of Philistia"** — Hebrew יֹשְׁבֵי פְּלָשֶׁת.
- **"the dukes of Edom shall be amazed" → "the chiefs of Edom were dismayed"** (v15) — Hebrew אַלּוּפֵי אֱדוֹם (Gen 36:15) and נִבְהֲלוּ, a perfect. **"the mighty men of Moab" → "the leaders of Moab"** — Hebrew אֵילֵי, literally "the rams of Moab": the word for a ram is used of leading men. **"trembling"** — Hebrew רַעַד.
- **"Fear and dread shall fall upon them" → "Dread and fear fall upon them"** (v16) — Hebrew אֵימָתָה וָפַחַד; both are words for terror, and neither is the usual "fear" (יִרְאָה). **"they shall be as still as a stone" → "they are still as a stone"** — Hebrew יִדְּמוּ, "be still" or "be silent".
- **"till thy people pass over"** (v16) — Hebrew עָבַר: pass over, or pass by. **"which thou hast purchased" → "that you got"** — Hebrew קָנִיתָ, "get, acquire, buy", as in Gen 4:1; 14:19. Of God the sense "create" is also defended (Deut 32:6).
- **"Thou shalt bring them in"** (v17) — imperfects, here future: the song looks beyond the sea. Some read them as past. **"the place … which thou hast made for thee to dwell in" → "the established place for your dwelling that you have wrought"** — Hebrew מָכוֹן לְשִׁבְתְּךָ פָּעַלְתָּ. מָכוֹן is a firm, fixed place, from the root of "established" in the next line (כּוֹנְנוּ); שֶׁבֶת is "sitting, dwelling"; פָּעַל is "work, make" (another verb than עָשָׂה).
- **"the Sanctuary, O Lord" → "the sanctuary, Adonai"** (v17) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי in the Masoretic Text; many Hebrew manuscripts and the Samaritan text have the name Yahweh here (cited from memory).
- **"for ever and ever"** (v18) — Hebrew לְעֹלָם וָעֶד, literally "to the age and onward".
- **"the horse of Pharaoh went in with his chariots and with his horsemen"** (v19) — Hebrew סוּס, a collective singular. **"brought again" → "brought back"**; **"on dry land" → "on the dry land"** — Hebrew יַבָּשָׁה, as in 14:22.
- **"a timbrel" → "the timbrel"** (v20) — the Hebrew has the article. תֹּף is a hand drum. "Answered them" (v21): "them" is masculine in the Hebrew.
- **"So Moses brought Israel from the Red sea" → "And Moses made Israel journey from the Sea of Reeds"** (v22) — Hebrew וַיַּסַּע, the causative of נָסַע "journey".
- **"the waters of Marah, for they were bitter"** (v23) — Hebrew מָרָה *Marah* is "bitter"; מַיִם "waters" is a plural. "Its name was called" is literally "he (one) called its name".
- **"murmured" → "grumbled"** (v24) — Hebrew וַיִּלֹּנוּ.
- **"shewed him a tree"** (v25) — Hebrew וַיּוֹרֵהוּ, "taught him, pointed out to him": the verb from which תּוֹרָה *torah* comes. עֵץ is "a tree" or "a piece of wood". **"were made sweet" → "became sweet"**.
- **"there he made for them a statute and an ordinance, and there he proved them" → "There he set for him a statute and an ordinance, and there he tested him"** (v25) — the Hebrew pronouns are singular: לוֹ "for him", נִסָּהוּ "he tested him"; the people as one (or Moses). Who "he" is the verse does not say: Yahweh, as v26 shows. מִשְׁפָּט is "judgment", here a ruling. נִסָּה is "test", not "tempt".
- **"If thou wilt diligently hearken" → "If you will surely listen"** (v26) — Hebrew שָׁמוֹעַ תִּשְׁמַע, an infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "hearing you hear". **"that which is right in his sight" → "what is right in his eyes"** — Hebrew הַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינָיו.
- **"I will put none of these diseases upon thee, which I have brought upon the Egyptians" → "all the sickness that I put on Egypt I will not put on you"** (v26) — Hebrew כָּל־הַמַּחֲלָה, a singular; one verb, שִׂים "put", both times; מִצְרַיִם is "Egypt".
- **"the LORD that healeth thee" → "Yahweh, your healer"** (v26) — Hebrew אֲנִי יְהוָה רֹפְאֶךָ, a participle: "the one healing you".
- **"wells of water" → "springs of water"** (v27) — Hebrew עֵינֹת, the plural of עַיִן "spring". **"encamped" → "camped"**.

### Notes

- **The Song of the Sea** — one of the oldest poetic passages in the Bible (often dated very early for its archaic Hebrew: old suffixes such as *-mo*, the relative *zu*, missing articles). It celebrates Yahweh's victory over Pharaoh at the sea and looks on to the trembling of the nations and to the mountain of Yahweh's inheritance.
- **Miriam's song** (v20–21) — a brief antiphonal response. Miriam is called "the prophetess" here — one of the few women in the OT with that title. She leads the women in timbrel-and-dance celebration.
- **Bitter waters and healing** (v22–26) — immediate post-exodus test: no water, then bitter water. The "tree" that sweetens the water has been read typologically (Christian tradition: the cross). The theological point: Yahweh who delivered from Egypt is also the healer of his people, contingent on their listening.
- **Twelve springs / seventy palms** (v27) — numerological echo: twelve tribes, seventy elders (and seventy descendants of Jacob, Gen 46:27). Elim as oasis of fullness after the bitter waters.
