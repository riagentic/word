## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"rod" → "staff"** (v2, 4, 17, 20) — Hebrew מַטֶּה *matteh*. Shepherd's staff — functional tool, badge of tribal leadership, symbol that Yahweh co-opts for the signs. "Rod" in modern English = thin stick for punishment; obscures the meaning.
- **"leprous as snow" → "diseased, like snow"** (v6) — Hebrew מְצֹרַעַת כַּשָּׁלֶג *metsora'at ka-shaleg*. *Tsara'at* is not the modern medical "leprosy" (Hansen's disease) but a broader category of skin-disease / ritually impure conditions (Lev 13–14). "Diseased" more accurate than specific "leprosy."
- **"slow of speech, and of a slow tongue" → "heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue"** (v10) — Hebrew כְבַד־פֶּה וּכְבַד לָשׁוֹן *kevad-peh u-khevad lashon*. *Kaved* = heavy. Moses describes himself as inarticulate / halting. Could mean stutter, speech impediment, or simply lack of rhetorical skill.
- **"dumb" → "mute"** (v11) — modernized ("dumb" in modern English = stupid, offensive for speech-impaired).
- **"O my Lord" → "O Lord"** (v10, 13) — Hebrew בִּי אֲדֹנָי *bi Adonai*. *Bi* = "excuse me / I beg of you." *Adonai* = my Lord. Honorific address to God.
- **"an ass" → "a donkey"** (v20) — modernized.
- **"harden his heart"** (v21) — Hebrew אֲחַזֵּק אֶת־לִבּוֹ *achazzeq et-libbo*. *Chazaq* = strengthen / harden. Key term throughout the plague narrative. Three verbs used: *chazaq* (strengthen), *kaved* (make heavy), *qashah* (make stubborn). Theological tension: does Yahweh harden Pharaoh's heart, or does Pharaoh harden his own, or both? The text uses all formulations across the chapters.
- **"bloody husband" → "bridegroom of blood"** (v25, 26) — Hebrew חֲתַן־דָּמִים *chatan-damim*. *Chatan* = bridegroom / son-in-law. The enigmatic phrase has occupied interpreters for millennia. Possible readings:
  1. "Blood-bridegroom" — circumcised male is "bloody-bridegroom" in a rite-of-passage sense.
  2. Zipporah protects Moses by enacting his vicarious circumcision through their son.
  3. A ritual formula surviving from an ancient Midianite / Kenite practice.
  Preserved as Hebrew idiom since meaning is genuinely obscure.
- **"sharp stone" → "flint"** (v25) — Hebrew צֹר *tsor* = flint / sharp stone. Traditional circumcision instrument before metal blades — archaically solemn, ritually correct.
- **"in the inn" → "at a lodging place"** (v24) — Hebrew בַּמָּלוֹן *ba-malon*. Traveler's rest-place. Not an inn in the commercial sense.

### Notes

- **The strange episode (v24–26)** — one of the most cryptic passages in the Hebrew Bible. Yahweh attacks Moses (or possibly his son; the pronouns are ambiguous) and Zipporah saves by circumcising their son. Interpretive possibilities:
  1. Moses had neglected to circumcise his son; Yahweh demands it before the commissioning proceeds.
  2. The phrase marks a rite of passage; the episode is a fragment of older ritual memory.
  3. A test that parallels Abraham's (ch 22) — radical obedience required at the threshold of commission.
  The text leaves it starkly unexplained.
- **Aaron as spokesman** — from here on, Aaron speaks publicly while Moses receives revelation. The mouth / heart division anticipates later priestly / prophetic distinctions.
- **"Israel is my son, my firstborn" (v22)** — covenantal naming. The plague of the firstborn (12:29) will mirror this: Egypt killed the Hebrew sons; Yahweh will kill Egyptian firstborn as judgment for refusing to release his firstborn-people.
