# Exodus 2

[1] A man of the house of Levi went and married a daughter of Levi.

[2] The woman conceived and bore a son; and when she saw that he was a fine child, she hid him three months.

[3] When she could hide him no longer, she took for him a basket of papyrus reeds, coated it with bitumen and pitch, put the child in it, and placed it among the reeds by the bank of the Nile.

[4] His sister stationed herself at a distance to see what would be done to him.

[5] The daughter of Pharaoh came down to bathe at the Nile, and her attendants walked along by the Nile. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her maid to fetch it.

[6] When she opened it, she saw the child — and behold, the boy was crying. She had compassion on him and said, "This is one of the Hebrews' children."

[7] Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call for you a nurse from the Hebrew women, who may nurse the child for you?"

[8] Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Go." So the young woman went and called the child's mother.

[9] Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will give you your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

[10] When the child grew up, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She called his name Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."

[11] In those days, when Moses had grown up, he went out to his brothers and looked on their forced labor. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his brothers.

[12] He looked this way and that, and when he saw that there was no one, he struck down the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

[13] When he went out the second day, behold, two Hebrews were fighting each other. He said to the one in the wrong, "Why are you striking your fellow?"

[14] He said, "Who made you a ruler and a judge over us? Are you thinking of killing me as you killed the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid and said, "Surely the matter is known."

[15] When Pharaoh heard of the matter, he sought to kill Moses; but Moses fled from the face of Pharaoh and dwelt in the land of Midian. He sat down by a well.

[16] Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters; they came, drew water, and filled the troughs to water their father's flock.

[17] But shepherds came and drove them away; so Moses stood up and saved them, and watered their flock.

[18] When they came to Reuel their father, he said, "How is it that you have come back so soon today?"

[19] They said, "An Egyptian delivered us from the hand of the shepherds, and also drew enough water for us and watered the flock."

[20] He said to his daughters, "Where is he? Why is it that you have left the man? Call him, that he may eat bread."

[21] And Moses was willing to dwell with the man; and he gave his daughter Zipporah to Moses.

[22] She bore a son, and he called his name Gershom, for he said, "I have become a sojourner in a foreign land."

[23] In the course of those many days, the king of Egypt died. The children of Israel groaned because of the forced labor, and they cried out; and their cry went up to *Elohim* because of the forced labor.

[24] *Elohim* heard their groaning, and *Elohim* remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.

[25] *Elohim* saw the children of Israel, and *Elohim* took notice.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"goodly child" → "fine child"** (v2) — Hebrew טוֹב *tov*. Often read as "good / beautiful." Same word used in Gen 1's refrain "*Elohim* saw that it was good." The baby carries the creational "good."
- **"ark of bulrushes" → "basket of papyrus reeds"** (v3) — Hebrew תֵּבָה גֹּמֶא *tevat gome*. *Tevah* is the same word as Noah's ark (Gen 6:14) — a deliberate echo: Moses' basket, like Noah's ark, is the small vessel of salvation through water. *Gome* = papyrus. Preserved with modernized material-terms.
- **"slime and pitch" → "bitumen and pitch"** (v3) — Hebrew בַּחֵמָר וּבַזָּפֶת *ba-chemar u-va-zafet*. As in Gen 11:3. Waterproofing.
- **"flags" → "reeds"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew בַּסּוּף *ba-suph*. *Suph* = reeds of the Nile. Same word as the "Sea of Reeds" (*Yam Suph*) that Israel will later cross. Subtle foreshadowing: Moses is saved through the *suph*; the nation will be saved through the *Yam Suph*.
- **"to wit" → "to see"** (v4) — archaic → modern.
- **"Moses" — Hebrew etymology preserved** (v10) — Hebrew מֹשֶׁה *Mosheh*, from *mashah* "to draw out." Pharaoh's daughter says "because *meshithihu* (I drew him out)." Striking: the Egyptian princess gives the Hebrew child a name from Hebrew etymology (though Egyptian scholars note *Mose* also occurs in Egyptian royal names — "born of" — suggesting a bilingual pun). Hebrew etymology made explicit.
- **"burdens" → "forced labor"** (v11) — Hebrew סִבְלֹתָם *sivlotam* = heavy loads / corvée labor. Same root as in 1:11.
- **"smiting" → "beating"** (v11) — Hebrew מַכֶּה *makkeh*. Physical assault, possibly to death.
- **"slew" → "struck down"** (v12) — same verb *hikkah* (hiphil of *nakah*). Moses' action parallels the Egyptian's.
- **"him that did the wrong" → "the one in the wrong"** (v13) — Hebrew לָרָשָׁע *la-rasha* = the wicked one. Moses identifies the aggressor.
- **"Wherefore smitest thou" → "Why are you striking"** (v13) — modernized.
- **"a prince and a judge" → "a ruler and a judge"** (v14) — Hebrew לְשַׂר וְשֹׁפֵט *le-sar we-shofet*. *Sar* = prince / ruler / official; *shofet* = judge / governor. Rhetorical rejection of Moses' self-appointed authority.
- **"Reuel" / "Jethro"** (v18) — the Midianite priest has two names in Exodus: here Reuel (2:18), later Jethro (3:1). Source criticism treats as different traditions; canonically read, possibly two names for one person (common in the ANE). 18:1 also uses "Jethro."
- **"Gershom — etymology preserved** (v22) — Hebrew גֵּרְשֹׁם *Gershom* from *ger* (resident alien) + *sham* (there). Moses' self-identification as a perpetual sojourner reflects his liminal identity: raised Egyptian, fleeing Egypt, now a ger in Midian.
- **"process of time" → "course of those many days"** (v23) — Hebrew בַּיָּמִים הָרַבִּים הָהֵם *ba-yamim ha-rabbim hahem* = in those many days. Moses spends a long time in Midian (40 years per later tradition, Acts 7:30).
- **"bondage" → "forced labor"** (v23) — Hebrew עֲבֹדָה *avodah*. Continuing the motif.
- **"groaning" (v24)** — Hebrew נַאֲקָתָם *na'aqatam*. Groaning under oppression.
- **"remembered his covenant" (v24)** — Hebrew וַיִּזְכֹּר אֱלֹהִים אֶת־בְּרִיתוֹ *wa-yizkor *Elohim* et berito*. The covenantal "remembering" — divine action, as in Gen 8:1, 19:29, 30:22.
- **"had respect unto them" → "took notice"** (v25) — Hebrew וַיֵּדַע אֱלֹהִים *wa-yeda Elohim*. *Yada* here = "knew" in the engaged sense — recognized / acknowledged / took knowledge-with-action toward. Not just observation; relational knowing that presages intervention.

### Notes

- **Moses' three 40-year segments** — later Jewish tradition divides Moses' 120 years into three parts: 40 in Egypt, 40 in Midian, 40 leading Israel. Exodus narrative confirms the 40 years in Midian (7:7 gives his age at the commissioning).
- **"Forty years in the wilderness" as formation** — the years in Midian shape Moses from displaced Egyptian prince into a shepherd-leader. The shepherd's staff he will carry into Pharaoh's court originates here.
- **Four divine verbs close the chapter** — "heard… remembered… saw… knew" (v24–25). The cascade of divine attention after long silence sets up the theophany of ch 3.
