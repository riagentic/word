# Exodus 10

[1] Yahweh said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh, for I have hardened his heart and the heart of his servants, that I may show these my signs among them,

[2] and that you may tell in the ears of your son and your grandson what I have done in Egypt, and my signs that I have done among them — that you may know that I am Yahweh."

[3] So Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh and said to him, "Thus says Yahweh, the *Elohim* of the Hebrews: 'How long will you refuse to humble yourself before me? Let my people go, that they may serve me.

[4] Or else, if you refuse to let my people go, behold, tomorrow I will bring locusts into your territory.

[5] They shall cover the surface of the land, so that one will not be able to see the land; and they shall eat the rest of what escaped — what remains to you from the hail — and they shall eat every tree growing for you from the field.

[6] They shall fill your houses, the houses of all your servants, and the houses of all the Egyptians — something that neither your fathers nor your fathers' fathers have seen since the day they were upon the earth until now.'" Then he turned and went out from Pharaoh.

[7] Pharaoh's servants said to him, "How long shall this man be a snare to us? Let the men go, that they may serve Yahweh their *Elohim*. Do you not yet know that Egypt is destroyed?"

[8] So Moses and Aaron were brought back to Pharaoh, and he said to them, "Go, serve Yahweh your *Elohim*. But who are the ones going?"

[9] Moses said, "We will go with our young and our old; with our sons and our daughters, with our flocks and our herds we will go, for it is a festival of Yahweh for us."

[10] He said to them, "So be Yahweh with you, as I will let you go with your little ones! Look out — evil is before you.

[11] Not so! Let the men go now and serve Yahweh, if that is what you desire." And they were driven out from Pharaoh's presence.

[12] Yahweh said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the land of Egypt for the locusts, that they may come up over the land of Egypt and eat every plant of the land, all that the hail has left."

[13] Moses stretched out his staff over the land of Egypt, and Yahweh brought an east wind on the land all that day and all that night; and when morning came, the east wind had brought the locusts.

[14] The locusts came up over all the land of Egypt and settled on all the territory of Egypt — very heavy. Never before had there been such locusts, nor shall there be such again.

[15] They covered the surface of the whole land, so that the land was darkened; and they ate every plant of the land and all the fruit of the trees that the hail had left. Not a green thing was left — no tree, no plant of the field — in all the land of Egypt.

[16] Then Pharaoh hurriedly called Moses and Aaron and said, "I have sinned against Yahweh your *Elohim*, and against you.

[17] Now therefore, forgive my sin please this once, and entreat Yahweh your *Elohim*, that he may take away from me only this death."

[18] So he went out from Pharaoh and entreated Yahweh.

[19] And Yahweh turned a very strong west wind, which lifted the locusts and drove them into the Sea of Reeds; not one locust was left in all the territory of Egypt.

[20] But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let the children of Israel go.

[21] Then Yahweh said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the heavens, that there may be darkness over the land of Egypt — darkness that can be felt."

[22] So Moses stretched out his hand toward the heavens, and there was a thick darkness in all the land of Egypt for three days.

[23] They could not see one another, nor did anyone rise from his place for three days. But all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.

[24] Pharaoh called to Moses and said, "Go, serve Yahweh; only let your flocks and your herds be left behind; let your little ones also go with you."

[25] But Moses said, "You yourself must also give us sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may offer them to Yahweh our *Elohim*.

[26] Our livestock too must go with us; not a hoof shall be left behind, for from them we must take to serve Yahweh our *Elohim*; and we do not know with what we must serve Yahweh until we arrive there."

[27] But Yahweh hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he would not let them go.

[28] Pharaoh said to him, "Get away from me! Take heed — see my face no more; for on the day you see my face you shall die."

[29] Moses said, "You have spoken well; I shall see your face no more."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"coast" → "territory"** (v4, 14, 19) — Hebrew גְּבוּל *gevul* = border / territory. "Coast" in 1611 English = border; modern English = seashore, misleading.
- **"herb" → "plant"** (v12, 15) — Hebrew עֵשֶׂב *esev*. Modernized.
- **"Red sea" → "Sea of Reeds"** (v19) — Hebrew יַם־סוּף *Yam Suph*. *Suph* = reeds / rushes. Not strictly the modern "Red Sea" (which LXX and Vulgate rendered ἐρυθρά θάλασσα "Red Sea" — the name stuck). Probable ancient reference: the Sea of Reeds = an inland reed-marsh or shallow sea in the eastern Nile delta, possibly one of the Bitter Lakes. KJV's "Red Sea" is the venerable traditional rendering; "Sea of Reeds" is more accurate to the Hebrew name.
- **"east wind" (v13)** — Hebrew רוּחַ הַקָּדִים *ruach ha-qadim*. Hot, dry desert wind (sirocco) that can bring locust plagues from Arabia.
- **"stayed" → "left behind"** (v24) — Hebrew יֻצָּג *yutsag* = placed / stationed / kept behind.
- **"serve the LORD" — Pharaoh tries to divide the release** — let men only (10:11), leave livestock (10:24). Moses insists on complete release. The theological point: Yahweh's service is total; partial concessions are not sufficient.

### Notes

- **Ninth plague — darkness** — a direct blow to Ra, the Egyptian sun god, and a foreshadowing of the firstborn-death coming in ch 12. "Darkness that can be felt" suggests a dust / sandstorm so thick that even light cannot penetrate — or a supernatural darkness beyond normal phenomena.
- **Goshen exempt** (v23) — the light in Israel's dwellings reiterates the distinction made with flies (8:22).
- **Final rupture** (v28–29) — Pharaoh's threat and Moses' acceptance mark the last face-to-face meeting before the final plague. The firstborn-plague and exodus follow.
