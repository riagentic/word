## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"a god to Pharaoh" → "as *Elohim* to Pharaoh"** (v1) — Hebrew נָתַתִּיךָ אֱלֹהִים לְפַרְעֹה *netattikha *Elohim* le-par'oh* = "I have placed you as *Elohim* to Pharaoh." Moses does not become God but takes the divine role in relation to Pharaoh (speaking with authority, performing signs); Aaron as his prophet (speaking his words).
- **"magicians" → "diviner-priests"** (v11, 22) — Hebrew חַרְטֻמִּים *chartummim*, as in Gen 41.
- **"enchantments" → "secret arts"** (v11, 22) — Hebrew לָטִים / לַהֲטֵיהֶם *latim / lahatehem* (different spellings). Esoteric / magical arts / occult practices.
- **"hardened" (v3, 13, 14, 22)** — multiple Hebrew verbs used for Pharaoh's heart:
  - *chazaq* (v13, 22) = strengthen / make firm;
  - *kaved* (v14) = make heavy;
  - *qashah* = make hard.
  Tension preserved across the plagues: sometimes Yahweh hardens (v3), sometimes it is passive "was hardened" (v13, 22), sometimes Pharaoh hardens his own heart (8:15, 32; 9:34). The three formulations cycle through the plagues. Theological question intact: divine sovereignty and human responsibility intertwined.
- **"secret arts" / "enchantments" — the magicians replicate the first two signs** (rod→serpent, water→blood, frogs) then fail at gnats onward (8:18). The plagues progressively overwhelm Egyptian magical competence.
- **"streams… rivers… ponds… pools" → "canals… Niles… ponds… reservoirs"** (v19) — Hebrew יְאֹרֵיהֶם *ye'orehem* (plural of *Ye'or* = Nile — the branches of the Nile delta / canals); מִקְוֵה מֵימֵיהֶם *miqweh* = collection / reservoir. Egyptian irrigation vocabulary.
- **"neither did he set his heart to this also" → "he did not take even this to heart"** (v23) — Hebrew וְלֹא־שָׁת לִבּוֹ גַּם־לָזֹאת *we-lo-shat libbo gam-lazot*. Heart-motif continues; Pharaoh's indifference to divine action.

### Notes

- **Structure of the plague narrative** — the ten plagues are carefully grouped (three triads + final): three pairs of three plus the plague of the firstborn. First plague in each triad: warning at the Nile in the morning. Second: warning at the palace. Third: no warning.
- **First plague: Nile to blood** — a direct assault on Egyptian religion. The Nile was deified (Hapi the god of the Nile flood). Turning Nile water to undrinkable blood is both plague and theological polemic: Yahweh strikes at the center of Egyptian religious-economic life.
- **Why did the magicians make it worse?** (v22) — if all the water was already blood, what water did the magicians turn to blood? The narrative logic: perhaps the Egyptians had dug for water (v24), and the magicians turned that to blood, confirming they shared Yahweh's power... or the narrative foregrounds their futile competition despite the uselessness.
