## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"obeisance" → "bowed down"** (v7) — modernized.
- **"travail" → "hardship"** (v8) — Hebrew תְּלָאָה *tela'ah* = hardship / distress. "Travail" archaic.
- **"dealt proudly" → "acted arrogantly"** (v11) — Hebrew זָדוּ *zadu* from *zid* = act presumptuously. The gods of Egypt (and by extension Pharaoh as their representative) acted arrogantly and were put in their place.
- **"to God-ward" → "before *Elohim*"** (v19) — Hebrew מוּל הָאֱלֹהִים *mul ha-Elohim* = in front of / opposite God. "To God-ward" = KJV archaism.
- **"hating covetousness" → "hating dishonest gain"** (v21) — Hebrew שֹׂנְאֵי בָצַע *sone'e vatsa*. *Betsa* = unjust gain / bribe-taking. Specifically bribery, not covetousness in general.
- **"ordinances" → "statutes"** (v20) — Hebrew הַחֻקִּים *ha-chuqqim*.

### Notes

- **Jethro's confession (v10–11)** — a Midianite priest declares Yahweh greater than all gods. Monolatrous confession (Yahweh is higher, not that others do not exist). The Midianite-Kenite connection to early Yahwism is a scholarly hypothesis based on Jethro's priestly role and Moses' formation in Midian.
- **Administrative organization (v21–26)** — Jethro's counsel introduces the hierarchical judicial system: chiefs over tens, fifties, hundreds, thousands. Practical wisdom from a foreign priest becomes institutional foundation for Israel's governance.
- **Structural placement** — ch 18 precedes the Sinai theophany (ch 19), which is historically curious: Jethro visits *after* Israel is at Sinai (v5) but the Sinai events seem to begin in ch 19. Probably structural / thematic arrangement: Jethro's model of delegation prepares for the covenantal structure about to be given.
