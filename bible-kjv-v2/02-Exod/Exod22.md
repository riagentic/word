# Exodus 22

[1] "If a man steals an ox or a sheep, and kills it or sells it, he shall restore five oxen for an ox and four sheep for a sheep.

[2] If the thief is found breaking in, and is struck so that he dies, there shall be no bloodguilt for him.

[3] But if the sun has risen upon him, there shall be bloodguilt for him. He shall surely make restitution; if he has nothing, then he shall be sold for his theft.

[4] If the stolen property is actually found alive in his hand — whether it is ox, donkey, or sheep — he shall restore double.

[5] If a man causes a field or vineyard to be grazed bare by letting his livestock loose, and it feeds in another man's field, from the best of his own field and from the best of his own vineyard he shall make restitution.

[6] If fire breaks out and catches in thorns, so that stacked grain, or standing grain, or the field is consumed, the one who kindled the fire shall surely make restitution.

[7] If a man gives his neighbor silver or goods to keep safe, and it is stolen from the man's house, if the thief is found, he shall pay double.

[8] If the thief is not found, the owner of the house shall come before the *Elohim*, to determine whether he has laid his hand on his neighbor's goods.

[9] For every breach of trust — whether for ox, donkey, sheep, clothing, or any lost thing of which one says, 'This is it' — the case of both parties shall come before the *Elohim*. The one whom the *Elohim* condemns shall pay double to his neighbor.

[10] If a man gives his neighbor a donkey, or an ox, or a sheep, or any animal to keep, and it dies, or is injured, or is driven away while no one sees it,

[11] an oath by Yahweh shall be between the two of them that he has not laid his hand on his neighbor's property; and the owner shall accept it, and he shall not make restitution.

[12] But if it is stolen from him, he shall make restitution to its owner.

[13] If it is torn in pieces, let him bring it as evidence; he shall not make restitution for what was torn.

[14] If a man borrows anything from his neighbor, and it is injured or dies, while its owner is not with it, he shall surely make restitution.

[15] If its owner is with it, he shall not make restitution; if it was hired, it came for its hire.

[16] If a man seduces a virgin who is not betrothed, and lies with her, he shall surely pay a bride-price and make her his wife.

[17] If her father utterly refuses to give her to him, he shall pay silver according to the bride-price of virgins.

[18] You shall not let a sorceress live.

[19] Whoever lies with an animal shall surely be put to death.

[20] Whoever sacrifices to any god, except to Yahweh alone, shall be devoted to destruction.

[21] You shall not wrong a resident alien nor oppress him, for you were resident aliens in the land of Egypt.

[22] You shall not afflict any widow or orphan.

[23] If you afflict them in any way, when they cry out to me, I will surely hear their cry;

[24] my anger shall burn, and I will kill you with the sword; and your wives shall become widows, and your children orphans.

[25] If you lend silver to any of my people, the poor among you, you shall not be to him like a moneylender; you shall not charge him interest.

[26] If you take your neighbor's cloak as a pledge, you shall return it to him before the sun goes down;

[27] for that is his only covering — it is his cloak for his skin. In what shall he sleep? And when he cries out to me, I will hear, for I am gracious.

[28] You shall not revile *Elohim*, nor curse a ruler of your people.

[29] You shall not delay offering the fullness of your harvest or the outflow of your winepress. The firstborn of your sons you shall give to me.

[30] Likewise you shall do with your oxen and with your sheep; seven days it shall be with its mother; on the eighth day you shall give it to me.

[31] You shall be holy men to me. You shall not eat any flesh that is torn by beasts in the field; you shall throw it to the dogs."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"breaking up" → "breaking in"** (v2) — Hebrew בַּמַּחְתֶּרֶת *ba-machteret* = "in the act of tunneling / breaking in" — specifically a nocturnal housebreak.
- **"no blood be shed for him" / "blood be shed for him" → "no bloodguilt for him" / "bloodguilt for him"** (v2, 3) — Hebrew אֵין לוֹ דָּמִים *eyn lo damim*. Legal idiom: "no blood [guilt] attaches." Self-defense against a night-burglar — uncertain identity, legitimate fear — is not murder. A daylight intruder is presumed not life-threatening.
- **"witch" → "sorceress"** (v18) — Hebrew מְכַשֵּׁפָה *mekhashephah* = female practitioner of *kashaph* (magic arts, incantations, potions). Not "witch" in the medieval / Disney sense; a sorcerer / magician who manipulates supernatural powers for harm or divination. Generic gender: the feminine form here reflects that practitioners were often women, but the prohibition extends to male practitioners too (cf. Deut 18:10).
- **"utterly destroyed" → "devoted to destruction"** (v20) — Hebrew יָחֳרָם *yochoram*. *Cherem* = placed under ban of destruction — a sacred devotion-to-death (cf. Jericho, Josh 6).
- **"vex a stranger" → "wrong a resident alien"** (v21) — Hebrew גֵּר לֹא־תוֹנֶה *ger lo-toneh*. *Ger* = resident alien (see Gen 15:13). *Yanah* (hiphil) = wrong / mistreat. The commandment is grounded in Israel's own experience: "you were *gerim* in Egypt."
- **"fatherless child" → "orphan"** (v22, 24) — Hebrew יָתוֹם *yatom*. Modernized.
- **"as an usurer" / "usury" → "like a moneylender" / "interest"** (v25) — Hebrew כְנֹשֶׁה / נֶשֶׁךְ *ke-nosheh / neshekh*. *Nosheh* = creditor / lender. *Neshekh* = interest / usury (literally "biting"). Loans to fellow Israelites from the poor were to be interest-free.
- **"raiment" → "cloak"** (v26, 27) — Hebrew שַׂלְמַת *salmat*. The outer garment — doubled as blanket at night.
- **"revile the gods" → "revile *Elohim*"** (v28) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים לֹא תְקַלֵּל *Elohim lo tqallel*. Hebrew אֱלֹהִים *Elohim* could mean:
  1. "God" (Yahweh).
  2. "the gods" (polytheistic reference — but the whole code is monotheistic, so strained).
  3. "the judges" (authorities, as in 21:6).
  Paul cites this in Acts 23:5 with the sense "ruler of your people." Ambiguity real; translation preserves "*Elohim*" with the judicial-authority sense likely dominant. KJV's plural "gods" misleads.
- **"first of thy ripe fruits, and of thy liquors" → "fullness of your harvest or the outflow of your winepress"** (v29) — Hebrew מְלֵאָתְךָ וְדִמְעֲךָ *mele'atkha we-dim'akha*. *Mele'ah* = fullness (of harvest). *Dema* = drop / teardrop / wine's first outflow. Firstfruits of both grain and grape.

### Notes

- **Covenant Code continues** — property law, sexual ethics, religious exclusivity, care for the vulnerable. Notable: strong protection for resident aliens, widows, orphans, and the poor. The "cry to me" theology (v23, 27) echoes Israel's own cry from Egypt — Yahweh as hearer of the afflicted.
- **No-interest law** — formative for later Jewish and Christian discussions of usury. The prohibition applies only to loans to fellow-covenant-members (not to foreigners — Deut 23:20) and specifically to the poor. Not a prohibition of all commercial lending.
- **"Seven days with its mother" (v30)** — humanitarian animal-ethic: minimum maternal contact before the young is taken (whether for sacrifice or market).
