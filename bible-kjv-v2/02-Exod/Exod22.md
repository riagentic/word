# Exodus 22

[1] "When a man steals an ox or a sheep and butchers it or sells it, five of the herd he shall repay for the ox and four of the flock for the sheep.

[2] If the thief is found in the breaking in and is struck and dies, there is no bloodguilt for him.

[3] If the sun has risen upon him, there is bloodguilt for him. He shall surely repay; if he has nothing, then he shall be sold for his theft.

[4] If the stolen thing is surely found in his hand, from ox to donkey to sheep, alive, he shall repay double.

[5] When a man causes a field or a vineyard to be grazed, and lets his animals go, and they graze in the field of another, with the best of his field and the best of his vineyard he shall repay.

[6] When fire goes out and finds thorns, and stacked grain or the standing grain or the field is consumed, he who kindled the burning shall surely repay.

[7] When a man gives to his neighbor money or vessels to keep, and it is stolen from the house of the man, if the thief is found, he shall repay double.

[8] If the thief is not found, then the owner of the house shall come near to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, whether he has not stretched out his hand against the property of his neighbor.

[9] For every matter of transgression, for an ox, for a donkey, for a sheep, for a cloak, for any lost thing of which one says, 'This is it,' the matter of the two of them shall come to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; he whom ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ condemns shall repay double to his neighbor.

[10] When a man gives to his neighbor a donkey or an ox or a sheep or any beast to keep, and it dies or is injured or is carried off, no one seeing,

[11] the oath of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ shall be between the two of them, whether he has not stretched out his hand against the property of his neighbor; and its owner shall take it, and he shall not repay.

[12] But if it is surely stolen from him, he shall repay its owner.

[13] If it is surely torn to pieces, he shall bring it as a witness; the torn thing he shall not repay.

[14] And when a man asks of his neighbor, and it is injured or dies, its owner not being with it, he shall surely repay.

[15] If its owner is with it, he shall not repay; if it is hired, it came for its wages.

[16] And when a man entices a virgin who is not betrothed and lies with her, he shall surely pay the bride-price for her to be his wife.

[17] If her father surely refuses to give her to him, he shall weigh out silver according to the bride-price of virgins.

[18] A sorceress you shall not let live.

[19] Everyone who lies with a beast shall surely be put to death.

[20] He who sacrifices to the gods shall be devoted to destruction, except to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ alone.

[21] And a sojourner you shall not wrong, and you shall not oppress him, for you were sojourners in the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[22] Any widow or fatherless one you shall not afflict.

[23] If you surely afflict him — for if he surely cries out to me, I will surely hear his outcry —

[24] then my anger shall burn, and I will kill you with the sword, and your wives shall be widows and your sons fatherless.

[25] If you lend money to my people, to the afflicted one with you, you shall not be to him as a creditor; you shall not lay interest upon him.

[26] If you surely take in pledge the cloak of your neighbor, by the going in of the sun you shall return it to him,

[27] for it is his only covering, it is his cloak for his skin; in what shall he lie? And it shall be, when he cries out to me, that I will hear, for I am gracious.

[28] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ you shall not make light of, and a chieftain among your people you shall not curse.

[29] Your fullness and your outflow you shall not delay. The firstborn of your sons you shall give to me.

[30] So you shall do with your ox, with your flock: seven days it shall be with its mother; on the eighth day you shall give it to me.

[31] And men of holiness you shall be to me; and flesh in the field, a torn thing, you shall not eat; to the dog you shall throw it."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v11, 20) — Hebrew יְהוָה.
- **"God" / "the judges" / "the gods" → "*Elohim*"** (v8, 9, 28) — see the entries on v8–9 and v28. In v20 לָאֱלֹהִים is "to the gods": other gods are meant, as the second half of the verse shows.
- **"If" → "When" / "If"** (throughout) — as in ch 21, כִּי "when" opens a main case (v1, 5, 6, 7, 10, 14, 16) and אִם "if" a case under it.
- **"kill it" → "butchers it"** (v1) — Hebrew טָבַח, to slaughter for food (the usual word for slaughter is שָׁחַט). **"restore five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep" → "five of the herd he shall repay for the ox and four of the flock for the sheep"** — Hebrew שׁוֹר "ox" and שֶׂה "sheep" are single animals (the שֶׂה a sheep or a goat); בָּקָר "herd" and צֹאן "flock" are the collectives. "Repay" is שִׁלֵּם throughout the chapter (v1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11–15; KJV "restore", "make restitution", "pay", "make it good").
- **"breaking up" → "in the breaking in"** (v2) — Hebrew בַּמַּחְתֶּרֶת, literally "in the digging through" (of a wall). **"smitten that he die" → "is struck and dies"** — Hebrew וְהֻכָּה וָמֵת.
- **"there shall no blood be shed for him" / "there shall be blood shed for him" → "there is no bloodguilt for him" / "there is bloodguilt for him"** (v2, 3) — Hebrew אֵין לוֹ דָּמִים, דָּמִים לוֹ, literally "there are no bloods for him", "bloods for him": the plural "bloods" is blood shed and the guilt for it. "For him" is the thief (no guilt for his blood) or the one who struck (no guilt on him); the Hebrew allows both. The sun "risen upon him": it is day, and he can be seen and known.
- **"he should make full restitution" → "He shall surely repay"** (v3) — infinitive absolute with its verb, שַׁלֵּם יְשַׁלֵּם, literally "repaying he shall repay". The same form in v4 (הִמָּצֵא תִמָּצֵא), v6, 14, v12 (גָּנֹב יִגָּנֵב), v13 (טָרֹף יִטָּרֵף), v16 (מָהֹר יִמְהָרֶנָּה), v17 (מָאֵן יְמָאֵן), v19 (מוֹת יוּמָת, literally "dying he shall be made to die"), v23 three times, v26 (חָבֹל תַּחְבֹּל); each is "surely" with the verb.
- **"If the theft be certainly found in his hand alive, whether it be ox, or ass, or sheep" → "If the stolen thing is surely found in his hand, from ox to donkey to sheep, alive"** (v4) — the Hebrew order. **"double"** (v4, 7, 9) — Hebrew שְׁנַיִם "two".
- **"cause a field or vineyard to be eaten, and shall put in his beast, and shall feed" → "causes a field or a vineyard to be grazed, and lets his animals go, and they graze"** (v5) — Hebrew יַבְעֶר … וּבִעֵר, the same verb twice, with the noun בְּעִיר "animals" (the written text has בעירה, read as בְּעִירוֹ "his animals"). The verb בער is also "burn", and "the burning" in v6 is from it; some read v5 too of a fire let loose. The meaning "graze" is the usual one and is kept; it is not certain.
- **"If fire break out, and catch in thorns" → "When fire goes out and finds thorns"** (v6) — Hebrew יָצָא "go out", מָצָא "find". **"stacks of corn" → "stacked grain"** — Hebrew גָּדִישׁ. **"be consumed"** — Hebrew נֶאֱכַל, literally "is eaten". **"he that kindled the fire" → "he who kindled the burning"** — Hebrew הַמַּבְעִר אֶת־הַבְּעֵרָה, verb and noun of one root.
- **"money or stuff" → "money or vessels"** (v7) — Hebrew כֶּסֶף "silver" (v7, 17, 25: "money" where payment is meant, "silver" where it is weighed, v17) and כֵּלִים, made articles of any kind.
- **"the master of the house shall be brought unto the judges, to see whether he have put his hand" → "the owner of the house shall come near to *Elohim*, whether he has not stretched out his hand"** (v8) — Hebrew וְנִקְרַב … אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים, "shall come near (or be brought near) to the Elohim", as in 21:6. "To see" is not in the Hebrew: the clause אִם־לֹא שָׁלַח יָדוֹ is the matter to be settled, in the form of an oath (v11). **"his neighbour's goods" → "the property of his neighbor"** (v8, 11) — Hebrew מְלֶאכֶת, the word for "work" (20:9), here what work has gained.
- **"For all manner of trespass" → "For every matter of transgression"** (v9) — Hebrew דְּבַר־פֶּשַׁע; דָּבָר is "word, matter", again in "the matter of the two of them". **"raiment" → "cloak"** — Hebrew שַׂלְמָה (v9, 26; the same garment as שִׂמְלָה v27).
- **"the cause of both parties shall come before the judges; and whom the judges shall condemn" → "the matter of the two of them shall come to *Elohim*; he whom *Elohim* condemns"** (v9) — Hebrew עַד הָאֱלֹהִים "as far as the Elohim", then אֲשֶׁר יַרְשִׁיעֻן אֱלֹהִים. The first has the article, the second has none, and the verb with the second is **plural**: literally "whom *Elohim* condemn". Readings: (1) "God", with a plural verb as in Gen 20:13; 35:7: the decision comes from God, by oath or lot at the sanctuary; (2) "the gods"; (3) "the judges" (KJV), who decide in God's name. The verse keeps the word and uses the English singular; the plural is noted here. הִרְשִׁיעַ is "declare wicked", that is, in the wrong.
- **"die, or be hurt, or driven away, no man seeing it" → "dies or is injured or is carried off, no one seeing"** (v10) — Hebrew נִשְׁבַּר, literally "is broken"; נִשְׁבָּה, literally "is taken captive" (by raiders).
- **"an oath of the LORD" → "the oath of Yahweh"** (v11) — Hebrew שְׁבֻעַת יְהוָה, an oath sworn by his name. **"the owner of it shall accept thereof" → "its owner shall take it"** — Hebrew וְלָקַח "and shall take": the oath, or (others) the animal that is left.
- **"let him bring it for witness" → "he shall bring it as a witness"** (v13) — Hebrew יְבִאֵהוּ עֵד. **"that which was torn" → "the torn thing"** — Hebrew הַטְּרֵפָה (v13, 31).
- **"borrow ought" → "asks"** (v14) — Hebrew שָׁאַל "ask"; no object is named: he asks for the use of an animal.
- **"if it be an hired thing, it came for his hire" → "if it is hired, it came for its wages"** (v15) — Hebrew אִם־שָׂכִיר הוּא בָּא בִּשְׂכָרוֹ. שָׂכִיר is elsewhere a hired man; so the clause can also be read "if he is a hired man, it comes on his wages" (the loss is set against his pay). The usual reading, of the hired animal, is kept; the other is possible.
- **"entice a maid" → "entices a virgin"** (v16) — Hebrew פִּתָּה "persuade, entice" (seduce) and בְּתוּלָה "virgin". **"he shall surely endow her to be his wife" → "he shall surely pay the bride-price for her to be his wife"** — Hebrew מָהֹר יִמְהָרֶנָּה לּוֹ לְאִשָּׁה: the verb is made from מֹהַר "bride-price" (v17; Gen 34:12), the sum paid to the bride's father; "dowry" (KJV) is the wrong direction. **"pay money" → "weigh out silver"** (v17) — Hebrew כֶּסֶף יִשְׁקֹל.
- **"Thou shalt not suffer a witch to live" → "A sorceress you shall not let live"** (v18) — Hebrew מְכַשֵּׁפָה לֹא תְחַיֶּה, in this order. מְכַשֵּׁפָה is the feminine of "sorcerer" (7:11; Deut 18:10): one who practises secret arts. חִיָּה (piel) is "let live, keep alive"; how she is to die the verse does not say.
- **"Whosoever lieth with a beast"** (v19) — Hebrew בְּהֵמָה.
- **"He that sacrificeth unto any god, save unto the LORD only, he shall be utterly destroyed" → "He who sacrifices to the gods shall be devoted to destruction, except to Yahweh alone"** (v20) — the Hebrew order; לָאֱלֹהִים has the article, "to the gods". יָחֳרָם is the passive of הֶחֱרִים: to give over to Yahweh beyond recall, as a rule by destroying (the *cherem*, Lev 27:29; Josh 6:17).
- **"vex a stranger" → "wrong a sojourner"** (v21) — Hebrew גֵּר: one who lives among a people not his own. הוֹנָה is "wrong, maltreat"; לָחַץ "oppress" is literally "press".
- **"fatherless child" → "fatherless one"** (v22, 24) — Hebrew יָתוֹם: one who has lost his father (his mother is the widow of v24). **"afflict"** (v22, 23) — Hebrew עִנָּה. In v22 "you" is plural; in v23 "you" is singular and the object is singular too, "him" (KJV "them"); v24 "you" is plural again.
- **"If thou afflict them in any wise, and they cry at all unto me, I will surely hear their cry" → "If you surely afflict him — for if he surely cries out to me, I will surely hear his outcry —"** (v23) — three infinitives absolute in a row. The sentence breaks off after the first clause and is taken up in v24, as in the Hebrew.
- **"my wrath shall wax hot" → "my anger shall burn"** (v24) — Hebrew וְחָרָה אַפִּי; אַף is "nose" and so "anger". **"your children" → "your sons"** — Hebrew בְּנֵיכֶם.
- **"lend money to any of my people that is poor by thee" → "lend money to my people, to the afflicted one with you"** (v25) — Hebrew אֶת־עַמִּי אֶת־הֶעָנִי עִמָּךְ, two objects side by side; עָנִי is the poor man bowed down (root of "afflict", v22). **"as an usurer … usury" → "as a creditor … interest"** — Hebrew נֹשֶׁה "one who has lent", and נֶשֶׁךְ, literally "a bite". "You shall not lay" is plural.
- **"by that the sun goeth down" → "by the going in of the sun"** (v26) — Hebrew עַד־בֹּא הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ, literally "until the sun goes in".
- **"his covering only … his raiment for his skin: wherein shall he sleep?" → "his only covering … his cloak for his skin; in what shall he lie?"** (v27) — Hebrew שָׁכַב "lie". The written text has כסותה "her / its covering", read as כְסוּתוֹ "his covering". **"gracious" kept** — Hebrew חַנּוּן, the adjective of חֵן "favor": one who shows favor.
- **"Thou shalt not revile the gods, nor curse the ruler of thy people" → "*Elohim* you shall not make light of, and a chieftain among your people you shall not curse"** (v28) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים לֹא תְקַלֵּל וְנָשִׂיא בְעַמְּךָ לֹא תָאֹר, the objects first. Two verbs: קִלֵּל (piel of "be light": treat as worthless, revile, call down evil on; "curses" in 21:17) and אָרַר "curse"; the same pair is rendered so in Gen 12:3. אֱלֹהִים has no article: (1) "God"; (2) "gods" (so KJV; the Septuagint has the plural, cited from memory); (3) "judges" (the Targum, cited from memory). The verse keeps the word. נָשִׂיא is "one lifted up", a chieftain. The second half is cited in Acts 23:5.
- **"the first of thy ripe fruits, and of thy liquors" → "Your fullness and your outflow"** (v29) — Hebrew מְלֵאָתְךָ וְדִמְעֲךָ. מְלֵאָה is "fullness" (the full yield: Num 18:27; Deut 22:9); דֶּמַע is found only here, literally "tear", so what drips or flows, usually understood of the juice of the press. The meaning of both is uncertain; "offer" is not in the Hebrew, only "you shall not delay".
- **"Likewise shalt thou do with thine oxen, and with thy sheep" → "So you shall do with your ox, with your flock"** (v30) — Hebrew שֹׁר "ox" and צֹאן "flock", with no "and".
- **"holy men" → "men of holiness"** (v31) — Hebrew אַנְשֵׁי־קֹדֶשׁ. **"flesh that is torn of beasts in the field" → "flesh in the field, a torn thing"** — Hebrew וּבָשָׂר בַּשָּׂדֶה טְרֵפָה; "of beasts" is not there. **"to the dogs" → "to the dog"** — the Hebrew is singular.

### Notes

- **Verse numbers** — in the Hebrew text our 22:1 is 21:37, and our 22:2–31 are 22:1–30.
- **The Covenant Code continues** — theft and damage (v1–15), the enticed virgin (v16–17), three offences punished with death (v18–20), the sojourner, the widow, the fatherless and the poor (v21–27), what is owed to *Elohim* (v28–31). The ground given in v21 is Israel's own time in Egypt, and in v23, 27 that Yahweh hears the cry of the afflicted.
- **Interest (v25)** — the loan here is to a poor man of the people; cf. Lev 25:35–37; Deut 23:19–20.
- **"Seven days with its mother" (v30)** — cf. Lev 22:27. How "the firstborn of your sons you shall give to me" (v29) is carried out is said in 13:13; 34:20: they are ransomed.
