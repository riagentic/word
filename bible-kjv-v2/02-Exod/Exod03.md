# Exodus 3

[1] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ was shepherding the flock of ⟦jethro|Yitro|Jethro⟧ his father-in-law, the priest of ⟦midian|Midyan|Midian⟧, and he drove the flock behind the wilderness and came to the mountain of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, to ⟦horeb|Chorev|Horeb⟧.

[2] And the angel of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ appeared to him in a flame of fire from the midst of the bush. And he saw, and behold, the bush was burning with fire, and the bush was not consumed.

[3] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said, "Let me turn aside, please, and see this great sight, why the bush does not burn up."

[4] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ saw that he turned aside to see, and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ called to him from the midst of the bush and said, "⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧!" And he said, "Here I am."

[5] And he said, "Do not come near here. Take off your sandals from your feet, for the place on which you are standing, it is holy ground."

[6] And he said, "I am the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of your father, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ and the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧." And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ hid his face, for he feared to look at ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[7] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said, "I have surely seen the affliction of my people who are in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, and their outcry I have heard because of their taskmasters, for I know their pains.

[8] And I have come down to deliver them from the hand of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ and to bring them up from that land to a good and broad land, to a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the ⟦canaanites|Kenaani|Canaanite⟧ and the ⟦hittites|Chitti|Hittite⟧ and the ⟦amorites|Emori|Amorite⟧ and the ⟦perizzites|Perizzi|Perizzite⟧ and the ⟦hivites|Chivvi|Hivite⟧ and the ⟦jebusites|Yevusi|Jebusite⟧.

[9] And now, behold, the outcry of the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ has come to me, and I have also seen the oppression with which the ⟦egyptians|Mitzrim|Egyptians⟧ are oppressing them.

[10] And now come, and let me send you to ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, and bring out my people, the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧."

[11] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, "Who am I, that I should go to ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, and that I should bring out the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧?"

[12] And he said, "For I will be with you; and this is the sign for you that I myself have sent you: when you bring out the people from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, you shall serve ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ on this mountain."

[13] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, "Behold, I am coming to the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, and I shall say to them, 'The ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of your fathers has sent me to you,' and they will say to me, 'What is his name?' What shall I say to them?"

[14] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ said to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, "**I will be who I will be.**" And he said, "Thus you shall say to the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, '**I will be has sent me to you.**'"

[15] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ said further to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, "Thus you shall say to the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, '⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of your fathers, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ and the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧, has sent me to you.' This is my name forever, and this is my remembrance from generation to generation.

[16] Go, and gather the elders of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ and say to them, '⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of your fathers, has appeared to me, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ and ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧, saying: I have surely visited you and what is done to you in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[17] And I said, I will bring you up from the affliction of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ to the land of the ⟦canaanites|Kenaani|Canaanite⟧ and the ⟦hittites|Chitti|Hittite⟧ and the ⟦amorites|Emori|Amorite⟧ and the ⟦perizzites|Perizzi|Perizzite⟧ and the ⟦hivites|Chivvi|Hivite⟧ and the ⟦jebusites|Yevusi|Jebusite⟧, to a land flowing with milk and honey.'

[18] And they will listen to your voice, and you shall come, you and the elders of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, to the king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, and you shall say to him, '⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of the ⟦hebrews|Ivrim|Hebrews⟧, has met with us; and now, let us go, please, a way of three days into the wilderness, and let us sacrifice to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.'

[19] And I know that the king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ will not allow you to go, and not by a strong hand.

[20] And I will stretch out my hand and strike ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ with all my wonderful deeds that I will do in its midst, and after that he will let you go.

[21] And I will give this people favor in the eyes of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, and it shall come to pass, when you go, that you shall not go empty.

[22] And a woman shall ask of her neighbor and of her who sojourns in her house articles of silver and articles of gold and cloaks, and you shall put them on your sons and on your daughters, and you shall strip ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"; "God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew יהוה and אֱלֹהִים. The two names alternate: "the angel of Yahweh" (v2), "Yahweh saw … *Elohim* called" (v4), "Yahweh said" (v7), "*Elohim* said" (v14, 15). With the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim": v1, 6, 11, 12, 13.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"kept the flock" → "was shepherding the flock"** (v1) — Hebrew הָיָה רֹעֶה. **"father in law"** — Hebrew חֹתֵן, the wife's father. He is Jethro here, Reuel in 2:18.
- **"led the flock to the backside of the desert" → "drove the flock behind the wilderness"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיִּנְהַג … אַחַר הַמִּדְבָּר: נָהַג is "drive" (Gen 31:18); אַחַר is "behind, after". Since the front is the east in Hebrew, "behind" may mean "to the west of".
- **"the mountain of God, even to Horeb" → "the mountain of *Elohim*, to Horeb"** (v1) — Hebrew הַר הָאֱלֹהִים חֹרֵבָה; "to Horeb" is the name with the ending of direction.
- **"the angel of the LORD" → "the angel of Yahweh"** (v2) — Hebrew מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה; the word מַלְאָךְ *mal'akh* means "messenger". In v4 it is Yahweh who sees and *Elohim* who calls from the bush.
- **"a flame of fire" kept; "a bush" → "the bush"** (v2) — Hebrew בְּלַבַּת־אֵשׁ: לַבָּה is found only here and is taken as a form of לֶהָבָה "flame". הַסְּנֶה has the article each time (five times in v2–4); the word is found again only in Deut 33:16, a thorny shrub of uncertain kind. **"looked" → "saw"** — Hebrew וַיַּרְא.
- **"was not consumed" kept** (v2) — Hebrew אֵינֶנּוּ אֻכָּל, literally "was not eaten".
- **"I will now turn aside" → "Let me turn aside, please"** (v3) — Hebrew אָסֻרָה־נָּא, a cohortative with נָא. **"sight"** — Hebrew מַרְאֶה, "a thing seen" (elsewhere "appearance"); it is from the verb "see", which stands six times in v2–4. **"why the bush is not burnt" → "why the bush does not burn up"** — Hebrew לֹא־יִבְעַר, the verb of v2 ("burning"): there the bush is on fire, here it does not burn away.
- **"Here am I" → "Here I am"** (v4) — Hebrew הִנֵּנִי, literally "behold me".
- **"Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes" → "Do not come near here. Take off your sandals"** (v5) — Hebrew אַל־תִּקְרַב הֲלֹם שַׁל־נְעָלֶיךָ; נַעַל is a sandal. **"holy ground"** — Hebrew אַדְמַת־קֹדֶשׁ, literally "ground of holiness"; the sentence takes up its subject again with "it" (הוּא).
- **"the God of thy father" kept** (v6) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי אָבִיךָ, "your father" in the singular, then the three names. The Samaritan text and Acts 7:32 have "your fathers" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept. "I am" has a stressed אָנֹכִי "I" and no verb.
- **"he was afraid to look upon God" → "he feared to look at *Elohim*"** (v6) — Hebrew יָרֵא "fear".
- **"their cry" → "their outcry"; "their sorrows" → "their pains"** (v7, 9) — Hebrew צְעָקָה (as in Gen 18:21) and מַכְאֹב "pain". "By reason of" is מִפְּנֵי, literally "from the face of". In the Hebrew the people is spoken of as one: "*his* taskmasters … *his* pains … to deliver *him* … to bring *him* up" (v7, 8); only "their outcry" is plural.
- **"taskmasters" kept** (v7) — Hebrew נֹגְשָׂיו, from נָגַשׂ "drive, press": those who drive the workers. Another word than "chiefs of forced labor" in 1:11.
- **"I have surely seen" kept** (v7) — Hebrew רָאֹה רָאִיתִי, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "seeing I have seen". So also "I have surely visited" (v16), פָּקֹד פָּקַדְתִּי.
- **"I am come down" → "I have come down"** (v8) — Hebrew וָאֵרֵד, "and I came down".
- **"out of the hand of the Egyptians" → "from the hand of Egypt"** (v8) — Hebrew מִצְרַיִם is the name of the land, used also for its people. It is rendered "Egypt" (v8, 20, 21, 22; in v20 with a singular pronoun, "in *its* midst") and "the Egyptians" only where the Hebrew has a plural verb with it (v9, "are oppressing").
- **"a land flowing with milk and honey" kept** (v8, 17) — Hebrew אֶרֶץ זָבַת חָלָב וּדְבָשׁ; זוּב is to flow, to run.
- **"the Canaanites, and the Hittites …" → "the Canaanite and the Hittite …"** (v8, 17) — the six names are singular in Hebrew, each with the article and joined by "and".
- **"oppression … oppress" kept** (v9) — Hebrew לַחַץ … לֹחֲצִים, one root: to press, to squeeze.
- **"Come now therefore, and I will send thee … that thou mayest bring forth" → "And now come, and let me send you … and bring out"** (v10) — Hebrew וְעַתָּה לְכָה וְאֶשְׁלָחֲךָ … וְהוֹצֵא: an imperative, a cohortative ("let me send"), and a second imperative, "bring out".
- **"Certainly I will be with thee" → "For I will be with you"** (v12) — Hebrew כִּי־אֶהְיֶה עִמָּךְ. כִּי is "for, because" (it may also only introduce the speech, or strengthen it: "surely"). אֶהְיֶה is the first person imperfect of הָיָה "be": the same form that stands three times in v14.
- **"this shall be a token unto thee, that I have sent thee" → "this is the sign for you that I myself have sent you"** (v12) — Hebrew וְזֶה־לְּךָ הָאוֹת כִּי אָנֹכִי שְׁלַחְתִּיךָ, with a stressed "I". What "this" points to is open: the words that follow (the serving on this mountain), or what goes before (the bush, or "I will be with you"). **"ye shall serve"** — the verb turns to the plural: Moses and the people. עָבַד "serve" is the word of the "service" of 1:14 and 2:23.
- **"when I come … and shall say"** → "I am coming … and I shall say" (v13) — Hebrew הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי בָא … וְאָמַרְתִּי … וְאָמְרוּ: three clauses joined by "and"; the "when" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"I AM THAT I AM" → "I will be who I will be"; "I AM hath sent me" → "I will be has sent me"** (v14) — Hebrew אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה … אֶהְיֶה שְׁלָחַנִי אֲלֵיכֶם *ehyeh asher ehyeh … ehyeh shelachani alekhem*. The one form אֶהְיֶה stands three times, and it is the form of v12 ("I will be with you") and of 4:12, 15 ("I will be with your mouth"); it is given one rendering in all these places. How the words are to be taken is open:
  - אֶהְיֶה is the imperfect of הָיָה "be, become, come to pass". The Hebrew imperfect is not a tense: "I will be" is its usual sense with this verb (so in v12), "I am" is also possible.
  - אֲשֶׁר is "who, which, that, what".
  - So: "I will be who I will be"; "I am who I am"; "I will be what I will be"; "I am the one who is". The Septuagint has "I am the one who is" (ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ ὤν), and "the one who is has sent me" (cited from memory).
  - The sentence may be heard as a promise (I will be there, as I will be there), as a statement about his being, or as a refusal to be pinned down by a name. The verse does not decide, and the translation does not either.
  - In the third place אֶהְיֶה stands as the subject of "has sent", like a name.
- **"the LORD God of your fathers" → "Yahweh, the *Elohim* of your fathers"** (v15, 16, 18) — Hebrew יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי אֲבֹתֵיכֶם. Here the name יהוה *YHWH* is given as the answer to "What is his name?" (v13). It is commonly explained as a third-person form of the verb of v14, "he is / he will be" or "he causes to be"; the verse itself sets the two side by side and says no more. The vowels of the name are not handed down (the Masoretic points are those of the word read in its place); "Yahweh" is the common scholarly form.
- **"this is my memorial unto all generations" → "this is my remembrance from generation to generation"** (v15) — Hebrew וְזֶה זִכְרִי לְדֹר דֹּר: זֵכֶר, from זָכַר "remember", is the way one is remembered and named, here set beside "my name"; לְדֹר דֹּר is literally "to generation, generation". "Forever" is לְעֹלָם. The Hebrew has no quotation marks: "This is my name …" may belong to the message for Israel or be said to Moses alone.
- **"gather … together" → "gather"** (v16) — Hebrew אָסַף. **"elders"** — Hebrew זְקֵנִים, literally "old men".
- **"the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, appeared unto me" → "has appeared to me, the *Elohim* of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob"** (v16) — the Hebrew order; here "*Elohim* of" stands once for the three names.
- **"I have surely visited you, and seen that which is done to you" → "I have surely visited you and what is done to you"** (v16) — Hebrew פָּקֹד פָּקַדְתִּי אֶתְכֶם וְאֶת־הֶעָשׂוּי לָכֶם: one verb with two objects; "seen" is not in the Hebrew. פָּקַד "visit" is the word of Joseph in Gen 50:24, 25 ("*Elohim* will surely visit you").
- **"And I have said" → "And I said"** (v17) — Hebrew וָאֹמַר.
- **"they shall hearken to thy voice" → "they will listen to your voice"** (v18) — Hebrew וְשָׁמְעוּ לְקֹלֶךָ.
- **"The LORD God of the Hebrews" → "Yahweh, the *Elohim* of the Hebrews"** (v18) — Hebrew יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי הָעִבְרִיִּים: before the king of Egypt Israel is called "the Hebrews", the name outsiders use (1:15–19; 2:6; Gen 39:14).
- **"hath met with us" kept** (v18) — Hebrew נִקְרָה עָלֵינוּ, the niphal of קָרָה "happen, befall": he has come upon us, the meeting came from his side.
- **"let us go, we beseech thee, three days' journey … that we may sacrifice" → "let us go, please, a way of three days … and let us sacrifice"** (v18) — Hebrew נֵלֲכָה־נָּא דֶּרֶךְ שְׁלֹשֶׁת יָמִים … וְנִזְבְּחָה, two cohortatives; דֶּרֶךְ is "way" (Gen 30:36).
- **"will not let you go" → "will not allow you to go"** (v19) — Hebrew לֹא־יִתֵּן אֶתְכֶם … לַהֲלֹךְ, literally "will not give you to go". "Let go" is kept for another verb (v20).
- **"no, not by a mighty hand" → "and not by a strong hand"** (v19) — Hebrew וְלֹא בְּיָד חֲזָקָה, literally "and not by a strong hand". The words are open: "not even by a strong hand" (he will resist even force), or "not unless by a strong hand" (he will yield only to force; so the Septuagint, cited from memory). חֲזָקָה is "strong", the root of the "strong" heart of 4:21.
- **"stretch out my hand … let you go"** (v20) — Hebrew וְשָׁלַחְתִּי אֶת־יָדִי … יְשַׁלַּח אֶתְכֶם: one root, שָׁלַח "send" — "I will send out my hand … he will send you away". **"smite" → "strike"** — Hebrew הִכָּה. **"my wonders" → "my wonderful deeds"** — Hebrew נִפְלְאֹתַי, from פָּלָא "be wonderful"; "wonder" is kept for מוֹפֵת (4:21).
- **"favour in the sight of" → "favor in the eyes of"** (v21) — Hebrew אֶת־חֵן הָעָם־הַזֶּה בְּעֵינֵי מִצְרָיִם, literally "the favor of this people in the eyes of Egypt". **"empty"** — Hebrew רֵיקָם.
- **"every woman shall borrow of her neighbour, and of her that sojourneth in her house, jewels" → "a woman shall ask of her neighbor and of her who sojourns in her house articles"** (v22) — Hebrew וְשָׁאֲלָה אִשָּׁה: שָׁאַל is "ask"; the word does not say "borrow". "Neighbor" is שְׁכֶנְתָּהּ, a woman who lives near (another word than רֵעַ); גָּרַת בֵּיתָהּ is literally "the sojourner (feminine) of her house". כְּלִי is any made thing: a vessel, a tool, an ornament (Gen 24:53). **"raiment" → "cloaks"** — Hebrew שְׂמָלֹת.
- **"ye shall spoil the Egyptians" → "you shall strip Egypt"** (v22) — Hebrew וְנִצַּלְתֶּם אֶת־מִצְרָיִם: the piel of נָצַל, literally "snatch away, strip off" (the root of "deliver" in v8). The usual word for plundering an enemy is another (בָּזַז).

### Notes

- **The name of God (v14–15)** — one of the most theologically and philosophically discussed passages in Hebrew Scripture. Traditions:
  - Jewish: the name is ineffable; practices of substitution (*Adonai*, *Ha-Shem*) developed to avoid its utterance.
  - Christian (Origen, Aquinas): "I AM THAT I AM" understood ontologically — Yahweh as the self-existent, uncaused Being (*esse ipsum subsistens*).
  - Modern historical-critical: *Ehyeh / Yahweh* more dynamically understood — "I will be there (with you)" — as relational promise, not metaphysical self-definition.
  - Linguistic scholarship: *YHWH* is commonly derived from *hawah / hayah* "to be / become", by some as a causative, "the one who causes to exist"; none of this is certain.
- **"Milk and honey" (v8, 17)** — a standing phrase for the plenty of the land. Milk = abundant livestock (goats, sheep); honey = abundant fruit (possibly date-honey rather than bee-honey). Pastoral and horticultural plenty.
- **Moses' reluctance begins** — the first of several objections (ch 4 continues). Moses resists the commission; Yahweh progressively answers each objection.
