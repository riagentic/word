# Exodus 20

[1] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ spoke all these words, saying,

[2] "I am ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, who brought you out from the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, from the house of slaves.

[3] You shall have no other gods before my face.

[4] You shall not make for yourself a carved image, or any form that is in the heavens above, or that is on the earth beneath, or that is in the waters under the earth.

[5] You shall not bow down to them, and you shall not serve them; for I, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, am a jealous ⟦el|El|God⟧, visiting the iniquity of fathers upon sons, upon the third and upon the fourth generation, for those who hate me,

[6] and doing ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ to thousands, for those who love me and keep my commandments.

[7] You shall not lift up the name of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ for emptiness, for ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will not clear the one who lifts up his name for emptiness.

[8] Remember the day of ⟦shabbat|the Shabbat|the Sabbath⟧, to sanctify it.

[9] Six days you shall labor and do all your work,

[10] and the seventh day is ⟦shabbat|a Shabbat|a Sabbath⟧ to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧. You shall not do any work, you and your son and your daughter, your slave and your slave woman, and your beast, and your sojourner who is in your gates.

[11] For six days ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ made the heavens and the earth, the sea and all that is in them, and he rested on the seventh day; therefore ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ blessed the day of ⟦shabbat|the Shabbat|the Sabbath⟧ and sanctified it.

[12] Honor your father and your mother, so that your days may be long upon the ground that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is giving you.

[13] You shall not murder.

[14] You shall not commit adultery.

[15] You shall not steal.

[16] You shall not answer against your neighbor as a false witness.

[17] You shall not covet the house of your neighbor; you shall not covet the wife of your neighbor, or his slave, or his slave woman, or his ox, or his donkey, or anything that is your neighbor's."

[18] And all the people were seeing the thunders and the torches and the voice of ⟦shofar|the shofar|the trumpet⟧ and the mountain smoking; and the people saw, and they swayed, and they stood far off.

[19] And they said to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, "Speak you with us, and let us hear; and let not ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ speak with us, lest we die."

[20] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said to the people, "Do not fear, for ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has come in order to test you, and in order that his fear may be before your faces, so that you do not sin."

[21] And the people stood far off, and ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ drew near to the dark cloud where ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ was.

[22] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, "Thus you shall say to the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧: 'You yourselves have seen that from the heavens I have spoken with you.

[23] You shall not make with me — gods of silver and gods of gold you shall not make for yourselves.

[24] An altar of earth you shall make for me, and you shall sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your flock and your herd. In every place where I cause my name to be remembered I will come to you and bless you.

[25] And if you make for me an altar of stones, you shall not build them as hewn stone; for you have waved your sword over it, and you have profaned it.

[26] And you shall not go up by steps upon my altar, that your nakedness may not be uncovered upon it.'"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim", in v20, 21; without it in v1, 19. In v3 and v23 the same word is "gods".
- **"You"** (v2–17) — every "you" and "your" in the ten words is singular in the Hebrew: each Israelite is addressed, as one man. From v22 on the "you" is plural (v22, 23), then singular again (v24–26).
- **"You shall not"** (v3–5, 7, 10, 13–17) — Hebrew לֹא with the imperfect: the form of a standing prohibition ("you shall never", "you are not to"), stronger than אַל "do not" (v19, 20).
- **"And God spake" → "And *Elohim* spoke"** (v1) — the name here is Elohim; "Yahweh" begins in v2. The text does not name the hearer; in 19:25 Moses is with the people.
- **"I am the LORD thy God" → "I am Yahweh your *Elohim*"** (v2) — Hebrew אָנֹכִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ; the words can also be divided "I, Yahweh, am your Elohim". **"out of the house of bondage" → "from the house of slaves"** — Hebrew מִבֵּית עֲבָדִים, the plural of עֶבֶד "slave" (the word of v10, 17).
- **"Thou shalt have no other gods before me" → "You shall have no other gods before my face"** (v3) — Hebrew לֹא יִהְיֶה־לְךָ אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים עַל־פָּנָיַ, word for word "there shall not be to you other gods upon my face". עַל־פָּנַי *al-panai* is "upon / before / against my face"; the phrase is used for "in the presence of, in front of" (Gen 11:28; Num 3:4), "beside, in addition to" and "in defiance of, to the face of" (Job 1:11; Isa 65:3). The verse does not choose between them: no other god is to be there where Yahweh is — and he is everywhere Israel is. The same phrase stands in v20 ("before your faces").
- **"any graven image" → "a carved image"** (v4) — Hebrew פֶּסֶל *pesel*, one word, from פָּסַל "hew, carve": a figure cut from wood or stone (later also of cast images, Isa 40:19). **"or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above" → "or any form that is in the heavens above"** — Hebrew וְכָל־תְּמוּנָה אֲשֶׁר, literally "and any form that [is] in the heavens above and that [is] on the earth beneath and that [is] in the waters under the earth": תְּמוּנָה *temunah* is the visible form or shape of a thing (Num 12:8; Deut 4:12); "likeness" is another word (דְּמוּת). The words "of any thing" are not in the Hebrew: the "form that is in the heavens" is the form of what is there. **"in the earth" → "on the earth"**.
- **"Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them" → "You shall not bow down to them, and you shall not serve them"** (v5) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה "bow down" and עָבַד "serve" (the verb of "slaves", v2, and of "labor", v9). The form תָעָבְדֵם is pointed as a passive-causative ("be made to serve them"); the meaning commonly given is the plain "serve them". "Them" is plural: the other gods of v3 and the images of v4.
- **"am a jealous God" → "am a jealous El"** (v5) — Hebrew אֵל קַנָּא *El qanna*. אֵל *El* is the short word for God (not *Elohim*, which stands just before it). קַנָּא is used only of God: "jealous" for what is his own, "zealous"; the root קנא is the heat of one who will not share what belongs to him alone (a husband for his wife, Num 5:14). The words can also be divided "for I am Yahweh your Elohim, a jealous El".
- **"visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation" → "visiting the iniquity of fathers upon sons, upon the third and upon the fourth generation"** (v5) — Hebrew פֹּקֵד עֲוֺן אָבֹת עַל־בָּנִים עַל־שִׁלֵּשִׁים וְעַל־רִבֵּעִים. פָּקַד is "visit": attend to, look into, call to account. "Fathers" and "sons" have no article. שִׁלֵּשִׁים and רִבֵּעִים are "third ones" and "fourth ones" (great-grandsons and their sons); the word "generation" is supplied.
- **"of them that hate me" → "for those who hate me"** (v5), **"unto thousands of them that love me" → "to thousands, for those who love me"** (v6) — Hebrew לְשֹׂנְאָי … לַאֲלָפִים לְאֹהֲבַי וּלְשֹׁמְרֵי מִצְוֺתָי: the same לְ "to, for, belonging to" stands before "those who hate me" and before "those who love me". It can be read "of" (the sons of those who hate me; thousands of those who love me), or "in the case of" (visiting … where they hate me; doing steadfast love … where they love me). "Thousands" may be thousands of persons or thousands of generations (cf. 34:7; Deut 7:9); the Hebrew says only "to thousands". The לְ is repeated before "keepers of my commandments".
- **"shewing mercy" → "doing steadfast love"** (v6) — Hebrew עֹשֶׂה חֶסֶד: עָשָׂה "do" and חֶסֶד *chesed*, loyal love within a bond. **"And"** — Hebrew וְ; "but" is not said.
- **"Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain" → "You shall not lift up the name of Yahweh your *Elohim* for emptiness"** (v7) — Hebrew לֹא תִשָּׂא אֶת־שֵׁם־יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ לַשָּׁוְא. נָשָׂא is "lift up, bear, carry, take up": to take the name upon the lips (Ps 16:4), as in an oath (cf. Ps 24:4), or to bear it. לַשָּׁוְא is "to / for the *shav*": שָׁוְא is what is empty — the false (Exod 23:1 "an empty report"; Ps 144:8), the worthless, what comes to nothing ("in vain", Jer 2:30). So the range: to swear falsely by the name, to use it for a lie or a worthless end, to speak it emptily. The Hebrew has the same word twice and so has the English. "In vain" (KJV) gives only the last of these in today's English.
- **"will not hold him guiltless" → "will not clear"** (v7) — Hebrew לֹא יְנַקֶּה, from נָקָה "be clear, free of guilt": he will not leave him unpunished.
- **"Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy" → "Remember the day of the Shabbat, to sanctify it"** (v8) — Hebrew זָכוֹר אֶת־יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת לְקַדְּשׁוֹ. זָכוֹר is not the usual imperative but the infinitive absolute, the form of a standing command. לְקַדְּשׁוֹ is "to sanctify it", "to make / treat it as holy", the verb of v11 ("sanctified it").
- **"Six days shalt thou labour" kept** (v9) — Hebrew תַּעֲבֹד, the verb עָבַד "serve, work" (v5). **"work"** (v9, 10) — Hebrew מְלָאכָה.
- **"But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work" → "and the seventh day is a Shabbat to Yahweh your *Elohim*. You shall not do any work"** (v10) — Hebrew וְיוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי שַׁבָּת לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ: "a Shabbat" has no article, and לַיהוָה is "to / for Yahweh". "In it" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates" → "you and your son and your daughter, your slave and your slave woman, and your beast, and your sojourner who is in your gates"** (v10) — Hebrew עֶבֶד "slave", אָמָה "slave woman", בְּהֵמָה "beast", גֵּר "sojourner". The wife is not named. The "you" is masculine singular in the Hebrew.
- **"For in six days the LORD made" → "For six days Yahweh made"** (v11) — Hebrew כִּי שֵׁשֶׁת־יָמִים עָשָׂה יְהוָה: the same words without a preposition as in v9 ("Six days you shall labor"): "through six days". **"and rested the seventh day" → "and he rested on the seventh day"** — Hebrew וַיָּנַח, from נוּחַ "rest, settle down". This is another verb than שָׁבַת "cease", from which *Shabbat* is named and which Gen 2:2, 3 and Exod 16:30 use. **"hallowed it" → "sanctified it"** — Hebrew וַיְקַדְּשֵׁהוּ, as v8.
- **"Honour thy father and thy mother"** (v12) — Hebrew כַּבֵּד, literally "make heavy, give weight to" (the root of כָּבוֹד "glory"). **"that thy days may be long upon the land" → "so that your days may be long upon the ground"** — Hebrew יַאֲרִכוּן יָמֶיךָ "your days may lengthen" (or "they may lengthen your days"); הָאֲדָמָה is "the ground, the soil", not אֶרֶץ "land".
- **"Thou shalt not kill" → "You shall not murder"** (v13) — Hebrew לֹא תִּרְצָח *lo tirtsach*, two words. רָצַח *ratsach* is its own verb, and not the common words for killing: הָרַג "kill", הֵמִית "put to death", הִכָּה "strike down". It is used of one person killing another within the community — of murder (Num 35:16–21; 1 Kgs 21:19; Hos 4:2), and in the law of the cities of refuge also of one who has killed a man without intent (Num 35:11; Deut 4:42). It is not used of killing in war or of the death penalty carried out by the court (once, Num 35:30, of the execution of the murderer). "Murder" is the nearest English word; "kill" says more than the Hebrew.
- **"Thou shalt not commit adultery"** (v14) — Hebrew לֹא תִּנְאָף; נָאַף is used of a man with another man's wife and of a wife with another man (Lev 20:10). **"Thou shalt not steal"** (v15) — Hebrew לֹא תִּגְנֹב; the verb has no object here.
- **"Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour" → "You shall not answer against your neighbor as a false witness"** (v16) — Hebrew לֹא־תַעֲנֶה בְרֵעֲךָ עֵד שָׁקֶר: עָנָה בְּ is "answer against", to give evidence in court; עֵד שָׁקֶר is "a witness of falsehood", a person ("as" is supplied). רֵעַ is "neighbor, fellow, the other man".
- **"Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house" → "You shall not covet the house of your neighbor"** (v17) — Hebrew לֹא תַחְמֹד, twice. חָמַד *chamad* is "desire, take delight in, want to have" (the root of "desirable", Gen 2:9; 3:6); it is the wanting that reaches for a thing (Exod 34:24; Deut 7:25; Josh 7:21; Mic 2:2). "House" stands first and alone; then the wife, the slave, the slave woman, the ox, the donkey. אֵשֶׁת is "wife / woman"; עֶבֶד, אָמָה as in v10.
- **"saw the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet" → "were seeing the thunders and the torches and the voice of the shofar"** (v18) — Hebrew רֹאִים, a participle; the people "see" the sounds too. הַקּוֹלֹת is literally "the voices" (thunder, as 19:16); הַלַּפִּידִם is "the torches", flames or flashes — not the word "lightnings" of 19:16 (בְּרָקִים); קוֹל הַשֹּׁפָר "the voice of the shofar".
- **"and when the people saw it, they removed" → "and the people saw, and they swayed"** (v18) — Hebrew וַיַּרְא הָעָם וַיָּנֻעוּ: the Masoretic Text has "saw" (the Samaritan text and the Septuagint read "feared", וַיִּירְאוּ, one letter more; cited from memory). נוּעַ is "sway, shake, stagger" (Isa 7:2), also "move to and fro". **"afar off" → "far off"** (v18, 21) — Hebrew מֵרָחֹק.
- **"Speak thou with us, and we will hear" → "Speak you with us, and let us hear"** (v19) — Hebrew וְנִשְׁמָעָה, a form of resolve or wish (cohortative); שָׁמַע is "hear" (and "listen, heed").
- **"Fear not: for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not" → "Do not fear, for *Elohim* has come in order to test you, and in order that his fear may be before your faces, so that you do not sin"** (v20) — Hebrew לְבַעֲבוּר נַסּוֹת אֶתְכֶם: נִסָּה is "test, put to the proof" (15:25; 16:4; 17:2; Gen 22:1), not "tempt". "Do not fear" (אַל־תִּירָאוּ) and "his fear" (יִרְאָתוֹ, the fear of him) are the same root: they are not to be afraid, and the fear of him is to stay with them. עַל־פְּנֵיכֶם is the phrase of v3, "upon / before your faces".
- **"the thick darkness" → "the dark cloud"** (v21) — Hebrew הָעֲרָפֶל, a heavy, dark cloud (Deut 4:11; 1 Kgs 8:12). **"drew near"** — Hebrew נִגַּשׁ.
- **"Ye have seen" → "You yourselves have seen"** (v22) — Hebrew אַתֶּם רְאִיתֶם, with the pronoun expressed, as 19:4. "From the heavens" stands first in its clause.
- **"Ye shall not make with me gods of silver, neither shall ye make unto you gods of gold" → "You shall not make with me — gods of silver and gods of gold you shall not make for yourselves"** (v23) — the Hebrew accents divide after אִתִּי "with me": "You shall not make [anything] with me; gods of silver and gods of gold you shall not make for yourselves". The first half has no object. KJV divides after "silver". אִתִּי is "with me, beside me".
- **"An altar of earth"** (v24) — Hebrew מִזְבַּח אֲדָמָה: אֲדָמָה "ground, soil" (the word of v12). **"thy sheep, and thine oxen" → "your flock and your herd"** — Hebrew צֹאן (sheep and goats) and בָּקָר (cattle). **"in all places where I record my name" → "In every place where I cause my name to be remembered"** — Hebrew בְּכָל־הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר אַזְכִּיר אֶת־שְׁמִי: literally "in all the place" (with the article); אַזְכִּיר is the causative of זָכַר "remember": "cause to be remembered, mention, have named". The Syriac reads "where you cause my name to be remembered" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text has "I".
- **"thou shalt not build it of hewn stone: for if thou lift up thy tool upon it, thou hast polluted it" → "you shall not build them as hewn stone; for you have waved your sword over it, and you have profaned it"** (v25) — Hebrew לֹא־תִבְנֶה אֶתְהֶן גָּזִית "you shall not build them (the stones) [as] cut stone". חַרְבְּךָ is "your sword" (חֶרֶב), here of the iron tool that cuts stone; הֵנַפְתָּ is "you have waved, swung" (הֵנִיף); the two perfects state the case as already done: whoever does it has profaned it. There is no "if" in the Hebrew. חִלֵּל is "profane", make common. "It" (feminine) is the stone, not the altar.
- **"Neither shalt thou go up by steps unto mine altar, that thy nakedness be not discovered thereon" → "And you shall not go up by steps upon my altar, that your nakedness may not be uncovered upon it"** (v26) — Hebrew עֶרְוָה "nakedness" and גִּלָּה "uncover" (Gen 9:21–23).

### Notes

- **"The ten words"** — the Hebrew name for v2–17 is עֲשֶׂרֶת הַדְּבָרִים "the ten words" (34:28; Deut 4:13; 10:4); this chapter calls them only "all these words" (v1). The Hebrew text has no numbering.
- **Numbering of the Ten Commandments** — different traditions divide them differently:
  - Jewish: v2 = #1 (self-identification), v3–6 = #2, v7 = #3, v8–11 = #4, v12 = #5, v13 = #6, v14 = #7, v15 = #8, v16 = #9, v17 = #10.
  - Catholic / Lutheran: v3–6 = #1 (treating no-other-gods and no-images together), then v7 = #2, ... splitting "coveting wife" from "coveting house" to make #9 and #10.
  - Protestant / Orthodox / most English tradition: v3 = #1, v4–6 = #2, v7 = #3, etc., with all "coveting" in #10.
  The traditions interpret the structural unities differently.
- **"Jealous El" and iniquity across generations (v5–6)** — the formula is famous for its asymmetry: the third and the fourth generation for iniquity, thousands for steadfast love. Ezek 18 later insists that each person dies for their own sin.
- **"You shall not murder" distinct from "you shall not kill"** — later Jewish and Christian discussion has grappled with the precise scope of *ratsach* versus other killing-verbs (*harag*, *hikkah*, etc.). Pacifist readings apply the commandment more broadly; the translation "murder" reflects the specific Hebrew verb (see Changes, v13).
- **Deut 5:6–21** gives the ten words again with differences of wording ("Keep" for "Remember" and another reason for the Shabbat, 5:12, 15; "a carved image, any form" without "or", 5:8; the wife before the house and a second verb in the last word, 5:21); this chapter is translated from its own Hebrew, and nothing is taken over from there.
- **Altar regulation (v24–26)** — the theology of humble / non-manipulated altars: altar of earth, uncut stones, no steps. Contrast to the elaborate altars Yahweh will later command for the dwelling / temple — here, simplicity of access to sacrifice is emphasized, not monumentality.
