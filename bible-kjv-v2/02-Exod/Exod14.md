## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v19) — Hebrew הָאֱלֹהִים, with the article.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (throughout) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"Egypt" / "the Egyptians"** (throughout) — one Hebrew word, מִצְרַיִם "Egypt". Where the Hebrew treats it as one (a singular verb or pronoun, or none) the verse has "Egypt": "Egypt was journeying" (v10), "the heart of Egypt" (v17), "Egypt said, Let me flee" (v25), "his chariots" (v26), "Egypt dead" (v30). Where the verb or pronoun is plural it has "the Egyptians" (v4, 9, 13, 18, 23, 27).
- **"encamp" → "camp"** (v2, 9) — Hebrew חָנָה, the verb of "camp" (v19, 20, 24). **"over against it" → "opposite it"** — Hebrew נִכְחוֹ. "That they turn back and camp" is literally "and let them turn back and camp"; the verse passes from "they" to "you".
- **"They are entangled in the land" → "They are confused in the land"** (v3) — Hebrew נְבֻכִים, "perplexed, wandering without a way" (Joel 1:18; Esth 3:15).
- **"I will harden Pharaoh's heart" → "I will make strong the heart of Pharaoh"** (v4, 8, 17) — Hebrew חִזֵּק, the piel of חָזַק "be strong", as in 11:10: he makes it firm. KJV has "harden" for three different verbs; the subject is kept as each verse has it (Yahweh in all three verses here). In v17 it is "the heart of Egypt", and the form is a participle: "I am making strong".
- **"I will be honoured upon Pharaoh" → "I will be glorified through Pharaoh"** (v4, 17, 18) — Hebrew וְאִכָּבְדָה בְּפַרְעֹה, the niphal of כָּבֵד "be heavy", the root of כָּבוֹד "glory"; passive or reflexive ("I will get myself glory"). בְּ is "in, through, upon". The same root is used of the "heavy" heart of Pharaoh (8:15; 9:34) and stands again in v25.
- **"his host" → "his force"** (v4, 9, 17, 28) — Hebrew חַיִל, strength, an armed force. "Host" is צָבָא (12:41); "camp" in v19, 20, 24 is מַחֲנֶה.
- **"was turned against the people" → "was turned toward the people"** (v5) — Hebrew וַיֵּהָפֵךְ … אֶל, "was turned (changed) toward". **"fled"** — Hebrew בָּרַח; "flee" in v25, 27 is נוּס.
- **"made ready his chariot" → "harnessed his chariot"** (v6) — Hebrew אָסַר "bind".
- **"captains over every one of them" → "officers over all of it"** (v7) — Hebrew שָׁלִשִׁם, literally "third men": the third man in a chariot, or a rank of officer; the sense is not certain. רֶכֶב "chariots" is a collective singular, hence "all of it". "Chosen" is בָּחוּר.
- **"with an high hand" kept** (v8) — Hebrew בְּיָד רָמָה, a hand lifted up: boldly, defiantly (Num 15:30; 33:3). "Were going out" is a participle.
- **"all the horses and chariots of Pharaoh" → "all the chariot horses of Pharaoh"** (v9) — Hebrew כָּל־סוּס רֶכֶב פַּרְעֹה, "all the horse of the chariots of Pharaoh". **"horsemen"** (v9, 17, 18, 23, 26, 28) — Hebrew פָּרָשִׁים; it may mean the horses of the chariot teams or their riders.
- **"drew nigh" → "drew near"** (v10) — Hebrew הִקְרִיב, a causative form, here without an object. **"marched after them" → "was journeying after them"** — Hebrew נֹסֵעַ, the usual verb for Israel's own journeying (v15, 19). **"were sore afraid" → "feared greatly"**.
- **"Because there were no graves in Egypt" → "Is it because there are no graves in Egypt"** (v11) — Hebrew הֲמִבְּלִי אֵין־קְבָרִים, a doubled negative: "is it from lack of there being no graves".
- **"Let us alone" → "Cease from us"** (v12) — Hebrew חֲדַל מִמֶּנּוּ. "The word" is הַדָּבָר. **"it had been better" → "it is better"** — the Hebrew has no verb.
- **"stand still" → "take your stand"** (v13) — Hebrew הִתְיַצְּבוּ, to station oneself. **"which he will shew to you" → "which he will do for you"** — Hebrew עָשָׂה. **"salvation"** — Hebrew יְשׁוּעָה.
- **"ye shall see them again no more for ever" → "you shall see them no more again, forever"** (v13) — Hebrew לֹא תֹסִיפוּ לִרְאֹתָם עוֹד עַד־עוֹלָם, literally "you shall not add to see them again, to the age". "Whom you have seen" can also be read "as you have seen".
- **"ye shall hold your peace" → "you shall be silent"** (v14) — Hebrew תַּחֲרִישׁוּן, "be silent, keep still"; a statement, not a command in form.
- **"that they go forward" → "that they journey on"** (v15) — Hebrew וְיִסָּעוּ. "You cry out" is singular: Moses.
- **"divide it" → "split it"** (v16, 21) — Hebrew בָּקַע, as of wood (Gen 22:3) and of the springs of the deep (Gen 7:11).
- **"dry ground" / "dry land"** (v16, 21, 22, 29) — two Hebrew words: יַבָּשָׁה "dry land" (v16, 22, 29) and חָרָבָה "dry ground" (v21).
- **"the angel of God" → "the angel of *Elohim*"** (v19) — Hebrew מַלְאַךְ הָאֱלֹהִים; מַלְאָךְ is "messenger". **"removed" → "set out"** — Hebrew נָסַע, twice in the verse.
- **"it was a cloud and darkness to them, but it gave light by night to these" → "there was the cloud and the darkness, and it lit up the night"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיְהִי הֶעָנָן וְהַחֹשֶׁךְ וַיָּאֶר אֶת־הַלָּיְלָה, word for word. The Hebrew is short and hard: what lit up the night is not said. KJV's "to them … to these" is not in the text. The Greek reads differently ("and the night passed", cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"caused the sea to go back" → "made the sea go"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיּוֹלֶךְ, the causative of "go"; "back" is not in the Hebrew. רוּחַ is "wind" here; קָדִים is the east wind.
- **"looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar" → "looked down on the camp of Egypt in the pillar"** (v24) — Hebrew וַיַּשְׁקֵף, to look out and down; בְּעַמּוּד "in" or "through" the pillar. **"troubled" → "threw … into confusion"** — Hebrew וַיָּהָם, to throw into panic.
- **"took off their chariot wheels, that they drave them heavily" → "took off the wheel of his chariots and drove it with heaviness"** (v25) — Hebrew וַיָּסַר אֵת אֹפַן מַרְכְּבֹתָיו וַיְנַהֲגֵהוּ בִּכְבֵדֻת. The Masoretic Text has וַיָּסַר, "he removed, turned aside". The Samaritan text and the Greek read "he bound" (וַיֶּאְסֹר, cited from memory), which many follow; the Masoretic Text is kept. "With heaviness" is from כָּבֵד "be heavy": the root of "I will be glorified" (v4, 17, 18). "Wheel", "his" and "it" are singular: Egypt as one. "Drove it" (נִהַג, as of the wind in 10:13) has Yahweh as its subject: he made it move heavily.
- **"Let us flee from the face of Israel" → "Let me flee from the face of Israel"** (v25) — Hebrew אָנוּסָה, first person singular: Egypt speaks as one.
- **"upon their chariots" → "upon his chariots"** (v26) — Hebrew רִכְבּוֹ, "his": Egypt's, or Pharaoh's.
- **"returned to his strength when the morning appeared" → "returned at the turning of morning to its steady flow"** (v27) — Hebrew לִפְנוֹת בֹּקֶר, literally "at the turning of morning"; לְאֵיתָנוֹ is literally "to its constancy": אֵיתָן is what is lasting and ever-flowing (of a stream, Amos 5:24). "Flow" is supplied.
- **"fled against it" → "were fleeing to meet it"** (v27) — Hebrew נָסִים לִקְרָאתוֹ, a plural participle.
- **"overthrew" → "shook off"** (v27) — Hebrew וַיְנַעֵר, to shake off, shake out (Neh 5:13; Ps 136:15).
- **"there remained not so much as one of them" → "not even one of them was left"** (v28) — Hebrew לֹא־נִשְׁאַר בָּהֶם עַד־אֶחָד.
- **"saved"** (v30) — Hebrew וַיּוֹשַׁע, the verb of "salvation" (v13). **"upon the sea shore" → "on the shore of the sea"** — Hebrew שְׂפַת הַיָּם, literally "the lip of the sea". "Dead" is singular.
- **"that great work which the LORD did upon the Egyptians" → "the great hand that Yahweh did against Egypt"** (v31) — Hebrew אֶת־הַיָּד הַגְּדֹלָה אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה יְהוָה בְּמִצְרַיִם, literally so: "hand" for a deed of power. בְּמִצְרַיִם is "in Egypt" or "against Egypt".
- **"believed the LORD, and his servant Moses" → "believed in Yahweh and in Moses his servant"** (v31) — Hebrew וַיַּאֲמִינוּ בַּיהוָה וּבְמֹשֶׁה, the verb of Gen 15:6 with בְּ "in" before both names. "Servant" is עֶבֶד, the word for "slave".

### Notes

- **Crossing the Sea of Reeds** — the central event of Israel's national memory, celebrated in the Song of the Sea (ch 15), Psalm 78, 106, and many prophetic texts. Literary features: the east wind and the returning sea (v21, 27), pillared theophany, decisive divine intervention after human cries.
- **Historical location** — traditional identification with an arm of the Red Sea proper; modern scholarship favors shallow northern lakes (Bitter Lakes, Lake Menzaleh, or the Reed Marshes) where wind-tide combinations could plausibly expose a passable strip briefly. In this chapter the Hebrew says only "the sea".
- **Structural shift** — v14 ("Yahweh will fight for you, and you shall be silent") marks a theological hinge: Israel's role is passive reception of divine action. This will recur throughout the conquest narrative (Joshua 6, Jericho) and in apocalyptic texts.
