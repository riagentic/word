## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"chide" → "quarreled"** (v2, 7) — Hebrew וַיָּרֶב *wa-yarev* from רִיב *riv* = contend / dispute / quarrel. Legal-contentious term — the people "bring a legal complaint" against Moses.
- **"tempt" → "test"** (v2, 7) — Hebrew נָסָה *nasah*. Same verb as the Akedah (Gen 22:1). Not "entice to sin" ("tempt" in modern English) but "test, put to the proof."
- **"Massah and Meribah" → "Massah (testing) and Meribah (quarreling)"** (v7) — Hebrew מַסָּה וּמְרִיבָה *Massah u-Merivah*; English glosses added to the place names for the wordplay (the people tested and quarreled).
- **"discomfited" → "defeated"** (v13) — Hebrew וַיַּחֲלֹשׁ *wa-yachalosh* = weakened / disabled / defeated. Modernized.
- **"book" → "scroll"** (v14) — Hebrew בַּסֵּפֶר *ba-sefer*, as in Gen 5:1.
- **"Jehovah-nissi" → "Yahweh-Nissi (Yahweh is my banner)"** (v15) — Hebrew יְהוָה נִסִּי *Yahweh Nissi*. *Nes* = banner / standard / flagpole. The altar-name declares Yahweh as Israel's battle-standard.
- **v16 — obscure verse** — Hebrew כִּי־יָד עַל־כֵּס יָהּ *ki-yad al-kes Yah*. MT difficult:
  1. "A hand against the throne of Yah" — either Amalek's defiance (a hand raised against Yah's throne) or Yahweh's oath-gesture (hand raised upon his throne, swearing).
  2. Some MSS read נֵס *nes* ("banner") instead of כֵּס *kes* ("throne") — "Because a hand is lifted against the banner of Yah."
  3. LXX interprets as an oath-formula.
  Translation: "a hand has been raised against the throne of Yah" — ambiguity of agent (Amalek's aggression or Yahweh's oath) preserved.

### Notes

- **Joshua's first appearance** (v9) — identified as a military commander. He will become Moses' successor, leading the entry into Canaan. Introduced without fanfare; the narrative treats him as known.
- **Moses' raised hands** — ambiguous action: prayer / intercession / military signal / channeling divine power through the staff. Later Christian iconography has the arms-outstretched position as prefiguring the cross.
- **"Amalek to be blotted out" (v14)** — perpetual enmity sworn. Historically played out in Saul (1 Sam 15), David, and Esther (where Haman is an Agagite — Amalekite descendant). The Amalek-ban is one of the most severe in Scripture, and one that later Jewish tradition has refracted in difficult ways.
