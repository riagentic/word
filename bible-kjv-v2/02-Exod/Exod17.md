[2] And the people contended with ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and said, "Give us water, that we may drink." And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said to them, "Why do you contend with me? Why do you test ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧?"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v9) — Hebrew הָאֱלֹהִים, with the article: "the staff of the Elohim".
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (v1, 7) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"journeyed … after their journeys" → "set out … by their journeys"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיִּסְעוּ … לְמַסְעֵיהֶם, verb and noun of one root (נָסַע "pull up" the tent pegs): by their stages, from camp to camp.
- **"according to the commandment of the LORD" → "at the mouth of Yahweh"** (v1) — Hebrew עַל־פִּי יְהוָה, literally "upon the mouth of Yahweh": at his word. "Commandment" is another word (מִצְוָה). **"pitched" → "camped"** — Hebrew חָנָה.
- **"did chide" → "contended"** (v2, twice), **"the chiding" → "the contention"** (v7) — Hebrew רִיב, verb and noun: to contend, dispute, bring a case. The name *Meribah* (מְרִיבָה "strife") is from the same root.
- **"Give us water that we may drink" kept** (v2) — the imperative תְּנוּ is plural: Moses and Aaron are addressed.
- **"tempt" → "test"** (v2, 7) — Hebrew נִסָּה, to put to the proof (as Gen 22:1; Exod 15:25; 16:4), not "entice to sin". The name *Massah* (מַסָּה "testing") is from this verb.
- **"murmured" → "grumbled"** (v3) — Hebrew לוּן with עַל, as in ch 16.
- **"to kill us and our children and our cattle" → "to put me and my sons and my livestock to death"** (v3) — the Hebrew is singular: אֹתִי וְאֶת־בָּנַי וְאֶת־מִקְנַי "me and my sons and my livestock"; the people speak as one man. לְהָמִית is the causative of "die"; בָּנִים "sons"; מִקְנֶה "livestock".
- **"What shall I do unto this people?" → "What shall I do for this people?"** (v4) — Hebrew לָעָם הַזֶּה: לְ is "to" or "for"; whether "to them" or "for them" is meant the Hebrew does not say. **"they be almost ready to stone me" → "A little more, and they will stone me"** — Hebrew עוֹד מְעַט וּסְקָלֻנִי, literally "yet a little, and they will stone me".
- **"Go on before" → "Pass on before"** (v5) — Hebrew עֲבֹר "pass over, pass on". **"wherewith thou smotest the river" → "with which you struck the Nile"** — Hebrew הַיְאֹר, the Egyptian word for the Nile (7:20).
- **"I will stand" → "I am standing"** (v6) — Hebrew הִנְנִי עֹמֵד, "behold me" with a participle. **"smite the rock" → "strike the rock"** — Hebrew וְהִכִּיתָ בַצּוּר, literally "strike on the rock". **"in the sight of" → "before the eyes of"** — Hebrew לְעֵינֵי.
- **"Massah, and Meribah" kept without glosses** (v7) — Hebrew מַסָּה וּמְרִיבָה *Massah u-Merivah*, "Testing and Strife"; the verse itself gives the reason for both names ("because of the contention … and because of their testing"). **"because they tempted" → "because of their testing"** — Hebrew עַל נַסֹּתָם, an infinitive.
- **"Is the LORD among us, or not?" → "Is Yahweh in our midst, or not?"** (v7) — Hebrew הֲיֵשׁ יְהוָה בְּקִרְבֵּנוּ אִם־אָיִן, literally "Is there Yahweh in our midst, or is there not?": the words יֵשׁ / אַיִן ask whether he is there at all.
- **"fight with Amalek" → "fight against Amalek"** (v9, 10) — Hebrew נִלְחַם בְּ; in v8 the Hebrew has עִם "with". **"to morrow"** (v9) — the Hebrew accents join "tomorrow" to what follows ("Tomorrow I am standing …"); it can also be read with "go out, fight against Amalek tomorrow".
- **"I will stand … with the rod of God in mine hand" → "I am standing … and the staff of *Elohim* in my hand"** (v9) — Hebrew אָנֹכִי נִצָּב, a participle; the last words are a clause of their own, "and the staff of the Elohim [is] in my hand". מַטֶּה is "staff" (v5).
- **"and fought with Amalek" → "to fight against Amalek"** (v10) — Hebrew לְהִלָּחֵם, an infinitive.
- **"when Moses held up his hand … when he let down his hand" → "when Moses raised his hand … when he rested his hand"** (v11) — Hebrew יָרִים "raise high" and יָנִיחַ "let rest" (from נוּחַ "rest"). The verb forms tell of what happened again and again: "whenever". "Hand" is singular in v11, "hands" plural in v12.
- **"stayed up his hands" → "supported his hands"** (v12) — Hebrew תָּמַךְ "hold fast, support". **"the one on the one side, and the other on the other side" → "one on this side and one on that side"** — Hebrew מִזֶּה אֶחָד וּמִזֶּה אֶחָד.
- **"his hands were steady" kept** (v12) — Hebrew וַיְהִי יָדָיו אֱמוּנָה, literally "his hands were firmness": the noun אֱמוּנָה *emunah*, which everywhere else is "faithfulness" (from אמן "be firm"), stands here for the first time in the Bible, in its plain bodily sense. **"until the going down of the sun" → "until the sun went down"** — Hebrew עַד־בֹּא הַשָּׁמֶשׁ, literally "until the going in of the sun".
- **"discomfited" → "weakened"** (v13) — Hebrew וַיַּחֲלֹשׁ, from חָלַשׁ "be weak" (Job 14:10; Joel 3:10): he made Amalek weak, laid him low. The verse does not say that Amalek was destroyed. "The edge of the sword" is literally "the mouth of the sword" (לְפִי־חָרֶב).
- **"for a memorial in a book" → "as a memorial in the book"** (v14) — Hebrew זִכָּרוֹן בַּסֵּפֶר; the vowels give "the book" (with the article). סֵפֶר is any written document. **"rehearse it in the ears of" → "put it in the ears of"** — Hebrew וְשִׂים בְּאָזְנֵי, "put, set".
- **"I will utterly put out" → "I will surely blot out"** (v14) — Hebrew infinitive absolute with its verb: מָחֹה אֶמְחֶה, literally "blotting out I will blot out". מָחָה is "wipe out", of writing "blot out". **"remembrance"** — Hebrew זֵכֶר, how one is remembered and named; זִכָּרוֹן "memorial" in the same verse is another word of the same root. כִּי before it is "that" or "for".
- **"Jehovah-nissi" → "Yahweh Nissi"** (v15) — Hebrew יְהוָה נִסִּי *Yahweh nissi*, two words: "Yahweh [is] my נֵס". נֵס *nes* is a pole or standard set up on a height as a rallying sign (Num 21:8, 9; Isa 5:26; 11:10): "Yahweh is my banner". The Septuagint has "the Lord my refuge" and the Vulgate "the Lord my exaltation" (both cited from memory); they do not render נֵס as "banner". "Jehovah" is a later mixed form of the Tetragrammaton with the vowels of *Adonai*.
- **"Because the LORD hath sworn that the LORD will have war with Amalek" → "For a hand is upon the throne of Yah: Yahweh has war against Amalek"** (v16) — Hebrew כִּי־יָד עַל־כֵּס יָהּ מִלְחָמָה לַיהוָה בַּעֲמָלֵק מִדֹּר דֹּר, word for word: "for / because / surely — a hand — upon / against — the *kes* of Yah — war — to Yahweh — against Amalek — from generation, generation". The Masoretic Text is translated as it stands; it is hard at three points:
  1. כֵּס *kes* is found only here. It is taken as a short form of כִּסֵּא "throne" (so the Samaritan text reads, cited from memory). Because of v15 many propose to read נֵס *nes* "banner" ("a hand upon the banner of Yah"); the two words differ by one letter, but this is a conjecture and not the text.
  2. Whose hand it is the verse does not say: the hand of Yahweh or of Moses raised to the throne in an oath (so KJV, "the LORD hath sworn"; the Hebrew has no word "sworn"), or the hand of Amalek raised against the throne of Yah. עַל is "upon" and "against". The English "a hand is upon the throne" leaves it open; "is" is supplied.
  3. The Septuagint has "with a hidden hand the Lord wars against Amalek" (cited from memory): it does not have the words "throne of Yah".
  יָהּ *Yah* is the short form of the name Yahweh (15:2). "Yahweh has war" is literally "war [is] to Yahweh". **"from generation to generation"** — Hebrew מִדֹּר דֹּר, "from generation, generation".

### Notes

- **Joshua's first appearance** (v9) — identified as a military commander. He will become Moses' successor, leading the entry into Canaan. Introduced without fanfare; the narrative treats him as known.
- **Moses' raised hands** — ambiguous action: prayer / intercession / military signal / channeling divine power through the staff. Later Christian iconography has the arms-outstretched position as prefiguring the cross.
- **"Amalek to be blotted out" (v14)** — perpetual enmity sworn. Historically played out in Saul (1 Sam 15), David, and Esther (where Haman is an Agagite — Amalekite descendant). The Amalek-ban is one of the most severe in Scripture, and one that later Jewish tradition has refracted in difficult ways.
