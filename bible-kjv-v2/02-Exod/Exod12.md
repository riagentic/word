# Exodus 12

[1] Yahweh said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt,

[2] "This month shall be for you the beginning of months; it shall be the first of the months of the year for you.

[3] Speak to all the congregation of Israel, saying: 'On the tenth day of this month they shall take for themselves, each man, a lamb according to their ancestral houses — a lamb for a household.

[4] And if the household is too small for a lamb, he and his neighbor nearest his house shall take one, according to the number of persons; according to what each person eats, you shall make your count for the lamb.

[5] Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male one year old; you may take it from the sheep or from the goats.

[6] You shall keep it until the fourteenth day of this month; then the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall slaughter it at twilight.

[7] They shall take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the lintel of the houses in which they eat it.

[8] They shall eat the flesh that night — roasted with fire. With unleavened bread and with bitter herbs they shall eat it.

[9] Do not eat any of it raw or boiled in water, but roasted with fire — its head with its legs and its inner parts.

[10] You shall let none of it remain until morning; whatever remains of it until morning you shall burn with fire.

[11] This is how you shall eat it: with your loins girded, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand; you shall eat it in haste. It is a Passover to Yahweh.

[12] For I will pass through the land of Egypt that night and strike all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments. I am Yahweh.

[13] The blood shall be for you a sign on the houses where you are; and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague shall be on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.

[14] This day shall be for you a memorial; and you shall keep it as a festival to Yahweh throughout your generations — as a permanent ordinance you shall keep it.

[15] Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread. On the first day you shall remove leaven from your houses, for anyone who eats what is leavened, from the first day to the seventh day, that person shall be cut off from Israel.

[16] On the first day there shall be a holy assembly, and on the seventh day a holy assembly for you. No work shall be done on these days; only what each person must eat — that alone may be done for you.

[17] You shall observe the Feast of Unleavened Bread, for on this very day I brought your hosts out of the land of Egypt. Therefore you shall observe this day throughout your generations as a permanent ordinance.

[18] In the first month, from the fourteenth day of the month at evening, you shall eat unleavened bread until the twenty-first day of the month at evening.

[19] For seven days no leaven shall be found in your houses; for whoever eats what is leavened, that person shall be cut off from the congregation of Israel — whether resident alien or native of the land.

[20] You shall eat nothing leavened; in all your dwellings you shall eat unleavened bread."

[21] Then Moses called all the elders of Israel and said to them, "Go and take for yourselves lambs according to your clans, and slaughter the Passover offering.

[22] Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood that is in the basin, and apply it to the lintel and to the two doorposts with the blood that is in the basin; and none of you shall go out of the door of his house until morning.

[23] For Yahweh will pass through to strike the Egyptians; and when he sees the blood on the lintel and on the two doorposts, Yahweh will pass over the door and will not let the Destroyer enter your houses to strike you.

[24] You shall keep this thing as a statute for you and for your sons forever.

[25] When you come to the land that Yahweh will give you, as he has promised, you shall keep this service.

[26] And when your children say to you, 'What do you mean by this service?',

[27] you shall say, 'It is the Passover-sacrifice of Yahweh, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt when he struck the Egyptians, and delivered our houses.'" And the people bowed their heads and worshiped.

[28] The children of Israel went and did so; as Yahweh had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.

[29] At midnight Yahweh struck all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon, and every firstborn of the livestock.

[30] Pharaoh rose up in the night, he and all his servants and all the Egyptians; and there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was no house in which someone was not dead.

[31] He called for Moses and Aaron by night and said, "Rise, go out from among my people, both you and the children of Israel; and go, serve Yahweh, as you have said.

[32] Also take your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone; and bless me also."

[33] The Egyptians pressed hard on the people, to send them out of the land in haste; for they said, "We are all dead men."

[34] So the people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneading-bowls bound up in their cloaks on their shoulders.

[35] The children of Israel had done as Moses told them: they had asked from the Egyptians articles of silver, articles of gold, and garments;

[36] and Yahweh had given the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they granted them what they asked. And they plundered the Egyptians.

[37] The children of Israel journeyed from Raamses to Succoth, about 600,000 men on foot, besides children.

[38] A mixed multitude also went up with them, and flocks and herds — very much livestock.

[39] They baked unleavened cakes of the dough that they had brought out from Egypt, for it was not leavened — because they were driven out of Egypt and could not delay, nor had they prepared provisions for themselves.

[40] The time that the children of Israel dwelt in Egypt was 430 years.

[41] At the end of 430 years, on that very day, all the hosts of Yahweh went out from the land of Egypt.

[42] It is a night of watching for Yahweh, for bringing them out from the land of Egypt. This is that night of Yahweh — a watching for all the children of Israel throughout their generations.

[43] Yahweh said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the ordinance of the Passover: no foreigner shall eat of it.

[44] But any slave bought with money shall eat of it after you have circumcised him.

[45] A resident foreigner and a hired worker shall not eat of it.

[46] In one house it shall be eaten; you shall not carry any of the flesh outside of the house, and you shall not break a bone of it.

[47] The whole congregation of Israel shall keep it.

[48] When a resident alien sojourns with you and would keep the Passover to Yahweh, let all his males be circumcised; then he may draw near and keep it, and he shall be as one born in the land. But no uncircumcised person shall eat of it.

[49] One law shall be for the native and for the resident alien who sojourns among you."

[50] Thus all the children of Israel did as Yahweh had commanded Moses and Aaron; so they did.

[51] On that very day Yahweh brought the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their hosts.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"Passover"** (v11, 21, 27, 43, 48) — Hebrew פֶּסַח *Pesach*; English retained. Etymology: likely from *pasach* = "to pass over / skip / leap" (v13 *pasachti* = "I will pass over").
- **"kill it in the evening" → "slaughter it at twilight"** (v6) — Hebrew בֵּין הָעַרְבָּיִם *beyn ha-arbayim* = "between the two evenings" — dusk / twilight (the transition from afternoon to evening). Rabbinic interpretation fixes this between noon and sunset.
- **"side posts" / "upper door post" → "doorposts" / "lintel"** (v7, 22, 23) — Hebrew מְזוּזֹת / מַשְׁקוֹף *mezuzot / mashqof*. *Mezuzah* = doorpost (now also used for the scripture-case on a doorpost); *mashqof* = lintel (crosspiece above door).
- **"sodden" → "boiled"** (v9) — archaic → modern.
- **"purtenance" → "inner parts"** (v9) — Hebrew קְרָבִים *qeravim* = inward parts / entrails. The whole lamb is to be roasted, including viscera — not butchered and sold.
- **"destroyer" → "the Destroyer"** (v23) — Hebrew הַמַּשְׁחִית *ha-mashchit* = the destroyer. Personified destroying-agent, whether angelic intermediary or the destructive dimension of Yahweh's own action. The text presents as distinct from Yahweh: "Yahweh will not let the Destroyer come in." Blurring of agency common in OT theology.
- **"mixed multitude" (v38)** — Hebrew עֵרֶב רַב *erev rav*. Not only Israelites left Egypt — other slave groups and sympathizers joined. The nation forming in the wilderness is already multi-ethnic.
- **"600,000… besides children"** (v37) — Hebrew שֵׁשׁ־מֵאוֹת אֶלֶף רַגְלִי *shesh-me'ot eleph ragli*. Adult males. With women and children, traditional estimate reaches 2–3 million. Historical / numerical debates intense: some argue *eleph* here = "military unit / troop" rather than literal thousand, reducing the number substantially; others argue for miraculous multiplication as narrated. Translation literal.
- **"430 years" (v40–41)** — LXX and Samaritan Pentateuch say "430 years in Egypt and Canaan" (halving the Egypt stay). Gal 3:17 follows LXX. MT's 430 years in Egypt specifically. Textual variant with theological consequences.
- **"night of watching" (v42)** — Hebrew לֵיל שִׁמֻּרִים *leyl shimmurim*. Two meanings in the verse: Yahweh's watching (protecting Israel); Israel's watching (annual commemoration). Double vigil.

### Notes

- **Passover as founding ritual** — the chapter is half narrative, half ritual instruction. The founding event (Israel's deliverance) is embedded in ongoing liturgical practice. The story shapes the ritual; the ritual makes the story perpetually present for each generation.
- **Universal access through circumcision (v48–49)** — the Passover is Israelite, but is open to any foreigner who undergoes circumcision. "One law for native and resident alien." A remarkably open ethno-religious boundary.
- **Foreshadowing of Christ** — Christian tradition reads the lamb without blemish (v5), the blood-sign (v13), and "not a bone shall be broken" (v46, cited in John 19:36) as typological of Jesus' death. Paul calls Christ "our Pesach, sacrificed for us" (1 Cor 5:7).
