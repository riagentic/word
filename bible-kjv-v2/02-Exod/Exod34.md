## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"God" → "El"** (v6, 14) — Hebrew אֵל רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן *El rachum ve-channun*; אֵל קַנָּא *El qanna*.
- **"Hew thee" → "Cut for yourself"** (v1) — Hebrew פְּסָל־לְךָ *psal-lekha*. Moses himself cuts these second tablets (compared to the first, which were Yahweh's work, 31:18). Moses' labor in the new covenant-tablets.
- **"tables" → "tablets"** (v1, 4, 28, 29) — modernized.
- **"brakest" → "shattered"** (v1) — modernized.
- **"merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth" → "compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in lovingkindness and faithfulness"** (v6) — Hebrew רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם וְרַב־חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת *rachum we-channun erekh appayim we-rav chesed we-emet*. Classic attribute-formula, repeated throughout the Old Testament (Num 14:18, Neh 9:17, Ps 86:15, 103:8, 145:8, Joel 2:13, Jonah 4:2). Translated with the standard rendering of each term: *rachum* (compassionate), *channun* (gracious), *erekh appayim* (slow to anger / long-faced), *chesed* (lovingkindness / covenant-love), *emet* (faithfulness / reliability).
- **"mercy" → "lovingkindness"** (v7) — Hebrew חֶסֶד *chesed*.
- **"by no means clear the guilty" (v7)** — Hebrew וְנַקֵּה לֹא יְנַקֶּה *we-naqqeh lo yenaqqeh*. Tension within the formula: Yahweh forgives but does not let guilt evaporate. Both mercy and justice.
- **"groves" → "Asherah-poles"** (v13) — Hebrew אֲשֵׁרָיו *asherav*. Wooden cult-objects dedicated to Asherah (Canaanite goddess, consort of El / Baal). KJV "groves" mistranslates — these are specific religious installations, not simply trees.
- **"go a whoring" → "whore"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew זָנָה *zanah*. Covenantal-marital metaphor: idolatry = marital infidelity toward Yahweh. Strong prophetic image (cf. Hos 1–3).
- **"earing" → "plowing"** (v21) — as in Gen 45:6. Archaic KJV term.
- **"Feast of Weeks" (v22)** — Hebrew חַג שָׁבֻעֹת *Chag Shavuot*. Seven weeks after Passover — Pentecost. The grain-harvest festival.
- **"Ten Words" (v28)** — Hebrew עֲשֶׂרֶת הַדְּבָרִים *aseret ha-devarim*. The proper Hebrew name: *Aseret ha-Dibrot* / Ten Utterances / Ten Words — what tradition calls "Ten Commandments." Not "commandments" *mitzvot* but "words" — declarations / speech-acts.
- **"shone" (v29, 30, 35)** — Hebrew קָרַן *qaran*. Obscure verb: root *qeren* = horn, hence "radiated / shot forth rays" (like horns of light). Jerome's Vulgate translated "horned" (*cornuta*), leading to Michelangelo's famous horned-Moses statue. The sense is "radiant / beamed light," not "grew horns."

### Notes

- **The attribute formula (v6–7)** — becomes the single most-quoted creedal statement within the OT. Israel remembers this revelation of Yahweh's character across centuries.
- **Covenant renewal** — chapter parallels ch 20–23 in structure but modified after the golden-calf crisis. The Ritual Decalogue of ch 34 differs from the Ethical Decalogue of ch 20 — some scholars see the two as alternative decalogue traditions.
- **Shining face of Moses** — Paul uses this in 2 Cor 3:7–18 to contrast the transient glory of Moses' face with the permanent glory of the new covenant. The veil becomes a theological metaphor for the unveiling in Christ.
