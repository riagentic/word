# Exodus 1

[1] These are the names of the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ who came to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ with ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧, each man with his household:

[2] ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧, ⟦simeon|Shimon|Simeon⟧, Levi, ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧,

[3] ⟦issachar|Yissachar|Issachar⟧, ⟦zebulun|Zevulun|Zebulun⟧, ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧,

[4] Dan, ⟦naphtali|Naftali|Naphtali⟧, Gad, and Asher.

[5] All the persons who came from the loins of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ were 70 in all; ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ was already in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[6] Then ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ died, and all his brothers, and all that generation.

[7] But the children of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ were fruitful and teemed and multiplied and grew very mighty; and the land was filled with them.

[8] A new king arose over ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, who did not know ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧.

[9] He said to his people, "Behold, the people of the children of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ are more numerous and mightier than we.

[10] Come, let us deal shrewdly with them, lest they multiply; and in the event of war, they may join those who hate us, fight against us, and go up from the land."

[11] So they set taskmasters over them to oppress them with their forced labor. They built store-cities for ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧: ⟦pithom|Pitom|Pithom⟧ and Raamses.

[12] But the more they oppressed them, the more they multiplied and the more they spread. So the ⟦egyptians|Mitzrim|Egyptians⟧ came to dread the children of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[13] The ⟦egyptians|Mitzrim|Egyptians⟧ made the children of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ serve with harshness,

[14] and they made their lives bitter with hard labor — with mortar and brick, and with every kind of labor in the field. In all their service, they worked them with harshness.

[15] The king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ said to the ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ midwives — the name of the one was ⟦shiphrah|Shifrah|Shiphrah⟧, and the name of the other Puah —

[16] and he said, "When you deliver the ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ women, look upon the birthstool: if it is a son, you shall put him to death; but if it is a daughter, she shall live."

[17] But the midwives feared ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and did not do as the king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ had spoken to them, but let the boys live.

[18] The king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ called the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this thing and let the boys live?"

[19] The midwives said to ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, "Because the ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ women are not like the ⟦egyptian|Mitzri|Egyptian⟧ women; for they are vigorous, and they give birth before the midwife comes to them."

[20] So ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ dealt well with the midwives; and the people multiplied and grew very mighty.

[21] And because the midwives feared ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, he made households for them.

[22] Then ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ commanded all his people, "Every son that is born you shall cast into the ⟦nile|Yeor|Nile⟧; but every daughter you shall let live."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"souls" → "persons"** (v5) — Hebrew נֶפֶשׁ *nephesh*, as in Gen 46.
- **"waxed exceeding mighty" → "grew very mighty"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיַּעַצְמוּ *wa-ya'atsmu* from *atsam* = be strong / numerous. Same root as later Israel's descriptor.
- **"a new king… which knew not Joseph"** (v8) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יָדַע אֶת־יוֹסֵף *asher lo-yada et-Yoseph*. *Yada* here = acknowledge / have-regard-for. Either a new dynasty ignorant of the prior vizier, or a king who deliberately repudiates the earlier arrangement. Historical suggestion: transition after the expulsion of the Hyksos (16th c. BCE), when the new native Egyptian dynasty rejected the Semitic-friendly policies of their predecessors.
- **"deal wisely" → "deal shrewdly"** (v10) — Hebrew נִתְחַכְּמָה *nitchakmah* — hithpael of *chakham* = act with calculation / cunning. Not wise in the moral sense; sinister state policy.
- **"join also unto our enemies" → "join those who hate us"** (v10) — Hebrew וְנוֹסַף גַּם־הוּא עַל־שֹׂנְאֵינוּ *we-nosaph gam-hu al-sonenu*. The fear: a fifth column in wartime.
- **"treasure cities" → "store-cities"** (v11) — Hebrew עָרֵי מִסְכְּנוֹת *arey miskenot*. Supply / storage cities for grain and military provisions — not "treasure" cities.
- **"they were grieved" → "they came to dread"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיָּקֻצוּ *wa-yaqutsu* = loathed / dreaded / came to detest.
- **"with rigour" → "with harshness"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew בְּפָרֶךְ *be-pharekh* = with crushing / brutal severity. Technical term for forced-labor brutality (cf. Lev 25:43, 46, 53).
- **"bondage" → "labor"** (v14) — Hebrew עֲבֹדָה *avodah* = service / labor / slavery. Same word as "service" later in v14.
- **"upon the stools" → "upon the birthstool"** (v16) — Hebrew עַל־הָאָבְנָיִם *al ha-ovnayim*. Dual form: "the two stones / two bricks" = the birth-stool (a pair of stones on which the laboring woman squatted). Specific ancient Near East / Egyptian midwifery apparatus.
- **"men children" → "boys"** (v17, 18) — Hebrew הַיְלָדִים *ha-yeladim* = the children / the boys.
- **"lively" → "vigorous"** (v19) — Hebrew חָיוֹת *chayot*. Could mean "lively / vigorous" or (punning) "like animals" / "like wild creatures." The midwives' tone is ambiguously deferential-or-dismissive.
- **"made them houses" → "made households for them"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיַּעַשׂ לָהֶם בָּתִּים *wa-ya'as lahem bottim*. *Beit* = house / household / family. Yahweh establishes families for the midwives (in some readings, making *them* the founders of households — a reward for their reverent disobedience).
- **"the river" → "the Nile"** (v22) — Hebrew הַיְאֹר *ha-Ye'or*, as in Gen 41.

### Notes

- **Transition from Genesis** — vv1–7 recap Gen 46:8–27 and Gen 50:22–26. Genesis ended with Joseph dying in Egypt; Exodus begins with the consequences: a new political reality, and the fulfillment of Gen 15:13's prediction of oppression.
- **Shiphrah and Puah (v15)** — named women in the act of saving lives, in a chapter where the king is unnamed. The narrator's focus-reversal is pointed: the history remembers the midwives; the Pharaoh's name is lost.
- **"Hebrew midwives" (v15)** — ambiguous: Hebrew *meyaledot ha-ivriyot* can mean "midwives of the Hebrews" (Egyptian women who served Hebrew clients) or "Hebrew midwives" (ethnic Hebrews themselves). Jewish tradition favors the latter (identifying them as Jochebed and Miriam).
- **"Pharaoh" unnamed** — intentional? The historical Pharaoh's name is omitted throughout the Exodus narrative. Dating debates (18th-dynasty Thutmose vs. 19th-dynasty Ramses) depend on archaeology, not the text.
