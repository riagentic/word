# Exodus 1

[1] And these are the names of the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ who came to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧; with ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ they came, each man and his house:

[2] ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧, ⟦simeon|Shimon|Simeon⟧, Levi and ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧,

[3] ⟦issachar|Yissachar|Issachar⟧, ⟦zebulun|Zevulun|Zebulun⟧ and ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧,

[4] Dan and ⟦naphtali|Naftali|Naphtali⟧, Gad and Asher.

[5] And all the souls that came out of the thigh of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ were seventy souls; and ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ was in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[6] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ died, and all his brothers, and all that generation.

[7] And the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ were fruitful and swarmed and multiplied and became very exceedingly mighty, and the land was filled with them.

[8] And a new king arose over ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, who did not know ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧.

[9] And he said to his people, "Behold, the people of the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ are many and mighty, more than we.

[10] Come, let us deal wisely with them, lest they multiply, and it come to pass, when war befalls, that they also are added to those who hate us, and fight against us, and go up from the land."

[11] And they set over them chiefs of forced labor, in order to afflict them with their burdens. And they built store cities for ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧: ⟦pithom|Pitom|Pithom⟧ and Raamses.

[12] And as they afflicted them, so they multiplied and so they spread abroad. And they felt loathing because of the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[13] And the ⟦egyptians|Mitzrim|Egyptians⟧ made the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ serve with harshness.

[14] And they made their lives bitter with hard service, with mortar and with bricks and with all service in the field — all their service in which they made them serve with harshness.

[15] And the king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ said to the ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ midwives, of whom the name of the one was ⟦shiphrah|Shifrah|Shiphrah⟧ and the name of the second Puah,

[16] and he said, "When you help the ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ women to bear, and you look upon the birthstool: if it is a son, you shall put him to death, and if it is a daughter, she shall live."

[17] And the midwives feared ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and did not do as the king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ had spoken to them, and they let the children live.

[18] And the king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ called for the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this thing and let the children live?"

[19] And the midwives said to ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, "Because the ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ women are not like the ⟦egyptian|Mitzri|Egyptian⟧ women, for they are lively; before the midwife comes to them, they bear."

[20] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ did good to the midwives, and the people multiplied and became very mighty.

[21] And it came to pass, because the midwives feared ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that he made houses for them.

[22] And ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ commanded all his people, saying, "Every son that is born, you shall throw him into the ⟦nile|Yeor|Nile⟧, and every daughter you shall let live."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*. In v17 and v21 the Hebrew has the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim"; in v20 it has none.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. The book itself begins with "And" (וְאֵלֶּה), joining it to Genesis (the same words as Gen 46:8).
- **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (v1, 7, 9, 12, 13) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל. In v1 they are the twelve sons of Jacob themselves; from v7 on the same words name the people.
- **"every man and his household came with Jacob" → "with Jacob they came, each man and his house"** (v1) — Hebrew אֵת יַעֲקֹב אִישׁ וּבֵיתוֹ בָּאוּ, in this order; בַּיִת is "house". The verb "came" stands twice in the verse.
- **"and" in the list** (v2–4) — the Hebrew has "and" before Judah, Benjamin, Naphtali and Asher only; so here.
- **"souls" kept; "loins" → "thigh"** (v5) — Hebrew כָּל־נֶפֶשׁ יֹצְאֵי יֶרֶךְ־יַעֲקֹב שִׁבְעִים נָפֶשׁ, literally "every soul of those who came out of the thigh of Jacob: seventy soul" (נֶפֶשׁ is singular both times, used for the whole number). יָרֵךְ is the thigh; "those who came out of his thigh" are his own offspring (Gen 46:26). The number seventy is that of Gen 46:27. The Septuagint has seventy-five here and in Gen 46:27, and so Acts 7:14 (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept. **"for Joseph was in Egypt already" → "and Joseph was in Egypt"** — Hebrew וְיוֹסֵף הָיָה בְמִצְרָיִם; the Hebrew has no "already".
- **"increased abundantly" → "swarmed"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁרְצוּ, the verb of the swarming things in Gen 1:20, 21; the first three verbs are those of the blessing of Gen 1:28 and 9:7 ("be fruitful … swarm … multiply").
- **"waxed exceeding mighty" → "became very exceedingly mighty"** (v7, 20) — Hebrew וַיַּעַצְמוּ בִּמְאֹד מְאֹד; עָצַם is to be mighty, strong in number. בִּמְאֹד מְאֹד "very exceedingly" as in Gen 17:2, 6 (v20 has one מְאֹד, "very"). **"the land was filled with them"** — הָאָרֶץ is "the land" (of Egypt) or "the earth".
- **"which knew not Joseph" → "who did not know Joseph"** (v8) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יָדַע אֶת־יוֹסֵף; יָדַע is "know". Whether he had never heard of Joseph or did not acknowledge him, the word does not say.
- **"more and mightier than we" → "many and mighty, more than we"** (v9) — Hebrew רַב וְעָצוּם מִמֶּנּוּ: two adjectives and "from us", which may be "more than we" or "too many and too mighty for us".
- **"them", "they" for the people** (v10–12) — the Hebrew speaks of the people as one man: "let us deal wisely with *him*, lest *he* multiply … *he* also is added … over *him* … to afflict *him* … and *he* built". English uses the plural for a people.
- **"let us deal wisely" kept** (v10) — Hebrew נִתְחַכְּמָה, the hitpael of חָכַם "be wise": "let us show ourselves wise toward him". The word says "wise"; that the wisdom is cunning comes from the context. **"Come"** — Hebrew הָבָה, literally "give".
- **"when there falleth out any war" → "when war befalls"** (v10) — Hebrew כִּי־תִקְרֶאנָה מִלְחָמָה. The verb is a plural (or an old energic) form with a singular noun; the Samaritan text is said to read "befalls us" (cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"join also unto our enemies" → "they also are added to those who hate us"** (v10) — Hebrew וְנוֹסַף גַּם־הוּא עַל־שֹׂנְאֵינוּ: a niphal ("be added") and the participle of שָׂנֵא "hate". **"and so get them up out of the land" → "and go up from the land"** — Hebrew וְעָלָה מִן־הָאָרֶץ, "go up", the word used of going from Egypt to Canaan (Gen 50:24).
- **"taskmasters" → "chiefs of forced labor"** (v11) — Hebrew שָׂרֵי מִסִּים: שַׂר is "prince, chief", here the head of a body of men; מַס is forced labor (Gen 49:15), here in the plural: the labor gangs. "Taskmaster" is kept for another word, נֹגֵשׂ (3:7; 5:6).
- **"afflict" kept; "burdens" kept** (v11, 12) — Hebrew עִנָּה, the word of Gen 15:13 ("they will afflict them four hundred years"), and סְבָלוֹת, loads that are carried. "*Their* burdens" has a plural suffix: the burdens the chiefs lay on, or the people's own.
- **"treasure cities" → "store cities"** (v11) — Hebrew עָרֵי מִסְכְּנוֹת, cities for stores (1 Kgs 9:19; 2 Chr 32:28). The Septuagint adds a third city, On (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"the more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and grew" → "as they afflicted them, so they multiplied and so they spread abroad"** (v12) — Hebrew כַּאֲשֶׁר … כֵּן … וְכֵן "as … so … and so"; the verbs are imperfects of repeated action. יִפְרֹץ is literally "broke out" (Gen 28:14; 30:30).
- **"they were grieved because of" → "they felt loathing because of"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיָּקֻצוּ מִפְּנֵי: קוּץ is to loathe, to feel a sickening disgust (Gen 27:46); with מִפְּנֵי, literally "from the face of", it is a loathing mixed with dread (Num 22:3). No subject is named; the Egyptians are meant.
- **"with rigour" → "with harshness"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew בְּפָרֶךְ, a rare word (Lev 25:43, 46, 53; Ezek 34:4), perhaps literally "with crushing".
- **"hard bondage" → "hard service"; "made them serve" kept** (v13, 14) — one root five times in two verses: וַיַּעֲבִדוּ "made serve", עֲבֹדָה "service" (three times), עָבְדוּ "served". The last clause is literally "all their service which they served through them with harshness": עָבַד בְּ is to work by means of someone, to make him serve. That clause hangs loosely on the sentence, as in the Hebrew.
- **"morter" → "mortar"** (v14) — Hebrew חֹמֶר, clay, as in Gen 11:3.
- **"the other" → "the second"** (v15) — Hebrew הַשֵּׁנִית.
- **"Hebrew midwives" kept** (v15) — Hebrew לַמְיַלְּדֹת הָעִבְרִיֹּת. As pointed, "Hebrew" is an adjective: the midwives are Hebrew women. The consonants also allow "the midwives of the Hebrew women", which would leave their own people open. עִבְרִי: see Gen 14:13.
- **"When ye do the office of a midwife to" → "When you help … to bear"** (v16) — Hebrew בְּיַלֶּדְכֶן, the piel of יָלַד "bear": to help a woman bear; the midwife is the מְיַלֶּדֶת, the one who does this.
- **"upon the stools" → "upon the birthstool"** (v16) — Hebrew עַל־הָאָבְנָיִם, a dual form found again only in Jer 18:3 (the potter's wheel, two discs). Literally perhaps "the two stones". The meaning is uncertain: the two stones or bricks on which a woman knelt to give birth is the best-supported sense.
- **"then ye shall kill him" → "you shall put him to death"** (v16) — Hebrew וַהֲמִתֶּן, the hifil of מוּת "die".
- **"saved the men children alive" → "let the children live"** (v17, 18, 22) — Hebrew וַתְּחַיֶּיןָ אֶת־הַיְלָדִים, the piel of חָיָה "live": to let live. יְלָדִים is "children" (masculine plural); the word does not say "boys", though the sons are meant.
- **"as the king of Egypt commanded them" → "had spoken to them"** (v17) — Hebrew דִּבֶּר "speak".
- **"lively" kept** (v19) — Hebrew חָיוֹת, found only here, from חָיָה "live": full of life. With other vowels the same consonants are "living things, animals" (חַיּוֹת). The exact sense is uncertain. The answer begins with כִּי "because". **"and are delivered ere the midwives come in unto them" → "before the midwife comes to them, they bear"** — Hebrew בְּטֶרֶם תָּבוֹא אֲלֵהֶן הַמְיַלֶּדֶת וְיָלָדוּ, in this order, literally "before the midwife comes to them — and they bear"; "midwife" is singular.
- **"dealt well with" → "did good to"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיֵּיטֶב, the hifil of יָטַב "be good".
- **"he made them houses" → "he made houses for them"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיַּעַשׂ לָהֶם בָּתִּים. "He" is not named; *Elohim* is the nearest subject. "For them" is a masculine form (לָהֶם), where a feminine would be expected for the midwives; it may be loosely used, or may point to the people of v20. "Houses" are households, families (2 Sam 7:11).
- **"charged" → "commanded … saying"** (v22) — Hebrew וַיְצַו … לֵאמֹר.
- **"Every son that is born ye shall cast into the river" → "Every son that is born, you shall throw him into the Nile"** (v22) — Hebrew כָּל־הַבֵּן הַיִּלּוֹד הַיְאֹרָה תַּשְׁלִיכֻהוּ, with the object first and taken up again by "him". The Hebrew does not say "to the Hebrews"; the Samaritan text and the Septuagint add it (cited from memory). הַיְאֹר *ha-Ye'or* is the Nile, an Egyptian word for "the river".

### Notes

- **Transition from Genesis** — vv1–7 recap Gen 46:8–27 and Gen 50:22–26. Genesis ended with Joseph dying in Egypt; Exodus begins with the consequences: a new political reality, and the fulfillment of Gen 15:13's prediction of oppression.
- **Shiphrah and Puah (v15)** — named women in the act of saving lives, in a chapter where the king is unnamed. The narrator's focus-reversal is pointed: the history remembers the midwives; the Pharaoh's name is lost.
- **"Hebrew midwives" (v15)** — ambiguous: Hebrew *meyaledot ha-ivriyot* can mean "midwives of the Hebrews" (Egyptian women who served Hebrew clients) or "Hebrew midwives" (ethnic Hebrews themselves). Jewish tradition favors the latter (identifying them as Jochebed and Miriam).
- **"Pharaoh" unnamed** — intentional? The historical Pharaoh's name is omitted throughout the Exodus narrative. Dating debates (18th-dynasty Thutmose vs. 19th-dynasty Ramses) depend on archaeology, not the text.
