# Exodus 16

[1] They set out from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came to the Wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their departure from the land of Egypt.

[2] The whole congregation of the children of Israel grumbled against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness,

[3] and the children of Israel said to them, "If only we had died by the hand of Yahweh in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the pots of meat, when we ate bread to the full! For you have brought us out into this wilderness to kill this whole assembly with hunger."

[4] Then Yahweh said to Moses, "Behold, I am about to rain bread from the heavens for you; and the people shall go out and gather each day's portion on its day, that I may test them, whether they will walk in my instruction or not.

[5] And on the sixth day, when they prepare what they bring in, it shall be twice as much as they gather daily."

[6] So Moses and Aaron said to all the children of Israel, "In the evening you shall know that Yahweh has brought you out of the land of Egypt;

[7] and in the morning you shall see the glory of Yahweh, because he has heard your grumblings against Yahweh. What are we, that you grumble against us?"

[8] Moses said, "When Yahweh gives you meat to eat in the evening, and bread in the morning to the full — because Yahweh has heard your grumblings that you grumble against him — what are we? Your grumblings are not against us but against Yahweh."

[9] Moses said to Aaron, "Say to all the congregation of the children of Israel, 'Draw near before Yahweh, for he has heard your grumblings.'"

[10] As Aaron spoke to the whole congregation of the children of Israel, they looked toward the wilderness, and behold, the glory of Yahweh appeared in the cloud.

[11] Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying,

[12] "I have heard the grumblings of the children of Israel. Speak to them, saying, 'At twilight you shall eat meat, and in the morning you shall be filled with bread; and you shall know that I am Yahweh your *Elohim*.'"

[13] In the evening quails came up and covered the camp; and in the morning the dew lay around the camp.

[14] When the dew that lay had evaporated, behold, on the face of the wilderness lay a fine, flake-like thing, fine as frost on the ground.

[15] When the children of Israel saw it, they said to one another, "Man-hu?" (What is it?) — for they did not know what it was. Moses said to them, "This is the bread that Yahweh has given you to eat.

[16] This is what Yahweh has commanded: 'Gather of it, each according to what he can eat — an omer for each person, according to the number of your persons; each man take for those in his tent.'"

[17] The children of Israel did so; they gathered, some more, some less.

[18] But when they measured it with the omer, the one who gathered much had no excess, and the one who gathered little had no lack; each gathered according to what he could eat.

[19] Moses said to them, "Let no one leave any of it over until morning."

[20] Yet some did not listen to Moses; they left some of it over until morning, and it bred worms and stank. Moses was angry with them.

[21] Morning by morning they gathered it, each according to what he could eat; and when the sun grew hot, it melted.

[22] On the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for each person. All the leaders of the congregation came and told Moses;

[23] and he said to them, "This is what Yahweh has said: 'Tomorrow is a day of solemn rest, a holy Sabbath to Yahweh. Bake what you will bake today, and boil what you will boil; and all that is left over, put aside for yourselves to be kept until the morning.'"

[24] They set it aside until the morning, as Moses commanded; and it did not stink, and there were no worms in it.

[25] Moses said, "Eat that today, for today is a Sabbath to Yahweh; today you will not find it in the field.

[26] Six days you shall gather it; but on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there shall be none."

[27] Yet on the seventh day some of the people went out to gather, and they found none.

[28] Yahweh said to Moses, "How long will you refuse to keep my commandments and my instructions?

[29] See! Yahweh has given you the Sabbath; therefore on the sixth day he gives you bread for two days. Let each man remain in his place; let no man go out of his place on the seventh day."

[30] So the people rested on the seventh day.

[31] The house of Israel called its name Manna. It was like coriander seed, white; and its taste was like wafers with honey.

[32] Moses said, "This is what Yahweh has commanded: 'Fill an omer of it to be kept for your generations, that they may see the bread with which I fed you in the wilderness when I brought you out of the land of Egypt.'"

[33] Moses said to Aaron, "Take a jar and put an omer-full of manna in it, and set it before Yahweh, to be kept for your generations."

[34] As Yahweh commanded Moses, Aaron set it before the Testimony, to be kept.

[35] The children of Israel ate manna for forty years, until they came to a habitable land; they ate manna until they came to the border of the land of Canaan.

[36] (An omer is the tenth of an ephah.)

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Wilderness of Sin" (v1)** — Hebrew מִדְבַּר־סִין *midbar-Sin*. Geographical region; not related to English "sin" (Hebrew word is unrelated).
- **"flesh pots" → "pots of meat"** (v3) — Hebrew סִיר הַבָּשָׂר *sir ha-basar*. Modernized.
- **"prove" → "test"** (v4) — Hebrew אֲנַסֶּנּוּ *anassennu*, as in 15:25.
- **"law" → "instruction"** (v4, 28) — Hebrew תּוֹרָה *torah*, as in 13:9.
- **"manna" — etymology preserved** (v15, 31) — Hebrew מָן הוּא *man hu* = "What is it?" The name *manna* is from the question. The substance remains unidentified botanically/chemically; possible identifications include tamarisk-tree sap or lichen, but the text presents it as a specifically miraculous provision. Wordplay preserved parenthetically.
- **"hoar frost" → "frost"** (v14) — archaic compound modernized.
- **"omer" (v16, 18, 22, 32, 33, 36)** — Hebrew עֹמֶר *omer*. Volume measure, roughly 2–4 liters. Preserved as technical measure.
- **"sabbath" → "Sabbath"** (v23, 25, 26, 29) — Hebrew שַׁבָּת *Shabbat*. Hebrew name preserved with capitalization. First explicit Sabbath-ordinance in Exodus — delivered through the manna-gathering pattern.
- **"seethe" → "boil"** (v23) — archaic → modern.
- **"Testimony" (v34)** — Hebrew עֵדֻת *edut* = testimony / witness. Proto-reference to the Ark of the Covenant (made later, Ex 25), where the manna jar would eventually be kept alongside the tablets.

### Notes

- **Manna theology** — the daily-bread provision (except on Sabbath, double portion the day before) teaches Israel rhythmic trust. No storage, no hoarding (v19–20): each day's sufficiency from Yahweh. Jesus echoes this in the Lord's Prayer ("give us this day our daily bread") and in John 6, where he identifies himself as the true bread from heaven, fulfilling the manna-type.
- **Sabbath institution** — the Sabbath is introduced operationally here, before the formal commandment of ch 20. Creation-precedent (Gen 2:2–3) is now made practical through the rhythm of gathering / not-gathering.
- **"Remain in his place" (v29)** — basis for rabbinic "Sabbath-limit" (*techum shabbat*) — the maximum distance walkable on Sabbath beyond one's settlement.
