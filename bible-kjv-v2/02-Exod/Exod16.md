[14] And the layer of dew went up, and behold, on the face of the wilderness a fine flaky thing, fine as the frost on the earth.

[35] And the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ ate the manna forty years, until they came to a land dwelt in; the manna they ate until they came to the end of the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v12) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (throughout) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל. **"congregation"** (v1, 2, 9, 10, 22) is עֵדָה, **"assembly"** (v3) is קָהָל: two words, kept apart.
- **"took their journey" → "set out"** (v1) — Hebrew נָסַע, literally "pull up" (the tent pegs). **"Wilderness of Sin"** — Hebrew מִדְבַּר־סִין *midbar-Sin*, a place name; nothing to do with the English word "sin".
- **"after their departing out of" → "of their going out from"** (v1) — Hebrew לְצֵאתָם, "to / of their going out": the month is counted from the going out.
- **"murmured" → "grumbled"** (v2, 7, 8, 9, 12) — Hebrew לוּן with עַל "against", and the noun תְּלֻנּוֹת "grumblings". In v2 and v7 the written text (ketiv) and the reading tradition (qere) have different stems of the same verb; the meaning is the same.
- **"Would to God we had died" → "Would that we had died"** (v3) — Hebrew מִי־יִתֵּן מוּתֵנוּ, literally "who will give our dying": the Hebrew way of saying a wish. The Hebrew has no "God" here.
- **"by the flesh pots" → "by the pot of flesh"** (v3) — Hebrew סִיר הַבָּשָׂר, singular. בָּשָׂר is "flesh", here meat (also v8, 12). **"to the full"** (v3, 8) — Hebrew לָשֹׂבַע / לִשְׂבֹּעַ, "to satiety"; the same root in v12, **"be filled" → "be sated"**.
- **"to kill this whole assembly" → "to put all this assembly to death"** (v3) — Hebrew לְהָמִית, the causative of "die".
- **"I will rain bread" → "I am causing bread to rain"** (v4) — Hebrew הִנְנִי מַמְטִיר, "behold me" with a participle: what is now beginning or about to happen.
- **"a certain rate every day" → "the matter of a day in its day"** (v4) — Hebrew דְּבַר־יוֹם בְּיוֹמוֹ, literally "the word / matter of a day in its day": what belongs to each day, on that day.
- **"prove them" → "test them"** (v4) — Hebrew נִסָּה, to put to the proof. The Hebrew has the singular ("test it / him … whether it will walk"): the people as one.
- **"my law" → "my torah"** (v4), **"my laws" → "my torot"** (v28) — Hebrew תּוֹרָה *torah*, "instruction, teaching"; plural *torot*.
- **"twice as much as they gather daily" → "double what they gather day by day"** (v5) — Hebrew מִשְׁנֶה עַל, "a double over"; יוֹם יוֹם "day, day".
- **"At even, then ye shall know … And in the morning, then ye shall see" kept** (v6, 7) — Hebrew עֶרֶב וִידַעְתֶּם … וּבֹקֶר וּרְאִיתֶם, literally "Evening — and you shall know … and morning — and you shall see". **"for that he heareth" → "when he hears"** (v7) — Hebrew בְּשָׁמְעוֹ, "in his hearing": when, or because, he hears.
- **v8** — the Hebrew sentence has no main clause ("In Yahweh's giving you … in Yahweh's hearing … — and we, what are we?"); it is left so. KJV supplies "This shall be". **"Not against us are your grumblings"** keeps the Hebrew order, which puts the stress on "not against us".
- **"they looked toward" → "they turned toward"** (v10) — Hebrew פָּנָה "turn". **"appeared"** — Hebrew נִרְאָה, literally "was seen".
- **"At even" → "Between the two evenings"** (v12) — Hebrew בֵּין הָעַרְבַּיִם, a dual: "between the two evenings". Which hours are meant is disputed (dusk, between sunset and dark; or from the sun's decline to sunset). In v6, 8, 13 the plain word עֶרֶב "evening" stands.
- **"the quails" → "the quail"** (v13) — Hebrew הַשְּׂלָו, a singular used for the whole flight. **"the dew lay" → "there was a layer of dew"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew שִׁכְבַת הַטַּל, literally "a lying of the dew". **"was gone up" → "went up"** (v14).
- **"a small round thing, as small as the hoar frost on the ground" → "a fine flaky thing, fine as the frost on the earth"** (v14) — Hebrew דַּק "thin, fine" twice (the word for the "thin" ears and cows of Gen 41; here of a thing in small grains). מְחֻסְפָּס is found only here and its meaning is not certain: "flaky, scale-like" is the best-supported sense; "round" is a guess. אֶרֶץ is "earth, land".
- **"It is manna" → "What is it?"** (v15) — Hebrew מָן הוּא *man hu*. The verse itself explains the words: "for they did not know what (מַה) it was". מָן is not the usual Hebrew word for "what" (that is מָה); it is taken as an old or dialect form of it, and the name of the food in v31, *man*, sounds the same — the wordplay cannot be carried into English. The words can also be read as a statement, "It is *man*" (so KJV). **"one to another" → "each man to his brother"** — Hebrew אִישׁ אֶל־אָחִיו.
- **"to eat" → "for food"** (v15) — Hebrew לְאָכְלָה, a noun; "to eat" (v8) is the verb.
- **"the thing which the LORD hath commanded" → "the word that Yahweh has commanded"** (v16, 32) — Hebrew הַדָּבָר, "word" or "thing"; with "commanded" it is the word.
- **"every man according to his eating" kept** (v16, 18, 21) — Hebrew אִישׁ לְפִי אָכְלוֹ, literally "each man by the mouth of his eating": as much as he eats. **"an omer for every man" → "an omer a head"** (v16) — Hebrew לַגֻּלְגֹּלֶת, "for the skull". **"your persons" → "your souls"** — Hebrew נֶפֶשׁ.
- **"omer"** (v16, 18, 22, 32, 33, 36) — Hebrew עֹמֶר, a measure of volume (about 2 litres); the word is kept, as is **"ephah"** (v36, about 22 litres). The sizes are estimates.
- **"some more, some less" → "he who gathered much and he who gathered little"** (v17, 18) — Hebrew הַמַּרְבֶּה וְהַמַּמְעִיט, literally "he who made much and he who made little"; "gathered" is supplied from the verb before. **"had nothing over … had no lack"** (v18) — Hebrew לֹא הֶעְדִּיף … לֹא הֶחְסִיר (cited in 2 Cor 8:15).
- **"leave of it" → "leave any of it over"** (v19, 20) — Hebrew הוֹתִיר, "make remain". **"some of them left" → "men left"** (v20) — Hebrew אֲנָשִׁים "men".
- **"hearkened not unto" → "did not listen to"** (v20) — Hebrew שָׁמַע אֶל. **"was wroth" → "was wrathful"** — Hebrew קָצַף, another word than "anger" (אַף).
- **"it bred worms"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיָּרֻם תּוֹלָעִים, literally "it rose / grew high with worms"; the verb is uncertain (from רוּם "be high" or a root "be wormy"). **"worm" → "maggot"** (v24) — Hebrew רִמָּה, another word than the תּוֹלָעִים "worms" of v20.
- **"every morning" → "morning by morning"** (v21) — Hebrew בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר. **"when the sun waxed hot, it melted" → "and the sun grew hot, and it melted"** — two clauses joined by "and".
- **"twice as much bread" → "double bread"** (v22) — Hebrew לֶחֶם מִשְׁנֶה. **"rulers" → "chieftains"** — Hebrew נְשִׂיאִים, "ones lifted up".
- **"the rest of the holy sabbath" → "a shabbaton, a holy Shabbat"** (v23) — Hebrew שַׁבָּתוֹן שַׁבַּת־קֹדֶשׁ, two nouns side by side, both without the article. שַׁבָּתוֹן *shabbaton* is formed from שַׁבָּת: a Shabbat-keeping, a ceasing. שַׁבַּת־קֹדֶשׁ is "a Shabbat of holiness". Both come from שָׁבַת "cease" (v30). This is the first time the noun *Shabbat* stands in the Bible. The words "to day" (KJV: "bake to day") are not in the Hebrew.
- **"lay up" kept** (v23, 24, 33, 34) — Hebrew הִנִּיחַ, "put down, deposit" (from נוּחַ "rest"). **"to be kept" → "for keeping"** (v23, 32, 33, 34) — Hebrew לְמִשְׁמֶרֶת, a noun from שָׁמַר "keep".
- **"on the seventh day, which is the sabbath, in it there shall be none" → "on the seventh day, a Shabbat, there will be none on it"** (v26) — Hebrew וּבַיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי שַׁבָּת לֹא יִהְיֶה־בּוֹ; it can also be divided "on the seventh day is a Shabbat; there will be none on it".
- **"How long refuse ye" → "How long have you refused"** (v28) — Hebrew perfect מֵאַנְתֶּם. The "you" is plural: Moses is addressed for the people.
- **"abide ye every man in his place" → "Sit, each man where he is"** (v29) — Hebrew שְׁבוּ אִישׁ תַּחְתָּיו, literally "sit, each man under himself": יָשַׁב is "sit, dwell", and תַּחְתָּיו is another word than the "place" (מָקוֹם) that follows. **"the bread of two days"** — Hebrew לֶחֶם יוֹמָיִם, a dual.
- **"rested" → "ceased"** (v30) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁבְּתוּ, the verb שָׁבַת "cease, stop" from which *Shabbat* is named (Gen 2:2, 3). "Rest" is another verb (נוּחַ, Exod 20:11).
- **"Manna" → "manna"** (v31, 33, 35) — Hebrew מָן *man*; "manna" is the form the word has in English (through the Greek and Aramaic). **"wafers made with honey" → "a wafer in honey"** (v31) — Hebrew צַפִּיחִת, found only here: a flat cake, the exact sense uncertain. גַּד is taken as coriander; the identification is old but not certain.
- **"Fill an omer of it" → "The fullness of the omer of it is for keeping"** (v32, 33) — Hebrew מְלֹא הָעֹמֶר, a noun, "the fullness of the omer"; the sentence has no verb ("is" is supplied). **"wherewith I have fed you" → "that I made you eat"** (v32) — Hebrew הֶאֱכַלְתִּי, the causative of "eat".
- **"a pot" → "one jar"** (v33) — Hebrew צִנְצֶנֶת אַחַת; the noun is found only here and the kind of vessel is not known (the Septuagint has "a golden jar", cited from memory; cf. Heb 9:4). **"put there"** — Hebrew שָׁמָּה "there, into it".
- **"the Testimony" → "the testimony"** (v34) — Hebrew הָעֵדֻת *ha-edut*: in the later chapters the tablets in the ark (25:16, 21). The ark has not yet been made at this point of the story.
- **"a land inhabited" → "a land dwelt in"** (v35) — Hebrew אֶרֶץ נוֹשָׁבֶת, from יָשַׁב "dwell". **"the borders of" → "the end of"** — Hebrew קְצֵה "end, edge". The second half puts "the manna" first: "the manna they ate".
- **"Now an omer" → "And the omer"** (v36) — Hebrew וְהָעֹמֶר, with the article, as "the ephah".

### Notes

- **Manna theology** — the daily-bread provision (except on the Shabbat, double the day before) teaches Israel rhythmic trust. No storage, no hoarding (v19–20): each day's sufficiency from Yahweh. Jesus echoes this in the Lord's Prayer ("give us this day our daily bread") and in John 6, where he identifies himself as the true bread from heaven, fulfilling the manna-type.
- **Shabbat institution** — the Shabbat is introduced operationally here, before the formal commandment of ch 20. Creation-precedent (Gen 2:2–3) is now made practical through the rhythm of gathering / not-gathering.
- **"Each man where he is" (v29)** — basis for rabbinic "Sabbath-limit" (*techum shabbat*) — the maximum distance walkable on the Shabbat beyond one's settlement.
