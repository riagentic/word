# Exodus 36

[1] "Betzalel and Oholiab and every skillful man, in whom Yahweh has put skill and understanding to know how to do every work for the service of the sanctuary, shall work according to all that Yahweh has commanded."

[2] Moses called Betzalel and Oholiab and every skillful man in whose heart Yahweh had put skill — everyone whose heart stirred him to come to the work to do it.

[3] They received from Moses all the contribution that the children of Israel had brought for the work of the service of the sanctuary, to do it. They continued to bring him freewill offerings morning after morning,

[4] so that all the skilled craftsmen who were doing every work of the sanctuary came, each from the work he was doing,

[5] and they said to Moses, "The people are bringing more than enough for the work that Yahweh has commanded to be done."

[6] So Moses gave command, and a proclamation was made throughout the camp: "Let no man or woman do any more work for the contribution of the sanctuary." So the people were restrained from bringing,

[7] for the material that they had was sufficient for all the work to be done, and more than enough.

[8] Every skillful man among those who did the work made the dwelling with ten curtains of fine twined linen, blue, purple, and scarlet yarns; with cherubim skillfully worked into them they were made.

[9] The length of each curtain was 28 cubits, and the width of each curtain 4 cubits; all the curtains were of the same size.

[10] He joined five curtains together, and the other five he joined together.

[11] He made loops of blue on the edge of the outermost curtain in the first set; likewise he did on the edge of the outermost curtain in the second set.

[12] Fifty loops he made in the one curtain, and fifty loops he made on the edge of the curtain in the second set; the loops were opposite one another.

[13] He made fifty clasps of gold and joined the curtains to one another with the clasps; so the dwelling became a unity.

[14] He made curtains of goats' hair for the tent over the dwelling — eleven curtains he made them.

[15] The length of each curtain was 30 cubits, and the width of each curtain 4 cubits; the eleven curtains were of the same size.

[16] He joined five curtains by themselves and six curtains by themselves.

[17] He made fifty loops on the edge of the outermost curtain in the first set, and fifty loops on the edge of the curtain in the second set.

[18] He made fifty clasps of bronze to join the tent together, that it might be a unity.

[19] He made a covering for the tent of tanned ram skins, and a covering of fine leather above that.

[20] Then he made the frames for the dwelling of acacia wood, standing upright.

[21] The length of each frame was 10 cubits, and the width of each frame a cubit and a half.

[22] Each frame had two tenons fitted parallel to one another; thus he did for all the frames of the dwelling.

[23] And he made the frames for the dwelling: twenty frames for the south side southward,

[24] with forty sockets of silver under the twenty frames — two sockets under one frame for its two tenons, and two sockets under another frame for its two tenons;

[25] and for the other side of the dwelling, the north side, he made twenty frames,

[26] with their forty sockets of silver — two sockets under one frame and two sockets under another frame;

[27] for the rear of the dwelling, westward, he made six frames.

[28] Two frames he made for the corners of the dwelling in the rear.

[29] They were doubled from beneath, and together they were complete at the top with one ring; thus he did for both of them at the two corners.

[30] There were eight frames, with their sockets of silver, sixteen sockets — under every frame, two sockets.

[31] He made bars of acacia wood: five for the frames of one side of the dwelling,

[32] and five bars for the frames of the other side of the dwelling, and five bars for the frames of the rear of the dwelling, westward.

[33] He made the middle bar to pass through from end to end, halfway up the frames.

[34] He overlaid the frames with gold, and made their rings of gold, as holders for the bars; and he overlaid the bars with gold.

[35] He made the veil of blue, purple, and scarlet yarns, and fine twined linen; with cherubim skillfully worked into it he made it.

[36] He made for it four pillars of acacia and overlaid them with gold; their hooks were of gold, and he cast for them four sockets of silver.

[37] He made a screen for the entrance of the tent, of blue, purple, and scarlet yarns and fine twined linen, embroidered with needlework;

[38] and its five pillars with their hooks. He overlaid their tops and their bands with gold; but their five sockets were of bronze.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"taches" → "clasps"** (v13, 18) — as in ch 26.
- **"boards" → "frames"** (v20ff) — as in ch 26.
- **"chapiters" → "tops"** (v38) — Hebrew רָאשֵׁיהֶם *rashehem* = their heads / tops. "Chapiter" is archaic English for column-top / capital.
- **Mostly parallels ch 26** — execution of instructions given there.

### Notes

- **"Too much to give"** (v5–7) — the only occurrence in the Bible of the faithful being restrained from over-contributing. Striking: Yahweh gets too much offering, not too little. The community's outpouring exceeds even the vast needs.
- **Repetition by design** — chs 36–39 repeat ch 25–30's content almost verbatim in execution-mode. The deliberate doubling emphasizes obedient fulfillment; what was said is done exactly as said. Literary redundancy is theological affirmation.
