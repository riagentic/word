# Exodus 28

[1] "Bring near to yourself Aaron your brother, and his sons with him, from among the children of Israel, to serve me as priests — Aaron, and Aaron's sons: Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.

[2] You shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother, for glory and for beauty.

[3] You shall speak to all the skillful of heart, whom I have filled with a spirit of skill, that they may make Aaron's garments to consecrate him, that he may serve me as priest.

[4] These are the garments that they shall make: a breastpiece, an ephod, a robe, a tunic of checker-work, a turban, and a sash. They shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother and his sons to serve me as priests.

[5] They shall take gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarns, and fine linen.

[6] They shall make the ephod of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarns, and of fine twined linen — skillfully worked.

[7] It shall have two shoulder-pieces attached to its two edges, so that it may be joined together.

[8] The skillfully woven band on it for fastening, of the same workmanship, shall be of one piece with it — of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarns, and fine twined linen.

[9] You shall take two onyx stones and engrave on them the names of the children of Israel:

[10] six of their names on the one stone and the names of the remaining six on the other stone, according to their birth order.

[11] With the work of an engraver in stone, as the engravings of a signet, you shall engrave the two stones with the names of the children of Israel. You shall set them in settings of gold filigree.

[12] You shall set the two stones on the shoulder-pieces of the ephod as memorial stones for the children of Israel; and Aaron shall bear their names before Yahweh on his two shoulders as a memorial.

[13] You shall make settings of gold filigree,

[14] and two chains of pure gold, twisted like cords; and you shall fasten the corded chains to the filigree settings.

[15] You shall make a breastpiece of judgment, skillfully worked. You shall make it in the style of the ephod — of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarns, and of fine twined linen.

[16] It shall be square when doubled; a span shall be its length and a span its width.

[17] You shall set in it mounted stones, in four rows: a row of ruby, chrysolite, and beryl shall be the first row;

[18] and the second row: turquoise, sapphire, and emerald;

[19] and the third row: jacinth, agate, and amethyst;

[20] and the fourth row: topaz, onyx, and jasper. They shall be set in gold filigree.

[21] The stones shall be according to the names of the sons of Israel, twelve, according to their names; they shall be like the engravings of a signet, each with its name, for the twelve tribes.

[22] You shall make for the breastpiece chains of pure gold, twisted like cords.

[23] You shall make for the breastpiece two rings of gold, and you shall put the two rings on the two ends of the breastpiece.

[24] You shall put the two twisted chains of gold in the two rings at the ends of the breastpiece.

[25] And the other two ends of the two twisted chains you shall fasten on the two filigree settings, and attach them to the shoulder-pieces of the ephod in front.

[26] You shall make two rings of gold, and put them at the two ends of the breastpiece, at its inner edge next to the ephod.

[27] You shall make two more rings of gold and attach them in front to the lower part of the two shoulder-pieces of the ephod, close to where it is joined, above the skillfully woven band of the ephod.

[28] They shall bind the breastpiece by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a blue cord, so that it may lie on the skillfully woven band of the ephod, and that the breastpiece not come loose from the ephod.

[29] So Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of Israel on the breastpiece of judgment on his heart, when he enters the Holy Place, as a continual memorial before Yahweh.

[30] In the breastpiece of judgment you shall put the Urim and the Thummim, and they shall be on Aaron's heart when he goes in before Yahweh; and Aaron shall bear the judgment of the children of Israel on his heart before Yahweh continually.

[31] You shall make the robe of the ephod all of blue.

[32] It shall have an opening for the head in the middle of it, with a woven binding around the opening — like the opening of a coat of mail — that it may not be torn.

[33] On its hem you shall make pomegranates of blue, purple, and scarlet yarns, around its hem; and bells of gold between them all around —

[34] a golden bell and a pomegranate, a golden bell and a pomegranate, around the hem of the robe.

[35] It shall be on Aaron when he ministers, so that its sound may be heard when he goes into the Holy Place before Yahweh and when he comes out, so that he does not die.

[36] You shall make a plate of pure gold and engrave on it, like the engravings of a signet, 'Holy to Yahweh.'

[37] You shall fasten it on a blue cord, so that it may be on the turban; it shall be on the front of the turban.

[38] It shall be on Aaron's forehead, and Aaron shall bear any iniquity in connection with the holy things that the children of Israel consecrate, of all their holy gifts; it shall always be on his forehead, that they may be accepted before Yahweh.

[39] You shall weave the tunic in checker-work of fine linen, and make a turban of fine linen, and you shall make a sash, the work of an embroiderer.

[40] For Aaron's sons you shall make tunics, sashes, and headdresses; you shall make them for glory and beauty.

[41] You shall put them on Aaron your brother and his sons with him; you shall anoint them, ordain them, and consecrate them, that they may serve me as priests.

[42] You shall make for them linen undergarments to cover their naked flesh; they shall reach from the waist to the thighs.

[43] They shall be on Aaron and on his sons when they go into the Tent of Meeting or when they come near the altar to minister in the Holy Place, that they not bear iniquity and die. This shall be a statute forever for him and for his offspring after him."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"minister unto me in the priest's office" → "serve me as priests"** (v1, 3, 4, 41) — Hebrew לְכַהֲנוֹ־לִי *le-khahano-li* = "to act as priest for me." *Kohen* = priest. Modernized verbal phrase.
- **"wise hearted" / "spirit of wisdom" → "skillful of heart" / "spirit of skill"** (v3) — Hebrew חַכְמֵי־לֵב / רוּחַ חָכְמָה *chakhme-lev / ruach chakhmah*. *Chakhmah* here = skill / craftsmanship (artisan expertise), not "wisdom" in the philosophical sense. "Skillful craftsmen" fits context.
- **"breastplate" → "breastpiece"** (v4, 15, 22, 23, 24, 26, 28, 29, 30) — Hebrew חֹשֶׁן *choshen*. Covered the high priest's chest. Not armor; ceremonial.
- **"mitre" → "turban"** (v4, 37, 39) — Hebrew מִצְנֶפֶת *mitsnefet*. Wound cloth head-covering, not a bishop's-miter-style hat. "Mitre" in KJV reflects medieval ecclesiastical imagination; "turban" is geographically / historically accurate.
- **"girdle" → "sash"** (v4, 39, 40) — Hebrew אַבְנֵט *avnet*. Waist-sash.
- **"broidered coat" → "tunic of checker-work"** (v4, 39) — Hebrew כְּתֹנֶת תַּשְׁבֵּץ *ketonet tashbetz*. Woven-checkered-pattern tunic.
- **"ouches" → "settings of gold filigree"** (v11, 13, 14, 25) — Hebrew מִשְׁבְּצֹת *mishbetsot*. Filigree / basket-setting for precious stones. "Ouches" is archaic English for a brooch-like setting.
- **"stones list" — classical identifications uncertain** (v17–20) — the twelve stones have been variously identified across translations. The ancient Hebrew gem-names are not always confidently identifiable with modern mineralogical categories. Translations give provisional identifications; the names and order matter symbolically (one per tribe) more than the specific gemology.
- **"Urim and Thummim" (v30)** — Hebrew אוּרִים וְתֻמִּים *Urim we-Tummim*. Divinatory objects worn by the high priest (mechanism unclear — possibly lot-stones). *Urim* = "lights"; *Tummim* = "completenesses / perfections." Preserved untranslated per convention.
- **"habergeon" → "coat of mail"** (v32) — Hebrew תַּחְרָא *tachra*. Coat of mail (neck-reinforced). "Habergeon" is medieval English. The robe's neck-hole is woven like armor's neck-opening to prevent tearing.
- **"pomegranates" / "bells" (v33–35)** — alternating around the hem. The sound of the bells and the color of the pomegranates. Perhaps apotropaic / signal-of-presence while serving in the sacred space.
- **"HOLINESS TO THE LORD" → "Holy to Yahweh"** (v36) — Hebrew קֹדֶשׁ לַיהוָה *qodesh la-Yahweh*. Engraved on the golden plate worn on the turban.
- **"linen breeches" → "linen undergarments"** (v42) — Hebrew מִכְנְסֵי־בָד *mikhnese-vad*. Modesty garments covering the priest's loins (cf. 20:26 on altar-step modesty).

### Notes

- **Aaronic priesthood established** — Aaron and sons are consecrated as the priestly line. Other Levites serve as assistants (Num 3). Non-Levites are excluded from priestly service (1 Sam 2, 1 Kgs 13).
- **Symbolic priestly garments** — the high priest bears Israel's names on shoulders (ephod stones) and on heart (breastpiece) — representing the nation before Yahweh. Typologically read as anticipating Christ as high priest (Hebrews).
- **Divinatory priesthood** — the Urim/Thummim grant the priest oracular function. The priest serves as divine-will-discerner for Israel in binary / multiple-choice questions (cf. 1 Sam 14:41, 23:9–12).
