# James 1

[1] James, a servant of *Elohim* and of the Lord *Yeshua* the *Mashiach*,

To the twelve tribes in the Dispersion:

Greetings.

[2] Count it all joy, my brothers, when you meet trials of various kinds,

[3] for you know that the testing of your faith produces steadfastness.

[4] And let steadfastness have its full effect, that you may be perfect and complete, lacking in nothing.

[5] If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask *Elohim*, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him.

[6] But let him ask in faith, with no doubting, for the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea that is driven and tossed by the wind.

[7] For that person must not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord;

[8] he is a double-minded man, unstable in all his ways.

[9] Let the lowly brother boast in his exaltation,

[10] and the rich in his humiliation, because like a flower of the grass he will pass away.

[11] For the sun rises with its scorching heat and withers the grass; its flower falls, and its beauty perishes. So also will the rich man fade away in the midst of his pursuits.

[12] Blessed is the man who remains steadfast under trial, for when he has stood the test he will receive the crown of life, which *Elohim* has promised to those who love him.

[13] Let no one say when he is tempted, "I am being tempted by *Elohim*," for *Elohim* cannot be tempted with evil, and he himself tempts no one.

[14] But each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed by his own desire.

[15] Then desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin, and sin when it is fully grown brings forth death.

[16] Do not be deceived, my beloved brothers.

[17] **Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow due to change.**

[18] Of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures.

[19] Know this, my beloved brothers: **let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger**;

[20] for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of *Elohim*.

[21] Therefore put away all filthiness and rampant wickedness and receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.

[22] But **be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves**.

[23] For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man who looks intently at his natural face in a mirror.

[24] For he looks at himself and goes away and at once forgets what he was like.

[25] But the one who looks into the perfect law, the law of liberty, and perseveres, being no hearer who forgets but a doer who acts, he will be blessed in his doing.

[26] If anyone thinks he is religious and does not bridle his tongue but deceives his heart, this person's religion is worthless.

[27] Religion that is pure and undefiled before *Elohim* the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"Jesus Christ" → "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (v1) — systematic dual substitution.
- **"scattered abroad" → "in the Dispersion"** (v1) — Greek ἐν τῇ διασπορᾷ *en tē diaspora*; "Dispersion" is the standard term; "scattered abroad" is a paraphrase.
- **"divers temptations" → "trials of various kinds"** (v2) — "divers" is KJV archaic for "various"; "temptations" in modern English implies moral temptation, but Greek πειρασμοί *peirasmoi* = "trials / tests / ordeals."
- **"the trying of your faith worketh patience" → "the testing of your faith produces steadfastness"** (v3) — Greek τὸ δοκίμιον ὑμῶν τῆς πίστεως *to dokimion hymōn tēs pisteōs* = "the tested quality of your faith / the testing of your faith"; "patience" → "steadfastness" (Greek ὑπομονήν *hypomonēn* = "patient endurance / steadfastness," not merely patience).
- **"upbraideth not" → "without reproach"** (v5) — Greek μὴ ὀνειδίζοντος *mē oneidizōntos* = "not reproaching / not upbraiding"; modernized.
- **"variableness, neither shadow of turning" → "no variation or shadow due to change"** (v17) — Greek παραλλαγὴ ἢ τροπῆς ἀποσκίασμα *parallagē ē tropēs aposkiasma*; astronomical metaphor: the heavenly lights shift and cast shadows; the Father of lights has no such shifting.
- **"Of his own will begat he us" → "Of his own will he brought us forth"** (v18) — Greek ἀποκύησεν *apokuēsen* = "gave birth to / brought forth"; "begat" is archaic; "brought us forth" preserves the birth metaphor.
- **"superfluity of naughtiness" → "rampant wickedness"** (v21) — Greek περισσείαν κακίας *perissleian kakias* = "abundance/overflow of wickedness"; "superfluity of naughtiness" is archaic and too mild in modern English.
- **"the engrafted word" → "the implanted word"** (v21) — Greek ἔμφυτον λόγον *emphyton logon* = "innate / implanted / engrafted word"; "implanted" is more naturally understood.
- **"beholding his natural face in a glass" → "looks intently at his natural face in a mirror"** (v23) — "glass" → "mirror"; "glass" is archaic for looking-glass.
- **"the perfect law of liberty" → "the perfect law, the law of liberty"** (v25) — Greek νόμον τέλειον τὸν τῆς ἐλευθερίας *nomon teleion ton tēs eleutherias*; the appositive structure preserved.
- **"Pure religion and undefiled before God" → "Religion that is pure and undefiled before *Elohim*"** (v27) — "God" → "*Elohim*"; restructured.
