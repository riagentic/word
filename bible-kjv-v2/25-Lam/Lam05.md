# Lamentations 5

[1] Remember, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, what has come upon us;
look and see our reproach.

[2] Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers,
our houses to foreigners.

[3] Fatherless we have become, and there is no father;
our mothers are like widows.

[4] Our water we have drunk for money;
our wood comes for a price.

[5] Upon our neck we are pursued;
we are weary, and no rest is given to us.

[6] To ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ we have given a hand,
to ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧, to be sated with bread.

[7] Our fathers have sinned, and they are not;
and we, we have carried their iniquities.

[8] Slaves have ruled over us;
there is none to tear us from their hand.

[9] With our soul we bring in our bread,
because of the sword of the wilderness.

[10] Our skin glows like an oven
because of the scorchings of famine.

[11] Women in ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧ they have humbled,
virgins in the cities of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧.

[12] Princes have been hanged by their hand;
the faces of elders have not been honored.

[13] Young men have carried the mill,
and boys have stumbled under the wood.

[14] Elders have ceased from the gate,
young men from their song.

[15] The exultation of our heart has ceased;
our dance has been turned to mourning.

[16] The crown of our head has fallen;
woe now to us, for we have sinned!

[17] Over this our heart has become unwell;
over these our eyes have grown dark,

[18] over Mount ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧, which is desolate;
foxes have walked about on it.

[19] You, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, sit forever;
your throne is to generation and generation.

[20] Why do you forget us forever,
forsake us for length of days?

[21] Bring us back, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, to you, and we shall return;
renew our days as of old.

[22] For if you have surely rejected us,
you have been wrathful against us exceedingly.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 19, 21) — Hebrew יהוה. No vocative "O" (v1, 19, 21).
- **"Remember, O LORD, what is come upon us: consider, and behold our reproach" → "Remember, Yahweh, what has come upon us; look and see our reproach"** (v1) — Hebrew זְכֹר "remember"; מֶה־הָיָה לָנוּ "what has been to us"; ketiv הביט, qere הַבִּיטָה "look" (the same sense); רְאֵה "see"; חֶרְפָּה "reproach" (its row; not "disgrace").
- **"Our inheritance is turned to strangers, our houses to aliens" → "Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers, our houses to foreigners"** (v2) — Hebrew נַחֲלָה "inheritance"; נֶהֶפְכָה "has been turned over" (הָפַךְ); זָרִים "strangers", נָכְרִים "foreigners" (their row, kept apart); בָּתִּים "houses".
- **"We are orphans and fatherless, our mothers are as widows" → "Fatherless we have become, and there is no father; our mothers are like widows"** (v3) — Hebrew יְתוֹמִים "fatherless" (its row; not "orphans": the mothers are alive, as the line says); הָיִינוּ "we have become"; ketiv אין "there is no", qere וְאֵין "and there is no" (the qere in the verse); אָב "father".
- **"We have drunken our water for money; our wood is sold unto us" → "Our water we have drunk for money; our wood comes for a price"** (v4) — Hebrew order (the object first); בְּכֶסֶף "for money" (the word is "silver", its row); בִּמְחִיר יָבֹאוּ "comes in for a price" (מְחִיר "price").
- **"Our necks are under persecution: we labour, and have no rest" → "Upon our neck we are pursued; we are weary, and no rest is given to us"** (v5) — Hebrew עַל צַוָּארֵנוּ "upon our neck" (צַוָּאר, its row); נִרְדָּפְנוּ "we are pursued" (רָדַף, passive); יָגַעְנוּ "we are weary"; ketiv לא "not", qere וְלֹא "and not" (the qere in the verse); הוּנַח "is given rest" (hofal of נוּחַ "rest").
- **"We have given the hand to the Egyptians, and to the Assyrians, to be satisfied with bread" → "To Egypt we have given a hand, to Assyria, to be sated with bread"** (v6) — Hebrew מִצְרַיִם and אַשּׁוּר, the land names (tokens `egypt`, `assyria`; not "the Egyptians"); נָתַנּוּ יָד "we have given a hand" (= submitted, made a pact); no "and"; לִשְׂבֹּעַ "to be sated" (שָׂבַע, its row).
- **"Our fathers have sinned, and are not; and we have borne their iniquities" → "Our fathers have sinned, and they are not; and we, we have carried their iniquities"** (v7) — ketiv אינם אנחנו "they are not, we", qere וְאֵינָם וַאֲנַחְנוּ "and they are not, and we" (the qere in the verse); אֲנַחְנוּ written beside the verb ("we, we"); סָבַל "carry" (a load: its row).
- **"Servants have ruled over us: there is none that doth deliver us out of their hand" → "Slaves have ruled over us; there is none to tear us from their hand"** (v8) — Hebrew עֲבָדִים "slaves" (the עֶבֶד row: men of no rank who rule); מָשַׁל "rule"; פֹּרֵק אֵין "a tearer away there is none" (פָּרַק "tear off", its row).
- **"We gat our bread with the peril of our lives because of the sword of the wilderness" → "With our soul we bring in our bread, because of the sword of the wilderness"** (v9) — Hebrew בְּנַפְשֵׁנוּ "with our soul" (= at the cost of our life: "peril" is not written); נָבִיא, an imperfect, "we bring in"; מִפְּנֵי "because of" (its row, of a cause; also v10); חֶרֶב הַמִּדְבָּר "the sword of the wilderness".
- **"Our skin was black like an oven because of the terrible famine" → "Our skin glows like an oven because of the scorchings of famine"** (v10) — Hebrew נִכְמָרוּ "glows" (grows hot: as Gen 43:30 of compassion; "black" is a guess from another root: uncertain); תַּנּוּר "oven"; זַלְעֲפוֹת "scorchings" (זַלְעָפָה, its row); רָעָב "famine".
- **"They ravished the women in Zion, and the maids in the cities of Judah" → "Women in Zion they have humbled, virgins in the cities of Judah"** (v11) — Hebrew עִנּוּ "they have humbled" (עִנָּה with a woman as object, reviewer row: Lam 5:11; that force is meant is plain from the siege and the line's setting, but the verb itself is "humble": said here); the Hebrew order (the object first); בְּתֻלֹת "virgins".
- **"Princes are hanged up by their hand: the faces of elders were not honoured" → "Princes have been hanged by their hand; the faces of elders have not been honored"** (v12) — Hebrew בְּיָדָם "by their hand" (by the enemies' hand, or hanged by their own hands: uncertain); נִתְלוּ "have been hanged"; פְּנֵי זְקֵנִים "the faces of elders"; נֶהְדָּרוּ "have been honored" (הָדַר, its row: shown deference).
- **"They took the young men to grind, and the children fell under the wood" → "Young men have carried the mill, and boys have stumbled under the wood"** (v13) — Hebrew טְחוֹן, only here, "the mill" (or "grinding"); נָשָׂאוּ "have carried" (the young men are the subject; "they took" is not written); נְעָרִים "boys" (its row); בָּעֵץ "under the wood" (lit. "in the wood"); כָּשַׁל "stumble".
- **"The elders have ceased from the gate, the young men from their musick" → "Elders have ceased from the gate, young men from their song"** (v14) — Hebrew זְקֵנִים, בַּחוּרִים without the article; שָׁבַת "cease" (its row; also v15); שַׁעַר "gate"; נְגִינָה "song" (as 3:14).
- **"The joy of our heart is ceased; our dance is turned into mourning" → "The exultation of our heart has ceased; our dance has been turned to mourning"** (v15) — Hebrew מְשׂוֹשׂ "exultation" (its row, as 2:15); נֶהְפַּךְ "has been turned"; מָחוֹל "dance"; אֵבֶל "mourning".
- **"The crown is fallen from our head: woe unto us, that we have sinned!" → "The crown of our head has fallen; woe now to us, for we have sinned!"** (v16) — Hebrew עֲטֶרֶת רֹאשֵׁנוּ "the crown of our head"; אוֹי־נָא לָנוּ "woe now to us" (אוֹי, its row; נָא "now", not dropped); כִּי "for".
- **"For this our heart is faint; for these things our eyes are dim" → "Over this our heart has become unwell; over these our eyes have grown dark"** (v17) — Hebrew עַל־זֶה … עַל־אֵלֶּה "over this … over these" (and v18 עַל "over": the three lines run together, so v17 ends with a comma); דָּוֶה "unwell" (as 1:13, 22); חָשְׁכוּ "have grown dark" (as 4:8).
- **"Because of the mountain of Zion, which is desolate, the foxes walk upon it" → "over Mount Zion, which is desolate; foxes have walked about on it"** (v18) — Hebrew הַר־צִיּוֹן "Mount Zion"; שָׁמֵם "desolate" (its row); שׁוּעָלִים "foxes" (or "jackals": uncertain); הִלְּכוּ, piel, "have walked about".
- **"Thou, O LORD, remainest for ever; thy throne from generation to generation" → "You, Yahweh, sit forever; your throne is to generation and generation"** (v19) — Hebrew אַתָּה "you" first (no "but"); תֵּשֵׁב "sit" (יָשַׁב of the body: sit enthroned; as Ps 9:7; 102:12); לְעוֹלָם "forever"; לְדֹר וָדוֹר "to generation and generation".
- **"Wherefore dost thou forget us for ever, and forsake us so long time?" → "Why do you forget us forever, forsake us for length of days?"** (v20) — Hebrew לָמָּה "why"; לָנֶצַח "forever" (נֶצַח, its row); עָזַב "forsake"; no "and"; לְאֹרֶךְ יָמִים "for length of days".
- **"Turn thou us unto thee, O LORD, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old" → "Bring us back, Yahweh, to you, and we shall return; renew our days as of old"** (v21) — Hebrew הֲשִׁיבֵנוּ "bring us back" (הֵשִׁיב, hifil of שׁוּב) and נָשׁוּבָה "we shall return" (qal), one root; ketiv ונשוב "and we shall return", qere וְנָשׁוּבָה (the same, with the cohortative "let us return": the qere in the verse, "we shall return"); חַדֵּשׁ "renew"; כְּקֶדֶם "as of old".
- **"But thou hast utterly rejected us; thou art very wroth against us" → "For if you have surely rejected us, you have been wrathful against us exceedingly"** (v22) — Hebrew כִּי אִם, word for word "for if"; מָאֹס מְאַסְתָּנוּ, infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "rejecting you have rejected us"; קָצַפְתָּ "you have been wrathful" (קָצַף, its row); עַד־מְאֹד "exceedingly" (lit. "unto much"). The meaning of the close is uncertain: "unless you have utterly rejected us and are very wrathful against us" (a doubt set after v21), "but you have utterly rejected us" (KJV), "even if you had utterly rejected us" (from memory): the verse keeps the words, "for if", and leaves the end open, as the Hebrew does.

### Notes
- **No acrostic** — the chapter has 22 verses, the number of the letters, but the verses do not open in alphabetical order; each verse is one line of two halves, without the long-and-short lament rhythm of ch. 1–4.
- **In the synagogue reading** v21 is read again after v22, so that the book does not end on v22 (as with Isa 66:23; Eccl 12:13; Mal 4:5).
- The numbering is the same in the Hebrew.
