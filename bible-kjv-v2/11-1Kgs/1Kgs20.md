[9] So he said to the messengers of ⟦ben-hadad|Ben Hadad|Ben-hadad⟧, "Tell my lord the king, 'All that you first sent for to your servant I will do, but this thing I cannot do.'" The messengers departed and brought him word again.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Syria / Syrians" → "Aram / Arameans"** (throughout).
- **"princes of the provinces" → "commanders of the provinces"** (v14, 15, 17, 19) — *sarei ha-medinot*.
- **"streets" → "bazaars"** (v34) — Hebrew *chutzot* here means trading-stations/markets (commercial quarters), not streets.
- **"ashes upon his face" → "bandage over his eyes"** (v38, 41) — Hebrew *apher al enav*. Disputed; traditional reading "ashes," but *apher* here likely = bandage/headband covering eyes.
- **"harness" → "armor"** (v11).
- **"heavy and displeased" → "resentful and sullen"** (v43) — *sar we-za'ef*.

### Notes
- **Ben-hadad / Hadad-ezer** — Aramean dynastic name. Aram-Damascus is Israel's chronic northern enemy.
- **Thirty-two kings** — vassal rulers. Confederation common in ANE coalition warfare.
- **Two victories, one prophet** — Yahweh grants wins not because Ahab deserves them but to show his sovereignty. "You shall know that I am Yahweh" twice (v13, 28) — echoes Ezekiel's later formula.
- **Hills vs valleys theology** (v23, 28) — Aramean polytheists thought Yahweh was a local hill-god. Their theological error becomes pretext for their defeat.
- **Aphek** — Transjordanian, likely Afeq east of Sea of Galilee.
- **Ahab's mercy to Ben-hadad** — politically pragmatic (buffer state against Assyria), theologically disobedient. Ben-hadad was under *cherem*.
- **Wounded prophet parable** — same technique as Nathan with David (2 Sam 12). King sentences himself.
- **"Your life for his life"** — fulfilled at Ramoth-gilead (ch 22).
- **Historical irony** — only a few years later Ahab and Ben-hadad fight together against Assyria at Qarqar (853 BC, in Shalmaneser III's annals).
