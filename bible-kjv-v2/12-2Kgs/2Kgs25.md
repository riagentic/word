# 2 Kings 25

[1] In the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month, ⟦nebuchadnezzar|Nevuchadnetzar|Nebuchadnezzar⟧ king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ came with all his army against ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧; he encamped against it, and they built a siege wall around it.

[2] The city was under siege until the eleventh year of King ⟦zedekiah|Tzidqiyahu|Zedekiah⟧.

[3] On the ninth day of the fourth month, the famine was severe in the city; there was no food for the people of the land.

[4] Then the city was breached. All the men of war fled by night by the way of the gate between the two walls, by the king's garden (now the ⟦chaldeans|Kasdim|Chaldeans⟧ were around the city); and the king went in the direction of the ⟦arabah|Aravah|Arabah⟧.

[5] The army of the ⟦chaldeans|Kasdim|Chaldeans⟧ pursued after the king and overtook him in the plains of ⟦jericho|Yericho|Jericho⟧; all his army was scattered from him.

[6] So they captured the king and brought him up to the king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ at ⟦riblah|Rivlah|Riblah⟧; they pronounced sentence upon him.

[7] They slaughtered the sons of ⟦zedekiah|Tzidqiyahu|Zedekiah⟧ before his eyes; they put out the eyes of ⟦zedekiah|Tzidqiyahu|Zedekiah⟧, bound him with bronze fetters, and brought him to ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧.

[8] In the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month — which was the nineteenth year of King ⟦nebuchadnezzar|Nevuchadnetzar|Nebuchadnezzar⟧ king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ — ⟦nebuzaradan|Nevuzaradan|Nebuzaradan⟧ the commander of the guard, a servant of the king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧, came to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[9] He burned the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, the king's house, and all the houses of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧; every great house he burned with fire.

[10] All the army of the ⟦chaldeans|Kasdim|Chaldeans⟧ who were with the commander of the guard broke down the walls around ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[11] The rest of the people who were left in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧, and the rest of the multitude, ⟦nebuzaradan|Nevuzaradan|Nebuzaradan⟧ the commander of the guard carried away into exile.

[12] But the commander of the guard left some of the poorest of the land to be vinedressers and plowmen.

[13] The bronze pillars that were in the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, the stands, and the bronze Sea that was in the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ the ⟦chaldeans|Kasdim|Chaldeans⟧ broke in pieces, and carried their bronze to ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧.

[14] The pots, shovels, snuffers, dishes, and all the bronze vessels used in the service they took away.

[15] The firepans and basins, whatever was of gold, in its gold, and whatever was of silver, in its silver, the commander of the guard took away.

[16] As for the two pillars, the one Sea, and the stands that ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧ had made for the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ — the bronze of all these vessels was beyond weighing.

[17] The height of one pillar was eighteen cubits — and the capital upon it was bronze; the height of the capital was three cubits, with interlaced work and pomegranates on the capital all around, all of bronze; the second pillar was like these, with the interlaced work.

[18] The commander of the guard took ⟦seraiah|Serayah|Seraiah⟧ the chief priest, ⟦zephaniah|Tzefanyah|Zephaniah⟧ the second priest, and the three keepers of the threshold.

[19] From the city he took a eunuch who was over the men of war, five men of the king's inner court who were found in the city, the secretary of the commander of the army, who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city.

[20] ⟦nebuzaradan|Nevuzaradan|Nebuzaradan⟧ the commander of the guard took them and brought them to the king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ at ⟦riblah|Rivlah|Riblah⟧.

[22] As for the people who remained in the land of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, whom ⟦nebuchadnezzar|Nevuchadnetzar|Nebuchadnezzar⟧ king of ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ had left, he made ⟦gedaliah|Gedalyahu|Gedaliah⟧ son of ⟦ahikam|Achiqam|Ahikam⟧ son of ⟦shaphan|Shafan|Shaphan⟧ governor over them.

[25] But in the seventh month, ⟦ishmael-other|Yishmael|Ishmael⟧ son of ⟦nethaniah|Netanyahu|Nethaniah⟧ son of Elishama, of the royal seed, came with ten men, and struck down ⟦gedaliah|Gedalyahu|Gedaliah⟧ so that he died, along with the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ and the ⟦chaldeans|Kasdim|Chaldeans⟧ who were with him at ⟦mizpah|Mitzpah|Mizpah⟧.

[30] His allowance was a continual allowance given him from the king, a portion each day, all the days of his life.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"forts" → "siege wall"** (v1) — *dayeq*.
- **"plain" → "Arabah"** (v4) — *ha-Arabah*; Jordan Rift valley.
- **"captain of the guard" → "commander of the guard"** (v8, 10, 11, 12, 15, 18, 20) — *rav-tabbachim*; literally "chief of the slaughterers," a Babylonian military title.
- **"brasen" / "brass" → "bronze"** (throughout).
- **"chapiter" → "capital"** (v17) — *koteret*.
- **"wreathen work" → "interlaced work"** (v17) — *sevakah*.
- **"officer" → "eunuch"** (v19) — *saris*.
- **"king's presence" → "king's inner court"** (v19) — *ro'ei penei ha-melek*.
- **"principal scribe of the host" → "secretary of the commander of the army"** (v19) — *sofer sar ha-tzava*.
- **"Evil-merodach" — retained** (v27) — Amel-Marduk; Nebuchadnezzar's son and successor (562-560 BC).
- **"seed royal" → "royal seed"** (v25).

### Notes
- **Siege 588-586 BC** — 18 months (tenth month of ninth year to fourth month of eleventh year). Ends with breach of walls mid-summer 586.
- **Zedekiah's fate** — sons killed before his eyes (last image he ever sees), then blinded. Typical Babylonian treatment of oath-breakers.
- **Riblah** — Nebuchadnezzar's forward headquarters in central Syria.
- **Nebuzaradan's month gap** — wall breach (fourth month) to temple burning (fifth month): ca. 30 days. Orderly imperial dismantling.
- **Temple destruction (586 BC)** — Solomon's temple, built ca. 960 BC. Stood ~374 years.
- **Pillars Jachin and Boaz** destroyed; Sea, wheeled stands broken up. Same items itemized in 1 Kgs 7. End of Solomonic era.
- **Gedaliah's administration at Mizpah** — Jer 40-41 parallel, with more detail. Jeremiah counseled submission; Ishmael's assassination ends the governorship within months.
- **Egypt flight** — Jer 42-44: Jeremiah taken along against his will; final prophecies against Judahite community in Egypt.
- **Jehoiachin's release (561 BC)** — Babylonian ration-tablets record his provisions. Closing note of hope: Davidic line still exists, still eats at a king's table. Kings ends in exile but not in despair — the lamp still burns, however faintly.
