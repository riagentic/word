## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"images" → "sacred pillars"** (v4) — *matzevot*.
- **"brasen" → "bronze"** (v4).
- **"Nehushtan" — retained** (v4) — pun: Hebrew *nachash* (serpent) + *nechoshet* (bronze) + *shan* diminutive — "Bronzy" / "little serpent."
- **"Tartan... Rabsaris... Rabshakeh" — retained as Assyrian titles** (v17) — *tartānu* (commander-in-chief), *rab ša rēši* (chief eunuch), *rab šāqê* (chief cupbearer); not personal names.
- **"bruised reed" → "splintered reed"** (v21) — *qaneh ha-ratzutz*.
- **"give pledges" → "make a wager"** (v23) — *hit'arev na*; place a bet.
- **"Syrian language" → "Aramaic"** (v26) — *Aramit*.
- **"Jews' language" → "Judahite language"** (v26, 28) — *Yehudit*; earliest attestation of "Hebrew"/Judean speech as distinct.
- **"Make an agreement with me by a present" → "Make your peace with me"** (v31) — *asu iti berakah*; "make a blessing with me" idiomatic surrender gesture.
- **"persuadeth" → "misleads"** (v32) — *yassit*.
- **"corn" → "grain"** (v32).
- **"drink their own piss" → "drink their own urine"** (v27) — ketiv *sheneyhem*, "their urine," the plain word (qere *mey raglehem*, "waters of their feet," is the euphemism for public reading); Kings does not soften.

### Notes
- **Hezekiah** — premier reformer king. Centralizes worship at Jerusalem; destroys Judah's high places. Cf. Isa 36-39; 2 Chr 29-32.
- **Nehushtan** — the Moses-made bronze serpent (Num 21) had become a cult object. Hezekiah demotes it to "just bronzy thing."
- **Fall of Samaria dated from Hezekiah's reign** (v9-12) — synchronism confirms the 722/1 BC event. Historical narrator's theological commentary.
- **Sennacherib's invasion (701 BC)** — well-documented in Assyrian annals (Taylor Prism): Sennacherib claims to have shut Hezekiah "like a caged bird" in Jerusalem. Assyrian records agree about tribute, diverge on outcome.
- **Lachish** — Sennacherib's forward base; famous Lachish relief from Nineveh depicts siege. Archaeological level III destruction matches.
- **Rabshakeh's fluent Hebrew** — Assyrian diplomatic officer trilingual. His speech is psychological warfare: undermine Egyptian alliance, Yahwist confidence, royal credibility.
- **Fuller's Field conduit** — Isa 7 scene, where Isaiah met Ahaz. Now Ahaz's son faces different crisis at same spot.
- **Vine and fig tree** (v31) — parody of covenant blessing (1 Kgs 4:25).
- **Theological climax** — "who are there among all the gods of the lands... that Yahweh should deliver Jerusalem?" Rabshakeh lumps Yahweh with defeated tribal gods. Chapter 19 responds.
