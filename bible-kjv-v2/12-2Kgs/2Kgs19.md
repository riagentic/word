## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"rebuke, and blasphemy" → "rebuke, and of disgrace"** (v3) — *ne'atzah*.
- **"blast" → "spirit"** (v7) — *ruach*; here a disturbing impulse Yahweh sends.
- **"servants" → "young men"** (v6) — *na'arei*; Rabshakeh's junior staff.
- **"Ethiopia" → "Cush"** (v9) — *Kush*; Nubia (modern Sudan). Tirhakah = Taharqa, Kushite 25th-Dynasty pharaoh.
- **"angel" → "Messenger"** (v35).
- **"Armenia" → "Ararat"** (v37) — *Urartu*; kingdom north of Assyria.
- **"forest of his Carmel" → "its densest forest"** (v23) — *ya'ar karmilo*; *karmel* here = "garden-land / densely planted" not the mountain.
- **"rivers of besieged places" → "streams of Egypt"** (v24) — Hebrew *ye'ori matzor*; *matzor* here probably = Egypt (not "siege"); Sennacherib's boast of drying Nile-branches.
- **"bank" → "siege-ramp"** (v32) — *solelah*.

### Notes
- **Hezekiah's prayer** — model of covenantal theology: enthroned God over all kingdoms, creator, hearer. Contrasts with Rabshakeh's polytheistic flattening.
- **Isaiah's oracle** — in poetic form; first poem directed at Sennacherib mockingly, second assures Hezekiah.
- **Hook in the nose** — Assyrian imperial image of captives led home on leashes; Assyrian reliefs at Nineveh depict exactly this. Yahweh turns Assyria's own symbol against them.
- **185,000 dead** — plague most likely (Josephus identifies it so, citing Berossus). Herodotus has a similar (perhaps echoing) tradition of mice eating Assyrian bowstrings at Pelusium. Assyrian annals omit defeat.
- **Sennacherib's death (681 BC)** — confirmed in Babylonian Chronicle; killed by sons in coup. Esarhaddon (not the sons named here) succeeds.
- **Taharqa** — Kushite pharaoh; some chronological debate, but plausible contemporary of later Sennacherib campaigns.
- **Fifteenth-year Jubilee pattern** (v29) — two-year fallow then sowing; echoes Torah year-of-release rhythm.
- **Remnant theology** — key theological theme; Isaiah 10-11 develops it; Paul echoes (Rom 9:27).
