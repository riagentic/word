# 2 Kings 7

[1] And Elisha said, "Hear the word of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧. Thus says ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧: 'About this time tomorrow a seah of fine flour shall be at a shekel, and two seahs of barley at a shekel, in the gate of ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧.'"

[2] And the officer who was the king's, on whose hand he leaned, answered the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and said, "Behold, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ is making windows in the heavens! Shall this word be?" And he said, "Behold, you are going to see with your eyes, and from there you shall not eat."

[3] And four men were lepers at the entrance of the gate. And they said, each man to his neighbor, "Why are we sitting here until we die?

[4] If we say, 'Let us come into the city,' then the famine is in the city, and we shall die there; and if we sit here, we shall die. And now, come, and let us fall away to the camp of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧. If they keep us alive, we shall live; and if they put us to death, we shall die."

[5] And they arose in the twilight to come to the camp of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧. And they came to the end of the camp of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧, and behold, there was no man there.

[6] And ⟦adonai|Adonai|the Lord⟧ had made the camp of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ hear a sound of chariots, a sound of horse, a sound of a great force. And they said, each man to his brother, "Behold, the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ has hired against us the kings of the ⟦hittites|Chittim|Hittites⟧ and the kings of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ to come upon us."

[7] And they arose and fled in the twilight, and they left their tents and their horses and their donkeys, the camp as it was, and they fled for their soul.

[8] And these lepers came to the end of the camp, and they came into one tent and ate and drank, and they carried from there silver and gold and garments, and they went and hid them. And they returned and came into another tent and carried from there, and they went and hid them.

[9] And they said, each man to his neighbor, "We are not doing right. This day is a day of news, and we are keeping silent; and we shall wait until the light of the morning, and iniquity will find us. And now, come, and let us go in and tell the house of the king."

[10] And they came and called to the gatekeeper of the city, and they told them, saying, "We came to the camp of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧, and behold, there was no man there, nor a human voice, but only the horse bound and the donkey bound, and tents as they were."

[11] And he called the gatekeepers, and they told it to the house of the king within.

[12] And the king arose by night and said to his servants, "Let me tell you, please, what ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ has done to us. They know that we are hungry, and they have gone out from the camp to hide themselves in the field, saying, 'When they come out from the city, we will seize them alive and come into the city.'"

[13] And one of his servants answered and said, "Let them take, please, five of the horses that are left, which are left in it — behold, they are like all the multitude of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ that are left in it; behold, they are like all the multitude of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ that have come to an end — and let us send and see."

[14] And they took two chariots of horses, and the king sent after the camp of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧, saying, "Go and see."

[15] And they went after them as far as the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧, and behold, all the way was full of garments and gear that ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ had thrown away in their haste. And the messengers returned and told the king.

[16] And the people went out and plundered the camp of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧. And a seah of fine flour was at a shekel, and two seahs of barley at a shekel, according to the word of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[17] And the king had appointed the officer on whose hand he leaned over the gate, and the people trampled him in the gate, and he died, as the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ had spoken, who spoke when the king came down to him.

[18] And it came to pass, when the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ spoke to the king, saying, "Two seahs of barley at a shekel and a seah of fine flour at a shekel shall be about this time tomorrow in the gate of ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧,"

[19] that the officer answered the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and said, "And behold, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ is making windows in the heavens! Shall it be according to this word?" And he said, "Behold, you are going to see with your eyes, and from there you shall not eat."

[20] And so it was to him: the people trampled him in the gate, and he died.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; "the man of Elohim" has the article.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"Syrians" → "Aram"** (throughout) — Hebrew אֲרָם, the one name for the land and the people; with plural verbs in v12, 15.
- **"To morrow about this time shall a measure of fine flour be sold for a shekel" → "About this time tomorrow a seah of fine flour shall be at a shekel"** (v1, 16, 18) — the Hebrew has no "sold"; סְאָה, the seah, is a dry measure (a third of an ephah); the shekel is a weight of silver, not a coin. **"in the gate of Samaria"** — the open place in the gate, where the market is.
- **"a lord on whose hand the king leaned" → "the officer who was the king's, on whose hand he leaned"** (v2, 17, 19) — Hebrew הַשָּׁלִישׁ, literally "third man": the third in a chariot, or a rank; uncertain. The Hebrew of v2 is אֲשֶׁר־לַמֶּלֶךְ נִשְׁעָן עַל־יָדוֹ, "who [belonged] to the king, leaning on his hand"; it is commonly read as "on whose hand the king leaned" (as v17 has it, and 5:18).
- **"Behold, if the LORD would make windows in heaven, might this thing be?" → "Behold, Yahweh is making windows in the heavens! Shall this word be?"** (v2, 19) — Hebrew הִנֵּה יְהוָה עֹשֶׂה אֲרֻבּוֹת בַּשָּׁמַיִם: a statement with a participle, said in scorn; it has no "if". It is commonly understood as "even if Yahweh were to make windows in the heavens …". אֲרֻבּוֹת are the "windows of the heavens" of Gen 7:11. הַדָּבָר is "the word" (of v1) or "the thing"; v19 has כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה, "according to this word", and begins with "And".
- **"thou shalt see it with thine eyes, but shalt not eat thereof" → "you are going to see with your eyes, and from there you shall not eat"** (v2, 19) — Hebrew הִנְּכָה רֹאֶה, "behold you, seeing"; "it" is not written; מִשָּׁם, "from there".
- **"four leprous men" → "four men were lepers"** (v3, 8) — Hebrew מְצֹרָעִים, men with צָרַעַת: a scaly outbreak of the skin that makes unclean (Lev 13); it is not the leprosy of modern medicine. **"they said one to another" → "each man to his neighbor"** (v3, 9), **"each man to his brother"** (v6) — two Hebrew phrases.
- **"Why sit we here until we die?"** (v3) — Hebrew עַד־מָתְנוּ, "until we have died".
- **"let us fall unto the host of the Syrians: if they save us alive … if they kill us, we shall but die" → "let us fall away to the camp of Aram. If they keep us alive … if they put us to death, we shall die"** (v4) — Hebrew נָפַל אֶל, "fall to": go over to; מַחֲנֶה, "camp" (throughout); the Hebrew has no "but".
- **"in the twilight"** (v5, 7) — Hebrew נֶשֶׁף: dusk or dawn; here the evening, since the night follows (v9, 12). **"the uttermost part of the camp" → "the end of the camp"** (v5, 8) — Hebrew קְצֵה.
- **"For the Lord had made the host of the Syrians to hear a noise of chariots, and a noise of horses, even the noise of a great host" → "And Adonai had made the camp of Aram hear a sound of chariots, a sound of horse, a sound of a great force"** (v6) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי, not the name Yahweh; קוֹל, "voice, sound", three times with no "and"; סוּס is a singular for the whole. **"the kings of Egypt"** — Hebrew מַלְכֵי מִצְרַיִם; the same consonants are also read as Musri, a land in the north beside the Hittites ("from memory").
- **"fled for their life" → "fled for their soul"** (v7) — Hebrew אֶל־נַפְשָׁם, as 1 Kgs 19:3.
- **"carried thence silver, and gold, and raiment, and went and hid it" → "carried from there silver and gold and garments, and they went and hid them"** (v8) — Hebrew טָמַן, hide in the ground; "them" is supplied both times.
- **"We do not well: this day is a day of good tidings, and we hold our peace: if we tarry till the morning light, some mischief will come upon us" → "We are not doing right. This day is a day of news, and we are keeping silent; and we shall wait until the light of the morning, and iniquity will find us"** (v9) — Hebrew לֹא־כֵן, "not so, not right"; בְּשֹׂרָה, "news" (the word itself does not say "good"); the Hebrew has no "if": two clauses joined by "and", meaning "should we wait …, then …". עָווֹן is "iniquity": guilt, or the punishment for it.
- **"called unto the porter of the city: and they told them" → "called to the gatekeeper of the city, and they told them"** (v10) — the Hebrew has the singular שֹׁעֵר and then "them". **"neither voice of man" → "nor a human voice"** — Hebrew אִישׁ "man" and then אָדָם "man, mankind". **"horses tied, and asses tied" → "the horse bound and the donkey bound"** — singulars for the whole.
- **"And he called the porters; and they told it to the king's house within" → "And he called the gatekeepers, and they told it to the house of the king within"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיִּקְרָא, a singular verb: "he called the gatekeepers", or "the gatekeepers called".
- **"I will now shew you" → "Let me tell you, please"** (v12) — Hebrew אַגִּידָה־נָּא. **"we shall catch them alive" → "seize them alive"** — Hebrew תָּפַשׂ. "In the field" follows the reading tradition (qere); the written text has a doubled article.
- **"Let some take, I pray thee, five of the horses that remain, which are left in the city, (behold, they are as all the multitude of Israel that are left in it: behold, I say, they are even as all the multitude of the Israelites that are consumed:) and let us send and see"** (v13) — the verse gives the Hebrew as it stands, with its repeated clause; "the city" is "in it" in the Hebrew. The repetition is often held to be a copyist's doubling; the verse keeps it. תַּמּוּ is "have come to an end". The written text (ketiv) has "the multitude" with the article the first time.
- **"two chariot horses" → "two chariots of horses"** (v14) — Hebrew שְׁנֵי רֶכֶב סוּסִים: two chariots with their teams. **"after the host of the Syrians" → "after the camp of Aram"**.
- **"garments and vessels, which the Syrians had cast away in their haste" → "garments and gear that Aram had thrown away in their haste"** (v15) — Hebrew כֵּלִים, "vessels, gear, weapons"; "in their haste" follows the qere.
- **"spoiled the tents of the Syrians" → "plundered the camp of Aram"** (v16) — Hebrew וַיָּבֹזּוּ אֵת מַחֲנֵה אֲרָם.
- **"the king appointed the lord … to have the charge of the gate: and the people trode upon him" → "the king had appointed the officer … over the gate, and the people trampled him"** (v17, 20) — Hebrew הִפְקִיד עַל; רָמַס.
- **"And it came to pass as the man of God had spoken … And that lord answered" → "And it came to pass, when the man of Elohim spoke … that the officer answered"** (v18, 19) — Hebrew וַיְהִי כְּדַבֵּר … וַיַּעַן: the two verses retell v1, 2 as one sentence.
- **"And so it fell out unto him" → "And so it was to him"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיְהִי־לוֹ כֵּן.

### Notes
- **The price** — a quarter of a qab of dove's dung at five of silver (6:25) the day before; now a seah of fine flour at one shekel.
- **The officer** — he sees the plenty and is trampled in the gate where it is sold.
- **Four lepers** — men kept outside the city bring it the news.
- **Sound of a great force** — the camp flees from a sound; no battle is fought.
- **"We are not doing right" (v9)** — the news of the day is to be told, not kept.
