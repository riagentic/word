## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"running issue" → "discharge"** (v4) — as in ch 15.
- **"stranger" → "outsider"** (v10, 12, 13) — Hebrew זָר *zar* = non-priest (not *ger* = foreigner). Clarifies: any non-priestly household member cannot eat priestly food.
- **"bear the iniquity of trespass" → "bear the iniquity of guilt"** (v16) — Hebrew *asham*.
- **"at your own will" → "so that you may be accepted"** (v19, 29) — as in ch 19:5. Hebrew *li-retzonkhem*.
- **"beeves" → "cattle"** (v19, 21) — archaic plural of *beef* = cattle.
- **"wen" → "discharge"** (v22) — Hebrew יַבֶּלֶת *yabbelet*. Uncertain — a wart, tumor, or running sore.
- **"under the dam" → "with its mother"** (v27) — *dam* archaic for mother animal.

### Notes
- **Two themes interwoven**: (1) priestly consumption of sacred food (v1–16); (2) integrity of offerings (v17–33). The priesthood and the animals must both be unblemished.
- **Priestly household inclusion** (v10–13) — slaves and home-born dependents eat sacred food; daughters married to outsiders do not; returned divorced / widowed daughters may resume eating.
- **Eight-day rule** (v27) — newborn animals stay seven days with mother before being sacrificed, paralleling the eight-day rule for circumcision (Gen 17:12). Possibly for mother/infant bonding; also reflects the primordial seven-day creation pattern.
- **"Not kill mother and young on the same day"** (v28) — humane law limiting sacrificial cruelty. Similar to Deut 22:6 (mother bird and nestlings).
- **Refrain: "I am Yahweh who consecrates you"** — the chapter's drumbeat; holiness-by-association with Yahweh's own holiness.
