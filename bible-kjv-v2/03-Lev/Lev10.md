## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"Uncover not your heads" → "Do not let your hair hang loose"** (v6) — Hebrew פָּרַע *para*. Mourning gesture: loosening / disheveling the hair, not uncovering a covered head. Priests forbidden to mourn publicly.
- **"holy and unholy" → "holy and common"** (v10) — Hebrew קֹדֶשׁ וְחֹל *qodesh ve-chol*. Not sacred/profane in moral sense; *chol* = ordinary / non-consecrated.
- **"strong drink" (v9)** — Hebrew שֵׁכָר *shekar*. Fermented drink, likely grain- or date-based beer; not distilled spirits (no distillation in antiquity).
- **"meat offering" → "grain offering"** (v12).
- **"wave breast and heave shoulder" → "breast of the elevation offering and the thigh of the contribution"** (v14, 15) — as in ch 7.

### Notes
- **Nadab and Abihu** — the first priestly apostasy, on the same day of inauguration. Interpretations: (1) wrong fire-source; (2) ignoring ritual order; (3) drunkenness (implied by v9's prohibition following immediately); (4) arrogance / encroachment. The narrative keeps the offense unspecified, focusing on the consequence.
- **"I will be sanctified in those who come near me"** (v3) — holiness is dangerous. Proximity to Yahweh requires exact obedience. Aaron's silence = acceptance of divine justice.
- **No mourning for the priest** (v6–7) — priests in active service cannot mourn (Lev 21:10–12). They are between the living and the holy; grief is suspended.
- **Priestly teaching role** (v10–11) — priests are not just cultic officiants but legal/ritual teachers: distinguishing holy/common, clean/unclean.
- **Aaron's counter-argument** (v19) — after losing two sons, Aaron implies he cannot eat the priestly portion of the sin offering while in a state of personal bereavement. Moses accepts this — a rare case of Aaron's ad hoc ritual reasoning prevailing.
