## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew וַ / וְ "and" at the head of a clause is kept. **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (throughout) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"the feasts of the LORD" → "the appointed times of Yahweh"** (v2, 4, 37, 44) — Hebrew מוֹעֲדִים, from יָעַד "appoint": fixed times (the word of Gen 1:14; 17:21; Exod 23:15). It is not the word "feast" (חַג, v6, 34, 39, 41), and it covers the Shabbat and the day of atonement, which are not feasts.
- **"which ye shall proclaim to be holy convocations"** kept (v2, 4, 37) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר־תִּקְרְאוּ אֹתָם מִקְרָאֵי קֹדֶשׁ: verb and noun of one root, קָרָא "call". מִקְרָא־קֹדֶשׁ is literally "a calling of holiness": a day called out as holy, or a calling together of the people. **"in their seasons" → "at their appointed time"** (v4) — Hebrew בְּמוֹעֲדָם, the same word again.
- **"the sabbath of rest" → "a Shabbat of shabbaton"** (v3, 32) — Hebrew שַׁבַּת שַׁבָּתוֹן. שַׁבָּתוֹן (alone in v24, 39) is a noun made from שַׁבָּת: a Shabbat-keeping, a ceasing; "rest" is not the root (שָׁבַת is "cease"). **"ye shall do no work therein" → "no work shall you do"** — "therein" is not in the Hebrew. **"dwellings" → "dwelling places"** (v3, 14, 17, 21, 31) — Hebrew מוֹשְׁבֹת.
- **"In the fourteenth day of the first month at even is the LORD's passover" → "In the first month, on the fourteenth of the month, between the two evenings, is a Pesach to Yahweh"** (v5) — Hebrew בֵּין הָעַרְבָּיִם, a dual, literally so; which hours are meant is disputed (the dusk between sunset and dark, or the time from the sun's decline to sunset). פֶּסַח לַיהוָה, "a Pesach to Yahweh" (Exod 12:11). The Hebrew has no verb in v5, 6; "is" is supplied.
- **"the feast of unleavened bread" → "the Feast of Matzot"** (v6) — Hebrew חַג הַמַּצּוֹת; חַג is a pilgrim feast; מַצּוֹת, "unleavened bread", is a plural: flat cakes baked without leaven. **"of the same month" → "of this month"** — Hebrew לַחֹדֶשׁ הַזֶּה.
- **"no servile work" → "no work of service"** (v7, 8, 21, 25, 35, 36) — Hebrew מְלֶאכֶת עֲבֹדָה, literally so: מְלָאכָה "work" and עֲבֹדָה "service, labor". The work of one's daily labor is meant; on the Shabbat (v3) and on the day of atonement (v28, 30, 31) it is "no work" at all.
- **"ye shall offer an offering made by fire" → "you shall bring near an offering by fire"** (v8, 25, 27, 36, 37) — Hebrew הִקְרִיב "bring near" and אִשֶּׁה (commonly taken from אֵשׁ "fire"; the derivation is not certain).
- **"a sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest" → "the sheaf of the first of your harvest"** (v10) — Hebrew עֹמֶר רֵאשִׁית קְצִירְכֶם. עֹמֶר is "sheaf" (Deut 24:19; Ruth 2:7); the same word is the measure "omer" in Exod 16:16–36, and Jewish tradition takes it so here (an omer of barley flour). רֵאשִׁית is "the first, the beginning"; "firstfruits" is another word (v17, 20).
- **"he shall wave the sheaf … to be accepted for you" → "he shall wave the sheaf … that you may be found pleasing"** (v11) — Hebrew הֵנִיף "wave" (move to and fro before Yahweh), and לִרְצֹנְכֶם as in 19:5; 22:19. **"on the morrow after the sabbath" → "on the morrow of the Shabbat"** (v11, 15, 16) — Hebrew מִמָּחֳרַת הַשַּׁבָּת. Which Shabbat is meant the text does not say: the weekly Shabbat in the days of the Matzot (so the Sadducees, the Samaritans and the Karaites), or the first day of the feast (v7) as a day of ceasing (so the Pharisees and later Jewish practice). On this hangs the day of the feast of v16–21.
- **"ye shall offer that day when ye wave the sheaf an he lamb without blemish of the first year" → "you shall offer, on the day when you wave the sheaf, a lamb without blemish, a year old"** (v12) — the verb is עָשָׂה "do, make" (so v19); תָּמִים is "whole, complete"; בֶּן־שְׁנָתוֹ is literally "a son of its year".
- **"the meat offering thereof shall be two tenth deals of fine flour mingled with oil … for a sweet savour" → "its grain offering: two tenths of fine flour mixed with oil … a soothing smell"** (v13) — Hebrew מִנְחָה; עֶשְׂרֹנִים "tenths" (of an ephah: the measure is not named); רֵיחַ נִיחֹחַ, from נוּחַ "rest": a smell that brings rest. **"the fourth part of an hin" → "a fourth of the hin"**. The Hebrew has no verb.
- **"neither bread, nor parched corn, nor green ears" → "bread and parched grain and fresh grain"** (v14) — Hebrew קָלִי, grain roasted at the fire, and כַּרְמֶל, newly ripe grain (2:14; the exact sense is not certain). **"the selfsame day" → "this very day"** (v14, 21, 28, 29, 30) — Hebrew עֶצֶם הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה, literally "the bone of this day". **"an offering unto your God" → "the offering of your Elohim"** — Hebrew קָרְבַּן, "what is brought near". **"a statute for ever" → "an everlasting statute"** (v14, 21, 31, 41) — Hebrew חֻקַּת עוֹלָם, literally "a statute of the age".
- **"ye shall count unto you … seven sabbaths shall be complete" → "you shall number for yourselves … seven Shabbatot, complete they shall be"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew סָפַר "number"; שֶׁבַע שַׁבָּתוֹת תְּמִימֹת. שַׁבָּתוֹת is the plural of "Shabbat"; here seven full weeks, each ending with its Shabbat, are meant (Deut 16:9 says "seven weeks"); the word is kept. **"the sheaf of the wave offering"** — Hebrew תְּנוּפָה, the noun of "wave".
- **"a new meat offering" → "a new grain offering"** (v16) — Hebrew מִנְחָה חֲדָשָׁה: of the new grain.
- **"two wave loaves of two tenth deals" → "bread of a wave offering, two loaves of two tenths"** (v17) — Hebrew לֶחֶם תְּנוּפָה שְׁתַּיִם שְׁנֵי עֶשְׂרֹנִים, literally "bread of waving, two, two tenths": "loaves" is supplied. **"baken with leaven" → "leavened they shall be baked"** — Hebrew חָמֵץ, "what is leavened". **"the firstfruits"** — Hebrew בִּכּוּרִים.
- **"one young bullock" → "one bull of the herd"** (v18) — Hebrew פַּר בֶּן־בָּקָר, literally "a bull, a son of the herd"; the phrase says nothing of age. **"of the first year" → "a year old"** (v18, 19) — literally "sons of a year". **"with their meat offering, and their drink offerings, even an offering made by fire, of sweet savour" → "with their grain offering and their drink offerings, an offering by fire of a soothing smell"**.
- **"Then ye shall sacrifice one kid of the goats" → "And you shall offer one male goat"** (v19) — Hebrew וַעֲשִׂיתֶם, "you shall do / make"; שְׂעִיר־עִזִּים, a he-goat.
- **"they shall be holy to the LORD for the priest"** kept (v20) — Hebrew קֹדֶשׁ יִהְיוּ לַיהוָה לַכֹּהֵן, literally "holiness they shall be to Yahweh, to the priest". "With the two lambs" is literally "upon two lambs".
- **"ye shall proclaim on the selfsame day, that it may be an holy convocation unto you" → "you shall proclaim on this very day: a holy convocation it shall be for you"** (v21) — the Hebrew has no "that"; "proclaim" has no object.
- **"thou shalt not make clean riddance of the corners of thy field when thou reapest" → "you shall not finish the edge of your field when you reap"** (v22) — as 19:9, with בְּקֻצְרֶךָ "in your reaping" for לִקְצֹר. **"gleaning … gather"**, **"the poor … the stranger" → "the afflicted … the sojourner"** — as 19:9–10.
- **"a sabbath, a memorial of blowing of trumpets" → "a shabbaton, a memorial of shouting"** (v24) — Hebrew שַׁבָּתוֹן זִכְרוֹן תְּרוּעָה. זִכָּרוֹן is "memorial"; תְּרוּעָה is a loud cry raised: a shout, or the blast of a horn (25:9; Num 10:5; 29:1; Josh 6:5). The Hebrew names no trumpets; that horns were blown on this day is the old understanding (cf. 25:9; Ps 81:3).
- **"Also on the tenth day" → "Only, on the tenth"** (v27; so v39) — Hebrew אַךְ "only, surely". **"a day of atonement" → "the day of atonement"** (v27) — Hebrew יוֹם הַכִּפֻּרִים, with the article, literally "the day of the atonements" (without the article in v28); כִּפֻּרִים is of one root with כִּפֶּר "make atonement" (literally "cover").
- **"ye shall afflict your souls"** kept (v27, 32) — Hebrew וְעִנִּיתֶם אֶת־נַפְשֹׁתֵיכֶם, word for word (16:29, 31). עִנָּה is "afflict, humble"; fasting is commonly understood (Isa 58:3, 5; Ps 35:13), but the Hebrew does not say "fast". **"that shall not be afflicted"** (v29) — the passive of the same verb. **"from among his people" → "from its peoples"** (v29) — Hebrew מֵעַמֶּיהָ, a plural: his kinsfolk; "soul" is the person.
- **"the same soul will I destroy" → "I will make that soul perish"** (v30) — Hebrew וְהַאֲבַדְתִּי, the causative of אָבַד "perish". "From the midst of its people" is singular here (עַמָּהּ).
- **"from even unto even, shall ye celebrate your sabbath" → "from evening to evening, you shall cease, your Shabbat"** (v32) — Hebrew תִּשְׁבְּתוּ שַׁבַּתְּכֶם, literally "you shall cease your ceasing": the verb שָׁבַת "cease" with its own noun שַׁבָּת as object (as 25:2). The sense is "you shall keep your Shabbat"; "keep" (שָׁמַר) is not the Hebrew word here.
- **"the feast of tabernacles" → "the Feast of Sukkot"** (v34) — Hebrew חַג הַסֻּכּוֹת, "the feast of the booths": סֻכָּה is a hut of branches (v42, 43), not a tent. **"for seven days unto the LORD" → "seven days, to Yahweh"** — the Hebrew order.
- **"it is a solemn assembly"** kept (v36) — Hebrew עֲצֶרֶת, from עָצַר "hold back, close up": a day of holding back (from work), or the closing gathering of a feast (Num 29:35; Deut 16:8; 2 Kgs 10:20; Joel 1:14). The exact sense is not certain; "solemn" is the customary English and is not a word of its own in the Hebrew.
- **"every thing upon his day" → "the matter of a day in its day"** (v37) — Hebrew דְּבַר־יוֹם בְּיוֹמוֹ, literally so (Exod 5:13; 16:4). **"a meat offering" → "grain offering"**.
- **"when ye have gathered in the fruit of the land, ye shall keep a feast unto the LORD" → "when you gather the yield of the land, you shall hold the feast of Yahweh"** (v39) — Hebrew תְּבוּאָה "yield"; תָּחֹגּוּ אֶת־חַג־יְהוָה, verb and noun of one root (so v41); "the feast of Yahweh", not "to". **"a sabbath" → "a shabbaton"** — Hebrew שַׁבָּתוֹן, as v24.
- **"the boughs of goodly trees, branches of palm trees, and the boughs of thick trees, and willows of the brook" → "the fruit of a tree of splendor, fronds of palm trees, and a bough of a leafy tree, and willows of the wadi"** (v40) — Hebrew פְּרִי עֵץ הָדָר, "fruit of a tree of splendor" (הָדָר "splendor, majesty"; Jewish tradition names the citron, *etrog*; the Hebrew names no kind); כַּפֹּת תְּמָרִים, literally "palms of palm trees" (כַּף, the palm of the hand): the fronds; עֲנַף עֵץ־עָבֹת, "a bough of a thick / leafy tree" (tradition: the myrtle); עַרְבֵי־נָחַל, "willows (or poplars: the kind is not certain) of the wadi".
- **"all that are Israelites born" → "every native in Israel"** (v42) — Hebrew כָּל־הָאֶזְרָח בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"I made the children of Israel to dwell in booths" → "in booths I made the sons of Israel dwell"** (v43) — the Hebrew puts "in booths" first.
- **"And Moses declared unto the children of Israel the feasts of the LORD" → "And Moshe spoke the appointed times of Yahweh to the sons of Israel"** (v44) — Hebrew וַיְדַבֵּר "spoke".

### Notes
- **Israel's liturgical calendar** — the appointed times of the chapter: Shabbat, Pesach / Matzot, the sheaf of the first of the harvest, the fiftieth day (Weeks, Pentecost), the memorial of shouting, the day of atonement, Sukkot. Each keyed to the agricultural cycle and salvation history.
- **Pesach + Matzot** — Pesach on the fourteenth of the first month between the two evenings; Matzot from the fifteenth, seven days. Commemorates exodus deliverance.
- **The sheaf and the fiftieth day (Shavuot)** — the sheaf of the first of the harvest in spring; fifty days later the new grain offering of two leavened loaves. The chapter gives this day no name; elsewhere it is the Feast of Weeks (Exod 34:22; Deut 16:10). NT Pentecost falls on this day (Acts 2).
- **Shouting, Atonement, Sukkot** — the seventh-month cluster: the first day (later Rosh Hashanah), the tenth (Yom Kippur), the fifteenth (Sukkot: ingathering + wilderness memory).
- **Booths** (v42–43) — dwelling in booths recalls the exodus. Both agricultural (harvest-hut) and historical significance.
- **Sukkot and John 7** — Jesus attended this feast (Jn 7:2, 37); his "rivers of living water" saying occurs at the Sukkot water-drawing ritual.
- **Pentecost & Weeks** — Later Jewish tradition connects Shavuot with the giving of the Torah at Sinai.
