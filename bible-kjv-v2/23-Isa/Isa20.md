# Isaiah 20

[1] In the year that the commander-in-chief came to Ashdod, when Sargon the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧ sent him, and he fought against Ashdod and captured it —

[2] at that time ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ spoke by ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ son of ⟦amoz|Amotz|Amoz⟧, saying, "Go, and loose the sackcloth from your waist and take off your sandals from your feet," and he did so, walking naked and barefoot.

[3] Then ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said, "As my servant ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ has walked naked and barefoot for three years as a sign and a portent against ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ and ⟦cush|Kush|Cush⟧,

[4] so shall the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧ lead away the ⟦egyptian|Mitzri|Egyptian⟧ captives and the ⟦cushite|Kushi|Cushite⟧ exiles, both the young and the old, naked and barefoot, with buttocks uncovered, the nakedness of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[5] Then they shall be dismayed and ashamed because of ⟦cush|Kush|Cush⟧ their hope and of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ their boast.

[6] And the inhabitants of this coastland will say in that day, 'Behold, this is what has happened to those in whom we hoped and to whom we fled for help to be delivered from the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧! And we, how shall we escape?'"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (vv2, 3) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **Short chapter** — entirely prose narrative; no poetic structure changes needed.

### Notes
- **Three-year sign-act** — Isaiah's extreme prophetic symbolism (cf. Ezekiel, Hosea).
- **Sargon II** — reigned 722–705 BC; Ashdod rebelled c. 711 BC.
