# Isaiah 36

[1] And it came to pass in the fourteenth year of King ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ that ⟦sennacherib|Sancheriv|Sennacherib⟧ king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧ went up against all the fortified cities of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ and seized them.

[2] And the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧ sent ⟦rabshakeh|Rav-Shaqeh|Rabshakeh⟧ from Lachish to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, to King ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧, with a heavy force. And he stood by the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway of the field of the washer.

[3] And there came out to him ⟦eliakim|Elyaqim|Eliakim⟧ the son of ⟦hilkiah|Chilqiyahu|Hilkiah⟧, who was over the house, and ⟦shebna|Shevna|Shebna⟧ the scribe, and ⟦joah|Yoach|Joah⟧ the son of ⟦asaph|Asaf|Asaph⟧, the recorder.

[4] And ⟦rabshakeh|Rav-Shaqeh|Rabshakeh⟧ said to them, "Say, please, to ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧: Thus says the great king, the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧: What is this trust with which you have trusted?

[5] I have said — only a word of the lips — counsel and might for the war. Now, in whom have you trusted, that you have rebelled against me?

[6] Behold, you have trusted in the staff of this crushed reed, in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, which, when a man leans on it, goes into his palm and pierces it. So is ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ to all who trust in him.

[7] And if you say to me, 'In ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ we have trusted,' is it not he whose high places and whose altars ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ has removed, and he said to ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ and to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, 'Before this altar you shall bow down'?

[8] And now, exchange pledges, please, with my lord the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧, and let me give you two thousand horses, if you are able to set for yourself riders upon them.

[9] And how will you turn back the face of one governor of the least of the servants of my lord? And you have trusted for yourself in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ for chariots and for horsemen.

[10] And now, is it apart from ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ that I have come up against this land to ruin it? ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ said to me, 'Go up to this land and ruin it.'"

[11] And ⟦eliakim|Elyaqim|Eliakim⟧ and ⟦shebna|Shevna|Shebna⟧ and ⟦joah|Yoach|Joah⟧ said to ⟦rabshakeh|Rav-Shaqeh|Rabshakeh⟧, "Speak, please, to your servants in ⟦aramaic|Aramit|Aramaic⟧, for we understand, and do not speak to us in ⟦judahite|Yehudit|the language of Judah⟧ in the ears of the people who are on the wall."

[12] And ⟦rabshakeh|Rav-Shaqeh|Rabshakeh⟧ said, "Is it to your lord and to you that my lord has sent me to speak these words? Is it not to the men who sit on the wall, to eat their excrement and to drink the water of their feet with you?"

[13] And ⟦rabshakeh|Rav-Shaqeh|Rabshakeh⟧ stood and called with a loud voice in ⟦judahite|Yehudit|the language of Judah⟧, and said, "Hear the words of the great king, the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧!

[14] Thus says the king: Let not ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ deceive you, for he will not be able to deliver you.

[15] And let not ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ make you trust in ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, saying, '⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ will surely deliver us; this city will not be given into the hand of the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧.'

[16] Do not listen to ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧, for thus says the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧: Make a blessing with me and come out to me, and eat each man his vine and each man his fig tree, and drink each man the water of his pit,

[17] until I come and take you to a land like your land, a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards.

[18] Lest ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ incite you, saying, '⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ will deliver us.' Have the gods of the nations delivered each his land from the hand of the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧?

[19] Where are the gods of ⟦hamath|Chamat|Hamath⟧ and Arpad? Where are the gods of ⟦sepharvaim|Sefarvayim|Sepharvaim⟧? And that they have delivered ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧ from my hand?

[20] Who among all the gods of these lands are they who have delivered their land from my hand, that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ should deliver ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ from my hand?"

[21] And they were silent and did not answer him a word, for it was the commandment of the king, saying, "You shall not answer him."

[22] And ⟦eliakim|Elyaqim|Eliakim⟧ the son of ⟦hilkiah|Chilqiyahu|Hilkiah⟧, who was over the house, and ⟦shebna|Shevna|Shebna⟧ the scribe, and ⟦joah|Yoach|Joah⟧ the son of ⟦asaph|Asaf|Asaph⟧, the recorder, came to ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ with garments torn, and they told him the words of ⟦rabshakeh|Rav-Shaqeh|Rabshakeh⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v7) — Hebrew *Elohim*. "The gods of the nations / of these lands" (v18–20) is the same word, of the gods of other peoples, plain "gods".
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **Parallel** (whole chapter) — the chapter stands almost word for word in 2 Kgs 18:13–37; the wording of 2 Kings is kept where the Hebrew is the same, and every place where Isaiah's Hebrew differs is translated as Isaiah has it (listed below, with the verse of 2 Kings). Nothing is levelled either way.
- **"Now it came to pass" → "And it came to pass"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיְהִי, which 2 Kgs 18:13 lacks ("And in the fourteenth year"). **"came up against all the defenced cities of Judah, and took them" → "went up against all the fortified cities of Judah and seized them"** — Hebrew עָלָה עַל; בְּצֻרוֹת; תָּפַשׂ. The name is the long form חִזְקִיָּהוּ here and through the chapter (2 Kgs 18:13 has חִזְקִיָּה).
- **"sent Rabshakeh … with a great army. And he stood … in the highway of the fuller's field" → "sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem, to King Hezekiah, with a heavy force. And he stood … on the highway of the field of the washer"** (v2) — Isaiah names only Rabshakeh; 2 Kgs 18:17 has "Tartan and Rab-saris and Rabshakeh", "they went up and came" twice, "they stood" and "which is on the highway". Here the verb is singular, וַיַּעֲמֹד, and no אֲשֶׁר stands: the conduit is "on the highway". Rabshakeh is an Assyrian title (the chief cupbearer), written without the article like a name; the earlier "the Rabshakeh — chief cupbearer/general —" put an article and an explanation into the verse. Hebrew חֵיל כָּבֵד "heavy force"; כּוֹבֵס "washer", one who washes cloth ("fuller" is the traditional word).
- **"Then came forth unto him Eliakim, Hilkiah's son, which was over the house … the recorder" → "And there came out to him Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, who was over the house … the recorder"** (v3, 22) — Hebrew אֵלָיו "to him" (2 Kgs 18:18 "to them", after "they called to the king", which Isaiah lacks); אֲשֶׁר עַל־הַבַּיִת; הַמַּזְכִּיר, "the one who brings to remembrance". שֶׁבְנָא is spelled with א here (2 Kgs 18:18, 26 שֶׁבְנָה).
- **"What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?" → "What is this trust with which you have trusted?"** (v4) — Hebrew הַבִּטָּחוֹן … בָּטָחְתָּ, noun and verb of one root. The verb בָּטַח stands with עַל (v5, 6, 9) and אֶל (v7, 15); English has "trust in" for both. **"Say, please"** — Hebrew אִמְרוּ־נָא.
- **"I say, sayest thou, (but they are but vain words) I have counsel and strength for war" → "I have said — only a word of the lips — counsel and might for the war"** (v5) — the Hebrew is as short as this: אָמַרְתִּי אַךְ־דְּבַר־שְׂפָתַיִם עֵצָה וּגְבוּרָה לַמִּלְחָמָה. Isaiah has אָמַרְתִּי "I have said" where 2 Kgs 18:20 has אָמַרְתָּ "you have said"; the MT is kept. With "I" the sense is most likely "I say: counsel and might for the war are only a word of the lips". The Isaiah scroll from Qumran and the versions read "you have said" (from memory); KJV's "sayest thou" is supplied.
- **"Lo, thou trustest in the staff of this broken reed … whereon if a man lean, it will go into his hand" → "Behold, you have trusted in the staff of this crushed reed … which, when a man leans on it, goes into his palm"** (v6) — Hebrew הִנֵּה "behold"; רָצוּץ "crushed"; כַּף "palm". Isaiah lacks the עַתָּה "now" and the לְּךָ "for yourself" of 2 Kgs 18:21 (v9 has לְךָ in both books).
- **"But if thou say to me, We trust in the LORD our God … and said to Judah and to Jerusalem, Ye shall worship before this altar?" → "And if you say to me, 'In Yahweh our Elohim we have trusted,' … 'Before this altar you shall bow down'?"** (v7) — Hebrew תֹאמַר, singular, to Hezekiah (2 Kgs 18:22 has the plural תֹאמְרוּן); בָּטָחְנוּ, a perfect; הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה "bow down". Isaiah lacks the closing "in Jerusalem" of 2 Kgs 18:22.
- **"Now therefore give pledges, I pray thee, to my master the king of Assyria, and I will give thee" → "And now, exchange pledges, please, with my lord the king of Assyria, and let me give you"** (v8) — Hebrew הִתְעָרֶב נָא אֶת, from עָרַב "be surety, give in pledge": make a wager or a bargain; וְאֶתְּנָה, "and let me give". Isaiah writes אֶת־אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר, literally "my lord the king, Assyria" (the article on "king" before a name, as in v16; 2 Kgs 18:23 אֶת־אֲדֹנִי אֶת־מֶלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר "with my lord, with the king of Assyria"): English has one form.
- **"How then wilt thou turn away the face of one captain … and put thy trust on Egypt" → "And how will you turn back the face of one governor … And you have trusted for yourself in Egypt"** (v9) — Hebrew הֵשִׁיב פְּנֵי (= refuse; kept); פַּחַת "governor"; וַתִּבְטַח לְךָ, a narrative past, "and you have trusted for yourself". "Riders" (v8, רֹכְבִים) and "horsemen" (v9, פָּרָשִׁים) are two words.
- **"And am I now come up without the LORD against this land to destroy it? … Go up against this land" → "And now, is it apart from Yahweh that I have come up against this land to ruin it? … Go up to this land and ruin it"** (v10) — Hebrew וְעַתָּה "and now"; מִבַּלְעֲדֵי; הִשְׁחִית "ruin". Isaiah has "against this land" (עַל־הָאָרֶץ) where 2 Kgs 18:25 has "against this place", and "go up to (אֶל) this land" where 2 Kgs has עַל "against".
- **"in the Syrian language; for we understand it … in the Jews' language, in the ears of the people" → "in Aramaic, for we understand … in the language of Judah, in the ears of the people"** (v11, 13) — Hebrew אֲרָמִית; יְהוּדִית, the speech of Judah. שֹׁמְעִים is "hear": "understand" is the sense of hearing a language (no "it"). Isaiah has no "the son of Hilkiah" in v11, and "speak to us" (אֵלֵינוּ) where 2 Kgs 18:26 has "with us" (עִמָּנוּ).
- **"Hath my master sent me to thy master … that they may eat their own dung, and drink their own piss with you?" → "Is it to your lord and to you that my lord has sent me … to eat their excrement and to drink the water of their feet with you?"** (v12) — the verse follows the reading tradition (qere): צוֹאָתָם, "their excrement, filth", and מֵימֵי רַגְלֵיהֶם, "the water of their feet". The written text (ketiv) has the coarse words, חראיהם, "their dung", and שיניהם, "their urine": both say the same thing. Isaiah writes הַאֶל "is it to" where 2 Kgs 18:27 has הַעַל, and has no "to them" after "said". "Hath he not sent me" is not written: "Is it not to the men". "Sit on the wall" is יֹשְׁבִים, "sit" or "dwell".
- **"Hear ye the words of the great king"** (v13) — Hebrew אֶת־דִּבְרֵי, plural (2 Kgs 18:28 "the word"). Isaiah has only "and said" (2 Kgs "and he spoke and said").
- **"he shall not be able to deliver you"** (v14) — Isaiah has no "from his hand" (מִיָּדוֹ, 2 Kgs 18:29).
- **"The LORD will surely deliver us: this city shall not be delivered" → "Yahweh will surely deliver us; this city will not be given"** (v15) — Hebrew הַצֵּל יַצִּילֵנוּ, literally "delivering he will deliver us"; נָתַן "give" (KJV "delivered" uses one English word for two verbs). Isaiah has no "and" before "this city" (2 Kgs 18:30 וְלֹא).
- **"Make an agreement with me by a present … and eat ye every one of his vine … the waters of his own cistern" → "Make a blessing with me … and eat each man his vine and each man his fig tree, and drink each man the water of his pit"** (v16) — Hebrew עֲשׂוּ־אִתִּי בְרָכָה: the word is "blessing"; a peace made with greetings and gifts is meant. בּוֹר "pit": a cistern cut in the rock. הַמֶּלֶךְ אַשּׁוּר with the article, as v8.
- **"a land of corn and wine" → "a land of grain and new wine"** (v17) — Hebrew דָּגָן; תִּירוֹשׁ. Isaiah ends the sentence here: "a land of the olive of fresh oil and of honey; and live, and do not die. And do not listen to Hezekiah, for he incites you" (2 Kgs 18:32) is not in Isaiah.
- **"Beware lest Hezekiah persuade you, saying … Hath any of the gods of the nations delivered his land" → "Lest Hezekiah incite you, saying … Have the gods of the nations delivered each his land"** (v18) — Hebrew פֶּן "lest": "Beware" is not written. הֵסִית "incite" (the verb of 2 Kgs 18:32). הַהִצִּילוּ is a plain question, "have they delivered" (2 Kgs 18:33 adds the infinitive absolute, "surely delivered"). אִישׁ אֶת־אַרְצוֹ "each his land".
- **"where are the gods of Sepharvaim? and have they delivered Samaria out of my hand?" → "Where are the gods of Sepharvaim? And that they have delivered Samaria from my hand?"** (v19) — Hebrew וְכִי־הִצִּילוּ, "and that they delivered" (2 Kgs 18:34 כִּי without "and"); "and have they delivered" is also possible, taking כִּי as emphatic. Isaiah has no "Hena and Ivvah".
- **"Who are they among all the gods of these lands"** (v20) — Hebrew הָאֲרָצוֹת הָאֵלֶּה, "these lands" (2 Kgs 18:35 "the lands"); אַרְצָם, "their land", singular.
- **"But they held their peace … for the king's commandment was, saying, Answer him not" → "And they were silent … for it was the commandment of the king, saying, 'You shall not answer him'"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיַּחֲרִישׁוּ, with no subject (2 Kgs 18:36 "and the people were silent"); לֹא תַעֲנֻהוּ.
- **"with their clothes rent" → "with garments torn"** (v22) — Hebrew קְרוּעֵי בְגָדִים. **"told him the words"** — Hebrew אֵת דִּבְרֵי, with the object marker (2 Kgs 18:37 without it).

### Notes
- **Parallel** — 2 Kgs 18:13–37; 2 Kgs 18:14–16 (Hezekiah's payment) has no counterpart in Isaiah. Compare also 2 Chr 32:1–19.
- **Sennacherib's campaign** — commonly dated 701 BC; his own annals tell of it.
- **Lachish** — a fortified city of Judah, south-west of Jerusalem; the camp of the king of Assyria.
- **The conduit of the upper pool** (v2) — the place of Isa 7:3, where Isaiah met Ahaz.
- **Hamath, Arpad, Sepharvaim** (v19) — cities taken by Assyria; compare 2 Kgs 17:24; Isa 37:13.
