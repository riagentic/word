# Isaiah 38

[1] In those days ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ became sick, to the point of death. And ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ the son of ⟦amoz|Amotz|Amoz⟧, the prophet, came to him and said to him, "Thus says ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧: Give command to your house, for you are dying, and you shall not live."

[2] And ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ turned his face around to the wall and prayed to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[3] And he said, "Ah, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, remember, please, how I have walked before you in truth and with a whole heart, and what is good in your eyes I have done." And ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ wept a great weeping.

[4] And the word of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ came to ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧, saying,

[5] "Go, and you shall say to ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧: Thus says ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of David your father: I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. Behold, I am adding to your days fifteen years.

[6] And from the palm of the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧ I will deliver you and this city, and I will shield this city.

[7] And this is the sign for you from ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will do this thing that he has spoken:

[8] Behold, I am bringing back the shadow of the steps, which has gone down on the steps of ⟦ahaz|Achaz|Ahaz⟧ with the sun, backward ten steps." And the sun returned ten steps, on the steps by which it had gone down.

[9] A writing of ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ king of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, when he had been sick, and he recovered from his sickness:

[10] I said, In the silence of my days
I shall go into the gates of Sheol;
I am deprived of the rest of my years.

[11] I said, I shall not see ⟦yah|Yah|the LORD⟧,
⟦yah|Yah|the LORD⟧ in the land of the living;
I shall not look on man any more
with those who dwell in cessation.

[12] My dwelling is pulled up and carried away from me
like the tent of a shepherd;
I have rolled up my life like the weaver;
from the thrum he cuts me off;
from day to night you make an end of me.

[13] I have composed myself until morning;
like a lion, so he breaks all my bones;
from day to night you make an end of me.

[14] Like a swift, a crane, so I chirp;
I moan like the dove;
my eyes have grown weak toward the height;
⟦adonai|Adonai|Lord⟧, I am oppressed; be surety for me.

[15] What shall I speak? And he has said to me,
and he, he has done it;
I shall walk slowly all my years
because of the bitterness of my soul.

[16] ⟦adonai|Adonai|Lord⟧, upon them they live,
and for all in them is the life of my spirit;
and may you restore me, and make me live.

[17] Behold, for ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧ it was bitter to me, bitter;
and you, you have longed for my soul out of the pit of nothingness,
for you have thrown behind your back all my sins.

[18] For Sheol does not give thanks to you,
death does not praise you;
those who go down to the pit do not hope
for your truth.

[19] The living, the living, he gives thanks to you,
like me, this day;
a father to sons makes known
your truth.

[20] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ — to save me!
And my strings we will play
all the days of our life
at the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[21] And ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ said, "Let them lift up a cake of figs, and let them rub it on the boil, and let him live."

[22] And ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ said, "What is the sign that I shall go up to the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧?"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved. **"the LORD, even the LORD" → "Yah, Yah"** (v11) — Hebrew יָהּ, the short form of the name, twice.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v5) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **Parallel** (v1–8, 21–22) — these verses stand in a longer form in 2 Kgs 20:1–11; the wording of 2 Kings is kept where the Hebrew is the same. Isaiah's Hebrew is shorter and in another order: every difference is translated as Isaiah has it and listed below; nothing is levelled either way. v9–20, the writing of Hezekiah, are not in Kings.
- **"was Hezekiah sick unto death … Set thine house in order: for thou shalt die, and not live" → "became sick, to the point of death … Give command to your house, for you are dying, and you shall not live"** (v1) — Hebrew חָלָה … לָמוּת, "was sick to die"; צַו לְבֵיתֶךָ, "command for your house", as 2 Sam 17:23; מֵת אַתָּה, a participle. **"Isaiah the prophet the son of Amoz" → "Isaiah the son of Amoz, the prophet"** — the Hebrew order.
- **"Then Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall, and prayed unto the LORD" → "And Hezekiah turned his face around to the wall and prayed to Yahweh"** (v2) — Hebrew וַיַּסֵּב, as 1 Kgs 21:4. Isaiah names Hezekiah and has no "saying" (2 Kgs 20:2); v3 opens with וַיֹּאמַר "and he said".
- **"Remember now, O LORD, I beseech thee, how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is good in thy sight. And Hezekiah wept sore" → "Ah, Yahweh, remember, please, how I have walked before you in truth and with a whole heart, and what is good in your eyes I have done … wept a great weeping"** (v3) — Hebrew אָנָּה "Ah"; the Hebrew has no "O"; נָא; הִתְהַלַּכְתִּי, "walked about"; בֶּאֱמֶת "in truth" (not "faithfulness": that is אֱמוּנָה); בְלֵב שָׁלֵם (2 Kgs לֵבָב, the same word "heart"); בְּכִי גָדוֹל.
- **"Then came the word of the LORD to Isaiah, saying" → "And the word of Yahweh came to Isaiah, saying"** (v4) — Isaiah has no "Isaiah had not gone out of the middle court" (2 Kgs 20:4).
- **"Go, and say to Hezekiah … I will add unto thy days fifteen years" → "Go, and you shall say to Hezekiah … Behold, I am adding to your days fifteen years"** (v5) — Hebrew הָלוֹךְ, an infinitive used as a command (2 Kgs 20:5 שׁוּב "return"); וְאָמַרְתָּ "and you shall say"; הִנְנִי יוֹסִף, a participle (2 Kgs 20:6 וְהֹסַפְתִּי "and I will add"). Isaiah has no "the leader of my people" and no "I am healing you; on the third day you shall go up to the house of Yahweh". "Your tears" is singular in the Hebrew.
- **"And I will deliver thee and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria: and I will defend this city" → "And from the palm of the king of Assyria I will deliver you and this city, and I will shield this city"** (v6) — Hebrew כַּף "palm"; גָּנַן עַל. Isaiah has no "on account of myself and on account of David my servant" here (2 Kgs 20:6; it stands in 37:35).
- **"And this shall be a sign unto thee from the LORD, that the LORD will do this thing that he hath spoken" → "And this is the sign for you from Yahweh, that Yahweh will do this thing that he has spoken"** (v7) — the Hebrew has no verb; אֲשֶׁר "that" (2 Kgs 20:9 כִּי); הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה "this thing" (2 Kgs "the thing"). In Isaiah no question is put to Hezekiah and he makes no choice (2 Kgs 20:8–10): the sign is given at once.
- **"Behold, I will bring again the shadow of the degrees, which is gone down in the sun dial of Ahaz, ten degrees backward. So the sun returned ten degrees, by which degrees it was gone down" → "Behold, I am bringing back the shadow of the steps, which has gone down on the steps of Ahaz with the sun, backward ten steps. And the sun returned ten steps, on the steps by which it had gone down"** (v8) — the verse as printed: הִנְנִי מֵשִׁיב, a participle; צֵל הַמַּעֲלוֹת "the shadow of the steps"; בְמַעֲלוֹת אָחָז "on the steps of Ahaz": "dial" and "degrees" are not the word (מַעֲלוֹת is "steps", the word of a stairway, Exod 20:26; 2 Kgs 20:9–11); בַּשֶּׁמֶשׁ "with (or by) the sun". יָרְדָה "it has gone down" is feminine, though צֵל "shadow" is masculine: the subject may be the sun ("on which the sun has gone down") or the steps; the Hebrew does not say. In the second half "the sun" returns (וַתָּשָׁב הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ), where 2 Kgs 20:11 has "he brought back the shadow". The earlier "the shadow cast by the declining sun on the dial of Ahaz" put an explanation into the verse.
- **"The writing of Hezekiah king of Judah, when he had been sick, and was recovered of his sickness" → "A writing of Hezekiah king of Judah, when he had been sick, and he recovered from his sickness"** (v9) — Hebrew מִכְתָּב, "a writing", without the article. Many read מִכְתָּם, the word of the Psalm headings (Ps 16; 56–60; one letter differs); the MT is kept. בַּחֲלֹתוֹ "in his being sick"; וַיְחִי מֵחָלְיוֹ, literally "and he lived from his sickness", as 2 Kgs 1:2 "recover from".
- **Layout** (v10–20) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; a long line is broken where the accents mark a pause (v10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 18–20). The text of v10–20 is among the hardest of the book: the MT is translated as printed and the doubts are named below; no emendation goes into a verse.
- **"I said in the cutting off of my days, I shall go to the gates of the grave: I am deprived of the residue of my years" → "I said, In the silence of my days I shall go into the gates of Sheol; I am deprived of the rest of my years"** (v10) — Hebrew בִּדְמִי יָמַי: דֳּמִי is "silence, stillness, a pause" (from דָּמָה "be still, cease"; "silence" as Ps 83:1); "in the midst / noontide of my days" and KJV "cutting off" are other readings of the same word: uncertain. שְׁאוֹל, Sheol, the plain word (not "the grave"). פֻּקַּדְתִּי, pual of פָּקַד, "I am visited / mustered / made to miss": "deprived of" is the sense taken; "I am consigned (for) the rest of my years" is also defended.
- **"I shall behold man no more with the inhabitants of the world" → "I shall not look on man any more with those who dwell in cessation"** (v11) — Hebrew הִבִּיט "look on"; אָדָם "man"; יוֹשְׁבֵי חָדֶל, "the dwellers of cessation" (חָדֶל from חָדַל "cease": where everything has ceased). "World" is חֶלֶד, the same letters in another order (Ps 49:1); it is often read here, and the MT is kept.
- **"Mine age is departed, and is removed from me as a shepherd's tent: I have cut off like a weaver my life: he will cut me off with pining sickness" → "My dwelling is pulled up and carried away from me like the tent of a shepherd; I have rolled up my life like the weaver; from the thrum he cuts me off"** (v12) — Hebrew דּוֹרִי: the word is elsewhere "generation" (דּוֹר); here "dwelling" (as the verb דּוּר "dwell", Ps 84:10), or "my lifetime": uncertain. נִסַּע "is pulled up", nifal of נָסַע, whose root sense is to pull up the tent pegs; נִגְלָה, nifal of גָּלָה, "is carried away" (into exile). רֹעִי, "of a shepherd" (an unusual form, also read "my shepherd"). קִפַּדְתִּי, found only here, "I have rolled up" (as a weaver rolls up the finished cloth): uncertain. דַּלָּה "thrum", the threads by which the web hangs on the loom (KJV takes it from דַּל "poor": "pining sickness"). יְבַצְּעֵנִי "he cuts me off". **"from day even to night wilt thou make an end of me" → "from day to night you make an end of me"** (v12, 13) — Hebrew תַּשְׁלִימֵנִי, hifil of שָׁלֵם, "bring to completion, finish".
- **"I reckoned till morning, that, as a lion, so will he break all my bones" → "I have composed myself until morning; like a lion, so he breaks all my bones"** (v13) — Hebrew שִׁוִּיתִי, piel of שָׁוָה "make level, smooth" (Ps 131:2 "I have smoothed … my soul"): here without an object, so "myself" is supplied; KJV "reckoned" is another sense of the verb. "I cried out" (שִׁוַּעְתִּי, from the versions and commentators, from memory) is not followed. כָּאֲרִי כֵּן, "like a lion, so".
- **"Like a crane or a swallow, so did I chatter: I did mourn as a dove: mine eyes fail with looking upward: O LORD, I am oppressed; undertake for me" → "Like a swift, a crane, so I chirp; I moan like the dove; my eyes have grown weak toward the height; Adonai, I am oppressed; be surety for me"** (v14) — Hebrew סוּס "swift" (the same letters as "horse"; Jer 8:7) and עָגוּר "crane" (or "thrush"): both birds uncertain; no "or" is written. אֲצַפְצֵף "chirp, twitter"; אֶהְגֶּה, the verb הָגָה, of a dove "moan". דַּלּוּ "are low, have grown weak"; לַמָּרוֹם "toward the height": "looking" is not written. אֲדֹנָי, not the name Yahweh; the Hebrew has no "O". עָשְׁקָה־לִּי, literally "oppression to me" (the pointing is unusual: a noun or a verb): "I am oppressed". עָרְבֵנִי, imperative of עָרַב "be surety for".
- **"What shall I say? he hath both spoken unto me, and himself hath done it: I shall go softly all my years in the bitterness of my soul" → "What shall I speak? And he has said to me, and he, he has done it; I shall walk slowly all my years because of the bitterness of my soul"** (v15) — Hebrew אֲדַבֵּר "speak"; וְאָמַר־לִי "and he has said to me"; וְהוּא, the pronoun set out; אֶדַּדֶּה, hitpael of דָּדָה, found only here and Ps 42:4 ("led in procession"): "walk slowly" is uncertain; עַל־מַר נַפְשִׁי, "upon / because of the bitterness of my soul".
- **"O Lord, by these things men live, and in all these things is the life of my spirit: so wilt thou recover me, and make me to live" → "Adonai, upon them they live, and for all in them is the life of my spirit; and may you restore me, and make me live"** (v16) — the Hebrew is word for word as printed: עֲלֵיהֶם יִחְיוּ "upon them they live", וּלְכָל־בָּהֶן "and for all in them" (masculine "them", then feminine "them"): what "them" refers to is not said; "men" and "things" are supplied in KJV. Who "they" are is not said. The Hebrew has no "O". וְתַחֲלִימֵנִי, from חָלַם "be strong, healthy" (Job 39:4), a yiqtol with "and" that can be a wish or a future ("and you will restore me"); וְהַחֲיֵנִי is an imperative, "and make me live".
- **"Behold, for peace I had great bitterness: but thou hast in love to my soul delivered it from the pit of corruption" → "Behold, for shalom it was bitter to me, bitter; and you, you have longed for my soul out of the pit of nothingness"** (v17) — Hebrew לְשָׁלוֹם "for shalom" (or "instead of shalom"); מַר־לִי מָר, "bitter to me, bitter". חָשַׁקְתָּ "you have longed for, been bound to" (Gen 34:8; Deut 7:7), with מִן "from, out of": the longing that drew the soul out of the pit. Many read חָשַׂכְתָּ "you have held back" (one letter differs; from memory); the MT is kept. שַׁחַת "pit"; בְּלִי, "without, nothing": "the pit of nothingness" ("of destruction", "of corruption" are senses taken from the context). **"thou hast cast all my sins behind thy back"** — Hebrew הִשְׁלַכְתָּ "thrown"; גֵּו "back".
- **"For the grave cannot praise thee, death can not celebrate thee: they that go down into the pit cannot hope for thy truth" → "For Sheol does not give thanks to you, death does not praise you; those who go down to the pit do not hope for your truth"** (v18, 19) — Hebrew שְׁאוֹל, Sheol (not "the grave"); תּוֹדֶךָּ, from הוֹדָה "give thanks"; יְהַלְלֶךָּ "praise"; שִׂבֵּר "hope, wait for"; בּוֹר "pit" (v17 has שַׁחַת: two words, one English); אֲמִתֶּךָ "your truth" (not "faithfulness").
- **"the father to the children shall make known thy truth" → "a father to sons makes known your truth"** (v19) — Hebrew אָב לְבָנִים, without the article, "sons" (בָּנִים); יוֹדִיעַ אֶל־אֲמִתֶּךָ, "makes known concerning your truth". **"as I do this day" → "like me, this day"** — Hebrew כָּמוֹנִי הַיּוֹם; "do" is not written.
- **"The LORD was ready to save me: therefore we will sing my songs to the stringed instruments … in the house of the LORD" → "Yahweh — to save me! And my strings we will play all the days of our life at the house of Yahweh"** (v20) — Hebrew יְהוָה לְהוֹשִׁיעֵנִי, without a verb: "Yahweh is (at hand) to save me" or a cry; KJV "was ready" is supplied. נְגִנוֹתַי "my strings, my string music" and נְנַגֵּן "we will play" (on strings), one root; "my" and "we" stand as written. עַל־בֵּית "upon / at the house".
- **"For Isaiah had said, Let them take a lump of figs, and lay it for a plaister upon the boil, and he shall recover" → "And Isaiah said, 'Let them lift up a cake of figs, and let them rub it on the boil, and let him live'"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר, "and he said": the pluperfect "had said" and "For" are not written. In Isaiah this verse and v22 stand after the writing of Hezekiah; in 2 Kgs 20:7–8 they stand before the sign. Isaiah's words differ from 2 Kgs 20:7: יִשְׂאוּ "let them lift up" (2 Kgs קְחוּ "take"), וְיִמְרְחוּ "and let them rub" (2 Kgs "and they took and put it"), וְיֶחִי, a jussive, "and let him live" (2 Kgs וַיֶּחִי "and he lived"). דְּבֶלֶת תְּאֵנִים, pressed figs; שְׁחִין "boil".
- **"Hezekiah also had said, What is the sign that I shall go up to the house of the LORD?" → "And Hezekiah said, 'What is the sign that I shall go up to the house of Yahweh?'"** (v22) — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר; "also had said" is not written. Isaiah has no "that Yahweh will heal me" and no "on the third day" (2 Kgs 20:8).

### Notes
- **Parallel** — 2 Kgs 20:1–11; compare 2 Chr 32:24.
- **The order** (v21–22) — the remedy and Hezekiah's question come in Isaiah after the writing; the book gives them where it gives them, and no order is restored.
- **The steps of Ahaz** (v8) — steps on which a shadow moved with the sun. How the sign came about is not said.
- **The writing of Hezekiah** (v9–20) — a song of one who was near death and lived: Sheol and the pit (v10, 17–18), the cry (v14), and thanksgiving at the house of Yahweh (v20).
