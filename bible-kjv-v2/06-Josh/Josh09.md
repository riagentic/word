# Joshua 9

[1] When all the kings who were beyond the Jordan in the hill country, in the lowlands, and along all the coast of the Great Sea toward Lebanon — the Hittite, the Amorite, the Canaanite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite — heard of this,

[2] they gathered together to fight against Joshua and Israel, as one.

[3] But when the inhabitants of Gibeon heard what Joshua had done to Jericho and to Ai,

[4] they acted shrewdly: they went as if they were envoys, took worn-out sacks on their donkeys, and wineskins — old, torn, and patched;

[5] and they wore worn-out, patched sandals on their feet, and old garments on themselves. All the bread of their provisions was dry and crumbled.

[6] They went to Joshua in the camp at Gilgal and said to him and to the men of Israel, "We have come from a distant country; now make a covenant with us."

[7] The men of Israel said to the Hivites, "Perhaps you dwell among us; how then can we make a covenant with you?"

[8] They said to Joshua, "We are your servants." Joshua said to them, "Who are you? And where do you come from?"

[9] They said to him, "From a very distant country your servants have come because of the name of Yahweh your *Elohim*; for we have heard the fame of him and all that he did in Egypt,

[10] and all that he did to the two kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan — to Sihon king of Heshbon and to Og king of Bashan, who was at Ashtaroth.

[11] So our elders and all the inhabitants of our land said to us, 'Take provisions in your hand for the journey and go to meet them; say to them, "We are your servants; now make a covenant with us."'

[12] This is our bread; we took it warm from our houses as our provision on the day we set out to come to you; but now, behold, it is dry and crumbled.

[13] These are the wineskins that we filled new, and behold, they are torn; and our garments and our sandals have worn out because of the very long journey."

[14] The men of Israel took some of their provisions, but did not seek counsel from the mouth of Yahweh.

[15] Joshua made peace with them and made a covenant with them, to let them live; and the chiefs of the congregation swore an oath to them.

[16] At the end of three days after they had made a covenant with them, they heard that they were their neighbors and dwelt among them.

[17] The children of Israel set out and came to their cities on the third day. Their cities were Gibeon, Kephirah, Beeroth, and Kiriath-jearim.

[18] The children of Israel did not strike them down, because the chiefs of the congregation had sworn to them by Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel. Then the whole congregation grumbled against the chiefs.

[19] But all the chiefs said to all the congregation, "We have sworn to them by Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel, and now we cannot touch them.

[20] This we will do to them: let them live, lest wrath be upon us because of the oath that we swore to them."

[21] The chiefs said to them, "Let them live." So they became hewers of wood and drawers of water for all the congregation, as the chiefs had spoken to them.

[22] Joshua summoned them and said to them, "Why did you deceive us, saying, 'We are very far from you,' when you dwell among us?

[23] Now therefore you are cursed, and none of you shall fail to be a slave — hewers of wood and drawers of water for the house of my *Elohim*."

[24] They answered Joshua, "Your servants were clearly told how Yahweh your *Elohim* commanded his servant Moses to give you all the land and to destroy all the inhabitants of the land from before you. We feared greatly for our lives because of you, and we did this thing.

[25] And now, behold, we are in your hand; do to us whatever seems good and right in your eyes to do."

[26] So he did to them thus, and delivered them out of the hand of the children of Israel, so that they did not kill them.

[27] On that day Joshua made them hewers of wood and drawers of water for the congregation and for the altar of Yahweh, to this day, in the place that he would choose.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"wilily" → "shrewdly"** (v4) — modernized.
- **"ambassadors" → "envoys"** (v4).
- **"bottles" → "wineskins"** (v4, 13) — Hebrew *nodot*. Skin containers, not bottles.
- **"clouted" → "patched"** (v5) — archaic.
- **"mouldy" → "crumbled"** (v5, 12) — Hebrew *niqqudim*. Possibly crumb-like or dry-and-broken rather than moldy.
- **"league" → "covenant"** (v6, 7, 11, 15, 16) — Hebrew בְּרִית *berit*.
- **"princes" → "chiefs"** (throughout).
- **"bondmen" → "slaves"** (v23).
- **"sore afraid" → "feared greatly"** (v24) — archaic.

### Notes
- **Gibeonite deception** — the Gibeonites fake distance to secure a non-aggression treaty. They understood Deut 20:10–18: distant cities could be offered peace; cities within the land must be destroyed.
- **"Did not seek counsel from Yahweh"** (v14) — Israel's key mistake. Urim and Thummim protocol not followed. The treaty stands because oath is sacred; but the decision was unwise.
- **Sacred oath honored** — despite deception, the covenant-by-oath binds. Ancient Near Eastern sworn oath was irrevocable once made, even if procured fraudulently. Saul later breaks this oath (2 Sam 21), and a famine follows — divine retribution for the broken Gibeonite covenant centuries later.
- **"Hewers of wood and drawers of water"** (v21, 23, 27) — perpetual menial sanctuary service. The Gibeonites become *netinim* — temple servants. Nehemiah 7:46 lists their descendants as returning from exile.
- **"The place he will choose"** (v27) — Deuteronomic phrase (Deut 12). Future Jerusalem/temple.
- **Saul's violation** (2 Sam 21:1–9) — three-year famine attributed to Saul's killing of Gibeonites. David makes amends by giving Saul's descendants to the Gibeonites. The Gibeonite treaty's sanctity stands for centuries.
