## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"hough their horses" → "hamstring their horses"** (v6, 9) — Hebrew עָקַר *aqar*. Cut the hamstrings — disabling warhorses without killing them.
- **"stood still in their strength" → "stood on mounds"** (v13) — Hebrew עֹמְדוֹת עַל־תִּלָּם *omdot al tilam*. "Still standing on their tel / mound." Archaeological vocabulary — *tel* = artificial mound from successive occupation.
- **"borders of Dor" → "Naphoth-dor"** (v2) — proper name preserved.
- **"utterly destroy" → "devote to destruction"** — *cherem*.
- **"harden their hearts"** (v20) — Hebrew לְחַזֵּק אֶת־לִבָּם *le-chazzeq et libbam*. Echoes the Pharaoh-hardening motif (Ex 7–11).

### Notes
- **Northern campaign** — parallels the southern campaign of ch 10. Hazor was Canaan's leading city; its fall is decisive.
- **Hazor's historical significance** — the largest Canaanite city-state, ~200 acres, strategic north-south crossroads. Yadin's excavations (1955–1969) found a massive Late Bronze Age destruction layer matching biblical dating.
- **Horses and chariots** (v4, 6, 9) — the first major chariot force Israel encounters. Israel's infantry must overcome mechanized warfare; Yahweh neutralizes the advantage by commanding hamstringing.
- **"Land of Goshen"** (v16) — a region of southern Palestine, distinct from Egyptian Goshen of Exodus.
- **Divine hardening** (v20) — theological statement: Canaanite resistance serves the purpose of their destruction. Paralleled with Pharaoh's hardening.
- **Anakim remnant** (v22) — in Gaza, Gath, Ashdod — i.e., Philistine territory. Goliath of Gath (1 Sam 17), described as a giant, is descendant of this remnant.
- **"Land had rest from war"** (v23) — temporary peace. The conquest is substantially complete; ch 12 summarizes, ch 13+ distributes.
