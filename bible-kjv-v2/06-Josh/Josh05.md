# Joshua 5

[1] When all the kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan to the west, and all the kings of the Canaanites who were by the sea, heard that Yahweh had dried up the waters of the Jordan from before the children of Israel until they had crossed over, their hearts melted, and no spirit was left in them because of the children of Israel.

[2] At that time Yahweh said to Joshua, "Make for yourself flint knives, and circumcise the children of Israel a second time."

[3] So Joshua made flint knives for himself and circumcised the children of Israel at Gibeath-haaraloth — the Hill of Foreskins.

[4] This is the reason why Joshua circumcised them: all the people who came out of Egypt — the males, all the men of war — had died in the wilderness on the way after coming out of Egypt.

[5] Though all the people who came out were circumcised, all the people born in the wilderness on the way after coming out of Egypt had not been circumcised.

[6] For the children of Israel walked forty years in the wilderness, until all the nation — the men of war who came out of Egypt — were consumed, because they did not obey the voice of Yahweh. To them Yahweh swore that he would not let them see the land that Yahweh had sworn to their fathers to give us — a land flowing with milk and honey.

[7] Their children, whom he raised up in their place — them Joshua circumcised, for they were uncircumcised, because they had not been circumcised on the way.

[8] When the whole nation had finished being circumcised, they remained in their places in the camp until they were healed.

[9] And Yahweh said to Joshua, "Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you." So the name of that place has been called Gilgal ("Rolling") to this day.

[10] The children of Israel encamped at Gilgal, and they kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the month in the evening, in the plains of Jericho.

[11] On the day after the Passover, on that very day, they ate of the produce of the land — unleavened bread and roasted grain.

[12] The manna ceased on the day after they ate the produce of the land; the children of Israel had no more manna, but they ate of the yield of the land of Canaan that year.

[13] When Joshua was by Jericho, he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man was standing before him with his drawn sword in his hand. Joshua went to him and said to him, "Are you for us, or for our adversaries?"

[14] He said, "No; rather as commander of the army of Yahweh I have now come." Then Joshua fell on his face to the earth and did homage, and said to him, "What does my lord say to his servant?"

[15] And the commander of Yahweh's army said to Joshua, "Remove your sandal from your foot, for the place where you are standing is holy." And Joshua did so.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"sharp knives" → "flint knives"** (v2, 3) — Hebrew חַרְבוֹת צֻרִים *charvot tzurim*. Lit. "swords of flint." Traditional surgical / ritual tool.
- **"Gibeath-haaraloth" added** (v3) — Hebrew גִּבְעַת הָעֲרָלוֹת *Giv'at ha-aralot*. Preserved as place-name with translation.
- **"Gilgal"** (v9) — Hebrew גִּלְגָּל from גָּלַל *galal* = "to roll." Wordplay: the reproach is "rolled away" (*galloti*).
- **"corn" → "produce / grain"** (v11, 12).
- **"captain of the host" → "commander of the army"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew שַׂר־צְבָא *sar-tzeva*.
- **"Loose thy shoe" → "Remove your sandal"** (v15) — parallels Moses at burning bush (Ex 3:5).
- **"reproach of Egypt"** (v9) — the shame of uncircumcision / slave-status. Rolled away by the new generation's covenant-sign.

### Notes
- **Circumcision en masse** (v2–9) — the wilderness generation had not been circumcised in the 40-year journey. The new generation is now ritually prepared for the covenant-land. Circumcision and Passover-celebration together re-inaugurate covenantal identity.
- **"Second time"** (v2) — debated: circumcision was not "literally" second — unless referring to Israel's collective covenantal re-circumcision after exodus.
- **Manna ceases** (v12) — the day after Passover (the "second day of unleavened bread"). Wilderness provisions end; land-produce begins. Transition complete.
- **"Commander of Yahweh's army"** (v13–15) — theophanic figure. Parallels Moses at Sinai. Jewish tradition identifies as the archangel Michael or the "Angel of Yahweh." Early Christian tradition often reads christologically.
- **"Are you for us or them?" — "No"** (v13–14) — the commander is for neither; he is Yahweh's. Joshua must join *his* side, not vice versa.
- **Holy ground** (v15) — same words as to Moses. Continuity of theophany and commission.
