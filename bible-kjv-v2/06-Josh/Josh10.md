# Joshua 10

[1] When Adoni-zedek king of Jerusalem heard that Joshua had captured Ai and devoted it to destruction, doing to Ai and its king as he had done to Jericho and its king, and that the inhabitants of Gibeon had made peace with Israel and were among them,

[2] they feared greatly, because Gibeon was a great city, like one of the royal cities, greater than Ai, and all its men were mighty.

[3] So Adoni-zedek king of Jerusalem sent to Hoham king of Hebron, to Piram king of Jarmuth, to Japhia king of Lachish, and to Debir king of Eglon, saying,

[4] "Come up to me and help me, and let us strike Gibeon, because it has made peace with Joshua and with the children of Israel."

[5] So the five kings of the Amorites — the king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish, and the king of Eglon — gathered themselves together and went up with all their armies, and encamped against Gibeon, making war against it.

[6] The men of Gibeon sent to Joshua at the camp at Gilgal, saying, "Do not withdraw your hand from your servants. Come up to us quickly and save us; help us, for all the kings of the Amorites who dwell in the hill country have gathered against us."

[7] So Joshua went up from Gilgal, he and all the warriors with him, all the mighty men of valor.

[8] Yahweh said to Joshua, "Do not fear them, for I have given them into your hand; not one of them shall stand before you."

[9] So Joshua came upon them suddenly, having marched up all night from Gilgal.

[10] Yahweh threw them into panic before Israel, and he struck them with a great slaughter at Gibeon, chased them along the way of the ascent of Beth-horon, and struck them down as far as Azekah and Makkedah.

[11] As they fled before Israel, while they were on the descent of Beth-horon, Yahweh cast down great stones from the heavens upon them, as far as Azekah, and they died. More died by the hailstones than the children of Israel killed with the sword.

[12] Then Joshua spoke to Yahweh on the day Yahweh gave the Amorites over to the children of Israel, and he said in the sight of Israel:

"Sun, stand still at Gibeon;
and Moon, in the Valley of Ajalon."

[13] And the sun stood still, and the moon stayed, until the nation took vengeance on its enemies. Is this not written in the Book of Yashar? The sun stopped in the middle of the heavens and did not hurry to go down for about a whole day.

[14] There has not been a day like it before or since, when Yahweh listened to the voice of a man; for Yahweh fought for Israel.

[15] Then Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal.

[16] Now the five kings had fled and hidden themselves in the cave at Makkedah.

[17] It was told to Joshua, "The five kings have been found hidden in the cave at Makkedah."

[18] Joshua said, "Roll great stones against the mouth of the cave, and set men by it to guard them;

[19] but do not stay yourselves — pursue your enemies and attack their rearguard. Do not allow them to enter their cities, for Yahweh your *Elohim* has given them into your hand."

[20] When Joshua and the children of Israel had finished striking them with a very great slaughter, until they were destroyed — the survivors who remained entered fortified cities —

[21] all the people returned to the camp to Joshua at Makkedah in peace. No one raised a word against any of the children of Israel.

[22] Then Joshua said, "Open the mouth of the cave and bring those five kings out of the cave to me."

[23] They did so and brought those five kings out of the cave to him — the king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish, and the king of Eglon.

[24] When they brought those kings to Joshua, Joshua called for all the men of Israel, and said to the commanders of the men of war who had gone with him, "Come near; put your feet on the necks of these kings." And they came near and put their feet on their necks.

[25] Joshua said to them, "Do not be afraid or dismayed; be strong and courageous. Thus shall Yahweh do to all your enemies against whom you fight."

[26] Afterward Joshua struck them down and killed them, and hung them on five trees; and they hung on the trees until evening.

[27] At sunset Joshua commanded, and they took them down from the trees and threw them into the cave where they had been hidden, and laid great stones over the mouth of the cave, which remain to this very day.

[28] On that day Joshua captured Makkedah and struck it with the edge of the sword; he devoted its king to destruction, it and every person in it — he left no survivor. He did to the king of Makkedah as he had done to the king of Jericho.

[29] Then Joshua passed from Makkedah, and all Israel with him, to Libnah, and fought against Libnah.

[30] Yahweh gave it also, and its king, into the hand of Israel; and he struck it with the edge of the sword, and every person in it. He left no survivor in it; he did to its king as he had done to the king of Jericho.

[31] Joshua passed from Libnah, and all Israel with him, to Lachish, and encamped against it and fought against it.

[32] Yahweh gave Lachish into the hand of Israel; he captured it on the second day and struck it with the edge of the sword, and every person in it, according to all that he had done to Libnah.

[33] Then Horam king of Gezer came up to help Lachish; Joshua struck him and his people down, until no survivor remained.

[34] From Lachish Joshua passed to Eglon, and all Israel with him; they encamped against it and fought against it.

[35] They captured it that day, struck it with the edge of the sword, and devoted every person in it to destruction that day, according to all that he had done to Lachish.

[36] Joshua went up from Eglon, and all Israel with him, to Hebron; and they fought against it.

[37] They captured it and struck it with the edge of the sword — its king, all its cities, and every person in it. He left no survivor, just as he had done to Eglon; he devoted it to destruction, and every person in it.

[38] Joshua turned back, and all Israel with him, to Debir, and fought against it.

[39] He captured it, and its king, and all its cities; they struck them with the edge of the sword, and devoted every person in it to destruction. He left no survivor. As he had done to Hebron, so he did to Debir and its king; and as he had done to Libnah and its king.

[40] So Joshua struck all the land — the hill country, the Negev, the lowlands, and the slopes — and all their kings. He left no survivor, but devoted to destruction all that breathed, as Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel had commanded.

[41] Joshua struck them from Kadesh-barnea to Gaza, and all the land of Goshen, as far as Gibeon.

[42] Joshua took all these kings and their land at one time, because Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel fought for Israel.

[43] Then Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"discomfited" → "threw into panic"** (v10) — Hebrew הָמַם *hamam*. Supernatural confusion.
- **"book of Jasher" → "Book of Yashar"** (v13) — Hebrew סֵפֶר הַיָּשָׁר *Sefer ha-Yashar*. Lost ancient Israelite poetic collection; also mentioned 2 Sam 1:18. "Yashar" = "upright."
- **"captains" → "commanders"** (v24).
- **"fenced cities" → "fortified cities"** (v20).
- **"the south / vale / springs" → "the Negev / lowlands / slopes"** (v40) — standard geographic terms.
- **"utterly destroyed" → "devoted to destruction"** — *cherem* throughout.
- **"hindmost" → "rearguard"** (v19).

### Notes
- **Sun standing still** (v12–14) — the most famous and debated passage. Ancient tradition: literal solar pause. Modern explanations: (1) literal miracle; (2) poetic language for extended daylight; (3) eclipse (sun "standing still" = sun not completing its course, i.e., darkened); (4) divine timing of the battle. The *Book of Yashar* citation suggests the narrative preserves ancient poetic tradition.
- **Hailstones from heaven** (v11) — Yahweh fights directly. More died by hail than by sword. The miracle at Beth-horon.
- **Adoni-zedek / "My Lord is righteous"** — king of Jerusalem. Compare Melchizedek ("King of Righteousness") of Gen 14 — also of Salem/Jerusalem. Jerusalem preserves the *tzedeq*-name.
- **Foot-on-neck** (v24) — ancient victory gesture. Commanders stepping on the defeated kings' necks as public sign of Yahweh's victory.
- **Southern campaign summary** (v28–43) — rapid-fire conquest narrative. Eight southern cities captured in series: Makkedah, Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron, Debir. Plus Gezer's king defeated.
- **Archaeology of southern sites** — Lachish and Debir have been excavated. Destruction layers dated variously; Iron Age destruction layers match some features, Late Bronze others. Scholarly debate continues.
