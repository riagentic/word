## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"trespass" → "treachery"** (v16, 20, 31) — *ma'al*.
- **"meat offering" → "grain offering"** (v23, 29).
- **"princes" → "chiefs"** (throughout).
- **"great altar to see to" → "a great altar, of imposing appearance"** (v10) — Hebrew *gadol le-mar'eh*.
- **"pattern" → "replica"** (v28) — Hebrew *tavnit*.
- **"The Lord God of gods" → "El, *Elohim*, Yahweh"** (v22) — Hebrew אֵל אֱלֹהִים יְהוָה *El Elohim YHWH*. Triple divine name, emphatic oath. Kept untranslated.
- **"Ed" (v34)** — Hebrew עֵד *Ed* = "witness." Etymology retained.
- **MT reading of v34 lacks the word "Ed"** — some manuscripts and the Hebrew read: "The Reubenites and Gadites called the altar — for it is a witness between us that Yahweh is *Elohim*." The name "Ed" is implied / supplied.

### Notes
- **Transjordan tribes fulfill vow** — after conquest they return home with plunder. Their loyalty to the covenant is complete.
- **The "altar of witness"** — a political-religious monument. They build it at the Jordan crossing, not as a sacrificial altar, but as a visible claim to Israelite identity: "We belong to Yahweh too."
- **Near-civil-war averted** (v12) — Israel mobilized for war, recognizing a potentially schismatic act. Dialogue and explanation prevent bloodshed.
- **Phinehas as mediator** — the zealous priest of Num 25 now serves as peace-negotiator. His presence lends weight.
- **"One altar" theology** (v19, 29) — Deut 12's centralization-of-worship is defended. Only the altar at Shiloh (later Jerusalem) is legitimate for sacrifice. Other altars risk schism.
- **Foreshadowing** — the divided-kingdom era (1 Kgs 12) will see Jeroboam actually build rival altars (Dan and Bethel). What Reuben-Gad avoided, the northern kingdom embraced.
