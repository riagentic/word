# Nehemiah 8

[1] All the people gathered together as one man in the square before the Water Gate; they told Ezra the scribe to bring the Scroll of the Law of Moses that Yahweh had commanded Israel.

[2] On the first day of the seventh month, Ezra the priest brought the Law before the assembly, both men and women and all who could understand with attentiveness.

[3] He read from it in the square before the Water Gate, from morning until midday, before the men, the women, and those who could understand; the ears of all the people were attentive to the Scroll of the Law.

[4] Ezra the scribe stood on a wooden platform that had been made for the purpose; beside him stood Mattithiah, Shema, Anaiah, Uriah, Hilkiah, and Maaseiah on his right hand; and on his left, Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, Hashum, Hashbaddanah, Zechariah, and Meshullam.

[5] Ezra opened the scroll in the sight of all the people (for he was above all the people). When he opened it, all the people stood up.

[6] Ezra blessed Yahweh, the great *Elohim*. All the people answered, "Amen! Amen!" with lifting up of their hands; they bowed down, and prostrated themselves before Yahweh with their faces to the ground.

[7] Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah, Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, and Pelaiah — the Levites — taught the people the Law while the people remained in their place.

[8] They read from the scroll, from the Law of *Elohim*, with interpretation; they gave the sense, so that the people understood the reading.

[9] Nehemiah — that is, the Governor — Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who taught the people, said to all the people, "This day is holy to Yahweh your *Elohim*. Do not mourn or weep." For all the people were weeping as they heard the words of the Law.

[10] Then he said to them, "Go your way, eat the fat, drink the sweet, and send portions to those for whom nothing is prepared, for this day is holy to the Lord. Do not be grieved, for the joy of Yahweh is your strength."

[11] So the Levites calmed all the people, saying, "Be still, for the day is holy; do not be grieved."

[12] All the people went their way to eat, drink, send portions, and make great rejoicing, because they had understood the words that had been declared to them.

[13] On the second day the heads of fathers' houses of all the people, the priests, and the Levites gathered to Ezra the scribe to give attention to the words of the Law.

[14] They found written in the Law that Yahweh had commanded through Moses that the children of Israel should dwell in booths — during the feast of the seventh month;

[15] and that they should publish and proclaim in all their cities and in Jerusalem, saying, "Go out to the hills and bring olive branches, branches of wild olive, myrtle branches, palm branches, and branches of leafy trees, to make booths, as it is written."

[16] So the people went out, brought them, and made themselves booths — each on the roof of his house, in their courts, in the courts of the house of *Elohim*, in the square of the Water Gate, and in the square of the Gate of Ephraim.

[17] All the assembly of those who had returned from the captivity made booths and dwelt in them; for from the days of Joshua son of Nun until that day the children of Israel had not done so. There was very great gladness.

[18] Day by day, from the first day to the last day, he read from the Scroll of the Law of *Elohim*. They kept the feast seven days; on the eighth day was a solemn assembly, according to the ordinance.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"pulpit" → "platform"** (v4) — *migdal-etz*.
- **"Jeshua the son of Nun" → "Joshua son of Nun"** (v17) — Joshua; both names same Hebrew.
- **"pine branches" → "branches of wild olive"** (v15) — Hebrew *etz shemen*; wild olive / oleaster, not pine. MT reads "olive branches and wild-olive branches."
- **"distinctly... gave the sense" → "with interpretation... gave the sense"** (v8) — *meforash*; traditionally read as hint of early Aramaic translation practice (targum).

### Notes
- **First day of seventh month** — Feast of Trumpets / Rosh Hashanah (Lev 23:24). Public Torah-reading unique development.
- **Square before Water Gate** — open plaza, inclusive of men, women, and children-old-enough.
- **Ezra on a platform** — visible, ceremonial. Reading from morning to noon (~6 hours).
- **Levites interpreting** (v7-8) — "with interpretation"; rabbinic tradition sees origin of Targum (Aramaic translation) and rabbinic exposition here.
- **"Joy of Yahweh is your strength"** (v10) — proverbial line. Festivals as strengthening, not mournful.
- **"Fat... sweet... portions"** — inclusive feast: share with those who have nothing prepared.
- **Feast of Booths** (v14) — Sukkot; not observed in this form since Joshua's day. Emphasizes return-from-wandering theme.
- **Daily Torah-reading** during the feast — institutional innovation of public literacy in scripture.
