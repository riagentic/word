# 1 Timothy 3

[1] Faithful is the word: if anyone reaches after oversight, he desires a good work.

[2] The overseer therefore must be irreproachable, a husband of one wife, temperate, sober-minded, orderly, hospitable, apt to teach,

[3] not given to wine, not a striker, but gentle, no fighter, not a lover of money,

[4] leading his own house well, having children in subjection with all dignity

[5] (but if anyone does not know how to lead his own house, how will he take care of the church of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧?),

[6] not newly planted, lest, being puffed up, he fall into the sentence of the devil.

[7] And he must also have a good testimony from those outside, lest he fall into reproach and a snare of the devil.

[8] Servants likewise must be dignified, not double-worded, not giving heed to much wine, not greedy for shameful gain,

[9] having the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience.

[10] And let these also first be tested, then let them serve, being blameless.

[11] Women likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, temperate, faithful in all things.

[12] Let servants be husbands of one wife, leading children and their own houses well;

[13] for those who have served well acquire for themselves a good standing and much boldness in faith that is in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[14] These things I write to you, hoping to come to you shortly,

[15] but if I delay, that you may know how one must conduct oneself in the house of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, which is the church of the living ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, pillar and support of the truth;

[16] and confessedly great is the mystery of godliness:

Who was manifested in flesh,
justified in spirit,
seen by angels,
proclaimed among nations,
believed on in the world,
taken up in glory.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Christ Jesus" → "*Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v13) — systematic name restoration.
- **"church"** (vv5, 15) — Greek ἐκκλησία *ekklēsia*, "assembly/congregation." In both verses it stands without the article (ἐκκλησίας θεοῦ; ἐκκλησία θεοῦ ζῶντος), as do "house of *Elohim*" and "pillar and support" in v15.
- **"This is a true saying" → "Faithful is the word"** (v1) — Greek Πιστὸς ὁ λόγος, as 1:15.
- **"desire the office of a bishop" → "reaches after oversight"** (v1) — Greek ἐπισκοπῆς ὀρέγεται: ἐπισκοπή, the task of an overseer (ἐπίσκοπος, v2), one word; ὀρέγομαι "stretch oneself toward", as 6:10. "Desires" renders the second verb, ἐπιθυμεῖ.
- **"A bishop" → "The overseer"** (v2) — Greek τὸν ἐπίσκοπον, as Titus 1:7.
- **"blameless" → "irreproachable"** (v2) — Greek ἀνεπίλημπτον, "not to be laid hold of"; also 5:7; 6:14. "Blameless" is ἀνέγκλητος (v10; Titus 1:6).
- **"vigilant, sober, of good behaviour" → "temperate, sober-minded, orderly"** (v2) — Greek νηφάλιον, σώφρονα, κόσμιον, as Titus 2:2; κόσμιος as 2:9.
- **"not given to wine, no striker" kept; "not greedy of filthy lucre" omitted** (v3) — Greek μὴ πάροινον, μὴ πλήκτην, as Titus 1:7; the third phrase is not in the critical text here (it stands in v8).
- **"patient, not a brawler, not covetous" → "gentle, no fighter, not a lover of money"** (v3) — Greek ἐπιεικῆ, ἄμαχον, as Titus 3:2; ἀφιλάργυρον, the negative of "lovers of money" (2 Tim 3:2; "love of money", 6:10).
- **"ruleth" → "leading"** (v4, 5, 12) — Greek προΐστημι, "stand before"; the same verb in 5:17 and Titus 3:8, 14 ("take the lead").
- **"gravity" → "dignity"; "grave" → "dignified"** (v4, 8, 11) — Greek σεμνότης, σεμνός, as Titus 2:2, 7.
- **"not a novice" → "not newly planted"** (v6) — Greek νεόφυτον, "newly planted".
- **"lifted up with pride" → "puffed up"; "the condemnation of the devil" → "the sentence of the devil"** (v6) — Greek τυφωθείς, as 6:4; 2 Tim 3:4; κρίμα τοῦ διαβόλου, a plain genitive: the sentence passed on the devil, or one that the devil brings.
- **"a good report of them which are without" → "a good testimony from those outside"** (v7) — Greek μαρτυρίαν καλὴν … ἀπὸ τῶν ἔξωθεν.
- **"deacons" → "servants"** (v8, 12) — Greek διάκονοι, the common word for a servant, as 4:6; the verb "serve" (v10, 13) is its root.
- **"must be"** (v8, 11) — supplied: the Greek has only accusatives that depend on "must be" (δεῖ … εἶναι) in v2.
- **"doubletongued" → "double-worded"; "not given to much wine" → "not giving heed to much wine"** (v8) — Greek διλόγους, from λόγος "word"; προσέχοντας, as 1:4; 4:1, 13.
- **"not greedy of filthy lucre" → "not greedy for shameful gain"** (v8) — Greek αἰσχροκερδεῖς, as Titus 1:7.
- **"holding" → "having"** (v9) — Greek ἔχοντας.
- **"let them use the office of a deacon, being found blameless" → "let them serve, being blameless"** (v10) — Greek διακονείτωσαν ἀνέγκλητοι ὄντες.
- **"their wives" → "women"** (v11) — Greek γυναῖκας, without "their": wives of the servants, or women who serve.
- **"sober" → "temperate"** (v11) — Greek νηφαλίους, as v2.
- **"they that have used the office of a deacon well purchase to themselves a good degree" → "those who have served well acquire for themselves a good standing"** (v13) — Greek οἱ καλῶς διακονήσαντες; βαθμόν, "a step".
- **"great boldness in the faith" → "much boldness in faith"** (v13) — Greek πολλὴν παρρησίαν ἐν πίστει, no article.
- **"how thou oughtest to behave thyself" → "how one must conduct oneself"** (v15) — Greek πῶς δεῖ … ἀναστρέφεσθαι; the infinitive has no subject: "one", or "you".
- **"the pillar and ground" → "pillar and support"** (v15) — Greek στῦλος καὶ ἑδραίωμα, what makes firm; no article.
- **"without controversy" → "confessedly"** (v16) — Greek ὁμολογουμένως, "by common confession"; the root of "confess" (6:12).
- **"God was manifest in the flesh" → "Who was manifested in flesh"** (v16) — the critical text reads Ὅς, "who"; KJV follows the later reading θεός. The relative has no antecedent in the sentence.
- **"in the Spirit" → "in spirit"; "unto the Gentiles" → "among nations"; "received up into glory" → "taken up in glory"** (v16) — Greek ἐν πνεύματι, ἐν ἔθνεσιν, ἐν δόξῃ: none of the six lines has an article ("the world" is English idiom for ἐν κόσμῳ). "In spirit" stands against "in flesh"; it can also be read "in the Spirit".
- **"seen of angels" → "seen by angels"** (v16) — Greek ὤφθη ἀγγέλοις: "was seen by" or "appeared to".

### Notes
- **v16** — an early Christian hymn or confession.
- **v2–7** — one sentence in Greek as far as v6; v5 is a parenthesis.
