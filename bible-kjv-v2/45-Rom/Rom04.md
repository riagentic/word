# Romans 4

[1] What then shall we say that ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, our forefather according to the flesh, has found?

[2] For if ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ was justified from works, he has a boast, but not toward ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[3] For what does the Scripture say? "**And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ believed ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness**."

[4] Now to the one who works, the wage is not reckoned according to grace but according to debt;

[5] but to the one who does not work, but believes upon him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned for righteousness,

[6] just as David also speaks of the blessedness of the man to whom ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ reckons righteousness apart from works:

[7] "**Blessed are they whose acts of lawlessness were forgiven,
and whose sins were covered;
[8] blessed is the man whose sin ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧ will by no means reckon**."

[9] Is this blessedness then upon the circumcision, or also upon the uncircumcision? For we say, "Faith was reckoned to ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ for righteousness."

[10] How then was it reckoned? To him being in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision but in uncircumcision;

[11] and he received a sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith that was in the uncircumcision, that he might be father of all who believe through uncircumcision, that the righteousness might be reckoned to them,

[12] and father of circumcision to those who are not from circumcision only, but also to those who walk in line with the footsteps of the faith, in uncircumcision, of our father ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧.

[13] For not through ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ was the promise to ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ or to his seed, that he should be heir of the world, but through righteousness of faith;

[14] for if those from ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ are heirs, faith has been emptied and the promise has been abolished;

[15] for the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ works out wrath; but where there is no law, neither is there transgression.

[16] Because of this it is from faith, that it may be according to grace, so that the promise may be firm to all the seed, not to that which is from the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ only, but also to that which is from the faith of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ (who is father of us all,

[17] just as it is written, "**I have appointed you a father of many nations**"), before him whom he believed, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, who makes the dead alive and calls the things that are not as being;

[18] who against hope, upon hope, believed, that he might become father of many nations according to what had been spoken, "**So shall your seed be**";

[19] and not being weak in the faith, he observed his own body, already made dead, being about a hundred years old, and the deadness of Sarah's womb,

[20] but toward the promise of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ he did not doubt in unbelief, but was empowered in faith, having given glory to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧

[21] and being fully convinced that what he has promised he is powerful also to do.

[22] Therefore also it was reckoned to him for righteousness.

[23] But it was not written because of him only, that it was reckoned to him,

[24] but also because of us, to whom it is going to be reckoned, to those who believe upon him who raised ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ our Lord from the dead,

[25] who was delivered up because of our trespasses and was raised because of our justification.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v24) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"law" → "*Torah*"** (v13, 14, 15, 16) — Greek νόμος; "the" stands only where the Greek has the article (v15, 16). In v15 "where there is no law" is the common noun.
- **"What shall we say then that Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh, hath found?" → "What then shall we say that *Avraham*, our forefather according to the flesh, has found?"** (v1) — Greek εὑρηκέναι Ἀβραὰμ τὸν προπάτορα ἡμῶν κατὰ σάρκα (SBLGNT Treg NA28): "according to the flesh" stands with "forefather". WH omits "has found" ("What then shall we say of *Avraham* …"); RP has "father" and puts "has found" before "according to the flesh". The Greek has no "gained".
- **"if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God" → "if *Avraham* was justified from works, he has a boast, but not toward *Elohim*"** (v2) — Greek ἐξ ἔργων, "from", as Gal 2:16; καύχημα, "a boast"; πρὸς θεόν, "toward".
- **"Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness" → "And *Avraham* believed *Elohim*, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness"** (v3) — Greek Ἐπίστευσεν δὲ Ἀβραὰμ τῷ θεῷ καὶ ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ εἰς δικαιοσύνην, as Gal 3:6; Jas 2:23; the δέ, "and", is part of the words quoted.
- **"counted" / "imputed" / "reckoned" → "reckoned"** (v3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 22, 23, 24) — Greek λογίζομαι, one verb eleven times, the keeping of an account; the KJV has three words for it.
- **"the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt" → "the wage is not reckoned according to grace but according to debt"** (v4) — Greek ὁ μισθός, "the wage"; κατὰ χάριν … κατὰ ὀφείλημα; the Greek has no "his" and no "gift".
- **"believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly" → "believes upon him who justifies the ungodly"** (v5) — Greek πιστεύοντι ἐπί with the accusative, also v24; τὸν ἀσεβῆ, singular.
- **"describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works" → "speaks of the blessedness of the man to whom *Elohim* reckons righteousness apart from works"** (v6) — Greek λέγει τὸν μακαρισμόν, "speaks the blessedness", the pronouncing of someone blessed (also v9); χωρὶς ἔργων.
- **"Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered" → "Blessed are they whose acts of lawlessness were forgiven, and whose sins were covered"** (v7) — Greek ἀνομίαι, plural of "lawlessness" (Heb 10:17); ἀφέθησαν, ἐπεκαλύφθησαν, simple pasts.
- **"Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin" → "blessed is the man whose sin *Yahweh* will by no means reckon"** (v8) — Greek οὗ, "whose" (SBLGNT WH Treg NA28); ᾧ, "to whom" (RP), is the KJV reading. οὐ μή is the strong negation. The Greek has κύριος, "Lord", without the article, which stands for the divine name of Ps 32:2.
- **"Cometh this blessedness then upon the circumcision only, or upon the uncircumcision also? for we say that faith was reckoned" → "Is this blessedness then upon the circumcision, or also upon the uncircumcision? For we say, 'Faith was reckoned …'"** (v9) — the Greek question has no verb and no "only"; "the circumcision" and "the uncircumcision" are the abstract nouns for the people (Gal 2:7). No "that" after "we say" (SBLGNT WH Treg NA28; + ὅτι RP). ἡ πίστις has the article.
- **"when he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision?" → "To him being in circumcision, or in uncircumcision?"** (v10) — Greek ἐν περιτομῇ ὄντι ἢ ἐν ἀκροβυστίᾳ, a dative participle; the Greek has no "before" and no "after".
- **"he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had yet being uncircumcised" → "he received a sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith that was in the uncircumcision"** (v11) — Greek σημεῖον … περιτομῆς, without articles: the sign that circumcision is; τῆς ἐν τῇ ἀκροβυστίᾳ, "the one in the uncircumcision". The Greek does not start a new sentence and has no "The purpose was".
- **"all them that believe, though they be not circumcised; that righteousness might be imputed unto them also" → "all who believe through uncircumcision, that the righteousness might be reckoned to them"** (v11) — Greek διʼ ἀκροβυστίας, "through uncircumcision" (while in it); the Greek has no "though". The printed text has no "also" (SBLGNT WH Treg NIV); "to them also" (+ καί) is read by NA28 and RP.
- **"the father of circumcision to them who are not of the circumcision only, but who also walk in the steps of that faith of our father Abraham, which he had being yet uncircumcised" → "father of circumcision to those who are not from circumcision only, but also to those who walk in line with the footsteps of the faith, in uncircumcision, of our father *Avraham*"** (v12) — Greek πατέρα περιτομῆς, without articles; τοῖς … ἀλλὰ καὶ τοῖς στοιχοῦσιν: the article stands twice, as if two groups were named, though the sense asks for one ("those who are not only circumcised but also walk"); the Greek is translated as it stands. στοιχέω, "walk in line" (Gal 5:25); τῆς ἐν ἀκροβυστίᾳ πίστεως, "the in-uncircumcision faith".
- **"the promise, that he should be the heir of the world, was not to Abraham, or to his seed, through the law, but through the righteousness of faith" → "not through *Torah* was the promise to *Avraham* or to his seed, that he should be heir of the world, but through righteousness of faith"** (v13) — Greek διὰ νόμου, διὰ δικαιοσύνης πίστεως, without articles; ἤ, "or"; σπέρμα, "seed", as Gal 3:16, also v16, 18. The clause has no verb.
- **"if they which are of the law be heirs, faith is made void, and the promise made of none effect" → "if those from *Torah* are heirs, faith has been emptied and the promise has been abolished"** (v14) — Greek οἱ ἐκ νόμου, "those from law", as "those from faith" (Gal 3:7); the Greek has no "adherents". κεκένωται, "has been emptied" (Phil 2:7); κατήργηται, "has been abolished" (3:3, 31).
- **"the law worketh wrath: for where no law is, there is no transgression" → "the *Torah* works out wrath; but where there is no law, neither is there transgression"** (v15) — Greek κατεργάζεται; δέ, "but" (SBLGNT WH Treg NA28); γάρ, "for" (RP), is the KJV reading.
- **"Therefore it is of faith, that it might be by grace; to the end the promise might be sure to all the seed" → "Because of this it is from faith, that it may be according to grace, so that the promise may be firm to all the seed"** (v16) — Greek Διὰ τοῦτο ἐκ πίστεως, ἵνα κατὰ χάριν, two clauses without a verb: "it is" and "it may be" are supplied; the Greek has no "depends" and no "rest". βεβαίαν, "firm", not "guaranteed".
- **"not to that only which is of the law, but to that also which is of the faith of Abraham" → "not to that which is from the *Torah* only, but also to that which is from the faith of *Avraham*"** (v16) — Greek τῷ ἐκ τοῦ νόμου … τῷ ἐκ πίστεως Ἀβραάμ, said of the seed; πίστεως Ἀβραάμ is a plain genitive, as "faith of *Yeshua*" in 3:26.
- **"(As it is written, I have made thee a father of many nations,)" → "(who is father of us all, just as it is written, 'I have appointed you a father of many nations')"** (v16, 17) — the brackets are those of the printed text. Greek τέθεικα, "I have set, appointed"; in the Greek "a father of many nations" stands first.
- **"before him whom he believed, even God, who quickeneth the dead, and calleth those things which be not as though they were" → "before him whom he believed, *Elohim*, who makes the dead alive and calls the things that are not as being"** (v17) — Greek κατέναντι οὗ ἐπίστευσεν θεοῦ; ζῳοποιοῦντος; καλοῦντος τὰ μὴ ὄντα ὡς ὄντα: "calls the things not being as being"; the Greek has no "into existence".
- **"Who against hope believed in hope" → "who against hope, upon hope, believed"** (v18) — Greek παρʼ ἐλπίδα ἐπʼ ἐλπίδι; εἰς τὸ γενέσθαι αὐτόν, "that he might become" (purpose or result), or the thing believed.
- **"being not weak in faith, he considered not his own body now dead" → "not being weak in the faith, he observed his own body, already made dead"** (v19) — the critical text has no "not" before "observed" (SBLGNT WH Treg NA28; + οὐ RP). The verse follows + ἤδη, "already" (WH Treg NA28 RP), against the printed SBLGNT (Holmes, WH margin), which omits it. κατενόησεν, "observe" (Jas 1:23); νενεκρωμένον, "made dead" (Heb 11:12); the Greek has no "as good as".
- **"the deadness of Sarah's womb" kept** (v19) — Greek τὴν νέκρωσιν, the noun of "made dead"; the Greek has no second "considered" and no "barrenness".
- **"He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God" → "toward the promise of *Elohim* he did not doubt in unbelief, but was empowered in faith, having given glory to *Elohim*"** (v20) — Greek εἰς τὴν ἐπαγγελίαν; οὐ διεκρίθη, "doubt, be divided" (Jas 1:6); τῇ ἀπιστίᾳ, τῇ πίστει, datives: "in" or "by"; ἐνεδυναμώθη, a passive; δούς, a past participle.
- **"being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform" → "being fully convinced that what he has promised he is powerful also to do"** (v21) — Greek πληροφορηθείς; ἐπήγγελται, a perfect; δυνατός, "powerful"; the Greek does not repeat "God".
- **"And therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness" → "Therefore also it was reckoned to him for righteousness"** (v22) — the verse follows διὸ καί, "therefore also" (WH Treg NA28 RP), against the printed SBLGNT (Holmes, WH margin), which omits καί. The Greek has no "his faith".
- **"it was not written for his sake alone … But for us also" → "it was not written because of him only … but also because of us"** (v23, 24) — Greek διʼ αὐτόν, διʼ ἡμᾶς, the preposition of v25; the Greek has no "the words".
- **"to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him" → "to whom it is going to be reckoned, to those who believe upon him"** (v24) — Greek οἷς μέλλει λογίζεσθαι, τοῖς πιστεύουσιν ἐπί; the Greek has no "if" and does not start a new sentence.
- **"Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification" → "who was delivered up because of our trespasses and was raised because of our justification"** (v25) — Greek παρεδόθη, the verb of 1:24; διά with the accusative twice, "because of": the second can look back (because we were justified) or forward (for the sake of our justification); the Greek keeps one preposition. παραπτώματα, "trespasses"; δικαίωσις, "justification"; the Greek has no "again".

### Notes
- **v3** quotes Gen 15:6.
- **v7–8** quotes Ps 32:1–2.
- **v17** quotes Gen 17:5.
- **v18** quotes Gen 15:5.
- **v16–18** are one sentence in Greek; "who" in v18 is *Avraham*.
