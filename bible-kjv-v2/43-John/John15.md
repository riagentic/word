# John 15

[1] "**I am the true vine, and my Father is the farmer.**

[2] Every branch in me not bearing fruit, he takes it away; and every one bearing fruit, he cleanses it, so that it may bear more fruit.

[3] Already you are clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.

[4] Abide in me, and I in you. Just as the branch cannot bear fruit from itself unless it abides in the vine, so neither can you unless you abide in me.

[5] I am the vine, you the branches. The one who abides in me and I in him, this one bears much fruit, because **apart from me you can do nothing**.

[6] If anyone does not abide in me, he is cast out as the branch and is withered; and they gather them and cast them into the fire, and they are burned.

[7] If you abide in me and my words abide in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will come to pass for you.

[8] In this my Father is glorified, that you bear much fruit and become my disciples.

[9] "Just as the Father loved me, I also loved you; abide in my love.

[10] If you keep my commandments, you will abide in my love, just as I have kept my Father's commandments and abide in his love.

[11] These things I have spoken to you so that my joy may be in you and your joy may be made full.

[12] "**This is my commandment, that you love one another just as I loved you.**

**[13] Greater love than this no one has, that someone lay down his soul for his friends.**

[14] You are my friends if you do the things which I command you.

[15] No longer do I call you slaves, because the slave does not know what his lord does; but you I have called friends, because all things that I heard from my Father I made known to you.

[16] You did not choose me, but I chose you, and I set you, that you should go and bear fruit and your fruit should remain, so that whatever you ask the Father in my name he may give you.

[17] These things I command you, that you love one another.

[18] "If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before you.

[19] If you were of the world, the world would have affection for its own; but because you are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world, because of this the world hates you.

[20] Remember the word that I said to you, 'A slave is not greater than his lord.' If they persecuted me, they will persecute you also; if they kept my word, they will keep yours also.

[21] But all these things they will do to you on account of my name, because they do not know the one who sent me.

[22] If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have sin; but now they have no pretext concerning their sin.

[23] The one who hates me hates my Father also.

[24] If I had not done among them the works which no one else did, they would not have sin; but now they have both seen and hated both me and my Father.

[25] But — that the word may be fulfilled which is written in their ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧: '**They hated me without cause**.'

[26] "When the advocate comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the Spirit of truth, which proceeds from the Father, that one will testify concerning me;

[27] and you also testify, because you are with me from the beginning.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v1 implied, throughout) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** — no direct occurrence in this chapter (Father is used instead), but consistent policy.
- **"husbandman" → "farmer"** (v1) — Greek γεωργός, one who works the land (2 Tim 2:6; Jas 5:7); the word for a vinedresser is ἀμπελουργός (Luke 13:7).
- **"he taketh away … he purgeth it" → "he takes it away … he cleanses it"** (v2) — Greek αἴρει … καθαίρει, two verbs that sound alike; καθαίρω "cleanse" (of a vine: prune) is the root of καθαροί "clean" in v3. The Greek sets "every branch in me not bearing fruit" first and takes it up with "it".
- **"Now ye are clean through the word" → "Already you are clean because of the word"** (v3) — Greek ἤδη; διὰ τὸν λόγον, "on account of the word".
- **"of itself" → "from itself"** (v4) — Greek ἀφ' ἑαυτοῦ. "abides" / "abide" are presents (μένῃ WH, NA28; μένητε WH, Treg, NA28).
- **"the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me" → "this one bears much fruit, because apart from me"** (v5) — Greek οὗτος; φέρει, the verb "bear" of v2, 4, 8, 16; χωρὶς ἐμοῦ. "you the branches" has no verb in the Greek.
- **"he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered; and men gather them … and they are burned" → "he is cast out as the branch and is withered; and they gather them … and they are burned"** (v6) — Greek ἐβλήθη … ἐξηράνθη, two aorists ("was cast out … was withered"), said of what is as good as done; ὡς τὸ κλῆμα, "as the branch", with the article; συνάγουσιν … βάλλουσιν, "they gather … they cast", with no subject named; βάλλω "cast" twice. καίεται is singular with the neuter plural "them".
- **"ye shall ask what ye will, and it shall be done unto you" → "ask whatever you wish, and it will come to pass for you"** (v7) — Greek αἰτήσασθε, an imperative (WH, Treg, NA28; KJV follows the future αἰτήσεσθε, RP); γενήσεται. "my words" is ῥήματα.
- **"Herein is my Father glorified" → "In this my Father is glorified"** (v8) — Greek ἐδοξάσθη, an aorist ("was glorified"), as in 13:31.
- **"so shall ye be my disciples" → "and become my disciples"** (v8) — Greek γένησθε, a subjunctive under the same "that" (WH, Treg, NA28; KJV follows the future γενήσεσθε, RP); ἐμοὶ μαθηταί, "disciples to me".
- **"As the Father hath loved me, so have I loved you: continue ye" → "Just as the Father loved me, I also loved you; abide"** (v9) — Greek ἠγάπησεν … ἠγάπησα, aorists; μείνατε, the verb "abide".
- **"that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full" → "so that my joy may be in you and your joy may be made full"** (v11) — Greek ᾖ "be" (WH, Treg, NA28; RP μείνῃ "abide"); πληρωθῇ, as in 1 John 1:4.
- **"as I have loved you" → "just as I loved you"** (v12) — Greek ἠγάπησα, an aorist.
- **"that a man lay down his life" → "that someone lay down his soul"** (v13) — Greek τις; τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτοῦ θῇ, as in 10:11–18; 13:37–38.
- **"whatsoever I command you" → "the things which I command you"** (v14) — Greek ἅ, plural (Treg, NA28; WH ὅ, RP ὅσα); ἐγώ, an emphatic "I".
- **"servants … the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth" → "slaves … the slave does not know what his lord does"** (v15, 20) — Greek δοῦλος; κύριος "lord", as in 13:16, the word v20 quotes.
- **"all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known" → "all things that I heard from my Father I made known"** (v15) — Greek ἤκουσα, ἐγνώρισα, aorists; εἴρηκα "I have called" is a perfect.
- **"Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you" → "You did not choose me, but I chose you, and I set you"** (v16) — Greek aorists; ἔθηκα, from τίθημι "set, place", the verb of "lay down" in v13.
- **"that your fruit should remain": "remain" kept** (v16) — Greek μένῃ, the verb μένω of v4–10 ("abide" of persons), here of a thing: "remain".
- **"These things I command you, that ye love one another" kept** (v17) — Greek ἵνα: "that" can give the content of the command or its purpose ("so that").
- **"ye know that it hated me before it hated you" → "know that it has hated me before you"** (v18) — Greek γινώσκετε, a command or a statement ("you know"); μεμίσηκεν, a perfect; πρῶτον ὑμῶν, "first, before you".
- **"the world would love his own" → "the world would have affection for its own"** (v19) — Greek ἐφίλει, the verb φιλέω (root of φίλος "friend", v13–15), not ἀγαπάω "love" (v9, 12, 17); τὸ ἴδιον is neuter, "what is its own". "you" is not in the Greek.
- **"therefore the world hateth you" → "because of this the world hates you"** (v19) — Greek διὰ τοῦτο.
- **"if they have kept my saying" → "if they kept my word"** (v20) — Greek τὸν λόγον μου ἐτήρησαν, an aorist; λόγος "word" as at the start of the verse.
- **"for my name's sake" → "on account of my name"** (v21) — Greek διὰ τὸ ὄνομά μου; εἰς ὑμᾶς "to you", literally "unto you" (WH, Treg, NA28; RP ὑμῖν).
- **"they had not had sin: but now they have no cloke for their sin" → "they would not have sin; but now they have no pretext concerning their sin"** (v22, 24) — Greek ἁμαρτίαν οὐκ εἴχοσαν; "guilty" is not in the Greek. πρόφασις is a pretext, a reason put forward (1 Thess 2:5).
- **"now have they both seen and hated both me and my Father" kept** (v24) — Greek καὶ ἑωράκασιν καὶ μεμισήκασιν, two perfects; ἐποίησεν "did" (WH, Treg, NA28; RP πεποίηκεν).
- **"But this cometh to pass, that the word might be fulfilled" → "But — that the word may be fulfilled"** (v25) — Greek ἀλλ' ἵνα πληρωθῇ, a purpose clause without a main verb, as in 13:18; "this cometh to pass" and "must" are not in the Greek.
- **"without a cause" → "without cause"** (v25) — Greek δωρεάν, "as a gift, for nothing" (Gal 2:21). The words of Ps 35:19; 69:4 as printed in the Greek here.
- **"But when the Comforter is come" → "When the advocate comes"** (v26) — Greek παράκλητος, as in 14:16, 26; "But" is not in the printed text (WH, NA28; Treg and RP add δέ).
- **"whom I will send … which proceedeth from the Father, he shall testify of me" → "whom I will send … which proceeds from the Father, that one will testify concerning me"** (v26) — Greek ὅν, masculine, with ὁ παράκλητος; ὅ, neuter, with τὸ πνεῦμα; ἐκεῖνος "that one", masculine, taking up ὁ παράκλητος. The genders are those of the two Greek nouns (see 14:17). μαρτυρέω "testify".
- **"And ye also shall bear witness, because ye have been with me" → "and you also testify, because you are with me"** (v27) — Greek μαρτυρεῖτε, a present, a statement or a command; ἐστε, a present.
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.

### Notes
- **v25** quotes Ps 35:19; 69:4.
