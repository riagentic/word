# John 20

[1] Now on the first day of the week ⟦mary|Miryam|Mary⟧ the Magdalene comes early, while it is still dark, to the tomb, and sees the stone taken away out of the tomb.

[2] So she runs and comes to ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ and to the other disciple, for whom ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ had affection, and says to them, "They took away the Lord out of the tomb, and we do not know where they laid him."

[3] So ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ went out, and the other disciple, and they were going to the tomb.

[4] Now the two were running together; and the other disciple ran ahead, faster than ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, and came first to the tomb,

[5] and stooping to look, he sees the linen cloths lying; yet he did not enter.

[6] So ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ also comes, following him, and he entered into the tomb; and he beholds the linen cloths lying,

[7] and the kerchief, which was upon his head, not lying with the linen cloths, but apart, rolled up in one place.

[8] So then the other disciple also entered, the one who came first to the tomb, and he saw and believed;

[9] for they did not yet know the Scripture, that he must rise from the dead.

[10] So the disciples went away again to their homes.

[11] But ⟦mary|Miryam|Mary⟧ was standing at the tomb outside, weeping. So as she was weeping, she stooped to look into the tomb,

[12] and she beholds two angels in white sitting, one at the head and one at the feet, where the body of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ had been lying.

[13] And those say to her, "Woman, why are you weeping?" She says to them, "They took away my Lord, and I do not know where they laid him."

[14] Having said these things, she turned backward, and beholds ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ standing, and she did not know that it was ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[15] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to her, "Woman, why are you weeping? Whom do you seek?" That one, thinking that he was the gardener, says to him, "Lord, if you carried him off, tell me where you laid him, and I will take him away."

[16] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to her, "⟦mary|Miryam|Mary⟧." That one, turning, says to him in ⟦hebrew|Ivrit|Hebrew⟧, "**⟦rabbouni|Rabbouni|Rabboni⟧!**" (which is to say, Teacher).

[17] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to her, "Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father; but go to my brothers and say to them, 'I ascend to my Father and your Father, and my ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.'"

[18] ⟦mary|Miryam|Mary⟧ the Magdalene comes, announcing to the disciples, "I have seen the Lord," and that he said these things to her.

[19] So when it was evening on that day, the first of the week, and the doors having been shut where the disciples were, because of the fear of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ came and stood in the midst, and says to them, "Peace to you."

[20] And having said this, he showed his hands and his side to them. So the disciples rejoiced, seeing the Lord.

[21] So ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to them again, "Peace to you. Just as the Father has sent me, I also send you."

[22] And having said this, he breathed on them and says to them, "Receive ⟦ruach-haqodesh|Ruach HaQodesh|the Holy Spirit⟧.

[23] If you forgive the sins of any, they have been forgiven them; if you hold those of any, they have been held."

[24] But ⟦thomas|Toma|Thomas⟧, one of the twelve, the one called the Twin, was not with them when ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ came.

[25] So the other disciples were saying to him, "We have seen the Lord." But he said to them, "Unless I see in his hands the mark of the nails, and put my finger into the mark of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will by no means believe."

[26] And after eight days his disciples were again inside, and ⟦thomas|Toma|Thomas⟧ with them. ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ comes, the doors having been shut, and stood in the midst and said, "Peace to you."

[27] Then he says to ⟦thomas|Toma|Thomas⟧, "Bring your finger here and see my hands, and bring your hand and put it into my side, and do not become unbelieving, but believing."

[28] ⟦thomas|Toma|Thomas⟧ answered and said to him, "**My Lord and my ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧!**"

[29] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to him, "Because you have seen me, have you believed? Blessed are those who did not see and believed."

[30] So then, many other signs also ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ did before the disciples, which are not written in this book;

[31] but these are written that you may believe that ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ is the ⟦mashiach|Mashiach|Christ⟧, the Son of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and that believing you may have life in his name.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v17, 28) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v31) — Hebrew messianic title.
- **"Peter" / "*Kefa*"** (v2–6) — Aramaic name used consistently.
- **"Thomas" / "*Didymos*"** (v24) — Greek ὁ λεγόμενος Δίδυμος, "the one called *Didymos*", the Greek word for "twin", which is what the Aramaic name *Toma* means; translated, as in 11:16.
- **"The first day of the week"** (v1, 19) — Greek τῇ μιᾷ τῶν σαββάτων (v19 without the article), literally "on the one of the sabbaths": σάββατα is here "week", and "day" is supplied by the feminine μιᾷ.
- **"cometh" → "comes"** (v1; also "sees", "runs", "says", "beholds", v1, 2, 5, 6, 12–19, 22, 26, 27, 29) — the Greek present tense in narration is kept.
- **"Mary Magdalene" → "Miryam the Magdalene"** (v1, 18) — Greek ἡ Μαγδαληνή. In v1 and 11 the Greek has the form Μαρία, in v16 and 18 the Semitic form Μαριάμ (critical text; the Byzantine text has Μαρία throughout).
- **"seeth the stone taken away from the sepulchre" → "sees the stone taken away out of the tomb"** (v1) — Greek ἠρμένον ἐκ τοῦ μνημείου; αἴρω "take away" also in v2, 13, 15.
- **"whom Jesus loved" → "for whom Yeshua had affection"** (v2) — Greek ἐφίλει (φιλέω), not ἠγάπα (ἀγαπάω "love") as in 13:23; 19:26; 21:7, 20.
- **"They have taken away … they have laid" → "They took away … they laid"** (v2, 13) — Greek aorists.
- **"did outrun Peter" → "ran ahead, faster than Kefa"** (v4) — Greek προέδραμεν τάχιον τοῦ Πέτρου.
- **"seeth" (v5) · "seeth" (v6) · "saw" (v8)** — three Greek verbs: βλέπει "sees", θεωρεῖ "beholds" (also v12, 14), εἶδεν "saw".
- **"the napkin, that was about his head … wrapped together in a place by itself" → "the kerchief, which was upon his head … apart, rolled up in one place"** (v7) — Greek σουδάριον (Latin *sudarium*, a sweat cloth); ἐπὶ τῆς κεφαλῆς αὐτοῦ, "his head": the name is not in the Greek; χωρὶς ἐντετυλιγμένον εἰς ἕνα τόπον.
- **"they knew not the scripture"** (v9) — Greek ᾔδεισαν "knew", not "understood".
- **"went away again unto their own home" → "went away again to their homes"** (v10) — Greek πρὸς αὐτούς, "to themselves" (αὐτούς Tregelles, NA28; αὑτούς Westcott-Hort, SBLGNT; ἑαυτούς Byzantine: the same letters with a different breathing, the sense is the same). "homes" is supplied: English cannot say "to themselves".
- **"stood without at the sepulchre" → "was standing at the tomb outside"** (v11) — Greek εἱστήκει πρὸς τῷ μνημείῳ ἔξω.
- **"they say" → "those say"; "She" → "That one"** (v13, 15, 16) — Greek ἐκεῖνοι, ἐκείνη.
- **"turned herself back" → "turned backward"** (v14) — Greek ἐστράφη εἰς τὰ ὀπίσω.
- **"Sir, if thou have borne him hence" → "Lord, if you carried him off"** (v15) — Greek Κύριε, the word of v2, 13, 18, 20, 25, 28; ἐβάστασας, the verb "bear" of 19:17.
- **"saith unto him, Rabboni; which is to say, Master" → "says to him in Hebrew, 'Rabbouni' (which is to say, Teacher)"** (v16) — Greek Ἑβραϊστί "in Hebrew" (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28; lacking in the Byzantine text); Ραββουνι is kept as the Greek has it (Aramaic *rabbuni*, "my master, my teacher"), with the Greek explanation Διδάσκαλε, "Teacher".
- **"Touch me not" → "Do not touch me"** (v17) — Greek Μή μου ἅπτου, a present imperative of ἅπτομαι "touch, take hold of": "do not go on touching me", "do not hold on to me".
- **"to my Father" → "to the Father"** (v17) — "my" (Byzantine μου) is not in the critical text in the first clause.
- **"I ascend … and to my God, and your God" → "I ascend … and my Elohim and your Elohim"** (v17) — Greek ἀναβαίνω, present; one πρός governs all four nouns.
- **"came and told the disciples that she had seen the Lord" → "comes, announcing to the disciples, 'I have seen the Lord'"** (v18) — Greek ἀγγέλλουσα … ὅτι Ἑώρακα (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28; the Byzantine text has ἑώρακεν "that she had seen"); the sentence then passes into reported speech.
- **"when the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews" → "the doors having been shut where the disciples were, because of the fear of the Jews"** (v19, 26) — Greek κεκλεισμένων, "shut" (the word does not say "locked"); "assembled" (Byzantine συνηγμένοι) is not in the critical text; τὸν φόβον τῶν Ἰουδαίων as in 19:38.
- **"Peace be unto you" → "Peace to you"** (v19, 21, 26) — Greek Εἰρήνη ὑμῖν, without verb.
- **"he shewed unto them his hands and his side" → "he showed his hands and his side to them"** (v20) — NA28 and SBLGNT; Westcott-Hort and Tregelles add καί, "both his hands and his side".
- **"were glad" → "rejoiced"** (v20) — Greek ἐχάρησαν.
- **"as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you" → "Just as the Father has sent me, I also send you"** (v21) — two Greek verbs for "send": ἀπέσταλκεν (ἀποστέλλω, perfect) and πέμπω (present).
- **"he breathed on them"** (v22) — Greek ἐνεφύσησεν, "he breathed in / upon", without object; "them" is supplied because English needs it. It is the verb of Gen 2:7 in the Septuagint.
- **"Receive ye the Holy Ghost" → "Receive the *Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v22) — Greek Λάβετε πνεῦμα ἅγιον, without article ("Receive Holy Spirit"), as in 1:33; Hebrew רוּחַ הַקֹּדֶשׁ.
- **"Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them; and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained" → "If you forgive the sins of any, they have been forgiven them; if you hold those of any, they have been held"** (v23) — Greek ἀφέωνται (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28; Byzantine ἀφίενται "are forgiven") and κεκράτηνται, both perfect passives; κρατέω is "hold, hold fast" ("retain"); the second clause has no noun ("those" is supplied).
- **"Didymus" → "the Twin"** (v24) — see above.
- **"said" → "were saying"** (v25) — Greek ἔλεγον, an imperfect.
- **"the print of the nails" → "the mark of the nails"** (v25) — Greek τύπος, a mark left by a blow, an imprint (elsewhere "pattern"). "I will not believe" → "I will by no means believe": οὐ μὴ πιστεύσω.
- **"Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands; and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it" → "Bring your finger here and see my hands, and bring your hand and put it"** (v27) — Greek φέρε … ἴδε … φέρε … βάλε, the verb "put" of v25.
- **"be not faithless, but believing" → "do not become unbelieving, but believing"** (v27) — Greek μὴ γίνου ἄπιστος ἀλλὰ πιστός: γίνου is "become" (or "be"); ἄπιστος and πιστός are the adjectives rendered elsewhere "unbeliever" and "faithful"; here the pair is "without faith / having faith".
- **"And Thomas answered and said" → "Toma answered and said"** (v28) — "And" (Byzantine καί) is not in the critical text.
- **"My Lord and my God" → "My Lord and my *Elohim*"** (v28) — Greek Ὁ κύριός μου καὶ ὁ θεός μου, both nouns with the article, said to Yeshua (εἶπεν αὐτῷ).
- **"Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed" → "Because you have seen me, have you believed?"** (v29) — "Thomas" is not in the Greek. The Greek order is kept; the sentence is printed as a question in SBLGNT and can equally be a statement ("you have believed").
- **"blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed" → "Blessed are those who did not see and believed"** (v29) — Greek aorist participles, οἱ μὴ ἰδόντες καὶ πιστεύσαντες; "yet" is not in the Greek.
- **"in the presence of his disciples" → "before the disciples"** (v30) — Greek ἐνώπιον τῶν μαθητῶν (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, SBLGNT; NA28 and the Byzantine text add αὐτοῦ "his").
- **"that ye might believe"** (v31) — the verse follows πιστεύσητε, aorist (Tregelles, NA28, Byzantine); Westcott-Hort and SBLGNT print the present πιστεύητε. The aorist can mean "come to believe", the present "go on believing"; English "believe" covers both.
- **"that believing ye might have life through his name" → "in his name"** (v31) — Greek ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι αὐτοῦ.
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
