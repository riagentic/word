# John 21

[1] After these things ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ manifested himself again to the disciples at the Sea of ⟦tiberias|Tveryah|Tiberias⟧; and he manifested himself thus.

[2] There were together ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, and ⟦thomas|Toma|Thomas⟧ the one called the Twin, and ⟦nathanael|Netanel|Nathanael⟧ the one from ⟦cana|Qanah|Cana⟧ of ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧, and the sons of ⟦zebedee|Zebedaios|Zebedee⟧, and two others of his disciples.

[3] ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ says to them, "I am going to fish." They say to him, "We also are coming with you." They went out and got into the boat, and in that night they caught nothing.

[4] But when early morning had already come, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ stood on the shore; yet the disciples did not know that it was ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[5] So ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to them, "Young children, you have not anything to eat, have you?" They answered him, "No."

[6] And he said to them, "Cast the net to the right side of the boat, and you will find." So they cast, and they no longer had strength to draw it, from the multitude of the fish.

[7] So that disciple whom ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ loved says to ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, "It is the Lord." So ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, having heard that it was the Lord, girded on the outer garment, for he was naked, and cast himself into the sea;

[8] but the other disciples came in the little boat, for they were not far from the land, but about two hundred cubits off, dragging the net of the fish.

[9] So when they got out onto the land, they see a charcoal fire laid, and fish lying on it, and bread.

[10] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to them, "Bring some of the fish which you caught now."

[11] So ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ went up and drew the net to the land, full of great fish, a hundred and fifty-three; and though there were so many, the net was not torn.

[12] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to them, "Come, have breakfast." Now none of the disciples dared to inquire of him, "Who are you?" knowing that it was the Lord.

[13] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ comes and takes the bread and gives it to them, and the fish likewise.

[14] This was now the third time that ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ was manifested to the disciples, having been raised from the dead.

[15] So when they had breakfasted, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, "⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧, son of ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧, do you love me more than these?" He says to him, "Yes, Lord; you know that I have affection for you." He says to him, "Feed my lambs."

[16] He says to him again a second time, "⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧, son of ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧, do you love me?" He says to him, "Yes, Lord; you know that I have affection for you." He says to him, "Shepherd my sheep."

[17] He says to him the third time, "⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧, son of ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧, do you have affection for me?" ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ was grieved because he said to him the third time, "Do you have affection for me?" and he said to him, "Lord, you know all things; you realize that I have affection for you." ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to him, "Feed my sheep.

[18] Amen, amen, I say to you, when you were younger, you used to gird yourself and walk where you wanted; but when you grow old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will gird you and carry you where you do not want."

[19] Now this he said, signifying by what kind of death he would glorify ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧. And having said this, he says to him, "Follow me."

[20] ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, turning, sees the disciple whom ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ loved following, who also reclined at the supper upon his breast and said, "Lord, who is the one delivering you up?"

[21] So ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, seeing this one, says to ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, "Lord, but what of this one?"

[22] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to him, "If I want him to abide until I come, what is that to you? You follow me."

[23] So this word went out among the brothers, that that disciple does not die. But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ did not say to him that he does not die, but, "If I want him to abide until I come, what is that to you?"

[24] This is the disciple who testifies about these things and who wrote these things, and we know that his testimony is true.

[25] Now there are also many other things which ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ did, which, if they should be written one by one, I suppose not even the world itself would have room for the books being written.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v19) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Peter" / "*Kefa*"** (v2–21) — Aramaic name used consistently; v15-17 give "Simon son of John" per the Greek Ἰωάννου.
- **"Thomas" / "*Didymos*"** (v2) — Greek ὁ λεγόμενος Δίδυμος, translated as in 11:16; 20:24. The brackets are dropped.
- **"shewed himself" → "manifested himself"** (v1, 14) — Greek φανερόω; v14 has the passive, "was manifested".
- **"at the sea of Tiberias"** (v1) — Greek ἐπὶ τῆς θαλάσσης, "at" or "upon" the sea.
- **"Nathanael of Cana in Galilee, and the sons of Zebedee" → "Netanel the one from Qanah of Galilee, and the sons of Zebedaios"** (v2) — Greek οἱ τοῦ Ζεβεδαίου, "those of Zebedaios": "sons" is supplied, as in Mark 1:19.
- **"saith" → "says"** (v3, 5, 7, 10, 12, 13, 15–17, 19–22; also "see", v9; "comes … takes … gives", v13; "sees", v20) — the Greek present tense in narration is kept.
- **"I go a fishing … We also go with thee" → "I am going to fish … We also are coming with you"** (v3) — Greek Ὑπάγω ἁλιεύειν … Ἐρχόμεθα καὶ ἡμεῖς σὺν σοί. "immediately" (Byzantine εὐθύς) is not in the critical text.
- **"caught"** (v3, 10) — Greek πιάζω, the verb rendered "seize" where it is said of a person (7:30; 10:39).
- **"when the morning was now come"** (v4) — γενομένης (NA28, SBLGNT, Byzantine); Westcott-Hort and Tregelles read γινομένης, "was now coming".
- **"Children, have ye any meat?" → "Young children, you have not anything to eat, have you?"** (v5) — Greek Παιδία; the question with μή expects "no"; προσφάγιον is what is eaten with bread, usually fish.
- **"they were not able to draw it for the multitude of fishes" → "they no longer had strength to draw it, from the multitude of the fish"** (v6) — Greek οὐκέτι … ἴσχυον; ἑλκύω "draw", as v11 and 6:44; 12:32.
- **"he girt his fisher's coat unto him, (for he was naked,)" → "girded on the outer garment, for he was naked"** (v7) — Greek τὸν ἐπενδύτην διεζώσατο, the verb of 13:4; γυμνός is "naked", also said of one wearing only an undergarment.
- **"in a little ship … as it were two hundred cubits" → "in the little boat … about two hundred cubits off"** (v8) — Greek πλοιάριον (v3, 6 πλοῖον "boat"); πηχῶν διακοσίων, about ninety metres. "dragging": σύρω, a different verb from "draw".
- **"fish"** (v5–13) — three Greek words: προσφάγιον (v5), ἰχθύς (v6, 8, 11) and ὀψάριον (v9, 10, 13), fish as food eaten with bread, as in 6:9, 11. In v9 and 13 ὀψάριον and ἄρτος are singular: "a fish" and "a loaf", or fish and bread in general.
- **"a fire of coals" → "a charcoal fire"** (v9) — Greek ἀνθρακιά, as in 18:18.
- **"Bring of the fish" → "Bring some of the fish"** (v10) — Greek ἀπὸ τῶν ὀψαρίων, "from the fish".
- **"went up"** (v11) — Greek ἀνέβη: into the boat, or up onto the shore. "to land" → "to the land": εἰς τὴν γῆν (critical text; Byzantine ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς). "yet was not the net broken" → "the net was not torn": ἐσχίσθη, the verb of 19:24.
- **"Come and dine" → "Come, have breakfast"** (v12, 15) — Greek ἀριστάω, to take the morning meal. "durst ask him" → "dared to inquire of him": ἐξετάσαι, "examine, question closely".
- **"after that he was risen from the dead" → "having been raised from the dead"** (v14) — Greek ἐγερθείς, a passive of ἐγείρω "raise".
- **"Simon, son of Jonas" → "Simon, son of John"** (v15, 16, 17) — Greek Σίμων Ἰωάννου, "Shimon of Yochanan" ("son" is supplied, as in 1:42 where the Greek has it); Ἰωάννου is the reading of Westcott-Hort, Tregelles and NA28, the Byzantine text has Ἰωνᾶ "of Jonah" (cf. Matt 16:17 *Bar-Yonah*).
- **"lovest thou me … thou knowest that I love thee" → "do you love me … you know that I have affection for you"** (v15–17) — two Greek verbs: Yeshua asks ἀγαπᾷς με (ἀγαπάω "love") the first and the second time, and φιλεῖς με (φιλέω, "have affection for", the verb of φίλος "friend") the third time; Kefa answers φιλῶ σε all three times. Whether John means a difference of sense, or varies his words as he does elsewhere, is disputed; the translation keeps the two words apart and does not decide.
- **"more than these"** (v15) — Greek πλέον τούτων: "more than these do", "more than you love these", or "more than these things".
- **"Feed my lambs · Feed my sheep · Feed my sheep" → "Feed my lambs · Shepherd my sheep · Feed my sheep"** (v15, 16, 17) — Greek Βόσκε τὰ ἀρνία μου · Ποίμαινε τὰ πρόβατά μου · Βόσκε τὰ πρόβατά μου: βόσκω "feed, pasture", ποιμαίνω "shepherd, tend as a shepherd"; ἀρνία "lambs", πρόβατα "sheep" (v17: Westcott-Hort, NA28, SBLGNT, Byzantine; Tregelles reads προβάτια, "little sheep").
- **"thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" → "you know all things; you realize that I have affection for you"** (v17) — two Greek verbs: οἶδας "you know" (as in v15, 16) and γινώσκεις, "you know" by perceiving and coming to know. "he said to him" follows εἶπεν (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, SBLGNT, Byzantine); NA28 has λέγει "he says".
- **"Verily, verily" → "Amen, amen"** (v18) — Greek ἀμὴν ἀμήν, left untranslated as John leaves it.
- **"when thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself … another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not" → "when you were younger, you used to gird yourself … another will gird you and carry you where you do not want"** (v18) — Greek νεώτερος, a comparative; ζώννυμι "gird", not "dress"; οἴσει ὅπου οὐ θέλεις, with no "to go".
- **"signifying by what death he should glorify God" → "signifying by what kind of death he would glorify Elohim"** (v19) — Greek σημαίνων ποίῳ θανάτῳ, as 12:33; 18:32. The brackets are dropped: the Greek has none.
- **"which also leaned on his breast at supper … which is he that betrayeth thee" → "who also reclined at the supper upon his breast … who is the one delivering you up"** (v20) — Greek ἀνέπεσεν, "reclined" (literally "fell back"), as 13:25; ὁ παραδιδούς σε, the present participle of παραδίδωμι "deliver up". "following" has no object in the Greek.
- **"Lord, and what shall this man do?" → "Lord, but what of this one?"** (v21) — Greek οὗτος δὲ τί; literally "but this one — what?"; the Greek has no verb.
- **"If I will that he tarry till I come" → "If I want him to abide until I come"** (v22, 23) — Greek θέλω "want"; μένειν, the verb μένω "abide".
- **"Then went this saying abroad among the brethren, that that disciple should not die" → "So this word went out among the brothers, that that disciple does not die"** (v23) — Greek λόγος "word"; οὐκ ἀποθνῄσκει, a present tense, twice.
- **"which testifieth of these things, and wrote these things"** (v24) — Greek ὁ μαρτυρῶν, a present participle, and ὁ γράψας, an aorist; "true" is ἀληθής.
- **"I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written" → "I suppose not even the world itself would have room for the books being written"** (v25) — Greek χωρήσειν (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, SBLGNT; NA28 and the Byzantine text have χωρῆσαι, the same sense); τὰ γραφόμενα βιβλία, a present participle. "one by one": καθ' ἕν. The closing "Amen" (Byzantine) is not in the critical text.
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.

### Notes
- **v15-17** — Greek varies between *agapao* and *phileo* in *Yeshua's* and *Kefa's* words, though the interpretive weight of the shift is debated.
