# Proverbs 1

[1] The proverbs of ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧ son of David,
king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧:

[2] to know wisdom and discipline,
to understand the sayings of understanding,

[3] to take the discipline of acting with insight,
righteousness and judgment and uprightness;

[4] to give the simple shrewdness,
to a young man knowledge and devising;

[5] let the wise hear and add teaching,
and the understanding one get steering;

[6] to understand a proverb and a figure,
the words of the wise and their riddles.

[7] The fear of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ is the beginning of knowledge;
wisdom and discipline the senseless despise.

[8] Hear, my son, the discipline of your father,
and do not abandon the ⟦torah|torah|teaching⟧ of your mother,

[9] for they are a wreath of favor for your head
and necklaces for your neck.

[10] My son, if sinners entice you,
do not be willing.

[11] If they say, "Come with us,
let us lie in ambush for blood,
let us lie hidden for the innocent without cause;

[12] let us swallow them alive like Sheol,
and whole, like those who go down to the pit;

[13] all precious wealth we shall find,
we shall fill our houses with spoil;

[14] your lot you shall cast in our midst,
one purse there shall be for all of us" —

[15] my son, do not walk in the way with them;
hold back your foot from their track,

[16] for their feet run to evil,
and they hasten to shed blood.

[17] For in vain the net is spread out
in the eyes of every winged one,

[18] and they lie in ambush for their blood,
they lie hidden for their souls.

[19] So are the paths of everyone gaining unjust gain:
it takes the soul of its owners.

[20] Wisdoms cry aloud in the street;
in the squares she gives her voice.

[21] At the head of the noisy places she calls;
at the entrances of the gates, in the city, she says her sayings:

[22] "How long, simple ones, will you love simpleness,
and scoffers desire scoffing for themselves,
and fools hate knowledge?

[23] Turn back to my reproof:
behold, I will pour out my spirit to you,
I will make my words known to you.

[24] Because I have called and you refused,
I have stretched out my hand and there is none who gives heed,

[25] and you let go all my counsel
and would have none of my reproof,

[26] I too will laugh at your calamity;
I will mock when your dread comes,

[27] when your dread comes like devastation
and your calamity comes like a tempest,
when distress and anguish come upon you.

[28] Then they will call me, and I will not answer;
they will seek me eagerly, and they will not find me.

[29] Because they hated knowledge
and the fear of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ they did not choose,

[30] they would have none of my counsel,
they spurned all my reproof.

[31] And they shall eat from the fruit of their way,
and from their counsels they shall be sated.

[32] For the turning away of the simple will kill them,
and the ease of fools will do away with them.

[33] But the one who listens to me shall abide in security,
and be at ease from the dread of evil."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; v1 and v11 are now split where the accents divide them (v11 has three cola).
- **"son of David, king of Israel"** (v1) — Hebrew בֶן־דָּוִד מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל; מִשְׁלֵי "proverbs" is the plural of מָשָׁל (v6), elsewhere "parable" (a saying built on a likeness); of the short sayings of this book "proverb". v2–6 hang on this title: a row of infinitives ("to know … to understand … to take … to give"), with no main verb.
- **"instruction" → "discipline"** (v2, 3, 7, 8) — Hebrew מוּסָר, from יָסַר "discipline, correct" (WISDOM.md); one word in all four verses.
- **"to perceive the words of understanding" → "to understand the sayings of understanding"** (v2) — Hebrew לְהָבִין אִמְרֵי בִינָה: verb and noun of one root (בִּין); אֵמֶר "saying" (beside דָּבָר "word", v6, 23).
- **"To receive the instruction of wisdom, justice, and judgment, and equity" → "to take the discipline of acting with insight, righteousness and judgment and uprightness"** (v3) — Hebrew לָקַחַת "to take" (WORDS-HE: "receive" only with a note); הַשְׂכֵּל, the hifil infinitive of שָׂכַל, "acting with insight" (not חָכְמָה "wisdom"); צֶדֶק "righteousness", מִשְׁפָּט "judgment", מֵישָׁרִים "uprightness" (plural, from יָשָׁר). The three nouns stand beside "discipline" with no "in"; each has its "and".
- **"subtilty … the young man … discretion" → "shrewdness … a young man … devising"** (v4) — Hebrew עָרְמָה "shrewdness" (good here; the word of the "shrewd" serpent, Gen 3:1), מְזִמָּה "devising" (a purpose thought out; the same word is bad in 12:2), פְּתָאיִם "the simple" (WISDOM.md); לְנַעַר singular without the article: "a young man" (נַעַר "boy", of a youth).
- **"A wise man will hear, and will increase learning" → "let the wise hear and add teaching"** (v5) — Hebrew וְיוֹסֶף is the short (jussive) form, so the line is a wish: "let … hear and add"; יִשְׁמַע and יִקְנֶה can be either, and the second line follows the first. לֶקַח "teaching", literally "what is received" (the root of לָקַח, v3).
- **"a man of understanding shall attain unto wise counsels" → "the understanding one get steering"** (v5) — Hebrew נָבוֹן "understanding" (adjective; "man" is not written); קָנָה "get, acquire" (as 4:5, 7); תַּחְבֻּלוֹת "steering" (plural, from the sailor's word חֹבֵל "rope-puller"; "guidance" is the sense).
- **"a proverb, and the interpretation" → "a proverb and a figure"** (v6) — Hebrew מְלִיצָה, only here and Hab 2:6: a saying whose sense has to be drawn out (root of מֵלִיץ "interpreter"); the exact meaning is uncertain. חִידָה "riddle".
- **"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction" → "… wisdom and discipline the senseless despise"** (v7) — Hebrew יִרְאַת יְהוָה "the fear of Yahweh"; רֵאשִׁית "beginning", also "first part, chief part": both are defended. The second line has no "but" and puts the objects first, as kept. אֱוִילִים "senseless ones" (WISDOM.md: כְּסִיל is "fool", v22, 32); בָּזוּ "despise" (a perfect of what is always so).
- **"the law of thy mother" → "the torah of your mother"** (v8) — Hebrew תּוֹרַת אִמֶּךָ: the word as written keeps the `torah` token (WISDOM.md); here it is a parent's teaching ("teaching" in traditional mode). אַל־תִּטֹּשׁ "do not abandon" (נָטַשׁ; "forsake" is עָזַב, 2:13; 4:2).
- **"an ornament of grace … chains" → "a wreath of favor … necklaces"** (v9) — Hebrew לִוְיַת חֵן (לִוְיָה "wreath", also 4:9); חֵן "favor" (WORDS-HE: not "grace"); עֲנָקִים "necklaces"; גַּרְגְּרֹת "neck" is plural in Hebrew ("throat").
- **"consent thou not" → "do not be willing"** (v10) — Hebrew אַל־תֹּבֵא, from אָבָה "be willing" (written with an א); the same verb in v25, 30 ("would have none of").
- **"let us lay wait … let us lurk privily for the innocent without cause" → "let us lie in ambush … let us lie hidden for the innocent without cause"** (v11, 18) — Hebrew אָרַב "lie in ambush"; צָפַן "hide away" (Ps 31:19), here with no object and with לְ: "lie hidden for", lurk. חִנָּם "for nothing, without cause"; it can go with "innocent" (innocent for no reason) or with the ambush (for no gain). לְדָם "for blood". In v18 the same two verbs return against the men themselves.
- **"Let us swallow them up alive as the grave; and whole" → "let us swallow them alive like Sheol, and whole"** (v12) — Hebrew כִּשְׁאוֹל "like Sheol" (not "the grave"); תְמִימִים "whole" (the word is also "blameless": "and the blameless, like those who go down to the pit" is the other reading). בּוֹר "pit".
- **"We shall find all precious substance" → "all precious wealth we shall find"** (v13) — Hebrew הוֹן "wealth", object first; שָׁלָל "spoil".
- **"Cast in thy lot among us; let us all have one purse" → "your lot you shall cast in our midst, one purse there shall be for all of us"** (v14) — Hebrew תַּפִּיל, an imperfect ("you shall cast", literally "make fall"), not an imperative; בְּתוֹכֵנוּ "in our midst"; יִהְיֶה "there shall be". The speech of the sinners ends here (v15 is the father again).
- **"refrain thy foot from their path" → "hold back your foot from their track"** (v15) — Hebrew מָנַע "hold back"; נְתִיבָה "track" (beside אֹרַח "path", v19; דֶּרֶךְ "way").
- **"make haste" → "hasten"** (v16) — Hebrew מִהַר.
- **"in vain the net is spread in the sight of any bird" → "in vain the net is spread out in the eyes of every winged one"** (v17) — Hebrew מְזֹרָה, from זָרָה "strew, scatter": the net is spread out (or the bait is strewn on it; uncertain); חִנָּם "in vain" here (as v11, "without cause": English needs two words); בְּעֵינֵי "in the eyes of"; בַּעַל כָּנָף literally "owner of a wing" (בַּעַל + noun: one marked by the thing). The point of the verse (the bird sees the net and is still taken, or the men are less wise than a bird that sees it) is not stated.
- **"they lay wait for their own blood; they lurk privily for their own lives" → "they lie in ambush for their blood, they lie hidden for their souls"** (v18) — Hebrew לְדָמָם, לְנַפְשֹׁתָם: the suffixes are their own blood and souls (v19); "own" is not written. נֶפֶשׁ "soul"; וְהֵם "and they", not "these men".
- **"So are the ways of every one that is greedy of gain; which taketh away the life of the owners thereof" → "So are the paths of everyone gaining unjust gain: it takes the soul of its owners"** (v19) — Hebrew בֹּצֵעַ בָּצַע, verb and noun of one root: gain got by violence or wrong (WORDS-HE בֶּצַע); אָרְחוֹת "paths"; בְּעָלָיו "its owners" (of the gain); נֶפֶשׁ "soul".
- **"Wisdom crieth without; she uttereth her voice in the streets" → "Wisdoms cry aloud in the street; in the squares she gives her voice"** (v20) — Hebrew חָכְמוֹת, plural as printed (also 9:1; Ps 49:3), with the verb תָּרֹנָּה and "her voice" in the singular: a plural of fullness ("Wisdom in all its fullness"); "Wisdom" in the singular is the traditional rendering. רָנַן "give a ringing cry"; חוּץ "street", רְחֹבוֹת "squares" (open places; not "markets"); נָתַן קוֹל "give her voice".
- **"in the chief place of concourse" → "At the head of the noisy places"** (v21) — Hebrew בְּרֹאשׁ הֹמִיּוֹת, "at the head of the noisy (ones)": a feminine plural participle of הָמָה "roar, be in uproar" with no noun; "places" is supplied. קָרָא "call" (as v24, 28); פִּתְחֵי שְׁעָרִים "the entrances of the gates"; אֲמָרֶיהָ תֹאמֵר "she says her sayings" (noun and verb of one root, אֵמֶר as v2).
- **"How long, ye simple ones, will ye love simplicity? and the scorners delight in their scorning, and fools hate knowledge?" → "How long, simple ones, will you love simpleness, and scoffers desire scoffing for themselves, and fools hate knowledge?"** (v22) — No "O" (the Hebrew has none). פֶּתִי "simpleness" (the abstract beside פְּתָיִם "simple ones"); לֵצִים "scoffers", לָצוֹן "scoffing"; חָמְדוּ לָהֶם "desire for themselves" (חָמַד, elsewhere "covet"); כְּסִילִים "fools". The Hebrew turns from "you" to "they" in the second and third lines; kept. "How long" is written once.
- **"Turn you at my reproof" → "Turn back to my reproof"** (v23) — Hebrew תָּשׁוּבוּ לְתוֹכַחְתִּי, an imperfect with no "if": a call ("turn back") or a condition ("if you turn back"); the verse has no "if". שׁוּב "turn back", תּוֹכַחַת "reproof" (WISDOM.md).
- **"I will pour out my spirit unto you, I will make known my words unto you"** (v23) — Hebrew אַבִּיעָה, hifil of נָבַע "bubble, gush forth": "pour out" (another verb than שָׁפַךְ); רוּחַ "spirit" (also "breath"); both verbs are cohortative, "I will", a resolve.
- **"ye refused … no man regarded" → "you refused … there is none who gives heed"** (v24) — Hebrew מֵאֵן "refuse" (no "to listen"); וְאֵין מַקְשִׁיב "and there is none giving heed" (הִקְשִׁיב).
- **"ye have set at nought all my counsel, and would none of my reproof" → "you let go all my counsel and would have none of my reproof"** (v25) — Hebrew פָּרַע "let loose, let go" (= leave unheeded); עֵצָה "counsel"; לֹא אֲבִיתֶם "you were not willing" (אָבָה, as v10, 30).
- **"I also will laugh at your calamity; I will mock when your fear cometh" → "I too will laugh at your calamity; I will mock when your dread comes"** (v26) — Hebrew אֵיד "calamity", פַּחַד "dread" (as v27, 33).
- **"When your fear cometh as desolation, and your destruction cometh as a whirlwind" → "when your dread comes like devastation and your calamity comes like a tempest"** (v27) — Hebrew כְשׁוֹאָה qere ("like devastation"); the written form (ketiv) כשאוה is another spelling of the same word. אֵידְכֶם "your calamity" (as v26, not "destruction"); סוּפָה "tempest"; the verb of the second line is אָתָה, a poetic "come" (the first and third have בּוֹא; English has one word). צָרָה וְצוּקָה "distress and anguish".
- **"they shall seek me early" → "they will seek me eagerly"** (v28) — Hebrew שִׁחֵר, from שַׁחַר "dawn": seek eagerly ("early" is the old sense from the root). קָרָא "call" (no "upon"). Wisdom now speaks of "them", not to "you".
- **"For that they hated knowledge" → "Because they hated knowledge"** (v29) — Hebrew תַּחַת כִּי, literally "in place of the fact that". The second line puts the object first, as kept; בָּחַר "choose".
- **"They would none of my counsel: they despised all my reproof" → "they would have none of my counsel, they spurned all my reproof"** (v30) — Hebrew לֹא־אָבוּ לַעֲצָתִי (אָבָה); נִאֵץ "spurn" (not בָּזָה "despise", v7).
- **"Therefore shall they eat … and be filled with their own devices" → "And they shall eat from the fruit of their way, and from their counsels they shall be sated"** (v31) — Hebrew וְ "and" (not "therefore"); מִפְּרִי "from the fruit"; מֹעֲצוֹת "counsels", another noun of the root of עֵצָה ("my counsel", v25, 30: their counsels against hers); שָׂבַע "be sated".
- **"For the turning away of the simple shall slay them, and the prosperity of fools shall destroy them" → "… will kill them, and the ease of fools will do away with them"** (v32) — Hebrew מְשׁוּבָה "turning away" (from שׁוּב, the "turn back" of v23); הָרַג "kill"; שַׁלְוָה "ease", the quiet of one untroubled (KJV "prosperity"; here the fools' careless ease; v33 "be at ease" is another word, שַׁאֲנַן); אִבֵּד "do away with" (piel of אָבַד "perish").
- **"whoso hearkeneth unto me shall dwell safely, and shall be quiet from fear of evil" → "the one who listens to me shall abide in security, and be at ease from the dread of evil"** (v33) — Hebrew שֹׁמֵעַ לִי "listening to me"; שָׁכַן "abide"; בֶּטַח "in security"; שַׁאֲנַן "be at ease"; מִפַּחַד רָעָה "from the dread of evil".

### Notes
- **Title and purpose (v1–7)** — the book's own preface; v7 states its principle (as Ps 111:10; Job 28:28).
- **The father's first speech (v8–19)** and **Wisdom's first speech (v20–33)**: Wisdom is grammatically feminine ("she", her "I have called").
