# Proverbs 25

[1] These also are proverbs of ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧,
which the men of ⟦hezekiah|Chizqiyahu|Hezekiah⟧ king of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ copied out.

[2] The glory of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is to hide a matter,
and the glory of kings is to search out a matter.

[3] The heavens for height and the earth for depth,
and the heart of kings — there is no searching.

[4] Remove the dross from the silver,
and there comes out a vessel for the refiner;

[5] remove the wicked before the king,
and his throne shall be established in righteousness.

[6] Do not honor yourself before the king,
and in the place of the great do not stand,

[7] for better that it be said to you, "Come up here,"
than your being put low before a noble
whom your eyes have seen.

[8] Do not go out to contend hastily,
lest — what will you do at its end,
when your neighbor puts you to shame?

[9] Contend your case with your neighbor,
and the secret counsel of another do not uncover,

[10] lest the one who hears bring disgrace on you,
and your report not turn back.

[11] Apples of gold in settings of silver —
a word spoken fitly.

[12] A ring of gold and an ornament of gold —
a wise reprover upon a hearing ear.

[13] Like the cold of snow in the day of harvest
is a faithful envoy to those who send him,
and the soul of his masters he brings back.

[14] Clouds and wind, and rain there is none —
a man boasting of a gift of falsehood.

[15] By slowness of anger a captain is persuaded,
and a soft tongue breaks a bone.

[16] Honey you have found: eat what is enough for you,
lest you be sated with it and vomit it.

[17] Make your foot rare in the house of your neighbor,
lest he be sated with you and hate you.

[18] A club and a sword and a sharpened arrow —
a man who answers against his neighbor as a false witness.

[19] A broken tooth and a slipping foot —
trust in one who deals treacherously, in a day of distress.

[20] One who takes off a garment on a day of cold,
vinegar upon natron,
and one who sings songs upon a bad heart.

[21] If the one who hates you is hungry, make him eat bread,
and if he is thirsty, give him water to drink,

[22] for coals you are heaping upon his head,
and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ will repay you.

[23] The north wind brings forth rain,
and indignant faces, a tongue of secrecy.

[24] Better to dwell on a corner of a roof
than a woman of strifes and a shared house.

[25] Cold water upon a weary soul,
and a good report from a land far off.

[26] A trampled spring and a ruined fountain —
a righteous one tottering before a wicked one.

[27] Eating much honey is not good,
and searching out their glory is glory.

[28] A city broken through, without a wall —
a man who has no restraint for his spirit.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v22) — Tetragrammaton preserved; **"God" → "Elohim"** (v2) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים.
- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order. Many sayings set the image first and the thing meant second, with no "like … so" (v11, 12, 14, 18, 19, 20, 23, 25, 26, 28): kept, with a dash where English needs a pause; the existing layout put the thing meant first and added "like". v1 is now split at the main accent; v7 and v8 have three lines where the Hebrew has its strongest pause (ole-weyored) inside the verse.
- **"which the men of Hezekiah … copied out"** (v1) — Hebrew הֶעְתִּיקוּ, hifil of עָתַק "move on": "moved over, transcribed"; the heading as printed (no "scribes", no "compiled"). מִשְׁלֵי "proverbs", plural of מָשָׁל (as 1:1, 6).
- **"to conceal a thing … to search out a matter" → "to hide a matter … to search out a matter"** (v2) — Hebrew הַסְתֵּר דָּבָר … חֲקֹר דָּבָר: the same noun twice (דָּבָר "word, matter": here no speech is meant). "and", not "but" (וּ).
- **"the heart of kings is unsearchable" → "the heart of kings — there is no searching"** (v3) — Hebrew וְלֵב מְלָכִים אֵין חֵקֶר, literally "and the heart of kings: there is no searching"; חֵקֶר is the noun of חָקַר "search out" (v2, 27). No verb in the first line.
- **"Take away the dross … and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer" → "Remove the dross … and there comes out a vessel for the refiner"** (v4, 5) — Hebrew הָגוֹ, an infinitive absolute used as a command, the same word at the head of v4 and v5: "remove"; וַיֵּצֵא "and there comes out"; צֹרֵף "refiner" (צָרַף "refine"). v4–5 are one comparison; v5 לִפְנֵי "before" (not "from the presence of"); וְיִכּוֹן "and shall be established".
- **"Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king" → "Do not honor yourself before the king"** (v6) — Hebrew אַל־תִּתְהַדַּר, hitpael of הָדַר "adorn, honor" (WORDS-HE: "show deference to"): to give oneself honor, put oneself forward. The second line keeps its order and its וּ "and".
- **"For better it is that it be said unto thee … than that thou shouldest be put lower in the presence of the prince whom thine eyes have seen" → "for better that it be said to you … than your being put low before a noble whom your eyes have seen"** (v7) — Hebrew אֲמָר־לְךָ and הַשְׁפִּילְךָ are infinitives ("saying to you" / "your being put low"), with no subject; נָדִיב "noble" (not "prince": שַׂר). The last line, "whom your eyes have seen", ends v7 in the Hebrew accents and goes with "a noble"; it is not moved to v8 as the existing text had it (ESV, from memory). "Whom" or "what": אֲשֶׁר may be either; "what your eyes have seen" as the object of v8 is the other reading, named here.
- **"Go not forth hastily to strive, lest thou know not what to do in the end thereof" → "Do not go out to contend hastily, lest — what will you do at its end"** (v8) — Hebrew לָרִב "to contend" (רִיב, WORDS-HE "contend"; "into court" is not written); מַהֵר "hastily". פֶּן מַה־תַּעֲשֶׂה "lest — what will you do": the sentence breaks off after "lest", kept with a dash. בְּאַחֲרִיתָהּ "at its end" (the end of the contention). הִכְלִים "put to shame".
- **"Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself; and discover not a secret to another" → "Contend your case with your neighbor, and the secret counsel of another do not uncover"** (v9) — Hebrew רִיבְךָ רִיב, noun and verb of one root ("your case, contend"); סוֹד "secret counsel" (WORDS-HE, Job 29:4); סוֹד אַחֵר "the secret counsel of another" (a genitive; KJV "to another"). גִּלָּה "uncover". רֵעַ "neighbor" in v8, 9, 17, 18 (the one word; "friend" in 27).
- **"Lest he that heareth it put thee to shame, and thine infamy turn not away" → "lest the one who hears bring disgrace on you, and your report not turn back"** (v10) — Hebrew יְחַסֶּדְךָ, piel of חָסַד II "bring disgrace": the letters are those of *chesed* (WORDS-HE: another word; no token). דִּבָּה "report" (a report that defames; WORDS-HE); לֹא תָשׁוּב "not turn back" (= it will not go away).
- **"A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver" → "Apples of gold in settings of silver — a word spoken fitly"** (v11) — Hebrew order kept, no "like". עַל־אָפְנָיו is uncertain: perhaps "upon its turns / wheels" (אֹפֶן "wheel"), at the right time or in the right way; "fitly" (KJV) gives the sense without choosing. מַשְׂכִּיּוֹת "settings" (also "carvings, images": uncertain).
- **"As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold" → "A ring of gold and an ornament of gold"** (v12) — Hebrew נֶזֶם "ring" (WORDS-HE: the word says only "ring"); כֶּתֶם is another word for gold (Job 28:16, 19: "fine gold" in Changes). מוֹכִיחַ "reprover", participle of the verb of תּוֹכַחַת "reproof"; עַל "upon".
- **"As the cold of snow in the time of harvest … for he refresheth the soul of his masters" → "Like the cold of snow in the day of harvest … and the soul of his masters he brings back"** (v13) — Hebrew בְּיוֹם "in the day"; צִיר "envoy" (not מַלְאָךְ "messenger"; the same letters as צִיר "hinge", 26:14); נֶאֱמָן "faithful"; וְ "and" (not "for"); נֶפֶשׁ "soul"; יָשִׁיב "brings back" (= revives, refreshes). אֲדֹנָיו is plural, as לְשֹׁלְחָיו "those who send him".
- **"Whoso boasteth himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain" → "Clouds and wind, and rain there is none — a man boasting of a gift of falsehood"** (v14) — Hebrew נְשִׂיאִים "clouds" (literally "things lifted up": rising vapors; the word of נָשִׂיא "chieftain"); וְגֶשֶׁם אָיִן "and rain there is none"; מִתְהַלֵּל "boasting" (hitpael of הִלֵּל, as 27:1); מַתַּת־שָׁקֶר "a gift of falsehood" (שֶׁקֶר "falsehood": a gift promised and not given).
- **"By long forbearing is a prince persuaded" → "By slowness of anger a captain is persuaded"** (v15) — Hebrew בְּאֹרֶךְ אַפַּיִם, literally "by length of nostrils" (the אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם "slow to anger" row); קָצִין "captain" (one who decides; WORDS-HE, not "prince"); יְפֻתֶּה, pual of the verb of פִּתָּה "entice": "is persuaded"; לָשׁוֹן רַכָּה "a soft tongue" (רַךְ "tender", Gen 18:7).
- **"Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee, lest thou be filled therewith" → "Honey you have found: eat what is enough for you, lest you be sated with it"** (v16) — Hebrew has no question particle: "honey you have found"; דַּיֶּךָּ "your enough"; שָׂבַע "be sated" (as v17); הֵקִיא "vomit".
- **"Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour's house; lest he be weary of thee" → "Make your foot rare in the house of your neighbor, lest he be sated with you"** (v17) — Hebrew הֹקַר "make rare, make precious", with מִן "from" (literally "make your foot rare from the house"); יִשְׂבָּעֲךָ "be sated with you" (the verb of v16: the one word links the two sayings).
- **"A man that beareth false witness against his neighbour is a maul" → "A club … a man who answers against his neighbor as a false witness"** (v18) — Hebrew order kept; מֵפִיץ "club" (literally "scatterer", a shattering weapon); חֵץ שָׁנוּן "a sharpened arrow"; עֹנֶה בְ "answers against" (WORDS-HE: = testifies against; kept), עֵד שָׁקֶר "a false witness" (literally "a witness of falsehood").
- **"Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth, and a foot out of joint" → "A broken tooth and a slipping foot — trust in one who deals treacherously, in a day of distress"** (v19) — Hebrew רֹעָה "broken" (from רָעַע "shatter"; also read "bad"); מוּעֶדֶת "slipping, wavering"; מִבְטָח "trust, what one trusts in"; בּוֹגֵד "one who deals treacherously" (בָּגַד); בְּיוֹם צָרָה "in a day of distress".
- **"As he that taketh away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he that singeth songs to an heavy heart" → "One who takes off a garment on a day of cold, vinegar upon natron, and one who sings songs upon a bad heart"** (v20) — Hebrew order and three members kept, with no "like" (the existing text put the singer first). נֶתֶר "natron" (a soda; vinegar on it fizzes and spoils it); שָׁר בַּשִּׁרִים "sings with songs"; עַל "upon"; לֶב־רָע "a bad heart" (רַע of the heart = sad, as Neh 2:2 "badness of heart"; "evil" would say wicked). מַעֲדֶה "takes off" (the root is uncertain).
- **"If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat" → "If the one who hates you is hungry, make him eat bread"** (v21) — Hebrew שֹׂנַאֲךָ "the one who hates you" (participle of שָׂנֵא; not אֹיֵב "enemy"); הַאֲכִלֵהוּ "make him eat", הַשְׁקֵהוּ "give him drink".
- **"For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and the LORD shall reward thee" → "for coals you are heaping upon his head, and Yahweh will repay you"** (v22) — Hebrew גֶּחָלִים "coals" ("of fire" is not written); חֹתֶה, a participle ("you are heaping"), of חָתָה "snatch up, scoop" (coals from a hearth, Isa 30:14): "heap" is the sense; שִׁלֵּם "repay" (WORDS-HE). Rom 12:20 quotes v21–22 in Greek: ὁ ἐχθρός σου "your enemy", ἄνθρακας πυρὸς "coals of fire", σωρεύσεις "you will heap"; the Hebrew words are kept here as they stand.
- **"The north wind driveth away rain: so doth an angry countenance a backbiting tongue" → "The north wind brings forth rain, and indignant faces, a tongue of secrecy"** (v23) — Hebrew תְּחוֹלֵל, polel of חִיל "writhe, bring forth" ("drives away", KJV, is not the sense of the verb); פָּנִים נִזְעָמִים "faces made indignant" (plural); לְשׁוֹן סָתֶר "a tongue of secrecy" (one that speaks in secret). The second line has no verb: the secret tongue brings forth indignant faces, as the wind brings rain; the Hebrew order is kept.
- **"It is better to dwell in a corner of the housetop, than with a brawling woman and in a wide house" → "Better to dwell on a corner of a roof than a woman of strifes and a shared house"** (v24) — Hebrew עַל־פִּנַּת־גָּג "on a corner of a roof" (the flat roof); מֵאֵשֶׁת מִדְיָנִים "than a woman of strifes" (qere מִדְיָנִים; the written form מדונים is the plural of מָדוֹן "strife", the same word); וּבֵית חָבֶר "and a house of sharing" (חָבֶר "association": a house held in common; not "wide"). "with … in" is not written. The same saying stands at 21:9, with לָשֶׁבֶת "to dwell" and מִדְיָנִים as written there; the English is the same.
- **"so is good news from a far country" → "and a good report from a land far off"** (v25) — Hebrew וּשְׁמוּעָה טוֹבָה "and a good report" (שְׁמוּעָה, what is heard); no "so". עָיֵף "weary".
- **"A righteous man falling down before the wicked is as a troubled fountain, and a corrupt spring" → "A trampled spring and a ruined fountain — a righteous one tottering before a wicked one"** (v26) — Hebrew מַעְיָן "spring", מָקוֹר "fountain" (WORDS-HE); נִרְפָּשׂ "trampled, fouled by feet"; מָשְׁחָת hofal of שָׁחַת "ruined"; מָט "tottering" (מוֹט). Both words are singular; "man" is not written.
- **"so for men to search their own glory is not glory" → "and searching out their glory is glory"** (v27) — Hebrew וְחֵקֶר כְּבֹדָם כָּבוֹד, literally "and the searching of their glory is glory": there is no "not" in the second line (KJV carries the negative over from the first line; "it is not glorious to seek one's own glory", the existing wording, adds "not" and "own"). Whose glory "their" is (that of the things searched, v2) is not said; the meaning of the line is disputed.
- **"He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls" → "A city broken through, without a wall — a man who has no restraint for his spirit"** (v28) — Hebrew עִיר פְּרוּצָה אֵין חוֹמָה "a city broken through, there is no wall"; מַעְצָר "restraint" (only here; root עָצַר); רוּחַ "spirit".

### Notes
- **Second Solomonic collection (25–29)** — v1 is its heading. Many of its sayings set two things side by side without a word of comparison.
- **v21–22** — quoted in Rom 12:20 (see Changes).
