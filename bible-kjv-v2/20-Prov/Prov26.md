# Proverbs 26

[1] Like snow in summer and like rain in harvest,
so glory is not fitting for a fool.

[2] Like a bird for flitting, like a swallow for flying,
so a curse without cause — to him it comes.

[3] A whip for the horse, a bridle for the donkey,
and a rod for the back of fools.

[4] Do not answer a fool according to his folly,
lest you become like him, you also.

[5] Answer a fool according to his folly,
lest he be wise in his eyes.

[6] One who cuts off feet, who drinks violence —
one who sends words by the hand of a fool.

[7] The legs hang down from the lame,
and a proverb in the mouth of fools.

[8] Like binding a stone in a sling,
so is one who gives glory to a fool.

[9] A thorn goes up into the hand of a drunkard,
and a proverb in the mouth of fools.

[10] An archer wounding everyone —
and one who hires a fool, and one who hires those passing by.

[11] Like a dog returning to its vomit,
a fool repeating his folly.

[12] You have seen a man wise in his eyes:
there is more hope for a fool than for him.

[13] The sluggard says, "A fierce lion is in the way,
a lion between the squares!"

[14] The door turns on its hinge,
and the sluggard on his bed.

[15] The sluggard hides his hand in the dish;
he is weary to bring it back to his mouth.

[16] The sluggard is wiser in his eyes
than seven who answer with sense.

[17] One who takes hold of the ears of a dog —
one passing by who grows furious over a contention not his.

[18] Like a madman who shoots firebrands, arrows and death,

[19] so is a man who has deceived his neighbor
and said, "Am I not playing?"

[20] Where there is no more wood the fire goes out,
and where there is no murmurer strife grows quiet.

[21] Charcoal for coals and wood for fire,
and a man of strifes to kindle contention.

[22] The words of a murmurer are like delicacies,
and they go down into the chambers of the belly.

[23] Silver of dross overlaid on earthenware —
burning lips and an evil heart.

[24] With his lips one who hates disguises himself,
and within him he sets deceit.

[25] When he makes his voice pleasing, do not believe him,
for seven abominations are in his heart.

[26] Hatred may be covered by deception;
his evil will be uncovered in the assembly.

[27] He who digs a pit will fall into it,
and he who rolls a stone, to him it will return.

[28] A tongue of falsehood hates those it crushes,
and a smooth mouth makes a downfall.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; v18 has no main pause in the Hebrew and stays one line, running on into v19. Sayings that set two things side by side without "like" (v6, 7, 9, 10, 17, 23) are kept so; the existing layout added "like … is".
- **"honour is not seemly for a fool" → "glory is not fitting for a fool"** (v1, 8) — Hebrew כָּבוֹד "glory" (WORDS-HE: one word, also 25:2, 27; "honor" is its sense here); נָאוֶה "fitting". כַּמָּטָר "and like rain": both members have "like".
- **"As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come" → "Like a bird for flitting, like a swallow for flying, so a curse without cause — to him it comes"** (v2) — qere לוֹ "to him"; the written form (ketiv) is לֹא "not": "a curse without cause does not come (alight)", the reading of KJV and of the existing text. The qere stands in the verse (WISDOM.md); who "him" is (the one who curses, so that the curse returns on him, or the one cursed) is not said. צִפּוֹר "bird", דְּרוֹר "swallow" (the kind is uncertain); לָנוּד "for flitting" (נוּד "wander, flutter"); קִלְלַת חִנָּם "a curse of no cause" (קְלָלָה "curse"; חִנָּם "without cause").
- **"and a rod for the fool's back" → "and a rod for the back of fools"** (v3) — Hebrew לְגֵו כְּסִילִים, plural; שֵׁבֶט "rod".
- **"Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him" / "Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit"** (v4, 5) — two sayings set side by side as printed; neither is softened to agree with the other. אִוֶּלֶת "folly"; תִּשְׁוֶה־לּוֹ "become like him" (שָׁוָה "be equal"), גַם־אָתָּה "you also" at the end; בְּעֵינָיו "in his eyes" ("own" is not written; so v12, 16).
- **"He that sendeth a message by the hand of a fool cutteth off the feet, and drinketh damage" → "One who cuts off feet, who drinks violence — one who sends words by the hand of a fool"** (v6) — Hebrew order kept (two participles, then the one meant); רַגְלַיִם "feet" (no "his own"); חָמָס "violence" (WORDS-HE); דְּבָרִים "words".
- **"The legs of the lame are not equal: so is a parable in the mouth of fools" → "The legs hang down from the lame, and a proverb in the mouth of fools"** (v7, 9) — Hebrew דַּלְיוּ is uncertain: from דָּלַל "hang loose" or דָּלָה "draw up"; "hang down" is the best-supported sense. מִפִּסֵּחַ "from the lame" (פִּסֵּחַ, WORDS-HE). מָשָׁל "proverb" (the word of 25:1). Neither verse has "so is".
- **"As he that bindeth a stone in a sling" → "Like binding a stone in a sling"** (v8) — Hebrew כִּצְרוֹר, an infinitive "like binding"; מַרְגֵּמָה "sling" (only here; from רָגַם "stone"; "a heap of stones" is also proposed, with צְרוֹר "a bag": uncertain).
- **"As a thorn goeth up into the hand of a drunkard" → "A thorn goes up into the hand of a drunkard"** (v9) — no "like" in the Hebrew; עָלָה a perfect of what happens; שִׁכּוֹר "drunkard" (שָׁכַר "be drunk").
- **"The great God that formed all things both rewardeth the fool, and rewardeth transgressors" → "An archer wounding everyone — and one who hires a fool, and one who hires those passing by"** (v10) — The verse is one of the most obscure in the book. רַב "archer" (as Job 16:13; also "master, great one", KJV "the great God", where no word for God stands); מְחוֹלֵל־כֹּל "wounding (piercing) everyone" (חָלַל "pierce"; also "bringing forth all", from חִיל, as 25:23); שֹׂכֵר "one who hires", twice; עֹבְרִים "those passing by". The existing wording "a passing fool or drunkard" adds "drunkard" (not written) and makes one person of the two.
- **"As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly" → "Like a dog returning to its vomit, a fool repeating his folly"** (v11) — Hebrew שָׁב "returning" (שׁוּב), שׁוֹנֶה "repeating, doing again"; no "so". 2 Pet 2:22 cites the first line.
- **"Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit?" → "You have seen a man wise in his eyes:"** (v12) — Hebrew רָאִיתָ, a perfect with no question particle: "you have seen" (= when you see); the question form is the translators'. תִּקְוָה "hope"; מִמֶּנּוּ "than for him". The second line stands again at 29:20.
- **"There is a lion in the way; a lion is in the streets" → "A fierce lion is in the way, a lion between the squares!"** (v13) — Hebrew שַׁחַל "fierce lion" (a poetic word, kept apart from אֲרִי "lion"; WORDS-HE, Job 4:10); בֵּין הָרְחֹבוֹת "between the squares" (רְחוֹב "open place", as 1:20). עָצֵל "sluggard" (v13–16). Compare 22:13.
- **"As the door turneth upon his hinges" → "The door turns on its hinge"** (v14) — Hebrew צִירָהּ, singular; no "as … so".
- **"The slothful hideth his hand in his bosom; it grieveth him to bring it again to his mouth" → "The sluggard hides his hand in the dish; he is weary to bring it back to his mouth"** (v15) — Hebrew טָמַן "hide" (WORDS-HE); צַלַּחַת "dish" (not "bosom"); נִלְאָה "be weary" (WORDS-HE: with לְ + infinitive). Compare 19:24.
- **"than seven men that can render a reason" → "than seven who answer with sense"** (v16) — Hebrew מְשִׁיבֵי טָעַם, literally "who bring back sense (taste)": הֵשִׁיב of a reply, טַעַם "sense" (WORDS-HE, 1 Sam 25:33); "men" is not written.
- **"He that passeth by, and meddleth with strife belonging not to him, is like one that taketh a dog by the ears" → "One who takes hold of the ears of a dog — one passing by who grows furious over a contention not his"** (v17) — Hebrew order kept; the accents put עֹבֵר "passing by" with the second line (not "a passing dog"). מִתְעַבֵּר, hitpael of the root of עֶבְרָה "fury": "grows furious" (as 14:16; 20:2); "meddles" (KJV) takes the verb from עָבַר "pass" or from עָרַב "mix in". רִיב "contention" (WORDS-HE, v21).
- **"As a mad man who casteth firebrands, arrows, and death, so is the man that deceiveth his neighbour, and saith, Am not I in sport?"** (v18, 19) — Hebrew מִתְלַהְלֵהַּ "madman" (only here: uncertain); זִקִּים "firebrands"; רִמָּה "deceive" (WORDS-HE, Josh 9:22), a perfect: "has deceived … and said"; מְשַׂחֵק "playing" (שִׂחֵק "play"; here "joking"). "Only" is not written.
- **"Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth" → "Where there is no more wood the fire goes out, and where there is no murmurer strife grows quiet"** (v20) — Hebrew בְּאֶפֶס עֵצִים "with an end of wood" (אֶפֶס, WORDS-HE "be gone"); נִרְגָּן "murmurer" (WORDS-HE: one who whispers against others; the root of נֵרְגַּן "murmur", Deut 1:27; also v22; not רָכִיל "talebearer"); מָדוֹן "strife" (WORDS-HE); שָׁתַק "grow quiet".
- **"As coals are to burning coals … so is a contentious man to kindle strife" → "Charcoal for coals and wood for fire, and a man of strifes to kindle contention"** (v21) — Hebrew פֶּחָם "charcoal", גֶּחָלִים "coals"; qere מִדְיָנִים, the written form מדונים (both the plural of מָדוֹן "strife", as 25:24); לְחַרְחַר רִיב "to kindle contention". No "so".
- **"The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly" → "The words of a murmurer are like delicacies, and they go down into the chambers of the belly"** (v22) — Hebrew מִתְלַהֲמִים (only here and 18:8) is uncertain: "things swallowed greedily, delicacies" (from a root "swallow"); KJV "wounds" is a guess. חַדְרֵי־בָטֶן "the chambers of the belly" (WORDS-HE). The same saying stands at 18:8, in the same words.
- **"Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross" → "Silver of dross overlaid on earthenware — burning lips and an evil heart"** (v23) — Masoretic text restored: כֶּסֶף סִיגִים "silver of dross" (dross-silver, a cheap glaze of silver slag; סִיג "dross", 25:4). The existing text read "glaze" from a regrouping of the letters as one word (כְּסַפְסַג, after a Ugaritic word); that reading is a conjecture and is named here only. צִפָּה "overlay" (WORDS-HE), חֶרֶשׂ "earthenware"; דֹּלְקִים "burning" (KJV; "fervent", "smooth" in some versions after the Septuagint, from memory).
- **"He that hateth dissembleth with his lips, and layeth up deceit within him" → "With his lips one who hates disguises himself, and within him he sets deceit"** (v24) — qere בִּשְׂפָתָיו "with his lips"; the written form בשפתו "with his lip" (singular): the sense is the same. יִנָּכֵר "makes himself unknown, disguises himself" (root of נָכְרִי "foreigner"); שִׁית "set" (WORDS-HE); מִרְמָה "deceit".
- **"When he speaketh fair, believe him not" → "When he makes his voice pleasing, do not believe him"** (v25) — Hebrew יְחַנֵּן קוֹלוֹ, piel of חָנַן (the root of חֵן "favor"): "makes his voice pleasing, full of favor"; קוֹלוֹ "his voice". תּוֹעֵבָה "abomination".
- **"Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shewed before the whole congregation" → "Hatred may be covered by deception; his evil will be uncovered in the assembly"** (v26) — Hebrew מַשָּׁאוֹן "deception" (only here; from נָשָׁא "deceive"); תִּגָּלֶה "will be uncovered" (גָּלָה); רָעָתוֹ "his evil" (the suffix is masculine: the hater of v24, not the hatred, שִׂנְאָה being feminine); בְּקָהָל "in the assembly" ("whole" is not written).
- **"Whoso diggeth a pit shall fall therein: and he that rolleth a stone, it will return upon him" → "He who digs a pit will fall into it, and he who rolls a stone, to him it will return"** (v27) — Hebrew כֹּרֶה "digs" (כָּרָה, WORDS-HE: another verb than חָפַר); שַׁחַת "pit" (WORDS-HE: beside בּוֹר); אֵלָיו תָּשׁוּב "to him it will return" ("starts it rolling" is not written).
- **"A lying tongue hateth those that are afflicted by it; and a flattering mouth worketh ruin" → "A tongue of falsehood hates those it crushes, and a smooth mouth makes a downfall"** (v28) — Hebrew לְשׁוֹן־שֶׁקֶר "a tongue of falsehood"; דַּכָּיו "its crushed ones" (uncertain; "its victims"); פֶּה חָלָק "a smooth mouth"; מִדְחֶה "downfall" (only here; from דָּחָה "push down"); עָשָׂה "make".

### Notes
- **The fool (v1–12), the sluggard (v13–16), the meddler, deceiver and whisperer (v17–28)** — three groups of sayings.
- **v4–5** — the two sayings stand next to each other in the Hebrew; each is true in its place.
