# Proverbs 23

[1] When you sit to eat with a ruler,
you shall surely understand what is before you,

[2] and you shall put a knife to your throat
if you are a master of soul.

[3] Do not crave his savory foods,
and it is bread of lies.

[4] Do not labor to become rich;
from your understanding cease.

[5] Will you make your eyes fly upon it? And it is not;
for it will surely make itself wings,
like an eagle it will fly to the heavens.

[6] Do not eat the bread of one evil of eye,
and do not crave his savory foods,

[7] for as he reckons in his soul, so is he;
"Eat and drink," he says to you,
but his heart is not with you.

[8] Your morsel that you have eaten you will vomit up,
and you will ruin your pleasant words.

[9] In the ears of a fool do not speak,
for he will despise the insight of your utterances.

[10] Do not move back the ancient border,
and into the fields of the fatherless do not enter,

[11] for their redeemer is strong;
he will contend their case with you.

[12] Bring your heart to discipline,
and your ear to the sayings of knowledge.

[13] Do not hold back discipline from a boy;
when you strike him with the rod, he will not die.

[14] You — with the rod you will strike him,
and his soul from Sheol you will deliver.

[15] My son, if your heart is wise,
my heart will rejoice, I too.

[16] And my kidneys will be jubilant
when your lips speak uprightness.

[17] Let your heart not envy sinners,
but rather be in the fear of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ all the day;

[18] for surely there is a latter end,
and your hope will not be cut off.

[19] Hear, you, my son, and be wise,
and lead your heart straight in the way.

[20] Do not be among drunkards of wine,
among gluttons of flesh for themselves,

[21] for the drunkard and the glutton will come to poverty,
and drowsiness clothes with rags.

[22] Listen to your father, the one who begot you,
and do not despise your mother when she is old.

[23] Buy truth and do not sell,
wisdom and discipline and understanding.

[24] The father of a righteous one will surely be glad,
and one who begets a wise one will rejoice in him.

[25] Let your father and your mother rejoice,
and let her who bore you be glad.

[26] Give, my son, your heart to me,
and let your eyes guard my ways.

[27] For a deep pit is a prostitute,
and a narrow well is a foreign woman.

[28] Yes, she lies in ambush like a robber,
and she increases the treacherous among mankind.

[29] Who has woe? Who has alas?
Who has strifes? Who has complaint?
Who has wounds without cause?
Who has redness of eyes?

[30] Those who linger over the wine,
those who come to search out mixed wine.

[31] Do not look at wine when it shows itself red,
when it gives its eye in the cup;
it walks about in uprightness.

[32] Its latter end: like a serpent it bites,
and like a viper it stings.

[33] Your eyes will see strange things,
and your heart will speak perverse things.

[34] And you will be like one who lies down in the heart of the sea,
and like one who lies down on the top of a mast.

[35] "They struck me — I was not sick; they beat me — I did not know.
When shall I awake?
I will seek it yet again."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v17) — Hebrew יהוה *YHWH*.
- **Layout** (v35) — the first line now holds both "they struck me" and "they beat me": the accents divide the verse after them and after "When shall I awake?".
- **"consider diligently what is before thee" → "you shall surely understand what is before you"** (v1) — Hebrew בִּין תָּבִין, the infinitive absolute with its verb ("understanding you shall understand"): "surely" (WORDS-HE); an imperfect, not an imperative. אֶת־אֲשֶׁר "what" (or "who") is before you; לִלְחוֹם "to eat" (literally "to eat bread"); מוֹשֵׁל "ruler".
- **"And put a knife to thy throat, if thou be a man given to appetite" → "and you shall put a knife to your throat if you are a master of soul"** (v2) — Hebrew וְשַׂמְתָּ "and you shall put" (a perfect with "and", going on from v1); שַׂכִּין "knife" (only here); לֹעַ "throat"; בַּעַל נֶפֶשׁ "master of soul" (בַּעַל + noun, as 22:24; נֶפֶשׁ "soul" is also the throat and its appetite: one with a great appetite is meant; "man" is not written).
- **"Be not desirous of his dainties: for they are deceitful meat" → "Do not crave his savory foods, and it is bread of lies"** (v3) — Hebrew תִּתְאָו "crave" (הִתְאַוָּה, as 21:26); מַטְעַמּוֹת "savory foods" (as Gen 27); וְהוּא "and it" (singular: the food as one); לֶחֶם כְּזָבִים "bread of lies" (כָּזָב).
- **"Labour not to be rich: cease from thine own wisdom" → "Do not labor to become rich; from your understanding cease"** (v4) — Hebrew יָגַע "labor"; בִּינָה "understanding" (not "wisdom"; "own" not written); חֲדָל "cease".
- **"Wilt thou set thine eyes upon that which is not? for riches certainly make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle toward heaven" → "Will you make your eyes fly upon it? And it is not; for it will surely make itself wings, like an eagle it will fly to the heavens"** (v5) — Hebrew הֲתָעִיף "will you make fly" and יָעוּף "it will fly" are the qere; the written forms התעוף ("will your eyes fly") and ועיף ("and it flies", or "and weary") say the same in other forms. בּוֹ "upon it" (the riches of v4, not named); וְאֵינֶנּוּ "and it is not"; עָשֹׂה יַעֲשֶׂה "it will surely make" (infinitive absolute); נֶשֶׁר "eagle"; הַשָּׁמָיִם "the heavens", with no preposition.
- **"him that hath an evil eye … his dainty meats" → "one evil of eye … his savory foods"** (v6) — Hebrew רַע עָיִן "evil of eye" (= grudging; set against "good of eye", 22:9); תִּתְאָיו is the qere, the written form תתאו a spelling of the same word.
- **"For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he" → "for as he reckons in his soul, so is he"** (v7) — Hebrew שָׁעַר, only here: "reckon, calculate" (from a root known in Aramaic; uncertain); בְּנַפְשׁוֹ "in his soul" (not "heart"; לִבּוֹ "his heart" is the last line).
- **"The morsel which thou hast eaten shalt thou vomit up, and lose thy sweet words" → "Your morsel that you have eaten you will vomit up, and you will ruin your pleasant words"** (v8) — Hebrew פִּתְּךָ "your morsel" (singular); תְקִיאֶנָּה "you will vomit it up" (קִיא); וְשִׁחַתָּ "you will ruin" (שִׁחֵת); הַנְּעִימִים "pleasant" (נָעִים).
- **"Speak not in the ears of a fool: for he will despise the wisdom of thy words" → "In the ears of a fool do not speak, for he will despise the insight of your utterances"** (v9) — Hebrew בְּאָזְנֵי "in the ears of"; שֵׂכֶל "insight" (WISDOM.md); מִלִּים "utterances" (beside דָּבָר "word").
- **"Remove not the old landmark" → "Do not move back the ancient border"** (v10) — as 22:28 (הִסִּיג גְּבוּל עוֹלָם); יְתוֹמִים "the fatherless"; תָּבֹא "enter".
- **"For their redeemer is mighty; he shall plead their cause with thee" → "for their redeemer is strong; he will contend their case with you"** (v11) — Hebrew גֹּאֵל "redeemer" (the one with the right and duty to buy back and defend a kinsman, Lev 25; who he is the verse does not name); חָזָק "strong"; יָרִיב אֶת־רִיבָם as 22:23; אִתָּךְ "with you" (against you).
- **"Apply thine heart unto instruction, and thine ears to the words of knowledge" → "Bring your heart to discipline, and your ear to the sayings of knowledge"** (v12) — Hebrew הָבִיאָה "bring"; מוּסָר "discipline"; אָזְנֶךָ "your ear" (singular); אִמְרֵי "sayings".
- **"Withhold not correction from the child: for if thou beatest him with the rod" → "Do not hold back discipline from a boy; when you strike him with the rod"** (v13) — Hebrew מָנַע "hold back"; מוּסָר "discipline" (as v12); נַעַר "boy"; כִּי "when" (or "for": "for if" is not written twice); הִכָּה "strike".
- **"Thou shalt beat him with the rod, and shalt deliver his soul from hell" → "You — with the rod you will strike him, and his soul from Sheol you will deliver"** (v14) — Hebrew אַתָּה stands first ("you", emphatic); the objects stand before the verbs; שְׁאוֹל "Sheol" (not "hell"); הִצִּיל "deliver".
- **"my heart shall rejoice, even mine" → "my heart will rejoice, I too"** (v15) — Hebrew גַם־אָנִי "also I".
- **"Yea, my reins shall rejoice, when thy lips speak right things" → "And my kidneys will be jubilant when your lips speak uprightness"** (v16) — Hebrew וְ "and"; כִּלְיוֹת "kidneys" (the inner man; WORDS-HE); עָלַז "be jubilant" (beside שָׂמַח "rejoice", v15); מֵישָׁרִים "uprightness".
- **"but be thou in the fear of the LORD all the day long" → "but rather be in the fear of Yahweh all the day"** (v17) — Hebrew כִּי אִם "but rather" (after a negative); the second line has no verb ("be" is supplied; the line can also go on from "envy": "be zealous rather in the fear of Yahweh", קִנֵּא being also "be zealous"). יְקַנֵּא "envy" (קִנֵּא).
- **"For surely there is an end; and thine expectation shall not be cut off" → "for surely there is a latter end, and your hope will not be cut off"** (v18) — Hebrew כִּי אִם here "for surely" (or "for if there is a latter end", with the next line as the result); אַחֲרִית "latter end" (what comes after, as v32; 24:14, 20; not "end" alone); תִּקְוָה "hope"; תִכָּרֵת "be cut off".
- **"Hear thou, my son, and be wise, and guide thine heart in the way" → "Hear, you, my son, and be wise, and lead your heart straight in the way"** (v19) — Hebrew שְׁמַע־אַתָּה "hear, you" (emphatic); אַשֵּׁר piel "lead straight" (another sense of the root of אִשֵּׁר "call happy", Gen 30:13: named here).
- **"Be not among winebibbers; among riotous eaters of flesh" → "Do not be among drunkards of wine, among gluttons of flesh for themselves"** (v20) — Hebrew סֹבְאֵי־יַיִן "drunkards of wine", זֹלֲלֵי בָשָׂר "gluttons of flesh" (the pair of Deut 21:20); לָמוֹ "for themselves".
- **"For the drunkard and the glutton shall come to poverty: and drowsiness shall clothe a man with rags" → "for the drunkard and the glutton will come to poverty, and drowsiness clothes with rags"** (v21) — Hebrew יִוָּרֵשׁ, nifal of יָרַשׁ, literally "be dispossessed"; נוּמָה "drowsiness" (only here); קְרָעִים "rags" (torn pieces); "a man" / "them" is not written.
- **"Hearken unto thy father that begat thee" → "Listen to your father, the one who begot you"** (v22) — Hebrew שְׁמַע לְ "listen to"; זֶה יְלָדֶךָ "this one begot you"; כִּי־זָקְנָה "when she is old" (or "because").
- **"Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding" → "Buy truth and do not sell, wisdom and discipline and understanding"** (v23) — Hebrew קְנֵה "buy" (קָנָה); "it" and "also" are not written: the three nouns of the second line are objects of both verbs. בִּינָה "understanding".
- **"The father of the righteous shall greatly rejoice: and he that begetteth a wise child shall have joy of him" → "The father of a righteous one will surely be glad, and one who begets a wise one will rejoice in him"** (v24) — Hebrew גִּיל יָגִיל "will surely be glad" (infinitive absolute; גִּיל "be glad", שָׂמַח "rejoice"), וְיוֹלֵד and יִשְׂמַח are the qere; the written forms גול יגול יולד וישמח are other spellings with the same sense (וישמח "and he will rejoice"). "child" is not written: חָכָם "a wise one".
- **"Thy father and thy mother shall be glad, and she that bare thee shall rejoice" → "Let your father and your mother rejoice, and let her who bore you be glad"** (v25) — Hebrew יִשְׂמַח and וְתָגֵל are short (jussive) forms: wishes; שָׂמַח "rejoice", גִּיל "be glad" (as v24).
- **"My son, give me thine heart, and let thine eyes observe my ways" → "Give, my son, your heart to me, and let your eyes guard my ways"** (v26) — Hebrew תִּצֹּרְנָה "let them guard" (נָצַר) is the qere; the written form תרצנה reads "let them delight in" (רָצָה).
- **"For a whore is a deep ditch; and a strange woman is a narrow pit" → "For a deep pit is a prostitute, and a narrow well is a foreign woman"** (v27) — Hebrew שׁוּחָה "pit" (as 22:14); זוֹנָה "prostitute"; בְּאֵר "well"; נָכְרִיָּה "foreign woman" (WISDOM.md).
- **"She also lieth in wait as for a prey, and increaseth the transgressors among men" → "Yes, she lies in ambush like a robber, and she increases the treacherous among mankind"** (v28) — Hebrew אַף־הִיא "yes, she"; חֶתֶף, only here: "robber" (or "prey": uncertain); אָרַב "lie in ambush"; בּוֹגְדִים "the treacherous" (as 21:18); בְּאָדָם "among mankind".
- **"who hath sorrow? who hath contentions? who hath babbling?" → "Who has alas? Who has strifes? Who has complaint?"** (v29) — Hebrew אֲבוֹי, only here: a cry, "alas" (beside אוֹי "woe"); מִדְיָנִים "strifes" is the qere (the written form מדונים has the same sense, as 21:19); שִׂיחַ "complaint"; חִנָּם "without cause"; חַכְלִלוּת "redness" or "dullness" of eyes (only here; uncertain).
- **"They that tarry long at the wine; they that go to seek mixed wine" → "Those who linger over the wine, those who come to search out mixed wine"** (v30) — Hebrew לַמְאַחֲרִים "to those who linger" (the answer to "who has"); לַחְקֹר "to search out" (חָקַר, reviewer row: they try it out); מִמְסָךְ "mixed wine".
- **"when it giveth his colour in the cup, when it moveth itself aright" → "when it gives its eye in the cup; it walks about in uprightness"** (v31) — Hebrew יִתְאַדָּם "shows itself red"; עֵינוֹ "its eye" (its gleam: the word is "eye"); בַּכּוֹס "in the cup" is the qere, the written form בכיס reads "in the purse"; יִתְהַלֵּךְ בְּמֵישָׁרִים "it walks about in uprightness" (= it goes down smoothly: as Song 7:9; מֵישָׁרִים as v16). The third line has no "when".
- **"At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder" → "Its latter end: like a serpent it bites, and like a viper it stings"** (v32) — Hebrew אַחֲרִיתוֹ "its latter end" (as v18); נָחָשׁ "serpent"; צִפְעֹנִי "viper" (kind uncertain); יַפְרִשׁ "stings" (uncertain: "pierces", or "spreads out" its poison).
- **"Thine eyes shall behold strange women, and thine heart shall utter perverse things" → "Your eyes will see strange things, and your heart will speak perverse things"** (v33) — Hebrew זָרוֹת, feminine plural of זָר: "strange things", or "strange women" as 22:14; not decided. תַּהְפֻּכוֹת "perverse things" (from הָפַךְ "turn"); יְדַבֵּר "will speak".
- **"Yea, thou shalt be as he that lieth down in the midst of the sea, or as he that lieth upon the top of a mast" → "And you will be like one who lies down in the heart of the sea, and like one who lies down on the top of a mast"** (v34) — Hebrew בְּלֶב־יָם "in the heart of the sea"; וּכְ "and like" (not "or"); שֹׁכֵב twice; חִבֵּל, only here: "mast" or "rigging" (from חֹבֵל "sailor": uncertain).
- **"They have stricken me, shalt thou say, and I was not sick; they have beaten me, and I felt it not: when shall I awake? I will seek it yet again" → "They struck me — I was not sick; they beat me — I did not know. When shall I awake? I will seek it yet again"** (v35) — The words "you will say" are not written: the drinker's speech begins without them. בַל־חָלִיתִי "I was not sick" (חָלָה); הֲלָמוּנִי "they beat me" (הָלַם "hammer"); בַּל־יָדָעְתִּי "I did not know"; אוֹסִיף אֲבַקְשֶׁנּוּ עוֹד "I will add, I will seek it yet" (יָסַף + verb: "again", once per chapter literal here).
