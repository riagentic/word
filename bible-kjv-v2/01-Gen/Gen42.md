# Genesis 42

[1] When ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ saw that there was grain in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ said to his sons, "Why are you looking at one another?"

[2] And he said, "Behold, I have heard that there is grain in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧. Go down there and buy for us from there, that we may live and not die."

[3] So ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧'s ten brothers went down to buy grain from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[4] But ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧, ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧'s brother, ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ did not send with his brothers, for he said, "Lest some harm befall him."

[5] So the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ came to buy grain among those who came, for the famine was in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧.

[6] Now ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ was the governor over the land; it was he who sold grain to all the people of the land. And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧'s brothers came and bowed themselves before him with their faces to the ground.

[7] ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ saw his brothers and recognized them, but he acted as a stranger to them and spoke harshly to them. He said to them, "Where have you come from?" They said, "From the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧, to buy food."

[8] ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him.

[9] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ remembered the dreams that he had dreamed about them, and said to them, "You are spies; you have come to see the nakedness of the land."

[10] They said to him, "No, my lord; your servants have come to buy food.

[11] We are all sons of one man; we are honest men. Your servants are not spies."

[12] He said to them, "No, it is the nakedness of the land you have come to see."

[13] They said, "We, your servants, are twelve brothers, sons of one man in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧; and behold, the youngest is with our father today, and one is no more."

[14] ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ said to them, "It is as I said to you: 'You are spies.'

[15] By this you shall be tested: as ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ lives, you shall not go from here unless your youngest brother comes here.

[16] Send one of you, that he may bring your brother, while you are kept in prison, that your words may be tested, whether there is truth in you; and if not, as ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ lives, you are spies."

[17] And he put them all together in custody for three days.

[18] On the third day ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ said to them, "Do this, and live — for I fear ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[19] If you are honest men, let one of your brothers be kept in your prison house, and the rest of you go, carry grain for the famine of your households;

[20] and bring your youngest brother to me, that your words may be verified, and that you may not die." And they did so.

[21] They said to one another, "Truly we are guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the anguish of his soul when he pleaded with us and we would not listen. Therefore this distress has come upon us."

[22] ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ answered them, saying, "Did I not say to you, 'Do not sin against the boy,' and you would not listen? And now behold, there is a reckoning for his blood."

[23] They did not know that ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ understood them, for an interpreter was between them.

[24] He turned away from them and wept; then he returned to them and spoke with them, and took ⟦simeon|Shimon|Simeon⟧ from among them and bound him before their eyes.

[25] Then ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ commanded to fill their bags with grain, to return each man's silver into his sack, and to give them provisions for the way; and thus was done for them.

[26] They loaded their donkeys with the grain and went on their way from there.

[27] When one of them opened his sack to give his donkey fodder at the lodging place, he saw his silver; behold, it was at the mouth of his sack.

[28] He said to his brothers, "My silver has been returned! And here it is, in my sack." Their hearts sank, and they turned trembling to one another, saying, "What is this that ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has done to us?"

[29] When they came to ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ their father in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧, they told him all that had befallen them, saying,

[30] "The man, the lord of the land, spoke harshly to us and took us for spies of the country.

[31] We said to him, 'We are honest men; we are not spies.

[32] We are twelve brothers, sons of our father; one is no more, and the youngest is with our father today in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧.'

[33] Then the man, the lord of the land, said to us, 'By this I shall know that you are honest men: leave one of your brothers with me, and take food for the famine of your households, and go;

[34] and bring your youngest brother to me. Then I shall know that you are not spies but honest men. I will give your brother back to you, and you may trade in the land.'"

[35] As they emptied their sacks, behold, each man's bundle of silver was in his sack. When they and their father saw their bundles of silver, they were afraid.

[36] ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ their father said to them, "You have bereaved me of my children: ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ is no more, and ⟦simeon|Shimon|Simeon⟧ is no more, and you would take ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧! All these things are against me."

[38] But he said, "My son shall not go down with you, for his brother is dead, and he alone is left. If harm should come to him on the way that you go, you would bring my gray hairs down to Sheol in sorrow."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"corn" → "grain"** (v1, 2, 3, 5, 19, 25, 26) — see ch 41.
- **"mischief" → "harm"** (v4, 38) — Hebrew אָסוֹן *ason* = harm / misfortune / calamity. Not the mild "mischief" of modern English.
- **"the nakedness of the land" (v9, 12)** — Hebrew עֶרְוַת הָאָרֶץ *erwat ha-aretz*. Idiom: "exposed / undefended parts / weaknesses" — military-intelligence terminology. Spies look for the weak spots in border defenses.
- **"true men" → "honest men"** (v11, 19, 31, 33, 34) — Hebrew כֵּנִים *kenim* = upright / reliable / straight. Modernized.
- **"by the life of Pharaoh" → "as Pharaoh lives"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew חֵי פַרְעֹה *chey par'oh*. Oath formula. Joseph swears by Pharaoh's life, the normal Egyptian oath, consistent with his court identity.
- **"the child" → "the boy"** (v22) — Hebrew הַיֶּלֶד *ha-yeled*. Joseph was 17 at the sale; Reuben still calls him "the boy" in memory.
- **"also his blood is required" → "there is a reckoning for his blood"** (v22) — Hebrew גַּם־דָּמוֹ הִנֵּה נִדְרָשׁ. Blood-guilt language (same verbal noun *darash* as 9:5, 6). They now face the reckoning they thought they had escaped.
- **"their heart failed them" → "their hearts sank"** (v28) — Hebrew וַיֵּצֵא לִבָּם *wa-yetse libbam* = "their heart went out." Terror / dread.
- **"the grave" → "Sheol"** (v38) — as in 37:35.
- **"money" → "silver"** (v25, 27, 28, 35) — Hebrew כֶּסֶף *kesef*. Silver by weight (no minted coinage at this period). Modernized to "silver" rather than the anachronistic "money."

### Notes

- **Joseph's dreams fulfilled (v6, 9)** — his boyhood dreams of sheaves and stars bowing (37:7, 9) come true, though his brothers do not know it. Joseph alone perceives the fulfillment; he "remembered" the dreams.
- **The brothers' admission (v21)** — more than two decades later, they finally acknowledge guilt, and specifically the agony of Joseph's pleading as they sold him. This first honest confession (overheard by Joseph, though they thought the interpreter kept him from understanding, v23) is the turning point of Joseph's approach to them.
- **Joseph weeps** (v24) — the first of several tears from Joseph during the brothers' visits (42:24; 43:30; 45:2, 14–15; 46:29; 50:17). Joseph's emotion is not concealed, even as he plays the role of stern Egyptian lord.
