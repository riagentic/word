## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"God" → "El"** (v1, 3) — Hebrew אֵל *El* ("the El who appeared to you", "who answered me"), not *Elohim*.
- **"strange gods" → "foreign gods"** (v2, 4) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי הַנֵּכָר *elohey ha-nekhar*. *Nekhar* = foreignness. Likely including Rachel's *teraphim* (31:34–35) and items taken in the plunder of Shechem (34:28–29).
- **"be clean" → "purify yourselves"** (v2) — Hebrew וְהִטַּהֲרוּ *we-hittaharu*. Ritual purification before approaching the sacred altar.
- **"hid them" → "buried them"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיִּטְמֹן *wa-yitmon*. *Taman* = bury / conceal in the ground. A deliberate act of burial, not mere hiding — foreign gods and ear-ornaments (which may have been amulets) interred ritually.
- **"oak" (v4, 8)** — Hebrew אֵלָה / אַלּוֹן *elah / allon*. Terebinth / oak. Continuing the sacred-tree motif in Genesis (Mamre, Moreh, Allon-Bachuth).
- **"terror of God" → "terror of *Elohim*"** (v5) — Hebrew חִתַּת אֱלֹהִים *chittat Elohim* = divine-sent dread. Protection after the Shechem massacre.
- **"God Almighty" → "El Shaddai"** (v11) — as in 17:1.
- **"Ben-oni… Benjamin" (v18)** — Hebrew בֶּן־אוֹנִי *Ben-Oni* = "son of my sorrow / suffering"; בִּנְיָמִין *Binyamin* literally = "son of the right" — could mean "son of the south" (right-hand = south in orienting-East cosmic geography) or "son of my right hand" (= favored son). Jacob reverses the name's valence from grief to favor.
- **"travailed, and she had hard labour" → "gave birth, and her labor was hard"** (v16) — Hebrew וַתֵּלֶד רָחֵל וַתְּקַשׁ בְּלִדְתָּהּ. Modernized.
- **"Migdal-Eder" (v21)** — Hebrew מִגְדַּל־עֵדֶר *Migdal-Eder* = "tower of the flock." A shepherds' watchtower / lookout. Micah 4:8 associates it messianically.
- **v22 Reuben's sin** — the act has immense consequence: Reuben, the firstborn, forfeits his primacy (cf. 49:3–4, 1 Chr 5:1). The narrative reports the act tersely ("Israel heard of it") and moves on without judgment; the judgment comes in Jacob's final blessing. The MT has a large paragraph-break mark here, signaling a deliberate pause.
- **"gave up the ghost" → "breathed his last"** (v29) — as in 25:8.
- **"Arbah" → "Kiriath-Arba"** (v27) — same place-form consistent with ch 23.

### Notes

- **Bethel re-visit** — the chapter closes Jacob's circular journey: he left for Haran from Bethel with a vision (ch 28); he returns to Bethel with a family and a renewed name, fulfilling the vow of 28:22. The two Bethel theophanies form brackets around the entire Jacob-in-Haran cycle.
- **Deaths** — Deborah the nurse (v8), Rachel (v19), Isaac (v29). The chapter is a threshold of loss and transition. Isaac's death brings together the estranged brothers Esau and Jacob once more.
- **Twelve sons completed** — Benjamin's birth completes the tribal roster. The formal list (v23–26) serves as a structural marker: the patriarchal line is now established.
