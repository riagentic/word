# Genesis 35

[1] *Elohim* said to Jacob, "Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there; and make there an altar to the *Elohim* who appeared to you when you fled from the face of Esau your brother."

[2] So Jacob said to his household and to all who were with him, "Put away the foreign gods that are among you, and purify yourselves, and change your garments.

[3] Let us arise and go up to Bethel, and I will make there an altar to the *Elohim* who answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me on the way that I have gone."

[4] So they gave to Jacob all the foreign gods that were in their hands, and the rings that were in their ears; and Jacob buried them under the oak that was by Shechem.

[5] Then they journeyed. A terror of *Elohim* fell on the cities that were around them, and they did not pursue the sons of Jacob.

[6] So Jacob came to Luz (that is, Bethel), which is in the land of Canaan, he and all the people with him.

[7] He built an altar there and called the place El-Bethel, because there *Elohim* had revealed himself to him when he fled from the face of his brother.

[8] Then Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, died and was buried below Bethel under the oak; and the name of it was called Allon-bachuth.

[9] *Elohim* appeared to Jacob again, when he came from Paddan-Aram, and blessed him.

[10] And *Elohim* said to him, "Your name is Jacob; your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel shall be your name." So he named him Israel.

[11] And *Elohim* said to him, "I am Almighty *Elohim*. Be fruitful and multiply; a nation and a company of nations shall come from you, and kings shall come from your loins.

[12] The land that I gave to Abraham and Isaac, to you I will give it; and to your offspring after you I will give the land."

[13] Then *Elohim* went up from him in the place where he had spoken with him.

[14] So Jacob set up a pillar in the place where he had spoken with him — a pillar of stone — and he poured out a drink offering on it, and poured oil on it.

[15] And Jacob called the name of the place where *Elohim* had spoken with him, Bethel.

[16] Then they journeyed from Bethel; and while they were still some distance from Ephrath, Rachel gave birth, and her labor was hard.

[17] When her labor was hardest, the midwife said to her, "Do not be afraid, for you have another son."

[18] As her soul was departing (for she died), she called his name Ben-oni; but his father called him Benjamin.

[19] So Rachel died, and was buried on the way to Ephrath (that is, Bethlehem).

[20] Jacob set up a pillar over her grave; it is the pillar of Rachel's grave to this day.

[21] Israel journeyed on and pitched his tent beyond Migdal-eder.

[22] While Israel dwelt in that land, Reuben went and lay with Bilhah his father's concubine; and Israel heard of it.

Now the sons of Jacob were twelve.

[23] The sons of Leah: Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun.

[24] The sons of Rachel: Joseph and Benjamin.

[25] The sons of Bilhah, Rachel's slave-girl: Dan and Naphtali.

[26] The sons of Zilpah, Leah's slave-girl: Gad and Asher. These are the sons of Jacob, who were born to him in Paddan-Aram.

[27] Jacob came to his father Isaac at Mamre, at Kiriath-Arba (that is, Hebron), where Abraham and Isaac had sojourned.

[28] The days of Isaac were 180 years.

[29] Isaac breathed his last and died, and was gathered to his people, old and full of days; and Esau and Jacob his sons buried him.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"strange gods" → "foreign gods"** (v2, 4) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי הַנֵּכָר *elohey ha-nekhar*. *Nekhar* = foreignness. Likely including Rachel's *teraphim* (31:34–35) and items taken in the plunder of Shechem (34:28–29).
- **"be clean" → "purify yourselves"** (v2) — Hebrew וְהִטַּהֲרוּ *we-hittaharu*. Ritual purification before approaching the sacred altar.
- **"hid them" → "buried them"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיִּטְמֹן *wa-yitmon*. *Taman* = bury / conceal in the ground. A deliberate act of burial, not mere hiding — foreign gods and ear-ornaments (which may have been amulets) interred ritually.
- **"oak" (v4, 8)** — Hebrew אֵלָה / אַלּוֹן *elah / allon*. Terebinth / oak. Continuing the sacred-tree motif in Genesis (Mamre, Moreh, Allon-Bachuth).
- **"terror of God" → "terror of *Elohim*"** (v5) — Hebrew חִתַּת אֱלֹהִים *chittat Elohim* = divine-sent dread. Protection after the Shechem massacre.
- **"God Almighty" → "Almighty *Elohim*"** (v11) — as in 17:1.
- **"Ben-oni… Benjamin" (v18)** — Hebrew בֶּן־אוֹנִי *Ben-Oni* = "son of my sorrow / suffering"; בִּנְיָמִין *Binyamin* literally = "son of the right" — could mean "son of the south" (right-hand = south in orienting-East cosmic geography) or "son of my right hand" (= favored son). Jacob reverses the name's valence from grief to favor.
- **"travailed, and she had hard labour" → "gave birth, and her labor was hard"** (v16) — Hebrew וַתֵּלֶד רָחֵל וַתְּקַשׁ בְּלִדְתָּהּ. Modernized.
- **"Migdal-Eder" (v21)** — Hebrew מִגְדַּל־עֵדֶר *Migdal-Eder* = "tower of the flock." A shepherds' watchtower / lookout. Micah 4:8 associates it messianically.
- **v22 Reuben's sin** — the act has immense consequence: Reuben, the firstborn, forfeits his primacy (cf. 49:3–4, 1 Chr 5:1). The narrative reports the act tersely ("Israel heard of it") and moves on without judgment; the judgment comes in Jacob's final blessing. The MT has a large paragraph-break mark here, signaling a deliberate pause.
- **"gave up the ghost" → "breathed his last"** (v29) — as in 25:8.
- **"Arbah" → "Kiriath-Arba"** (v27) — same place-form consistent with ch 23.

### Notes

- **Bethel re-visit** — the chapter closes Jacob's circular journey: he left for Haran from Bethel with a vision (ch 28); he returns to Bethel with a family and a renewed name, fulfilling the vow of 28:22. The two Bethel theophanies form brackets around the entire Jacob-in-Haran cycle.
- **Deaths** — Deborah the nurse (v8), Rachel (v19), Isaac (v29). The chapter is a threshold of loss and transition. Isaac's death brings together the estranged brothers Esau and Jacob once more.
- **Twelve sons completed** — Benjamin's birth completes the tribal roster. The formal list (v23–26) serves as a structural marker: the patriarchal line is now established.
