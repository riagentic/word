## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And" kept at the head of the clauses** (throughout) — Hebrew וַ / וְ; "so", "then", "but" are not put in its place.
- **"God" → "El"** (v1, 3) — Hebrew אֵל *El* ("the El who appeared to you", "who answers me"), not *Elohim*. "Appeared" is נִרְאָה, "was seen".
- **"when thou fleddest from the face of"** (v1, 7) — Hebrew בְּבָרְחֲךָ מִפְּנֵי, literally so.
- **"household" → "house"** (v2) — Hebrew בֵּיתוֹ.
- **"strange gods" → "foreign gods"** (v2, 4) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי הַנֵּכָר, literally "the gods of foreignness". "In their hand" (v4) is singular, בְּיָדָם.
- **"be clean" → "cleanse yourselves"; "garments" → "cloaks"** (v2) — Hebrew וְהִטַּהֲרוּ, from טָהֵר "be clean"; שִׂמְלֹת, the outer wrap.
- **"who answered me … and was with me" → "who answers me … and he was with me"** (v3) — Hebrew הָעֹנֶה, a participle, "the one answering"; then וַיְהִי, a past.
- **"earrings" → "rings"** (v4) — Hebrew נְזָמִים, "rings"; "that were in their ears" follows.
- **"hid them"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיִּטְמֹן, "hid" (in the ground); "bury" is another verb, קָבַר (v8, 19, 29).
- **"oak" → "terebinth" (v4); "oak" (v8)** — two Hebrew words: אֵלָה (v4) and אַלּוֹן (v8). Both are large trees; which kinds is not certain.
- **"the terror of God" → "a dread of *Elohim*"** (v5) — Hebrew חִתַּת אֱלֹהִים, without the article. It may be a dread sent by *Elohim*, or "a mighty dread".
- **"(that is, Bethel)" → "— it is Bethel —"** (v6, 19, 27) — Hebrew הִוא, "it is", a remark of the text itself in the Hebrew word order.
- **"God appeared unto him" → "*Elohim* had been revealed to him"** (v7) — Hebrew נִגְלוּ אֵלָיו הָאֱלֹהִים. The verb נִגְלוּ is **plural**, "they were revealed", and אֱלֹהִים has the article. Both readings: "*Elohim* was revealed to him" (the one God, with a plural verb as in 20:13), or "the gods / the divine beings were revealed to him" (the angels of *Elohim* of 28:12). The English "had been revealed" leaves the number open; the token keeps *Elohim* as everywhere. The verb is "reveal", not "appear" (v1, 9).
- **"El-beth-el"** (v7) — Hebrew אֵל בֵּית־אֵל, "El of Beth-El", the name given to the place. Some versions have only "Bethel" (from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"Rebekah's nurse"** (v8) — Hebrew מֵינֶקֶת, "the one who suckled". "He called its name" is active, וַיִּקְרָא שְׁמוֹ, with no named subject; אַלּוֹן בָּכוּת is "oak of weeping".
- **"thy name shall not be called any more Jacob"** (v10) — Hebrew לֹא־יִקָּרֵא שִׁמְךָ עוֹד יַעֲקֹב; "And he called his name Israel", וַיִּקְרָא אֶת־שְׁמוֹ.
- **"God Almighty" → "El Shaddai"** (v11) — Hebrew אֵל שַׁדַּי, kept as the name (as in 17:1). The meaning of שַׁדַּי is not certain; "Almighty" comes from the old versions.
- **"a company of nations shall be of thee" → "an assembly of nations shall be from you"** (v11) — Hebrew קְהַל גּוֹיִם יִהְיֶה מִמֶּךָּ. "Come out of your loins": מֵחֲלָצֶיךָ יֵצֵאוּ.
- **"to thy seed"** (v12) — Hebrew זֶרַע.
- **"went up from him"** (v13) — Hebrew וַיַּעַל מֵעָלָיו, literally "went up from upon him".
- **"set up a pillar … a pillar of stone"** (v14, 20) — Hebrew וַיַּצֵּב … מַצֵּבָה, verb and noun of one root. "Poured out a drink offering": וַיַּסֵּךְ … נֶסֶךְ; "poured oil": וַיִּצֹק, another verb.
- **"the place where *Elohim* had spoken with him"** (v15) — the Hebrew adds שָׁם, "there" ("where … there"), which English does not repeat.
- **"there was but a little way to come to Ephrath" → "there was still a stretch of land to come to Ephrath"** (v16) — Hebrew כִּבְרַת־הָאָרֶץ, a measure of distance of unknown length (also 48:7; 2 Kgs 5:19); the meaning is uncertain.
- **"travailed, and she had hard labour" → "bore, and she had hard labor in her bearing"** (v16, 17) — Hebrew וַתֵּלֶד … וַתְּקַשׁ בְּלִדְתָּהּ, literally "she bore and it was hard for her in her bearing"; v17 בְהַקְשֹׁתָהּ בְּלִדְתָּהּ.
- **"thou shalt have this son also" → "this also is a son for you"** (v17) — Hebrew כִּי־גַם־זֶה לָךְ בֵּן.
- **"as her soul was in departing, (for she died)" → "as her soul was going out — for she died —"** (v18) — Hebrew בְּצֵאת נַפְשָׁהּ כִּי מֵתָה: נֶפֶשׁ "soul", יָצָא "go out".
- **"Ben-oni … Benjamin"** (v18) — Hebrew בֶּן־אוֹנִי, "son of my sorrow" (or "of my strength"); בִּנְיָמִין, "son of the right hand" (the right side is also the south).
- **"upon her grave" → "upon her burial place"** (v20) — Hebrew קְבֻרָה. "To this day" is עַד־הַיּוֹם, "to the day".
- **"spread his tent" → "pitched his tent"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיֵּט אָהֳלֹה, "stretched out". מִגְדַּל־עֵדֶר is "tower of the drove"; kept as the name.
- **"when Israel dwelt in that land" → "while Israel abode in that land"** (v22) — Hebrew בִּשְׁכֹּן, שָׁכַן "abide" (not יָשַׁב "dwell").
- **"and Israel heard it. Now the sons of Jacob were twelve" → "and Israel heard. And the sons of Jacob were twelve."** (v22) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל, "and Israel heard", with no object; then, in the same verse, וַיִּהְיוּ בְנֵי־יַעֲקֹב שְׁנֵים עָשָׂר, a clause that was missing from our verse and is restored. Between the two the Masoretic Text has a paragraph break in the middle of the verse. The Septuagint is commonly cited for an added clause after "heard" ("and it appeared evil before him", from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept. The act is judged in 49:3–4.
- **"Reuben, Jacob's firstborn" → "the firstborn of Jacob, Reuben"** (v23) — the Hebrew order; each name is joined by "and".
- **"handmaid" → "maidservant"** (v25, 26) — Hebrew שִׁפְחָה.
- **"which were born"** (v26) — Hebrew יֻלַּד, singular ("was born"), for the group.
- **"unto Mamre, unto the city of Arbah" → "to Mamre, to Kiriath-Arba"** (v27) — Hebrew מַמְרֵא קִרְיַת הָאַרְבַּע, "the city of the Arba (the four)", here with the article; the same place-name as in 23:2.
- **"an hundred and fourscore years" → "a hundred and eighty years"** (v28) — Hebrew מְאַת שָׁנָה וּשְׁמֹנִים שָׁנָה, "a hundred years and eighty years"; the number is given in ordinary English form.
- **"gave up the ghost" → "expired"** (v29) — Hebrew וַיִּגְוַע, beside וַיָּמָת "and died".
- **"gathered unto his people" → "gathered to his peoples"** (v29) — Hebrew אֶל־עַמָּיו, plural: his kinsfolk.

### Notes

- **Bethel re-visit** — the chapter closes Jacob's circular journey: he left for Haran from Bethel with a vision (ch 28); he returns to Bethel with a family and a renewed name, fulfilling the vow of 28:22. The two Bethel theophanies form brackets around the entire Jacob-in-Haran cycle.
- **Deaths** — Deborah the nurse (v8), Rachel (v19), Isaac (v29). Isaac's burial names Esau and Jacob together once more.
- **Twelve sons** — Benjamin's birth completes the twelve. The list (v22b–26) says "born to him in Paddan Aram", though Benjamin was born in the land of Canaan (v16–18).
