# Genesis 13

[1] So Abram went up from Egypt, he and his wife and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the Negev.

[2] Abram was very rich in livestock, in silver, and in gold.

[3] He journeyed on by stages from the Negev to Bethel, to the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Ai,

[4] to the place of the altar that he had made there at the first; and there Abram called on the name of Yahweh.

[5] Lot, who was going with Abram, also had flocks and herds and tents.

[6] The land could not support them living together, for their possessions were so great that they could not dwell together,

[7] and there was strife between the herders of Abram's livestock and the herders of Lot's livestock. At that time the Canaanites and the Perizzites were dwelling in the land.

[8] So Abram said to Lot, "Please let there be no strife between me and you, and between my herders and your herders, for we are brothers.

[9] Is not the whole land before you? Please separate yourself from me. If you take the left hand, I will go right; or if you take the right hand, I will go left."

[10] Lot lifted up his eyes and saw all the plain of the Jordan, that it was well watered everywhere — before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah — like the garden of Yahweh, like the land of Egypt, as you go toward Zoar.

[11] So Lot chose for himself all the plain of the Jordan, and Lot journeyed eastward; thus they separated from each other.

[12] Abram dwelt in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelt among the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent as far as Sodom.

[13] Now the men of Sodom were wicked and great sinners against Yahweh.

[14] Yahweh said to Abram, after Lot had separated from him, "Lift up your eyes now and look from the place where you are — northward, southward, eastward, and westward;

[15] for all the land that you see, to you I will give it, and to your offspring forever.

[16] I will make your offspring as the dust of the earth, so that if anyone could count the dust of the earth, your offspring could also be counted.

[17] Arise, walk through the land, through its length and its breadth, for to you I will give it."

[18] So Abram moved his tent, and came and settled by the oaks of Mamre, which are in Hebron; and there he built an altar to Yahweh.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"south" → "Negev"** (v1, 3) — as in 12:9.
- **"cattle" → "livestock"** (v2, 5, 7) — Hebrew מִקְנֶה *miqneh* = livestock / acquired herd-animals; broader than "cattle."
- **"went on his journeys" → "journeyed on by stages"** (v3) — Hebrew וַיֵּלֶךְ לְמַסָּעָיו *wa-yelekh le-massa'av* = "he went by his stages / stopping-places." The nomadic pattern of travel — moving, camping, moving again.
- **"Hai" → "Ai"** (v3) — as in 12:8.
- **"the land was not able to bear them" → "the land could not support them"** (v6) — Hebrew לֹא־נָשָׂא אֹתָם הָאָרֶץ *lo-nasa otam ha-aretz* = "the land did not carry / bear them." Economic capacity rather than physical burden; "support" modernizes.
- **"herdmen" → "herders"** (v7, 8) — modernized.
- **"we be brethren" → "we are brothers"** (v8) — modernized. (Note: Lot is Abram's nephew, not brother; *ach* in Hebrew covers extended kinship — "kinsman.")
- **"plain of Jordan" (v10, 11, 12)** — Hebrew כִּכַּר הַיַּרְדֵּן *kikkar ha-Yarden*. *Kikkar* = round / disc-shape — the "circle" / oval depression of the lower Jordan valley around Sodom. "Plain" is traditional.
- **"even as the garden of the LORD" → "like the garden of Yahweh"** (v10) — Hebrew כְּגַן־יְהוָה *ke-gan Yahweh*. "The garden of Yahweh" = Eden (Gen 2–3; cf. Isa 51:3). Lot sees the Jordan plain as paradise-like; the narrator ominously adds "before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah."
- **"before the LORD" → "against Yahweh"** (v13) — Hebrew לַיהוָה *la-Yahweh*. Preposition *le* here carries a sense of offense-against / before-the-face-of. Their sinfulness was before Yahweh's face.
- **"plain of Mamre" → "oaks of Mamre"** (v18) — Hebrew אֵלֹנֵי מַמְרֵא *eloney Mamre*. Same *elon* issue as 12:6. Plural here: oaks of Mamre. A sacred-grove site.
- **"dust of the earth" as uncountable multitude** (v16) — first of several "as many as" comparisons in Abraham's covenant (dust here; stars in 15:5; sand in 22:17). The *dust* image especially: humanity was formed from dust (2:7, 3:19); now the promise of innumerable offspring uses the same substance, turning the ground-bound creaturely origin into a source of proliferation.

### Notes

- **Abram's generosity** — he gives Lot first choice of land, though as the elder / covenanted one he could have claimed priority. The narrative contrast with Lot (who chooses by sight — "lifted up his eyes and saw") comes into sharper focus when Yahweh subsequently invites Abram to "lift up your eyes and look" (v14) — the same action, but now directed by covenant rather than appetite.
- **Sodom and Gomorrah foreshadowed** — the narrator's parenthetical ("before Yahweh destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah") looks ahead to ch 19. Lot, by choosing toward Sodom, has chosen his later trouble.
