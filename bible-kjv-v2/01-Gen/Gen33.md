# Genesis 33

[1] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ lifted up his eyes and saw, and behold, ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧ was coming, and with him four hundred men. And he divided the children to Leah and to Rachel and to the two maidservants.

[2] And he put the maidservants and their children first, and Leah and her children after, and Rachel and ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ last.

[3] And he himself passed over before them, and he bowed down to the earth seven times, until he came near to his brother.

[4] And ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧ ran to meet him and embraced him and fell on his neck and kissed him; and they wept.

[5] And he lifted up his eyes and saw the women and the children, and he said, "Who are these to you?" And he said, "The children with whom ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has favored your servant."

[6] And the maidservants came near, they and their children, and they bowed down.

[7] And Leah also came near, and her children, and they bowed down; and afterward ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ came near, and Rachel, and they bowed down.

[8] And he said, "What is all this camp to you, which I met?" And he said, "To find favor in the eyes of my lord."

[9] And ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧ said, "I have much, my brother; let what is yours be yours."

[10] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ said, "No, please; if, please, I have found favor in your eyes, then take my offering from my hand; for therefore I have seen your face as one sees the face of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and you were pleased with me.

[11] Take, please, my blessing that has been brought to you, because ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has favored me and because I have all." And he pressed him, and he took it.

[12] And he said, "Let us journey and go, and let me go before you."

[13] And he said to him, "My lord knows that the children are tender, and that the nursing flock and herd are upon me; and if they drive them hard one day, all the flock will die.

[14] Let my lord, please, pass over before his servant, and I will lead on gently, at the pace of the cattle that are before me and at the pace of the children, until I come to my lord, to Seir."

[15] And ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧ said, "Let me, please, set with you some of the people who are with me." And he said, "Why this? Let me find favor in the eyes of my lord."

[16] And ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧ returned on that day on his way to Seir.

[17] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ journeyed to ⟦succoth|Sukkot|Succoth⟧ and built himself a house, and for his livestock he made booths; therefore he called the name of the place ⟦succoth|Sukkot|Succoth⟧.

[18] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ came safe to the city of Shechem, which is in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧, when he came from ⟦paddan-aram|Paddan Aram|Paddan-Aram⟧; and he camped before the city.

[19] And he bought the portion of the field where he had pitched his tent, from the hand of the sons of ⟦hamor|Chamor|Hamor⟧, the father of Shechem, for a hundred ⟦qesitah|qesitahs|pieces of money⟧.

[20] And he set up there an altar and called it ⟦el-elohe-israel|El Elohei Yisrael|El-Elohe-Israel⟧.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And" kept at the head of the clauses** (throughout) — Hebrew וַ / וְ; "so", "but", "then" are not put in its place.
- **"looked" → "saw"** (v1, 5) — Hebrew וַיִּשָּׂא … עֵינָיו וַיַּרְא, "lifted up his eyes and saw".
- **"divided the children unto"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיַּחַץ … עַל, the verb of 32:7 ("he divided the people").
- **"handmaids" → "maidservants"** (v1, 2, 6) — Hebrew שְׁפָחוֹת.
- **"after … hindermost" → "after … last"** (v2) — Hebrew אַחֲרֹנִים both times, "behind, last".
- **"passed over before them"** (v3) — Hebrew עָבַר, the verb of 32:10, 16, 21–23, 31; so also v14.
- **"bowed himself to the ground" → "bowed down to the earth"** (v3, 6, 7) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ אַרְצָה; הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה is "bow down", to men and to God alike.
- **"kissed him"** (v4) — in the Masoretic Text the word וַיִּשָּׁקֵהוּ carries a dot over each letter (the printed text shows them); these scribal marks are old and their purpose is not known. The word is translated as it stands.
- **"Who are those with thee?" → "Who are these to you?"** (v5) — Hebrew מִי־אֵלֶּה לָּךְ.
- **"which God hath graciously given thy servant" → "with whom *Elohim* has favored your servant"** (v5, 11) — Hebrew חָנַן, the verb of חֵן "favor" (v8, 10, 15), with two objects; "given" is not in the Hebrew. "Your slave", "his slave" (v5, 14): עֶבֶד, as in 32:4, 18, 20.
- **"What meanest thou by all this drove" → "What is all this camp to you"** (v8) — Hebrew מִי לְךָ כָּל־הַמַּחֲנֶה הַזֶּה, literally "who to you is all this camp"; מַחֲנֶה is "camp", the word of 32:2, 7, 8, 10, 21, not "drove" (עֵדֶר).
- **"find grace in the sight of" → "find favor in the eyes of"** (v8, 10, 15) — Hebrew מָצָא חֵן בְּעֵינֵי.
- **"I have enough … keep that thou hast unto thyself" → "I have much … let what is yours be yours"** (v9) — Hebrew יֶשׁ־לִי רָב; יְהִי לְךָ אֲשֶׁר־לָךְ. Jacob answers (v11) יֶשׁ־לִי־כֹל, "I have all"; KJV has "enough" both times.
- **"receive my present" → "take my offering"; "Take … my blessing"** (v10, 11) — Hebrew לָקַח "take" both times, and again "he took it"; מִנְחָה "offering" (32:13, 18, 20, 21); בְּרָכָה "blessing" (v11), the word of ch. 27, where Jacob took Esau's blessing.
- **"for therefore I have seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God, and thou wast pleased with me"** (v10) — Hebrew כִּי עַל־כֵּן רָאִיתִי פָנֶיךָ כִּרְאֹת פְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים וַתִּרְצֵנִי, translated clause by clause; "truly", "to see … is like", "since" are not in the Hebrew. פְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים, "the face of *Elohim*", looks back to Peniel (32:30); the words can also be read "the face of a divine being". "You were pleased with me": רָצָה.
- **"urged" → "pressed"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיִּפְצַר־בּוֹ.
- **"And he said"** (v12, 13, 15) — the Hebrew does not name the speaker; "Esau" (v12) and "Jacob" (v13, 15) are not supplied.
- **"I will go before thee" → "let me go before you"** (v12) — Hebrew וְאֵלְכָה לְנֶגְדֶּךָ, a wish; לְנֶגֶד is "in front of, over against", which may also mean "alongside".
- **"the flocks and herds with young are with me" → "the nursing flock and herd are upon me"** (v13) — Hebrew עָלוֹת עָלָי: עָלוֹת, animals giving suck; עָלַי, "upon me" = in my care.
- **"if men should overdrive them" → "if they drive them hard"** (v13) — Hebrew וּדְפָקוּם, "and they knock (beat) them", third person plural with no named subject. "If I drive them" follows the Septuagint and the Samaritan text (from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"according as the cattle … be able to endure" → "at the pace of the cattle"** (v14) — Hebrew לְרֶגֶל הַמְּלָאכָה … וּלְרֶגֶל הַיְלָדִים, literally "at the foot of the work … and at the foot of the children". מְלָאכָה is "work", here what a man's work has brought him: his animals (not the word מִקְנֶה "livestock" of v17). "Lead on gently": אֶתְנָהֲלָה לְאִטִּי, "I will lead myself on at my gentle pace".
- **"leave with thee" → "set with you"** (v15) — Hebrew אַצִּיגָה, "let me place".
- **"What needeth it?" → "Why this?"** (v15) — Hebrew לָמָּה זֶּה. אֶמְצָא־חֵן is "let me find / I shall find favor".
- **"Succoth"; "booths"** (v17) — Hebrew סֻכֹּת *sukkot*, "booths", shelters of branches; the place has its name from them. "He called the name": קָרָא שֵׁם, active.
- **"Shalem, a city of Shechem" → "safe to the city of Shechem"** (v18) — Hebrew שָׁלֵם עִיר שְׁכֶם. שָׁלֵם is either the adjective "whole, safe" ("Jacob came safe to the city of Shechem") or a place name ("to Salem, a city of Shechem", KJV; so also the Septuagint, from memory). The adjective is followed (see 28:21, "and I return in peace to my father's house"); the other reading is possible. The word is not *shalom* itself but its adjective.
- **"pitched his tent before the city" → "camped before the city"** (v18) — Hebrew וַיִּחַן, the verb of מַחֲנֶה "camp"; אֶת־פְּנֵי הָעִיר, literally "at the face of the city".
- **"a parcel of a field" → "the portion of the field"; "at the hand of" → "from the hand of"** (v19) — Hebrew חֶלְקַת הַשָּׂדֶה; מִיַּד. "Pitched": נָטָה, literally "stretched out".
- **"pieces of money" → "qesitahs"** (v19) — Hebrew קְשִׂיטָה *qesitah*, a weight of silver of unknown value (also Josh 24:32; Job 42:11). The word is kept.
- **"erected there an altar" → "set up there an altar"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיַּצֶּב, the verb used of setting up a pillar (35:14, 20), only here of an altar.
- **"El-elohe-Israel"** (v20) — Hebrew אֵל אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, the name Jacob gives the altar: "El, the *Elohim* of Israel", or as a sentence "El is the *Elohim* of Israel". "Israel" is here Jacob himself (32:28). The three Hebrew words are kept as the name.

### Notes

- **The reconciliation** — Esau's response (run, embrace, fall on neck, kiss, weep) is warmly unexpected after ch 32's dread. Jacob had feared armed conflict and gone to great lengths; Esau comes to meet him as a brother.
- **Seir and Succoth (v14–17)** — Jacob says "until I come to my lord, to Seir", and then journeys to Succoth instead. The text does not say whether he meant to deceive or changed his plan; the brothers are next named together at their father's burial (35:29).
