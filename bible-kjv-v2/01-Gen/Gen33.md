# Genesis 33

[1] Jacob lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, Esau was coming, and with him 400 men. So he divided the children between Leah, Rachel, and the two slave-girls.

[2] He put the slave-girls and their children in front, Leah and her children after them, and Rachel and Joseph last.

[3] He himself passed on before them and bowed to the ground seven times until he drew near to his brother.

[4] But Esau ran to meet him, and embraced him, and fell on his neck and kissed him; and they wept.

[5] Then he lifted up his eyes and saw the women and the children, and said, "Who are these with you?" He said, "The children whom *Elohim* has graciously given your servant."

[6] Then the slave-girls drew near, they and their children, and they bowed themselves.

[7] Leah too with her children drew near and bowed; and after them Joseph and Rachel drew near and bowed.

[8] He said, "What do you mean by all this camp that I met?" He said, "To find favor in the sight of my lord."

[9] But Esau said, "I have plenty, my brother; keep what you have for yourself."

[10] Jacob said, "No, please, if I have found favor in your sight, then accept my present from my hand — because truly to see your face is like seeing the face of *Elohim*, since you have received me favorably.

[11] Please accept my blessing-gift which was brought to you, because *Elohim* has dealt graciously with me and because I have all I need." So he urged him, and he took it.

[12] Then Esau said, "Let us start our journey, and let us go; and I will go alongside you."

[13] But Jacob said to him, "My lord knows that the children are delicate, and that the flocks and herds with young are with me; if I drive them hard for one day, all the flocks will die.

[14] Please let my lord pass on ahead of his servant, and I will travel slowly, at the pace of the herds before me and of the children, until I come to my lord at Seir."

[15] Esau said, "Let me then leave with you some of the people who are with me." But Jacob said, "Why? Let me just find favor in the sight of my lord."

[16] So Esau returned that day on his way to Seir.

[17] But Jacob journeyed to Succoth, and built himself a house, and made booths for his livestock; therefore the place is called Succoth.

[18] Jacob came safely to the city of Shechem, which is in the land of Canaan, when he came from Paddan-Aram; and he camped in front of the city.

[19] He bought the parcel of land on which he had pitched his tent from the sons of Hamor, Shechem's father, for 100 qesitahs.

[20] And he erected there an altar and called it El-Elohe-Israel.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"handmaids" → "slave-girls"** (v1, 2, 6) — as elsewhere.
- **"drove" → "camp"** (v8) — Hebrew מַחֲנֶה *machaneh* = camp / company. Same word as 32:2's Mahanaim. Not "drove" (herd); Esau has already passed through all the droves of gifts and is asking about the human company traveling with them.
- **"therefore I have seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God" → "because truly to see your face is like seeing the face of *Elohim*"** (v10) — Hebrew עַל־כֵּן רָאִיתִי פָנֶיךָ כִּרְאֹת פְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים. Jacob's language deliberately links his seeing Esau's face with his seeing God's face at Peniel (32:30) — his reconciliation with Esau completes the theophanic encounter.
- **"my blessing" (v11)** — Hebrew בִּרְכָתִי *birkhati*. "My blessing-gift" (the same word covers both blessing and the tangible gift that expresses it). Subtle echo of the blessing Jacob stole (ch 27): now he returns a gift to his wronged brother, a restitution of sorts.
- **"I have enough" → "I have all I need"** (v11) — Hebrew יֶשׁ־לִי־כֹל *yesh-li-khol* = "I have everything / all." Jacob's response to Esau's "I have plenty" (v9, *yesh li rav*): Jacob claims completeness, not mere sufficiency.
- **"I will go before thee" → "I will go alongside you"** (v12) — Hebrew אֵלְכָה לְנֶגְדֶּךָ *elkhah le-negdekha* = "I will go in front of / alongside / opposite you" (escort / travel in company with).
- **"overdrive them" → "drive them hard"** (v13) — Hebrew וּדְפָקוּם *u-defaqum* from *daphaq* = knock / drive hard. Modernized.
- **"what needeth it" → "why"** (v15) — archaic → modern.
- **"Succoth" — Hebrew etymology preserved** (v17) — Hebrew סֻכּוֹת *Sukkot* = "shelters / booths" — the same word as the later Feast of Booths / Tabernacles. Named from Jacob's livestock shelters.
- **"Shalem, a city of Shechem" → "safely to the city of Shechem"** (v18) — Hebrew שָׁלֵם עִיר שְׁכֶם. *Shalem* could be:
  1. Adverb: "safely / in peace" → "Jacob came safely to Shechem."
  2. Place-name: "Salem, a city of Shechem" (KJV; possibly a district).
  Most modern scholars favor #1 (the adverbial reading), which fits the Hebrew syntax better and the context (Jacob has survived both the Laban and Esau dangers).
- **"pieces of money" → "qesitahs"** (v19) — Hebrew קְשִׂיטָה *qesitah*. Ancient monetary unit of unknown value (only three occurrences in Bible, all archaic contexts). Preserved.
- **"El-elohe-Israel" → "El Elohe-Yisra'el" with gloss** (v20) — Hebrew אֵל אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל. "El, the *Elohim* of Israel" — the altar-name. Preserved.

### Notes

- **The reconciliation** — Esau's response (run, embrace, fall on neck, kiss, weep) is warmly unexpected after ch 32's dread. Jacob had feared armed conflict and gone to great lengths; Esau simply forgives.
- **Jacob's subtle deflection (v14–17)** — Jacob promises to follow Esau to Seir, but then diverges to Succoth instead. Whether this is a strategic lie or a revision of intent, the brothers do not travel together again; the next meeting will be at their father's death (35:29).
