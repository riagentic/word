# Genesis 1

[1] In the beginning, *Elohim* created the heavens and the earth.

[2] The earth was wild and waste, and darkness was over the face of the deep; and the Spirit of *Elohim* was hovering over the face of the waters.

[3] And *Elohim* said, "Let there be light," and there was light.

[4] *Elohim* saw that the light was good, and *Elohim* separated the light from the darkness.

[5] *Elohim* called the light "Day," and the darkness he called "Night." And there was evening, and there was morning — day one.

[6] And *Elohim* said, "Let there be a vault in the midst of the waters, and let it separate the waters from the waters."

[7] So *Elohim* made the vault and separated the waters under the vault from the waters above the vault. And it was so.

[8] *Elohim* called the vault "Heavens." And there was evening, and there was morning — a second day.

[9] And *Elohim* said, "Let the waters under the heavens be gathered to one place, and let the dry ground appear." And it was so.

[10] *Elohim* called the dry ground "Land," and the gathered waters he called "Seas." And *Elohim* saw that it was good.

[11] And *Elohim* said, "Let the land sprout vegetation: seed-bearing plants and fruit trees bearing fruit with seed in it, each according to its kind, upon the land." And it was so.

[12] The land brought forth vegetation: plants bearing seed according to their kinds, and trees bearing fruit with seed in it according to their kinds. And *Elohim* saw that it was good.

[13] And there was evening, and there was morning — a third day.

[14] And *Elohim* said, "Let there be lights in the vault of the heavens to separate the day from the night, and let them be for signs, for appointed times, for days, and for years,

[15] and let them be lights in the vault of the heavens to give light upon the earth." And it was so.

[16] *Elohim* made the two great lights — the greater light to govern the day, and the lesser light to govern the night — and the stars.

[17] *Elohim* set them in the vault of the heavens to give light upon the earth,

[18] to govern the day and the night, and to separate the light from the darkness. And *Elohim* saw that it was good.

[19] And there was evening, and there was morning — a fourth day.

[20] And *Elohim* said, "Let the waters swarm with swarms of living beings, and let birds fly above the earth across the face of the vault of the heavens."

[21] So *Elohim* created the great sea creatures and every living being that moves, with which the waters swarm, according to their kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind. And *Elohim* saw that it was good.

[22] *Elohim* blessed them, saying, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let the birds multiply on the earth."

[23] And there was evening, and there was morning — a fifth day.

[24] And *Elohim* said, "Let the land bring forth living beings according to their kinds: livestock, creeping things, and wild animals of the land according to their kinds." And it was so.

[25] *Elohim* made the wild animals of the land according to their kinds, the livestock according to their kinds, and every creeping thing of the ground according to its kind. And *Elohim* saw that it was good.

[26] Then *Elohim* said, "Let us make humankind in our image, according to our likeness, and let them rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of the heavens, the livestock, all the earth, and every creeping thing that creeps on the earth."

[27] So *Elohim* created the human in his own image; in the image of *Elohim* he created him; male and female he created them.

[28] *Elohim* blessed them, and *Elohim* said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth and subdue it. Rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of the heavens, and every living thing that moves on the earth."

[29] And *Elohim* said, "Behold, I have given you every seed-bearing plant on the face of all the earth, and every tree that has seed-bearing fruit. They shall be yours for food.

[30] And to every wild animal of the earth, every bird of the heavens, and every creature that creeps on the earth — everything that has the breath of life in it — I have given every green plant for food." And it was so.

[31] *Elohim* saw everything he had made, and behold, it was very good. And there was evening, and there was morning — the sixth day.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים *Elohim*, the divine name used throughout the chapter. Grammatically plural (literally "gods / divine ones") but consistently takes singular verbs here, expressing fullness / majesty of the one God. Name kept in original form rather than translated to generic English "God."
- **"heaven" → "heavens"** (v1, and throughout where שָׁמַיִם *shamayim* appears) — Hebrew *shamayim* is grammatically plural. KJV collapses to singular.
- **"without form, and void" → "wild and waste"** (v2) — Hebrew תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ *tohu va-bohu*. Concrete pair meaning "wasteland and desolation / chaos," not abstract formlessness. Same phrase in Jer 4:23 describes a depopulated land, confirming the concrete sense.
- **"the deep" — kept** (v2) — Hebrew תְהוֹם *tehom*. Anarthrous ("Tehom" without article), cognate with Akkadian *Tiamat*, the primordial chaos-sea deity of the Enuma Elish. Many scholars read as a personified primordial entity that Genesis deliberately demythologizes (it is not a rival power here — just "the deep"). Rendered "the deep" for readability; readers should know the loaded background.
- **"Spirit of God" → "Spirit of *Elohim*"** (v2) — Hebrew רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים *Ruach Elohim*. *Ruach* has threefold range: "spirit / wind / breath." No single English word captures all three. "Spirit of *Elohim*" is traditional; some scholars render "mighty wind" (reading *Elohim* as superlative), but the divine-Spirit reading fits the context and canonical use better.
- **"moved upon" → "hovering over"** (v2) — Hebrew מְרַחֶפֶת *merachefet*. Picture of a bird brooding / fluttering over its young (same root in Deut 32:11 where an eagle hovers over its nest). KJV "moved" is far too weak.
- **"firmament" → "vault"** (v6, 7, 8, 14, 15, 17, 20) — Hebrew רָקִיעַ *raqia*, from root רקע ("to beat / hammer thin," like a metal sheet). Ancient Hebrew cosmology pictured a solid dome holding back the waters above. KJV "firmament" via Latin *firmamentum* preserves the solidity but is now opaque. "Vault" conveys the solid domed structure.
- **"Heaven" → "Heavens"** (v8) — Hebrew שָׁמַיִם *shamayim*, same word as v1. Kept in the same form for consistency: the text explicitly names the *raqia* as *shamayim*, so the name in v8 must match "heavens" in v1.
- **"after his kind" → "according to its / their kind(s)"** (v11, 12, 21, 24, 25) — Hebrew לְמִינוֹ / לְמִינֵהֶם *le-mino / le-minehem*. KJV "his" is archaic neuter (= "its"); modernized.
- **"seasons" → "appointed times"** (v14) — Hebrew מוֹעֲדִים *mo'adim*. Technical term for religious festivals / sacred appointed times (used throughout Lev 23 for the feast cycle). KJV "seasons" misleads toward spring/summer/fall/winter; the text refers to liturgical calendar markers.
- **v14 four-item list restored** — Hebrew: לְאֹתֹת וּלְמוֹעֲדִים וּלְיָמִים וְשָׁנִים = "for signs, for appointed times, for days, and for years" (four parallel items). The prior draft had collapsed "signs" into a modifier ("signs to mark appointed times…"), losing the parallelism. Restored.
- **"moving creature that hath life" → "living beings" / "swarms of living beings"** (v20, 21) — Hebrew שֶׁרֶץ נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה *sheretz nephesh chayyah*. *Nephesh chayyah* = "living being / animating soul." The cognate accusative (יִשְׁרְצוּ … שֶׁרֶץ *yishretzu … sheretz* = "swarm swarms") is an intensifier — preserved as "swarm with swarms of."
- **"whales" → "great sea creatures"** (v21) — Hebrew תַּנִּינִם *tanninim*. Covers large sea creatures, sea serpents, and sea dragons (cf. Isa 27:1, Ps 74:13, Ezek 32:2). KJV "whales" is far too narrow and zoologically anachronistic.
- **"fowl" → "birds"** (v20, 21, 22, 26, 28, 30) — Hebrew עוֹף *'oph*. "Fowl" in modern English is restricted to domestic / game birds; Hebrew covers all flying creatures.
- **"cattle" → "livestock"** (v24, 25, 26) — Hebrew בְּהֵמָה *behemah*. Domestic animals generally, not only bovines. KJV "cattle" in 1611 meant domestic animals broadly; modern English restricts it to cows.
- **"beast of the earth" → "wild animals of the land"** (v24, 25) — Hebrew חַיְתוֹ־אֶרֶץ / חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ *chayto-eretz / chayyat ha-aretz*. Distinguished from domestic livestock; "wild animals" captures the contrast in modern English.
- **"man" → "humankind" (v26) / "the human" (v27)** — Hebrew אָדָם *adam*. Collective noun meaning "human / humankind," derived from אֲדָמָה *adamah* ("ground"). Not a personal name in this chapter. Rendered differently in v26 and v27 because the Hebrew is differently marked: v26 anarthrous נַעֲשֶׂה אָדָם ("let us make *adam*" — generic, collective) → "humankind"; v27 definite הָאָדָם ("*ha-adam*" — the human as a created referent) → "the human." The Hebrew distinction is preserved; no other single English word covers both usages cleanly.
- **"have dominion" → "rule"** (v26, 28) — Hebrew רָדָה *radah*. Governance / stewardship action. Accurate in KJV but archaic; modernized.
- **"replenish" → "fill"** (v28) — Hebrew מָלֵא *male*. In 1611 English "replenish" meant "fill fully"; in modern English it means "refill," wrongly implying a prior human population. Corrected.
- **"meat" → "food"** (v29, 30) — Hebrew אָכְלָה *okhlah*. In 1611 English "meat" meant "food" generically (any edible). Modern "meat" = flesh — the exact opposite of the text, which gives only plants as food here.
- **"wherein there is life" → "has the breath of life in it"** (v30) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר־בּוֹ נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה *asher-bo nephesh chayyah*. *Nephesh chayyah* is animating-life / living breath, not abstract "life."

### Notes (context, not changes)

- **"In the beginning" (v1)** — Hebrew בְּרֵאשִׁית *Bereshit*. Two defended readings:
  1. **Absolute**: "In the beginning, *Elohim* created…" (traditional Jewish liturgy, Christian tradition, LXX, Vulgate).
  2. **Construct**: "When *Elohim* began to create the heavens and the earth — [v2] the earth being wild and waste … [v3] *Elohim* said: Let there be light." (Rashi, Ibn Ezra, and most modern critical scholars: Speiser, Westermann, Alter.) Grammatically slightly better supported, since *bereshit* is anarthrous (a noun in construct state, not "in the [beginning]").
  The absolute rendering is kept here for readability and canonical familiarity; the construct reading is genuinely plausible and the chapter's theology (cosmos-ordering, not *ex nihilo* creation in philosophical sense) may even fit it better.
- **"created" (v1, 21, 27)** — Hebrew בָּרָא *bara*. In the Tanakh, takes only God as subject; denotes a distinctly divine act of bringing-into-being. Often argued to mean creation *ex nihilo*, but the verb itself does not demand that — it marks divine origination; the *ex nihilo* inference is theological, built on v1's context.
- **"Let us make" (v26)** — plural נַעֲשֶׂה *na'aseh*. Three defended readings: (a) plural of majesty; (b) address to the heavenly court / divine council (cf. 1 Kgs 22:19, Job 1:6, Ps 82); (c) inner-divine plurality (classical Trinitarian reading). No single reading provable from the verse alone; left as-is in translation.
- **"over all the earth" (v26)** — MT has וּבְכָל־הָאָרֶץ "and over all the earth," which is odd in a list of creatures. Syriac Peshitta and one DSS fragment read "and over every wild animal of the earth" (*chayyat ha-aretz*), a likely dittography / scribal loss in MT. Translation keeps MT; the variant is a real candidate.
- **v22 asymmetry** — fish are told to "fill the waters," birds only to "multiply on the earth," not to "fill" it. Hebrew preserves this asymmetry (יִרֶב בָּאָרֶץ — no *male* for birds). Translation preserves it.
- **"the two great lights" (v16)** — Hebrew just הַמְּאֹרֹת הַגְּדֹלִים "the great lights" (definite plural); "two" is inferred from immediate context ("greater… lesser"). "Two" kept for clarity; not an addition.
