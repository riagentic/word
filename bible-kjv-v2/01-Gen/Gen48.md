# Genesis 48

[1] After these things, someone told Joseph, "Behold, your father is ill." So he took with him his two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim.

[2] When Jacob was told, "Behold, your son Joseph has come to you," Israel summoned his strength and sat up on the bed.

[3] Jacob said to Joseph, "Almighty *Elohim* appeared to me at Luz in the land of Canaan and blessed me,

[4] and said to me, 'Behold, I will make you fruitful and multiply you, and will make you into a company of peoples, and I will give this land to your offspring after you as an everlasting possession.'

[5] Now your two sons, who were born to you in the land of Egypt before I came to you in Egypt, are mine: Ephraim and Manasseh shall be mine, just as Reuben and Simeon.

[6] Any children born to you after them shall be yours; they shall be called by the name of their brothers in their inheritance.

[7] As for me, when I came from Paddan, Rachel died beside me in the land of Canaan on the way, when there was still some distance to Ephrath; and I buried her there on the way to Ephrath (that is, Bethlehem)."

[8] When Israel saw Joseph's sons, he said, "Who are these?"

[9] Joseph said to his father, "They are my sons, whom *Elohim* has given me here." And he said, "Please bring them to me, that I may bless them."

[10] Now the eyes of Israel were dim from age; he could not see. So he brought them near to him, and he kissed them and embraced them.

[11] And Israel said to Joseph, "I had not thought to see your face, and behold, *Elohim* has also let me see your offspring."

[12] Then Joseph brought them out from between his knees, and bowed himself with his face to the ground.

[13] And Joseph took them both, Ephraim in his right hand toward Israel's left hand, and Manasseh in his left hand toward Israel's right hand, and brought them near to him.

[14] But Israel stretched out his right hand and laid it on the head of Ephraim, who was the younger, and his left hand on the head of Manasseh, crossing his hands intentionally — for Manasseh was the firstborn.

[15] He blessed Joseph and said,

"The *Elohim* before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked,
the *Elohim* who has shepherded me all my life long to this day,

[16] the Messenger who redeemed me from all evil —
bless the boys.
Let my name be named on them,
and the name of my fathers Abraham and Isaac;
and let them grow into a multitude in the midst of the earth."

[17] When Joseph saw that his father had laid his right hand on the head of Ephraim, it was displeasing to him; and he took hold of his father's hand to move it from Ephraim's head to Manasseh's head.

[18] Joseph said to his father, "Not so, my father; for this one is the firstborn. Put your right hand on his head."

[19] But his father refused and said, "I know, my son, I know. He also shall become a people, and he also shall be great. Yet truly his younger brother shall be greater than he, and his offspring shall become a multitude of nations."

[20] So he blessed them that day, saying, "By you Israel shall pronounce blessing, saying, 'May *Elohim* make you like Ephraim and like Manasseh.'" Thus he put Ephraim before Manasseh.

[21] Then Israel said to Joseph, "Behold, I am about to die, but *Elohim* will be with you and will bring you back to the land of your fathers.

[22] Moreover, I have given to you one portion more than to your brothers, which I took from the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"God Almighty" → "Almighty *Elohim*"** (v3) — as in 17:1.
- **"a multitude of people" → "a company of peoples"** (v4) — as in 28:3.
- **"thy issue" → "Any children born to you"** (v6) — Hebrew מוֹלַדְתְּךָ *moladtekha* = your offspring / descendants. Modernized.
- **"guiding his hands wittingly" → "crossing his hands intentionally"** (v14) — Hebrew שִׂכֵּל אֶת־יָדָיו *sikkel et-yadav*. *Sakhal* (piel) = act wisely / deliberately — or, by homonym, "cross" (from *shikkul* sense of swapping). Jacob deliberately reverses hands to put right hand on the younger. "Wittingly" in modern English = knowingly; the Hebrew means more specifically "acted with deliberate intent" — and (punning with *sakhol* "to be bereaved") his hands were "crossed / of the bereaved" (blessing the younger where the older would normally go, reversing primogeniture as he himself had benefited from the reversal).
- **"The God which fed me" → "The *Elohim* who has shepherded me"** (v15) — Hebrew הָאֱלֹהִים הָרֹעֶה אֹתִי *ha-Elohim ha-ro'eh oti*. *Ra'ah* = to shepherd / pasture. Not "fed" generically; the shepherd-image underlies. First explicit Bible reference to Yahweh as shepherd (fully developed in Ps 23).
- **"Angel" → "Messenger"** (v16) — Hebrew הַמַּלְאָךְ *ha-mal'akh*. See 16:7. The *mal'akh* who "redeemed me from all evil" refers to Jacob's protector (possibly the one who wrestled at Peniel, ch 32).
- **"redeemed" (v16)** — Hebrew הַגֹּאֵל *ha-go'el*. *Go'el* = kinsman-redeemer (the relative who recovers a family member from bondage or danger). First use of the word in connection with divine action.
- **"In thee shall Israel bless" → "By you Israel shall pronounce blessing"** (v20) — Hebrew בְּךָ יְבָרֵךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל. Ephraim and Manasseh become the paradigm of blessing — parents invoke their names for children. To this day, the traditional Jewish Friday-night blessing for boys is "May *Elohim* make you like Ephraim and Manasseh."
- **"one portion above thy brethren" (v22)** — Hebrew שְׁכֶם אַחַד עַל־אַחֶיךָ *shekhem achad al-achekha*. *Shekhem* means "shoulder" / "portion," but also the place name Shechem. A pun: "one Shechem more than your brothers." This becomes Joseph's burial place (Josh 24:32). Reference to Jacob's capture "with sword and bow" is obscure — not recorded elsewhere in Genesis (ch 34's taking of Shechem was done by his sons Simeon and Levi, not Jacob personally). May refer to an unrecorded incident, or a retrospective claim of responsibility.

### Notes

- **Reversal of primogeniture** — repeated pattern across Genesis: Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Ephraim over Manasseh. God's choices do not follow birth-order. Anticipates Israel's own choice "not because you were more numerous than any other" (Deut 7:7).
- **Adoption of grandsons (v5–6)** — Jacob promotes Ephraim and Manasseh to full tribal status, equivalent to his own sons. This gives Joseph a double portion in effect (as two tribes). Later, Levi's landless status (being the priestly tribe) leaves room for the twelve-tribe system to include Ephraim and Manasseh while Levi is counted separately.
- **Joseph's blessing vs. sons' blessing** — v15 Jacob "blessed Joseph" but the blessing is given on the boys' heads. Joseph is blessed through his sons — his line is counted twice.
