# Genesis 34

[1] Dinah, the daughter of Leah whom she had borne to Jacob, went out to see the daughters of the land.

[2] When Shechem son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the land, saw her, he seized her and lay with her and violated her.

[3] And his soul was drawn to Dinah the daughter of Jacob, and he loved the young woman, and spoke to the heart of the young woman.

[4] Shechem spoke to his father Hamor, saying, "Get me this girl as a wife."

[5] Jacob heard that he had defiled Dinah his daughter; but his sons were with his livestock in the field, so Jacob kept silent until they came home.

[6] Hamor, Shechem's father, went out to Jacob to speak with him.

[7] The sons of Jacob had come in from the field when they heard of it; and the men were pained and very angry, because he had done an outrageous thing in Israel by lying with Jacob's daughter — a thing that ought not to be done.

[8] Hamor spoke with them, saying, "The soul of my son Shechem longs for your daughter; please give her to him as a wife.

[9] Intermarry with us — give your daughters to us, and take our daughters for yourselves.

[10] You shall dwell with us; the land is open before you — dwell and trade in it and acquire holdings in it."

[11] Shechem said to her father and her brothers, "Let me find favor in your eyes, and whatever you say to me I will give.

[12] Make the bride-price and the gift as high as you like; I will give whatever you demand of me. Only give me the girl as a wife."

[13] The sons of Jacob answered Shechem and Hamor his father with deceit, because he had defiled Dinah their sister,

[14] and said to them, "We cannot do this thing — to give our sister to a man who is uncircumcised, for that would be a disgrace to us.

[15] Only on this condition will we consent to you: if you will become like us, by having every male among you circumcised.

[16] Then we will give our daughters to you, and we will take your daughters for ourselves; and we will dwell with you and become one people.

[17] But if you will not listen to us about being circumcised, then we will take our daughter and go."

[18] Their words pleased Hamor and Shechem son of Hamor.

[19] And the young man did not delay to do the thing, because he delighted in Jacob's daughter. Now he was the most honored of all his father's household.

[20] So Hamor and Shechem his son came to the gate of their city, and spoke to the men of their city, saying,

[21] "These men are at peace with us; let them dwell in the land and trade in it, for the land is wide enough for them. Let us take their daughters for ourselves as wives, and let us give them our daughters.

[22] Only on this condition will the men consent to dwell with us, to become one people — that every male among us be circumcised, as they are circumcised.

[23] Will not their livestock, their property, and all their animals be ours? Only let us consent to them, and they will dwell with us."

[24] All who went out of the gate of his city listened to Hamor and to Shechem his son; and every male was circumcised, all who went out of the gate of his city.

[25] On the third day, when they were in pain, two of the sons of Jacob, Simeon and Levi, the brothers of Dinah, each took his sword and came against the unsuspecting city, and killed every male.

[26] They killed Hamor and Shechem his son with the edge of the sword, and took Dinah out of Shechem's house, and went away.

[27] The sons of Jacob came upon the slain and plundered the city, because they had defiled their sister.

[28] They took their flocks, their herds, their donkeys — whatever was in the city and whatever was in the field.

[29] All their wealth, all their little ones, and their wives they took captive, and plundered everything that was in the houses.

[30] Then Jacob said to Simeon and Levi, "You have brought trouble on me, making me odious among the inhabitants of the land, among the Canaanites and the Perizzites. My men are few in number; they will gather against me and strike me, and I will be destroyed, I and my household."

[31] But they said, "Should he treat our sister like a prostitute?"

## Changes

- **"defiled her" → "violated her"** (v2) — Hebrew וַיְעַנֶּהָ *wa-ye'anneha* (from *anah*) = humbled / violated / oppressed — technical term for rape / sexual violation in OT legal texts (Deut 22:24, 29). Same root as Hagar's affliction and Leah's affliction in other contexts.
- **"his soul clave unto" → "his soul was drawn to"** (v3) — Hebrew וַתִּדְבַּק נַפְשׁוֹ *wa-tidbaq naphsho*. *Davaq* = cling (same verb as "cleave" in Gen 2:24). Shechem's attachment is real but follows after the assault — the order of his action (rape first, then love) is morally damning.
- **"spake kindly unto the damsel" → "spoke to the heart of the young woman"** (v3) — Hebrew וַיְדַבֵּר עַל־לֵב הַנַּעֲרָה *wa-ydaber al-lev ha-na'arah*. Idiom: "speak to the heart" = speak tenderly / try to win over / console.
- **"damsel" → "young woman / girl"** (v3, 4, 12) — Hebrew נַעֲרָה *na'arah*.
- **"prince of the country" → "prince of the land"** (v2) — Hebrew נְשִׂיא הָאָרֶץ *nesi ha-aretz*.
- **"wrought folly" → "done an outrageous thing"** (v7) — Hebrew עָשָׂה נְבָלָה *asah nevalah*. *Nevalah* = outrage / senseless wickedness; stronger than "folly." Technical term for sexual crime (Deut 22:21, Judg 19:23–24, 20:6, 10).
- **"dowry and gift" → "bride-price and gift"** (v12) — Hebrew מֹהַר וּמַתָּן *mohar u-mattan*. *Mohar* = bride-wealth paid by groom's family to bride's family (distinct from modern "dowry," which is paid by bride's family to groom's). Corrected to the ancient Near Eastern practice.
- **"reproach" → "disgrace"** (v14) — Hebrew חֶרְפָּה *cherpah* = reproach / disgrace.
- **"peaceable" → "at peace"** (v21) — Hebrew שְׁלֵמִים *shelemim*. From *shalom* — wholesome / in integrity / at peace with.
- **"their substance" → "their property"** (v23) — Hebrew קִנְיָנָם *qinyanam*. Property / acquired goods.
- **"boldly" → "came against the unsuspecting city"** (v25) — Hebrew בֶּטַח *betach*. Ambiguous:
  1. "Confidently / boldly" — referring to Simeon and Levi (KJV).
  2. "Unsuspectingly / secure" — referring to the city (better Hebrew sense: the city was at ease, having just been ritually cut).
  The second reading is overwhelmingly favored: the point is that the Shechemites were incapacitated and trusting, making the attack especially treacherous.
- **"harlot" → "prostitute"** (v31) — Hebrew זוֹנָה *zonah*. Modernized.

### Notes

- **"The rape of Dinah"** — the story has been read various ways: as a rape followed by twisted reconciliation attempt; as consensual (Shechem's "love" came first, then... but "*wa-ye'anneha*" is legally strong); as a tribal conflict story. Dinah's own voice is absent; the brothers speak and act on her behalf. The text's moral stance is complex: it condemns both Shechem's violation and the brothers' treachery (Jacob's deathbed speech curses Simeon and Levi for this, 49:5–7).
- **Simeon and Levi's deceit** — they exploit the religious sign of the covenant (circumcision) as a weapon to disable and kill. A particularly dark moment: covenantal mark used for genocidal ends. Jacob condemns them pragmatically (v30: "you'll get us killed"); the deeper moral indictment comes later (49:5–7).
- **"Should he treat our sister like a prostitute?" (v31)** — the brothers' retort has ethical force (the violation was real and must have consequence) but does not justify the disproportion and treachery of their response. The narrative holds the question open.
