# Genesis 20

[1] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ journeyed from there to the land of the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧, and dwelt between ⟦kadesh|Qadesh|Kadesh⟧ and Shur; and he sojourned in Gerar.

[2] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ said of Sarah his wife, "She is my sister." And ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ king of Gerar sent and took Sarah.

[3] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ came to ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ in a dream of the night, and said to him, "Behold, you are about to die because of the woman whom you have taken, and she is married to a husband."

[4] And ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ had not come near to her; and he said, "⟦adonai|Adonai|Lord⟧, will you kill a nation, even a righteous one?

[5] Did he not himself say to me, 'She is my sister'? And she, even she herself said, 'He is my brother.' In the integrity of my heart and in the innocence of my hands I have done this."

[6] And the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ said to him in the dream, "I also have known that in the integrity of your heart you have done this, and I also withheld you from sinning against me; therefore I did not allow you to touch her.

[7] And now, return the wife of the man, for he is a prophet, and he will pray for you, and live. And if you do not return her, know that you shall surely die, you and all who are yours."

[8] And ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ rose early in the morning and called all his servants, and spoke all these words in their ears; and the men feared greatly.

[9] And ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ called ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ and said to him, "What have you done to us? And what have I sinned against you, that you have brought upon me and upon my kingdom a great sin? Deeds that are not done you have done with me."

[10] And ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ said to ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, "What did you see, that you did this thing?"

[11] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ said, "Because I said, Surely there is no fear of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in this place, and they will kill me on account of my wife.

[12] And also, truly, she is my sister, the daughter of my father, only not the daughter of my mother; and she became my wife.

[13] And it came to pass, when ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ caused me to wander from the house of my father, that I said to her, 'This is your ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ which you shall do with me: at every place where we come, say of me, He is my brother.'"

[14] And ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ took flock and herd, and slaves and maidservants, and gave them to ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧; and he returned to him Sarah his wife.

[15] And ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ said, "Behold, my land is before you; dwell where it is good in your eyes."

[16] And to Sarah he said, "Behold, I have given a thousand of silver to your brother; behold, it is for you a covering of the eyes to all who are with you; and with all, and you are set right."

[17] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ prayed to the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ healed ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ and his wife and his slave women, and they bore.

[18] For ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ had surely closed up every womb of the house of ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧, on account of Sarah, the wife of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v18) — Tetragrammaton preserved. The chapter has *Elohim* throughout and Yahweh only in its last verse.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim", in v6 and v17.
- **"And" kept** (throughout) — Hebrew וַ / וְ at the head of each clause.
- **"toward the south country" → "to the land of the Negev"** (v1) — as in 12:9. "Dwelt" (יָשַׁב) and "sojourned" (גּוּר, the verb of the sojourner) are two verbs; וַיָּגָר *wa-yagor* sounds like "Gerar".
- **"said of Sarah"** (v2) — Hebrew אֶל־שָׂרָה, literally "to Sarah": here "about / concerning".
- **"in a dream by night" → "in a dream of the night"** (v3) — Hebrew בַּחֲלוֹם הַלָּיְלָה.
- **"thou art but a dead man" → "you are about to die"** (v3) — Hebrew הִנְּךָ מֵת *hinneka met*, a participle: "behold you, dying".
- **"for she is a man's wife" → "and she is married to a husband"** (v3) — Hebrew וְהִוא בְּעֻלַת בָּעַל *be'ulat ba'al*, literally "owned by an owner / lord"; וְ is "and".
- **"Lord" → "Adonai"** (v4) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי *Adonai*, pointed as the form used of God (see 18:3).
- **"wilt thou slay also a righteous nation?" → "will you kill a nation, even a righteous one?"** (v4) — Hebrew הֲגוֹי גַּם־צַדִּיק תַּהֲרֹג, in the Hebrew order. "Come near": קָרַב.
- **"innocency of my hands" → "innocence of my hands"** (v5) — Hebrew נִקְיֹן כַּפַּי, literally "of my palms". "Integrity": תֹּם *tom*, wholeness, the root of "blameless" (17:1).
- **"Yea, I know" → "I also have known"** (v6) — Hebrew גַּם אָנֹכִי יָדַעְתִּי, a perfect, with the pronoun written out; "I also" stands twice in the verse.
- **"suffered I thee not to touch her" → "I did not allow you to touch her"** (v6) — Hebrew לֹא־נְתַתִּיךָ, literally "I did not give you".
- **"restore the man his wife" → "return the wife of the man"** (v7, 14) — Hebrew הָשֵׁב, "bring back", one verb in v7 (twice) and v14. "Her" after "return" in v7b is supplied.
- **"prophet"** (v7) — Hebrew נָבִיא *navi*, the first time the word stands in the Bible; here of one who prays for another.
- **"thou shalt live" → "and live"** (v7) — Hebrew וֶחְיֵה, a command. "You shall surely die": מוֹת תָּמוּת, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "dying you shall die". "All that are thine" → "all who are yours".
- **"told all these things in their ears" → "spoke all these words in their ears"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיְדַבֵּר אֶת־כָּל־הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה. "Servants": עֲבָדָיו, the king's men; the same word is "slaves" in v14. "Were sore afraid" → "feared greatly".
- **"what have I offended thee" → "what have I sinned against you"** (v9) — Hebrew חָטָאתִי, the same root as "a great sin" (חֲטָאָה גְדֹלָה). "Deeds that are not done": מַעֲשִׂים אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יֵעָשׂוּ, one root.
- **"What sawest thou"** (v10) — Hebrew מָה רָאִיתָ, literally so: what did you have in view.
- **"Because I thought" → "Because I said"** (v11) — Hebrew כִּי אָמַרְתִּי. "For my wife's sake" → "on account of my wife": עַל־דְּבַר, literally "on the matter of" (also v18).
- **"the fear of God" → "fear of *Elohim*"** (v11) — Hebrew יִרְאַת אֱלֹהִים *yir'at Elohim*.
- **"And yet indeed she is my sister" → "And also, truly, she is my sister"** (v12) — Hebrew וְגַם־אָמְנָה. "Only not": אַךְ לֹא.
- **"when God caused me to wander"** (v13) — Hebrew הִתְעוּ אֹתִי אֱלֹהִים *hit'u oti Elohim*. The verb is plural: literally "when Elohim / gods caused (they) me to wander". *Elohim* is a plural form which, of the one God, normally takes a singular verb (as in v3, 6, 17). The plural verb here can be the grammatical agreement with the plural form (as in 35:7), or Abraham may be speaking to a foreign king in the king's own way: "when the gods caused me to wander". English "caused" does not show the number; the Hebrew is not changed.
- **"thy kindness which thou shalt shew unto me" → "your *chesed* which you shall do with me"** (v13) — Hebrew חַסְדֵּךְ אֲשֶׁר תַּעֲשִׂי עִמָּדִי. *Chesed*, as in 19:19: loyal love.
- **"sheep, and oxen, and menservants, and womenservants" → "flock and herd, and slaves and maidservants"** (v14) — Hebrew צֹאן וּבָקָר וַעֲבָדִים וּשְׁפָחֹת. שִׁפְחָה "maidservant" (as 16:1) is a different word from אָמָה "slave woman" in v17.
- **"dwell where it pleaseth thee" → "dwell where it is good in your eyes"** (v15) — Hebrew בַּטּוֹב בְּעֵינֶיךָ שֵׁב.
- **"a thousand pieces of silver" → "a thousand of silver"** (v16) — Hebrew אֶלֶף כֶּסֶף; "pieces" (or "shekels") is not in the Hebrew.
- **"a covering of the eyes" (v16)** — Hebrew הוּא־לָךְ כְּסוּת עֵינַיִם *kesut eynayim*, literally so. The Hebrew is hard and is translated as it stands. הוּא can be "it" (the silver) or "he" (your brother). A "covering of the eyes" is commonly understood as a gift that makes people shut their eyes to what has happened — a public amends; others take it of a veil. The phrase is found only here.
- **"and with all other: thus she was reproved" → "and with all, and you are set right"** (v16) — Hebrew וְאֵת כֹּל וְנֹכָחַת *we-et kol we-nokhachat*, word for word: "and-with all and-set-right". The Masoretic accents put the main break before these words. נֹכָחַת is a passive (niphal) form of יכח "decide, set right, reprove": as pointed, a participle, "(you are) one set right" = shown to be in the right; it is also read "and you are reproved" (KJV). How "and with all" joins the sentence is not clear ("and before all"; or with the preceding: "to all who are with you and with all"). The words are kept in their order.
- **"and they bare children" → "and they bore"** (v17) — Hebrew וַיֵּלֵדוּ, no object. "Maidservants" → "slave women": אַמְהֹתָיו.
- **"had fast closed up all the wombs" → "had surely closed up every womb"** (v18) — Hebrew עָצֹר עָצַר יְהוָה בְּעַד כָּל־רֶחֶם, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "restraining had restrained about every womb". The verb עָצַר is the one Sarai uses in 16:2 ("Yahweh has kept me from bearing").

### Notes

- **Second "wife-sister" story** — the same thing happens as in 12:10–20, with another king (Pharaoh → Abimelech), and again in 26:6–11 with Isaac. What Abraham says in v12 is told only here.
- **Abimelech** — the foreign king acts in the integrity of his heart, and *Elohim* says so (v6); the one who has spoken untruly is Abraham.
- **"Prophet" first occurrence** (v7) — Hebrew נָבִיא *navi*. Abraham is called a prophet as one who prays for Abimelech and his house.
