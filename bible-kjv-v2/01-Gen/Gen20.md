## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"toward the south country" → "toward the land of the Negev"** (v1) — as in 12:9.
- **"a dead man" → "about to die"** (v3) — Hebrew הִנְּךָ מֵת *hinneka met*. Participle: "behold, you are dying / dead." Idiom of imminent doom.
- **"a man's wife" → "a married woman"** (v3) — Hebrew בְּעֻלַת בָּעַל *be'ulat ba'al*. "Owned of a husband / bonded to a husband." Technical marital status.
- **"Lord"** (v4) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי *Adonai*. Address form; KJV "Lord" retained.
- **"innocency" → "innocence"** (v5) — modernized.
- **"the fear of God" → "fear of *Elohim*"** (v11) — Hebrew יִרְאַת אֱלֹהִים *yir'at Elohim*. Reverence / moral accountability before God; a paradigmatic concept of wisdom literature.
- **"kindness" → "loyalty"** (v13) — Hebrew חֶסֶד *chesed*. As in 19:19. Here Abraham defines a chesed-act as one that saves his life.
- **"a covering of the eyes" (v16)** — Hebrew כְּסוּת עֵינַיִם *kesut eynayim*. Obscure idiom. Possibilities:
  1. A vindication / public demonstration that Sarah is cleared (a "covering over" any accusation).
  2. A veil for modesty (a bride-price compensating for her exposure).
  3. Compensation for "the eyes" that saw the transgression.
  Traditional reading: public vindication; translation attempts to capture this sense with "before everyone you are vindicated."
- **"thus she was reproved" → "before everyone you are vindicated"** (v16) — Hebrew וְאֵת כֹּל וְנֹכָחַת *we-et-kol we-nokhachat* — difficult. *Nokhach* (niphal) could mean "reproved" or "vindicated / cleared." Context (Abimelech is paying Abraham to clear Sarah's name publicly) supports vindication, not reproof.
- **"fast closed up all the wombs" → "tightly closed every womb"** (v18) — Hebrew עָצֹר עָצַר יְהוָה *atsor atsar* — infinitive absolute for emphasis. The full fertility of Abimelech's household was shut. Echoes Sarai's own earlier language in 16:2 (*atsarani*). Unusually for Hebrew Bible, the intimate punishment (infertility) works as divine protection of Sarah's husband-line at the same time as divine judgment.

### Notes

- **Second "wife-sister" story** — the same scenario as 12:10–20 with different king (Pharaoh → Abimelech). Two readings: variant traditions of one event (source-critical); or a genuine repeat of Abraham's deception (canonical). Either way, the narrative shows Abraham falling into the same failure twice. Abraham's half-truth excuse (v12) is novel here; makes his lying more complex but not more honorable.
- **Abimelech's integrity** — a pagan king displays genuine moral rectitude while the covenant-bearer Abraham is the one lying. The narrative is not flattering to Abraham. Abimelech appeals to *Elohim* as "righteous judge" (cf. 18:25); the narrative takes pagan piety seriously.
- **"Prophet" first occurrence** (v7) — Hebrew נָבִיא *navi*. Abraham's first designation as prophet — specifically in the capacity of intercessor (he prays for Abimelech's household).
