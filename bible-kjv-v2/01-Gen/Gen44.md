# Genesis 44

[1] Then he commanded the steward of his house, saying, "Fill the men's sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put each man's silver in the mouth of his sack.

[2] And put my cup, the silver cup, in the mouth of the sack of the youngest, along with his grain money." And he did as Joseph told him.

[3] As soon as the morning was light, the men were sent away, they and their donkeys.

[4] When they had gone out of the city and were not yet far off, Joseph said to his steward, "Up, follow the men; and when you overtake them, say to them, 'Why have you repaid evil for good?

[5] Is it not from this that my lord drinks, and by which he indeed divines? You have done evil in what you did.'"

[6] When he overtook them, he spoke these very words to them.

[7] They said to him, "Why does my lord speak such words as these? Far be it from your servants to do such a thing!

[8] Behold, the silver that we found in the mouth of our sacks, we brought back to you from the land of Canaan. How then should we steal silver or gold from your lord's house?

[9] With whomever of your servants it is found, let him die; and we also will be slaves to my lord."

[10] He said, "Now let it be according to your words: the one with whom it is found shall be my slave, and the rest of you shall be blameless."

[11] Then each man quickly lowered his sack to the ground, and each man opened his sack.

[12] He searched, beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest; and the cup was found in Benjamin's sack.

[13] Then they tore their garments, and each man loaded his donkey and returned to the city.

[14] When Judah and his brothers came to Joseph's house, he was still there; and they fell before him on the ground.

[15] Joseph said to them, "What deed is this that you have done? Do you not know that such a man as I can surely divine?"

[16] Judah said, "What shall we say to my lord? What shall we speak? How shall we justify ourselves? *Elohim* has found out the iniquity of your servants. Behold, we are my lord's slaves, both we and he in whose hand the cup was found."

[17] But he said, "Far be it from me to do so! The man in whose hand the cup was found, he shall be my slave; and as for you, go up in peace to your father."

[18] Then Judah came near to him and said, "Oh, my lord, please let your servant speak a word in my lord's ears, and let not your anger burn against your servant, for you are just like Pharaoh.

[19] My lord asked his servants, saying, 'Have you a father or a brother?'

[20] And we said to my lord, 'We have an aged father, and a young son of his old age; his brother is dead, and he alone is left of his mother, and his father loves him.'

[21] Then you said to your servants, 'Bring him down to me, that I may set my eyes on him.'

[22] And we said to my lord, 'The boy cannot leave his father; for if he should leave his father, his father would die.'

[23] But you said to your servants, 'Unless your youngest brother comes down with you, you shall not see my face again.'

[24] When we went up to your servant my father, we told him the words of my lord.

[25] And our father said, 'Go again, buy us a little food.'

[26] But we said, 'We cannot go down. If our youngest brother is with us, then we will go down; for we may not see the man's face unless our youngest brother is with us.'

[27] Then your servant my father said to us, 'You know that my wife bore me two sons;

[28] and one went out from me, and I said, "Surely he has been torn in pieces," and I have not seen him since.

[29] If you take this one also from me, and harm comes to him, you will bring down my gray hairs in evil to Sheol.'

[30] Now therefore, when I come to your servant my father and the boy is not with us — since his life is bound up in the boy's life —

[31] then it will come about, when he sees that the boy is not with us, that he will die; and your servants will bring down the gray hairs of your servant our father in sorrow to Sheol.

[32] For your servant became surety for the boy to my father, saying, 'If I do not bring him back to you, then I shall bear the blame before my father forever.'

[33] Now therefore, please let your servant remain in place of the boy as a slave to my lord, and let the boy go up with his brothers.

[34] For how can I go up to my father if the boy is not with me? I could not bear to look upon the evil that would come upon my father."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"divineth" → "divines"** (v5, 15) — Hebrew יְנַחֵשׁ *yenachesh*. From *nachash* = practice divination, the same root as *nachash* (serpent). Joseph claims (as part of the pretense) to practice cup-divination (hydromancy — reading patterns in liquid), a known Egyptian practice. KJV translates directly; preserved as standard rendering.
- **"God forbid" → "Far be it"** (v7, 17) — Hebrew חָלִילָה *chalilah*. Idiomatic negation / protest, not a theological interjection. "God forbid" adds "God" that isn't in the Hebrew; "Far be it" is the standard modern rendering (cf. 18:25).
- **"bondmen / bondman" → "slaves / slave"** (v9, 10, 16, 17, 33) — Hebrew עֲבָדִים / עָבֶד *avadim / eved*. Slaves in plain language. KJV "bondmen" softens.
- **"wot ye not" → "Do you not know"** (v15) — archaic → modern.
- **"*Elohim* has found out the iniquity of your servants" (v16)** — Judah's remarkable statement: he accepts the hidden cup as divine judgment for hidden, long-ago wrong (selling Joseph). Takes corporate guilt. This is the moral turning point of the Joseph cycle.
- **"grey hairs with sorrow to the grave" → "gray hairs in sorrow to Sheol"** (v29, 31) — As in 37:35, 42:38. Sheol preserved.
- **"life is bound up in the lad's life" → "life is bound up in the boy's life"** (v30) — Hebrew נַפְשׁוֹ קְשׁוּרָה בְנַפְשׁוֹ *naphsho qeshurah be-naphsho*. *Nephesh* = life / soul / self. The father's very selfhood is tied to the boy's — a tender, intimate phrase.

### Notes

- **Judah's speech (v18–34)** — the longest continuous speech in Genesis, and the moral climax of the Joseph cycle. Judah, who once proposed selling Joseph (37:26–27), now offers himself as substitute for Benjamin. The brother whose loss would grieve Jacob most has been replaced in Judah's heart by his father's grief itself. This transformation — from casual fratricide-by-sale to substitutionary self-offering — prepares Joseph's revelation in ch 45.
- **Joseph's test** — he deliberately set up the trap to see if the brothers would abandon Benjamin (Jacob's new favorite) as they had once abandoned Joseph. Their refusal, climaxing in Judah's offer, proves the change.
- **"You are just like Pharaoh" (v18)** — Judah's way of approaching the supreme authority. Within the narrative's irony: Judah does not know that this Egyptian lord is in fact greater than "just like Pharaoh"; he is both Egyptian vizier and wronged brother.
