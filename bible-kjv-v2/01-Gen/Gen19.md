# Genesis 19

[1] The two messengers came to Sodom in the evening, and Lot was sitting in the gate of Sodom. When Lot saw them, he rose to meet them, and bowed himself with his face to the ground

[2] and said, "Behold now, my lords, turn aside, please, into your servant's house and spend the night, and wash your feet; then you may rise up early and go on your way." But they said, "No, we will spend the night in the open square."

[3] But he pressed them strongly, so they turned aside to him and entered his house. He made them a feast, baked unleavened bread, and they ate.

[4] Before they lay down, the men of the city, the men of Sodom, surrounded the house — from the young to the old, all the people to the last man.

[5] And they called to Lot and said to him, "Where are the men who came to you tonight? Bring them out to us, that we may know them."

[6] Lot went out to them at the entrance and shut the door after him.

[7] He said, "Please, my brothers, do not do this wickedness.

[8] Look, I have two daughters who have not known a man; let me bring them out to you, and do to them whatever is good in your eyes; only do nothing to these men, because they have come under the shadow of my roof."

[9] But they said, "Stand back!" And they said, "This one came to sojourn, and he keeps judging! Now we will deal worse with you than with them." They pressed hard against the man, against Lot, and drew near to break down the door.

[10] But the men reached out their hand and pulled Lot inside with them into the house, and shut the door.

[11] And they struck the men at the entrance of the house with blinding light, from the least to the greatest, so that they wore themselves out trying to find the entrance.

[12] Then the men said to Lot, "Have you anyone else here? A son-in-law, your sons, your daughters, and anyone else you have in the city — bring them out of the place.

[13] For we are about to destroy this place, because the outcry against them has become great before the face of Yahweh, and Yahweh has sent us to destroy it."

[14] Lot went out and spoke to his sons-in-law, who were to marry his daughters, and said, "Up, leave this place, for Yahweh is about to destroy the city." But he seemed like one joking to his sons-in-law.

[15] As dawn broke, the messengers urged Lot, saying, "Up, take your wife and your two daughters who are here, lest you be swept away in the iniquity of the city."

[16] But he lingered, so the men grasped his hand and the hand of his wife and the hands of his two daughters, Yahweh being merciful to him; and they brought him out and set him outside the city.

[17] When they had brought them outside, he said, "Flee for your life! Do not look behind you, and do not stop anywhere in the plain. Flee to the hill country, lest you be swept away."

[18] Lot said to them, "Oh no, my lord!

[19] Behold now, your servant has found favor in your sight, and you have magnified your lovingkindness that you have shown me in saving my life; but I cannot flee to the hill country, lest disaster overtake me and I die.

[20] Behold now, this city is near enough to flee to, and it is a little one; let me flee there — is it not a little one? — and my life will be spared."

[21] He said to him, "Behold, I grant you this favor also: I will not overthrow the city that you have named.

[22] Hurry, flee there, for I cannot do anything until you arrive there." Therefore the name of the city was called Zoar.

[23] The sun had risen on the earth when Lot entered Zoar.

[24] Then Yahweh rained on Sodom and Gomorrah sulfur and fire from Yahweh out of the heavens;

[25] and he overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and what grew on the ground.

[26] But Lot's wife, behind him, looked back, and she became a pillar of salt.

[27] Abraham rose early in the morning and went to the place where he had stood before Yahweh.

[28] He looked down toward Sodom and Gomorrah, toward all the land of the plain, and saw the smoke of the land going up like the smoke of a kiln.

[29] So when *Elohim* destroyed the cities of the plain, *Elohim* remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow when he overthrew the cities in which Lot had lived.

[30] Lot went up from Zoar and settled in the hill country with his two daughters, for he was afraid to stay in Zoar. He dwelt in a cave, he and his two daughters.

[31] The firstborn said to the younger, "Our father is old, and there is no man in the land to come in to us according to the custom of all the earth.

[32] Come, let us make our father drink wine, and we will lie with him, that we may keep offspring alive through our father."

[33] So they made their father drink wine that night; and the firstborn went in and lay with her father, and he did not know when she lay down or when she rose.

[34] On the next day the firstborn said to the younger, "Look, I lay last night with my father. Let us make him drink wine this night also; then you go in and lie with him, that we may keep offspring alive through our father."

[35] So they made their father drink wine that night also; and the younger rose and lay with him, and he did not know when she lay down or when she rose.

[36] So both the daughters of Lot became pregnant by their father.

[37] The firstborn bore a son and called his name Moab; he is the ancestor of the Moabites to this day.

[38] The younger also bore a son and called his name Ben-ammi; he is the ancestor of the Ammonites to this day.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"angels" → "messengers"** (v1, 15) — Hebrew מַלְאָכִים *mal'akhim*. As in 16:7.
- **"even" → "evening"** (v1) — archaic → modern.
- **"sat in the gate" (v1)** — Hebrew יֹשֵׁב בְּשַׁעַר־סְדֹם. Civic gate — the open area at the city gate was where elders / officials sat to conduct business and legal matters. Lot had gained a civic position in Sodom.
- **"tarry all night" → "spend the night"** (v2) — modernized.
- **"in the street" → "in the open square"** (v2) — Hebrew בָרְחוֹב *ba-rechov* = public square / open area. Not "street" in modern sense (implying a narrow lane).
- **"compassed" → "surrounded"** (v4) — Hebrew נָסַבּוּ *nasabbu*. Modernized.
- **"that we may know them" (v5)** — Hebrew וְנֵדְעָה אֹתָם *we-ned'ah otam*. *Yada* here carries sexual intent (as in 4:1 "Adam knew Eve"). The Sodomites' demand is homosexual gang rape. Traditional reading; minority view (e.g., Boswell, Helminiak) argues for non-sexual "know" = interrogate. The parallel with v8 where Lot offers his daughters "who have not known a man" (clearly sexual) makes the non-sexual reading strained. Literal "know" preserved; sexual sense inescapable from context.
- **"brethren" → "my brothers"** (v7) — modernized. Lot addresses them with community-kin language in a desperate plea.
- **"do not so wickedly" → "do not do this wickedness"** (v7) — Hebrew אַל־תָּרֵעוּ *al-tare'u*. Root *ra'a* (to do evil).
- **Lot offers his daughters (v8)** — the text records without comment, but the act is ethically monstrous. The patriarchal-honor logic (guest protection trumps daughters' safety) is clearly the value at play; whether the narrator approves is debated. No change to text.
- **"Stand back" → "Stand back!"** (v9) — Hebrew גֶּשׁ־הָלְאָה *gesh-hal'ah* = "Come on, away!" — an angry rebuke.
- **"he will needs be a judge" → "he keeps judging"** (v9) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁפֹּט שָׁפוֹט *wa-yishpot shafot* = infinitive-absolute construction: "keeps judging / acts as judge." A resident alien (*ger*) taking it upon himself to correct the native residents.
- **"blindness" → "blinding light"** (v11) — Hebrew סַנְוֵרִים *sanverim*. Used only here and 2 Kgs 6:18 — not the ordinary word for blindness (*iwer*). The rare term likely denotes a dazzling / confusing light that disorients rather than simple loss of sight. Preserved as "blinding light."
- **"the cry of them is waxen great" → "the outcry against them has become great"** (v13) — same *za'aqah* as 18:20–21.
- **"he seemed as one that mocked" → "he seemed like one joking"** (v14) — Hebrew כִּמְצַחֵק *kimtsacheq* — same root as Isaac (laugh). Lot's urgent warning sounds like humor to his sons-in-law; the *tsachaq* that names Isaac recurs as a bitter irony around Lot.
- **"lest thou be consumed" → "lest you be swept away"** (v15, 17) — Hebrew פֶּן־תִּסָּפֶה *pen-tissapheh* — niphal of *safah* = be swept away / destroyed. Clearer modern verb.
- **"mercy" → "lovingkindness"** (v19) — Hebrew חֶסֶד *chesed*. The key Hebrew covenantal-love term: steadfast loyal love / covenant faithfulness. No single English equivalent. "Lovingkindness" is traditional and captures chesed better than "mercy" (though still insufficient).
- **"is it not a little one?"** (v20) — Hebrew wordplay preserved: Zoar's name comes from *mits'ar* (v20) = "little / insignificant thing." Gloss added at v22.
- **"accepted thee concerning this thing" → "grant you this favor"** (v21) — Hebrew נָשָׂאתִי פָנֶיךָ *nasa'ti fanekha* = "lifted up your face" = granted your petition. Idiom.
- **"brimstone and fire" → "sulfur and fire"** (v24) — Hebrew גָּפְרִית וָאֵשׁ *gophrit wa-esh*. *Gophrit* = sulfur (geologically plausible for the Dead Sea region, which has sulfurous deposits). "Brimstone" is an archaic English word.
- **"pillar of salt" (v26)** — Hebrew נְצִיב מֶלַח *netsiv melach*. *Netsiv* = standing stone / pillar. The salt pillars of the Mt. Sodom formation still exist (Lisan peninsula, Dead Sea — highly saline erosional features). The text names one such formation as "Lot's wife."
- **"furnace" → "kiln"** (v28) — Hebrew כְבְשָׁן *kivshan* = smelting furnace / kiln. Heavy industrial smoke, not a domestic oven.
- **"God remembered Abraham" (v29)** — as in 8:1, *zakhar* = covenantal action, not just mental recall. Lot is saved for Abraham's sake, on the reckoning of the ch 18 intercession.
- **"preserve seed of our father" → "keep offspring alive through our father"** (v32, 34) — Hebrew נְחַיֶּה מֵאָבִינוּ זָרַע *nechayyeh me-avinu zara*. *Chayah* (piel) = keep alive / preserve. The daughters assume universal catastrophe and act to continue the human line. Their plan is still incestuous, but their motive is sketched as lineage-preservation rather than lust.
- **"Moab" — etymology of name** (v37) — Hebrew מוֹאָב *Mo'av*. Possibly from מֵאָב *me-av* ("from father") — an explicit reference to incestuous origin, though the MT does not make the etymology explicit (unlike LXX and Josephus, which gloss it).
- **"Ben-Ammi" — explicit gloss** (v38) — Hebrew בֶּן־עַמִּי *Ben-Ammi* = "son of my kinsman / people." The mother acknowledges the incestuous origin in the name: her son is from her own kin.

### Notes

- **Sodom's sin** — the text emphasizes inhospitality, sexual violence against strangers, and the preceding "outcry" (suggesting systemic injustice). Ezek 16:49–50 interprets: "This was the iniquity of your sister Sodom: she and her daughters had pride, fullness of bread, and careless ease, and did not strengthen the hand of the poor and needy." Jude 7 adds "gross sexual immorality." Reducing Sodom's sin to only homosexuality is a later, narrower reading; the text is multi-faceted.
- **Lot's daughters (v30–38)** — the origin story of Moab and Ammon, Israel's Transjordan neighbors. The incest-etiology is polemical / bitter — a narrator's explanation of hostile neighbors' suspect ancestry. Yet the line continues: Ruth the Moabitess becomes David's great-grandmother (Ruth 4), and in Matthew 1:5 Ruth is in Jesus' genealogy — a reversal of the polemic that the text itself allows.
- **Lot's wife (v26)** — fleeting reference. Why she looked back is not specified; later tradition fills in lingering attachment to the doomed city. The pillar of salt is etiological (explaining a landscape feature) and moral (illustrating the cost of attachment to condemned places).
