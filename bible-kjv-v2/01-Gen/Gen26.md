[1] And there was a famine in the land, besides the first famine that was in the days of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧. And ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ went to ⟦abimelech|Avimelech|Abimelech⟧ king of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧, to Gerar.

[15] And all the wells that the slaves of his father had dug in the days of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ his father, the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ stopped them up and filled them with dust.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And" kept** (throughout) — Hebrew וַ / וְ at the head of each clause; the chain of clauses is not turned into "so", "when", "then".
- **"dwell" → "abide"; "Sojourn"; "dwelt"** (v2, 3, 6, 17) — three Hebrew verbs, three English ones: שָׁכַן "abide" (v2), גּוּר "sojourn", to live as a stranger without land of one's own (v3), יָשַׁב "dwell" (v6, 17).
- **"perform the oath" → "establish the oath"** (v3) — Hebrew וַהֲקִמֹתִי, "make stand".
- **"seed" kept** (v3, 4, 24) — Hebrew זֶרַע *zera*, a singular collective noun: "seed", not "offspring" or "descendants".
- **"shall … be blessed" → "shall bless themselves"** (v4) — Hebrew וְהִתְבָּרֲכוּ, the hitpael (reflexive) stem, as in 22:18: the nations will use this seed's name when they bless themselves. The form can also be read as a passive, "shall be blessed", which is what the niphal says in 12:3; 18:18; 28:14.
- **"obeyed my voice" → "listened to my voice"** (v5) — Hebrew שָׁמַע בְּקֹלִי, "heard / listened to my voice"; Hebrew has no separate word "obey". "Because" is עֵקֶב אֲשֶׁר, "as a consequence that", as in 22:18.
- **"my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws"** (v5) — four Hebrew nouns, four renderings: מִשְׁמֶרֶת "charge" (what is given to be kept; from the verb "kept" that governs it: "he kept my keeping"), מִצְוֹת "commandments", חֻקּוֹת "statutes", תּוֹרֹת "laws", the plural of *torah*, "instruction, law".
- **"asked him of his wife" → "asked about his wife"** (v7) — the Hebrew has no "him".
- **"for he feared to say, 'My wife' — 'lest …'"** (v7) — the Hebrew passes from the narrator's words into Isaac's own thought without a word such as "thinking". "Good in appearance" is טוֹבַת מַרְאֶה, word for word.
- **"when he had been there a long time" → "when the days had grown long for him there"** (v8) — Hebrew כִּי אָרְכוּ־לוֹ שָׁם הַיָּמִים, word for word.
- **"sporting with" → "laughing with"** (v8) — Hebrew מְצַחֵק אֵת, the same participle as in 21:9 and the root of Isaac's own name (*Yitzchaq*, "he laughs"): *Yitzchaq metzacheq*. The piel of this verb covers laughing, jesting (19:14), playing (Exod 32:6) and making sport of someone (39:14, 17). Here, with "his wife" and seen by one who then knows she is his wife, it means the fond play of a husband and wife; the word itself says no more than "laughing / playing with".
- **"of a surety" → "Surely"; "Because I said"** (v9) — Hebrew אַךְ הִנֵּה, "surely, behold". אָמַרְתִּי, "I said", is the Hebrew way of saying "I thought"; kept.
- **"might lightly have lien" → "A little more and … would have lain"** (v10) — Hebrew כִּמְעַט שָׁכַב, "as a little, (and) he had lain": it almost happened.
- **"brought guiltiness" → "brought guilt"** (v10) — Hebrew אָשָׁם *asham*, guilt (also the name of the guilt offering).
- **"this man or his wife"** (v11) — the Hebrew has "and" (וּבְאִשְׁתּוֹ); English needs "or" to say that touching either one is meant.
- **"shall surely be put to death"** (v11) — Hebrew מוֹת יוּמָת, literally "dying he shall be put to death", the infinitive absolute with its verb; so also "We have surely seen" (v28), רָאוֹ רָאִינוּ, "seeing we have seen".
- **"received … an hundredfold" → "found … a hundredfold"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיִּמְצָא … מֵאָה שְׁעָרִים, literally "found a hundred measures", that is, a hundred for each one sown. The noun שְׁעָרִים in this sense stands only here.
- **"waxed great … went forward, and grew until he became very great" → "became great … went on becoming greater and greater until he was very great"** (v13) — the Hebrew uses the one root גדל "be great" three times; no word for "rich" or "wealthy" is in the verse.
- **"possession of flocks, and possession of herds, and great store of servants" → "livestock of the flock and livestock of the herd and a great body of slaves"** (v14) — Hebrew מִקְנֶה "livestock", צֹאן "flock" (sheep and goats), בָּקָר "herd" (cattle); עֲבֻדָּה רַבָּה is a collective noun from עֶבֶד "slave" (only here and Job 1:3).
- **"servants" → "slaves"** (v15, 19, 25, 32) — Hebrew עֶבֶד; "my servant" in v24 (עַבְדִּי, of Abraham before Yahweh) is the same word.
- **"earth" → "dust"** (v15) — Hebrew עָפָר.
- **"thou art much mightier than we"** (v16) — Hebrew עָצַמְתָּ מִמֶּנּוּ מְאֹד; the מִן can also mean "too mighty for us".
- **"valley" → "wadi"** (v17, 19) — Hebrew נַחַל *nachal*, a stream bed that is dry for most of the year.
- **"digged again"** (v18) — Hebrew וַיָּשָׁב … וַיַּחְפֹּר, literally "returned and dug", the Hebrew way of saying "dug again". "That they had dug": the Hebrew verb has no named subject.
- **"springing water" → "living water"** (v19) — Hebrew מַיִם חַיִּים *mayim chayyim*, word for word: running water from a spring, not standing water.
- **"herdmen … did strive" → "shepherds … contended"; "strove with him" → "quarreled with him"** (v20–22) — Hebrew רֹעֶה "shepherd"; רִיב "contend" (v20, 21, 22) is a different verb from הִתְעַשְּׂקוּ (v20, only here), from which the well is named.
- **"Esek", "Sitnah", "Rehoboth"** (v20, 21, 22) — the verse gives the names only, as the Hebrew does. עֵשֶׂק *Eseq* means "quarrel"; שִׂטְנָה *Sitnah* "enmity, accusation" (the root of *satan*, "adversary"); רְחֹבוֹת *Rechovot* "broad places", from the verb in "has made room" (הִרְחִיב).
- **"the same night" → "in that night"** (v24) — Hebrew בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא.
- **"digged a well"** (v25) — here the verb is כָּרָה, another word than חָפַר "dig" in the rest of the chapter (v32 uses חָפַר of this same well); English has no second plain word.
- **"one of his friends" → "his friend"** (v26) — Hebrew מֵרֵעֵהוּ, the noun מֵרֵעַ "friend, companion" (Judg 14:20; Prov 19:7). It may be a title at court (the king's friend, cf. 2 Sam 15:37), but the word says only "friend".
- **"seeing ye hate me" → "when you hate me"** (v27) — Hebrew וְאַתֶּם שְׂנֵאתֶם אֹתִי, "and you, you hate me".
- **"Let there be now an oath"** (v28) — Hebrew אָלָה *alah*, an oath that calls a curse on the one who breaks it; a different word from "oath" (שְׁבֻעָה) in v3. "Now" is the particle נָא.
- **"make a covenant" → "cut a covenant"** (v28) — Hebrew וְנִכְרְתָה בְרִית, word for word.
- **"That thou wilt do us no hurt" → "that you will do us no evil"** (v29) — Hebrew אִם־תַּעֲשֵׂה עִמָּנוּ רָעָה, literally "if you do evil with us …", the oath formula: the curse on the one who does it is left unsaid. רָעָה "evil" stands against טוֹב "good" in the same verse.
- **"in peace"** (v29, 31) — Hebrew בְּשָׁלוֹם *be-shalom*.
- **"sware one to another" → "swore each man to his brother"** (v31) — Hebrew אִישׁ לְאָחִיו, the Hebrew way of saying "to one another".
- **"Shebah"** (v33) — Hebrew שִׁבְעָה *Shivah*: the word means "seven" and sounds like "oath" (שְׁבֻעָה, v3; "swore", v31). Hence *Beer Sheva*, "well of the oath / well of seven" (21:31).
- **"forty years old"** (v34) — Hebrew בֶּן־אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה, literally "a son of forty years". "As wife" is singular in Hebrew (אִשָּׁה), said once for both women.
- **"grief of mind" → "bitterness of spirit"** (v35) — Hebrew מֹרַת רוּחַ *morat ruach*.

### Notes

- **Third "wife-sister" episode** — Isaac repeats his father's deception (12 and 20). The narrator's chain of these stories (three in all) shows a family pattern of fear-driven dissembling across two generations.
- **Wells and covenantal presence** — water in arid land is survival; the chapter's three named wells (Esek, Sitnah, Rehoboth) trace Isaac's progress from contested space to promised space. The final Beersheba well formalizes covenant with the neighbors.
- **Esau's Hittite wives (v34–35)** — sets up ch 27. The pattern Abraham established (do not take wives from the Canaanites, 24:3) Esau violates; the bitterness prepares Rebekah to act decisively for Jacob.
