## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"river" → "Nile"** (v1, 2, 3, 17, 18) — Hebrew יְאֹר *Ye'or*. Egyptian-loan word specifically for the Nile (from Egyptian *iteru*). Unlike the generic *nahar* (river). Clarified.
- **"kine" → "cows"** (v2, 3, 4, 18, 19, 20, 26, 27) — "Kine" is archaic plural of "cow."
- **"well favoured / ill favoured" → "beautiful / ugly of form"** (v2, 3, 18, 19, 21) — Hebrew יְפוֹת מַרְאֶה / רָעוֹת מַרְאֶה = lit. "beautiful of sight / evil of sight."
- **"fatfleshed / leanfleshed" → "plump / gaunt of flesh"** (v2, 3, 18, 20) — Hebrew בְּרִיאֹת / דַּקּוֹת *beri'ot / daqqot*. Modernized.
- **"a meadow" → "the reed grass"** (v2, 18) — Hebrew בָּאָחוּ *ba-achu*. *Achu* = riverside reed / papyrus plants. Egyptian-loan word (Egyptian *akh*). Specifically the wetland vegetation of the Nile banks. Not any meadow.
- **"corn" → "grain"** (v5, 22, 35, 49, 57) — Hebrew dagan / bar. "Corn" in 1611 English = grain (wheat, barley); modern English "corn" = maize (American) or wheat (British). Clarified.
- **"rank" → "plump / full"** (v5, 7, 22, 24) — Hebrew בְּרִיאוֹת / מְלֵאוֹת *beri'ot / mele'ot*. "Rank" in 1611 English = lush / full; modern English = offensive-smelling.
- **"blasted" → "scorched"** (v6, 23, 27) — Hebrew שְׁדוּפוֹת *shedufot* = burned / withered (by hot east wind — the sirocco / *hamsin*). Modernized.
- **"magicians" → "diviner-priests"** (v8, 24) — Hebrew חַרְטֻמִּים *chartummim*. Egyptian-loan term (from *ḥry-tp* "chief") for professional priestly diviners / lector-priests who handled oracles, dream-interpretation, and rituals. "Magicians" misleads toward stage-magic; the Hebrew denotes a respected priestly class.
- **"spirit was troubled" (v8)** — Hebrew וַתִּפָּעֶם רוּחוֹ *wa-tippa'em rucho* = "his spirit was agitated / beaten." Visceral distress.
- **"an answer of peace" → "a favorable answer"** (v16) — Hebrew יַעֲנֶה אֶת־שְׁלוֹם פַּרְעֹה *ya'aneh et-shelom par'oh*. Either "answer [concerning] Pharaoh's peace / welfare" or "a peace-answer" = favorable / settling response.
- **"Spirit of God" → "Spirit of *Elohim*"** (v38) — Hebrew רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים *Ruach Elohim*. A pagan pharaoh recognizes Joseph's divine endowment — striking in its generosity.
- **"arrayed him in vestures of fine linen" → "clothed him in garments of fine linen"** (v42) — Hebrew שֵׁשׁ *shesh* = fine linen, the Egyptian royal-priestly fabric.
- **"Bow the knee" → "Avrekh!"** (v43) — Hebrew אַבְרֵךְ *avrekh*. Obscure Egyptian-loan title or command. Possible derivations:
  1. Egyptian *ib-r.k* ("heart-pay-attention") = "make way!"
  2. Hebrew etymology: "bow the knee" (*barakh*), traditional.
  3. A title for a high Egyptian official.
  Hebrew text preserves the foreign word untranslated; translation keeps the transliteration (because the original sound itself is part of what marks Joseph's elevation into Egyptian court life).
- **"Zaphnath-paaneah" → "Tsaphenat-Paneach"** (v45) — Hebrew צָפְנַת פַּעְנֵחַ *Tsaphenat-Pa'neach*. Egyptian name meaning uncertain: possibly "the god speaks and he lives" / "revealer of hidden things." Joseph's Egyptian identity.
- **"priest of On" (v45, 50)** — Hebrew כֹּהֵן אֹן *kohen On*. On = Heliopolis (Egyptian *Iunu*), center of Ra / sun-god cult. Joseph marries into the highest Egyptian priestly caste — a Hebraic-Egyptian syncretism that will later trouble Jewish interpreters; the text itself reports it without comment.
- **"Manasseh / Ephraim — Hebrew etymologies preserved** (v51, 52):
  - *Menasheh* ← *nashah* "to make forget" — "*Elohim* has made me forget my hardship."
  - *Ephrayim* ← *parah* "to be fruitful" — "*Elohim* has made me fruitful."

### Notes

- **Joseph's political ascent** — from prison to second-in-Egypt in one day. The narrative compresses vast political change: Joseph is made vizier, granted royal seal, chariot, new name, and aristocratic marriage. Historical setting plausibly Second Intermediate Period (Hyksos rule, c. 1650–1550 BCE), during which a Semitic foreigner could plausibly rise to such power; this is disputed dating.
- **Joseph's Egyptianization** — name, wife, dress, office: Joseph becomes fully Egyptian on the surface. The narrative holds this tension throughout (his later self-identification to his brothers will be in Hebrew, with theological speech about Yahweh's providence).
- **Manasseh / Ephraim's names** — Joseph distances himself from his Hebrew origins ("*Elohim* has made me forget all my hardship and all my father's house" — including his father!). The text notes this ambivalence without endorsing it; the reconciliation of chs 45–46 partly resolves the amnesia.
