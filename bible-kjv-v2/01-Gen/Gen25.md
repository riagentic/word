# Genesis 25

[1] Abraham took another wife, whose name was Keturah.

[2] She bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.

[3] Jokshan fathered Sheba and Dedan. The sons of Dedan were the Asshurim, the Letushim, and the Leummim.

[4] The sons of Midian: Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah. All these were the children of Keturah.

[5] Abraham gave all that he had to Isaac.

[6] But to the sons of his concubines Abraham gave gifts, and while he was still alive he sent them away from his son Isaac, eastward to the east country.

[7] These are the days of the years of Abraham's life which he lived: 175 years.

[8] Abraham breathed his last and died in a good old age, an old man, full of years; and he was gathered to his people.

[9] His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah, in the field of Ephron son of Zohar the Hittite, which is east of Mamre,

[10] the field that Abraham purchased from the sons of Heth. There Abraham was buried, with Sarah his wife.

[11] After the death of Abraham, *Elohim* blessed Isaac his son; and Isaac settled by Beer-lahai-roi.

[12] These are the generations of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's slave-girl, bore to Abraham.

[13] These are the names of the sons of Ishmael, by their names according to their generations: the firstborn of Ishmael, Nebaioth; and Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,

[14] Mishma, Dumah, Massa,

[15] Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah.

[16] These are the sons of Ishmael and these are their names, by their villages and by their encampments; twelve chieftains according to their peoples.

[17] These are the years of the life of Ishmael: 137 years. He breathed his last and died, and was gathered to his people.

[18] They settled from Havilah to Shur, which is opposite Egypt, in the direction of Asshur. He settled in the face of all his brothers.

[19] These are the generations of Isaac, Abraham's son: Abraham fathered Isaac.

[20] Isaac was 40 years old when he took Rebekah, the daughter of Bethuel the Aramean of Paddan-Aram, sister of Laban the Aramean, to be his wife.

[21] Isaac entreated Yahweh for his wife, because she was barren; and Yahweh granted his entreaty, and Rebekah his wife conceived.

[22] The children struggled together within her, and she said, "If it is so, why this to me?" And she went to inquire of Yahweh.

[23] Yahweh said to her,

"Two nations are in your womb,
and two peoples from within you shall be separated.
The one people shall be stronger than the other people,
and the elder shall serve the younger."

[24] When her days to give birth were fulfilled, behold, there were twins in her womb.

[25] The first came out red, all over like a hairy mantle, so they called his name Esau.

[26] Afterward his brother came out, with his hand grasping Esau's heel; so his name was called Jacob. Isaac was 60 years old when she bore them.

[27] When the boys grew up, Esau was a skillful hunter, a man of the field; Jacob was a quiet man, living in tents.

[28] Isaac loved Esau, because game was in his mouth; but Rebekah loved Jacob.

[29] Once Jacob was boiling a stew, when Esau came in from the field, exhausted.

[30] Esau said to Jacob, "Let me gulp down some of that red stuff — for I am exhausted." (Therefore his name was called Edom.)

[31] Jacob said, "Sell me your birthright, today."

[32] Esau said, "Look, I am about to die; what use is a birthright to me?"

[33] Jacob said, "Swear to me, today." So he swore to him and sold his birthright to Jacob.

[34] Then Jacob gave Esau bread and stew of lentils; and he ate and drank, and rose and went his way. So Esau despised his birthright.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"gave up the ghost" → "breathed his last"** (v8, 17) — Hebrew גָּוַע *gava* = expire / breathe out the last. "Gave up the ghost" is archaic KJV idiom.
- **"full of years" (v8)** — Hebrew וְשָׂבֵעַ *we-save'a* = sated / satisfied. A life full and enough, not just long.
- **"gathered to his people" (v8, 17)** — Hebrew וַיֵּאָסֶף אֶל־עַמָּיו. Idiom for death as rejoining ancestors in Sheol — not merely burial (see v9 where the burial is a separate act).
- **"castles" → "encampments"** (v16) — Hebrew טִירֹתָם *tirotam*. Tent-camps / nomadic settlement-enclosures, not fortified castles.
- **"twelve princes" → "twelve chieftains"** (v16) — as in 17:20.
- **"before Egypt… toward Assyria" → "opposite Egypt… in the direction of Asshur"** (v18) — Hebrew geographic terminology: *al-pene* = "facing / opposite"; *be-vo'aka* = "as you come / in the direction of."
- **"in the presence of all his brethren" → "in the face of all his brothers"** (v18) — echoes 16:12 (Ishmael's prophesied destiny). Same ambiguity: "alongside" vs. "in defiance of."
- **"Syrian / Padan-aram" → "Aramean / Paddan-Aram"** (v20) — Hebrew אֲרַמִּי / פַּדַּן אֲרָם *Arammi / Paddan Aram*. "Syrian" is the Greek/Latin transmission; the original peoples are Arameans. Paddan-Aram = the plain / flat country of Aram (Upper Mesopotamia, near Haran).
- **"intreated" → "entreated"** (v21) — modernized spelling.
- **"why am I thus?" → "why this to me?"** (v22) — Hebrew לָמָּה זֶּה אָנֹכִי *lamah zzeh anokhi* — compressed, obscure. "Why then this — me?" Rebekah's existential lament; something is wrong with her pregnancy or with her.
- **"the elder shall serve the younger" (v23)** — Hebrew וְרַב יַעֲבֹד צָעִיר *we-rav ya'avod tsa'ir*. Reversal of ancient primogeniture. Note: syntactically, Hebrew lacks an object marker — the sentence is technically ambiguous ("the greater shall serve the lesser" or "the greater, the lesser shall serve"). Traditional reading (elder serves younger) is supported by the subsequent Esau-Jacob narrative; the rare syntactic ambiguity has invited the occasional reversal-reading.
- **"hairy garment" → "hairy mantle"** (v25) — Hebrew אַדֶּרֶת שֵׂעָר *adderet se'ar*. *Adderet* = official / ceremonial mantle / prophet's cloak (cf. Elijah's mantle, 1 Kgs 19:13). Esau born looking like a wild-man wearing an animal-skin cloak.
- **"Esau" — wordplay** (v25) — Hebrew עֵשָׂו *Esav*. Possibly from שֵׂעָר *se'ar* ("hair"): Esav from Se'ar via metathesis. Also linked to *asah* "made / done" — a finished-looking / fully-grown baby.
- **"Jacob" — etymology preserved** (v26) — Hebrew יַעֲקֹב *Ya'aqov* from עָקֵב *aqev* = "heel." Jacob = "heel-grasper," which will come to mean "trickster / supplanter" (cf. 27:36). Wordplay shown.
- **"cunning hunter" → "skillful hunter"** (v27) — Hebrew יֹדֵעַ צַיִד *yode'a tsayid* = "one who knew hunting." "Cunning" in modern English = sly; Hebrew = skilled.
- **"plain man" → "quiet man"** (v27) — Hebrew אִישׁ תָּם *ish tam*. *Tam* = whole / complete / blameless / simple (same root as *tamim* used of Noah). "Plain" in KJV meant unpretentious / unadorned; modern "plain" = ordinary, losing the moral weight. "Quiet" captures contrast with Esau's wild energy; *tam* has moral overtones too.
- **"because he did eat of his venison" → "because game was in his mouth"** (v28) — Hebrew כִּי־צַיִד בְּפִיו *ki-tsayid be-fiv*. Literally "because hunt / game was in his mouth" — he enjoyed the taste of Esau's catches. Preserved literally for its earthy phrasing.
- **"sod pottage" → "was boiling a stew"** (v29) — Hebrew וַיָּזֶד יַעֲקֹב נָזִיד *wa-yazed Ya'aqov nazid*. *Nazid* = boiled food / stew. "Pottage" is archaic.
- **"red pottage… the same red… Edom"** (v30) — Hebrew הָלְעִיטֵנִי נָא מִן־הָאָדֹם הָאָדֹם הַזֶּה *hal'iteni-na min-ha-adom ha-adom ha-zeh*. *Ha'it* = stuff / cram (in mouth); *adom* = red. Repetition: "the red the red" — Esau's blurt-words as he gasps. Name *Edom* (ancient nation descended from Esau) etymologized from *adom* ("red"). Preserved with wordplay gloss.
- **"despised" (v34)** — Hebrew בָּזָה *bazah*. Treated with contempt / thought nothing of. The narrator's moral verdict on Esau's exchange.

### Notes

- **Abraham's late marriage** — Keturah's sons become eponymous ancestors of Arabian tribes (Midian, Sheba, Dedan). Gen 25 ends the Abraham cycle by placing his full legacy: Isaac (covenant), Ishmael (promised nation), and the Keturah line (Arab peoples).
- **Esau and Jacob** — set up by birth (v22–26) as perpetual rivals / polar opposites. Esau the red, hairy, hunter / Jacob the smooth, tent-dwelling schemer. Later Israelite ethnography reads Esau as Edom and Jacob as Israel — the contrast is national, not only personal.
- **Birthright sale (v29–34)** — transactional absurdity: an eternal status-right (inheriting the covenant blessing) traded for a single bowl of food. The narrative condemns Esau ("despised his birthright") while also raising questions about Jacob's hard bargaining.
