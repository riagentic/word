## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Adam" → "the human"** (v1, 25) — Hebrew הָאָדָם *ha-adam* still with article in v1 and v25. The proper name "Adam" without article stabilizes in Gen 5. (Scholars differ on exact point of transition; KJV treats v1 as proper name, but the Hebrew still marks it as the collective / articular form.)
- **"knew Eve his wife"** (v1) — idiom retained; "knew" = sexual union in biblical Hebrew (יָדַע *yada*, intimate knowing).
- **"I have gotten a man from the LORD" → "I have acquired (*qaniti*) a man with Yahweh"** (v1) — Hebrew קָנִיתִי אִישׁ אֶת־יְהוָה *qaniti ish et-Yhwh*. Wordplay: Cain = *Qayin*, from root *qanah* (acquire / create / produce). The particle אֶת *et* before Yahweh is ambiguous: normally the direct-object marker ("I have acquired a man — Yahweh!" — blasphemous or messianic), but here more likely the preposition "with" ("with Yahweh's help"). Translation picks the more grammatically standard reading ("with Yahweh").
- **"Cain / Abel" — Hebrew etymology made explicit** (v1–2) — *Qayin* ("acquired") and *Hevel* ("breath / vapor / fleeting thing" — same word Ecclesiastes uses for "vanity"). Abel's name foreshadows his brief life.
- **"in process of time" → "in the course of time"** (v3) — Hebrew מִקֵּץ יָמִים *miqqets yamim* = at the end of some days. Modernized.
- **"firstlings" → "firstborn"** (v4) — Hebrew בְּכֹרוֹת *bekhorot*. Modernized.
- **"fat thereof" → "fat portions"** (v4) — Hebrew חֶלְבֵהֶן *chelvehen*. The choice parts — sacrificial fat was considered the best portion offered to the deity (cf. Lev 3).
- **"had respect unto" → "looked with favor on"** (v4, 5) — Hebrew וַיִּשַׁע *va-yisha* (from שָׁעָה *sha'ah* = look, gaze, regard with favor). "Had respect" in modern English means social deference, which misses the sense; the Hebrew means the divine gaze was directed favorably toward the offering.
- **"wroth" → "angry"** (v5, 6) — archaic → modern.
- **"countenance fell" → "face fell"** (v5, 6) — Hebrew פָּנָיו נָפְלוּ *panaw naflu*. Literal.
- **"shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him" → "is there not lifting up? But if you do not do well, sin crouches at the door; its desire is for you, but you must rule over it"** (v7) — Hebrew: הֲלוֹא אִם־תֵּיטִיב שְׂאֵת וְאִם לֹא תֵיטִיב לַפֶּתַח חַטָּאת רֹבֵץ וְאֵלֶיךָ תְּשׁוּקָתוֹ וְאַתָּה תִּמְשָׁל־בּוֹ. The first clause is famously obscure. שְׂאֵת *se'et* = "lifting up" — could mean acceptance (face lifted up, in contrast to Cain's fallen face), or forgiveness, or a raising of the offering. Rendered literally. "Sin crouches (*rovets*)" — participle, picturing a predatory animal lying in wait at the door. Its "desire" (*teshuqah*) and Cain's "rule" (*mashal*) — identical vocabulary to 3:16, which strongly suggests that 3:16 also describes a power struggle rather than romantic longing.
- **"Cain talked with Abel" → "Cain said to his brother Abel, 'Let us go out to the field'"** (v8) — MT (Masoretic Text) has a gap: "Cain said to his brother Abel, and when they were in the field…" — no content for what Cain said. Samaritan Pentateuch, LXX, Peshitta, and Vulgate all include "Let us go out to the field" (נֵלְכָה הַשָּׂדֶה). Almost certainly original; a scribal loss in MT. Restored here.
- **"slew" → "killed"** (v8) — modernized.
- **"voice of thy brother's blood" → "voice of your brother's blood"** (v10) — Hebrew קוֹל דְּמֵי אָחִיךָ *qol deme achikha*. *Deme* is plural ("bloods"), possibly indicating "shed blood" specifically. Literal rendering kept.
- **"earth" → "ground"** (v11, 12, 14) — Hebrew אֲדָמָה *adamah* = ground / soil. Distinct from אֶרֶץ *eretz* (land / earth). Consistent with the human / ground wordplay (*adam / adamah*). KJV varies; clarified.
- **"a fugitive and a vagabond" → "a ceaseless wanderer"** (v12, 14) — Hebrew נָע וָנָד *na wa-nad*. Two related verbs: "wandering and roaming" — hendiadys for a restless, rootless existence. KJV's doublet reads as two distinct conditions; Hebrew is one.
- **"Therefore whosoever slayeth Cain…" → "Not so — whoever kills Cain…"** (v15) — MT: לָכֵן *lakhen* = "therefore." LXX, Samaritan, Peshitta, Vulgate read לֹא כֵן *lo khen* = "not so." The MT "therefore" is difficult (why would killing Cain *because* he feared being killed be logical?); the "not so" reading fixes the logic: Yahweh denies Cain's expectation and promises sevenfold vengeance on his killer. Translated with "not so"; both readings are in play.
- **"set a mark" → "put a sign"** (v15) — Hebrew אוֹת *ot* = sign / mark. Nature not specified. "Mark of Cain" is traditional; the Hebrew word is the same used for the rainbow in 9:12 and other covenantal signs.
- **"presence" → "face"** (v16) — Hebrew מִפְּנֵי *mi-pene*. See 3:8.
- **"builded" → "was building"** (v17) — archaic form → Hebrew participle *boneh*.
- **"begat" → "fathered"** (v18) — Hebrew יָלַד *yalad* (hiphil) = caused to be born / fathered. "Begat" is archaic.
- **"such as dwell in tents, and of such as have cattle" → "those who live in tents and keep livestock"** (v20) — modernized; content unchanged.
- **"harp and organ" → "lyre and pipe"** (v21) — Hebrew כִּנּוֹר וְעוּגָב *kinnor we-'ugav*. *Kinnor* = lyre (stringed instrument); KJV "harp" is in the right family but wrong specific type. *'Ugav* = a pipe / flute (wind instrument); KJV "organ" is seriously wrong (the pipe organ is a medieval European invention).
- **"an instructer of every artificer in brass and iron" → "who forged all kinds of tools of bronze and iron"** (v22) — Hebrew לֹטֵשׁ כָּל־חֹרֵשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת וּבַרְזֶל *lotesh kol-choresh nechoshet u-varzel*. *Lotesh* = sharpener / forger; *choresh* = craftsman / smith; *nechoshet* = bronze / copper (not "brass" — brass is a copper-zinc alloy unknown in the ancient Near East before later periods; *nechoshet* = copper or bronze). KJV "brass" is anachronistic.
- **"slain a man to my wounding, and a young man to my hurt" → "killed a man for wounding me, a boy for striking me"** (v23) — Hebrew כִּי אִישׁ הָרַגְתִּי לְפִצְעִי וְיֶלֶד לְחַבֻּרָתִי. The preposition לְ *le* here means "in requital for" — the killing is Lamech's retaliation for being wounded. KJV "to my wounding" is obscure.
- **"seventy and sevenfold" → "seventy-sevenfold"** (v24) — numeric idiom modernized. (Note: the same idiom reappears in Matt 18:22 in Jesus' reversal of Lamech's vengeance formula.)
- **"Enos" → "Enosh"** (v26) — Hebrew אֱנוֹשׁ *Enosh* = "man / mortal." KJV transliteration slightly off. Preserved consonants.
- **"then began men to call upon the name of the LORD" → "then people began to call on the name of Yahweh"** (v26) — Hebrew אָז הוּחַל לִקְרֹא בְּשֵׁם יְהוָה *az huchal liqro b'shem Yahweh*. Impersonal passive: "it was begun to call on the name of Yahweh." *Huchal* sometimes read as "to profane" (root *chalal* II), producing the reverse meaning: "then the name of Yahweh was profaned" (rabbinic reading in Bereshit Rabbah). Traditional translation ("began to call") retained; alternative reading is ancient and worth noting.

### Notes

- **"Cain's wife" (v17)** — an old puzzle; text does not address it. The narrative assumes other people exist (cf. Cain's fear of being killed by "whoever finds me" in v14), but only names Adam's direct line. The early Genesis genealogies are selective, not exhaustive.
- **Lamech's song (v23–24)** — the first poem attributed to a human in the Bible. A boast of disproportionate vengeance: where Yahweh's protection on Cain was sevenfold, Lamech's self-imposed vengeance is seventy-sevenfold. Jesus inverts this formula for forgiveness in Matt 18:22.
