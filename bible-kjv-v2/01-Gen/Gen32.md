# Genesis 32

[1] ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ went on his way, and messengers of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ met him.

[2] When ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ saw them, he said, "This is ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧'s camp!" So he called the name of that place ⟦mahanaim|Machanayim|Mahanaim⟧ ("two camps").

[3] ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ sent messengers ahead of him to ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧ his brother in the land of Seir, the country of Edom.

[4] He commanded them, "Thus you shall say to my lord, to ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧: 'Thus says your servant ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧: I have sojourned with ⟦laban|Lavan|Laban⟧ and stayed until now.

[5] I have cattle, donkeys, flocks, and male and female slaves. And I have sent to tell my lord, that I may find favor in your sight.'"

[6] The messengers returned to ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧, saying, "We came to your brother ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧; and he is also coming to meet you, and 400 men are with him."

[7] Then ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ was greatly afraid and distressed; and he divided the people with him, and the flocks and herds and camels, into two companies,

[8] thinking, "If ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧ comes to the one company and strikes it, then the company that is left shall escape."

[9] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ said, "O ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of my father ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of my father ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ who said to me, 'Return to your country and to your kindred, and I will deal well with you':

[11] Please deliver me from the hand of my brother, from the hand of ⟦esau|Esav|Esau⟧; for I fear him, lest he come and strike me down — the mothers with the children.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"angels of God" → "messengers of *Elohim*"** (v1) — as elsewhere.
- **"God's host" → "*Elohim*'s camp"** (v2) — Hebrew מַחֲנֵה אֱלֹהִים *machaneh Elohim*. *Machaneh* = army / camp / encampment. Jacob meets a second camp in addition to his own — hence the place-name Mahanaim ("two camps").
- **"sent messengers" (v3)** — Hebrew מַלְאָכִים *mal'akhim* — same word as v1 "messengers of *Elohim*." Deliberate echo: Jacob surrounded by divine *mal'akhim*, and Jacob sends human *mal'akhim*.
- **"mercies and truth" → "lovingkindnesses and faithfulness"** (v10) — Hebrew חֲסָדִים … אֱמֶת *chasadim … emet*, as in 24:27.
- **"the mother with the children" → "the mothers with the children"** (v11) — Hebrew אֵם עַל־בָּנִים *em al-banim*. Technical phrase from ancient Near Eastern warfare referring to the total annihilation of enemy lineage (mothers and their children). "Mothers" clarifies collective use.
- **"I will appease him with the present" / "lift up my face" (v20)** — Hebrew אֲכַפְּרָה פָנָיו … יִשָּׂא פָנָי. Deliberate fourfold face-language: "I will cover his face… see his face… he will lift my face." *Kaphar* ("cover / appease / atone") is the later sacrificial-atonement root. Jacob uses atonement-language for his encounter with Esau — dense foreshadowing.
- **"Jabbok" — etymology** (v22) — Hebrew יַבֹּק *Yabboq* — a tributary of the Jordan. Phonetically echoes Jacob's name (*Ya'aqov*) and the wrestling verb (*ya'aveq*, v24, from *avaq*). Name of place, Jacob, and wrestling all share triliteral consonants — lost in English but central to the Hebrew text.
- **"eleven sons" → "eleven children"** (v22) — Hebrew יְלָדָיו *yeladav* = his children. Includes Dinah (see 30:21), though she is feminine; most English translations say "sons." "Children" is more accurate.
- **"there wrestled a man with him" → "a man wrestled with him"** (v24) — Hebrew וַיֵּאָבֵק אִישׁ עִמּוֹ *wa-ye'aveq ish immo*. Verb *avaq* = to grapple / wrestle (from the "dust" they raise). Hapax legomenon here and v25. The man is not identified until v30 (Jacob's own identification as divine).
- **"hollow of his thigh" → "socket of Jacob's thigh"** (v25, 32) — Hebrew כַּף־יֶרֶכוֹ *kaph-yerekho*. *Kaph* = hollow / palm / socket; *yarekh* = hip / thigh / loin. The hip-socket, specifically.
- **"out of joint" → "dislocated"** (v25) — Hebrew וַתֵּקַע *wa-teqa*. Modernized medical term.
- **"as a prince hast thou power" → "you have striven"** (v28) — Hebrew שָׂרִיתָ *sarita* from שָׂרָה *sarah* = "to strive / contend / persist." The name *Yisra'el* = "El strives / one who strives with El / El rules." The etymology preserved. KJV's "as a prince" follows the Hebrew שָׂר *sar* (prince / ruler) as alternative root-derivation; the verb-based reading is more secure.
- **"Peniel / Penuel" — gloss** (v30, 31) — two spellings of the same name, פְּנִיאֵל / פְּנוּאֵל *Peni-El / Penu-El* = "face of El." Jacob gives the place-name to commemorate seeing *Elohim*'s face.
- **"halted upon his thigh" → "limped because of his thigh"** (v31) — Hebrew וְהוּא צֹלֵעַ עַל־יְרֵכוֹ. The wound is permanent; Jacob limps away from the encounter.
- **"the sinew which shrank" → "the sinew of the thigh-socket / hip"** (v32) — Hebrew גִּיד הַנָּשֶׁה *gid ha-nasheh*. Meaning of *nasheh* disputed: "that shrank" (traditional); or "that was dislodged / touched"; or a nerve (sciatic) or tendon. The food-taboo reflex — observant Jews to this day remove the sciatic nerve from kosher meat (tradition of *nikur*). Specifics of the anatomy: likely the sciatic nerve or adjacent tendon at the hip.

### Notes

- **Who did Jacob wrestle?** Text calls it "a man" (v24), then Jacob says "I have seen *Elohim* face to face" (v30); Hos 12:4 says "he strove with an angel." Interpretations:
  1. Yahweh himself in theophanic form.
  2. A divine messenger (angel).
  3. Esau's guardian spirit (rabbinic tradition, cast as the angel of Esau).
  4. Jacob's own conscience / Esau-fear externalized.
  The text leaves the identity hidden — when Jacob asks the name (v29), the answer is withheld.
- **The rename** — Jacob ("heel-grasper / supplanter") becomes Israel ("striver with God"). The old name of dishonest cunning replaced by an honorable struggler's name. The blessing secured here is direct from God, not stolen (contrast 27). Yet Jacob limps — the blessing costs him something permanent.
- **Climax of the Jacob cycle** — Jacob spends the next day meeting Esau (ch 33), and the reconciliation succeeds. The wrestling foreshadows the reunion: if Jacob survives the divine encounter, he can survive Esau.
