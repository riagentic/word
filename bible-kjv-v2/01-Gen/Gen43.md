# Genesis 43

[1] The famine was severe in the land.

[2] When they had finished eating the grain that they had brought from Egypt, their father said to them, "Go again; buy us a little food."

[3] But Judah said to him, "The man solemnly warned us, saying, 'You shall not see my face unless your brother is with you.'

[4] If you will send our brother with us, we will go down and buy you food.

[5] But if you will not send him, we will not go down; for the man said to us, 'You shall not see my face unless your brother is with you.'"

[6] Israel said, "Why did you treat me so badly by telling the man that you had another brother?"

[7] They said, "The man questioned us carefully about ourselves and our kindred, saying, 'Is your father still alive? Have you another brother?' And we told him according to what he asked. Could we possibly have known that he would say, 'Bring your brother down'?"

[8] Then Judah said to Israel his father, "Send the boy with me, and let us arise and go, that we may live and not die — both we and you and our little ones.

[9] I myself will be surety for him; of my hand you shall require him. If I do not bring him back to you and set him before you, then let me bear the blame forever.

[10] For if we had not delayed, surely by now we could have returned twice."

[11] Then Israel their father said to them, "If it must be so, then do this: take some of the choice produce of the land in your vessels and carry down a present to the man — a little balm and a little honey, spices and myrrh-resin, pistachios and almonds.

[12] Take double the silver in your hand; and carry back in your hand the silver that was returned in the mouth of your sacks — perhaps it was an oversight.

[13] Take also your brother, and arise, go again to the man.

[14] May Almighty *Elohim* grant you mercy before the man, that he may release to you your other brother, and Benjamin. As for me, if I am bereaved, I am bereaved."

[15] So the men took this present; and they took double the silver in their hand, and Benjamin, and arose and went down to Egypt, and stood before Joseph.

[16] When Joseph saw Benjamin with them, he said to the steward of his house, "Bring these men into the house, slaughter an animal and prepare it, for these men shall dine with me at noon."

[17] The man did as Joseph said and brought the men to Joseph's house.

[18] The men were afraid because they had been brought to Joseph's house, and they said, "It is because of the silver that was returned in our sacks at the first time that we have been brought in — to roll upon us and fall upon us and take us as slaves, with our donkeys."

[19] So they approached the steward of Joseph's house and spoke to him at the entrance of the house,

[20] and said, "Oh, my lord, we really did come down the first time to buy food.

[21] And when we came to the lodging place and opened our sacks, behold, each man's silver was in the mouth of his sack — our silver in full weight. So we have brought it back in our hand.

[22] And we have brought other silver down in our hand to buy food. We do not know who put our silver in our sacks."

[23] He said, "Peace be to you; do not be afraid. Your *Elohim* and the *Elohim* of your father has put treasure in your sacks for you. I received your silver." Then he brought Simeon out to them.

[24] The man brought the men into Joseph's house, gave them water, and they washed their feet; and he gave their donkeys fodder.

[25] They prepared the present for Joseph's coming at noon, for they had heard that they would eat bread there.

[26] When Joseph came home, they brought into the house the present that was in their hand to him, and bowed themselves to him to the ground.

[27] He asked them about their welfare, and said, "Is your father well, the old man of whom you spoke? Is he still alive?"

[28] They said, "Your servant, our father, is well; he is still alive." And they bowed their heads and prostrated themselves.

[29] He lifted up his eyes and saw his brother Benjamin, his mother's son, and said, "Is this your youngest brother, of whom you spoke to me?" And he said, "May *Elohim* be gracious to you, my son."

[30] Then Joseph hurried out, for his inward parts were stirred over his brother, and he sought a place to weep. He entered his inner chamber and wept there.

[31] Then he washed his face and came out; and controlling himself, he said, "Serve the meal."

[32] They served him by himself, and them by themselves, and the Egyptians who ate with him by themselves, because the Egyptians could not eat bread with the Hebrews; for that is an abomination to the Egyptians.

[33] They sat before him, the firstborn according to his birthright, and the youngest according to his youth; and the men looked at one another in astonishment.

[34] Joseph sent portions from before himself to them, but Benjamin's portion was five times as much as any of theirs. So they drank and became merry with him.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"sore" → "severe"** (v1) — modernized.
- **"did solemnly protest" → "solemnly warned"** (v3) — Hebrew הָעֵד הֵעִד *ha'ed he'id*. Infinitive-absolute construction intensifying *ud* (to bear witness / warn). "Protest" in modern English = complain; Hebrew means legal warning.
- **"straitly" → "carefully"** (v7) — archaic → modern.
- **"of the best fruits in the land in your vessels" → "some of the choice produce of the land in your vessels"** (v11) — Hebrew מִזִּמְרַת הָאָרֶץ *mi-zimrat ha-aretz*. *Zimrah* = song / best / praise (unclear here; possibly "best / praised products"). Translated "choice produce."
- **"spices, and myrrh, nuts, and almonds" → "spices and myrrh-resin, pistachios and almonds"** (v11) — Hebrew נְכֹאת וָלֹט בָּטְנִים וּשְׁקֵדִים. *Nekhot* = gum (see 37:25); *lot* = myrrh-resin / ladanum; *botnim* = pistachio nuts (hapax); *sheqedim* = almonds. Trade-goods of the Levant — cruel irony: they are sending to the man they now depend on the same trade-goods that the caravan to which they sold Joseph carried (37:25).
- **"God Almighty" → "Almighty *Elohim*"** (v14) — as in 17:1.
- **"ruler of his house" → "steward of his house"** (v16, 19) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר עַל־בֵּיתוֹ *asher al-beito* = "the one over his house" = majordomo / household steward.
- **"seek occasion against us" → "roll upon us"** (v18) — Hebrew לְהִתְגֹּלֵל עָלֵינוּ *le-hitgolel aleynu* = "to roll themselves on us / fall upon us." Hostile attack idiom.
- **"the inn" → "the lodging place"** (v21) — as in 42:27.
- **"bowels did yearn upon his brother" → "inward parts were stirred over his brother"** (v30) — Hebrew כִּי־נִכְמְרוּ רַחֲמָיו *ki-nikhmeru rachamav*. *Rachamim* = compassion / mercies, literally "wombs" (feminine-affective imagery of maternal compassion). *Nikhmar* = became warm / was stirred. KJV literal "bowels" is archaic English idiom for the seat of compassion.
- **"abomination" (v32)** — Hebrew תּוֹעֵבָה *to'evah*. Ritual disgust / unapproachability. Egyptian caste-ritual separation from foreigners is historically attested; shepherd-Hebrews would have been especially unclean to Egyptian temple-purity standards.
- **"marvelled" → "looked at one another in astonishment"** (v33) — Hebrew וַיִּתְמְהוּ הָאֲנָשִׁים אִישׁ אֶל־רֵעֵהוּ *wa-yitmehu ha-anashim ish el-re'ehu*. They are astonished that the Egyptian lord has seated them in birth order — how could he know?
- **"messes" → "portions"** (v34) — "Mess" in 1611 English = serving of food; now archaic (and has opposite sense — a mess is disorder).

### Notes

- **Judah steps up** — v8–9: Judah offers himself as surety for Benjamin, where Reuben had offered his own sons (42:37; a strange and troubling offer). Judah's leadership takes shape here and culminates in his great speech of ch 44.
- **The double portion / five-fold portion** — Benjamin's fivefold portion at table is a test: how will the brothers respond to obvious favoritism of the new youngest toward whom they once felt the old envy they directed at Joseph?
- **Joseph's repeated tears** — the narrative foregrounds Joseph's emotion even as he keeps up the pretense. The severity is measured (test them with Benjamin) while the affection is unmistakable (he goes out to weep).
