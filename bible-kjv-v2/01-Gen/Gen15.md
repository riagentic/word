## Changes

- **"the word of the LORD" → "the word of Yahweh"** (v1, 4) — Hebrew דְּבַר־יְהוָה *devar-Yahweh*. First appearance of this prophetic formula in the Bible (it becomes the standard phrase for prophetic revelation).
- **"Fear not" → "Do not be afraid"** (v1) — modernized.
- **"I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward" → "I am your shield; your reward shall be very great"** (v1) — Hebrew אָנֹכִי מָגֵן לָךְ שְׂכָרְךָ הַרְבֵּה מְאֹד. Syntactically ambiguous: KJV reads both clauses as Yahweh's self-identification ("I am your shield, I am your great reward"). Alternative (arguably better Hebrew grammar): "I am your shield; your reward shall be very great" — shield is Yahweh's self-description, "great reward" is a separate promise. Translation follows the second reading.
- **"Lord GOD" → "Lord Yahweh"** (v2, 8) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה *Adonai Yahweh*. Combined address: *Adonai* ("my Lord" — respectful address form) + the Tetragrammaton. The pointed vocalization יְהוִה (with vowels of *Elohim* rather than *Adonai*) is a Masoretic device to signal "read *Elohim*" (to avoid saying *Adonai Adonai*). KJV renders as "Lord GOD" (small-caps GOD = Yahweh). Preserved as compound address: *Adonai* is translated "Lord" (a title of address), the Tetragrammaton is given as "Yahweh".
- **"the steward of my house is this Eliezer of Damascus" → "the heir of my house is Eliezer of Damascus"** (v2) — Hebrew וּבֶן־מֶשֶׁק בֵּיתִי הוּא דַּמֶּשֶׂק אֱלִיעֶזֶר. Notoriously obscure phrase: *ben-mesheq* could mean "son of possession / acquisition / heir" or "son of running / running my house." Wordplay with Damascus (*Dammeseq*). Translation follows the heir-reading (context requires an heir-figure, since Abram is complaining about having no son).
- **"seed" → "offspring"** (v3, 5, 13, 18) — as elsewhere.
- **"born in my house" → "slave born in my house"** (v3) — Hebrew בֶּן־בֵּיתִי *ben-beti* = "son of my house" = house-slave born into the household (distinct from purchased slaves).
- **"out of thine own bowels" → "your very own son"** (v4) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר יֵצֵא מִמֵּעֶיךָ *asher yetse mi-me'ekha* = "who comes out from your own inward parts." KJV literal "bowels" is crude in modern English; Hebrew idiom = one's own biological child.
- **"tell the stars" → "count the stars"** (v5) — Hebrew סְפֹר *sefor* = count. "Tell" in 1611 English meant "count" (cf. bank teller); now means "narrate."
- **"believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness" → "trusted in Yahweh, and he reckoned it to him as righteousness"** (v6) — Hebrew וְהֶאֱמִן בַּיהוָה וַיַּחְשְׁבֶהָ לּוֹ צְדָקָה. *He'emin* = trusted / considered firm (hiphil of *aman* = be firm / believed / have "amen"-trust). "Trusted" is more accurate than "believed" which in modern English suggests intellectual assent. *Chashav* = reckon / count / compute (accounting term). *Tsedaqah* = righteousness / right-relatedness. Pivotal verse quoted in Rom 4:3, Gal 3:6, James 2:23.
- **"of a surety" → "for certain"** (v13) — archaic → modern.
- **"stranger in a land" → "resident aliens in a land"** (v13) — Hebrew גֵּר *ger*. Legal-technical term for the resident foreigner without full citizen rights; as in 12:10.
- **"afflict" → "oppress"** (v13) — Hebrew וְעִנּוּ *we-innu* = oppress / afflict / humble. Same verb used of Egyptian oppression in Ex 1:11–12.
- **"great substance" → "great possessions"** (v14) — Hebrew בִּרְכֻשׁ גָּדוֹל *birkhush gadol* = with great possessions. Foreshadows the Israelites leaving Egypt with Egyptian wealth (Ex 12:35–36).
- **"not yet full" → "not yet complete"** (v16) — Hebrew לֹא־שָׁלֵם *lo-shalem*. *Shalem* = whole / complete. The Amorites have not yet filled up the measure of their wrongdoing; Yahweh will not prematurely dispossess them.
- **"smoking furnace, and a burning lamp" → "smoking firepot and a flaming torch"** (v17) — Hebrew תַּנּוּר עָשָׁן וְלַפִּיד אֵשׁ. *Tannur* = clay oven / firepot (not a large furnace). *Lappid* = torch. Both small objects — portable, visible — moving between the halves as divine self-identification.
- **"river of Egypt" (v18)** — Hebrew נְהַר מִצְרַיִם *nehar Mitzrayim*. Usually = Nile, but here may refer to the Wadi el-Arish ("Brook of Egypt," *nachal Mitzrayim*), the southern boundary of later Israel. Translations differ; *nehar* here is "river," pointing to Nile — an idealized expansive boundary.

### Notes

- **The covenant-cutting ritual (v9–17)** — parties to a covenant walked between halved animals, invoking on themselves the fate of the animals should they break the covenant (cf. Jer 34:18–19). Here only Yahweh (represented by the firepot and torch) passes through. The covenant is unilateral: Yahweh alone pledges and bears the self-maledictory obligation. Abram is the recipient, not a co-oath-taker.
- **"Credited as righteousness" (v6)** — central text for Paul's doctrine of justification (Rom 4). Translation note: *tsedaqah* is covenantal right-standing, not merely moral virtue. The accounting metaphor (*chashav*) is Pauline gold.
- **400 vs 430 years** — v13 gives 400, Ex 12:40 gives 430; rounded vs. precise. LXX of Ex 12:40 includes "in Egypt and in Canaan," harmonizing; MT has "in Egypt" alone. Small textual crux.
- **Ten peoples listed (v19–21)** — the fullest list of pre-Israelite Canaanite peoples in the Bible. Ten is symbolic of completeness. Other lists (Ex 3:8, Deut 7:1, Josh 3:10) have six or seven; this list includes three otherwise rare names (Kenites, Kenizzites, Kadmonites).
