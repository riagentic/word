## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"Dodanim" → "Rodanim"** (v4) — Hebrew MT has דֹּדָנִים *Dodanim*; the parallel list in 1 Chr 1:7, Samaritan Pentateuch, LXX, and several MT manuscripts read רֹדָנִים *Rodanim* (Rhodians, inhabitants of Rhodes). *Rodanim* fits the geographic logic (Javan = Ionia, Kittim = Cyprus, Rodanim = Rhodes — all Aegean). A dalet/resh scribal confusion (graphically near-identical in Hebrew: ד vs. ר). *Rodanim* is almost certainly original.
- **"isles of the Gentiles" → "coastland peoples"** (v5) — Hebrew אִיֵּי הַגּוֹיִם *iye ha-goyim*. *Iyim* = islands / coastlands (Mediterranean coastal areas, not just islands). *Goyim* = nations / peoples. "Gentiles" is a Christian-theological gloss; the Hebrew simply means "nations."
- **"tongue" → "language"** (v5, 20, 31) — modernized.
- **"families" → "clans"** (v5, 18, 20, 31, 32) — Hebrew מִשְׁפָּחָה *mishpachah* = extended family / clan / tribal unit. Larger than a nuclear family; "clan" captures the sociological unit.
- **"Mizraim" → "Mizraim"** (v6, 13) — Hebrew מִצְרַיִם *Mizraim* = Egypt (the Hebrew name). Dual form, possibly indicating Upper + Lower Egypt. KJV transliterates but misses the tzade-sound. "Egypt" would be an accurate translation; "Mizraim" preserves the Hebrew name given that the chapter is cataloging peoples by their original-language names.
- **"Phut" → "Put"** (v6) — Hebrew פּוּט *Put* (region near Egypt, possibly Libya or eastern Libya / Cyrenaica). KJV transliterates "Phut" via LXX; modern scholarship standardizes "Put."
- **"he began to be a mighty one" → "he was the first to be a mighty warrior"** (v8) — Hebrew הוּא הֵחֵל לִהְיוֹת גִּבֹּר בָּאָרֶץ. *Hechel* = began / was the first. *Gibbor* = mighty warrior / hero (same word as the "mighty ones" of old in 6:4). Nimrod = the first great conqueror of the post-flood era.
- **"mighty hunter before the LORD" — ambiguous** (v9) — Hebrew לִפְנֵי יְהוָה *liphne Yahweh* = "before Yahweh" or "in the face of Yahweh." Could be:
  1. "Under Yahweh's approval" (standard / KJV).
  2. "In defiance of Yahweh" (against him — rabbinic reading, given Nimrod's later negative associations with Babel).
  Ambiguous in Hebrew; translation retains the ambiguity.
- **"went forth Asshur, and builded" → "he went out to Asshur, and he built"** (v11) — Hebrew מִן־הָאָרֶץ הַהִוא יָצָא אַשּׁוּר. Two readings:
  1. "From that land Asshur went out" (treating Asshur as person / subject — KJV).
  2. "From that land he (Nimrod) went out to Asshur" (treating Asshur as destination).
  Reading 2 fits the narrative (Nimrod's empire extends from Shinar into Assyria) and has ancient support. Translation follows reading 2.
- **"the city Rehoboth" → "Rehoboth-Ir"** (v11) — Hebrew וְאֶת־רְחֹבֹת עִיר *we-et Rechovot Ir*. *Rechovot-Ir* = "Broad-places of the city," likely a proper compound name (like Kiryath-Arba), not "the city [of] Rehoboth" + separate. Rendered as compound.
- **"Arphaxad" → "Arpachshad"** (v22, 24) — Hebrew אַרְפַּכְשַׁד *Arpakhshad*. KJV transliteration loses the *ksh* cluster.
- **"Salah" → "Shelah"** (v24) — Hebrew שֶׁלַח *Shelach*. KJV misplaces the shin as simple 's' — "Shelah" standard modern.
- **"Peleg" — Hebrew etymology preserved** (v25) — Hebrew פֶּלֶג *Peleg*, from root פלג "to divide / split." Wordplay: "his days the earth was divided" (*niphlegah*). Often taken as a memory of the Babel dispersion (ch 11) or of some other population division. Name-gloss preserved.

### Notes

- **The Table of Nations (Gen 10)** — a stylized ethnography of the known world from the perspective of ancient Israel. Seventy nations (by traditional counting) descended from Noah's three sons. Not a modern genealogical tree — peoples are grouped by language, geography, and political affiliation, so some overlaps occur (e.g., Sheba appears both under Cush in v7 and under Joktan in v28).
- **Relation to ch 11 (Babel)** — the dispersion described here *precedes* Babel in the canonical order, but Babel narratively *causes* the linguistic division already presupposed in 10:5, 20, 31. The two chapters are best read as overlapping accounts, not strict chronology.
- **Nimrod (v8–12)** — shadowy figure; in later Jewish tradition the archetypal tyrant (founder of Babel, opposer of Abraham). Hebrew נִמְרֹד *Nimrod* is sometimes connected to *marad* ("to rebel"). The text itself is neutral-to-positive ("mighty hunter / before Yahweh") but sets him as the first empire-builder, whose kingdom begins in Babel.
- **Philistines from Caphtor (v14)** — Caphtor = Crete (or more broadly the Aegean). Confirms extra-biblical evidence that the Philistines were Sea Peoples of Aegean origin, not native Semites.
