## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"Get thee out of thy country" → "Go from your land"** (v1) — Hebrew לֶךְ־לְךָ *lekh-lekha* = "go for yourself" / "go to yourself" — an intensified imperative. Famously doubled form: the same formula addresses Abraham at the binding of Isaac (22:2). Traditional literal "get thee" is archaic; the emphatic quality is often glossed as "go forth" or left implicit.
- **"thy kindred" → "your kindred"** — modernized pronouns throughout.
- **"families" → "clans"** (v3) — as in Gen 10:5. Hebrew מִשְׁפְּחֹת *mishpechot*.
- **"shall be blessed" / "find blessing"** (v3) — Hebrew וְנִבְרְכוּ *we-nivrekhu* (niphal of *barakh*). Two defended readings:
  1. Passive: "all the clans… shall be blessed" (traditional; KJV, LXX, NT citations in Acts 3:25, Gal 3:8).
  2. Reflexive: "all the clans… shall bless themselves" (by invoking Abraham as paradigm of blessing — "may we be blessed like Abraham"). The reflexive reading is grammatically common for niphal. Parallel passages use the unambiguous hitpael (*yitbarekhu*, 22:18, 26:4). Rendered "find blessing" — neutral between the two, capturing that Abraham becomes the reference point of blessing for all peoples.
- **"curse him that curseth thee" → "the one who treats you with contempt I will curse"** (v3) — Hebrew וּמְקַלֶּלְךָ אָאֹר *u-meqallelkha a'or*. Two different verbs for "curse" in one verse: *qallel* (make light of / treat with contempt / curse lightly) applied to what the antagonist does; *arar* (formal imprecation / put under ban) applied to what Yahweh does in response. The Hebrew distinguishes "contempt" from "full curse" — KJV collapses both to "curse." Preserved.
- **"the souls that they had gotten" → "the people they had acquired"** (v5) — Hebrew הַנֶּפֶשׁ אֲשֶׁר־עָשׂוּ *ha-nephesh asher-asu*. *Nephesh* here = persons (by metonymy, as in English "souls on board"). *Asu* = "made" — could mean: (a) persons acquired (slaves and dependents); (b) persons made / won over (rabbinic reading: proselytes converted to Abraham's faith). Literal "acquired" is standard; the rabbinic reading is a reverent gloss.
- **"Sichem" → "Shechem"** (v6) — Hebrew שְׁכֶם *Shekhem*. Modernized transliteration.
- **"plain of Moreh" → "oak of Moreh"** (v6) — Hebrew אֵלוֹן מוֹרֶה *elon Moreh*. *Elon* = great tree / oak / terebinth (from אַיִל *ayil*, "mighty"). KJV "plain" derives from LXX δρῦς taken as "place"; the Hebrew is specifically a sacred tree — a common site of theophany (cf. 13:18, 14:13; Moreh = "teacher" / "oracle-giver," likely indicating a divinatory sacred site). Corrected.
- **"seed" → "offspring"** (v7) — as in earlier chapters.
- **"Hai" → "Ai"** (v8) — Hebrew הָעַי *ha-Ai*. KJV transliterates the article as part of the name. "Ai" standard.
- **"south" → "Negev"** (v9) — Hebrew הַנֶּגֶב *ha-Negev*. Refers to the specific arid region south of Judah, not a generic direction. "South" in KJV loses the place name.
- **"going on still" → "continually pulling up stakes"** (v9) — Hebrew הָלוֹךְ וְנָסוֹעַ *halokh we-nasoa*. *Nasoa* (from *nasa* = pull up stakes / set out) is nomadic travel vocabulary. "Pulling up stakes" captures the nomadic image in the verb.
- **"sojourn" → "live as a resident alien"** (v10) — Hebrew לָגוּר *la-gur*. *Gur* = dwell as a stranger / resident foreigner (not a native, not just a visitor). Technical legal category in ancient Near East — *ger* = resident alien. "Sojourn" is accurate but now archaic.
- **"grievous" → "severe"** (v10) — modernized.
- **"fair" → "beautiful"** (v11, 14) — Hebrew יְפַת־מַרְאֶה *yephat-mar'eh* = "beautiful of appearance." "Fair" in modern English is ambiguous (just / light-skinned / passable). *Yephat* clearly means beautiful.
- **"my soul shall live" → "my life may be spared"** (v13) — *Nephesh* here = life (same metonymy as v5). "Soul" in modern English carries metaphysical weight the Hebrew does not; context is bodily survival.
- **"entreated Abram well" → "dealt well with Abram"** (v16) — Hebrew הֵיטִיב *hetiv* = did good to. "Entreated" in 1611 English = "treated"; now means "pleaded with."
- **"he asses… she asses" → "male donkeys… female donkeys"** (v16) — Hebrew חֲמֹרִים *chamorim* and אֲתֹנֹת *atonot*. Different Hebrew words for donkeys by sex.
- **"menservants, and maidservants" → "male and female servants"** (v16) — Hebrew עֲבָדִים וּשְׁפָחֹת *avadim u-shphachot*. Modernized.
- **"plagued Pharaoh… with great plagues" → "struck Pharaoh… with great plagues"** (v17) — Hebrew וַיְנַגַּע *wa-yenagga* (from נגע, "to touch / strike / afflict"). Same root as the later Mosaic plagues on Pharaoh (Ex 7–12).

### Notes

- **The Abrahamic call (v1–3)** — the pivot of Genesis. After the universal prehistory (chs 1–11), the narrative narrows to one family through whom Yahweh will work for the blessing of all. Structure: five-clause blessing (great nation / blessed / great name / be a blessing / clans blessed), following five acts of self-assertion that end in curse in chs 3–11.
- **"Wife-sister" episode (v10–20)** — the first of three: Abraham/Sarah in Egypt (12), Abraham/Sarah with Abimelech (20), Isaac/Rebekah with Abimelech (26). Source criticism treats them as variant traditions of one story; read canonically, they form a sequence of patriarchal failures and divine protection.
- **Abram's half-truth** — Sarai was in fact Abram's half-sister (20:12). The word is technically accurate but deceptive in context. The narrator does not justify or explicitly condemn; later readers have read the episode as a failure of faith.
