# Genesis 37

[1] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ dwelt in the land of his father's sojournings, in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧.

[2] These are the generations of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧.

⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧, seventeen years old, was pasturing the flock with his brothers, and he was a boy with the sons of Bilhah and with the sons of Zilpah, the wives of his father. And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ brought their evil report to their father.

[3] And ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ loved ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ more than all his sons, for he was a son of old age to him; and he made him a long tunic.

[4] And his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, and they hated him and were not able to speak to him ⟦shalom|in shalom|in peace⟧.

[5] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ dreamed a dream and told it to his brothers, and they hated him yet more.

[6] And he said to them, "Hear, please, this dream that I have dreamed.

[7] And behold, we were binding sheaves in the midst of the field, and behold, my sheaf rose and also stood upright, and behold, your sheaves went around and bowed down to my sheaf."

[8] And his brothers said to him, "Will you surely reign over us? Or will you surely rule over us?" And they hated him yet more for his dreams and for his words.

[9] And he dreamed yet another dream and recounted it to his brothers, and he said, "Behold, I have dreamed a dream again, and behold, the sun and the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me."

[10] And he recounted it to his father and to his brothers, and his father rebuked him and said to him, "What is this dream that you have dreamed? Shall we surely come, I and your mother and your brothers, to bow down to you to the earth?"

[11] And his brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the word.

[12] And his brothers went to pasture the flock of their father in Shechem.

[13] And ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ said to ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧, "Are not your brothers pasturing in Shechem? Come, and I will send you to them." And he said to him, "Here I am."

[14] And he said to him, "Go, please, see ⟦shalom|the shalom|the peace⟧ of your brothers and ⟦shalom|the shalom|the peace⟧ of the flock, and bring me back word." And he sent him from the valley of ⟦hebron|Chevron|Hebron⟧, and he came to Shechem.

[15] And a man found him, and behold, he was wandering in the field; and the man asked him, saying, "What are you seeking?"

[16] And he said, "My brothers I am seeking; tell me, please, where they are pasturing."

[17] And the man said, "They have set out from here, for I heard them saying, 'Let us go to ⟦dothan|Dotan|Dothan⟧.'" And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ went after his brothers and found them in ⟦dothan|Dotan|Dothan⟧.

[18] And they saw him from afar, and before he came near to them they plotted against him to put him to death.

[19] And they said each man to his brother, "Behold, this master of dreams is coming.

[20] And now, come, and let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits, and we will say, 'An evil living thing has eaten him.' And we shall see what his dreams will be."

[21] And ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ heard, and he delivered him from their hand and said, "We shall not strike his soul."

[22] And ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ said to them, "Do not shed blood; throw him into this pit that is in the wilderness, and do not stretch out a hand against him" — in order to deliver him from their hand, to return him to his father.

[23] And it came to pass, when ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ came to his brothers, that they stripped ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ of his tunic, the long tunic that was on him,

[24] and they took him and threw him into the pit. And the pit was empty; there was no water in it.

[25] And they sat down to eat bread, and they lifted up their eyes and saw, and behold, a caravan of ⟦ishmaelites|Yishmeelim|Ishmaelites⟧ coming from ⟦gilead|Gilad|Gilead⟧, and their camels bearing gum and balm and resin, going to bring them down to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[26] And ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ said to his brothers, "What gain is there if we kill our brother and cover his blood?

[27] Come, and let us sell him to the ⟦ishmaelites|Yishmeelim|Ishmaelites⟧, and let not our hand be upon him, for he is our brother, our flesh." And his brothers listened.

[28] And ⟦midianites|Midyani|Midianite⟧ men, traders, passed by; and they drew ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ and brought him up out of the pit, and they sold ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ to the ⟦ishmaelites|Yishmeelim|Ishmaelites⟧ for twenty pieces of silver. And they brought ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[29] And ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ returned to the pit, and behold, ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ was not in the pit; and he tore his garments.

[30] And he returned to his brothers and said, "The child is not; and I, where am I to go?"

[31] And they took the tunic of ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ and slaughtered a male goat and dipped the tunic in the blood.

[32] And they sent the long tunic and brought it to their father and said, "This we have found. Recognize, please, whether it is the tunic of your son or not."

[33] And he recognized it and said, "The tunic of my son! An evil living thing has eaten him. ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ is surely torn to pieces."

[34] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ tore his cloaks and put sackcloth on his loins and mourned for his son many days.

[35] And all his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him, and he refused to be comforted and said, "For I will go down to my son mourning, to Sheol." And his father wept for him.

[36] And the ⟦midianites|Medanim|Midianites⟧ sold him into ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, to ⟦potiphar|Potifar|Potiphar⟧, a eunuch of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, the chief of the guards.

## Changes

- **"feeding the flock" → "pasturing the flock"** (v2) — Hebrew רֹעֶה *ro'eh*, shepherding. אֶת־אֶחָיו is taken as "with his brothers"; בַּצֹּאן is literally "among the flock".
- **"seventeen years old"** (v2) — Hebrew בֶּן־שְׁבַע־עֶשְׂרֵה שָׁנָה, literally "a son of seventeen years".
- **"the lad was with" → "he was a boy with"** (v2) — Hebrew וְהוּא נַעַר *we-hu na'ar*, without the article. נַעַר is "boy", also used of an attendant; some take it here as "he was a helper to the sons of Bilhah".
- **"their evil report"** (v2) — Hebrew דִּבָּתָם רָעָה, literally "their report, an evil one": a report about them. רָעָה is "evil".
- **"more than all his children" → "more than all his sons"** (v3) — Hebrew מִכָּל־בָּנָיו. "A son of old age to him" is בֶן־זְקֻנִים הוּא לוֹ.
- **"coat of many colours" → "long tunic"** (v3, 23, 32) — Hebrew כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים *ketonet passim*. כְּתֹנֶת is a tunic; the meaning of פַּסִּים is uncertain. The best-supported sense takes it from פַּס, the flat of the hand or foot: a tunic reaching to the palms and the soles, a long garment. "Of many colours" is the rendering of the Septuagint here (ποικίλον, from memory). The same garment is named only once more, on Tamar the daughter of David (2 Sam 13:18–19). One rendering is kept in both places, without certainty.
- **"could not speak peaceably unto him" → "were not able to speak to him in peace"** (v4) — Hebrew דַּבְּרוֹ לְשָׁלֹם, literally "to speak him for peace": שָׁלוֹם *shalom*. The same word is in v14, "see the peace of your brothers and the peace of the flock" (KJV "whether it be well with"): their well-being.
- **"they hated him yet the more"** (v5, 8) — Hebrew וַיּוֹסִפוּ עוֹד שְׂנֹא אֹתוֹ, literally "and they added still to hate him".
- **"told" / "recounted"** (v5, 9, 10) — two Hebrew verbs: הִגִּיד (v5) and סִפֵּר (v9, 10).
- **"made obeisance" → "bowed down"** (v7, 9, 10) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה *hishtachawah*, to bow down to the ground; in v10 with אַרְצָה "to the earth".
- **"Shalt thou indeed reign" → "Will you surely reign"** (v8, 10, 33) — the Hebrew infinitive absolute with its verb: מָלֹךְ תִּמְלֹךְ "reigning will you reign", מָשׁוֹל תִּמְשֹׁל "ruling will you rule", בוֹא נָבוֹא "coming shall we come", טָרֹף טֹרַף "torn, he is torn".
- **"envied him" → "were jealous of him"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיְקַנְאוּ־בוֹ.
- **"observed the saying" → "kept the word"** (v11) — Hebrew שָׁמַר אֶת־הַדָּבָר *shamar et ha-davar*: שָׁמַר "keep", דָּבָר "word".
- **"feed the flock" → "pasturing"** (v13, 16) — the Hebrew has no object in these two verses.
- **"Here am I" → "Here I am"** (v13) — Hebrew הִנֵּנִי, literally "behold me".
- **"They are departed hence" → "They have set out from here"** (v17) — Hebrew נָסְעוּ, the verb of pulling up tent pegs.
- **"conspired against him to slay him" → "plotted against him to put him to death"** (v18) — Hebrew לַהֲמִיתוֹ, "to cause him to die"; "kill" in v20, 26 is another verb, הָרַג.
- **"one to another" → "each man to his brother"** (v19) — Hebrew אִישׁ אֶל־אָחִיו.
- **"this dreamer" → "this master of dreams"** (v19) — Hebrew בַּעַל הַחֲלֹמוֹת הַלָּזֶה, literally "that master (owner) of the dreams".
- **"Some evil beast hath devoured him" → "An evil living thing has eaten him"** (v20, 33) — Hebrew חַיָּה רָעָה אֲכָלָתְהוּ. חַיָּה is "living thing", an animal, here a beast of prey; רָעָה is "evil"; אָכַל is "eat".
- **"Let us not kill him" → "We shall not strike his soul"** (v21) — Hebrew לֹא נַכֶּנּוּ נָפֶשׁ, literally "we shall not strike him, soul (נֶפֶשׁ *nephesh*)": strike him so as to take his life. The form is a plain negative statement, not "let us not".
- **"lay no hand upon him" → "do not stretch out a hand against him"** (v22) — Hebrew וְיָד אַל־תִּשְׁלְחוּ־בוֹ, literally "send a hand". "Their hand" (v21, 22) is singular in Hebrew.
- **"And it came to pass"** (v23) — Hebrew וַיְהִי, kept.
- **"looked" → "saw"** (v25) — Hebrew וַיִּרְאוּ. "A company" is אֹרְחָה, a caravan.
- **"spicery and balm and myrrh" → "gum and balm and resin"** (v25) — Hebrew נְכֹאת וּצְרִי וָלֹט *nekhot u-tsri walot*, trade goods of Gilead; all three are uncertain. They are taken as aromatic gums: *nekhot* perhaps tragacanth, *tsri* the balm of Gilead, *lot* perhaps ladanum. The same three words stand in Gen 43:11.
- **"What profit … conceal his blood" → "What gain … cover his blood"** (v26) — Hebrew בֶּצַע, gain; כִּסָּה, cover.
- **"our brother and our flesh" → "our brother, our flesh"** (v27) — Hebrew אָחִינוּ בְשָׂרֵנוּ, without "and" and without "own".
- **"were content" → "listened"** (v27) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁמְעוּ, "heard", the verb of v21 ("Reuben heard"); here of heeding.
- **"Ishmaelites … Midianite men … Medanites"** (v25, 27, 28, 36) — the Hebrew names the traders differently each time: יִשְׁמְעֵאלִים (v25, 27, 28), אֲנָשִׁים מִדְיָנִים סֹחֲרִים "Midianite men, traders" (v28), הַמְּדָנִים *ha-Medanim* "the Medanites" (v36; compare Medan beside Midian, Gen 25:2). In v28 the Hebrew does not say who "they" are who drew Joseph up and sold him: the brothers or the Midianites. In Gen 39:1 Potiphar buys him from the Ishmaelites. Nothing is harmonised; each name is translated as it stands.
- **"twenty pieces of silver"** (v28) — Hebrew בְּעֶשְׂרִים כָּסֶף, "for twenty of silver"; "pieces" is supplied for English (shekels are meant). The valuation of a male from five to twenty years old in Lev 27:5 is 20 shekels.
- **"rent his clothes" → "tore his garments" / "tore his cloaks"** (v29, 34) — two Hebrew words: בְּגָדָיו (v29) and שִׂמְלֹתָיו (v34), plural.
- **"The child is not"** (v30) — Hebrew הַיֶּלֶד אֵינֶנּוּ. "Where am I to go" is literally "and I, whither am I going?"
- **"killed a kid of the goats" → "slaughtered a male goat"** (v31) — Hebrew שְׂעִיר עִזִּים, a he-goat; "kid" (גְּדִי) is another word (Gen 38:17).
- **"know now whether" → "Recognize, please, whether"** (v32) — Hebrew הַכֶּר־נָא, the same words as in Gen 38:25; v33 וַיַּכִּירָהּ "and he recognized it".
- **"It is my son's coat" → "The tunic of my son!"** (v33) — Hebrew כְּתֹנֶת בְּנִי, two words without a verb.
- **"For I will go down"** (v35) — Hebrew כִּי־אֵרֵד; כִּי "for" opens his words and may also be read as "surely".
- **"the grave" → "Sheol"** (v35) — Hebrew שְׁאֹלָה, "to Sheol", the realm of the dead (cf. Job 14:13, Ps 16:10). The name is kept.
- **"Midianites" → "Medanim"** (v36) — Hebrew הַמְּדָנִים *ha-Medanim*, "the Medanites", not *Midyanim* as in v28; ESV and most versions read "Midianites". "Into Egypt" is אֶל־מִצְרָיִם.
- **"an officer of Pharaoh's" → "a eunuch of Pharaoh"** (v36) — Hebrew סְרִיס *saris*. The word means "eunuch" and is also the title of a court officer, whether or not he is castrated (eunuch / officer); Potiphar has a wife (Gen 39:7). One rendering is kept for the word.
- **"captain of the guard" → "chief of the guards"** (v36) — Hebrew שַׂר הַטַּבָּחִים *sar ha-tabbachim*. שַׂר is the head of a body of men (elsewhere "prince"; here and in ch 39–40 "chief"). הַטַּבָּחִים is plural, literally "the slaughterers": butchers or cooks, and hence executioners or the royal bodyguard. Which office is meant is not certain.

### Notes

- **Beginning of the Joseph cycle** — longest continuous narrative in Genesis (chs 37–50), marked by novelistic complexity: dreams, fate, recognition, reversal.
- **Joseph's dreams** — the imagery of the dreams (sheaves bowing, sun/moon/stars bowing) seals the brothers' hostility. The dream motif recurs throughout the cycle (cupbearer, baker, Pharaoh).
- **Reuben and Judah** — two of the brothers try to moderate the crime. Reuben plans to return Joseph to his father (v22); Judah proposes selling him (v26–27). Judah's more active role here anticipates ch 38 and ch 44.
- **v12** — the Masoretic Text marks the word אֶת before "the flock" with dots above it (puncta extraordinaria); the translation is not affected.
