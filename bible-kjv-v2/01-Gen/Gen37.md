# Genesis 37

[1] ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ dwelt in the land of his father's sojournings, in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧.

[2] These are the generations of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧.

⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧, when he was 17 years old, was pasturing the flock with his brothers; he was a youth, with the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives. And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ brought a bad report of them to their father.

[3] Now ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ loved ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ more than all his children because he was the son of his old age; and he made him a long ornamented tunic.

[4] When his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, they hated him and could not speak peaceably to him.

[5] ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers, they hated him even more.

[6] He said to them, "Hear, please, this dream that I have dreamed.

[7] There we were, binding sheaves in the field, when behold, my sheaf rose up and even stood upright, and behold, your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to my sheaf."

[8] His brothers said to him, "Will you indeed reign over us? Or will you indeed rule over us?" So they hated him even more for his dreams and for his words.

[9] Then he dreamed another dream and told it to his brothers, saying, "Behold, I have dreamed another dream; and behold, the sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me."

[10] He told it to his father and to his brothers; and his father rebuked him and said to him, "What is this dream that you have dreamed? Shall I and your mother and your brothers indeed come to bow down to the ground before you?"

[11] His brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the matter in mind.

[12] Then his brothers went to pasture their father's flock in Shechem.

[13] ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ said to ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧, "Are not your brothers pasturing the flock in Shechem? Come, I will send you to them." And he said to him, "Here I am."

[14] He said to him, "Go now, see whether it is well with your brothers and well with the flock, and bring me word again." So he sent him from the valley of ⟦hebron|Chevron|Hebron⟧, and he came to Shechem.

[15] A man found him wandering in the field, and the man asked him, "What are you seeking?"

[16] He said, "I am seeking my brothers; tell me, please, where they are pasturing the flock."

[17] The man said, "They have gone from here, for I heard them say, 'Let us go to ⟦dothan|Dotan|Dothan⟧.'" So ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ went after his brothers and found them in ⟦dothan|Dotan|Dothan⟧.

[18] They saw him from afar; and before he came near them, they conspired against him to kill him.

[19] They said to one another, "Here comes this dreamer!

[20] Come now, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits; then we will say that a savage beast has devoured him. We shall see what will become of his dreams."

[21] But ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ heard it and delivered him out of their hands, saying, "Let us not take his life."

[22] And ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ said to them, "Shed no blood; throw him into this pit that is in the wilderness, but do not lay a hand on him" — so that he might rescue him from their hand to restore him to his father.

[23] So when ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ came to his brothers, they stripped ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ of his tunic, the long ornamented tunic that was on him,

[24] and they took him and threw him into the pit. The pit was empty; there was no water in it.

[25] Then they sat down to eat bread; and they lifted up their eyes and looked, and behold, a caravan of ⟦ishmaelites|Yishmeelim|Ishmaelites⟧ was coming from ⟦gilead|Gilad|Gilead⟧, with their camels bearing gum, balm, and resin, going to carry them down to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[26] ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ said to his brothers, "What profit is it if we kill our brother and conceal his blood?

[27] Come, let us sell him to the ⟦ishmaelites|Yishmeelim|Ishmaelites⟧, and let our hand not be upon him, for he is our brother, our own flesh." And his brothers listened to him.

[28] When the ⟦midianites|Midyani|Midianite⟧ traders passed by, they drew ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ up and lifted him out of the pit, and they sold ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ to the ⟦ishmaelites|Yishmeelim|Ishmaelites⟧ for twenty pieces of silver; and they brought ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[29] When ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ returned to the pit and saw that ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ was not in the pit, he tore his clothes.

[30] He returned to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone! And I — where shall I go?"

[31] Then they took ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧'s tunic, slaughtered a young goat, and dipped the tunic in the blood;

[32] and they sent the long ornamented tunic and brought it to their father, and said, "We have found this; please examine it, whether it is your son's tunic or not."

[33] He recognized it and said, "It is my son's tunic! A savage beast has devoured him. ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ is surely torn in pieces."

[34] ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ tore his garments, put sackcloth on his loins, and mourned for his son many days.

[35] All his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted, and said, "I shall go down to my son, mourning, to Sheol." Thus his father wept for him.

[36] Meanwhile the ⟦midianites|Medanim|Midianites⟧ sold him in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ to ⟦potiphar|Potifar|Potiphar⟧, an officer of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, the captain of the guard.

## Changes

- **"feeding the flock" → "pasturing the flock"** (v2) — Hebrew רֹעֶה *ro'eh*. Shepherding; modernized.
- **"the lad was with" → "he was a youth, with"** (v2) — Hebrew וְהוּא נַעַר *we-hu na'ar*. "A youth" (not the article "the lad"); 17 years old.
- **"coat of many colours" → "long ornamented tunic"** (v3, 23, 32) — Hebrew כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים *ketonet passim*. *Passim* uncertain:
  1. "Long-sleeved" / "reaching to the extremities" (Hebrew *pas* = palm or sole of foot) — a long robe of the elite class, not for fieldwork. This is the better-supported reading.
  2. "Multicolored" / "patterned" — following LXX *poikilon* and later tradition.
  The same garment appears only elsewhere on Tamar, a royal princess (2 Sam 13:18), supporting the "robe of distinction" reading. KJV "coat of many colours" follows LXX/Vulgate but probably misses the main sense, which is a mark of status / preference, not color per se.
- **"made obeisance" → "bowed down"** (v7, 9, 10) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה *hishtachawah* = prostrated themselves. Prostration before a superior — the point of Joseph's dreams.
- **"observed the saying" → "kept the matter in mind"** (v11) — Hebrew שָׁמַר אֶת־הַדָּבָר *shamar et ha-davar*. *Shamar* = guard / keep. Jacob reserves judgment.
- **"slay" → "kill"** (v18, 20, 26) — modernized.
- **"evil beast" / "savage beast" (v20, 33)** — Hebrew חַיָּה רָעָה *chayyah ra'ah*. "Evil" here = harmful (modifying "wild animal"), not morally evil.
- **"Ishmeelites… Midianites" (v25–28)** — two different designations for the same caravan (or two overlapping groups). Text flexes between them — often taken as a source-critical seam. Canonically read, the two names may be synonymous (Ishmaelites = a generic ethnic term for nomadic traders, Midianites = a specific tribe within that category). Or the brothers sold him to Midianites who then sold him on to Ishmaelites (v36 has Midianites selling him to Potiphar). Text is ambiguous; translated as it stands.
- **"spicery and balm and myrrh" → "gum, balm, and resin"** (v25) — Hebrew נְכֹאת וּצְרִי וָלֹט *nekhot u-tsri walot*. Trade goods of Gilead:
  - *Nekhot* = tragacanth gum / storax gum.
  - *Tsri* = balm of Gilead (a resin-aromatic, medicinal).
  - *Lot* = ladanum / myrrh-resin.
  All aromatic resins; "myrrh" is one specific tradition of *lot*. Modernized.
- **"twenty pieces of silver" (v28)** — Hebrew בְּעֶשְׂרִים כָּסֶף. The price of a young male slave in Levitical law (Lev 27:5) is 20 shekels, consistent with this price.
- **"the grave" → "Sheol"** (v35) — Hebrew שְׁאוֹל *She'ol*. The realm of the dead in Hebrew cosmology — not simply "grave" but the shadowy underworld (cf. Job 14:13, Ps 16:10). Preserved.
- **"Midianites" → "Medanim"** (v36) — Hebrew הַמְּדָנִים *ha-Medanim*, "the Medanites" (cf. Medan, Gen 25:2), not *Midyanim* as in v28; ESV and most versions read "Midianites".
- **"captain of the guard" (v36)** — Hebrew שַׂר הַטַּבָּחִים *sar ha-tabbachim*. Literally "chief of the executioners / slaughterers" — possibly the chief executioner, chief cook, or chief of bodyguards. A senior royal-household official in any case.

### Notes

- **Beginning of the Joseph cycle** — longest continuous narrative in Genesis (chs 37–50), marked by novelistic complexity: dreams, fate, recognition, reversal.
- **Joseph's dreams** — the textile / celestial imagery of the dreams (sheaves bowing, sun/moon/stars bowing) seals the brothers' hostility. The dream motif recurs throughout the cycle (butler, baker, Pharaoh) — Joseph is the interpreter of dreams.
- **Reuben and Judah** — two of the brothers try to moderate the crime. Reuben plans to return Joseph secretly; Judah proposes selling him. Both motives are ambiguous (Reuben perhaps trying to regain his father's favor; Judah pragmatically profit-seeking). Judah's more active role here anticipates ch 38 and ch 44.
