## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"troubled at his presence" → "dismayed at his presence"** (v3) — Hebrew נִבְהֲלוּ *nivhalu* = terrified / dismayed. Stronger than "troubled."
- **"earing nor harvest" → "plowing nor harvest"** (v6) — Hebrew חָרִישׁ וְקָצִיר *charish we-qatsir* = plowing-time and harvest. "Earing" in 1611 English = plowing (from "ear" / "ard"); now archaic.
- **"preserve you a posterity" → "preserve for you a remnant"** (v7) — Hebrew שְׁאֵרִית *she'erit* = remnant / remainder. Key theological term (later developed in the prophets).
- **"a great deliverance" (v7)** — Hebrew לִפְלֵיטָה גְדֹלָה *li-phletah gedolah*. *Peletah* = escape / deliverance. Joseph theologically reinterprets the brothers' crime: the betrayal became the means of salvation for the covenant family.
- **"fall not out" → "quarrel"** (v24) — Hebrew אַל־תִּרְגְּזוּ בַּדָּרֶךְ *al-tirgezu ba-darekh*. *Ragaz* = tremble / be agitated. Could mean "do not be anxious on the journey" or "do not quarrel along the way." Traditional reading = quarrel, fitting the group dynamics of blame-casting that might arise.
- **"heart fainted" → "heart became faint"** (v26) — Hebrew וַיָּפָג לִבּוֹ *wa-yapag libbo*. Jacob's heart goes numb / shuts down at the shocking news.
- **"three hundred pieces of silver"** (v22) — significant: fifteen times the 20 pieces for which Joseph was sold (37:28). Exaggerated / symbolic favor.

### Notes

- **Joseph's theological reading (v5–8)** — Joseph interprets his own suffering as Yahweh's providence for deliverance. The brothers' evil and God's good coexist: "you sold me here; *Elohim* sent me" (v4–5). This providential reading becomes foundational for Israelite / Jewish theology and recurs in 50:20.
- **The land of Goshen (v10)** — the eastern Nile delta (Wadi Tumilat region), good pastureland for semi-nomadic shepherds, geographically separated from the main Egyptian population centers — fitting for a shepherd-clan that the Egyptians found ritually unclean (43:32, 46:34).
- **Pharaoh's generosity (v16–20)** — surprising scope. Probably reflects the Hyksos-period Semitic ruler theory (a Semitic-origin Pharaoh would welcome Semitic immigrants). Or: extraordinary gratitude toward the vizier who has saved Egypt. Text does not identify the Pharaoh.
