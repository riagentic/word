## Changes

- **"dukes" → "chiefs"** (v15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21, 29, 30, 40–43) — Hebrew אַלּוּפִים *allufim*. Tribal chieftains / clan-heads. "Duke" (via Latin *dux*) is medieval European; Hebrew just means "thousand-captain" / "clan chief."
- **"Aholibamah" → "Oholibamah"** (v2, 5, 14, 18, 25, 41) — Hebrew אָהֳלִיבָמָה *Oholivamah*. Modern transliteration.
- **"found the mules" → "found the hot springs"** (v24) — Hebrew הוּא אֲשֶׁר מָצָא אֶת־הַיֵּמִם בַּמִּדְבָּר. The word יֵמִם *yemim* is a hapax legomenon (only here). KJV "mules" (following some ancient tradition) is unlikely; rabbinic speculation gave "mules" because Anah was supposedly first to breed them. Most modern scholars: "hot springs" (based on etymology related to *yam* "water"), giving a coherent reading — he discovered thermal springs while pasturing donkeys. Some other proposed readings: "emim" (demons / giants), "water-source." "Hot springs" is the majority modern reading, consistent with the volcanic / geothermal region of Edom.
- **"Hadad / Hadar"** (v35, 39) — MT v35 has *Hadad*, v39 has *Hadar*. 1 Chr 1:50 has *Hadad* in both slots; probable scribal variation (d/r visual confusion in Hebrew). Left as MT.
- **"by the river" → "on the River"** (v37) — Hebrew עַל־הַנָּהָר. Likely the Euphrates.

### Notes

- **Why this long Esau genealogy?** The Genesis structure dedicates one chapter each to the non-covenant lines (Ishmael, ch 25; Esau, ch 36) before focusing entirely on the covenant line. The chapter establishes Edom as a serious, structured nation with kings "before any king reigned in Israel" (v31) — Edom's political development preceded Israel's monarchy.
- **Note v31's implication** — "before any king reigned over the children of Israel" implies the text knows of later Israelite kings; this is one data point in the discussion of the composition history of Genesis (the phrase reads as post-monarchical retrospect).
- **Overlapping / redundant lists** — the chapter interleaves different tribal registers (Esau's sons, Horite sons, chiefs of Edom, kings of Edom, later chiefs by place), sometimes with different names for the same figures. Indicates composite source material; translated as it stands.
- **Horites** — pre-Edomite inhabitants of Seir, later displaced (Deut 2:12, 22). Possibly identified with the Hurrians; etymology disputed.
- **Amalek (v12)** — Timna's son by Eliphaz, the ancestral eponym of Amalek, Israel's archetypal desert enemy (Ex 17:8–16, Deut 25:17–19). His mother is called a "concubine" — the marginal-descent perhaps reflecting ancient hostility.
