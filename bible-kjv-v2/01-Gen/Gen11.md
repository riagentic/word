# Genesis 11

[1] The whole earth had one language and the same words.

[2] As people migrated from the east, they found a plain in the land of Shinar and settled there.

[3] And they said to one another, "Come, let us make bricks and burn them thoroughly." They used brick for stone, and bitumen for mortar.

[4] Then they said, "Come, let us build ourselves a city, and a tower with its top in the heavens; let us make a name for ourselves, lest we be scattered over the face of the whole earth."

[5] But Yahweh came down to see the city and the tower that the children of humankind had built.

[6] And Yahweh said, "Look, they are one people, and they all have one language; and this is only the beginning of what they will do. Now nothing they plan to do will be impossible for them.

[7] Come, let us go down and there confuse their language, so that they may not understand one another's speech."

[8] So Yahweh scattered them from there over the face of all the earth, and they left off building the city.

[9] Therefore its name was called Babel, because there Yahweh confused the language of all the earth; and from there Yahweh scattered them over the face of all the earth.

[10] These are the generations of Shem. Shem was 100 years old when he fathered Arpachshad, two years after the flood.

[11] Shem lived after he fathered Arpachshad 500 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[12] Arpachshad lived 35 years and fathered Shelah.

[13] Arpachshad lived after he fathered Shelah 403 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[14] Shelah lived 30 years and fathered Eber.

[15] Shelah lived after he fathered Eber 403 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[16] Eber lived 34 years and fathered Peleg.

[17] Eber lived after he fathered Peleg 430 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[18] Peleg lived 30 years and fathered Reu.

[19] Peleg lived after he fathered Reu 209 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[20] Reu lived 32 years and fathered Serug.

[21] Reu lived after he fathered Serug 207 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[22] Serug lived 30 years and fathered Nahor.

[23] Serug lived after he fathered Nahor 200 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[24] Nahor lived 29 years and fathered Terah.

[25] Nahor lived after he fathered Terah 119 years, and fathered sons and daughters.

[26] Terah lived 70 years and fathered Abram, Nahor, and Haran.

[27] These are the generations of Terah. Terah fathered Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran fathered Lot.

[28] Haran died in the lifetime of his father Terah, in the land of his birth, in Ur of the Chaldeans.

[29] Abram and Nahor took wives for themselves. The name of Abram's wife was Sarai, and the name of Nahor's wife was Milcah, the daughter of Haran, the father of Milcah and Iscah.

[30] But Sarai was barren; she had no child.

[31] Terah took Abram his son and Lot son of Haran, his grandson, and Sarai his daughter-in-law, his son Abram's wife; and they set out together from Ur of the Chaldeans to go to the land of Canaan. But when they came to Haran, they settled there.

[32] The days of Terah were 205 years; and Terah died in Haran.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"one language, and of one speech" → "one language and the same words"** (v1) — Hebrew שָׂפָה אֶחָת וּדְבָרִים אֲחָדִים *sapha echat u-devarim achadim*. *Sapha* = lip / language; *devarim achadim* = "same words / uniform vocabulary." Hendiadys for "one shared speech." KJV's "speech" for *devarim* is weak; "the same words" captures the Hebrew precisely.
- **"Go to" → "Come"** (v3, 4, 7) — Hebrew הָבָה *havah* = "give / come now!" — an exhortative particle. "Go to" in 1611 English was the standard exhortation; unintelligible now.
- **"burn them throughly" → "burn them thoroughly"** (v3) — spelling modernized.
- **"slime… morter" → "bitumen… mortar"** (v3) — Hebrew חֵמָר *chemar* = bitumen / asphalt (naturally occurring in Mesopotamia, used for waterproofing and binding). "Slime" misleads; Hebrew is specific.
- **"whose top may reach unto heaven" → "with its top in the heavens"** (v4) — Hebrew וְרֹאשׁוֹ בַשָּׁמַיִם *we-rosho va-shamayim*. Not ambition to touch the sky literally, but the architectural reality of a ziggurat whose upper stage reaches the heavens (Mesopotamian ziggurats were understood as linking earth and heaven). Preserved.
- **"make us a name" → "make a name for ourselves"** (v4) — Hebrew וְנַעֲשֶׂה־לָּנוּ שֵׁם. Modernized. (Ironic contrast later: Abraham, who makes no attempt at self-promotion, is the one whom Yahweh promises to make a great name — 12:2.)
- **"children of men" → "children of humankind"** (v5) — Hebrew בְּנֵי הָאָדָם *bene ha-adam*. Humanity generally.
- **"this they begin to do" → "this is only the beginning of what they will do"** (v6) — Hebrew וְזֶה הַחִלָּם לַעֲשׂוֹת *we-zeh hachillam la'asot*. "This is their beginning to do" — more than KJV's flat rendering.
- **"nothing will be restrained" → "nothing will be impossible"** (v6) — Hebrew לֹא־יִבָּצֵר מֵהֶם *lo-yibbatser mehem*. Niphal of *batsar* = cut off / unreachable / impossible. "Nothing they plan will be out of reach."
- **"imagined" → "plan"** (v6) — Hebrew זָמָם *zamam* = devise / plot / plan. *Not* the *yetser* of 6:5 / 8:21. "Imagined" in modern English suggests fantasy; *zamam* is deliberate planning.
- **"confound" → "confuse"** (v7, 9) — Hebrew בָּלַל *balal* = to mix / confuse. KJV "confound" is accurate but archaic; retained the wordplay with *Bavel* (v9) explicitly.
- **"Babel" — Hebrew etymology preserved** (v9) — Hebrew בָּבֶל *Bavel*. Wordplay: *Bavel* ← *balal* ("confuse"). The Akkadian name of the city is *Babili* = "gate of the god" (*bab-ili*); the Hebrew text subverts this prestigious etymology with a derisive pun. Original Babylonian theology: heaven's gate. Israelite counter-theology: confusion. Preserved parenthetically.
- **Name forms modernized**: "Arphaxad" → "Arpachshad," "Salah" → "Shelah." See Gen 10 notes.

### Notes

- **Babel structure** — tight chiastic composition (A. one language / A'. scattered / B. build a city / B'. left off building / C. Yahweh comes down / C'. Yahweh confuses). The architecture mirrors what it narrates: a monument built up, Yahweh's intervention, dispersion out.
- **"Let us go down" (v7)** — plural, paralleling "let us make humanity" (1:26) and "like one of us" (3:22). Same translational options apply.
- **Lifespans continue declining** — from ch 5 (900s) to ch 11 (200s / 400s) — a narrative pattern of shortening human life-spans until Abraham (175) and on to Moses (120). Text may imply a post-flood moral / physical attenuation.
- **"Ur of the Chaldeans" (v28, 31)** — the "Chaldeans" (*Kasdim*) as a people are attested in Mesopotamia only from the 9th c. BCE onward, centuries after Abraham would have lived. Often treated as an anachronistic updating for a later audience (the Chaldeans were the dominant power in Mesopotamia by the time the text reached its final form under the Neo-Babylonian empire). Location of Ur debated: traditional identification with Sumerian Ur in southern Mesopotamia; alternative (Gordon, Cyrus) with an Urfa / Haran region Ur in northern Mesopotamia, closer to Haran.
- **Abram / Sarai / Haran** — spellings kept as in MT; the name changes to Abraham / Sarah come in ch 17.
