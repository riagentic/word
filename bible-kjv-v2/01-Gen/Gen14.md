## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"king of nations" → "king of Goiim"** (v1, 9) — Hebrew גּוֹיִם *Goiim*. Usually translated "nations" / "peoples." But here it appears as a proper name (parallel to Shinar, Elam, Ellasar — all geographic / ethnic names). Tidal = ruler of a people or territory called "Goiim" — a specific region, not a generic "ruler of nations." Some scholars identify it with the Hittite-era "Umman-Manda" (hordes) or a specific northern coalition. Preserved as name.
- **"joined together" → "joined forces"** (v3) — Hebrew חָבְרוּ *chavru* = allied / joined. Military alliance, modernized.
- **"the salt sea" → "the Salt Sea"** (v3) — the Dead Sea. Hebrew identifies it as *Yam ha-Melach*. Standard name.
- **"smote" → "struck down"** (v5, 7, 15) — Hebrew הִכָּה *hikkah* (hiphil of *nakah*) = strike / defeat in battle.
- **"Rephaims / Zuzims / Emims" → "Rephaim / Zuzim / Emim"** (v5) — Hebrew *-im* is already plural; KJV adds redundant English "-s."
- **"Horites in their mount Seir" → "Horites in their hill country of Seir"** (v6) — Hebrew הַר *har* = hill country / mountain region. "Mount Seir" in KJV is accurate but reads as a single peak; the Hebrew refers to a highland region.
- **"the country of the Amalekites" — anachronistic?** (v7) — Amalek is Esau's grandson (Gen 36:12); naming the territory "Amalekite" here is prospective (the narrator knows who would later inhabit it). Similar with "Dan" in v14, named long before the tribe of Dan existed.
- **"confederate" → "allies"** (v13) — Hebrew בַּעֲלֵי בְרִית *ba'alei berit* = "lords of covenant" = covenant partners / allies.
- **"brother" → "kinsman" (v14, 16)** — Hebrew אָח *ach*. Lot is Abram's nephew; *ach* in Hebrew covers extended kinship. "Kinsman" more accurate than English "brother."
- **"armed his trained servants" → "mustered his trained men"** (v14) — Hebrew וַיָּרֶק אֶת־חֲנִיכָיו *wa-yareq et-chanikhav*. *Heriq* = drew out / deployed (as in drawing a sword). *Chanikhim* = trained men / retainers (hapax legomenon; Egyptian-loan-word candidate from early 2nd millennium BCE execration texts, suggesting archaic origin for this narrative). Military mustering.
- **"slimepits" → "bitumen pits"** (v10) — Hebrew בֶּאֱרֹת בֶּאֱרֹת חֵמָר *be'erot be'erot chemar* = "pits, pits of bitumen." Same *chemar* as Babel (11:3) and Moses' basket (Ex 2:3). "Slime" in modern English misleads.
- **"victuals" → "provisions"** (v11) — Hebrew אָכְלָם *okhlam* = their food. Modernized.
- **"the most high God" → "El Elyon"** (v18, 19, 20, 22) — Hebrew אֵל עֶלְיוֹן *El Elyon*, kept as the name (*El*, not *Elohim*). Melchizedek is priest of El Elyon, a pre-Israelite designation of the supreme deity; Abram in v22 identifies this title with Yahweh.
- **"heaven and earth" → "heavens and earth"** (v19, 22) — Hebrew *shamayim* plural; as in Gen 1.
- **"gave him tithes of all" → "gave him a tenth of everything"** (v20) — Hebrew מַעֲשֵׂר *ma'aser* = a tenth / tithe. Modernized.
- **"I have lift up mine hand" → "I have lifted up my hand"** (v22) — oath gesture (hand raised to heaven). Modernized.
- **"shoelatchet" → "sandal-strap"** (v23) — Hebrew שְׂרוֹךְ־נַעַל *serokh-na'al*. "Shoe-latchet" archaic for "sandal-thong / sandal-strap."

### Notes

- **Historicity debate** — the coalition of eastern kings is hard to match to known Mesopotamian rulers. Names have near-parallels (Amraphel sometimes linked to Hammurabi; linguistic details fit the early 2nd millennium BCE) but no confirmed identification. The archaic Egyptian-loan vocabulary (*chanikhim*) argues for early composition rather than late invention.
- **Melchizedek (v18–20)** — sudden, unmotivated appearance; leaves as abruptly. Priest of the Most High *Elohim* in Salem (usually identified as Jerusalem). In Ps 110:4 YHWH swears a royal priesthood "after the order of Melchizedek"; Hebrews 5–7 develops this messianically. Within Genesis, he represents a pre-Israelite Canaanite monotheism that Abram affirms and ties to Yahweh.
- **Abram refuses spoils from Sodom (v22–23)** — preserves his independence and his claim that his wealth comes from Yahweh's blessing alone (cf. 13:2, 24:35) — not from human patronage. Anticipates the logic of the covenant blessing.
