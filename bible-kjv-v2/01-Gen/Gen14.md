# Genesis 14

[1] And it came to pass in the days of ⟦amraphel|Amrafel|Amraphel⟧ king of Shinar, ⟦arioch|Aryoch|Arioch⟧ king of Ellasar, ⟦chedorlaomer|Kedorlaomer|Chedorlaomer⟧ king of Elam, and Tidal king of ⟦goiim|Goyim|Goiim⟧,

[2] that they made war with Bera king of ⟦sodom|Sedom|Sodom⟧, and with Birsha king of ⟦gomorrah|Amorah|Gomorrah⟧, ⟦shinab|Shinav|Shinab⟧ king of Admah, and ⟦shemeber|Shemever|Shemeber⟧ king of ⟦zeboiim|Tzevoyim|Zeboiim⟧, and the king of Bela — that is ⟦zoar|Tzoar|Zoar⟧.

[4] Twelve years they served ⟦chedorlaomer|Kedorlaomer|Chedorlaomer⟧, and the thirteenth year they rebelled.

[5] And in the fourteenth year ⟦chedorlaomer|Kedorlaomer|Chedorlaomer⟧ came, and the kings who were with him, and they struck the ⟦rephaim|Refaim|Rephaim⟧ in ⟦ashteroth-karnaim|Ashterot Qarnayim|Ashteroth-Karnaim⟧, and the Zuzim in Ham, and the Emim in ⟦shaveh-kiriathaim|Shaveh Qiryatayim|Shaveh-Kiriathaim⟧,

[6] and the ⟦horites|Chori|Horite⟧ in their hill country, Seir, as far as ⟦el-paran|Eil Paran|El-Paran⟧, which is by the wilderness.

[7] And they turned back and came to ⟦en-mishpat|Ein Mishpat|En-Mishpat⟧ — that is ⟦kadesh|Qadesh|Kadesh⟧ — and struck all the field of the ⟦amalekites|Amaleqi|Amalekite⟧, and also the ⟦amorites|Emori|Amorite⟧ who dwelt in ⟦hazazon-tamar|Chatzatzon Tamar|Hazazon-Tamar⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And" kept at the head of the clauses** (throughout) — Hebrew וַ / וְ.
- **"And it came to pass" kept; "these kings" not supplied** (v1, 2) — Hebrew וַיְהִי בִּימֵי … עָשׂוּ מִלְחָמָה, "it came to pass in the days of … they made war"; the Hebrew does not repeat a subject.
- **"king of nations" → "king of Goiim"** (v1, 9) — Hebrew גּוֹיִם *goyim* is the ordinary word "nations"; beside Shinar, Ellasar and Elam it stands like the name of a land or people. "Tidal king of nations" is equally possible.
- **"Zeboiim"** (v2, 8) — written צביים, read (qere) צְבוֹיִם; the name is the same.
- **"which is" → "that is"** (v2, 3, 7, 8, 17) — Hebrew הִיא / הוּא, the narrator's own explanation of an older name; it is part of the Hebrew text, not a bracket of the translator.
- **"were joined together in the vale" → "joined together at the valley"** (v3) — Hebrew חָבְרוּ אֶל־עֵמֶק, "joined toward / at the valley"; "forces" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"in the thirteenth year" → "the thirteenth year"** (v4) — Hebrew וּשְׁלֹשׁ־עֶשְׂרֵה שָׁנָה, literally "and thirteen years they rebelled", without the preposition "in" that v5 has (וּבְאַרְבַּע עֶשְׂרֵה). The usual sense is "in the thirteenth year"; "for thirteen years" is not excluded by the form.
- **"smote" → "struck"** (v5, 7, 15, 17) — Hebrew הִכָּה *hikkah*; v17 "the slaughter of" is the same verb (מֵהַכּוֹת, "from striking").
- **"Rephaims / Zuzims / Emims" → "Rephaim / Zuzim / Emim"** (v5) — Hebrew *-im* is already plural.
- **"Horites in their mount Seir" → "the Horite in their hill country, Seir"** (v6) — Hebrew הַחֹרִי, singular for the people; בְּהַרְרָם שֵׂעִיר, "in their mountain, Seir" (הַר of a region).
- **"country of the Amalekites" → "field of the Amalekite"** (v7) — Hebrew שְׂדֵה הָעֲמָלֵקִי; "the Amorite" likewise singular.
- **"joined battle with them" → "drew up for battle with them"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיַּעַרְכוּ אִתָּם מִלְחָמָה, "they set a battle in order with them". "With" (אֵת, v2, 8, 9) is "with" in the sense "against"; v9 ends literally "four kings with the five".
- **"full of slimepits" → "pits upon pits of bitumen"** (v10) — Hebrew בֶּאֱרֹת בֶּאֱרֹת חֵמָר, literally "wells, wells of bitumen": the doubled noun means "many, nothing but". בְּאֵר is elsewhere "well"; חֵמָר as 11:3.
- **"the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled, and fell there" → "the king of Sodom and of Gomorrah fled, and they fell there"** (v10) — Hebrew מֶלֶךְ־סְדֹם וַעֲמֹרָה, "king" once and singular, with plural verbs. "Fell there" (וַיִּפְּלוּ־שָׁמָּה) does not say who fell or how (fell into the pits, or threw themselves down there); the king of Sodom is alive in v17. The Hebrew has no "some".
- **"to the mountain" kept** (v10) — Hebrew הֶרָה.
- **"the enemies" not supplied** (v11) — the Hebrew has only "they took".
- **"victuals" → "food"** (v11) — Hebrew אָכְלָם.
- **"Abram's brother's son" kept in the Hebrew order** (v12) — Hebrew וַיִּקְחוּ אֶת־לוֹט וְאֶת־רְכֻשׁוֹ בֶּן־אֲחִי אַבְרָם, "Lot and his goods, the son of Abram's brother"; not "nephew".
- **"one that had escaped" → "the fugitive"** (v13) — Hebrew הַפָּלִיט, with the article.
- **"Abram the Hebrew" kept** (v13) — Hebrew הָעִבְרִי *ha-ivri*, the first time the word stands in the Bible. It is formed from עֵבֶר: either the name Eber (10:21, 25; 11:14–17), "the descendant of Eber", or the noun "the region beyond", "the one from the other side". The Septuagint is cited for "the one who crossed over" (ὁ περάτης, from memory).
- **"dwelt" → "was abiding"** (v13) — Hebrew שֹׁכֵן, not יָשַׁב "dwell" (v7, 12).
- **"confederate with Abram" → "covenant allies of Abram"** (v13) — Hebrew בַּעֲלֵי בְרִית־אַבְרָם, literally "owners (lords) of the covenant of Abram".
- **"his brother" kept** (v14, 16) — Hebrew אָחִיו; Lot is the son of Abram's brother (v12), אָח is used of a kinsman.
- **"armed his trained servants" → "drew out his trained men"** (v14) — Hebrew וַיָּרֶק אֶת־חֲנִיכָיו: הֵרִיק "empty out, draw out" (as a sword from its sheath). חָנִיךְ is found only here; "trained" (root חָנַךְ "train, dedicate") is the best-supported sense, and the meaning is not certain. "Servants" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"three hundred and eighteen" kept** (v14) — the Hebrew puts the smaller number first: "eighteen and three hundred".
- **"his servants" → "his slaves"** (v15) — Hebrew עֲבָדָיו.
- **"on the left hand of Damascus" → "north of Damascus"** (v15) — Hebrew מִשְּׂמֹאל לְדַמָּשֶׂק, literally "on the left of Damascus": the left hand of one who faces east is the north.
- **"the king's dale" → "the King's Valley"** (v17) — Hebrew עֵמֶק הַמֶּלֶךְ; the same word עֵמֶק as "the valley of Shaveh" and "the valley of Siddim".
- **"Melchizedek king of Salem"** (v18) — Hebrew מַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק מֶלֶךְ שָׁלֵם: the name means "my king is righteousness" or "king of righteousness"; שָׁלֵם *Shalem* is of one root with שָׁלוֹם "peace".
- **"the most high God" → "El Elyon"** (v18, 19, 20, 22) — Hebrew אֵל עֶלְיוֹן: אֵל "El, God" (not *Elohim*) and עֶלְיוֹן "Most High". In v22 Abram joins the name to Yahweh: יְהוָה אֵל עֶלְיוֹן.
- **"priest of" kept** (v18) — Hebrew כֹהֵן לְאֵל עֶלְיוֹן, literally "priest to El Elyon".
- **"Blessed be Abram of the most high God" → "Blessed be Abram by El Elyon"** (v19) — Hebrew בָּרוּךְ אַבְרָם לְאֵל עֶלְיוֹן, literally "blessed (is / be) Abram to El Elyon".
- **"possessor of heaven and earth" → "possessor of heavens and earth"** (v19, 22) — Hebrew קֹנֵה שָׁמַיִם וָאָרֶץ. קָנָה is "get, acquire, buy", so its participle is "owner, possessor". In a few places where God is the subject (Deut 32:6; Ps 139:13; Prov 8:22) the sense "make, create" is defended, and many render "maker / creator of heavens and earth" here. The verse keeps the ordinary sense of the word; the other is possible. *Shamayim* is plural and both nouns stand without the article.
- **"delivered" → "delivered up"** (v20) — Hebrew מִגֵּן, a rare verb (piel), "hand over"; of one sound with מָגֵן "shield" in 15:1. "Adversaries" is צָרֶיךָ (צַר, not אֹיֵב "enemy").
- **"he gave him tithes of all" → "he gave him a tenth of all"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיִּתֶּן־לוֹ מַעֲשֵׂר מִכֹּל. The Hebrew does not name the giver: "Abram" is not in the verse. By the flow of the sentence the last subject is Melchizedek; Heb 7:2, 4 reads it as Abraham giving to Melchizedek.
- **"Give me the persons" → "Give me the souls"** (v21) — Hebrew הַנֶּפֶשׁ, "the soul" (singular for the whole group: the people), as 12:5.
- **"I have lift up mine hand" → "I have lifted up my hand"** (v22) — the gesture of an oath: "I have sworn".
- **"That I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet" → "that from a thread even to a sandal strap I will not take anything of all that is yours"** (v23) — the Hebrew oath form, literally "if from a thread even to a sandal strap, and if I take of all that is yours": the sworn "if I do …" means "I will not". The verse gives the meaning.
- **"lest thou shouldest say" → "and you shall not say"** (v23) — Hebrew וְלֹא תֹאמַר.
- **"Save only" → "Apart from me: only"** (v24) — Hebrew בִּלְעָדַי רַק, literally "apart from me" (nothing for me); the Hebrew has no verb "I will take".
- **"portion" → "share"** (v24) — Hebrew חֵלֶק, twice.

### Notes

- **The kings** — the coalition of eastern kings cannot be matched with certainty to rulers known from other sources.
- **Later names** — "the field of the Amalekite" (v7; Amalek is Esau's grandson, 36:12) and "Dan" (v14; the city received that name in Judg 18:29) are named as the later reader knew them.
- **Melchizedek (v18–20)** — he appears without introduction and is not mentioned again in Genesis. Ps 110:4 speaks of a priest "after the order of Melchizedek"; Heb 5–7 builds on both texts. Salem is commonly identified with Jerusalem (Ps 76:2).
- **Abram refuses the goods (v22–24)** — he takes nothing for himself, but does not bind his allies by his oath.
