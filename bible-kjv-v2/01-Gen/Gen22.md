## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*. In v1, 3, 9 the Hebrew has the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim"; in v8, 12 it has none. From v11 on the name is Yahweh (v11, 14, 15, 16).
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"it came to pass after these things" kept** (v1, 20) — Hebrew וַיְהִי אַחַר הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה; דְּבָרִים is "words" or "things".
- **"tempt" → "tested"** (v1) — Hebrew נִסָּה *nissah* (piel) = to test / prove / try. "Tempt" in modern English = entice to sin; the Hebrew means "put to the proof." The subject stands before the verb: "the Elohim — he tested Abraham".
- **"Behold, here I am" / "Here am I" → "Here I am"** (v1, 7, 11) — Hebrew הִנֵּנִי *hinneni*, literally "behold me": the answer of one who is present and attending.
- **"Take now thy son" → "Take, please, your son"** (v2) — Hebrew קַח־נָא *qach-na*. The particle נָא softens the imperative ("please" / "now").
- **"thine only son" → "your only one"** (v2, 12, 16) — Hebrew יְחִידְךָ *yechidkha*, from יָחִיד "only, single one"; the word "son" stands before it and is not repeated in it. Abraham has another son, Ishmael, who has been sent away (21:14); the Hebrew still says "your only one". The Septuagint has "your beloved" here (cited from memory).
- **"whom thou lovest" → "whom you love"** (v2) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר־אָהַבְתָּ, a perfect ("whom you have loved") of a verb of feeling: present in English. The first use of the verb "love" in the Bible. The four objects stand in a row, each with אֶת: "your son, your only one whom you love, Isaac".
- **"get thee into" → "get yourself to"** (v2) — Hebrew וְלֶךְ־לְךָ *lekh-lekha*, literally "go to / for you", the same words as in 12:1. The לְךָ turns the command back on the one addressed (it may also be heard as "for yourself", "by yourself"); the English reflexive carries it. The same form stands in v5, "Seat yourselves" (שְׁבוּ־לָכֶם).
- **"the land of Moriah" (v2)** — Hebrew אֶרֶץ הַמֹּרִיָּה *eretz ha-Moriyyah*, with the article: "the land of the Moriah". The name is found again only in 2 Chr 3:1 ("Mount Moriah", the place of Solomon's temple). Its meaning is not known; the chapter plays on the verb רָאָה "see" (v8, 14) beside it. The old versions do not give a name here (Septuagint "the high land", Vulgate "the land of vision", Syriac "of the Amorites": cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"offer him there for a burnt offering" → "offer him up there as a burnt offering"** (v2, 13) — Hebrew הַעֲלֵהוּ … לְעֹלָה, literally "make him go up … for a going-up": the verb and the noun are one root (עָלָה "go up"); the burnt offering is the one that goes up whole in smoke.
- **"which I will tell thee of" → "that I will say to you"** (v2, 3, 9) — Hebrew אָמַר "say"; "tell" is another verb (v20).
- **"ass" → "donkey"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew חֲמוֹר.
- **"two of his young men" → "his two young men"** (v3) — Hebrew שְׁנֵי נְעָרָיו. **"young men" / "the boy"** (v3, 5, 12, 19) — one Hebrew word, נַעַר *na'ar*: in v5 Abraham speaks to his נְעָרִים about "the נַעַר". The word covers a boy, a young man and an attendant; it says nothing certain about Isaac's age.
- **"clave the wood for the burnt offering" → "split wood for a burnt offering"** (v3) — Hebrew עֲצֵי עֹלָה, without the article; in v6 "the wood of the burnt offering" has it.
- **"rose up, and went" → "arose and went"** (v3) — two verbs in the Hebrew.
- **"Abide ye here" → "Seat yourselves here"** (v5) — Hebrew שְׁבוּ־לָכֶם פֹּה: יָשַׁב "sit, remain".
- **"go yonder and worship, and come again to you" → "will go as far as there, and we will bow down, and we will return to you"** (v5) — three verbs in the first person plural, in the form of resolve (cohortative): "let us go … and bow down … and return". Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה is "bow down" to the ground. "*We* will return": Abraham speaks as if both will come back (cf. Heb 11:19).
- **"laid it upon" → "put it on"** (v6, 9) — Hebrew שִׂים "put, set"; "it" is supplied in v6. **"knife"** (v6, 10) — Hebrew מַאֲכֶלֶת, from אָכַל "eat": a large knife for cutting up meat (Judg 19:29).
- **"spake … and said" → "said … and he said"** (v7) — the Hebrew has וַיֹּאמֶר "and he said" four times in the verse.
- **"lamb" → "sheep"** (v7, 8) — Hebrew שֶׂה *seh*: one animal of the flock, a sheep (or a goat), of any age. "Lamb" is another word (כֶּבֶשׂ). What is offered in v13 is a ram, a grown male sheep.
- **"God will provide himself a lamb" → "*Elohim* will see for himself the sheep"** (v8) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים יִרְאֶה־לּוֹ הַשֶּׂה *Elohim yir'eh-lo ha-seh*, word for word. The verb is רָאָה "see"; "see for oneself" something is to look it out, choose it, provide it (41:33; 1 Sam 16:1). The same verb names the place in v14, so "see" is kept. The last words, "my son", are an address; in the Hebrew order they stand directly after "for a burnt offering".
- **"an altar" → "the altar"** (v9) — the Hebrew has the article.
- **"bound Isaac" (v9)** — Hebrew וַיַּעֲקֹד *wa-ya'aqod*. The verb occurs only here in the Bible; it gives the passage its Jewish name, the *Aqedah* ("binding").
- **"slay" → "slaughter"** (v10) — Hebrew שָׁחַט *shachat*, to kill by cutting the throat; the usual word for slaughtering an animal, also for sacrifice.
- **"angel of the LORD" → "angel of Yahweh"** (v11, 15) — Hebrew מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה; the word מַלְאָךְ *mal'akh* means "messenger". He speaks as Yahweh himself ("from me", v12; "By myself I have sworn", v16).
- **"Lay not thine hand upon the lad" → "Do not stretch out your hand against the boy"** (v12) — Hebrew אַל־תִּשְׁלַח יָדְךָ אֶל, the same verb as in v10 ("stretched out his hand"); אֶל is "to, toward".
- **"that thou fearest God" → "that you fear *Elohim*"** (v12) — Hebrew יְרֵא אֱלֹהִים אַתָּה, literally "a fearer of Elohim are you". **"seeing thou hast not withheld" → "and you have not withheld"** — Hebrew וְלֹא "and … not".
- **"behold behind him a ram" → "behold, a ram behind"** (v13) — Hebrew וְהִנֵּה־אַיִל אַחַר: the Masoretic Text has אַחַר "behind" (or "after"), with no "him". Many witnesses read אֶחָד "one / a single ram", which differs by one letter (some Hebrew manuscripts, the Samaritan text, the Septuagint: cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"Jehovah-jireh" → "Yahweh Yireh"** (v14) — Hebrew יְהוָה יִרְאֶה *Yahweh yir'eh*, "Yahweh sees / will see": the qal of רָאָה "see", the very words of v8 ("*Elohim* will see for himself"). "Will provide" is the sense drawn from v8, not a second meaning of the word. "Jehovah" is a later mixed form of the Tetragrammaton with the vowels of *Adonai*.
- **"In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen" → "On the mountain of Yahweh it shall be seen"** (v14) — Hebrew בְּהַר יְהוָה יֵרָאֶה *be-har Yahweh yera'eh*: the same verb in the niphal, "be seen, appear". No subject is named: "it shall be seen" or "he shall be seen" (the niphal is the usual word for Yahweh "appearing", 12:7; 18:1). So the two forms stand side by side: Yahweh *sees* (יִרְאֶה), and on his mountain it / he *is seen* (יֵרָאֶה). The consonants are the same in both; only the vowels differ. The accents join "mountain" to "Yahweh" ("the mountain of Yahweh"). **"to this day" → "today"** — Hebrew הַיּוֹם.
- **"By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD" → "By myself I have sworn, declares Yahweh"** (v16) — Hebrew בִּי נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי נְאֻם־יְהוָה; נְאֻם is a noun, "the declaration of Yahweh". Yahweh swears by himself (Heb 6:13). The oath has כִּי "that" twice: before "because" (v16) and again before "I will surely bless" (v17); English has it once.
- **"in blessing I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed" → "I will surely bless you, and I will surely multiply your seed"** (v17) — Hebrew infinitive absolute with its verb: בָרֵךְ אֲבָרֶכְךָ וְהַרְבָּה אַרְבֶּה, literally "blessing I will bless you and multiplying I will multiply".
- **"seed" kept; "the gate of his enemies" kept** (v17, 18) — Hebrew זֶרַע *zera*, a singular noun, used of one descendant or of all together; the pronoun that follows is singular too: אֹיְבָיו "his enemies". English "their enemies" would decide for the many; the Hebrew does not (cf. Gal 3:16).
- **"sea shore" → "the shore of the sea"** (v17) — Hebrew שְׂפַת הַיָּם, literally "the lip of the sea".
- **"shall … be blessed" → "shall bless themselves"** (v18) — Hebrew וְהִתְבָּרֲכוּ *we-hitbarakhu*, the hitpael (reflexive stem) of בָּרַךְ: "bless themselves in your seed", that is, name your seed when they wish a blessing for themselves (cf. 48:20). The promise in 12:3 and 18:18 has the niphal וְנִבְרְכוּ, which is passive or reflexive ("be blessed"); here and in 26:4 the form is the hitpael. The hitpael can also be read as a passive, and so the Septuagint and the New Testament have it ("be blessed", Acts 3:25; cited from memory).
- **"because thou hast obeyed my voice" → "because you have listened to my voice"** (v18) — Hebrew שָׁמַעְתָּ בְּקֹלִי; עֵקֶב אֲשֶׁר "because" is literally "as a consequence of the fact that" (עֵקֶב from "heel").
- **"dwelt" kept** (v19) — Hebrew יָשַׁב.
- **"she hath also born children" → "she also has borne sons"** (v20) — Hebrew בָּנִים "sons". **"begat" → "begot"** (v23) — Hebrew יָלַד of the father.
- **"concubine" (v24)** — Hebrew פִּילֶגֶשׁ *pilegesh*: a wife of lower standing than a full wife.

### Notes

- **The Aqedah (the Binding)** — central Jewish and Christian text, with immense interpretive weight:
  - Theological: test of Abraham's faith (Heb 11:17–19), emblem of covenantal fidelity.
  - Typological (Christian): Isaac as prefigure of Christ, carrying his own wood to the place of sacrifice (cf. Jn 19:17).
  - Rabbinical: Isaac's consent in sacrifice (he is old enough to carry wood, old enough to resist); the *zekhut* (merit) of Isaac's willingness becomes atoning in later tradition.
  - Modern ethical: the "teleological suspension of the ethical" (Kierkegaard) — Abraham is asked to do what is both forbidden (murder) and impossible (undoing the covenant).
- **Child sacrifice** — widespread in Canaan and the ancient Near East; forbidden in Israelite law (Lev 18:21, 20:2–5). The Aqedah affirms the cost / seriousness of what Yahweh demands *and* draws a red line: Yahweh ultimately rejects the child sacrifice that surrounding cults practice.
- **Isaac's disappearance** — v19 says only "Abraham returned"; Isaac is not named again until 24:4, and does not appear again until 24:62. The chapter's final verses redirect attention to Nahor's line — setting up Rebekah for ch 24.
