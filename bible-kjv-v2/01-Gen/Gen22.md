## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"tempt" → "tested"** (v1) — Hebrew נִסָּה *nissah* (piel) = to test / prove / try. "Tempt" in modern English = entice to sin; the Hebrew means "put to the proof." Key distinction: *Elohim* does not entice Abraham to sin; *Elohim* tests Abraham's fear / faithfulness.
- **"and said" — "Abraham" → "here I am"** (v1, 7, 11) — Hebrew הִנֵּנִי *hinneni* = "behold me" = "I am here, attending, ready." Formal response of the attentive servant. Preserved as "Here I am."
- **"Take now thy son" → "Take your son, please"** (v2) — Hebrew קַח־נָא *qach-na*. The particle *-na* softens the imperative ("please") — a courteous-urgent plea, not a bare command. The same *-na* appears often in divine requests that the person has latitude to refuse.
- **"thine only son" → "your only one"** (v2, 12, 16) — Hebrew יְחִידְךָ *yechidkha* = your only / unique / precious one. From *yachid* = only / unique / one-and-only. Ishmael is Abraham's other son — still alive — but Isaac is the covenant-only-one. The Hebrew term stresses preciousness and singularity.
- **"whom thou lovest" → "whom you love"** (v2) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר־אָהַבְתָּ. First explicit mention of love in the Bible, and it is a father's love for his doomed son. Striking narrative choice.
- **"the land of Moriah" (v2)** — Hebrew אֶרֶץ הַמֹּרִיָּה *Eretz ha-Moriyyah*. 2 Chr 3:1 identifies Moriah with Mt. Zion / the temple mount. Etymology ambiguous: possibly from *ra'ah* (see) — "the land of seeing / vision" (cf. v14), or from a proper name otherwise unknown.
- **"ass" → "donkey"** (v3, 5) — modernized ("ass" now a vulgar anatomical term in modern English).
- **"clave the wood" → "split wood"** (v3) — modernized.
- **"Abide ye here" → "Stay here"** (v5) — modernized.
- **"we will come again to you" → "we will return to you"** (v5) — Hebrew וְנָשׁוּבָה. Plural: "*we* will return" — Abraham's word of faith (or evasion): he speaks as if both will return, when he has been commanded to kill the son. Heb 11:19 reads this as Abraham's faith in resurrection.
- **"God will provide himself a lamb" → "*Elohim* will see to the lamb for himself"** (v8) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים יִרְאֶה־לּוֹ הַשֶּׂה *Elohim yir'eh-lo ha-seh*. Verb *ra'ah* = see / consider / provide. Same verb as in v14 (Yahweh Yireh). Deliberate echo. "Provide" is derivative of "see to"; the Hebrew root is one.
- **"bound Isaac" (v9)** — Hebrew וַיַּעֲקֹד *wa-ya'aqod*. The binding verb gives the episode its Jewish title, the *Akedah* (binding). The verb occurs only here in the Bible.
- **"slay" → "slaughter"** (v10) — Hebrew שָׁחַט *shachat*. Technical term for cultic slaughter of a sacrificial animal. Its application to a human child marks the horror; the word is then transferred to the ram (v13 "offered it up").
- **"angel of the LORD" → "messenger of Yahweh"** (v11, 15) — as elsewhere.
- **"Jehovah-Jireh" → "Yahweh Yireh"** (v14) — Hebrew יְהוָה יִרְאֶה *Yahweh Yireh* = "Yahweh sees / will see / will provide." "Jehovah" is a Christian-Latin vocalization of the Tetragrammaton with Masoretic pointing; "Yahweh" is the probable original vocalization. Preserved.
- **"In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen" → "On the mount of Yahweh it shall be seen / provided"** (v14) — Hebrew בְּהַר יְהוָה יֵרָאֶה *be-har Yahweh yera'eh*. Niphal of *ra'ah*: "shall be seen" or "shall be provided for." Traditional prophetic pun: the mountain where Yahweh is seen is the mountain where Yahweh provides. Later identified with the temple mount (2 Chr 3:1).
- **"in blessing I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed" → "I will surely bless you, and surely multiply your offspring"** (v17) — Hebrew infinitive absolute construction: *barekh avarekhkha we-harbah arbeh*. KJV literal; modern idiom "surely / certainly" captures the intensifying force.
- **"blessed" → "find blessing"** (v18) — here the verb is hitpael יִתְבָּרְכוּ *yitbarekhu* — reflexive form, unambiguously meaning "they shall bless themselves / use your name in blessing formulae." The ambiguous niphal of 12:3, 18:18 is resolved here in the direction of reflexive — which favors the reflexive reading back at 12:3 too. "Find blessing in" captures both senses.
- **"concubine" (v24)** — Hebrew פִּילֶגֶשׁ *pilegesh*. Secondary wife / consort — lesser status than a full wife, but legitimate partner.

### Notes

- **The Akedah (the Binding)** — central Jewish and Christian text, with immense interpretive weight:
  - Theological: test of Abraham's faith (Heb 11:17–19), emblem of covenantal fidelity.
  - Typological (Christian): Isaac as prefigure of Christ, carrying his own wood to the place of sacrifice (cf. Jn 19:17).
  - Rabbinical: Isaac's consent in sacrifice (he is old enough to carry wood, old enough to resist); the *zekhut* (merit) of Isaac's willingness becomes atoning in later tradition.
  - Modern ethical: the "teleological suspension of the ethical" (Kierkegaard) — Abraham is asked to do what is both forbidden (murder) and impossible (undoing the covenant).
- **Child sacrifice** — widespread in Canaan and the ancient Near East; forbidden in Israelite law (Lev 18:21, 20:2–5). The Akedah affirms the cost / seriousness of what Yahweh demands *and* draws a red line: Yahweh ultimately rejects the child sacrifice that surrounding cults practice.
- **Isaac's disappearance** — after v19 (which says "Abraham returned") Isaac is not mentioned again until 24:62, and the narrative from this point foregrounds Abraham, then skips to Isaac's wife-choosing. The chapter's final verses redirect attention to Nahor's line — setting up Rebekah for ch 24.
