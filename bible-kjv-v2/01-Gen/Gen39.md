## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"a prosperous man" → "a successful man"** (v2) — Hebrew אִישׁ מַצְלִיחַ *ish matsliach*. From *tsalach* = prosper / succeed in one's endeavors. "Prosperous" in modern English suggests wealth; Hebrew means broader success.
- **"a goodly person, and well favoured" → "beautiful in form and beautiful in appearance"** (v6) — Hebrew יְפֵה־תֹאַר וִיפֵה מַרְאֶה *yephe-to'ar wi-yphe mar'eh*. Same phrase used of Rachel (29:17). Deliberate echo: the son inherits the mother's striking beauty.
- **"wotteth not" → "has no concern"** (v8) — Hebrew לֹא־יָדַע *lo-yada*. Archaic KJV "wotteth" from Old English "to know" = Hebrew *yada*. Here contextual: "does not concern himself with."
- **"how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?" (v9)** — Hebrew וְאֵיךְ אֶעֱשֶׂה הָרָעָה הַגְּדֹלָה הַזֹּאת וְחָטָאתִי לֵאלֹהִים. Joseph explicitly frames adultery as sin against *Elohim* — notable because the woman is Egyptian, the man is enslaved; Joseph brings the moral dimension beyond the household / social realm into the divine.
- **"lie by her, or to be with her" → "to lie beside her or to be with her"** (v10) — literal Hebrew: Joseph avoids even her physical proximity.
- **"prison" → "house of confinement"** (v20–23) — Hebrew בֵּית הַסֹּהַר *beit ha-sohar*. Unique term in the Hebrew Bible; probably technical Egyptian-loan term — hence the translator's footnote-note. Modern "prison" ok; preserved as literal "house of confinement" for the biblical term's specificity.
- **"keeper of the prison" → "warden of the house of confinement"** (v21, 22, 23) — Hebrew שַׂר בֵּית־הַסֹּהַר *sar beit ha-sohar* = "chief / prince of the house of confinement."
- **"shewed him mercy" → "extended lovingkindness to him"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיֵּט אֵלָיו חָסֶד *wa-yet elav chased*. *Chesed*, as elsewhere. Expressed as deliberate action.

### Notes

- **Pattern: Yahweh with Joseph** — the chapter emphasizes fourfold that "Yahweh was with Joseph" (v2, 3, 21, 23). Through enslavement, false accusation, imprisonment — Yahweh's presence and favor follow. The chapter's theological point: divine blessing is not absence of trouble but presence within trouble.
- **Parallel with the Tamar-Judah story (ch 38)** — Joseph resists illicit sex; Judah seeks it. Joseph preserves loyalty to his master; Judah breaks loyalty to Tamar. The narrative's placement of 38 and 39 side-by-side draws the contrast.
- **Cloak motif** — Joseph's ornamental tunic is stripped from him by brothers (37:23); here his garment is stripped by Potiphar's wife. Twice Joseph is betrayed by what others do with his clothes, twice he is stripped of his identity and descends lower.
