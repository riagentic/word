# Genesis 2

[1] Thus the heavens and the earth were completed, and all their array.

[2] On the seventh day *Elohim* finished the work he had done, and he rested on the seventh day from all the work he had done.

[3] *Elohim* blessed the seventh day and set it apart as holy, because on it he rested from all the work of creating that he had done.

[4] These are the generations of the heavens and the earth when they were created, on the day that Yahweh *Elohim* made earth and heavens.

[5] No shrub of the field had yet appeared on the earth, and no plant of the field had yet sprung up, because Yahweh *Elohim* had not sent rain on the earth, and there was no human to till the ground.

[6] But a stream would rise from the earth and water the whole face of the ground.

[7] Then Yahweh *Elohim* formed the human from the dust of the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the human became a living being.

[8] Yahweh *Elohim* planted a garden in Eden, in the east, and there he placed the human he had formed.

[9] Out of the ground Yahweh *Elohim* made grow every tree pleasing to the sight and good for food — and the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and bad.

[10] A river went out from Eden to water the garden, and from there it divided and became four headwaters.

[11] The name of the first is Pishon; it winds through the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold,

[12] and the gold of that land is good. Bdellium and onyx stone are there too.

[13] The name of the second river is Gihon; it winds through the whole land of Cush.

[14] The name of the third river is Tigris; it flows east of Asshur. And the fourth river is the Euphrates.

[15] Yahweh *Elohim* took the human and placed him in the garden of Eden to work it and to keep it.

[16] And Yahweh *Elohim* commanded the human, saying, "From every tree of the garden you may freely eat;

[17] but from the tree of the knowledge of good and bad you shall not eat, for on the day you eat of it you shall surely die."

[18] Then Yahweh *Elohim* said, "It is not good for the human to be alone. I will make for him a helper corresponding to him."

[19] So out of the ground Yahweh *Elohim* formed every wild animal of the field and every bird of the heavens, and brought them to the human to see what he would call them; and whatever the human called each living being, that was its name.

[20] The human gave names to all the livestock, to the birds of the heavens, and to every wild animal of the field; but for the human no helper corresponding to him was found.

[21] So Yahweh *Elohim* caused a deep sleep to fall on the human, and he slept; and he took one of his sides and closed up the flesh in its place.

[22] And Yahweh *Elohim* built the side he had taken from the human into a woman and brought her to the human.

[23] And the human said,

"This one, at last, is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh!
This one shall be called Woman,
for from Man was this one taken."

[24] Therefore a man leaves his father and his mother and clings to his wife, and they become one flesh.

[25] And the two of them were naked, the human and his wife, and they were not ashamed.

## Changes

- **"host" → "array"** (v1) — Hebrew צָבָא *tsava* = army / ordered host / array of heavenly and earthly beings. "Host" in modern English reads as innkeeper or broadcaster; the military / ordered-multitude sense is lost. "Array" preserves the sense of the fully-assembled, ordered whole.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v2, 3) — as in ch 1.
- **"LORD God" → "Yahweh *Elohim*"** (v4 onward) — Hebrew יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים *YHWH Elohim*. The Tetragrammaton (four-letter personal divine name, probably vocalized Yahweh) appears here for the first time in the Bible. KJV renders YHWH as "LORD" (small-caps) following Jewish reading tradition of substituting *Adonai* for the unspoken Name. Original divine names preserved.
- **"sanctified" → "set apart as holy"** (v3) — Hebrew קָדַשׁ *qadash* (piel) = make holy / set apart for sacred use. "Sanctified" is accurate but archaic.
- **"every plant of the field before it was in the earth" → "no shrub of the field had yet appeared on the earth"** (v5) — KJV's syntax is archaic and confusing. Hebrew is a "not yet" construction (טֶרֶם *terem*) — explicitly stating these had not yet come into being. Content unchanged; structure clarified.
- **"mist" → "stream"** (v6) — Hebrew אֵד *ed*, a rare word. KJV "mist" follows LXX πηγή / traditional Jewish reading; but Akkadian cognate *edu* = "underground water flow / stream rising up," confirmed by Ugaritic parallels. Modern scholarship: subterranean freshwater welling up to the surface. "Stream" captures this better than "mist."
- **"man" → "the human"** (v5, 7, 8, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 25) — Hebrew הָאָדָם *ha-adam* (with definite article). Not yet a personal name. Wordplay with אֲדָמָה *adamah* ("ground") preserved in the translation: "human… from the ground." See also "formed the human (*ha-adam*) from the dust of the ground (*ha-adamah*)."
- **"living soul" → "living being"** (v7) — Hebrew נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה *nephesh chayyah*. Exact same phrase used in Gen 1:20, 24, 30 for sea creatures, livestock, and wild animals. KJV translates it "living soul" only here (for the human), creating a false distinction not in the Hebrew. The text says the human is a *nephesh chayyah* just like other animate creatures — the distinctive human feature in the verse is the divine breath (*nishmat chayyim*), not the *nephesh* designation.
- **"eastward in Eden" → "in Eden, in the east"** (v8) — Hebrew בְּעֵדֶן מִקֶּדֶם *b'Eden mi-qedem*. *Eden* is the place name (also meaning "delight / luxuriance"); *mi-qedem* = "in / from the east." The garden is in Eden, and Eden is located in the east (from the perspective of the Israelite narrator).
- **"good and evil" → "good and bad"** (v9, 17) — Hebrew טוֹב וָרָע *tov va-ra*. *Ra* covers "bad / harmful / disagreeable / evil" — a broader range than English "evil," which has strong moral/spiritual overtones. In many contexts (including arguably here, since it pairs with experiential *da'at* "knowing"), "bad" is more accurate. The phrase is likely a merism meaning "everything" — the full range of moral / experiential discernment, not only moral good vs. moral evil.
- **"four heads" → "four headwaters"** (v10) — Hebrew רָאשִׁים *rashim* in this context = source-heads / branches of a river. "Heads" is literal but opaque in modern English.
- **"compasseth" → "winds through"** (v11, 13) — Hebrew סָבַב *sabav* = to encircle / wind around. "Compass" (verb) is archaic.
- **"Ethiopia" → "Cush"** (v13) — Hebrew כּוּשׁ *Kush*. KJV follows LXX Αἰθιοπία. "Cush" is the original name; it covers Nubia / upper Nile region in biblical usage, overlapping modern Sudan / southern Egypt, not modern Ethiopia. Original name kept.
- **"Hiddekel" → "Tigris"** (v14) — Hebrew חִדֶּקֶל *Ḥiddeqel*. KJV transliterates the Hebrew; it's the known name of the Tigris (Akkadian *Idiglat*, cognate). Rendered with the modern name for readability; transliteration unnecessary since the identification is secure.
- **"Assyria" → "Asshur"** (v14) — Hebrew אַשּׁוּר *Asshur*. Could denote the city of Asshur (ancient Assyrian capital) or the nation; translation preserves the ambiguity of the Hebrew.
- **"to dress it and to keep it" → "to work it and to keep it"** (v15) — Hebrew לְעָבְדָהּ וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ *le-ovdah u-leshomrah*. *Avad* = work / serve / cultivate. *Shamar* = guard / keep. "Dress" in 1611 English meant to cultivate; in modern English it means to put clothes on or season food.
- **"an help meet for him" → "a helper corresponding to him"** (v18, 20) — Hebrew עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ *ezer ke-negdo*. *Ezer* = "help / helper" — used elsewhere predominantly for God himself aiding his people (Ex 18:4, Deut 33:7, Ps 33:20, 115:9–11), so it carries no connotation of subordinate status. *Ke-negdo* = "corresponding to him / opposite him / matching him" — a counterpart, a partner in equivalence, not a subordinate. KJV's "help meet" (= "fitting help," old English) has been misread as "helpmate" implying a lesser helper; this is a common and serious mistranslation of the sense.
- **"fowl of the air" → "birds of the heavens"** (v19, 20) — Hebrew עוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם *'oph ha-shamayim*, literally "birds of the heavens" (not "air"). Consistent with Gen 1.
- **"every living creature" → "each living being"** (v19) — Hebrew נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה *nephesh chayyah*. Consistent with the correction made in v7.
- **"Adam" → "the human"** (v19, 20, 21) — Hebrew הָאָדָם *ha-adam* — still with the definite article. KJV switches inconsistently to the proper name "Adam" here; the Hebrew text does not. (The unambiguous transition from *ha-adam* to *Adam* as a personal name happens later — by Gen 4–5 the name is used without article.)
- **"rib" → "side"** (v21, 22) — Hebrew צֵלָע *tsela*. Used c. 40 times in the Hebrew Bible; almost always means "side" (of a building, the tabernacle, a hill, etc.). It is translated "rib" *only* here (in the KJV tradition via LXX πλευρά, which can mean either). The Hebrew just as naturally reads "one of his sides" — taking a whole side of the human, making the sexed pair from one originally undifferentiated being. This reading has ancient rabbinic support (Bereshit Rabbah) and is textually defensible. Rendered "side."
- **"made he a woman" → "built … into a woman"** (v22) — Hebrew וַיִּבֶן *wa-yiven*. Unusual verb for forming a body: *banah* = "to build," used of constructing houses, walls, cities. Creates a wordplay linking the woman's formation to architecture/construction; "made" flattens it.
- **"This is now bone of my bones…"** (v23) — Hebrew זֹאת הַפַּעַם *zot ha-pa'am* = "this one, at this time / at last" — an exclamation of recognition after the failed search among the animals. "This is now" in KJV is stiff.
- **"Woman… Man"** (v23) — Hebrew wordplay: אִשָּׁה *ishah* (woman) ← אִישׁ *ish* (man). English cannot reproduce the sound pair perfectly, but the Hebrew terms are given here so the wordplay is visible.
- **"cleave unto his wife" → "clings to his wife"** (v24) — Hebrew דָּבַק *davaq* = cling, stick fast, bind to. "Cleave" in modern English more often means the opposite (split) — a false friend.
- **v25 "both naked"** — Hebrew עֲרוּמִּים *arummim* ("naked"). A deliberate sound-play with עָרוּם *arum* ("cunning / shrewd") applied to the serpent in the very next verse (3:1). The wordplay is lost in translation but should be noted going into ch 3.

### Notes (context, not changes)

- **Two creation accounts** — Gen 1:1–2:4a and Gen 2:4b–25 have distinct vocabulary, style, and order of creation (ch 1: plants → animals → humans, male + female together; ch 2: human → plants/animals → woman from human's side). Classical source criticism assigns them to different strata (Priestly vs. Jahwist); they are read canonically as complementary. Not a translation issue but worth knowing as you read.
- **Divine name transition** — ch 1 uses *Elohim* alone; ch 2 introduces the compound *Yahweh Elohim* ("Yahweh, the God"). From ch 3 onward the names appear more independently.
