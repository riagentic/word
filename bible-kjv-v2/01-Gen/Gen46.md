# Genesis 46

[1] So Israel set out with all that he had, and came to Beersheba, and offered sacrifices to the *Elohim* of his father Isaac.

[2] *Elohim* spoke to Israel in the visions of the night and said, "Jacob, Jacob!" And he said, "Here I am."

[3] He said, "I am *Elohim*, the *Elohim* of your father. Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, for I will make you into a great nation there.

[4] I myself will go down with you to Egypt, and I myself will also surely bring you up again; and Joseph shall close your eyes with his hand."

[5] Then Jacob rose up from Beersheba; and the sons of Israel carried Jacob their father, their little ones, and their wives in the wagons that Pharaoh had sent to carry him.

[6] They took their livestock and their goods that they had acquired in the land of Canaan, and came to Egypt — Jacob and all his offspring with him,

[7] his sons and his sons' sons with him, his daughters and his sons' daughters; all his offspring he brought with him to Egypt.

[8] These are the names of the sons of Israel who came to Egypt: Jacob and his sons. Reuben, Jacob's firstborn.

[9] The sons of Reuben: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi.

[10] The sons of Simeon: Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul the son of a Canaanite woman.

[11] The sons of Levi: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.

[12] The sons of Judah: Er, Onan, Shelah, Perez, and Zerah; but Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan. The sons of Perez were Hezron and Hamul.

[13] The sons of Issachar: Tola, Puvah, Iob, and Shimron.

[14] The sons of Zebulun: Sered, Elon, and Jahleel.

[15] These are the sons of Leah, whom she bore to Jacob in Paddan-Aram, together with his daughter Dinah; all his sons and his daughters numbered thirty-three persons.

[16] The sons of Gad: Ziphion, Haggi, Shuni, Ezbon, Eri, Arodi, and Areli.

[17] The sons of Asher: Imnah, Ishvah, Ishvi, Beriah, and Serah their sister. The sons of Beriah: Heber and Malchiel.

[18] These are the sons of Zilpah, whom Laban gave to Leah his daughter; she bore these to Jacob — sixteen persons.

[19] The sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife: Joseph and Benjamin.

[20] To Joseph in the land of Egypt were born Manasseh and Ephraim, whom Asenath the daughter of Potiphera, priest of On, bore to him.

[21] The sons of Benjamin: Bela, Becher, Ashbel, Gera, Naaman, Ehi, Rosh, Muppim, Huppim, and Ard.

[22] These are the sons of Rachel who were born to Jacob — fourteen persons in all.

[23] The sons of Dan: Hushim.

[24] The sons of Naphtali: Jahzeel, Guni, Jezer, and Shillem.

[25] These are the sons of Bilhah, whom Laban gave to Rachel his daughter; and she bore these to Jacob — seven persons in all.

[26] All the persons who came with Jacob to Egypt, who were his direct descendants, besides the wives of Jacob's sons, were sixty-six persons in all.

[27] The sons of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt, were two persons. All the persons of the house of Jacob who came to Egypt were seventy.

[28] He sent Judah before him to Joseph to lead the way before him to Goshen; and they came to the land of Goshen.

[29] Joseph harnessed his chariot and went up to meet Israel his father at Goshen; and he presented himself to him, and fell on his neck and wept on his neck a long while.

[30] Then Israel said to Joseph, "Now let me die, since I have seen your face and know that you are still alive."

[31] Joseph said to his brothers and to his father's household, "I will go up and tell Pharaoh, and will say to him, 'My brothers and my father's household, who were in the land of Canaan, have come to me;

[32] and the men are shepherds, for they have been keepers of livestock; and they have brought their flocks, their herds, and all that they have.'

[33] When Pharaoh calls you and says, 'What is your occupation?'

[34] then you shall say, 'Your servants have been keepers of livestock from our youth until now, both we and our fathers' — in order that you may dwell in the land of Goshen, for every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"visions of the night" (v2)** — Hebrew בְּמַרְאֹת הַלַּיְלָה *be-mar'ot ha-laylah*. Nocturnal visions / dream-revelations.
- **"Joseph shall put his hand upon thine eyes" → "Joseph shall close your eyes with his hand"** (v4) — Hebrew וְיוֹסֵף יָשִׁית יָדוֹ עַל־עֵינֶיךָ. Ancient idiom: to close the eyes of the dying — a ritual filial duty. Joseph, whom Jacob thought dead, will instead perform for Jacob the closing-of-eyes at his own death.
- **"souls" → "persons"** (v15, 18, 22, 25, 26, 27) — Hebrew נֶפֶשׁ *nephesh*. Here by metonymy = persons (as in 12:5).
- **"Phallu" → "Pallu"** (v9) — Hebrew פַּלּוּא *Pallu*. KJV transliteration slightly off.
- **"Phuvah" / "Job" → "Puvah" / "Iob"** (v13) — Hebrew standardized. The "Job" here is not the Job of the book of Job — different name (יוֹב *Yov* vs. אִיּוֹב *Iyyov*); some MSS have *Yashuv*.
- **"Becher" (v21) onwards** — the Benjaminite list here has more names than the Numbers 26 list; source-critical issue not resolved in translation.
- **"seventy" (v27)** — the tradition of seventy descendants has sustained meaning (70 nations in Gen 10; 70 elders in Ex 24; 70 disciples in Luke 10). LXX reads "seventy-five" (adding Joseph's additional descendants); the difference lies behind Stephen's speech in Acts 7:14. MT's "seventy" retained.
- **"presented himself unto him" → "presented himself to him"** (v29) — Hebrew וַיֵּרָא אֵלָיו *wa-yera elav* — niphal of *ra'ah* = he appeared to him / showed himself. Same verb used of divine theophanies (e.g., 12:7 "Yahweh appeared to Abram"). Joseph's appearance to Jacob is deliberately theophanic in language.

### Notes

- **"Abomination to the Egyptians" (v34)** — sheep were ritually unclean to certain Egyptian cults (priests of Thebes in particular). Positioning the Hebrews in Goshen as shepherds isolates them from Egyptian society — politically providential, since it preserves their distinct identity during the growing-into-a-nation period.
- **The descent into Egypt** — fulfills the covenantal prediction of Gen 15:13–16 (four-hundred-year sojourn in a foreign land). Jacob fears (v3 "do not be afraid"); he must be reassured that leaving the land of promise for Egypt is within Yahweh's plan.
