# Ezra 9

[1] When these things were done, the officials approached me, saying, "The people of Israel, the priests, and the Levites have not separated themselves from the peoples of the lands; they have adopted the abominations of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Jebusites, the Ammonites, the Moabites, the Egyptians, and the Amorites.

[2] They have taken some of their daughters to be wives for themselves and their sons, so that the holy seed has mingled itself with the peoples of the lands; and the hand of the officials and leaders has been foremost in this unfaithfulness."

[3] When I heard this thing, I tore my garment and my robe, tore out hair from my head and my beard, and sat down appalled.

[4] Then everyone who trembled at the words of the *Elohim* of Israel gathered to me, on account of the unfaithfulness of those who had returned from exile. I sat appalled until the evening grain offering.

[5] At the evening grain offering I arose from my fasting, and with my garment and my robe torn, I fell upon my knees and spread out my hands to Yahweh my *Elohim*.

[6] I said: "O my *Elohim*, I am ashamed and humiliated to lift up my face to you, my *Elohim*; for our iniquities have mounted over our head, and our guilt has grown to the heavens.

[7] From the days of our fathers until this day we have been in great guilt; because of our iniquities we, our kings, and our priests have been delivered into the hand of the kings of the lands — to the sword, to captivity, to plunder, and to shame of face, as it is this day.

[8] But now for a brief moment favor has been shown from Yahweh our *Elohim*, to leave us a remnant to escape, and to give us a stake in his holy place, that our *Elohim* may brighten our eyes and give us a little reviving in our bondage.

[9] For we are slaves; yet in our bondage our *Elohim* has not forsaken us, but has extended steadfast love to us in the sight of the kings of Persia, to give us reviving — to set up the house of our *Elohim*, to repair its ruins, and to give us a wall in Judah and Jerusalem.

[10] Now, O our *Elohim*, what shall we say after this? For we have forsaken your commandments,

[11] which you commanded by your servants the prophets, saying, 'The land that you go to possess is an unclean land, with the uncleanness of the peoples of the lands, with their abominations that have filled it from end to end with their impurity.

[12] Therefore do not give your daughters to their sons, or take their daughters for your sons; do not seek their peace or their prosperity forever — that you may be strong, eat the good of the land, and leave it as an inheritance to your children forever.'

[13] After all that has come upon us for our evil deeds and for our great guilt — since you our *Elohim* have punished us less than our iniquities deserved and have given us such a deliverance as this —

[14] shall we again break your commandments and intermarry with the peoples of these abominations? Would you not be angry with us until you had consumed us, so that there should be no remnant, no survivor?

[15] O Yahweh the *Elohim* of Israel, you are righteous; for we are left as a remnant, as it is this day. Behold, we are before you in our guilt, though no one can stand before you because of this."

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"princes... rulers" → "officials... leaders"** (v1, 2) — *sarim / seganim*.
- **"trespass" → "unfaithfulness / guilt"** (v2, 4, 6, 7, 13, 14, 15) — *ma'al*; *ashmah*.
- **"astonied" → "appalled"** (v3, 4) — *meshomem*.
- **"evening sacrifice" → "evening grain offering"** (v4, 5) — *minchah*.
- **"heaviness" → "fasting"** (v5) — *ta'anit*.

### Notes
- **Mixed marriages crisis** — post-exilic community's most divisive internal issue. Ezra judges wholesale intermarriage as covenant unfaithfulness.
- **"Holy seed"** (v2) — biological theology of election applied to marriage. Malachi 2, Neh 13 parallel concerns.
- **List of peoples** — some extinct by Ezra's day (Hittites, Jebusites); rhetorical: covers all unclean categories.
- **Self-mortification** — hair-tearing, tearing of robes, appalled sitting — extreme ritual grief. Rabbinic tradition sees Ezra's response as definitive.
- **Prayer pattern** — confession, historical recital, current grace, plea. Classic Second-Temple prayer form (cf. Dan 9, Neh 9).
- **"Punished less than our iniquities deserved"** — theological balance: Yahweh's discipline restrained by mercy.
- **"A stake in his holy place"** — secure foothold in the temple-promised land. Precarious but real.
