# Ezra 3

[1] When the seventh month came, and the children of Israel were in their cities, the people gathered themselves as one man to Jerusalem.

[2] Then Jeshua son of Jozadak, with his brothers the priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, with his brothers, arose and built the altar of the *Elohim* of Israel, to offer burnt offerings on it as it is written in the Law of Moses the man of *Elohim*.

[3] They set the altar on its foundation, for fear was upon them because of the peoples of the lands; and they offered burnt offerings on it to Yahweh, burnt offerings morning and evening.

[4] They kept the Feast of Booths as it is written, and offered the daily burnt offerings by number, according to the ordinance, as each day required;

[5] and afterward the continual burnt offering, the offerings for the new moons and for all the appointed feasts of Yahweh that are sanctified, and for everyone who offered a freewill offering to Yahweh.

[6] From the first day of the seventh month they began to offer burnt offerings to Yahweh; but the foundation of the temple of Yahweh had not yet been laid.

[7] They gave silver also to the masons and the carpenters; and food, drink, and oil to the Sidonians and the Tyrians, to bring cedar logs from Lebanon to the sea of Joppa, according to the grant that they had from Cyrus king of Persia.

[8] In the second year of their coming to the house of *Elohim* at Jerusalem, in the second month, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, Jeshua son of Jozadak, the rest of their brothers the priests and the Levites, and all who had come out of the captivity to Jerusalem began; they appointed the Levites, from twenty years old and upward, to oversee the work of the house of Yahweh.

[9] Jeshua with his sons and his brothers, Kadmiel and his sons, the sons of Judah, stood together to oversee the workmen in the house of *Elohim*; also the sons of Henadad, with their sons and brothers, the Levites.

[10] When the builders laid the foundation of the temple of Yahweh, they set the priests in their vestments, with trumpets, and the Levites the sons of Asaph with cymbals, to praise Yahweh according to the ordinance of David king of Israel.

[11] They sang responsively in praising and giving thanks to Yahweh:

"For he is good;
for his steadfast love — endures forever toward Israel."

All the people shouted with a great shout in praising Yahweh, because the foundation of the house of Yahweh had been laid.

[12] But many of the priests, Levites, and heads of fathers' houses — old men who had seen the first house — wept with a loud voice when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes; and many shouted aloud for joy.

[13] The people could not distinguish the sound of the shout of joy from the sound of the people's weeping, for the people shouted with a great shout, and the sound was heard far away.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"set feasts" → "appointed feasts"** (v5) — *mo'adim*.
- **"money" → "silver"** (v7).
- **"meat" → "food"** (v7) — *okel*; general provisions, not just flesh.
- **"Zidon" → "Sidon"** (v7).
- **"ancient men" → "old men"** (v12) — *zeqenim*.

### Notes
- **Seventh month** — Tishri. Begins with Feast of Trumpets, Yom Kippur, and Sukkot. Appropriate month for restoration.
- **Altar first** — even before temple foundation, daily sacrifice restored. Worship prioritized over building.
- **"Fear of the peoples of the lands"** (v3) — hostile neighbors (Samarians, Arabs). Worship a defiant act.
- **Second year, second month** (v8) — builds begin roughly 536 BC. Parallels Solomon's temple-start.
- **"The sons of Judah"** (v9) — MT *bnei Yehudah*; in this Levite list likely = "the sons of Hodaviah" (2:40; Neh 7:43 Hodevah), the two names being close in Hebrew. MT kept.
- **Phoenician cedar again** — repeats Solomon's pattern with Hiram. Cyrus's grant provides the Persian state-level funding.
- **Mixed tears and shouts** (v12-13) — older generation remembers First Temple's glory; younger rejoices at any restoration. Hag 2:3 echoes this ambivalence.
- **"His chesed endures forever"** — standard liturgical refrain (Ps 136). Continuity with pre-exilic worship.
