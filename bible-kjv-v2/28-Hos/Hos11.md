# Hosea 11

[1] **When Israel was a child, I loved him,
and out of Egypt I called my son.**

[2] The more they were called, the more they went away;
they kept sacrificing to the Baals
and burning offerings to idols.

[3] Yet it was I who taught Ephraim to walk;
I took them up by their arms,
but they did not know that I healed them.

[4] I led them with cords of kindness,
with the bands of love,
and I became to them as one who eases the yoke on their jaws,
and I bent down to them and fed them.

[5] They shall not return to the land of Egypt,
but Assyria shall be their king,
because they have refused to return to me.

[6] The sword shall rage against their cities,
consume the bars of their gates,
and devour them because of their own counsels.

[7] My people are bent on turning away from me,
and though they call out to the Most High,
he shall not raise them up at all.

[8] How can I give you up, O Ephraim?
How can I hand you over, O Israel?
How can I make you like Admah?
How can I treat you like Zevoyim?
My heart recoils within me;
my compassion grows warm and tender.

[9] I will not execute my burning anger;
I will not again destroy Ephraim;
for I am *Elohim* and not a man,
the Holy One in your midst,
and I will not come in wrath.

[10] They shall go after Yahweh;
he will roar like a lion;
when he roars, his children shall come trembling from the west;

[11] they shall come trembling like birds from Egypt,
and like doves from the land of Assyria,
and I will return them to their homes, declares Yahweh.

[12] Ephraim has surrounded me with lies,
and the house of Israel with deceit,
but Judah still walks with *Elohim*
and is faithful to the Holy One.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **Poetry formatted with blockquote indentation** (throughout) — KJV prose; Hebrew is among the most tender poetry in the prophets.
- **"As they called them, so they went from them" → "The more they were called, the more they went away"** (v2) — Hebrew כְּקָרְאָם כֵּן הָלְכוּ מִפְּנֵיהֶם; the Hebrew idiom means the more insistently they were called, the more they departed.
- **"burned incense to graven images" → "burning offerings to idols"** (v2) — Hebrew יְקַטְּרוּן לַפְּסִלִים; "offerings" more general.
- **"taking them by their arms" → "I took them up by their arms"** (v3) — modernized.
- **"I drew them with cords of a man" → "I led them with cords of kindness"** (v4) — Hebrew בְּחַבְלֵי אָדָם *bechavlei adam* = "with cords of a human being" = cords of human kindness; "kindness" better than literal.
- **"as they that take off the yoke on their jaws" → "as one who eases the yoke on their jaws"** (v4) — modernized; the image is of loosening the yoke to let the animal eat.
- **"I laid meat unto them" → "I bent down to them and fed them"** (v4) — Hebrew אַגִּישׁ *aggish* + אוֹכִיל *okil*; "bent down" captures the intimate pastoral image.
- **"none at all would exalt him" → "he shall not raise them up at all"** (v7) — the subject pronoun is ambiguous in Hebrew; the KJV "exalt him" follows one reading; "raise them up" follows another; rendered to match context.
- **"mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together" → "My heart recoils within me; my compassion grows warm and tender"** (v8) — Hebrew נֶהְפַּךְ עָלַי לִבִּי *nehpach alai libbi* = "my heart is overturned within me"; נִכְמְרוּ נִחוּמָי *nichmeru nichumai* = "my compassions are heated" — the tender emotional language is preserved.
- **"I will not enter into the city" → "I will not come in wrath"** (v9) — Hebrew לֹא אָבוֹא בְּעִיר; textually debated; "in wrath" follows the emotional context (divine relenting from anger) rather than the topographic "into the city."
- **"walketh after the Lord" → "go after Yahweh"** (v10) — modernized.

### Notes
- **v1** — "Out of Egypt I called my son": quoted Matt 2:15 (Yeshua's return from Egypt).
