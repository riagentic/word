## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **The shorter Greek text** — the oldest Greek text lacks v1–5, 10–11, 17–19, the second half of v29 and v30 (from memory). The Masoretic Text is translated in full.
- **"the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David" → "was bound to the soul of David"** (v1) — Hebrew נִקְשְׁרָה, from קָשַׁר "bind, tie" (Gen 44:30). נֶפֶשׁ is "soul". **"loved him as his own soul"** (v1, 3) — Hebrew כְּנַפְשׁוֹ, "as his soul". In v1 the reading tradition (qere) is translated; the written form differs only in spelling.
- **"love"** (v1, 3, 16, 20, 22, 28) — one Hebrew verb, אָהֵב, of Jonathan, of all Israel and Judah, of Michal and of Saul's servants; it is "love" each time.
- **"would let him go no more home" → "did not let him return"** (v2) — Hebrew וְלֹא נְתָנוֹ לָשׁוּב, literally "did not give him to return".
- **"Jonathan and David made a covenant" → "cut a covenant"** (v3) — Hebrew כָּרַת בְּרִית. The verb is singular ("and Jonathan cut, and David"). **"because he loved him"** — Hebrew בְּאַהֲבָתוֹ אֹתוֹ, literally "in his loving him".
- **"his garments, even to his sword" kept** (v4) — Hebrew מַדָּיו, as 17:38; וְעַד "and even to", three times.
- **"behaved himself wisely" → "acted with insight"** (v5, 14, 15, 30) — Hebrew הִשְׂכִּיל: to act with insight, and so to succeed; both are in the word. In v30 the simple stem שָׂכַל stands, found only here. **"he was accepted in the sight of" → "it was good in the eyes of"** (v5) — Hebrew וַיִּיטַב בְּעֵינֵי.
- **"singing and dancing" → "to sing, and the dances"** (v6) — Hebrew לָשִׁיר וְהַמְּחֹלוֹת (reading tradition; the written form is unclear), as it stands. **"tabrets" → "timbrels"**. **"instruments of musick" → "three-stringed instruments"** — Hebrew שָׁלִשִׁים, an instrument named only here; the word has to do with "three" (three strings, three corners or three bars), and what it was is not known.
- **"answered one another as they played" → "the women who were playing answered"** (v7) — Hebrew עָנָה "answer" (of singing in turn); שִׂחֵק "play, make merry". "One another" is not in the Hebrew. **"his thousands"** — the reading tradition; the written text has "his thousand".
- **"Saul was very wroth, and the saying displeased him" → "burned with anger greatly, and this word was bad in his eyes"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיִּחַר לְשָׁאוּל, literally "it burned to Saul"; וַיֵּרַע בְּעֵינָיו. **"ascribed" → "given"** — Hebrew נָתַן. **"what can he have more but the kingdom" → "what more is there for him but the kingship"** — Hebrew וְעוֹד לוֹ אַךְ הַמְּלוּכָה, literally "and still for him only the kingship".
- **"Saul eyed David" → "was eyeing David"** (v9) — Hebrew עוֹיֵן (reading tradition), a verb made from עַיִן "eye", found only here.
- **"the evil spirit from God came upon Saul" → "an evil spirit of Elohim rushed upon Saul"** (v10) — Hebrew וַתִּצְלַח רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים רָעָה אֶל־שָׁאוּל, the verb of 10:10 and 16:13; "a spirit of Elohim, an evil one", as 16:15.
- **"he prophesied" kept** (v10) — Hebrew וַיִּתְנַבֵּא, the same word as of Saul among the prophets (10:10; 19:23, 24) and of the prophets themselves. It names the behavior of one seized by a spirit, speech or frenzy; here the spirit is an evil one, and "raved" is the sense commonly given. The word is not changed.
- **"javelin" → "spear"** (v10, 11) — Hebrew חֲנִית, with the article.
- **"Saul cast the javelin; for he said, I will smite David even to the wall" → "hurled the spear, and he said, 'I will strike David and the wall'"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיָּטֶל, "hurled" (the same letters can be read "lifted"); אַכֶּה בְדָוִד וּבַקִּיר, literally "I will strike into David and into the wall": pin him to it. **"avoided out of his presence" → "turned away from his face"**.
- **"was afraid of" → "feared" / "was afraid of"** (v12, 15, 29) — two verbs: יָרֵא "fear" (v12, 29) and יָגֹר "be afraid of" (v15).
- **"captain over a thousand" → "commander of a thousand"** (v13) — Hebrew שַׂר־אָלֶף.
- **"valiant" → "a son of ability"** (v17) — Hebrew בֶּן־חַיִל. **"For Saul said"** — Hebrew וְשָׁאוּל אָמַר, "and Saul said": to himself; so also v21.
- **"what is my life, or my father's family" → "who is my life, the clan of my father"** (v18) — Hebrew וּמִי חַיַּי מִשְׁפַּחַת אָבִי, as the Masoretic Text points it: "who" and "my life". The words are difficult; many read the same letters as "my kin" (a conjecture) and take "the clan of my father" as its explanation.
- **"at the time when Merab … should have been given" → "at the time of giving Merab"** (v19) — Hebrew בְּעֵת תֵּת.
- **"the thing pleased him" → "the thing was right in his eyes"** (v20, 26) — Hebrew וַיִּשַׁר … בְּעֵינָיו.
- **"that she may be a snare to him" → "and let her be a snare to him"** (v21) — Hebrew וּתְהִי, a wish. **"Thou shalt this day be my son in law in the one of the twain" → "A second time you shall become son-in-law to me today"** — Hebrew בִּשְׁתַּיִם, literally "in two" (Job 33:14): a second time, or by the second daughter. The word is difficult.
- **"be the king's son in law" → "become son-in-law to the king"** (v18, 21–23, 26, 27) — Hebrew הִתְחַתֵּן בְּ, the verb of חָתָן "son-in-law".
- **"Commune with David secretly" → "Speak to David in secret"** (v22) — Hebrew בַּלָּט. **"the king hath delight in thee"** — Hebrew חָפֵץ בְּ.
- **"Seemeth it to you a light thing … seeing that I am a poor man, and lightly esteemed" → "Is it a light thing in your eyes …? And I am a poor man and made light of"** (v23) — Hebrew הַנְקַלָּה and נִקְלֶה, two forms of "be light".
- **"The king desireth not any dowry" → "The king has no delight in a bride-price"** (v25) — Hebrew אֵין־חֵפֶץ … בְּמֹהַר: חֵפֶץ is the noun of "delights" in v22; מֹהַר is the price paid for a bride to her father. **"foreskins"** — Hebrew עָרְלוֹת, the plain word. **"Saul thought" → "Saul meant"** — Hebrew חָשַׁב.
- **"the days were not expired" → "the days were not full"** (v26) — Hebrew וְלֹא מָלְאוּ הַיָּמִים.
- **"slew of the Philistines two hundred men" → "struck down among the Philistines two hundred men"** (v27) — so the Masoretic Text; the Septuagint has "a hundred" (from memory; so also 2 Sam 3:14). **"they gave them in full tale to the king" → "they gave them in full to the king"** — Hebrew וַיְמַלְאוּם, literally "and they filled them", the verb of "full" in v26; the plural "they" stands in the Masoretic Text.
- **"and that Michal Saul's daughter loved him" → "and Michal the daughter of Saul loved him"** (v28) — in the Hebrew a clause of its own. The Septuagint has "and all Israel loved him" (from memory).
- **"Saul was yet the more afraid of David" → "feared David yet more"** (v29) — Hebrew וַיֹּאסֶף … לֵרֹא … עוֹד, literally "added to fear … still". **"became David's enemy continually" → "was an enemy to David all the days"**.
- **"the princes of the Philistines" → "the commanders of the Philistines"** (v30) — Hebrew שָׂרֵי, "princes", of an army "commanders". **"his name was much set by" → "his name was very precious"** — Hebrew וַיִּיקַר.

### Notes
- **Jonathan's love** — one of OT's great friendship narratives. Jonathan, the natural heir to Saul, freely yields to David. The robe-giving (v4) is symbolic abdication.
- **Women's song** (v7) — Saul's thousands, David's ten-thousands. Poetic parallelism (the second line intensifying the first is normal Hebrew poetry), but Saul reads it as slight.
- **Spear-throwing** (v11) — Saul's first attempt on David's life. Twice. David evades.
- **Merab then Michal** — two daughters successively offered. Michal loves David; Saul weaponizes this.
- **100 foreskins** (v25) — gruesome bride-price. Saul expects David to die attempting it. David doubles the count.
- **Saul's growing dread** — v12, v15, v29. The king's paranoia deepens as his popularity wanes relative to David's.
