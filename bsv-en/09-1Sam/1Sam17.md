# 1 Samuel 17

[1] And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ gathered their camps for war, and they were gathered at ⟦socoh|Sochoh|Socoh⟧, which belongs to ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, and they camped between ⟦socoh|Sochoh|Socoh⟧ and ⟦azekah|Azeqah|Azekah⟧, in ⟦ephes-dammim|Efes-Dammim|Ephes-dammim⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **The shorter Greek text** — the oldest Greek text of this chapter is much shorter than the Masoretic Text: it lacks v12–31, v41, v48b, v50 and v55–58 (and 18:1–5), the parts in which David comes to the army as an unknown shepherd (from memory). The Masoretic Text is translated in full.
- **"armies" → "camps"** (v1, 4, 46, 53) — Hebrew מַחֲנֶה "camp", also of the host in it. **"to battle" → "for war"** (v1, 13) — Hebrew מִלְחָמָה, "war", and of one fight "battle" (v2, 8, 20, 28, 47): one word.
- **"set the battle in array" → "drew up for battle"; "armies" → "battle lines"** (v2, 8, 10, 20–23, 26, 36, 45, 48) — Hebrew עָרַךְ מִלְחָמָה, literally "arrange battle", and the noun of the same root, מַעֲרָכָה "arrangement": the ordered line of an army. v21 has it three times: "arranged, arrangement to meet arrangement".
- **"the valley of Elah" kept; "a valley" → "the valley"** (v2, 3, 19) — two Hebrew words: עֵמֶק הָאֵלָה, "the valley of the terebinth", a broad valley, and in v3 הַגַּיְא, a narrow valley, the ravine between the two hills. English has one word.
- **"on a mountain on the one side" → "on the mountain on this side … on that side"** (v3) — Hebrew אֶל־הָהָר מִזֶּה, twice the same words.
- **"a champion" → "the champion"** (v4, 23) — Hebrew אִישׁ־הַבֵּנַיִם, literally "the man of the between": the man who steps into the space between two armies to fight for his side. The expression stands only in this chapter; "champion" is the commonly accepted sense. In v51 "champion" of the KJV is another word, גִּבּוֹר "mighty one".
- **"six cubits and a span" kept** (v4) — so the Masoretic Text (about three meters). The Septuagint and a Samuel scroll from Qumran have "four cubits and a span" (from memory).
- **"brass" → "bronze"** (v5, 6, 38) — Hebrew נְחֹשֶׁת.
- **"armed with a coat of mail" → "clothed with armor of scales"** (v5, 38) — Hebrew שִׁרְיוֹן קַשְׂקַשִּׂים: שִׁרְיוֹן is body armor, קַשְׂקֶשֶׂת a scale (of a fish, Lev 11:9). "Mail" is armor of rings. The shekel is a weight (five thousand shekels: well over fifty kilograms).
- **"greaves of brass upon his legs" → "greaves of bronze were on his legs"** (v6) — Hebrew מִצְחַת, a singular, found only here; it is taken as a leg guard, and the sense is not certain. **"a target of brass" → "a javelin of bronze"** — Hebrew כִּידוֹן, a weapon for throwing or striking (Josh 8:18); its kind is not certain.
- **"the staff of his spear" → "the shaft of his spear"** (v7) — the written text has חץ "arrow"; the reading tradition (qere) has עֵץ "wood", and it is translated. **"his spear's head" → "the blade of his spear"** — Hebrew לַהֶבֶת, literally "flame". **"one bearing a shield" → "the bearer of the shield"** (v7, 41) — Hebrew צִנָּה, the large shield that covers the body; מָגֵן is the small one.
- **"servants to Saul" → "slaves of Saul"** (v8, 9) — Hebrew עֲבָדִים, the same word in both verses; v9 speaks of bondage, so "slaves" in both. In v32, 34, 36, 58 the word is the speaker's "your servant". **"choose"** (v8) — Hebrew בְּרוּ, a form found only here; "choose" is the commonly accepted sense.
- **"and to kill me" → "and strikes me down"** (v9, 25–27, 36, 46, 57) — Hebrew הִכָּה "strike", "strike down" where killing is meant; in v35, 49, 50 "strike". **"prevail against him" → "overcome him"** (v9) — Hebrew אוּכַל־לוֹ, literally "am able for him".
- **"I defy" → "I have reproached"** (v10, 25, 26, 36, 45) — Hebrew חֵרֵף "reproach, taunt", the verb of חֶרְפָּה "reproach" (v26). The perfect in v10 is said in the act of speaking.
- **"the son of that Ephrathite" → "the son of this Ephrathite man"** (v12) — Hebrew בֶּן־אִישׁ אֶפְרָתִי הַזֶּה, with "this", as it stands. **"went among men for an old man" → "was old, advanced among men"** — Hebrew זָקֵן בָּא בַאֲנָשִׁים, literally "old, come among men". The words are difficult; some versions have "advanced in years" (from memory).
- **"went and followed Saul" → "went; they went after Saul"** (v13) — the Hebrew has the verb twice. **"eldest" / "youngest"** (v13, 14, 28) — literally "the great ones", "the small one".
- **"went and returned from Saul to feed his father's sheep" → "was going and returning from Saul to shepherd the flock of his father"** (v15) — Hebrew participles; רָעָה "shepherd", צֹאן "flock".
- **"presented himself" → "took his stand"** (v16) — Hebrew הִתְיַצֵּב. "Morning and evening" is literally "rising early and doing at evening".
- **"parched corn" → "parched grain"; "run to the camp" → "run them to the camp"** (v17) — Hebrew וְהָרֵץ, the causative: "make run", carry quickly.
- **"ten cheeses" → "ten cuts of milk"** (v18) — Hebrew חֲרִצֵי הֶחָלָב, found only here: slices or portions of milk, taken as cheeses. **"the captain of their thousand" → "the commander of the thousand"** — Hebrew שַׂר־הָאָלֶף. **"look how thy brethren fare" → "your brothers you shall visit as to their shalom"** — Hebrew תִּפְקֹד לְשָׁלוֹם: פָּקַד "visit, attend to", שָׁלוֹם "peace, well-being". **"take their pledge"** — Hebrew עֲרֻבָּה, a token brought back as proof.
- **v19** — the Hebrew has no verb; the verse may be the end of Jesse's words ("are") or the narrator's ("were").
- **"left the sheep with a keeper, and took" → "left the flock with a keeper, and he lifted up"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיִּשָּׂא "and he lifted up", with no object named. **"to the trench" → "to the enclosure"** — Hebrew מַעְגָּל, the round of the camp or its ring of wagons (26:5, 7); the sense is not certain. **"the host" → "the force"** — Hebrew חַיִל; it has the article, "the force that goes out". **"shouted for the battle"** — Hebrew וְהֵרֵעוּ בַּמִּלְחָמָה.
- **"his carriage" → "the gear"; "saluted his brethren" → "asked about the shalom of his brothers"** (v22) — Hebrew כֵּלִים, any made things; the same word is "bag" in v40, 49 and Goliath's "gear" in v54. וַיִּשְׁאַל … לְשָׁלוֹם is the usual greeting.
- **"out of the armies of the Philistines" → "from the battle lines of the Philistines"** (v23) — so the reading tradition (qere); the written text has "from the caves of".
- **"fled from him" → "fled from his face"** (v24) — Hebrew מִפָּנָיו.
- **"taketh away the reproach" → "removes reproach"** (v26) — Hebrew הֵסִיר, the verb of v39 and v46.
- **"the living God" → "the living *Elohim*"** (v26, 36) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים חַיִּים: the adjective is plural, as the form of the noun is. It may be read "living gods"; of the one Elohim of Israel the plural form is the plural of the name (Deut 5:26).
- **"Eliab's anger was kindled" → "the anger of Eliab burned"** (v28) — Hebrew וַיִּחַר־אַף, literally "the nose burned". **"thy pride, and the naughtiness of thine heart" → "your presumption and the evil of your heart"** — Hebrew זָדוֹן and רֹעַ.
- **"Is there not a cause?" → "Is it not a word?"** (v29) — Hebrew הֲלוֹא דָּבָר הוּא. דָּבָר is "word" or "matter": either "was it not only a word?" or "is it not a matter (of weight)?". The Hebrew is left open.
- **"he sent for him" → "he took him"** (v31) — Hebrew וַיִּקָּחֵהוּ.
- **"Let no man's heart fail because of him" → "Let not the heart of a man fall because of him"** (v32) — Hebrew אַל־יִפֹּל לֵב־אָדָם עָלָיו; עָלָיו is "upon him": "because of him" (the Philistine) or "within him". The Septuagint has "the heart of my lord" (from memory).
- **"thou art but a youth" → "you are a boy"; "stripling" → "lad"** (v33, 42, 55, 56, 58) — Hebrew נַעַר, a boy or a young man; "but / only" is not in the Hebrew. "From his youth" (v33) is the same root. In v56 the word is עֶלֶם, a grown youth.
- **"there came a lion, and a bear, and took a lamb out of the flock" → "when the lion came, and the bear, and took a sheep from the drove"** (v34, 35) — the verbs have the form of repeated acts ("would go out … would strike"); only "And he rose against me" is a single past. "The lion" and "the bear" have the article; "the bear" has the object marker, which is difficult. שֶׂה is one animal of the flock, עֵדֶר a drove.
- **"out of the paw of the lion" → "from the hand of the lion"** (v37) — Hebrew יָד "hand" three times. **"the LORD be with thee" → "Yahweh will be with you"** — Hebrew יִהְיֶה, the plain future form; it may be meant as a wish.
- **"armed David with his armour" → "clothed David with his garments"** (v38, 39) — Hebrew מַדָּיו, his clothes (of a soldier's dress, Judg 3:16), not the word for armor.
- **"he assayed to go; for he had not proved it" → "he undertook to walk, for he had not tested them"** (v39) — Hebrew וַיֹּאֶל לָלֶכֶת "he undertook / made a beginning to walk"; the reason that follows fits it loosely. The Septuagint has "he grew weary walking" (from memory). "Them" is supplied.
- **"staff" → "rod"; "staves" → "the rods"** (v40, 43) — Hebrew מַקֵּל, a cut stick; in v43 plural, with the article. **"brook" → "wadi"** — Hebrew נַחַל. **"in a shepherd's bag which he had, even in a scrip" → "in the shepherds' bag that he had, and in the pouch"** — Hebrew בִּכְלִי הָרֹעִים … וּבַיַּלְקוּט: כְּלִי is "vessel"; יַלְקוּט stands only here, and its "and" is difficult (perhaps "that is, in the pouch").
- **"disdained" → "despised"** (v42) — Hebrew בָּזָה. **"of a fair countenance" → "with a beautiful appearance"** — Hebrew עִם־יְפֵה מַרְאֶה, as 16:12.
- **"cursed David by his gods"** (v43) — Hebrew קִלֵּל, "make light of, curse"; בֵּאלֹהָיו is "by his gods" or "by his god".
- **"fowls of the air" → "birds of the heavens"; "wild beasts of the earth" → "living things of the earth"** (v44, 46) — Hebrew עוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם; בֶּהֱמַת הַשָּׂדֶה (v44), חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ (v46).
- **"with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield" → "with sword and with spear and with javelin"** (v45) — Hebrew כִּידוֹן, as v6.
- **"the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied" → "Yahweh of Hosts, the *Elohim* of the battle lines of Israel, whom you have reproached"** (v45) — Hebrew יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת אֱלֹהֵי מַעַרְכוֹת יִשְׂרָאֵל אֲשֶׁר חֵרַפְתָּ. צְבָאוֹת is "hosts, armies"; "armies of Israel" in the KJV is another word, the "battle lines" of v8. אֲשֶׁר may look back to the Elohim ("whom") or to the battle lines ("which"): v10, 26, 36 say that he reproached the battle lines.
- **"will the LORD deliver thee into mine hand" → "Yahweh will shut you up in my hand"** (v46) — Hebrew יְסַגֶּרְךָ, from סָגַר "shut": to hand over. In v47 "give you into our hand" is נָתַן. **"take thine head from thee" → "remove your head from upon you"**. **"the carcases" → "the carcass"** — Hebrew פֶּגֶר, a singular for the whole. **"that all the earth may know" → "and all the earth shall know"** (v46, 47) — Hebrew וְיֵדְעוּ, "and they shall know"; the form can also give the purpose ("that … may know"). **"a God in Israel" → "an *Elohim* for Israel"** — Hebrew לְיִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"the LORD saveth not with sword and spear: for the battle is the LORD's" kept in the Hebrew order** (v47) — Hebrew לַיהוָה הַמִּלְחָמָה, "to Yahweh is the battle".
- **"ran toward the army" → "ran to the battle line"** (v48).
- **"put his hand in his bag" → "stretched out his hand to the bag"** (v49) — Hebrew שָׁלַח יָד.
- **"prevailed over" → "was stronger than"** (v50) — Hebrew וַיֶּחֱזַק … מִן, from חָזַק "be strong". **"slew him" → "put him to death"** (v50, 51) — Hebrew הֵמִית in v50; in v51 another stem of the same verb, וַיְמֹתְתֵהוּ, "made an end of him": the two verses tell the death twice.
- **"their champion" → "their mighty one"** (v51) — Hebrew גִּבּוֹרָם.
- **"until thou come to the valley" → "until you come to Gai"** (v52) — Hebrew עַד־בּוֹאֲךָ גַיְא, "until your coming to a valley"; the word has no article and is taken here as a place name. The Septuagint has "Gath" (from memory). **"the wounded" → "the slain"** — Hebrew חֲלָלִים. **"by the way to Shaaraim" → "on the way of Shaaraim"** — the name means "two gates".
- **"from chasing after" → "from burning after"; "spoiled their tents" → "pillaged their camps"** (v53) — Hebrew דָּלַק "burn", of hot pursuit; שָׁסַס, another verb than בָּזַז "plunder".
- **"his armour" → "his gear"** (v54) — Hebrew כֵּלָיו.
- **"captain of the host" → "the commander of the host"** (v55) — Hebrew שַׂר הַצָּבָא. **"As thy soul liveth, O king, I cannot tell" → "By the life of your soul, O king, I do not know"** — Hebrew חֵי־נַפְשְׁךָ הַמֶּלֶךְ אִם־יָדָעְתִּי: an oath, literally "the life of your soul, the king, if I know".
- **"Enquire thou" → "Ask, you"** (v56) — Hebrew שְׁאַל אַתָּה.
- **"I am the son of" → "The son of"** (v58) — the Hebrew has no "I am".

### Notes
- **Goliath of Gath** — possibly descendant of the Anakim remnant (Josh 11:22). Elsewhere (2 Sam 21:19) "Elhanan" kills Goliath; 1 Chr 20:5 has "Lahmi, the brother of Goliath." Textual tradition has complexity here.
- **Two David-introductions** — ch 16 (Saul's musician) and ch 17 (unknown shepherd; Saul asks "whose son?") seem to reflect two separate traditions. The Septuagint of 1 Sam 17 is shorter than the Masoretic Text and lacks the passages that create the tension (v12–31, 55–58). Textual-critical issue.
- **"Five smooth stones"** (v40) — one for Goliath, four for his brothers (2 Sam 21:15–22, four Philistine giants). Possibly.
- **"The battle is Yahweh's"** (v47) — theological statement. Victory is not technological but divine.
- **David's age** — "boy" (*na'ar*), a word that reaches from a child to a young man. Old enough to fight; young enough to be dismissed.
- **Head brought to Jerusalem** (v54) — anachronistic (Jerusalem not yet Israelite until David captures it, 2 Sam 5). Perhaps the head was taken there later, or the text reflects later memory.
