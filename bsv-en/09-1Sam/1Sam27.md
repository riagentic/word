# 1 Samuel 27

[1] And David said to his heart, "Now I shall be swept away one day by the hand of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧. There is nothing good for me, but I will surely escape to the land of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧, and ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ will despair of me, of seeking me any more in all the territory of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, and I shall escape from his hand."

[2] And David arose and passed over, he and the six hundred men who were with him, to Achish the son of Maoch, the king of ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧.

[3] And David dwelt with Achish in ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧, he and his men, each man and his house, David and his two wives, ⟦ahinoam|Achinoam|Ahinoam⟧ the ⟦jezreel|Yizreelit|Jezreelitess⟧ and ⟦abigail|Avigayil|Abigail⟧ the wife of ⟦nabal|Naval|Nabal⟧, the ⟦carmel|Karmelit|Carmelitess⟧.

[4] And it was told to ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ that David had fled to ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧, and he did not seek him any more again.

[5] And David said to Achish, "If, please, I have found favor in your eyes, let them give me a place in one of the cities of the field, and let me dwell there; and why should your servant dwell in the city of the kingdom with you?"

[6] And Achish gave him ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧ on that day; therefore ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧ has belonged to the kings of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ to this day.

[7] And the number of the days that David dwelt in the field of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ was a year and four months.

[8] And David and his men went up and made a raid on the ⟦geshurites|Geshuri|Geshurites⟧ and the ⟦girzites|Girzi|Girzites⟧ and the ⟦amalekites|Amaleqim|Amalekites⟧, for they were those dwelling in the land which is from of old, as you come to Shur and as far as the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[9] And David would strike the land, and he would not let live man or woman, and he would take flock and herd and donkeys and camels and garments; and he returned and came to Achish.

[10] And Achish said, "Have you not made a raid today?" And David said, "On the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧ of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, and on the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧ of the ⟦jerahmeelites|Yerachmeeli|Jerahmeelites⟧, and to the ⟦negev|Negev|Negeb⟧ of the ⟦kenites|Qenim|Kenites⟧."

[11] And David would not let live man or woman, to bring them to ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧, saying, "Lest they tell about us, saying, 'Thus David did.'" And thus was his custom all the days that he dwelt in the field of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧.

[12] And Achish believed David, saying, "He has surely made himself stink among his people, in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, and he shall be to me a servant forever."

## Changes

- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"said in his heart" → "said to his heart"** (v1) — Hebrew אֶל־לִבּוֹ.
- **"I shall now perish" → "Now I shall be swept away"** (v1) — Hebrew אֶסָּפֶה, niphal of סָפָה "sweep away", the verb of 26:10.
- **"there is nothing better for me than that I should speedily escape" → "There is nothing good for me, but I will surely escape"** (v1) — Hebrew אֵין־לִי טוֹב כִּי הִמָּלֵט אִמָּלֵט: "there is no good for me", then כִּי "but / for" and the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "escaping I will escape". The Hebrew has no comparison ("better … than") and no "speedily". The Septuagint is reported to have "unless I escape" (cited from memory).
- **"Saul shall despair of me, to seek me any more in any coast of Israel" → "Saul will despair of me, of seeking me any more in all the territory of Israel"** (v1) — Hebrew וְנוֹאַשׁ מִמֶּנִּי … לְבַקְשֵׁנִי; גְּבוּל is "border", and the land within it.
- **"passed over with the six hundred men" → "passed over, he and the six hundred men"** (v2) — Hebrew הוּא וְשֵׁשׁ־מֵאוֹת אִישׁ.
- **"every man with his household" → "each man and his house"** (v3) — Hebrew אִישׁ וּבֵיתוֹ.
- **"Abigail the Carmelitess, Nabal's wife" → "Abigail the wife of Nabal, the Carmelitess"** (v3) — Hebrew אֵשֶׁת־נָבָל הַכַּרְמְלִית: the adjective is feminine and so belongs to Abigail, not to Nabal (30:5 has the masculine, "Nabal the Carmelite"). The earlier text had "Nabal the Carmelite" here.
- **"he sought no more again for him" → "he did not seek him any more again"** (v4) — Hebrew וְלֹא־יָסַף עוֹד לְבַקְשׁוֹ (qere; the written text has the imperfect יוֹסִף, with the same sense), literally "and he did not add any more to seek him".
- **"If I have now found grace" → "If, please, I have found favor"** (v5) — Hebrew אִם־נָא; חֵן "favor".
- **"in some town in the country" → "in one of the cities of the field"; "the royal city" → "the city of the kingdom"** (v5) — Hebrew בְּאַחַת עָרֵי הַשָּׂדֶה; עִיר הַמַּמְלָכָה. **"that I may dwell" → "and let me dwell"** — Hebrew וְאֵשְׁבָה, a cohortative.
- **"pertaineth unto" → "has belonged to"** (v6) — Hebrew הָיְתָה לְ, a perfect.
- **"the country of the Philistines" → "the field of the Philistines"** (v7, 11) — Hebrew שְׂדֵה פְלִשְׁתִּים; שָׂדֶה "field", open country.
- **"a full year and four months" → "a year and four months"** (v7) — Hebrew יָמִים וְאַרְבָּעָה חֳדָשִׁים, literally "days and four months": יָמִים "days" stands for a year (Lev 25:29; Judg 17:10; 1 Sam 1:21). "Full" is not in the Hebrew. The Septuagint is reported to have only "four months" (cited from memory).
- **"invaded" → "made a raid on"** (v8, 10) — Hebrew פָּשַׁט אֶל / עַל, literally "strip off", of a sudden attack for plunder (23:27; 30:1, 14).
- **"the Gezrites" → "the Girzites"** (v8) — the written text (ketiv) has הַגִּרְזִי "the Girzite"; the reading tradition (qere) has הַגִּזְרִי "the Gizrite" (as if "men of Gezer", which lies far to the north). The name is not known from elsewhere; the form of the lexicon is kept. All three names are singular collectives in the Hebrew ("the Geshurite and the Girzite and the Amalekite").
- **"for those nations were of old the inhabitants of the land" → "for they were those dwelling in the land which is from of old"** (v8) — Hebrew כִּי הֵנָּה יֹשְׁבוֹת הָאָרֶץ אֲשֶׁר מֵעוֹלָם, word for word; the Hebrew is difficult. The pronoun and the participle are feminine plural ("they", as of peoples or towns); "nations" is not in the Hebrew; אֲשֶׁר מֵעוֹלָם "which [is / are] from of old" may go with "the land" or with those who dwell in it. Many read מִטֵּלָם "from Telam" (a place, 15:4) for מֵעוֹלָם, with part of the Greek tradition (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept. **"as thou goest to Shur, even unto" → "as you come to Shur and as far as"** — Hebrew בּוֹאֲךָ שׁוּרָה וְעַד.
- **"smote the land, and left neither man nor woman alive, and took away" → "would strike the land, and he would not let live man or woman, and he would take"** (v9, 11) — the Hebrew verbs (וְהִכָּה, וְלֹא יְחַיֶּה, וְלָקַח) tell what David did again and again; the last two verbs of v9 (וַיָּשָׁב וַיָּבֹא) are in the simple narrative form. חִיָּה (piel) "let live". **"the sheep, and the oxen" → "flock and herd"** — Hebrew צֹאן וּבָקָר, without articles. **"apparel" → "garments"** — Hebrew בְּגָדִים.
- **"Whither have ye made a road to day?" → "Have you not made a raid today?"** (v10) — Hebrew אַל־פְּשַׁטְתֶּם הַיּוֹם. The Masoretic Text has אַל, the negative "not" (used elsewhere with a wish or a command), before a perfect: literally "You have not made a raid today". The Hebrew is difficult; it is rendered as a question. David's answer ("On …") fits a question "on whom?" or "where?", and so the old versions and some Hebrew manuscripts read ("against whom", "where": cited from memory); KJV "whither" follows that. The earlier text had "Against whom have you made a raid today?"
- **"the south" → "the Negev"** (v10) — Hebrew נֶגֶב, the dry south. The three parts are joined by "and", with עַל "on" twice and אֶל "to" the third time; "or" is not in the Hebrew. The peoples are singular collectives ("the Jerahmeelite", "the Kenite").
- **"saved neither man nor woman alive, to bring tidings to Gath" → "would not let live man or woman, to bring them to Gath"** (v11) — Hebrew לְהָבִיא גַת "to bring to Gath"; "them" is supplied, "tidings" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"So did David, and so will be his manner" → "'Thus David did.' And thus was his custom"** (v11) — Hebrew כֹּה־עָשָׂה דָוִד וְכֹה מִשְׁפָּטוֹ; מִשְׁפָּט is "judgment", here the fixed way of a man, his custom. Where the words of those who might "tell" end is not marked: the last clause ("and thus was his custom all the days that he dwelt …") is taken as the narrator's, because of the past "he dwelt"; it may also be read as part of what would be told.
- **"He hath made his people Israel utterly to abhor him" → "He has surely made himself stink among his people, in Israel"** (v12) — Hebrew הַבְאֵשׁ הִבְאִישׁ בְּעַמּוֹ בְיִשְׂרָאֵל, the infinitive absolute with its verb: literally "stinking he has stunk among his people, in Israel" (13:4; Gen 34:30); = he has made himself odious. **"believed David"** — Hebrew וַיַּאֲמֵן … בְּדָוִד, "believed in David", trusted him.
- **"my servant for ever" → "to me a servant forever"** (v12) — Hebrew לְעֶבֶד עוֹלָם, literally "a slave of the age" (Deut 15:17).

### Notes
- **David in Philistine service** — morally complicated. He pretends loyalty to Achish while actually raiding the southern Canaanite/Amalekite populations. Outright deception.
- **Ziklag** — David's base. A Philistine-granted city that becomes his capital-in-waiting. Will host the historic transition (ch 30, 2 Sam 2).
- **The dark side of David** — he destroys whole populations to prevent information leakage. Moral darkening as his situation deepens.
- **"Kings of Judah"** (v6) — editorial note confirming a later-than-events composition: the writer knows of a line of Judahite kings following David.
- **16 months duration** (v7) — substantive period as a Philistine vassal. During this time the Saul–Philistine war is building.
