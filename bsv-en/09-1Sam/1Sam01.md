# 1 Samuel 1

[1] And there was a certain man from ⟦ramathaim-zophim|Ramatayim-Tzofim|Ramathaim-zophim⟧, from the hill country of ⟦ephraim|Efrayim|Ephraim⟧, and his name was ⟦elkanah|Elqanah|Elkanah⟧ son of ⟦jeroham|Yerocham|Jeroham⟧ son of Elihu son of ⟦tohu|Tochu|Tohu⟧ son of ⟦zuph|Tzuf|Zuph⟧, an ⟦ephrathite|Efrati|Ephrathite⟧.

[2] And he had two wives: the name of the one was ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧, and the name of the second Peninnah. And Peninnah had children, and ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ had no children.

[3] And that man would go up from his city from year to year to bow down and to sacrifice to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ of Hosts in Shiloh. And there the two sons of Eli, ⟦hophni|Chofni|Hophni⟧ and ⟦phinehas|Pinchas|Phinehas⟧, were priests to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[4] And the day came, and ⟦elkanah|Elqanah|Elkanah⟧ sacrificed. And he would give portions to Peninnah his wife and to all her sons and her daughters,

[5] and to ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ he would give one portion, a double one, for it was ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ he loved; and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ had shut her womb.

[6] And her rival would vex her, even with vexation, for the sake of making her fret, because ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ had shut up her womb.

[7] And so he would do year by year: as often as she went up into the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, so she would vex her; and she wept and would not eat.

[8] And ⟦elkanah|Elqanah|Elkanah⟧ her husband said to her, "⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧, why do you weep? And why do you not eat? And why is your heart sad? Am I not better to you than ten sons?"

[9] And ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ arose after the eating in Shiloh and after the drinking. And Eli the priest was sitting on the seat by the doorpost of the temple of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[10] And she was bitter of soul, and she prayed to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, and she wept greatly.

[11] And she vowed a vow and said, "⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧ of Hosts, if you will surely look on the affliction of your slave woman, and remember me, and not forget your slave woman, and give to your slave woman a seed of men, then I will give him to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ all the days of his life, and a razor shall not come up upon his head."

[12] And it came to pass, as she prayed long before ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, that Eli was watching her mouth.

[13] And ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧, she was speaking upon her heart; only her lips were moving, and her voice was not heard. And Eli reckoned her to be a drunken woman.

[14] And Eli said to her, "How long will you make yourself drunk? Remove your wine from upon you."

[15] And ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ answered and said, "No, my lord. I am a woman hard of spirit, and wine and strong drink I have not drunk; and I poured out my soul before ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[16] Do not take your slave woman for a ⟦belial|daughter of Beliyyaal|daughter of worthlessness⟧, for out of the abundance of my complaint and my vexation I have spoken until now."

[17] And Eli answered and said, "Go in ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧, and may the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ give your asking that you have asked from him."

[18] And she said, "Let your maidservant find favor in your eyes." And the woman went on her way and ate, and her face was hers no more.

[19] And they rose early in the morning and bowed down before ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, and they returned and came to their house, to Ramah. And ⟦elkanah|Elqanah|Elkanah⟧ knew ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ his wife, and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ remembered her.

[20] And it came to pass at the turnings of the days that ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ conceived and bore a son; and she called his name ⟦samuel|Shmuel|Samuel⟧, "for from ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ I have asked him."

[21] And the man ⟦elkanah|Elqanah|Elkanah⟧ went up, and all his house, to sacrifice to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ the sacrifice of the days and his vow.

[22] And ⟦hannah|Channah|Hannah⟧ did not go up, for she said to her husband, "Until the boy is weaned; then I will bring him, and he shall appear before the face of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ and dwell there forever."

[23] And ⟦elkanah|Elqanah|Elkanah⟧ her husband said to her, "Do what is good in your eyes; sit until you have weaned him. Only may ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ establish his word." And the woman sat and nursed her son until she weaned him.

[24] And she brought him up with her when she had weaned him, with three bulls and one ephah of meal and a jar of wine, and she brought him to the house of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ at Shiloh. And the boy was a boy.

[25] And they slaughtered the bull and brought the boy to Eli.

[26] And she said, "Oh, my lord! By the life of your soul, my lord, I am the woman who stood with you here to pray to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[27] For this boy I prayed, and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ has given me my asking that I asked from him.

[28] And I also have made him one asked for ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧: all the days that he is, he is one asked for ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧." And he bowed down there to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v17) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **Book name** — Hebrew *Shemuel*. English "1 Samuel" / "I Samuel." MT counts Samuel as one book; LXX and later Christian tradition divide into two.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"Now there was a certain man of Ramathaim-zophim" → "And there was a certain man from Ramathaim-zophim"** (v1) — Hebrew אִישׁ אֶחָד, literally "one man". The place has the article and a dual ending: הָרָמָתַיִם צוֹפִים "the two heights, Zophim" (or "of the Zuphites"); in v19 the same place is הָרָמָה "the Ramah". "The hill country of Ephraim" is הַר אֶפְרַיִם, literally "the mountain of Ephraim".
- **"the other" → "the second"** (v2) — Hebrew הַשֵּׁנִית. "Wives" is נָשִׁים "women"; the one word is "woman" and "wife" (v15, 18, 19, 23, 26), as אִישׁ is "man" and "husband" (v8, 21, 22, 23).
- **"yearly" → "from year to year"** (v3) — Hebrew מִיָּמִים יָמִימָה, literally "from days to days". **"year by year"** (v7) is שָׁנָה בְשָׁנָה, "year in year". **"the yearly sacrifice" → "the sacrifice of the days"** (v21) — Hebrew זֶבַח הַיָּמִים, kept word for word: "the days" is the year's round, as in v3.
- **"to worship" → "to bow down"** (v3, 19, 28) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה, to bow to the ground.
- **"the LORD of hosts" → "Yahweh of Hosts"** (v3, 11) — Hebrew יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת *Yahweh tzeva'ot*, the first place the title stands in the Bible. צְבָאוֹת is the plural of צָבָא "host": an army, an ordered multitude (of Israel, Exod 12:41; of the heavens, Gen 2:1).
- **"the priests of the LORD, were there" → "And there … were priests to Yahweh"** (v3) — Hebrew כֹּהֲנִים לַיהוָה, "priests to / for Yahweh".
- **"And when the time was that Elkanah offered" → "And the day came, and Elkanah sacrificed"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיְהִי הַיּוֹם וַיִּזְבַּח, literally "and it was the day, and Elkanah sacrificed". What follows in v4b–7a is told in forms of repeated action ("he would give", "she would vex"); the story of that day goes on in v7b ("and she wept").
- **"a worthy portion" → "one portion, a double one"** (v5) — Hebrew מָנָה אַחַת אַפָּיִם *manah achat appayim*. The meaning is uncertain. אַפַּיִם is the dual of אַף "nose", used for "face" and for "anger"; word for word the phrase is "one portion of two faces" (or "of the face", "of anger"). "Double" is the old understanding of the Masoretic Text (a portion for two persons). The Septuagint has "one portion, because she had no child; only, Elkanah loved Hannah" (reading אֶפֶס "only" for אַפַּיִם; cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"for he loved Hannah: but the LORD had shut up her womb" → "for it was Hannah he loved; and Yahweh had shut her womb"** (v5) — Hebrew כִּי אֶת־חַנָּה אָהֵב, with "Hannah" before the verb; then plain וַ "and". In v6 the Hebrew adds בְּעַד: סָגַר … בְּעַד רַחְמָהּ "had shut up (behind) her womb".
- **"her adversary also provoked her sore, for to make her fret" → "her rival would vex her, even with vexation, for the sake of making her fret"** (v6) — Hebrew צָרָתָהּ "her rival", the word for a fellow wife (the verb in Lev 18:18). וְכִעֲסַתָּה … גַּם־כַּעַס is literally "and she vexed her, also vexation": verb and noun of one root; the same root in v7 ("she would vex her") and v16 ("my vexation"). **"to make her fret"** — Hebrew הַרְּעִמָהּ, a form of רָעַם "thunder"; in this sense it stands only here, and the meaning ("make her rage", "make her complain", "humble her") is uncertain.
- **"as he did so" → "And so he would do"** (v7) — Hebrew יַעֲשֶׂה, masculine: "he" (Elkanah) "would do". It is often read as "so it was done"; the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"why is thy heart grieved?" → "why is your heart sad?"** (v8) — Hebrew יֵרַע לְבָבֵךְ, literally "is your heart bad / evil": the verb of רַע, here of sorrow, not of guilt.
- **"after they had eaten in Shiloh, and after they had drunk" → "after the eating in Shiloh and after the drinking"** (v9) — Hebrew אַחֲרֵי אָכְלָה … וְאַחֲרֵי שָׁתֹה: two verbal nouns with no subject ("after eating … after drinking"). The first may also be read "after she had eaten"; v7–8 say that she would not eat, and v18 that she ate afterwards.
- **"sat upon a seat by a post" → "was sitting on the seat by the doorpost"** (v9) — Hebrew הַכִּסֵּא with the article; the word is also "throne" (2:8). הֵיכָל "temple" is the word for a great house, a palace; the house at Shiloh is also called "the house of Yahweh" (v7, 24) and "the tent of meeting" (2:22).
- **"in bitterness of soul" → "bitter of soul"** (v10) — Hebrew מָרַת נָפֶשׁ. **"prayed unto" → "prayed to"** — Hebrew עַל־יְהוָה, literally "upon / over Yahweh"; in v26 the Hebrew has אֶל "to", in v12 לִפְנֵי "before".
- **"wept sore" → "wept greatly"** (v10) — Hebrew וּבָכֹה תִבְכֶּה, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "and weeping she wept" (she went on weeping). "Surely", our usual word for this form (v11), would speak of certainty where the Hebrew speaks of the weeping itself.
- **"O LORD of hosts" → "Yahweh of Hosts"** (v11) — the Hebrew has no "O".
- **"if thou wilt indeed look" → "if you will surely look"** (v11) — Hebrew אִם־רָאֹה תִרְאֶה, literally "if seeing you will see".
- **"handmaid" → "slave woman"** (v11, 16) / **"maidservant"** (v18) — two Hebrew words: אָמָה (v11 three times, v16) and שִׁפְחָה (v18).
- **"a man child" → "a seed of men"** (v11) — Hebrew זֶרַע אֲנָשִׁים, word for word: offspring that will be a man among men, a son.
- **"there shall no razor come upon his head" → "a razor shall not come up upon his head"** (v11) — Hebrew וּמוֹרָה לֹא־יַעֲלֶה עַל־רֹאשׁוֹ, the words of Judg 13:5. מוֹרָה "razor" is another word than תַּעַר (Num 6:5). The Hebrew does not use the word *nazir* here.
- **"as she continued praying" → "as she prayed long"** (v12) — Hebrew הִרְבְּתָה לְהִתְפַּלֵּל, literally "she made much to pray". **"marked her mouth" → "was watching her mouth"** — Hebrew שֹׁמֵר, the verb "keep, guard".
- **"she spake in her heart" → "she was speaking upon her heart"** (v13) — Hebrew עַל־לִבָּהּ, "upon / to her heart". **"her lips moved"** — Hebrew נָעוֹת, of the verb "sway, quiver". **"thought she had been drunken" → "reckoned her to be a drunken woman"** — Hebrew וַיַּחְשְׁבֶהָ … לְשִׁכֹּרָה.
- **"How long wilt thou be drunken? put away thy wine from thee" → "How long will you make yourself drunk? Remove your wine from upon you"** (v14) — Hebrew תִּשְׁתַּכָּרִין, the reflexive stem; מֵעָלָיִךְ "from upon you".
- **"of a sorrowful spirit" → "hard of spirit"** (v15) — Hebrew קְשַׁת־רוּחַ, literally "hard of spirit": one on whose spirit it lies hard. The phrase stands only here. The Septuagint has "a woman for whom the day is hard" (cited from memory).
- **"Count not thine handmaid for a daughter of Belial" → "Do not take your slave woman for a daughter of Beliyyaal"** (v16) — Hebrew אַל־תִּתֵּן … לִפְנֵי, literally "do not give your slave woman before a daughter of Beliyyaal" (do not set her down as one). בְּלִיַּעַל is a common noun, "worthlessness", not a name; the same phrase in the masculine stands in 2:12.
- **"complaint and grief" → "my complaint and my vexation"** (v16) — Hebrew שִׂיחִי "my complaint" (what one turns over and speaks out) and כַּעְסִי, the "vexation" of v6.
- **"Go in peace" → "Go in *shalom*"** (v17) — Hebrew לְשָׁלוֹם.
- **"grant thee thy petition that thou hast asked of him" → "give your asking that you have asked from him"** (v17, 27) — Hebrew שֵׁלָתֵךְ אֲשֶׁר שָׁאַלְתְּ: noun and verb of the one root שָׁאַל "ask". The root runs through the chapter and is kept as "ask" each time: v17, v20 ("I have asked him"), v27, v28 (twice), and 2:20. "Give" is the plain verb נָתַן.
- **"her countenance was no more sad" → "her face was hers no more"** (v18) — Hebrew וּפָנֶיהָ לֹא־הָיוּ־לָהּ עוֹד, word for word "and her face was not to her any more". The Hebrew is difficult and has no word "sad": what is meant is the face she had worn until then. The Septuagint has "her face fell no more" (cited from memory).
- **"to their house to Ramah"** (v19) — Hebrew הָרָמָתָה, "to the Ramah".
- **"when the time was come about after Hannah had conceived, that she bare a son" → "at the turnings of the days that Hannah conceived and bore a son"** (v20) — Hebrew לִתְקֻפוֹת הַיָּמִים, literally "at the circuits of the days": when the days (the year, v3, 21) had come round. The order of the Hebrew is kept: the time, then "conceived and bore".
- **"Because I have asked him of the LORD" → "for from Yahweh I have asked him"** (v20) — Hebrew כִּי מֵיְהוָה שְׁאִלְתִּיו. The name שְׁמוּאֵל *Shemuel* is explained by the verb שָׁאַל "ask"; the name itself sounds like "name of El" or "heard of El", and the word that fits the verb letter for letter is שָׁאוּל *Sha'ul* "asked" (v28), the name of Saul.
- **"I will not go up until the child be weaned" → "Until the boy is weaned"** (v22) — the Hebrew sentence is broken off: "Until the boy is weaned — and I will bring him". נַעַר is "boy" throughout (v22, 24, 25, 27).
- **"that he may appear before the LORD, and there abide for ever" → "and he shall appear before the face of Yahweh and dwell there forever"** (v22) — Hebrew וְנִרְאָה אֶת־פְּנֵי יְהוָה, "be seen at the face of Yahweh", the phrase of Exod 23:17; עַד־עוֹלָם, "to the age".
- **"tarry" → "sit"** / **"abode" → "sat"** (v23) — Hebrew יָשַׁב "sit, dwell", the verb of v22 ("dwell there"). **"establish his word"** — Hebrew יָקֵם, "make stand". No word of Yahweh has been told before this; the Septuagint and a Qumran scroll have "establish what has gone out of your mouth" (cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"three bullocks" → "three bulls"** (v24) — Hebrew בְּפָרִים שְׁלֹשָׁה, "with three bulls"; v25 then has "the bull", one. The Septuagint and a Qumran scroll (4QSamᵃ) read "a three-year-old bull" (the same letters divided otherwise; cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"flour" → "meal"** (v24) — Hebrew קֶמַח, beside סֹלֶת "fine flour". **"a bottle of wine" → "a jar of wine"** — Hebrew נֵבֶל, a vessel for wine: a jar or a skin; which is not certain.
- **"the child was young" → "the boy was a boy"** (v24) — Hebrew וְהַנַּעַר נָעַר, the same word twice. The clause is difficult; it is commonly taken as "the boy was (still) young".
- **"they slew a bullock" → "they slaughtered the bull"** (v25) — Hebrew שָׁחַט, with the article: "the bull".
- **"as thy soul liveth" → "By the life of your soul"** (v26) — Hebrew חֵי נַפְשְׁךָ; חֵי is "life of". **"Oh, my lord"** — Hebrew בִּי אֲדֹנִי, literally "upon me, my lord", the words of one who asks leave to speak.
- **"that stood by thee here, praying" → "who stood with you here to pray"** (v26) — Hebrew הַנִּצֶּבֶת עִמְּכָה … לְהִתְפַּלֵּל.
- **"Therefore also I have lent him to the LORD; as long as he liveth he shall be lent to the LORD" → "And I also have made him one asked for Yahweh: all the days that he is, he is one asked for Yahweh"** (v28) — Hebrew הִשְׁאִלְתִּהוּ, the causative of שָׁאַל "ask": to let someone have what he asks, and so "lend" (Exod 12:36); and שָׁאוּל *sha'ul*, "asked". The verse answers v27 with the same root: she asked him from Yahweh, and she makes him an asked one for Yahweh. "Lent" hides the play and says that he is to be given back. **"all the days that he is"** — Hebrew כָּל־הַיָּמִים אֲשֶׁר הָיָה, literally "all the days that he has been"; the Septuagint has "that he lives" (cited from memory).
- **"he worshipped the LORD there" → "he bowed down there to Yahweh"** (v28) — the Hebrew does not say who: "he" may be Elkanah, Eli or the boy.

### Notes
- **Transition book** — 1 Samuel bridges Judges (no king) and Kings (monarchy). Samuel is the last judge, first post-Moses prophet, and kingmaker.
- **Hannah's prayer pattern** — barren woman story (like Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel). Prayer → response → child who becomes deliverer.
- **Shiloh sanctuary** (v3) — center of Israelite worship from Joshua through Samuel. Later destroyed (implied by Jer 7:12).
- **Nazirite dedication** (v11) — "a razor shall not come up upon his head" — the words used of Samson (Judg 13:5); the chapter does not use the word *nazir*.
- **Eli family** (v3) — Eli the priest and his sons Hophni and Phinehas. The corrupt priestly family that forms the book's other major family.
- **"One asked for Yahweh"** (v28) — Hebrew *sha'ul* — "asked", which is also the name of Saul. Samuel, the one asked from Yahweh, is the first king-anointer; the first king is named *Sha'ul* (Saul, "asked").
