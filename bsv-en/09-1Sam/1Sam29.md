# 1 Samuel 29

[1] And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ gathered all their camps to ⟦aphek|Afeq|Aphek⟧, and ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ were camping at the spring that is in ⟦jezreel|Yizreel|Jezreel⟧.

[2] And the lords of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ were passing on by hundreds and by thousands, and David and his men were passing on at the rear with Achish.

[3] And the commanders of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ said, "What are these ⟦hebrews|Ivrim|Hebrews⟧?" And Achish said to the commanders of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧, "Is not this David, the servant of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, who has been with me these days or these years, and I have not found in him anything from the day of his falling away until this day?"

[4] And the commanders of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ were wrathful with him, and the commanders of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ said to him, "Send the man back, and let him return to his place where you have appointed him, and let him not go down with us into the battle, and let him not become an adversary to us in the battle. And with what should this one make himself pleasing to his lord? Is it not with the heads of those men?

[5] Is not this David, of whom they answer in the dances, saying,

'⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ has struck down his thousands,
and David his ten thousands'?"

[6] And Achish called to David and said to him, "As ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ lives, surely you are upright, and good in my eyes is your going out and your coming in with me in the camp, for I have not found evil in you from the day of your coming to me until this day; but in the eyes of the lords you are not good.

[7] And now, return and go in ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧, and you shall not do evil in the eyes of the lords of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧."

[8] And David said to Achish, "For what have I done, and what have you found in your servant from the day that I was before you until this day, that I may not come and fight against the enemies of my lord the king?"

[9] And Achish answered and said to David, "I know that you are good in my eyes, as an angel of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; but the commanders of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ have said, 'He shall not go up with us into the battle.'

[10] And now, rise early in the morning, and the servants of your lord who came with you; and you shall rise early in the morning, and when it is light for you, go."

[11] And David rose early, he and his men, to go in the morning, to return to the land of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧. And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ went up to ⟦jezreel|Yizreel|Jezreel⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v6) — Tetragrammaton preserved. The Philistine Achish swears by Yahweh.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v9) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"gathered together all their armies to Aphek: and the Israelites pitched by a fountain" → "gathered all their camps to Aphek, and Israel were camping at the spring"** (v1) — Hebrew מַחֲנֵיהֶם "their camps"; אֲפֵקָה "to Aphek"; וְיִשְׂרָאֵל חֹנִים, "Israel" (not "the Israelites") with a plural participle; בַּעַיִן "at the spring", with the article.
- **"the lords of the Philistines" kept** (v2, 6, 7) — Hebrew סַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים: סֶרֶן *seren* is a word of the Philistines' own, used only of the rulers of their five cities (Josh 13:3). **"princes" → "commanders"** (v3, 4, 9) — Hebrew שָׂרֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים: שַׂר "prince", of an army "commander". The two words are kept apart. "Lord" in v4, 8, 10 ("his lord", "my lord", "your lord") is a third word, אָדוֹן.
- **"passed on" → "were passing on"; "in the rereward" → "at the rear"** (v2) — Hebrew עֹבְרִים, participles; בָּאַחֲרֹנָה "at the last".
- **"What do these Hebrews here?" → "What are these Hebrews?"** (v3) — Hebrew מָה הָעִבְרִים הָאֵלֶּה, with no verb and no "here".
- **"which hath been with me these days, or these years" kept** (v3) — Hebrew זֶה יָמִים אוֹ־זֶה שָׁנִים, literally "this days or this years": for some time, a year or more (27:7).
- **"I have found no fault in him since he fell unto me" → "I have not found in him anything from the day of his falling away"** (v3) — Hebrew מְאוּמָה "anything"; מִיּוֹם נָפְלוֹ, "from the day of his falling": נָפַל "fall" is said of one who goes over to another side (Jer 37:13). "To me" is not in the Masoretic Text; the old versions have it (cited from memory).
- **"were wroth with him" → "were wrathful with him"** (v4) — Hebrew קָצַף.
- **"Make this fellow return" → "Send the man back"; "which thou hast appointed him" → "where you have appointed him"** (v4) — Hebrew הָשֵׁב אֶת־הָאִישׁ, "make the man return"; אֲשֶׁר הִפְקַדְתּוֹ שָׁם.
- **"lest in the battle he be an adversary to us" → "and let him not become an adversary to us in the battle"** (v4) — Hebrew וְלֹא־יִהְיֶה־לָּנוּ לְשָׂטָן: שָׂטָן *satan*, a common noun here, "adversary, opposer" (Num 22:22; 1 Kgs 11:14). צַר has the same English in this translation; this is the other word.
- **"for wherewith should he reconcile himself unto his master? should it not be with the heads of these men?" → "And with what should this one make himself pleasing to his lord? Is it not with the heads of those men?"** (v4) — Hebrew יִתְרַצֶּה, the reflexive of רָצָה "be pleased with"; אֲדֹנָיו "his lord" (Saul); הָאֲנָשִׁים הָהֵם "those men", the Philistine fighting men.
- **"of whom they sang one to another in dances" → "of whom they answer in the dances"** (v5) — Hebrew יַעֲנוּ־לוֹ, from עָנָה "answer": they sing in answer to one another (18:7; 21:11). **"Saul slew his thousands" → "Saul has struck down his thousands"** — Hebrew הִכָּה "strike"; literally "struck among his thousands". The written text (ketiv) has the singular "his ten thousand"; the reading tradition (qere) has the plural, as 18:7.
- **"Surely, as the LORD liveth, thou hast been upright" → "As Yahweh lives, surely you are upright"** (v6) — Hebrew כִּי־יָשָׁר אַתָּה, with no verb ("are").
- **"with me in the host" → "with me in the camp"** (v6) — Hebrew בַּמַּחֲנֶה.
- **"nevertheless the lords favour thee not" → "but in the eyes of the lords you are not good"** (v6) — Hebrew וּבְעֵינֵי הַסְּרָנִים לֹא־טוֹב אָתָּה, set against "good in my eyes".
- **"go in peace" → "go in *shalom*"; "that thou displease not the lords" → "and you shall not do evil in the eyes of the lords"** (v7) — Hebrew בְּשָׁלוֹם; וְלֹא־תַעֲשֶׂה רָע בְּעֵינֵי.
- **"But what have I done? … so long as I have been with thee unto this day, that I may not go fight" → "For what have I done … from the day that I was before you until this day, that I may not come and fight"** (v8) — Hebrew כִּי "for"; הָיִיתִי לְפָנֶיךָ "I was before you"; אָבוֹא וְנִלְחַמְתִּי "come and fight". "My lord the king" may be heard of Achish or of Saul.
- **"as an angel of God" → "as an angel of *Elohim*"** (v9) — Hebrew כְּמַלְאַךְ אֱלֹהִים; מַלְאָךְ is "messenger" (the earlier text had "a messenger of *Elohim*"); "angel of Elohim" is the one rendering of this phrase in this translation (2 Sam 14:17; 19:27).
- **"with thy master's servants that are come with thee: and as soon as ye be up early in the morning, and have light, depart" → "and the servants of your lord who came with you; and you shall rise early in the morning, and when it is light for you, go"** (v10) — Hebrew word for word; "rise early in the morning" stands twice, first in the singular, then in the plural. The Septuagint is reported to have a longer text between the two ("and go to the place where I appointed you …", cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"rose up early to depart in the morning" → "rose early, he and his men, to go in the morning"** (v11) — Hebrew לָלֶכֶת בַּבֹּקֶר.

### Notes
- **Providential delivery** — David is spared the dilemma of fighting Israel. The Philistine commanders' suspicion saves David from impossible choice.
- **David's acting** (v8) — pretends indignation. His true feelings hidden; probably relief.
- **"Good … as an angel of *Elohim*"** (v9) — Achish's high praise. The irony: David is in fact deceiving him.
- **Structural counterpoint** — while Saul seeks the dead Samuel in desperation (ch 28), David is freed from the fight by Philistine politics (ch 29). Yahweh's providence operates behind both scenes.
