[4] And ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ spoke good of David to ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ his father and said to him, "Let not the king sin against his servant, against David, for he has not sinned against you, and for his deeds are very good toward you.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"that they should kill David" → "to put David to death"** (v1, 2, 5, 11, 15, 17) — Hebrew הֵמִית "put to death", one verb through the chapter; in v6, 11 its passive, "be put to death". **"delighted much in David"** (v1) — Hebrew חָפֵץ בְּ, as 18:22.
- **"take heed to thyself until the morning, and abide in a secret place" → "take heed to yourself, please, in the morning, and sit in secret"** (v2) — Hebrew בַבֹּקֶר "in the morning"; וְיָשַׁבְתָּ בַסֵּתֶר, יָשַׁב "sit, dwell".
- **"I will commune with my father of thee; and what I see, that I will tell thee" → "I myself will speak of you to my father, and I will see what, and I will tell you"** (v3) — Hebrew וְרָאִיתִי מָה, literally "and I will see what"; the pronoun "I" is expressed twice.
- **"spake good of David"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיְדַבֵּר … בְּדָוִד טוֹב. **"against his servant, against David"** — the preposition stands twice. **"his works have been to thee-ward very good" → "his deeds are very good toward you"**.
- **"he did put his life in his hand" → "he put his soul in his palm"** (v5) — Hebrew נַפְשׁוֹ "his soul", כַּפּוֹ "his palm". **"slew" → "struck down"** — Hebrew הִכָּה. **"wrought a great salvation"** — Hebrew תְּשׁוּעָה. **"didst rejoice" → "were glad"** — Hebrew שָׂמַח. **"without a cause" kept** — Hebrew חִנָּם, "for nothing".
- **"As the LORD liveth, he shall not be slain" → "As Yahweh lives, he shall not be put to death"** (v6) — Hebrew חַי־יְהוָה אִם־יוּמָת: the oath formula, literally "living is Yahweh, if he shall be put to death".
- **"all those things" → "all these words"** (v7) — Hebrew הַדְּבָרִים. **"as in times past" → "as yesterday and the day before"** — Hebrew כְּאֶתְמוֹל שִׁלְשׁוֹם.
- **"slew them with a great slaughter" → "struck them a great blow"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיַּךְ בָּהֶם מַכָּה גְדוֹלָה: verb and noun of one root. "There was war again" is literally "the war added to be".
- **"the evil spirit from the LORD was upon Saul" → "an evil spirit of Yahweh was upon Saul"** (v9) — Hebrew רוּחַ יְהוָה רָעָה, "a spirit of Yahweh, an evil one"; here the name is Yahweh and there is no "from" (16:14 "an evil spirit from Yahweh"; 16:15; 18:10 "an evil spirit of Elohim"). The preposition is אֶל "to". **"played with his hand" → "was playing with the hand"** — Hebrew בְּיָד, without "his".
- **"to smite David even to the wall with the javelin" → "to strike with the spear David and the wall"** (v10) — Hebrew לְהַכּוֹת בַּחֲנִית בְּדָוִד וּבַקִּיר, as 18:11. **"that night" → "in that night"** — Hebrew בַּלַּיְלָה הוּא, an unusual form; the Septuagint joins the words to v11 (from memory).
- **"If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain" → "If you do not let your soul escape tonight, tomorrow you are put to death"** (v11) — Hebrew מְמַלֵּט אֶת־נַפְשְׁךָ: the verb of "escaped" (v10, 12, 17, 18) in its causative stem; נֶפֶשׁ "soul". מוּמָת is a participle.
- **"fled, and escaped"** (v10, 12, 18) — "fled" is נוּס in v10 and בָּרַח in v12, 18; English has one word for both.
- **"an image" → "the teraphim"** (v13, 16) — Hebrew הַתְּרָפִים, with the article: household images (Gen 31:19). The word has a plural form; whether one figure or several is meant is not said ("them" is supplied in v13; "at its head" is literally "at his head-place"). **"a pillow of goats' hair for his bolster" → "the quilt of goats' hair … at its head"** — Hebrew כְּבִיר הָעִזִּים, found only here: something plaited or woven of goats' hair; what it was is not known. "Hair" is supplied (literally "of the goats"). **"a cloth" → "the cloth"** — Hebrew בֶּגֶד "garment".
- **"sent away mine enemy" → "let my enemy go"** (v17) — Hebrew שִׁלַּח, the same verb as "Let me go". **"why should I kill thee?" → "why should I put you to death?"**.
- **"dwelt in Naioth"** (v18, 19, 22, 23) — the reading tradition (qere) נָיוֹת; the written text has נוית. The word may be a common noun ("dwellings", "the camp" of the prophets); it is kept as a name.
- **"when they saw" → "he saw"** (v20) — the Masoretic Text has the singular וַיַּרְא; the old versions have the plural (from memory). **"the company of the prophets"** — Hebrew לַהֲקַת, found only here; "company" is the commonly accepted sense. **"standing as appointed over them" → "standing, stationed over them"** — Hebrew עֹמֵד נִצָּב עֲלֵיהֶם, two participles.
- **"prophesying … prophesied"** (v20, 21, 23, 24) — Hebrew נִבְּאִים of the prophets, and הִתְנַבֵּא of the messengers and of Saul: the word of 10:10 and 18:10. It names the behavior of one on whom the spirit is; what was said or done is not told.
- **"the Spirit of God was upon"** (v20, 23) — Hebrew וַתְּהִי עַל, "was upon"; not the "rushed" of 10:10.
- **"sent messengers again the third time" → "again sent messengers, a third time"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיֹּסֶף … שְׁלִשִׁים, literally "added and sent messengers, third ones".
- **"a great well" → "the great pit"** (v22) — Hebrew בּוֹר הַגָּדוֹל, a cistern; the grammar is unusual. The Septuagint has "the cistern of the threshing floor that is on the bare height" (from memory). **"And one said"** — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר "and he said", with no subject.
- **"he went thither" → "he went there"** (v23) — Hebrew שָׁם "there"; the Septuagint has "from there" (from memory). **"he went on, and prophesied" → "he went, going on and prophesying"** — Hebrew וַיֵּלֶךְ הָלוֹךְ וַיִּתְנַבֵּא.
- **"stripped off his clothes" → "stripped off his garments"; "lay down naked" → "fell naked"** (v24) — Hebrew וַיִּפֹּל עָרֹם: נָפַל "fall", עָרֹם "naked". The word can be used of one who has put off only his outer garment (Isa 20:2); the verse says "naked".
- **"Is Saul also among the prophets?"** (v24) — Hebrew הֲגַם שָׁאוּל בַּנְּבִיאִם, the saying of 10:11, 12.

### Notes
- **Jonathan's intercession** (v1–7) — saves David briefly. Demonstrates Saul's still-functional rationality; but only briefly.
- **Second spear-throw** (v10) — Saul's violence recurs. David no longer returns.
- **Michal's deception** (v11–17) — saves David via window-escape. The *teraphim* in her house (indicative of household images) are used as a prop.
- **"Let me go; why should I put you to death?"** (v17) — Michal's claim to Saul protects herself. Clever diplomacy; whether David actually threatened her is unclear.
- **Second "Is Saul also among the prophets?"** (v24) — the proverb from ch 10 recurs. Saul's prophetic ecstasy has become uncontrolled incapacitation.
- **Prophetic protection** — Samuel's prophetic community has contagious spirit-presence. Even hostile messengers are swept into prophecy.
