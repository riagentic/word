## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"hearkened unto your voice" → "listened to your voice"** (v1, 14, 15) — Hebrew שָׁמַע בְּקוֹל; "obey" is not a Hebrew word here. **"have made a king over you" → "have made a king reign over you"** (v1) — Hebrew וָאַמְלִיךְ … מֶלֶךְ, the causative of "reign" with its own noun: "I have made a king to be king".
- **"walketh before you"** (v2) — Hebrew מִתְהַלֵּךְ לִפְנֵיכֶם, "walks about before you", of the king and then of Samuel: the one who goes at the head, in view of all. **"old and grayheaded" → "have grown old and gray"** — two verbs in the Hebrew.
- **"Behold, here I am" → "Here I am"** (v3) — Hebrew הִנְנִי, literally "behold me".
- **"witness against me" → "Answer against me"** (v3) — Hebrew עֲנוּ בִי, "answer against" = give testimony against (Exod 20:16).
- **"his anointed"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew מְשִׁיחוֹ *meshicho*, "his anointed one": the king, Saul.
- **"whom have I defrauded? whom have I oppressed?" → "whom have I extorted from? Whom have I crushed?"** (v3, 4) — Hebrew עָשַׁק "wrong by force or fraud, extort" and רָצַץ "crush, break".
- **"bribe" → "ransom"** (v3) — Hebrew כֹּפֶר *kofer*, the price paid for a life (Exod 21:30; Num 35:31), here money taken to let a guilty man go; "bribe" is another word (שֹׁחַד). **"to blind mine eyes therewith" → "that I should hide my eyes with it"** — Hebrew וְאַעְלִים עֵינַי בּוֹ, "and I hid my eyes by it": to look away. The Septuagint has another text here, "and a sandal? Answer against me" (cited from memory; cf. Sirach 46:19); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"And they answered, He is witness" → "And he said, 'Witness.'"** (v5) — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר עֵד: the verb is singular in the printed text and the answer is the one word "witness". The singular may be the people speaking as one; many manuscripts and the old versions have the plural "and they said" (from memory).
- **"It is the LORD that advanced Moses and Aaron" → "It is Yahweh who made Moses and Aaron"** (v6) — Hebrew עָשָׂה "made": made them what they were, appointed them. The Hebrew sentence has no main verb: "Yahweh, who made Moses and Aaron and who brought up your fathers from the land of Egypt." "It is" is supplied. The Septuagint has "Yahweh is witness, who …" (from memory).
- **"stand still, that I may reason with you" → "take your stand, and let me enter into judgment with you"** (v7) — Hebrew הִתְיַצְּבוּ "station yourselves" and וְאִשָּׁפְטָה אִתְּכֶם, the nifal of שָׁפַט "judge": to go to law with someone. **"of all the righteous acts" → "as to all the righteous acts"** — Hebrew אֵת כָּל־צִדְקוֹת יְהוָה, literally "all the righteousnesses of Yahweh", joined to the verb only by the object marker; the construction is hard, and the Septuagint adds "and I will tell you" (from memory). "Did with you": Hebrew עָשָׂה אִתְּכֶם.
- **"made them dwell in this place"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיֹּשִׁבוּם, plural: Moses and Aaron are the subject, though neither entered the land. The Septuagint has the singular, "he made them dwell" (from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept. **"cried" → "cried out"** (v8, 10) — Hebrew זָעַק.
- **"captain of the host" → "the commander of the host"** (v9) — Hebrew שַׂר־צְבָא.
- **"and said"** (v10) — the reading tradition has the plural וַיֹּאמְרוּ; the written text has the singular.
- **"Baalim and Ashtaroth" → "the Baals and the Ashtaroth"** (v10) — both plural with the article in Hebrew.
- **"Bedan"** (v11) — Hebrew בְּדָן, as the Masoretic Text prints it. No judge of this name is known from the book of Judges (the name stands in 1 Chr 7:17). The Septuagint and the Syriac have "Barak" (from memory; cf. Heb 11:32); Jewish tradition took Bedan as Samson, "the one from Dan". The Masoretic Text is kept. "Samuel" names himself in the third person; some versions have "Samson" (from memory).
- **"on every side" → "all around"; "ye dwelled safe" → "you dwelt in security"** (v11) — Hebrew מִסָּבִיב; בֶּטַח.
- **"children of Ammon" → "sons of Ammon"** (v12) — Hebrew בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן. **"when the LORD your God was your king" → "and Yahweh your Elohim was your king"** — Hebrew וַיהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם מַלְכְּכֶם, a clause without a verb joined by "and": "while Yahweh your Elohim is your king".
- **"whom ye have desired" → "whom you have asked for"** (v13) — Hebrew שְׁאֶלְתֶּם, the verb "ask" (v17, 19), which sounds in the name Saul (שָׁאוּל "asked"). **"hath set a king over you"** — Hebrew נָתַן, literally "has given a king over you".
- **"If ye will fear the LORD … then shall both ye and also the king … continue following the LORD" → "If you fear Yahweh … and you shall be, both you and also the king …, after Yahweh your Elohim."** (v14) — the Hebrew sentence is left as it stands. It may be a condition without its conclusion ("If you fear … and are after Yahweh —", the good outcome left unsaid), or its last clause may be the conclusion ("then you shall be … after Yahweh"). The Hebrew וִהְיִתֶם "and you shall be" allows both; nothing is supplied. **"rebel against the commandment" → "rebel against the mouth"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew מָרָה אֶת־פִּי יְהוָה.
- **"against you, as it was against your fathers" → "against you and against your fathers"** (v15) — Hebrew בָּכֶם וּבַאֲבֹתֵיכֶם, "against you and against your fathers": so the Masoretic Text. "As it was" is not in the Hebrew. The Septuagint has "and against your king" (from memory).
- **"stand and see" → "take your stand and see"; "will do" → "is doing"** (v16) — Hebrew הִתְיַצְּבוּ; עֹשֶׂה is a participle.
- **"he shall send thunder" → "he will give thunders"** (v17, 18) — Hebrew וְיִתֵּן קֹלוֹת: the verb is "give", and the word is "voices", the plural of קוֹל, the usual word for thunder.
- **"your wickedness is great" → "your evil is great"** (v17, 19, 20, 25) — Hebrew רָעָה "evil", the same word through the speech.
- **"Pray for thy servants" → "Pray on behalf of your servants"** (v19, 23) — Hebrew הִתְפַּלֵּל בְּעַד. "Servants" is the word "slaves", the people's courtesy before Samuel. **"that we die not" → "and let us not die"** — Hebrew וְאַל־נָמוּת.
- **"turn not aside from following the LORD" → "do not turn aside from after Yahweh"** (v20) — Hebrew מֵאַחֲרֵי יְהוָה.
- **"And turn ye not aside: for then should ye go after vain things" → "And you shall not turn aside, for that is after the emptiness"** (v21) — Hebrew וְלֹא תָּסוּרוּ כִּי אַחֲרֵי הַתֹּהוּ, literally "and you shall not turn aside, for after the emptiness": the clause after כִּי "for" has no verb, and "that is" is supplied; the old versions do not translate the "for" (from memory). הַתֹּהוּ *ha-tohu* is the "waste / emptiness" of Gen 1:2, here of other gods; the verbs and "they" that follow are plural.
- **"will not forsake" → "will not abandon"** (v22) — Hebrew נָטַשׁ, "leave to itself"; "forsake" is עָזַב (v10). **"it hath pleased the LORD" → "Yahweh has undertaken"** — Hebrew הוֹאִיל, "resolve, set oneself to do".
- **"God forbid that I should sin" → "far be it from me to sin"** (v23) — Hebrew חָלִילָה לִּי, literally "a profane thing to me". **"the good and the right way" kept** — Hebrew יָשָׁר, "straight, right". "Teach" is הוֹרָה, the verb of *torah*.
- **"consider how great things he hath done for you" → "see what great things he has done with you"** (v24) — Hebrew רְאוּ אֵת אֲשֶׁר־הִגְדִּל עִמָּכֶם, literally "see that which he has made great with you".
- **"if ye shall still do wickedly" → "if you surely do evil"** (v25) — Hebrew infinitive absolute with its verb, הָרֵעַ תָּרֵעוּ, literally "doing evil you do evil". **"consumed" → "swept away"** — Hebrew סָפָה.

### Notes
- **Samuel's farewell address** — delivered at the kingdom's renewal. Prophetic accountability-oath + national confession.
- **Samuel's integrity oath** (v3) — five questions. The people testify: he has not extorted, crushed, or taken a ransom. Contrast with his sons and Eli's sons.
- **Historical recital** — from exodus to judges. Yahweh's pattern: salvation, apostasy, oppression, repentance, deliverance. The monarchy-request is the latest chapter.
- **Thunder in wheat harvest** (v17) — unusual in Palestine (dry season). Confirmation-sign that Samuel has divine authority.
- **"Yahweh was your king"** (v12) — the theological frame. The monarchy-request is sin; but Yahweh grants and works within it.
- **Samuel's intercessory commitment** (v23) — he will continue to pray despite his displeasure. Model of persistent prayer for the national people.
- **Conditional promise** (v14–15, 25) — both people and king must listen. The monarchy is not a safety-net but a shared covenantal partnership.
