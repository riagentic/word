# 1 John 1

[1] That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we beheld and our hands handled, concerning the word of life —

[2] and the life was manifested, and we have seen and testify and declare to you the eternal life, which was with the Father and was manifested to us —

[3] that which we have seen and heard we declare also to you, so that you also may have fellowship with us; and indeed our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧;

[4] and these things we write, that our joy may be made full.

[5] And this is the message which we have heard from him and announce to you, that **⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is light, and in him there is no darkness at all**.

[6] If we say that we have fellowship with him and walk in the darkness, we lie and do not do the truth.

[7] But **if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ his Son cleanses us from all sin**.

[8] If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.

[9] **If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.**

[10] If we say that we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v5) — systematic.
- **"Jesus Christ" → "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (v3) — systematic.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v7) — systematic; NA28 has Ἰησοῦ alone, KJV's "Jesus Christ" follows later manuscripts.
- **"which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled" → "which we beheld and our hands handled"** (v1) — Greek ἐθεασάμεθα … ἐψηλάφησαν: two aorists after two perfects ("have heard", "have seen"); the tenses are kept. θεάομαι "behold" (also 4:12, 14) is a different verb from "see" (ὁράω). ψηλαφάω is "feel, handle", a different word from "touch" (ἅπτομαι, 5:18).
- **"For the life was manifested, and we have seen it, and bear witness" → "and the life was manifested, and we have seen and testify"** (v2) — Greek καί, "and", not "for"; "have seen" and "testify" have no object of their own, the one object "the eternal life" comes after "declare". φανερόω is "manifest" throughout the letter.
- **"shew" / "declare" → "declare"; "declare" → "announce"** (v2, 3, 5) — Greek ἀπαγγέλλομεν in v2 and v3 (one English word), ἀναγγέλλομεν in v5 (another).
- **"and truly our fellowship" → "and indeed our fellowship"** (v3) — Greek καὶ … δέ; the sentence has no verb ("is" is supplied for English).
- **"write we unto you, that your joy may be full" → "we write, that our joy may be made full"** (v4) — NA28 γράφομεν ἡμεῖς, "we write" with an emphatic "we" (KJV's "unto you" follows the reading ὑμῖν), and ἡ χαρὰ ἡμῶν, "our joy" (many manuscripts read "your"). πεπληρωμένη is a perfect passive, as in 2 John 12.
- **"God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" → "*Elohim* is light, and in him there is no darkness at all"** (v5) — Greek σκοτία … οὐκ ἔστιν οὐδεμία, a double negative for emphasis.
- **"walk in darkness" → "walk in the darkness"** (v6) — Greek ἐν τῷ σκότει, with the article, as "in the light" in v7; καί is "and", not "while".
- **"do not the truth" → "do not do the truth"** (v6) — Greek οὐ ποιοῦμεν τὴν ἀλήθειαν; ποιέω is "do" throughout the letter (2:17, 29; 3:4, 7–10, 22; 5:2).
- **"But if we walk in the light" kept** (v7) — Greek ἐὰν δέ (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA27, RP). NA28: ἐὰν ἐν τῷ φωτὶ περιπατῶμεν, without δέ, "If we walk".
- **"faithful and just" → "faithful and righteous"** (v9) — Greek δίκαιος, the same word as in 2:1, 29; 3:7, and the root of "unrighteousness" (ἀδικία) in the same verse.
