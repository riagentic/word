# Mark 7

[1] And the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ and some of the scribes, having come from ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧, gather together to him,

[2] and having seen some of his disciples, that they eat the loaves with common hands, that is, unwashed —

[3] for the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ and all the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, unless they wash their hands with the fist, do not eat, holding the tradition of the elders,

[4] and from the marketplace, unless they ⟦immerse|immerse|baptize⟧ themselves, they do not eat; and there are many other things which they have received to hold: washings of cups and pitchers and copper vessels and beds —

[5] and the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ and the scribes ask him, "Why do your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat the bread with common hands?"

[6] But he said to them, "Well did ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ prophesy about you hypocrites, as it is written,

'**This people honors me with the lips,
but their heart is far away from me;
[7] but in vain do they revere me,
teaching doctrines, precepts of men**.'

[8] Leaving the commandment of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, you hold the tradition of men."

[9] And he was saying to them, "Well do you set aside the commandment of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that you may keep your tradition!

[10] For ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said, '**Honor your father and your mother**,' and, '**The one who speaks evil of father or mother, let him die the death**.'

[11] But you say, 'If a man says to his father or his mother, "⟦qorban|Qorban|Corban⟧ (which is, Gift), whatever you might be profited from me"' —

[12] you no longer let him do anything for his father or his mother,

[13] annulling the word of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ by your tradition which you delivered. And many such similar things you do."

[14] And calling the crowd to himself again, he was saying to them, "Hear me, all of you, and understand:

[15] **There is nothing from outside the man, entering into him, which is able to defile him; but the things coming out of the man are the things defiling the man.**"

[17] And when he entered into a house away from the crowd, his disciples were asking him about the parable.

[18] And he says to them, "So are you also without understanding? Do you not perceive that everything from outside entering into the man is not able to defile him,

[19] because it does not enter into his heart but into the belly, and goes out into the latrine?" — cleansing all the foods.

[20] And he was saying, "That which comes out of the man, that defiles the man.

[21] For from within, out of the heart of men, the bad reasonings come out: sexual immoralities, thefts, murders,

[22] adulteries, acts of covetousness, acts of evil, guile, licentiousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, senselessness.

[23] All these evil things come out from within and defile the man."

[24] But from there, rising, he went away into the borders of ⟦tyre|Tzor|Tyre⟧. And entering into a house he wanted no one to know, and he was not able to escape notice;

[25] but immediately a woman, hearing about him, whose little daughter had an unclean spirit, came and fell down at his feet.

[26] Now the woman was a Greek, a Syrophoenician by race; and she was requesting him that he would cast the demon out of her daughter.

[27] And he was saying to her, "Let the children first be filled, for it is not good to take the bread of the children and throw it to the little dogs."

[28] But she answered and says to him, "Lord, even the little dogs under the table eat from the crumbs of the young children."

[29] And he said to her, "Because of this word, go; the demon has gone out of your daughter."

[30] And going away to her house she found the young child laid upon the bed and the demon gone out.

[31] And again, going out from the borders of ⟦tyre|Tzor|Tyre⟧, he came through ⟦sidon|Tzidon|Sidon⟧ to the Sea of ⟦galilee|the Galil|Galilee⟧, in the midst of the borders of the Decapolis.

[32] And they bring to him one deaf and speaking with difficulty, and they appeal to him that he would lay his hand on him.

[33] And taking him away from the crowd privately, he put his fingers into his ears, and spitting, he touched his tongue,

[34] and looking up into heaven he groaned, and says to him, "***Ephphatha***," which is, "Be opened."

[35] And his hearing was opened, and the bond of his tongue was loosed, and he was speaking rightly.

[36] And he enjoined them that they should tell no one; but as much as he was enjoining them, they were proclaiming it all the more abundantly.

[37] And they were astonished beyond all measure, saying, "He has done all things well; he makes both the deaf to hear and the speechless to speak."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv8, 9, 13) — divine name retained.
- **"v16 absent"** — Mark 7:16 ("If any man have ears to hear, let him hear") is not in the text followed (WH NA28 omit; Treg RP have it); noted in ### Notes.
- **"And" / present tenses kept** (throughout) — Mark's καί at the start of sentences and his present tenses ("gather", v1; "ask", v5; "says", v18, 28, 34; "bring … appeal", v32) are kept as they are. "Immediately" (v25) is Greek *euthys*.
- **"they found fault" dropped; the sentence left unfinished** (v2–5) — in the Greek v1–5 are one sentence that breaks off after v2 ("and having seen …"), turns aside in v3–4 to explain the custom, and begins again in v5 ("and … ask him"). RP completes v2 with ἐμέμψαντο, "they found fault"; WH Treg NA28 do not have it.
- **"defiled hands" → "common hands"** (v2, 5) — Greek κοιναῖς χερσίν, "common", that is, not set apart, ritually unclean. The verb "defile" (v15, 18, 20, 23) is κοινόω, "make common".
- **"eat bread" → "eat the loaves" / "eat the bread"** (v2 / v5) — Greek τοὺς ἄρτους (plural), τὸν ἄρτον (singular).
- **"wash their hands oft" → "wash their hands with the fist"** (v3) — Greek πυγμῇ, literally "with a fist"; what manner of washing is meant is not known (with a clenched fist, up to the wrist, with a handful of water).
- **"holding the tradition" kept** (v3, 4, 8) — Greek κρατέω "hold"; παράδοσις "tradition", literally "what is delivered" (v13: "which you delivered", παρεδώκατε, the same root).
- **"except they wash" → "unless they immerse themselves"** (v4) — Greek βαπτίσωνται (Treg NA28 RP), the verb βαπτίζω "immerse"; WH reads ῥαντίσωνται, "sprinkle themselves". "Washings" in the same verse is βαπτισμούς, from the same root. "From the marketplace": the Greek has no verb ("when they come").
- **"pots, brasen vessels, and of tables" → "pitchers and copper vessels and beds"** (v4) — Greek ξεστῶν (the Latin *sextarius*, a measure and jug), χαλκίων, κλινῶν (Treg NA28 RP; WH omits "and beds"). κλίνη is a bed, also the couch on which one reclines at table; the same word in v30.
- **"worship" → "revere"** (v7) — Greek σέβονται; "worship" stands for λατρεύω. "Teaching doctrines, precepts of men": Greek διδάσκοντες διδασκαλίας ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων, two nouns side by side.
- **"Full well ye reject" → "Well do you set aside"** (v9) — Greek Καλῶς ἀθετεῖτε; Καλῶς is the same word as "Well" in v6. "Keep" is τηρήσητε (WH Treg and the SBLGNT); NA28 reads στήσητε, "establish".
- **"Whoso curseth" → "The one who speaks evil of"** (v10) — Greek ὁ κακολογῶν. "Let him die the death": Greek θανάτῳ τελευτάτω, "let him come to his end by death", the Greek form of the Hebrew מוֹת יוּמָת (Ex 21:17).
- **"It is Corban, that is to say, a gift" → "Qorban (which is, Gift)"** (v11) — Greek Κορβᾶν, ὅ ἐστιν Δῶρον: the Hebrew word קָרְבָּן, kept in the Greek and translated there as "gift". The words "given to God" are not in the Greek. The sentence of v11 is left unfinished in Greek and runs on into v12.
- **"suffer" → "let"** (v12) — Greek ἀφίημι, the verb of "leaving" in v8.
- **"Making … of none effect" → "annulling"** (v13) — Greek ἀκυροῦντες.
- **"the people" → "the crowd"** (v14, 17, 33) — Greek ὄχλος.
- **"into the house" → "into a house"** (v17) — Greek εἰς οἶκον, without the article; it can also mean "home".
- **"a man" → "the man"** (v15, 18, 20, 23) — Greek ὁ ἄνθρωπος with the article: man in general, any human being.
- **"purging all meats" → "cleansing all the foods"** (v19) — Greek καθαρίζων πάντα τὰ βρώματα (WH Treg NA28), a masculine participle that has no noun to agree with in the sentence. It is best taken with "he says" (v18): by saying this he was cleansing all the foods. RP reads καθαρίζον (neuter), which makes it part of the saying. The verse leaves the participle as it stands. "Draught" → "latrine": Greek ἀφεδρών.
- **"evil thoughts" → "the bad reasonings"** (v21) — Greek οἱ διαλογισμοὶ οἱ κακοί; κακός here, πονηρός ("evil") in v22–23. In v21–22 the first six words are plural in Greek (acts), the last six singular. The order "sexual immoralities, thefts, murders, adulteries" is that of WH Treg NA28.
- **"lasciviousness" → "licentiousness"** (v22) — Greek ἀσέλγεια. "An evil eye" (ὀφθαλμὸς πονηρός) is the Hebrew expression for envy or stinginess (Deut 15:9; Prov 28:22). "Foolishness" → "senselessness": Greek ἀφροσύνη.
- **"the borders of Tyre and Sidon" → "the borders of *Tzor*"** (v24) — the verse follows Τύρου (NA28 and the SBLGNT); WH Treg RP add καὶ Σιδῶνος, "and Sidon".
- **"he could not be hid" → "he was not able to escape notice"** (v24) — Greek λαθεῖν.
- **"young daughter" → "little daughter"** (v25) — Greek θυγάτριον, as in 5:23.
- **"devil" → "demon"** (v26, 29, 30) — Greek *daimonion*; "unclean spirit" in v25.
- **"besought" → "was requesting"** (v26) — Greek ἠρώτα (ἐρωτάω, "request" of asking for something).
- **"it is not meet" → "it is not good"** (v27) — Greek οὐ … καλόν.
- **"dogs" → "little dogs"** (v27, 28) — Greek κυνάρια, a diminutive: house dogs.
- **"Yes, Lord: yet the dogs" → "Lord, even the little dogs"** (v28) — the verse follows Κύριε (NA28 and the SBLGNT); WH Treg RP read Ναί, κύριε, "Yes, Lord". "The children's crumbs" → "the crumbs of the young children": Greek παιδίων here, τέκνα ("children") in v27.
- **"For this saying go thy way" → "Because of this word, go"** (v29) — Greek διὰ τοῦτον τὸν λόγον.
- **"her daughter laid upon the bed" → "the young child laid upon the bed"** (v30) — Greek τὸ παιδίον (WH Treg NA28).
- **"coasts" → "borders"** (v24, 31) — Greek ὅρια. "Through *Tzidon*": Greek ἦλθεν διὰ Σιδῶνος (WH Treg NA28).
- **"had an impediment in his speech" → "speaking with difficulty"** (v32) — Greek μογιλάλος; "speechless" in v37 is another word (ἄλαλος).
- **"beseech" → "appeal to"** (v32) — Greek παρακαλοῦσιν, as in chapter 5.
- **"sighed" → "groaned"** (v34) — Greek ἐστέναξεν.
- **"straightway his ears were opened" → "his hearing was opened"** (v35) — Greek αἱ ἀκοαί, "hearings". WH Treg and the SBLGNT have no "immediately" here; NA28 and RP add εὐθέως. "The string of his tongue" → "the bond of his tongue": Greek δεσμός. "Plain" → "rightly": Greek ὀρθῶς.
- **"charged" → "enjoined"** (v36) — Greek διαστέλλομαι, as in 5:43. The Greek does not name Jesus in this verse.
- **"a great deal" → "all the more abundantly"** (v36) — Greek μᾶλλον περισσότερον.
- **"the dumb" → "the speechless"** (v37) — Greek ἀλάλους. "Beyond all measure": Greek ὑπερπερισσῶς.

### Notes
- **v6-7** quotes Isa 29:13 in a Greek form close to the Septuagint; the verse translates the Greek as Mark has it.
- **v10** cites Ex 20:12; 21:17.
- **v11** — *qorban* = "offering", something dedicated to God; a vow that withdrew it from the parents' use.
- **v16** (not in the text followed): "If any man have ears to hear, let him hear."
- **v34** preserves Aramaic (*Ephphatha*, Greek Εφφαθα) and the Greek translates it: Διανοίχθητι, "Be opened".
