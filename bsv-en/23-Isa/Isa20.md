# Isaiah 20

[1] In the year that Tartan came to Ashdod, when Sargon the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧ sent him, and he fought against Ashdod and captured it —

[2] at that time ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ spoke by the hand of ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ son of ⟦amoz|Amotz|Amoz⟧, saying, "Go, and loose the sackcloth from upon your loins, and your sandal you shall pull off from upon your feet." And he did so, walking naked and barefoot.

[3] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said, "As my servant ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ has walked naked and barefoot, three years a sign and a wonder against ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ and against ⟦cush|Kush|Cush⟧,

[4] so shall the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧ drive the captives of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ and the exiles of ⟦cush|Kush|Cush⟧, young men and old men, naked and barefoot, and bared of buttocks, the nakedness of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[5] And they shall be dismayed and ashamed because of ⟦cush|Kush|Cush⟧, their expectation, and because of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, their beauty.

[6] And the inhabitant of this coastland shall say in that day, 'Behold, such is our expectation, where we fled for help, to be delivered from the face of the king of ⟦assyria|Ashur|Assyria⟧! And how shall we, we escape?'"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v2, 3) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And"** (v1–6) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; kept (v3 "And Yahweh said" for וַיֹּאמֶר, not "Then").
- **"Tartan"** (v1) — Hebrew תַּרְתָּן, an Assyrian title (the chief commander), without article as 2 Kgs 18:17. **"(when Sargon … sent him,) and fought against Ashdod, and took it;" → "when Sargon … sent him, and he fought against Ashdod and captured it"** — לָכַד "capture"; the sentence runs on into v2 as in the Hebrew.
- **"spake the LORD by Isaiah" → "Yahweh spoke by the hand of Isaiah"** (v2) — Hebrew בְּיַד "by the hand of". **"Go and loose the sackcloth from off thy loins, and put off thy shoe from thy foot" → "Go, and loose the sackcloth from upon your loins, and your sandal you shall pull off from upon your feet"** — וּפִתַּחְתָּ "and loose" (weqatal after the imperative); מֵעַל "from upon"; מָתְנַיִם "loins"; נַעַלְךָ "your sandal" (singular), the object first; תַחֲלֹץ "you shall pull off" (as Deut 25:9); רַגְלֶיךָ "your feet" (plural). **"walking naked and barefoot"** — הָלֹךְ עָרוֹם וְיָחֵף, as printed: עָרוֹם is "naked"; whether he wore nothing or only an undergarment is not said.
- **"Like as my servant Isaiah hath walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder" → "As my servant Isaiah has walked naked and barefoot, three years a sign and a wonder"** (v3) — Hebrew עַבְדִּי "my servant"; the accents close the first half after "barefoot", so "three years" goes with "a sign and a wonder" (whether he walked so for three years or the sign points to three years is not said); מוֹפֵת "wonder"; "for" is not written; עַל … וְעַל "against … and against" (or "upon, concerning").
- **"lead away the Egyptians prisoners, and the Ethiopians captives, young and old" → "drive the captives of Egypt and the exiles of Cush, young men and old men"** (v4) — Hebrew נָהַג "drive"; שְׁבִי מִצְרַיִם "the captives of Egypt", גָּלוּת כּוּשׁ "the exiles of Cush" (the names, not "Egyptians", "Cushites"); נְעָרִים וּזְקֵנִים "young men and old men". **"even with their buttocks uncovered, to the shame of Egypt" → "and bared of buttocks, the nakedness of Egypt"** — וַחֲשׂוּפַי שֵׁת, literally "and bared ones of buttock"; עֶרְוַת מִצְרָיִם "the nakedness of Egypt" ("to the shame" is not written).
- **"they shall be afraid and ashamed of Ethiopia their expectation, and of Egypt their glory" → "they shall be dismayed and ashamed because of Cush, their expectation, and because of Egypt, their beauty"** (v5) — Hebrew חַתּוּ וָבֹשׁוּ "be dismayed and ashamed" (as 2 Kgs 19:26); מִן … וּמִן "because of … and because of"; מַבָּט "expectation" (what one looks to, from נָבַט "look"); תִּפְאֶרֶת "beauty".
- **"the inhabitant of this isle" → "the inhabitant of this coastland"** (v6) — Hebrew יֹשֵׁב, singular (collective); אִי "coastland". **"Behold, such is our expectation, whither we flee for help" → "Behold, such is our expectation, where we fled for help"** — נַסְנוּ, a perfect; שָׁם "there" (with אֲשֶׁר "where"); עֶזְרָה "help". **"to be delivered from the king of Assyria" → "to be delivered from the face of the king of Assyria"** — לְהִנָּצֵל מִפְּנֵי, "from the face of" (of flight). **"and how shall we escape?" → "And how shall we, we escape?"** — וְאֵיךְ נִמָּלֵט אֲנָחְנוּ, the pronoun written after the verb for emphasis.

### Notes
- **Sargon II** — reigned 722–705 BC; the campaign against Ashdod is dated about 711 BC.
