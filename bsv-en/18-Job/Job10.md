# Job 10

[1] "My soul loathes my life;
let me leave my complaint upon myself;
let me speak in the bitterness of my soul.

[2] I will say to ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧: Do not condemn me;
make me know on what account you contend with me.

[3] Is it good to you that you oppress,
that you reject the labor of your palms,
and on the counsel of the wicked you have shone forth?

[4] Have you eyes of flesh?
Or do you see as mortal man sees?

[5] Are your days as the days of mortal man,
or your years as the days of a mighty man,

[6] that you seek for my iniquity
and inquire after my sin,

[7] although you know that I am not wicked,
and there is none who delivers from your hand?

[8] Your hands shaped me and made me,
together all around, and you swallow me up.

[9] Remember, please, that like clay you made me,
and to dust you will make me return.

[10] Did you not pour me out like milk
and make me congeal like cheese?

[11] With skin and flesh you clothed me,
and with bones and sinews you wove me together.

[12] Life and ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ you have done with me,
and your oversight has kept my spirit.

[13] And these you hid in your heart;
I know that this is with you.

[14] If I sinned, then you would keep watch on me,
and from my iniquity you will not clear me.

[15] If I am wicked, woe to me!
And were I righteous, I may not lift up my head —
sated with dishonor, and see my affliction!

[16] And it rises high: like a fierce lion you hunt me,
and you return and show yourself wonderful against me.

[17] You renew your witnesses before me,
and you increase your vexation with me;
changes and a host are with me.

[18] And why did you bring me out from the womb?
I would have expired, and no eye would have seen me.

[19] As though I had not been, I would be;
from the belly to the grave I would be carried.

[20] Are not my days few? Then cease,
and set yourself away from me, that I may brighten up a little,

[21] before I go, and I shall not return,
to the land of darkness and the shadow of death,

[22] a land of dimness like gloom,
of the shadow of death and no order,
and it shines like gloom."

## Changes

- **"God" → "Eloah"** (v2) — Hebrew אֱלוֹהַּ.
- **"My soul is weary of my life; I will leave my complaint upon myself" → "My soul loathes my life; let me leave my complaint upon myself"** (v1) — Hebrew נָקְטָה נַפְשִׁי בְּחַיָּי, "my soul loathes (is weary) with my life" (נָקַט, rare); אֶעֶזְבָה and אֲדַבְּרָה are cohortatives, "let me leave", "let me speak"; עָלַי "upon myself" (= let it loose against me). שִׂיחַ "complaint, musing" (9:27).
- **"shew me wherefore thou contendest with me" → "make me know on what account you contend with me"** (v2) — Hebrew הוֹדִיעֵנִי "make me know"; עַל מַה "on what (account)"; רִיב "contend" (in law).
- **"that thou shouldest oppress, that thou shouldest despise the work of thine hands, and shine upon the counsel of the wicked?" → "that you oppress, that you reject the labor of your palms, and on the counsel of the wicked you have shone forth?"** (v3) — Hebrew תַעֲשֹׁק, the verb the word list gives as "extort" (English "extort" needs an object, and none is written: "oppress" here); תִמְאַס "reject"; יְגִיעַ כַּפֶּיךָ "the labor of your palms"; הוֹפָעְתָּ "you have shone forth" (perfect; "while" is not written).
- **"seest thou as man seeth?" → "do you see as mortal man sees?"** (v4) — Hebrew אֱנוֹשׁ "mortal man" (v4, 5); אִם "or".
- **"Are thy days as the days of man? are thy years as man's days" → "Are your days as the days of mortal man, or your years as the days of a mighty man"** (v5) — Hebrew אֱנוֹשׁ, then גֶּבֶר "mighty man": two words.
- **"That thou enquirest after mine iniquity, and searchest after my sin?" → "that you seek for my iniquity and inquire after my sin"** (v6) — Hebrew תְבַקֵּשׁ "seek" and תִדְרוֹשׁ, which also means "seek": English has one word, so דָּרַשׁ is "inquire after" here.
- **"Thou knowest that I am not wicked" → "although you know that I am not wicked"** (v7) — Hebrew עַל־דַּעְתְּךָ, literally "upon your knowing" (= in spite of your knowing); אֶרְשָׁע "I am wicked" (not "guilty"). מַצִּיל "one who delivers".
- **"Thine hands have made me and fashioned me together round about; yet thou dost destroy me" → "Your hands shaped me and made me, together all around, and you swallow me up"** (v8) — Hebrew עִצְּבוּנִי "shaped me" (עִצֵּב, piel; the same letters as עָצַב "be grieved", another sense); יַחַד סָבִיב "together all around" (others take סָבִיב with the next word and change the text: the Masoretic Text is kept); וַתְּבַלְּעֵנִי "and you swallow me up" (the verb of 8:18; "and now", "yet" are not written). The earlier text split this two-line verse into three lines; the Hebrew has two.
- **"Remember, I beseech thee, that thou hast made me as the clay; and wilt thou bring me into dust again?" → "Remember, please, that like clay you made me, and to dust you will make me return"** (v9) — Hebrew זְכָר־נָא, with נָא "please"; חֹמֶר "clay" (of the potter; elsewhere "mortar"); the second line has no question particle: וְאֶל־עָפָר תְּשִׁיבֵנִי "and to dust you will make me return".
- **"poured me out as milk, and curdled me like cheese" → "pour me out like milk and make me congeal like cheese"** (v10) — Hebrew תַּתִּיכֵנִי "pour me out"; תַּקְפִּיאֵנִי, hifil of קָפָא "congeal".
- **"Thou hast clothed me with skin and flesh, and hast fenced me with bones and sinews" → "With skin and flesh you clothed me, and with bones and sinews you wove me together"** (v11) — Hebrew תְּסֹכְכֵנִי "you wove me together" (as Ps 139:13; the same letters as סָכַךְ "screen, cover", whence KJV "fenced"). The imperfects tell what was done.
- **"Thou hast granted me life and favour, and thy visitation hath preserved my spirit" → "Life and chesed you have done with me, and your oversight has kept my spirit"** (v12) — Hebrew חַיִּים וָחֶסֶד עָשִׂיתָ עִמָּדִי, "life and *chesed* you have done with me" (token); פְּקֻדָּתְךָ "your oversight" (from פָּקַד "visit, attend to"); שָׁמְרָה "has kept".
- **"And these things hast thou hid in thine heart" → "And these you hid in your heart"** (v13) — Hebrew וְאֵלֶּה "and these" ("things" is not written); צָפַנְתָּ "you hid"; כִּי־זֹאת עִמָּךְ "that this is with you" (= this was your purpose).
- **"If I sin, then thou markest me, and thou wilt not acquit me from mine iniquity" → "If I sinned, then you would keep watch on me, and from my iniquity you will not clear me"** (v14) — Hebrew חָטָאתִי "I sinned"; וּשְׁמַרְתָּנִי, a perfect with וְ after the condition, "then you would keep me", here of keeping watch on a man (שָׁמַר "keep"); תְנַקֵּנִי "clear me" (as 9:28).
- **"If I be wicked, woe unto me; and if I be righteous, yet will I not lift up my head. I am full of confusion; therefore see thou mine affliction" → "If I am wicked, woe to me! And were I righteous, I may not lift up my head — sated with dishonor, and see my affliction!"** (v15) — Hebrew רָשַׁעְתִּי "I am wicked"; אַלְלַי "woe"; the second line has no "if": וְצָדַקְתִּי "and I am righteous" (the condition is carried over from the first line); שְׂבַע קָלוֹן "sated with dishonor"; וּרְאֵה עָנְיִי, as pointed "and see my affliction!" (imperative). Many change one letter to רְוֵה "drenched (with my affliction)": the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"For it increaseth. Thou huntest me as a fierce lion: and again thou shewest thyself marvellous upon me" → "And it rises high: like a fierce lion you hunt me, and you return and show yourself wonderful against me"** (v16) — Hebrew וְיִגְאֶה "and it rises high" (the verb of 8:11; "it" is not named: the head of v15, or Job himself); שַׁחַל "fierce lion" (a poetic word for the lion, kept apart from אַרְיֵה "lion"; "fierce" marks the word, the kind is uncertain); תְּצוּדֵנִי "you hunt me"; וְתָשֹׁב תִּתְפַּלָּא־בִי "and you return (= again) and show yourself wonderful against me" (the root of נִפְלָאוֹת "wonderful deeds").
- **"Thou renewest thy witnesses against me, and increasest thine indignation upon me; changes and war are against me" → "You renew your witnesses before me, and you increase your vexation with me; changes and a host are with me"** (v17) — Hebrew נֶגְדִּי "before me, in front of me"; כַּעַשְׂךָ "your vexation"; עִמָּדִי, עִמִּי "with me" (= against me); חֲלִיפוֹת וְצָבָא "changes and a host" (fresh troops in relays: the sense; the earlier "wave after wave of troops come upon me" interpreted it).
- **"Wherefore then hast thou brought me forth out of the womb? Oh that I had given up the ghost" → "And why did you bring me out from the womb? I would have expired"** (v18) — Hebrew וְלָמָּה "and why"; רֶחֶם "womb"; אֶגְוַע "I would have expired".
- **"I should have been carried from the womb to the grave" → "from the belly to the grave I would be carried"** (v19) — Hebrew מִבֶּטֶן "from the belly" (רֶחֶם "womb" is v18); קֶבֶר "grave".
- **"Are not my days few? cease then, and let me alone, that I may take comfort a little" → "Are not my days few? Then cease, and set yourself away from me, that I may brighten up a little"** (v20) — the qere is וַחֲדָל "and cease" and וְשִׁית "and set" (imperatives); the ketiv יחדל ישית is "let him cease, let him set" (third person). שִׁית "set" has no object: "yourself" is supplied ("your hand" or "your eye" is also understood). אַבְלִיגָה "brighten up" (9:27).
- **"Before I go whence I shall not return, even to the land of darkness and the shadow of death" → "before I go, and I shall not return, to the land of darkness and the shadow of death"** (v21) — Hebrew וְלֹא אָשׁוּב "and I shall not return". צַלְמָוֶת, as pointed "shadow of death" (צֵל + מָוֶת); many take it as one word "deep darkness" (from a root "be dark"); the pointing is kept, the other sense named.
- **"A land of darkness, as darkness itself; and of the shadow of death, without any order, and where the light is as darkness" → "a land of dimness like gloom, of the shadow of death and no order, and it shines like gloom"** (v22) — Hebrew אֶרֶץ עֵיפָתָה "a land of dimness" (עֵיפָה, Amos 4:13); אֹפֶל "gloom" (twice); וְלֹא סְדָרִים "and no orders" (only here); וַתֹּפַע "and it shines" (the land; "where the light is" is not written).

### Notes
- **Address to Eloah direct** throughout.
- **Clay-pottery imagery** (v9–11) — poetic embryology, beautiful and tender: poured out like milk, congealed like cheese, clothed with skin.
- **"You wove me together"** echoed in Ps 139:13.
- **Paradox of divine attention** — Eloah hunts Job *because* he made him. The tender maker becomes the relentless hunter.
- **"Land of darkness"** — Sheol is not named; the land of the dead is described by doubling *tzalmawet* — "no order, and it shines like gloom."
