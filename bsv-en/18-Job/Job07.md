# Job 7

[1] "Is there not a warfare for mortal man upon earth,
and like the days of a hired man are his days?

[2] Like a slave who pants for the shade,
and like a hired man who waits for his wage,

[3] so I have been made to inherit months of emptiness,
and nights of toil they have counted out to me.

[4] If I lie down, then I say, 'When shall I rise?'
and the evening stretches on,
and I am sated with tossings until the twilight.

[5] My flesh is clothed with maggots and a crust of dust;
my skin hardens and is loathsome.

[6] My days are swifter than a shuttle,
and they come to an end without hope.

[7] Remember that my life is wind;
my eye will not return to see good.

[8] The eye of him who sees me will not watch me;
your eyes are on me, and I am not.

[9] A cloud comes to an end and goes;
so he who goes down to Sheol will not come up.

[10] He will not return again to his house,
and his place will not recognize him again.

[11] I also will not withhold my mouth;
let me speak in the distress of my spirit;
let me muse in the bitterness of my soul.

[12] Am I a sea, or a sea monster,
that you set a watch over me?

[13] When I say, 'My couch will comfort me,
my bed will bear my musing',

[14] then you dismay me with dreams,
and from visions you terrify me,

[15] and my soul chooses strangling,
death rather than my bones.

[16] I reject; not forever shall I live.
Cease from me, for my days are vapor.

[17] What is mortal man, that you make him great,
and that you set your heart on him,

[18] and visit him every morning,
every moment try him?

[19] How long will you not look away from me,
not let me go until I swallow my spittle?

[20] I have sinned: what do I do to you, you guard of mankind?
Why have you set me as a mark for you,
and I have become a burden upon myself?

[21] And why do you not forgive my transgression and make my iniquity pass away?
For now I shall lie down in the dust,
and you will seek me, and I am not."

## Changes

- **"Is there not an appointed time to man upon earth? are not his days also like the days of an hireling?" → "Is there not a warfare for mortal man upon earth, and like the days of a hired man are his days?"** (v1) — Hebrew צָבָא, the word "host", here of a soldier's service for a set term (as Isa 40:2); אֱנוֹשׁ "mortal man"; "upon" is the qere עֲלֵי (ketiv על, the same sense); שָׂכִיר "hired man" (so v2). One question in the Hebrew.
- **"As a servant earnestly desireth the shadow, and as an hireling looketh for the reward of his work" → "Like a slave who pants for the shade, and like a hired man who waits for his wage"** (v2) — Hebrew עֶבֶד "slave"; שָׁאַף "pant for" (as 5:5); צֵל "shade"; קִוָּה "wait for"; פָעֳלוֹ, literally "his work" (what his work earns).
- **"So am I made to possess months of vanity, and wearisome nights are appointed to me" → "so I have been made to inherit months of emptiness, and nights of toil they have counted out to me"** (v3) — Hebrew הָנְחַלְתִּי, hofal of נָחַל "inherit"; יַרְחֵי "months" (יֶרַח, the poetic word); שָׁוְא "emptiness"; עָמָל "toil"; מִנּוּ "they counted out" (מָנָה "count"; no subject named).
- **"When I lie down, I say, When shall I arise, and the night be gone? and I am full of tossings to and fro unto the dawning of the day" → "If I lie down, then I say, 'When shall I rise?' and the evening stretches on, and I am sated with tossings until the twilight"** (v4) — Hebrew אִם "if"; וּמִדַּד־עָרֶב, literally "and the measuring out of evening" (uncertain); "the night be gone" is not written; שָׂבַע "be sated"; נְדֻדִים "tossings"; נֶשֶׁף "twilight" (of evening or of dawn). The verse is three lines in the Hebrew.
- **"My flesh is clothed with worms and clods of dust; my skin is broken, and become loathsome" → "My flesh is clothed with maggots and a crust of dust; my skin hardens and is loathsome"** (v5) — Hebrew רִמָּה "maggots" (a collective; תּוֹלֵעָה is "worm"); "crust" is the qere וְגוּשׁ (ketiv וגיש, a spelling of the same word). רָגַע "harden" (uncertain: "be still", "crack"); וַיִּמָּאֵס, nifal of מָאַס "reject" ("is loathsome"), or of מָסַס "melt" ("runs"); uncertain.
- **"My days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle, and are spent without hope" → "My days are swifter than a shuttle, and they come to an end without hope"** (v6) — Hebrew אֶרֶג (from אָרַג "weave": "shuttle" or "loom"); "weaver's" is not written. וַיִּכְלוּ "they come to an end" (as 4:9). בְּאֶפֶס תִּקְוָה "with no hope": תִּקְוָה is also "thread" (Josh 2:18), a play beside the shuttle.
- **"O remember that my life is wind: mine eye shall no more see good" → "Remember that my life is wind; my eye will not return to see good"** (v7) — Hebrew רוּחַ "wind, breath" (one word); לֹא־תָשׁוּב … לִרְאוֹת, literally "will not return to see" (= will not again see); "O" is not written.
- **"The eye of him that hath seen me shall see me no more: thine eyes are upon me, and I am not" → "The eye of him who sees me will not watch me; your eyes are on me, and I am not"** (v8) — Hebrew שׁוּר "watch" (not רָאָה "see"); רֹאִי "him who sees me".
- **"As the cloud is consumed and vanisheth away: so he that goeth down to the grave shall come up no more" → "A cloud comes to an end and goes; so he who goes down to Sheol will not come up"** (v9) — Hebrew כָּלָה "come to an end" (as v6); וַיֵּלַךְ "and goes"; "as" is not written; שְׁאוֹל *Sheol* (not "the grave").
- **"neither shall his place know him any more" → "his place will not recognize him again"** (v10) — Hebrew הִכִּיר "recognize" (as 4:16).
- **"Therefore I will not refrain my mouth; I will speak in the anguish of my spirit; I will complain in the bitterness of my soul" → "I also will not withhold my mouth; let me speak in the distress of my spirit; let me muse in the bitterness of my soul"** (v11) — Hebrew גַּם־אֲנִי "I also" ("therefore" is not written); חָשַׂךְ "withhold"; אֲדַבְּרָה, אָשִׂיחָה, cohortatives ("let me"); צַר "distress" (narrowness); שִׂיחַ "muse" (so v13: "my musing"; also of complaint).
- **"Am I a sea, or a whale, that thou settest a watch over me?" → "Am I a sea, or a sea monster, that you set a watch over me?"** (v12) — Hebrew יָם, without the article ("Sea" as a name, as in Canaanite myth, is possible); תַּנִּין "sea monster" (not "whale"; exact sense uncertain); מִשְׁמָר "watch" (lit. "a keeping").
- **"When I say, My bed shall comfort me, my couch shall ease my complaint" → "When I say, 'My couch will comfort me, my bed will bear my musing'"** (v13) — Hebrew עַרְשִׂי "my couch", מִשְׁכָּבִי "my bed"; יִשָּׂא בְשִׂיחִי, literally "will lift up in my musing" (bear a share of it).
- **"Then thou scarest me with dreams, and terrifiest me through visions" → "then you dismay me with dreams, and from visions you terrify me"** (v14) — Hebrew חִתַּתַּנִי (חָתַת "be dismayed", piel "dismay"); בִּעֵת "terrify"; חֶזְיֹנוֹת "visions" (as 4:13).
- **"So that my soul chooseth strangling, and death rather than my life" → "and my soul chooses strangling, death rather than my bones"** (v15) — Hebrew מֵעַצְמוֹתָי "rather than my bones" (KJV "my life"; some read עַצְּבוֹתַי "my pains", a change of the Hebrew).
- **"I loathe it; I would not live alway: let me alone; for my days are vanity" → "I reject; not forever shall I live. Cease from me, for my days are vapor"** (v16) — Hebrew מָאַסְתִּי "I reject", with no object ("it" is not written; as 42:6); לֹא־לְעֹלָם אֶחְיֶה "not forever shall I live", a plain imperfect ("would" is not written); חֲדַל מִמֶּנִּי "cease from me"; הֶבֶל "vapor" (breath).
- **"What is man, that thou shouldest magnify him? and that thou shouldest set thine heart upon him?" → "What is mortal man, that you make him great, and that you set your heart on him"** (v17) — Hebrew אֱנוֹשׁ "mortal man"; תְגַדְּלֶנּוּ "make him great"; שִׁית לֵב אֶל "set the heart on" (Ps 8:4 has the like question with זָכַר "remember").
- **"And that thou shouldest visit him every morning, and try him every moment?" → "and visit him every morning, every moment try him?"** (v18) — Hebrew פָּקַד "visit" (for good or for ill); בָּחַן "try" (as metal is tried; נִסָּה is "test").
- **"How long wilt thou not depart from me, nor let me alone till I swallow down my spittle?" → "How long will you not look away from me, not let me go until I swallow my spittle?"** (v19) — Hebrew כַּמָּה "how long"; שָׁעָה מִן "look away from"; תַרְפֵּנִי "let me go" (hifil of רָפָה); רֹק "spittle".
- **"I have sinned; what shall I do unto thee, O thou preserver of men? why hast thou set me as a mark against thee, so that I am a burden to myself?" → "I have sinned: what do I do to you, you guard of mankind? Why have you set me as a mark for you, and I have become a burden upon myself?"** (v20) — Hebrew חָטָאתִי "I have sinned" (no "if" is written; it may be read as a supposition, "if I have sinned"); אֶפְעַל "do, work"; נֹצֵר הָאָדָם "guard of mankind" (נָצַר "guard", here of keeping watch); מִפְגָּע "mark" (only here; from פָּגַע "meet, strike"); עָלַי "upon myself" is the MT; a scribal tradition and the Greek have "upon you" (from memory). The verse is three lines in the Hebrew.
- **"And why dost thou not pardon my transgression, and take away mine iniquity? for now shall I sleep in the dust; and thou shalt seek me in the morning, but I shall not be" → "And why do you not forgive my transgression and make my iniquity pass away? For now I shall lie down in the dust, and you will seek me, and I am not"** (v21) — Hebrew נָשָׂא פֶּשַׁע, literally "lift, bear, take away transgression"; תַעֲבִיר, hifil of עָבַר, "make pass away"; אֶשְׁכָּב "lie down"; שִׁחֲרְתַּנִי "you will seek me" (שִׁחֵר, of the root of שַׁחַר "dawn": "seek early", or "seek earnestly"). The verse is three lines in the Hebrew.

### Notes
- **Sea and sea monster** (v12) — in Ugaritic myth Baal defeats Yam (Sea) and the dragon. Job asks if he is a primeval monster requiring divine surveillance.
- **"What is *enosh*?"** (v17) — turns Ps 8's question. There: wonder at God's care. Here: complaint about God's unrelenting scrutiny.
- **Job speaks to Eloah directly** (v7–21; no divine name is used) — he turns from the friends and argues upward.
- **First direct complaint against Eloah** in the dialogues: excessive divine attention is itself burden.
- **Death-wish** (v15-16, 21) — "lie down in the dust"; Sheol as escape from the divine gaze.
