# Acts 28

[1] And having been saved through, then we came to know that the island is called ⟦malta|Melite|Malta⟧.

[2] And the barbarians were showing us no common humanity; for having kindled a fire they welcomed us all, because of the rain that had set in and because of the cold.

[3] And when ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ had twisted together a certain multitude of dry sticks and laid them on the fire, a viper, having come out from the heat, fastened on his hand.

[4] And when the barbarians saw the beast hanging from his hand, they were saying to one another, "Surely this man is a murderer, whom, having been saved through out of the sea, Justice did not let live."

[5] So then he, having shaken off the beast into the fire, suffered nothing bad;

[6] but they were expecting that he was about to swell up or to fall down suddenly dead. But when they had been expecting for a long while and beheld nothing amiss happening to him, having changed their minds they were saying that he was a god.

[7] Now in the parts around that place there were plots of land belonging to the first man of the island, Publius by name, who, having received us, lodged us in a friendly way for three days.

[8] And it came to pass that the father of Publius was lying down, held by fevers and dysentery; to whom ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having gone in and prayed, having laid his hands on him, healed him.

[9] And when this had come about, the rest also in the island who had sicknesses were approaching and were being healed,

[10] who also honored us with many honors, and as we were putting out to sea they laid on the things for our needs.

[11] And after three months we put out to sea in a ship that had wintered in the island, a ship of ⟦alexandria|Alexandreia|Alexandria⟧, with the Dioscuri as its sign.

[12] And having put in at ⟦syracuse|Syrakousai|Syracuse⟧, we remained three days,

[13] from where, having cast off, we arrived at ⟦rhegium|Rhegion|Rhegium⟧. And after one day, a south wind having come on, on the second day we came to Puteoli,

[14] where, having found brothers, we were exhorted to remain with them seven days; and so we came to ⟦rome|Roma|Rome⟧.

[15] And from there the brothers, having heard the things concerning us, came to meet us as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns; whom ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ having seen, having given thanks to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, took courage.

[16] And when we entered into ⟦rome|Roma|Rome⟧, it was permitted to ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ to remain by himself, with the soldier guarding him.

[17] And it came to pass after three days that he called together those who were the first men of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧; and when they had come together he was saying to them, "I, men, brothers, having done nothing contrary to the people or to the ancestral customs, was delivered up a prisoner from ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ into the hands of the Romans,

[18] who, having examined me, were wishing to release me, because there was no cause of death in me;

[19] but when the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ contradicted, I was compelled to call upon Caesar — not as having anything of which to accuse my nation.

[20] For this cause therefore I appealed to you, to see you and to speak to you; for on account of the hope of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ I have this chain lying around me."

[21] And they said to him, "We neither accepted letters concerning you from ⟦judea|Yehudah|Judea⟧, nor did anyone of the brothers, having arrived, report or speak anything evil concerning you.

[22] But we think it right to hear from you what you think; for concerning this sect it is known to us that everywhere it is contradicted."

[23] And having arranged a day with him, they came to him into the lodging, more of them, to whom he was setting it forth, solemnly testifying the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and persuading them concerning ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, both from the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ of ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and from the prophets, from early until evening.

[24] And some were being persuaded by the things said, but others were disbelieving;

[25] and being out of accord with one another, they were departing, after ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ had said one word: "Well did the ⟦ruach-haqodesh|Ruach HaQodesh|Holy Spirit⟧ speak through ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ the prophet to your fathers,

[26] saying, '**Go to this people and say,
"With hearing you will hear and by no means understand,
and looking you will look and by no means see."
[27] For the heart of this people was made thick,
and with the ears they heard heavily,
and their eyes they shut,
lest they should see with the eyes
and hear with the ears
and understand with the heart
and turn, and I will heal them.**'

[28] Let it therefore be known to you that to the nations this salvation of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has been sent; they also will hear."

[30] And he abode two whole years in his own hired lodging, and was accepting all who came in to him,

[31] proclaiming the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and teaching the things concerning the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ with all boldness, unhindered.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v23, 31) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v31) — Hebrew messianic title.
- **"Holy Ghost" → "*Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v25) — consistent rendering.
- **"whose sign was Castor and Pollux" → "with the Dioscuri as its sign"** (v11) — Greek παρασήμῳ Διοσκούροις, "marked with the Dioskouroi" ("sons of Zeus", the twins Castor and Pollux); the name is kept as the Greek has it.
- **"Puteoli"** (v13) — modern Pozzuoli near Naples; Greek Ποτίολοι *Potioloi*; retained.
- **v26-27** — OT quotation: Isa 6:9-10, translated from the Greek as Acts prints it, which is the Greek of the Septuagint (so Matt 13:14–15). οὐ μή "by no means"; ἐπαχύνθη "was made thick, fat"; βαρέως ἤκουσαν "heard heavily"; ἐκάμμυσαν "shut"; ἰάσομαι is a future indicative, "and I will heal them". The Hebrew of Isa 6:10 has commands ("Make the heart of this people fat"). Paul applies it to Jewish unbelief, as does *Yeshua* in John 12:40; Matthew 13:14-15; Mark 4:12; Luke 8:10.
- **v29 absent** — KJV v29 ("And when he had said these words, the Jews departed, and had great reasoning among themselves") is only in the Byzantine text (RP); omitted.
- **The book ends open** — v30-31: Paul preaches in Rome for two years "unhindered" (ἀκωλύτως *akōlytōs*); Luke does not narrate Paul's death; the movement continues spreading.
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
- **"when they were escaped, then they knew that the island was called Melita" → "having been saved through, then we came to know that the island is called Malta"** (v1) — Greek διασωθέντες as 27:44; ἐπέγνωμεν (WH, Treg, NA28), first person; Μελίτη (Treg, NA28, RP; WH Μελιτήνη); καλεῖται, present.
- **"the barbarous people shewed us no little kindness … received us every one, because of the present rain" → "the barbarians were showing us no common humanity … welcomed us all, because of the rain that had set in"** (v2) — Greek βάρβαροι, people who do not speak Greek (1 Cor 14:11), not "savages"; οὐ τὴν τυχοῦσαν φιλανθρωπίαν, "love for mankind" (Titus 3:4; 27:3 "humanely"); προσελάβοντο; τὸν ἐφεστῶτα.
- **"gathered a bundle of sticks" → "twisted together a certain multitude of dry sticks"** (v3) — Greek συστρέψαντος; φρυγάνων τι πλῆθος (WH, Treg, NA28 have τι). "Out of the heat" → "from the heat": ἀπὸ τῆς θέρμης (WH, Treg, NA28), "away from" or "because of" the heat.
- **"the venomous beast … they said among themselves, No doubt … though he hath escaped the sea, yet vengeance suffereth not to live" → "the beast … they were saying to one another, 'Surely … having been saved through out of the sea, Justice did not let live'"** (v4) — Greek θηρίον, "beast"; "venomous" is not in the Greek; Πάντως; διασωθέντα; ἡ δίκη, "justice, the penalty" (2 Thess 1:9), here spoken of as a power that pursues, perhaps the goddess Dike; οὐκ εἴασεν, aorist: they speak of his death as already settled.
- **"felt no harm" → "suffered nothing bad"** (v5) — Greek ἔπαθεν οὐδὲν κακόν.
- **"they looked when he should have swollen … after they had looked a great while, and saw no harm come to him" → "they were expecting that he was about to swell up … when they had been expecting for a long while and beheld nothing amiss happening to him"** (v6) — Greek προσεδόκων, προσδοκώντων; πίμπρασθαι, "swell up" or "burn with fever"; θεωρούντων; ἄτοπον as 25:5; μεταβαλόμενοι.
- **"In the same quarters were possessions of the chief man of the island, whose name was Publius; who received us, and lodged us three days courteously" → "in the parts around that place there were plots of land belonging to the first man of the island, Publius by name, who, having received us, lodged us in a friendly way for three days"** (v7) — Greek χωρία as 4:34; τῷ πρώτῳ τῆς νήσου, a title known from inscriptions of Malta; ἐξένισεν as 10:23; φιλοφρόνως.
- **"lay sick of a fever and of a bloody flux: to whom Paul entered in, and prayed" → "was lying down, held by fevers and dysentery; to whom Paul, having gone in and prayed"** (v8) — Greek πυρετοῖς, plural; συνεχόμενον; κατακεῖσθαι; ἰάσατο.
- **"others also, which had diseases in the island, came, and were healed" → "the rest also in the island who had sicknesses were approaching and were being healed"** (v9) — Greek οἱ λοιποί; ἀσθενείας as John 11:4; προσήρχοντο; ἐθεραπεύοντο, a different verb from "healed" in v8 (ἰάομαι).
- **"when we departed, they laded us with such things as were necessary" → "as we were putting out to sea they laid on the things for our needs"** (v10) — Greek ἀναγομένοις; ἐπέθεντο τὰ πρὸς τὰς χρείας (WH, Treg, NA28 plural).
- **"we departed in a ship of Alexandria" → "we put out to sea in a ship … a ship of Alexandria"** (v11) — Greek ἀνήχθημεν (WH, Treg, NA28); Ἀλεξανδρίνῳ, an adjective, "an Alexandrian one".
- **"landing at Syracuse, we tarried there" → "having put in at Syracuse, we remained"** (v12) — Greek καταχθέντες as 27:3; ἐπεμείναμεν; no "there".
- **"we fetched a compass, and came to Rhegium: and after one day the south wind blew, and we came the next day" → "having cast off, we arrived at Rhegium. And after one day, a south wind having come on, on the second day we came"** (v13) — Greek περιελόντες (WH, NA28; 2–2, NA28 decides), the verb of 27:40, "having cast off" (the anchors); Treg, RP περιελθόντες, "having gone around", which KJV follows; κατηντήσαμεν; δευτεραῖοι.
- **"were desired to tarry with them seven days: and so we went toward Rome" → "we were exhorted to remain with them seven days; and so we came to Rome"** (v14) — Greek παρεκλήθημεν, also "we were encouraged"; παρʼ αὐτοῖς (WH, Treg, NA28); εἰς τὴν Ῥώμην ἤλθαμεν.
- **"when the brethren heard of us, they came to meet us as far as Appii forum, and The three taverns … he thanked God" → "the brothers, having heard the things concerning us, came to meet us as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns … having given thanks to God"** (v15) — Greek Ἀππίου Φόρου καὶ Τριῶν Ταβερνῶν, the Latin names *Appii Forum* and *Tres Tabernae*, two stations on the Appian Way; εὐχαριστήσας; θάρσος.
- **"when we came to Rome, the centurion delivered the prisoners to the captain of the guard: but Paul was suffered to dwell by himself with a soldier that kept him" → "when we entered into Rome, it was permitted to Paul to remain by himself, with the soldier guarding him"** (v16) — Greek εἰσήλθομεν; ἐπετράπη τῷ Παύλῳ (WH, Treg, NA28); the words about the centurion are Byzantine; μένειν, the verb μένω ("abide" of persons), here of a bare stay: "remain"; τῷ φυλάσσοντι αὐτὸν στρατιώτῃ, with the article.
- **"Paul called the chief of the Jews together … Men and brethren, though I have committed nothing against the people, or customs of our fathers, yet was I delivered prisoner" → "he called together those who were the first men of the Jews … I, men, brothers, having done nothing contrary to the people or to the ancestral customs, was delivered up a prisoner"** (v17) — Greek αὐτόν (WH, Treg, NA28); τοὺς ὄντας τῶν Ἰουδαίων πρώτους; Ἐγώ, ἄνδρες ἀδελφοί; τοῖς ἔθεσι τοῖς πατρῴοις, as 24:14, no "our"; παρεδόθην.
- **"would have let me go" → "were wishing to release me"** (v18) — Greek ἐβούλοντο ἀπολῦσαι; αἰτίαν θανάτου as 13:28.
- **"when the Jews spake against it, I was constrained to appeal unto Caesar; not that I had ought to accuse my nation of" → "when the Jews contradicted, I was compelled to call upon Caesar — not as having anything of which to accuse my nation"** (v19) — Greek ἀντιλεγόντων as 13:45; ἠναγκάσθην; ἐπικαλέσασθαι as 25:11; οὐχ ὡς … ἔχων τι κατηγορεῖν.
- **"For this cause therefore have I called for you, to see you, and to speak with you: because that for the hope of Israel I am bound with this chain" → "For this cause therefore I appealed to you, to see you and to speak to you; for on account of the hope of Israel I have this chain lying around me"** (v20) — Greek παρεκάλεσα ὑμᾶς ἰδεῖν καὶ προσλαλῆσαι: "I appealed to you, to see and speak to you", or "I appealed to see you and speak to you"; "you" stands once in the Greek. τὴν ἅλυσιν ταύτην περίκειμαι.
- **"We neither received letters out of Judaea concerning thee, neither any of the brethren that came shewed or spake any harm of thee" → "We neither accepted letters concerning you from Judea, nor did anyone of the brothers, having arrived, report or speak anything evil concerning you"** (v21) — Greek ἐδεξάμεθα; παραγενόμενος; ἀπήγγειλεν; πονηρόν.
- **"we desire to hear of thee what thou thinkest … we know that every where it is spoken against" → "we think it right to hear from you what you think … it is known to us that everywhere it is contradicted"** (v22) — Greek ἀξιοῦμεν as 15:38; φρονεῖς; αἱρέσεως, "sect" as 24:5; γνωστὸν ἡμῖν ἐστιν; ἀντιλέγεται as v19.
- **"when they had appointed him a day, there came many to him into his lodging; to whom he expounded and testified the kingdom of God … from morning till evening" → "having arranged a day with him, they came to him into the lodging, more of them, to whom he was setting it forth, solemnly testifying the kingdom of God … from early until evening"** (v23) — Greek Ταξάμενοι as 15:2; ἦλθον (WH, Treg, NA28) for the printed ἧκον (RP), no difference in English; ξενίαν as Phlm 22; πλείονες, a comparative, "more" (than before), or "very many"; ἐξετίθετο as 11:4, without object; διαμαρτυρόμενος; πρωΐ. "The law of Moses": τοῦ νόμου Μωϋσέως.
- **"some believed the things which were spoken, and some believed not" → "some were being persuaded by the things said, but others were disbelieving"** (v24) — Greek ἐπείθοντο, ἠπίστουν, imperfects.
- **"when they agreed not among themselves, they departed … Well spake the Holy Ghost by Esaias the prophet unto our fathers" → "being out of accord with one another, they were departing … Well did the Holy Spirit speak through Isaiah the prophet to your fathers"** (v25) — Greek ἀσύμφωνοι; ἀπελύοντο, the verb "send away, release" in the middle voice; ῥῆμα ἕν; ὑμῶν (WH, Treg, NA28), RP ἡμῶν "our".
- **"Be it known therefore unto you, that the salvation of God is sent unto the Gentiles, and that they will hear it" → "Let it therefore be known to you that to the nations this salvation of God has been sent; they also will hear"** (v28) — Greek τοῦτο τὸ σωτήριον (WH, Treg, NA28 have "this"), a different word from σωτηρία (27:34), as Eph 6:17; ἀπεστάλη; αὐτοὶ καὶ ἀκούσονται, without object.
- **"Paul dwelt two whole years in his own hired house, and received all that came in unto him" → "he abode two whole years in his own hired lodging, and was accepting all who came in to him"** (v30) — Greek Ἐνέμεινεν (WH, Treg, NA28), "abode in", without "Paul"; ἐν ἰδίῳ μισθώματι, "in his own hired thing": a rented dwelling, or "at his own expense, on his own earnings"; "lodging" is supplied; ἀπεδέχετο as 2:41.
- **"Preaching … those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ, with all confidence, no man forbidding him" → "proclaiming … the things concerning the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness, unhindered"** (v31) — Greek κηρύσσων; παρρησίας; ἀκωλύτως, one word, the last of the book.

### Notes
- **v26-27** quotes Isa 6:9-10.
- **v29** absent from earliest MSS.
