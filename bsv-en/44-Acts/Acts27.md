# Acts 27

[1] And when it was judged that we should sail away to ⟦italy|Italia|Italy⟧, they were delivering up both ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ and certain other prisoners to a centurion, Julius by name, of the Augustan cohort.

[2] And having gone on board a ship of ⟦adramyttium|Adramyttion|Adramyttium⟧, which was about to sail to the places along Asia, we put out to sea, ⟦aristarchus|Aristarchos|Aristarchus⟧, a ⟦macedonians|Makedon|Macedonian⟧ of ⟦thessalonica|Thessalonike|Thessalonica⟧, being with us.

[3] And on the next we put in at ⟦sidon|Tzidon|Sidon⟧; and Julius, treating ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ humanely, permitted him, having gone to the friends, to attain care.

[4] And from there, having put out to sea, we sailed under ⟦cyprus|Kypros|Cyprus⟧, because the winds were contrary,

[5] and having sailed across the open sea that is along ⟦cilicia|Kilikia|Cilicia⟧ and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra of ⟦lycia|Lykia|Lycia⟧.

[6] And there the centurion, having found a ship of ⟦alexandria|Alexandreia|Alexandria⟧ sailing to ⟦italy|Italia|Italy⟧, put us on board it.

[7] And sailing slowly for a considerable number of days, and having come with difficulty off ⟦cnidus|Knidos|Cnidus⟧, the wind not letting us go on, we sailed under ⟦crete|Krete|Crete⟧, off Salmone,

[8] and coasting along it with difficulty we came to a certain place called Fair Havens, near to which was a city, Lasea.

[9] And when considerable time had passed and the voyage was already dangerous, because even the Fast had already passed by, ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ was advising,

[10] saying to them, "Men, I behold that the voyage is about to be with injury and much loss, not only of the load and of the ship but also of our souls."

[11] But the centurion was persuaded by the helmsman and the shipowner rather than by the things said by ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧.

[12] And the harbor being unsuitable for wintering, the majority laid down a counsel to put out to sea from there, if somehow they might be able, having arrived at ⟦phoenix|Phoinix|Phoenix⟧, to winter there — a harbor of ⟦crete|Krete|Crete⟧ looking toward Lips and toward Choros.

[13] And when a south wind blew gently, thinking that they had taken hold of their purpose, having weighed anchor they were coasting along ⟦crete|Krete|Crete⟧, closer in.

[14] But after not long there beat down from it a typhonic wind, the one called Eurakylon;

[15] and when the ship was seized and could not face the wind, having given way we were being carried along.

[16] And running under a certain small island called ⟦cauda|Kauda|Cauda⟧, we had strength with difficulty to get control of the skiff,

[17] which having lifted up, they were using helps, undergirding the ship; and fearing lest they should fall upon the Syrtis, having let down the gear, thus they were being carried along.

[18] And as we were being violently storm-tossed, on the next day they were making a casting out,

[19] and on the third day with their own hands they threw out the tackle of the ship.

[20] And when neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and no small storm was lying upon us, at last all hope of our being saved was being stripped away.

[21] And when there was much going without food, then ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having stood in their midst, said, "You ought indeed, O men, having obeyed me, not to have put out to sea from ⟦crete|Krete|Crete⟧, and to have been spared this injury and loss.

[22] And now I advise you to be of good cheer, for there will be no throwing away of a soul from among you, but only of the ship;

[23] for there stood by me this night an angel of the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ whose I am, whom I also worship,

[24] saying, 'Do not fear, ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧; you must stand before Caesar; and behold, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has granted you all those sailing with you.'

[25] Therefore be of good cheer, men; for I believe ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that it will be so, in the way in which it has been spoken to me.

[26] But we must fall upon a certain island."

[27] And when the fourteenth night came, as we were being carried about in the Adria, about the middle of the night the sailors were surmising that some country was drawing near to them;

[28] and having sounded, they found twenty fathoms; and having gone a little farther and sounded again, they found fifteen fathoms;

[29] and fearing lest somewhere we should fall upon rough places, having thrown four anchors from the stern, they were praying for day to come.

[30] And as the sailors were seeking to flee out of the ship and had let down the skiff into the sea, on a pretext, as though they were about to stretch out anchors from the bow,

[31] ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ said to the centurion and to the soldiers, "Unless these remain in the ship, you cannot be saved."

[32] Then the soldiers cut off the ropes of the skiff and let it fall away.

[33] And until day was about to come, ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ was exhorting all to partake of food, saying, "Today is the fourteenth day that, expecting, you continue without food, having taken nothing;

[34] therefore I exhort you to partake of food, for this is for your salvation; for not a hair from the head of any of you will perish."

[35] And having said these things and having taken bread, he gave thanks to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ before all, and having broken it he began to eat.

[36] And all, having become of good cheer, themselves also took food.

[37] And we were, all the souls in the ship, two hundred seventy-six.

[38] And having been sated with food, they were lightening the ship, casting out the wheat into the sea.

[39] And when day came, they did not recognize the land, but they were observing a certain bay having a beach, onto which they were taking counsel whether they might be able to thrust out the ship.

[40] And having cast off the anchors, they were letting them go into the sea, at the same time having loosened the bands of the rudders; and having lifted up the foresail to the blowing wind, they were holding for the beach.

[41] But having fallen upon a place of two seas, they ran the vessel aground; and the bow, having stuck fast, remained unshakable, but the stern was being broken up by the force of the waves.

[42] And the soldiers' counsel was that they should kill the prisoners, lest anyone, having swum out, should escape;

[43] but the centurion, wishing to save ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ through, hindered them from the intention, and commanded those able to swim, having thrown themselves off first, to go out upon the land,

[44] and the rest, some on planks and some on certain of the things from the ship; and so it came to pass that all were saved through, upon the land.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v23, 24, 25, 35) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** — no direct occurrence in this chapter.
- **"Holy Ghost" / Spirit** — no explicit occurrence in this chapter.
- **"the fast" → "the Fast"** (v9) — Greek τὴν νηστείαν; refers to *Yom Kippur*, the Day of Atonement (10 Tishri); the season after which sailing in the Mediterranean was considered dangerous (roughly mid-October); significant Jewish calendar reference.
- **"Adria" → "the Adria"** (v27) — Greek ἐν τῷ Ἀδρίᾳ, with the article and without "sea"; in ancient geography the Adria was the sea south of Italy and west of Greece (now the Ionian Sea), not only the modern narrow Adriatic.
- **"the quicksands" → "the Syrtis"** (v17) — Greek τὴν Σύρτιν; the shallows and sandbanks off the north African coast (Syrtis Major/Minor); navigation hazard; the name is kept.
- **"Clauda" → "Cauda"** (v16) — Greek Καῦδα (WH, Treg, NA28; RP Κλαύδην); small island south of Crete.
- **"fathoms" kept** (v28) — Greek ὀργυιάς *orgyias*, the stretch of both arms, about 1.8 m each; "fathom" is the same measure.
- **"Euroclydon" → "Eurakylon"** (v14) — Greek Εὐρακύλων *Eurakylōn* (WH, Treg, NA28), the name of a wind, made of Εὖρος (the east wind) and the Latin *Aquilo* (the north wind): a north-east wind; the name is kept as the Greek has it. KJV "Euroclydon" is the Byzantine reading Εὐροκλύδων.
- **"toward the south west and north west" → "toward Lips and toward Choros"** (v12) — Greek κατὰ λίβα καὶ κατὰ χῶρον, two wind names: λίψ, the south-west wind, and χῶρος (Latin *Caurus*, *Corus*), the north-west wind; kept as names. κατά is either "toward" (a harbor facing south-west and north-west) or "down, along" the direction in which these winds blow (a harbor facing north-east and south-east).
- **"the south wind" kept** (v13) — Greek νότου, the common word for the south wind and for the south (28:13).
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
- **"when it was determined that we should sail into Italy, they delivered … unto one named Julius, a centurion of Augustus' band" → "when it was judged that we should sail away to Italy, they were delivering up … to a centurion, Julius by name, of the Augustan cohort"** (v1) — Greek ἐκρίθη; ἀποπλεῖν; παρεδίδουν, imperfect; σπείρης Σεβαστῆς, as 10:1 and 25:21. "Prisoners" is δεσμώτας here and in v42; δέσμιος in 25:14.
- **"entering into a ship of Adramyttium, we launched, meaning to sail by the coasts of Asia" → "having gone on board a ship of Adramyttium, which was about to sail to the places along Asia, we put out to sea"** (v2) — Greek μέλλοντι (WH, Treg, NA28), of the ship; RP μέλλοντες, "we being about"; εἰς τοὺς κατὰ τὴν Ἀσίαν τόπους; ἀνήχθημεν as 13:13. "A Macedonian of Thessalonica" kept: Μακεδόνος Θεσσαλονικέως, "a Macedonian, a Thessalonian".
- **"the next day we touched at Sidon. And Julius courteously entreated Paul, and gave him liberty to go unto his friends to refresh himself" → "on the next we put in at Sidon; and Julius, treating Paul humanely, permitted him, having gone to the friends, to attain care"** (v3) — Greek τῇ ἑτέρᾳ, without "day"; κατήχθημεν, the opposite of ἀνάγομαι; φιλανθρώπως, the root of "love for mankind" (Titus 3:4); χρησάμενος, the verb "use", here "deal with"; τοὺς φίλους, no "his"; ἐπιμελείας τυχεῖν.
- **"when we had launched from thence, we sailed under Cyprus" kept in sense** (v4) — Greek ἀναχθέντες; ὑπεπλεύσαμεν, "sailed under", that is, under its lee, sheltered by it (so v7; v16 "running under").
- **"sailed over the sea of Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Myra, a city of Lycia" → "sailed across the open sea that is along Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra of Lycia"** (v5) — Greek τὸ πέλαγος τὸ κατὰ τὴν Κιλικίαν; κατήλθομεν; no "a city".
- **"a ship of Alexandria sailing into Italy; and he put us therein" → "a ship of Alexandria sailing to Italy, put us on board it"** (v6) — Greek πλοῖον Ἀλεξανδρῖνον, an adjective, "an Alexandrian ship"; ἐνεβίβασεν.
- **"when we had sailed slowly many days, and scarce were come over against Cnidus, the wind not suffering us … over against Salmone" → "sailing slowly for a considerable number of days, and having come with difficulty off Cnidus, the wind not letting us go on … off Salmone"** (v7) — Greek ἐν ἱκαναῖς ἡμέραις; μόλις; μὴ προσεῶντος, ἐάω "let" with προσ- "further"; κατά.
- **"hardly passing it … The fair havens; nigh whereunto was the city of Lasea" → "coasting along it with difficulty … Fair Havens, near to which was a city, Lasea"** (v8) — Greek παραλεγόμενοι (so v13); πόλις without article.
- **"when much time was spent, and when sailing was now dangerous, because the fast was now already past, Paul admonished them" → "when considerable time had passed and the voyage was already dangerous, because even the Fast had already passed by, Paul was advising"** (v9) — Greek Ἱκανοῦ χρόνου; τοῦ πλοός; καὶ τὴν νηστείαν, "even the Fast"; παρῄνει, imperfect, without object (so v22).
- **"Sirs, I perceive that this voyage will be with hurt and much damage, not only of the lading and ship, but also of our lives" → "Men, I behold that the voyage is about to be with injury and much loss, not only of the load and of the ship but also of our souls"** (v10) — Greek Ἄνδρες; θεωρῶ; ὕβρεως, elsewhere "mistreatment" of persons (2 Cor 12:10), here the damage done by the sea; ζημίας as Phil 3:7; φορτίου as Gal 6:5; ψυχῶν, "souls", that is, lives.
- **"the centurion believed the master and the owner of the ship, more than" → "the centurion was persuaded by the helmsman and the shipowner rather than"** (v11) — Greek ἐπείθετο; κυβερνήτῃ, the one who steers; ναυκλήρῳ.
- **"because the haven was not commodious to winter in, the more part advised to depart thence also, if by any means they might attain to Phenice, and there to winter" → "the harbor being unsuitable for wintering, the majority laid down a counsel to put out to sea from there, if somehow they might be able, having arrived at Phoenix, to winter there"** (v12) — Greek ἀνευθέτου; ἔθεντο βουλήν; ἀναχθῆναι ἐκεῖθεν (WH, Treg, NA28; no "also"); καταντήσαντες. "There" is supplied.
- **"when the south wind blew softly, supposing that they had obtained their purpose, loosing thence, they sailed close by Crete" → "when a south wind blew gently, thinking that they had taken hold of their purpose, having weighed anchor they were coasting along Crete, closer in"** (v13) — Greek Ὑποπνεύσαντος; δόξαντες; κεκρατηκέναι; ἄραντες, "having lifted": "anchor" is supplied; ἆσσον, a comparative, "nearer" (to the land).
- **"not long after there arose against it a tempestuous wind" → "after not long there beat down from it a typhonic wind"** (v14) — Greek ἔβαλεν κατʼ αὐτῆς, "threw itself down from it": "it" is feminine, Crete (the ship is neuter); the words can also mean "against it". τυφωνικός, "like a typhon", a whirlwind.
- **"when the ship was caught, and could not bear up into the wind, we let her drive" → "when the ship was seized and could not face the wind, having given way we were being carried along"** (v15) — Greek συναρπασθέντος as 6:12; ἀντοφθαλμεῖν, "look in the eye"; ἐπιδόντες, "having given over" (15:30), without object; ἐφερόμεθα (so v17).
- **"a certain island … we had much work to come by the boat" → "a certain small island … we had strength with difficulty to get control of the skiff"** (v16) — Greek νησίον, a diminutive; ἰσχύσαμεν μόλις περικρατεῖς γενέσθαι; σκάφης, the ship's small boat (v30, 32).
- **"when they had taken up … fearing lest they should fall into the quicksands, strake sail, and so were driven" → "having lifted up … fearing lest they should fall upon the Syrtis, having let down the gear, thus they were being carried along"** (v17) — Greek ἄραντες; βοηθείαις, "helps" (Heb 4:16), here ropes or cables passed around the hull; ἐκπέσωσιν, "fall out", the seaman's word for being driven out of the course and ashore (v26, 29; v32 "fall away"); χαλάσαντες τὸ σκεῦος, "having let down the vessel / implement": which piece of gear is meant (the sail, the yard, a sea anchor) is not said.
- **"we being exceedingly tossed with a tempest, the next day they lightened the ship" → "as we were being violently storm-tossed, on the next day they were making a casting out"** (v18) — Greek σφοδρῶς χειμαζομένων; ἐκβολὴν ἐποιοῦντο, the noun of ἐκβάλλω "cast out" (v38): the throwing overboard of cargo.
- **"we cast out with our own hands the tackling" → "with their own hands they threw out the tackle"** (v19) — Greek ἔρριψαν (WH, Treg, NA28), third person; RP first person. σκευήν, the ship's equipment.
- **"no small tempest lay on us, all hope that we should be saved was then taken away" → "no small storm was lying upon us, at last all hope of our being saved was being stripped away"** (v20) — Greek χειμῶνος; ἐπικειμένου; λοιπόν; περιῃρεῖτο, imperfect, as Heb 10:11; τοῦ σῴζεσθαι, "save", here of rescue from the sea (so v31; v34 "salvation").
- **"after long abstinence Paul stood forth … Sirs, ye should have hearkened unto me, and not have loosed from Crete, and to have gained this harm and loss" → "when there was much going without food, then Paul, having stood … You ought indeed, O men, having obeyed me, not to have put out to sea from Crete, and to have been spared this injury and loss"** (v21) — Greek Πολλῆς ἀσιτίας ὑπαρχούσης; πειθαρχήσαντας as 5:29; ἀνάγεσθαι; κερδῆσαι, literally "to have gained": in Greek one "gains" a loss by escaping it. A word-for-word "gained this injury" would say the opposite in English, so the verse gives the meaning.
- **"I exhort you to be of good cheer: for there shall be no loss of any man's life among you, but of the ship" → "I advise you to be of good cheer, for there will be no throwing away of a soul from among you, but only of the ship"** (v22) — Greek παραινῶ as v9; εὐθυμεῖν; ἀποβολὴ ψυχῆς οὐδεμία, ἀποβολή as Rom 11:15 (a different word from ζημία "loss", v21); πλὴν τοῦ πλοίου.
- **"the angel of God, whose I am, and whom I serve" → "an angel of the God whose I am, whom I also worship"** (v23) — Greek τοῦ θεοῦ οὗ εἰμι … ἄγγελος, "angel" without article; ᾧ καὶ λατρεύω, "worship" in the sense of serving God. NA28 adds ἐγώ, "I".
- **"thou must be brought before Caesar: and, lo, God hath given thee" → "you must stand before Caesar; and behold, God has granted you"** (v24) — Greek παραστῆναι; κεχάρισται, as 25:11.
- **"Wherefore, sirs, be of good cheer: for I believe God, that it shall be even as it was told me" → "Therefore be of good cheer, men; for I believe God, that it will be so, in the way in which it has been spoken to me"** (v25) — Greek πιστεύω τῷ θεῷ, a dative; οὕτως ἔσται καθʼ ὃν τρόπον λελάληταί μοι.
- **"we must be cast upon" → "we must fall upon"** (v26) — Greek ἐκπεσεῖν, as v17.
- **"as we were driven up and down in Adria, about midnight the shipmen deemed that they drew near to some country" → "as we were being carried about in the Adria, about the middle of the night the sailors were surmising that some country was drawing near to them"** (v27) — Greek διαφερομένων; κατὰ μέσον τῆς νυκτός; ὑπενόουν as 25:18; προσάγειν τινὰ αὐτοῖς χώραν: the land is the subject, as seamen speak.
- **"sounded, and found it twenty fathoms: and when they had gone a little further" → "having sounded, they found twenty fathoms; and having gone a little farther"** (v28) — Greek βολίσαντες, "having cast the lead"; βραχὺ διαστήσαντες, "having set a short interval".
- **"fearing lest we should have fallen upon rocks, they cast four anchors out of the stern, and wished for the day" → "fearing lest somewhere we should fall upon rough places, having thrown four anchors from the stern, they were praying for day to come"** (v29) — Greek μή που κατὰ τραχεῖς τόπους (WH, Treg, NA28) ἐκπέσωμεν; ῥίψαντες; ηὔχοντο, the verb "pray" (26:29), also "wish".
- **"as the shipmen were about to flee … under colour as though they would have cast anchors out of the foreship" → "as the sailors were seeking to flee … on a pretext, as though they were about to stretch out anchors from the bow"** (v30) — Greek ζητούντων; προφάσει; ἐκτείνειν.
- **"Except these abide in the ship" → "Unless these remain in the ship"** (v31) — Greek μείνωσιν, the verb μένω ("abide" of persons), here of a stay in a place: "remain"; so of the bow in v41.
- **"let her fall off" → "let it fall away"** (v32) — Greek εἴασαν αὐτὴν ἐκπεσεῖν.
- **"while the day was coming on, Paul besought them all to take meat … that ye have tarried and continued fasting" → "until day was about to come, Paul was exhorting all to partake of food … that, expecting, you continue without food"** (v33) — Greek Ἄχρι οὗ ἡμέρα ἤμελλεν γίνεσθαι; παρεκάλει; μεταλαβεῖν τροφῆς as 2:46; προσδοκῶντες, "expecting, waiting"; ἄσιτοι διατελεῖτε.
- **"I pray you to take some meat: for this is for your health: for there shall not an hair fall" → "I exhort you to partake of food, for this is for your salvation; for not a hair … will perish"** (v34) — Greek παρακαλῶ; μεταλαβεῖν (WH, Treg, NA28); σωτηρίας, "salvation", here the saving of their lives; ἀπολεῖται (WH, Treg, NA28), RP πεσεῖται "fall".
- **"in presence of them all" → "before all"; "they also took some meat" → "themselves also took food"** (v35–36) — Greek ἐνώπιον πάντων; εὔθυμοι γενόμενοι; προσελάβοντο τροφῆς.
- **"we were in all in the ship two hundred threescore and sixteen souls" → "we were, all the souls in the ship, two hundred seventy-six"** (v37) — Greek αἱ πᾶσαι ψυχαὶ ἐν τῷ πλοίῳ διακόσιαι ἑβδομήκοντα ἕξ (Treg, NA28, RP); WH ὡς ἑβδομήκοντα ἕξ, "about seventy-six".
- **"when they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship, and cast out the wheat" → "having been sated with food, they were lightening the ship, casting out the wheat"** (v38) — Greek κορεσθέντες τροφῆς as 1 Cor 4:8; ἐκούφιζον; ἐκβαλλόμενοι.
- **"they knew not the land: but they discovered a certain creek with a shore, into the which they were minded, if it were possible, to thrust in the ship" → "they did not recognize the land, but they were observing a certain bay having a beach, onto which they were taking counsel whether they might be able to thrust out the ship"** (v39) — Greek ἐπεγίνωσκον; κατενόουν; κόλπον, the word "bosom" (John 1:18), here a bay; αἰγιαλόν; ἐβουλεύοντο (WH, Treg, NA28); ἐξῶσαι (Treg, NA28, RP), "thrust out" (7:45), that is, drive the ship ashore; WH ἐκσῶσαι, "bring safely out".
- **"when they had taken up the anchors, they committed themselves unto the sea, and loosed the rudder bands, and hoised up the mainsail to the wind, and made toward shore" → "having cast off the anchors, they were letting them go into the sea, at the same time having loosened the bands of the rudders; and having lifted up the foresail to the blowing wind, they were holding for the beach"** (v40) — Greek περιελόντες, the verb of v20 ("strip away"), here of cutting the anchors loose; εἴων εἰς τὴν θάλασσαν, "they were letting [them] go into the sea"; πηδαλίων, plural, the two steering oars; ἀρτέμωνα, a sail named only here, taken as the small sail at the bow; τῇ πνεούσῃ, "to the blowing", "wind" supplied; κατεῖχον, "hold fast", here "hold course".
- **"falling into a place where two seas met, they ran the ship aground; and the forepart stuck fast, and remained unmoveable, but the hinder part was broken with the violence of the waves" → "having fallen upon a place of two seas, they ran the vessel aground; and the bow, having stuck fast, remained unshakable, but the stern was being broken up by the force of the waves"** (v41) — Greek τόπον διθάλασσον, "a two-sea place": a bank or point with sea on both sides, or a channel; ναῦν, a different word from πλοῖον "ship", only here in the NT; ἀσάλευτος as Heb 12:28; ἐλύετο, the verb "loose"; ὑπὸ τῆς βίας τῶν κυμάτων (Treg, NA28, RP); WH has only "by the force".
- **"lest any of them should swim out, and escape" → "lest anyone, having swum out, should escape"** (v42) — Greek βουλή, "counsel" as v12; ἐκκολυμβήσας διαφύγῃ.
- **"willing to save Paul, kept them from their purpose; and commanded that they which could swim should cast themselves first into the sea, and get to land" → "wishing to save Paul through, hindered them from the intention, and commanded those able to swim, having thrown themselves off first, to go out upon the land"** (v43) — Greek βουλόμενος; διασῶσαι, σῴζω "save" with διά "through" (1 Pet 3:20), of bringing safe through danger; ἐκώλυσεν; βουλήματος; ἀπορίψαντας; "into the sea" is not in the Greek.
- **"some on boards, and some on broken pieces of the ship. And so it came to pass, that they escaped all safe to land" → "some on planks and some on certain of the things from the ship; and so it came to pass that all were saved through, upon the land"** (v44) — Greek ἐπί τινων τῶν ἀπὸ τοῦ πλοίου, "on some of the things (or: of those) from the ship": the Greek does not say "broken pieces", and the words could also mean "on some of the men from the ship"; διασωθῆναι as v43.
