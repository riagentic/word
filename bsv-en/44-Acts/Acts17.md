# Acts 17

[1] And having journeyed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to ⟦thessalonica|Thessalonike|Thessalonica⟧, where there was a synagogue of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧.

[2] And according to what was customary for ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, he entered in to them, and for three ⟦shabbat|Shabbats|Sabbaths⟧ he discussed with them from the Scriptures,

[3] opening up and setting before them that the ⟦mashiach|Mashiach|Christ⟧ had to suffer and to rise from the dead, and that "this one is the ⟦mashiach|Mashiach|Christ⟧, the ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ whom I proclaim to you."

[4] And some of them were persuaded and were allotted to ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ and ⟦silas|Sila|Silas⟧, and of the revering ⟦greeks|Yevanim|Greeks⟧ a great multitude, and of the first women not a few.

[5] But the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, having become jealous and having taken to themselves some evil men of the market people, and having made a crowd, were throwing the city into commotion; and having come upon the house of ⟦jason|Iason|Jason⟧, they were seeking to bring them forth to the populace.

[6] And not having found them, they were dragging ⟦jason|Iason|Jason⟧ and some brothers before the city rulers, crying, "These who have unsettled the inhabited earth are present here also,

[7] whom ⟦jason|Iason|Jason⟧ has received; and these all act contrary to the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧."

[8] And they troubled the crowd and the city rulers, who were hearing these things,

[9] and having taken the security from ⟦jason|Iason|Jason⟧ and the rest, they released them.

[10] And the brothers immediately, through the night, sent out both ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ and ⟦silas|Sila|Silas⟧ to ⟦berea|Beroia|Berea⟧; who, having arrived, went away into the synagogue of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧.

[11] Now these were more noble than those in ⟦thessalonica|Thessalonike|Thessalonica⟧, who accepted the word with all readiness, day by day examining the Scriptures, whether these things were so.

[12] So many of them believed, and of the Greek women of high standing and of men not a few.

[13] But when the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ from ⟦thessalonica|Thessalonike|Thessalonica⟧ came to know that in ⟦berea|Beroia|Berea⟧ also the word of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ was proclaimed by ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, they came there also, shaking and troubling the crowds.

[14] And then immediately the brothers sent ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ out to go as far as to the sea; and both ⟦silas|Sila|Silas⟧ and ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧ stayed behind there.

[15] And those conducting ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ brought him as far as ⟦athens|Athenai|Athens⟧, and having received a commandment for ⟦silas|Sila|Silas⟧ and ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧, that they should come to him as quickly as possible, they went off.

[16] Now while ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ was waiting for them in ⟦athens|Athenai|Athens⟧, his spirit was being provoked within him as he beheld the city being full of idols.

[17] So he was discussing in the synagogue with the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ and with those who were revering, and in the marketplace every day with those who happened to be there.

[18] And some also of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers were conferring with him, and some were saying, "What might this seed-picker want to say?" and others, "He seems to be a proclaimer of strange divinities" — because he was preaching ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ and the resurrection.

[19] And having laid hold of him, they brought him to the ⟦areopagus|Areios Pagos|Areopagus⟧, saying, "Can we come to know what this new teaching is, which is being spoken by you?

[20] For you bring some strange things into our ears; we wish therefore to come to know what these things mean."

[21] Now all ⟦athenians|Athenaioi|Athenians⟧ and the strangers sojourning there had leisure for nothing other than to say something or to hear something newer.

[22] And ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, having stood in the midst of the ⟦areopagus|Areios Pagos|Areopagus⟧, said: "Men of ⟦athens|Athenai|Athens⟧, in all things I behold you as more divinity-fearing.

[23] For going through and looking closely at your objects of reverence, I found also an altar on which had been inscribed, '**To an unknown god**.' What therefore you, being ignorant, show godliness toward, this I proclaim to you.

[24] The ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ who made the world and all the things in it, this one, being Lord of heaven and earth, does not dwell in sanctuaries made with hands,

[25] nor is he attended by human hands, needing anything besides, himself giving to all life and breath and all things;

[26] and he made from one every nation of men to dwell upon all the face of the earth, having marked out prescribed times and the boundaries of their dwelling,

[27] to seek ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, if perhaps indeed they might feel after him and find him, and indeed he is not far from each one of us;

[28] for in him we live and move and are, as also some of the poets among you have said, '**For we are also his race.**'

[29] Being therefore the race of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, we ought not to suppose the divine to be like gold or silver or stone, an engraved work of the craft and reflection of man.

[30] So then ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, having overlooked the times of the ignorance, now charges men that all everywhere repent,

[31] because he set a day in which he is about to judge the inhabited earth in righteousness, in a man whom he marked out, having provided assurance to all, having raised him up from the dead."

[32] And having heard of a resurrection of the dead, some were mocking, but others said, "We will hear you about this yet again."

[33] Thus ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧ went out from their midst.

[34] But some men, having been joined to him, believed, among whom also were ⟦dionysius|Dionysios|Dionysius⟧ the ⟦areopagite|Areopagites|Areopagite⟧ and a woman, Damaris by name, and others with them.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v3, 7, 18) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v3) — Hebrew messianic title.
- **"Holy Ghost" / Spirit** — no explicit occurrence in this chapter; "his spirit" in v16 is Paul's.
- **"baptize"** — no direct occurrence in this chapter.
- **"church / synagogue"** — *synagōgē* retained as "synagogue"; no church in this chapter.
- **"Dionysios" / "*Dionysios*"** (v34) — Greek name transliterated more closely to original form.
- **"Damaris"** (v34) — Greek Δάμαρις *Damaris*; retained.
- **"TO THE UNKNOWN GOD" → "To an unknown god"** (v23) — the inscription is Greek, Ἀγνώστῳ θεῷ *Agnōstō theō*, without article: "to an unknown god"; "god" in lower case because it names a deity of the Athenians' worship, from which Paul's speech begins.
- **Greek poet quoted** (v28) — "For we are also his race" is Aratus, *Phaenomena* 5 (nearly the same in Cleanthes, *Hymn to Zeus*); the Greek marks only these words as a quotation ("as also some of the poets among you have said"). "In him we live and move and are" is often ascribed to Epimenides of Crete, but the Greek does not mark it as a quotation, and the verse no longer sets it out as one.
- **"the times of this ignorance" → "the times of the ignorance"** (v30) — Greek τῆς ἀγνοίας, with the article, as 3:17; it takes up "being ignorant" (ἀγνοοῦντες, v23) and "unknown" (ἀγνώστῳ).
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.
- **"had passed through" → "having journeyed through"** (v1) — Greek Διοδεύσαντες. "A synagogue of the *Yehudim*" is a noun in Greek, not "Jewish" (also v10).
- **"as his manner was" → "according to what was customary for Paul"; "three sabbath days reasoned with them" → "for three *Shabbats* he discussed with them"** (v2) — Greek κατὰ τὸ εἰωθός; ἐπὶ σάββατα τρία (no word "days"; the plural can also mean "weeks"); διελέξατο, as Mark 9:34, also v17 and 18:4, 19; 19:8–9.
- **"opening and alleging" → "opening up and setting before them"** (v3) — Greek διανοίγων, as 16:14; παρατιθέμενος, the verb of 16:34 ("set before"); "them" is supplied. "Christ must needs have suffered, and risen again" → "the *Mashiach* had to suffer and to rise": ἔδει παθεῖν καὶ ἀναστῆναι.
- **"this Jesus, whom I preach unto you, is Christ" → "this one is the *Mashiach*, the *Yeshua* whom I proclaim to you"** (v3) — Greek οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ χριστός, ὁ Ἰησοῦς ὃν ἐγὼ καταγγέλλω ὑμῖν (WH, NA28); Treg χριστὸς Ἰησοῦς, RP ὁ χριστὸς Ἰησοῦς. The Greek passes into direct speech without a word "saying". The words can also be taken "this *Yeshua*, whom I proclaim to you, is the *Mashiach*".
- **"believed, and consorted with" → "were persuaded and were allotted to"** (v4) — Greek ἐπείσθησαν; προσεκληρώθησαν, a passive of κλῆρος "lot": "were assigned as by lot to", or "cast in their lot with". "Devout Greeks" → "the revering *Yevanim*": σεβομένων, as 13:43; 16:14. "Chief women" → "the first women": γυναικῶν τῶν πρώτων, as 13:50; the words can also mean "wives of the first men".
- **"the Jews which believed not, moved with envy, took unto them certain lewd fellows of the baser sort" → "the *Yehudim*, having become jealous and having taken to themselves some evil men of the market people"** (v5) — Greek ζηλώσαντες (as 13:45 ζῆλος); "which believed not" is Byzantine; προσλαβόμενοι, as 18:26; τῶν ἀγοραίων ἄνδρας τινὰς πονηρούς: ἀγοραῖος is "of the marketplace" (ἀγορά, v17), of those who hang about there.
- **"gathered a company, and set all the city on an uproar, and assaulted the house" → "having made a crowd, were throwing the city into commotion; and having come upon the house"** (v5) — Greek ὀχλοποιήσαντες (ὄχλος "crowd"); ἐθορύβουν, imperfect, the verb of θόρυβος; ἐπιστάντες, as 4:1. "To the people" → "to the populace": δῆμον, as 12:22.
- **"they drew" → "they were dragging"; "the rulers of the city" → "the city rulers"; "that have turned the world upside down" → "who have unsettled the inhabited earth"** (v6) — Greek ἔσυρον, imperfect; πολιτάρχας, the title of the magistrates of Thessalonica; ἀναστατώσαντες, as Gal 5:12; οἰκουμένην. "Crying": βοῶντες.
- **"do contrary to" → "act contrary to"** (v7) — Greek πράσσουσι, as 3:17; ἀπέναντι. "Another king": βασιλέα ἕτερον. "Has received": ὑποδέδεκται, of receiving a guest, as Jas 2:25.
- **"they troubled the people" → "they troubled the crowd"** (v8) — Greek ἐτάραξαν, active: the accusers are the subject; τὸν ὄχλον.
- **"had taken security … they let them go" → "having taken the security … they released them"** (v9) — Greek λαβόντες τὸ ἱκανόν, a Latin legal phrase (*satis accipere*), "take bail"; no word "money"; ἀπέλυσαν.
- **"sent away … by night" → "through the night, sent out"; "went into" → "went away into"** (v10) — Greek διὰ νυκτὸς ἐξέπεμψαν; ἀπῄεσαν.
- **"These were more noble" kept; "received" → "accepted"; "readiness of mind" → "readiness"; "searched the scriptures daily" → "day by day examining the Scriptures"** (v11) — Greek οὗτοι, with no noun ("Jews" is not repeated); εὐγενέστεροι, "of better birth", so "more noble-minded"; ἐδέξαντο; προθυμίας; ἀνακρίνοντες; καθʼ ἡμέραν (Treg, NA28; the verse follows them: WH, RP τὸ καθʼ ἡμέραν; no difference in English). "Whether these things were so": εἰ ἔχοι ταῦτα οὕτως, an optative.
- **"honourable women which were Greeks" → "the Greek women of high standing"** (v12) — Greek τῶν Ἑλληνίδων γυναικῶν τῶν εὐσχημόνων, as 13:50; in Mark 15:43 the same word is rendered "honorable".
- **"had knowledge" → "came to know"; "preached" → "proclaimed"; "stirred up the people" → "shaking and troubling the crowds"** (v13) — Greek ἔγνωσαν; κατηγγέλη; σαλεύοντες καὶ ταράσσοντες τοὺς ὄχλους (WH, Treg, NA28; RP has only "shaking").
- **"to go as it were to the sea" → "to go as far as to the sea"; "abode there still" → "stayed behind there"** (v14) — Greek ἕως ἐπὶ τὴν θάλασσαν (WH, Treg, NA28; RP ὡς, "as it were"); ὑπέμειναν, the verb rendered "endure" elsewhere, here in its first sense "remain behind".
- **"they that conducted Paul" kept; "receiving a commandment … for to come to him with all speed" → "having received a commandment …, that they should come to him as quickly as possible"; "departed" → "went off"** (v15) — Greek καθιστάνοντες, the verb "appoint, set down", here of bringing a person to a place; ἐντολήν; ὡς τάχιστα; ἐξῄεσαν.
- **"was stirred in him, when he saw the city wholly given to idolatry" → "was being provoked within him as he beheld the city being full of idols"** (v16) — Greek παρωξύνετο, imperfect, as 1 Cor 13:5, and the verb of παροξυσμός (15:39); θεωροῦντος; κατείδωλον, an adjective, "full of idols".
- **"disputed he … with the devout persons … daily with them that met with him" → "he was discussing … with those who were revering … every day with those who happened to be there"** (v17) — Greek διελέγετο, imperfect; τοῖς σεβομένοις, with no object and no noun; κατὰ πᾶσαν ἡμέραν; τοὺς παρατυγχάνοντας.
- **"encountered him" → "were conferring with him"; "What will this babbler say?" → "What might this seed-picker want to say?"** (v18) — Greek συνέβαλλον, as 4:15; Τί ἂν θέλοι … λέγειν; σπερμολόγος, "seed-picker": a bird that picks up seeds, and so a man who picks up scraps of others' talk and passes them on.
- **"a setter forth of strange gods" → "a proclaimer of strange divinities"** (v18) — Greek Ξένων δαιμονίων καταγγελεύς. δαιμόνιον is the word rendered "demon" elsewhere; in the mouth of Athenians it is their ordinary word for a divine being, so "divinities" (the charge against Socrates used the same words). ξένος is "strange, foreign" (v20 ξενίζοντα, v21 ξένοι). "He preached unto them" → "he was preaching": εὐηγγελίζετο; "unto them" is Byzantine.
- **"they took him" → "having laid hold of him"; "May we know" → "Can we come to know"; "whereof thou speakest" → "which is being spoken by you"** (v19) — Greek ἐπιλαβόμενοι, as 16:19; Δυνάμεθα γνῶναι; ἡ ὑπὸ σοῦ λαλουμένη. "To the Areopagus": ἐπὶ τὸν Ἄρειον Πάγον, "the Hill of Ares", the hill and the council named after it.
- **"certain strange things to our ears … what these things mean" kept** (v20) — Greek ξενίζοντα … εἰς τὰς ἀκοὰς ἡμῶν, "into our hearings"; τίνα θέλει ταῦτα εἶναι, "what these things want to be", as 2:12. "We would know" → "we wish … to come to know": βουλόμεθα γνῶναι.
- **"strangers which were there spent their time in nothing else, but either to tell, or to hear some new thing" → "the strangers sojourning there had leisure for nothing other than to say something or to hear something newer"** (v21) — Greek οἱ ἐπιδημοῦντες ξένοι, as 2:10; ηὐκαίρουν, "had opportune time for"; καινότερον, a comparative.
- **"stood in the midst of Mars' hill" → "having stood in the midst of the Areopagus"; "Ye men of Athens" → "Men of Athens"** (v22) — Greek Σταθείς; Ἄνδρες Ἀθηναῖοι, "Men, Athenians", as 1:11; 2:22.
- **"in all things ye are too superstitious" → "in all things I behold you as more divinity-fearing"** (v22) — Greek κατὰ πάντα ὡς δεισιδαιμονεστέρους ὑμᾶς θεωρῶ. δεισιδαίμων is made of "fear" (δείδω) and δαίμων (the word of δαιμόνια, "divinities", v18); it can be meant as praise ("religious") or as blame ("superstitious"), and the Greek leaves that open. The form is a comparative with no second term: "more … (than others)", "rather …".
- **"beheld your devotions" → "looking closely at your objects of reverence"; "with this inscription" → "on which had been inscribed"** (v23) — Greek ἀναθεωρῶν; τὰ σεβάσματα, things revered (σέβομαι "revere"); ἐπεγέγραπτο.
- **"Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you" → "What therefore you, being ignorant, show godliness toward, this I proclaim to you"** (v23) — Greek ὃ … τοῦτο, neuter (WH, Treg, NA28); RP ὃν … τοῦτον, "whom … him". ἀγνοοῦντες, "not knowing", takes up ἀγνώστῳ "unknown"; εὐσεβεῖτε, the verb of εὐσέβεια "godliness", as 1 Tim 5:4; καταγγέλλω.
- **"God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord" → "The God who made the world and all the things in it, this one, being Lord"** (v24) — Greek οὗτος is kept. "Dwelleth not in temples made with hands" → "does not dwell in sanctuaries made with hands": χειροποιήτοις ναοῖς (ναός "sanctuary"; "temple" is ἱερόν); "made with hands" as 7:48.
- **"neither is worshipped with men's hands, as though he needed any thing" → "nor is he attended by human hands, needing anything besides"** (v25) — Greek θεραπεύεται, "is served, tended" (the verb "heal" elsewhere; θεράπων "attendant"); ἀνθρωπίνων (WH, Treg, NA28); προσδεόμενός τινος, "needing something in addition"; no "as though". "To all": πᾶσι, with no noun.
- **"hath made of one blood all nations of men" → "he made from one every nation of men"** (v26) — Greek ἐξ ἑνός (WH, Treg, NA28), "from one", with no noun; RP adds αἵματος, "blood". The words can be read "he made every nation … to dwell" or "he made [created] … every nation, to dwell".
- **"hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation" → "having marked out prescribed times and the boundaries of their dwelling"** (v26) — Greek ὁρίσας, the verb of v31 and 10:42; προστεταγμένους καιρούς; τὰς ὁροθεσίας τῆς κατοικίας (κατοικεῖν "dwell" in the same verse).
- **"That they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him" → "to seek God, if perhaps indeed they might feel after him"** (v27) — Greek ζητεῖν τὸν θεόν (WH, Treg, NA28), an infinitive beside "to dwell"; εἰ ἄρα γε ψηλαφήσειαν, the verb of 1 John 1:1 ("handled"), of feeling with the hands as in the dark. "Though he be not far" → "and indeed he is not far": καί γε οὐ μακρὰν … ὑπάρχοντα.
- **"have our being" → "are"; "certain also of your own poets" → "also some of the poets among you"; "For we are also his offspring" → "For we are also his race"** (v28) — Greek ἐσμέν; κινούμεθα can be middle ("move") or passive ("are moved"); τῶν καθʼ ὑμᾶς ποιητῶν; Τοῦ γὰρ καὶ γένος ἐσμέν: γένος as 7:13; 13:26, "race, descent, offspring".
- **"we ought not to think that the Godhead is like unto gold … graven by art and man's device" → "we ought not to suppose the divine to be like gold … an engraved work of the craft and reflection of man"** (v29) — Greek τὸ θεῖον, a neuter adjective, "the divine": not θεός "God", not θεότης "deity"; the neuter is kept. νομίζειν; χαράγματι, a thing engraved or stamped (the word "mark" in Rev 13:16); τέχνης, the noun of τεχνῖται "craftsmen" (19:24); ἐνθυμήσεως, as Heb 4:12.
- **"winked at" → "having overlooked"; "commandeth all men every where to repent" → "charges men that all everywhere repent"** (v30) — Greek ὑπεριδών; τὰ νῦν παραγγέλλει (Treg, NA28, RP; WH ἀπαγγέλλει, "reports") τοῖς ἀνθρώποις πάντας πανταχοῦ μετανοεῖν.
- **"hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world … by that man whom he hath ordained" → "he set a day in which he is about to judge the inhabited earth … in a man whom he marked out"** (v31) — Greek ἔστησεν; μέλλει κρίνειν; τὴν οἰκουμένην; ἐν ἀνδρί, "in a man", without article: ἐν can mean "by", and is kept as "in" beside "in righteousness"; ᾧ ὥρισεν.
- **"whereof he hath given assurance unto all men, in that he hath raised him" → "having provided assurance to all, having raised him up"** (v31) — Greek πίστιν παρασχὼν πᾶσιν ἀναστήσας αὐτόν: πίστις is the word "faith"; πίστιν παρέχειν is the set Greek phrase for giving a pledge or proof (a ground for faith), so the verse has "assurance". Word for word it is "having provided faith to all", which in English would say that he gave all men faith. παρέχω as 16:16. ἀναστήσας is ἀνίστημι "raise up" (ἐγείρω is "raise").
- **"the resurrection of the dead" → "a resurrection of the dead"; "mocked" → "were mocking"; "We will hear thee again of this matter" → "We will hear you about this yet again"** (v32) — Greek ἀνάστασιν νεκρῶν, without articles; ἐχλεύαζον, imperfect, as 2:13; περὶ τούτου καὶ πάλιν.
- **"So Paul departed from among them" → "Thus Paul went out from their midst"** (v33) — Greek οὕτως … ἐξῆλθεν ἐκ μέσου αὐτῶν.
- **"clave unto him" → "having been joined to him"** (v34) — Greek κολληθέντες, as 5:13; 9:26.

### Notes
- **v28** quotes Aratus, *Phaenomena* 5 (compare Cleanthes, *Hymn to Zeus* 4).
