# 2 Kings 6

[1] And the sons of the prophets said to Elisha, "Behold, now, the place where we dwell before you is too narrow for us.

[2] Let us go, please, as far as the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧, and let us take from there each man one beam, and let us make for ourselves a place there to dwell there." And he said, "Go."

[3] And the one said, "Be willing, please, and go with your servants." And he said, "I will go."

[4] And he went with them. And they came to the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧ and cut the trees.

[5] And it came to pass, as the one was felling the beam, that the iron fell into the water. And he cried out and said, "Alas, my lord! And it was asked for."

[6] And the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ said, "Where did it fall?" And he showed him the place. And he cut off a piece of wood and threw it there, and he made the iron float.

[7] And he said, "Lift it up for yourself." And he stretched out his hand and took it.

[8] And the king of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ was fighting against ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧. And he took counsel with his servants, saying, "At such and such a place shall be my camp."

[9] And the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ sent to the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, saying, "Take heed to yourself, not to pass this place, for there ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ is coming down."

[10] And the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ sent to the place that the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ had said to him; and he warned him, and he took heed there, not once and not twice.

[11] And the heart of the king of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ was storm-tossed over this thing, and he called to his servants and said to them, "Will you not tell me who of ours is for the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧?"

[12] And one of his servants said, "No, my lord the king; for Elisha the prophet who is in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ tells the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ the words that you speak in your bedroom."

[13] And he said, "Go and see where he is, and I will send and take him." And it was told him, saying, "Behold, he is in ⟦dothan|Dotan|Dothan⟧."

[14] And he sent there horses and chariots and a heavy force, and they came by night and encircled the city.

[15] And the minister of the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ rose early to arise, and he went out, and behold, a force was surrounding the city, and horse and chariots. And his young man said to him, "Alas, my lord! How shall we do?"

[16] And he said, "Do not fear, for more are those who are with us than those who are with them."

[17] And Elisha prayed and said, "⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, open, please, his eyes, and let him see." And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ opened the eyes of the young man, and he saw, and behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire around Elisha.

[18] And they came down to him, and Elisha prayed to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ and said, "Strike, please, this nation with blindness." And he struck them with blindness, according to the word of Elisha.

[19] And Elisha said to them, "This is not the way, and this is not the city. Go after me, and I will lead you to the man whom you seek." And he led them to ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧.

[20] And it came to pass, when they came to ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧, that Elisha said, "⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, open the eyes of these, and let them see." And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ opened their eyes, and they saw, and behold, they were in the midst of ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧.

[21] And the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ said to Elisha, when he saw them, "Shall I strike down? Shall I strike down, my father?"

[22] And he said, "You shall not strike down. Those whom you have taken captive with your sword and with your bow, are you striking them down? Put bread and water before them, and let them eat and drink and go to their lord."

[23] And he made for them a great feast, and they ate and drank, and he sent them away, and they went to their lord. And the raiding bands of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ came no more again into the land of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[24] And it came to pass after this that ⟦ben-hadad|Ben Hadad|Ben-hadad⟧ king of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ gathered all his camp, and he went up and besieged ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧.

[25] And there was a great famine in ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧, and behold, they were besieging it, until a donkey's head was at eighty of silver, and the fourth of the qab of dove's dung at five of silver.

[26] And it came to pass, as the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ was passing by on the wall, that a woman cried out to him, saying, "Save, my lord the king!"

[27] And he said, "Let ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ not save you! From where shall I save you? From the threshing floor or from the winepress?"

[28] And the king said to her, "What ails you?" And she said, "This woman said to me, 'Give your son, and let us eat him today, and my son we will eat tomorrow.'

[29] And we boiled my son and ate him. And I said to her on the next day, 'Give your son, and let us eat him.' And she hid her son."

[30] And it came to pass, when the king heard the words of the woman, that he tore his garments — and he was passing by on the wall — and the people saw, and behold, the sackcloth was on his flesh within.

[31] And he said, "Thus may ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ do to me, and thus may he add: the head of Elisha the son of ⟦shaphat|Shafat|Shaphat⟧ shall not stand on him today."

[32] And Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him. And he sent a man from before him. Before the messenger came to him, he himself said to the elders, "Have you seen that this son of the murderer has sent to remove my head? See, when the messenger comes, shut the door and press him back with the door. Is not the sound of the feet of his lord behind him?"

[33] He was still speaking with them, and behold, the messenger was coming down to him. And he said, "Behold, this evil is from ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧. Why should I wait for ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ any longer?"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; "the man of Elohim" has the article (אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים).
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"Syria / Syrians" → "Aram"** (v8, 9, 11, 23, 24) — Hebrew אֲרָם, the one name for the land and for the people; in v9 it stands with a plural participle ("Aram are coming down").
- **"the place where we dwell with thee is too strait for us" → "the place where we dwell before you is too narrow for us"** (v1) — Hebrew יֹשְׁבִים שָׁם לְפָנֶיךָ: יָשַׁב is "dwell" and "sit"; "sit before you" (as pupils, 4:38) is as possible. צַר is "narrow". הִנֵּה־נָא, "behold, now".
- **"take thence every man a beam" → "each man one beam"** (v2) — Hebrew קוֹרָה אֶחָת. **"where we may dwell" → "to dwell there"** — the Hebrew says "there" three times in the verse.
- **"And one said, Be content, I pray thee" → "And the one said, Be willing, please"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew הָאֶחָד, with the article: one of them. הוֹאֶל, "be willing, consent".
- **"they cut down wood" → "cut the trees"** (v4) — Hebrew גָּזַר, "cut"; הָעֵצִים.
- **"the axe head fell into the water" → "the iron fell into the water"** (v5, 6) — Hebrew הַבַּרְזֶל, "the iron": the axe is not named. The word stands with אֶת, the mark of the object, though it is the subject. **"felling"** — Hebrew מַפִּיל, "making fall".
- **"Alas, master! for it was borrowed" → "Alas, my lord! And it was asked for"** (v5) — Hebrew שָׁאוּל, "asked": asked from someone, so borrowed, or begged. The verb says only "ask".
- **"he cut down a stick, and cast it in thither; and the iron did swim" → "he cut off a piece of wood and threw it there, and he made the iron float"** (v6) — Hebrew עֵץ, "wood, tree"; וַיָּצֶף is the causing form, "made float".
- **"Take it up to thee" → "Lift it up for yourself"** (v7) — Hebrew הָרֶם לָךְ. **"put out his hand" → "stretched out his hand"**.
- **"warred against Israel" → "was fighting against Israel"** (v8) — Hebrew הָיָה נִלְחָם. **"In such and such a place shall be my camp"** — Hebrew תַּחֲנֹתִי, found only here: its form is unusual and its meaning uncertain ("my camping"; others correct it to "you shall hide").
- **"Beware that thou pass not such a place; for thither the Syrians are come down" → "Take heed to yourself, not to pass this place, for there Aram is coming down"** (v9) — Hebrew הִשָּׁמֶר מֵעֲבֹר, "keep yourself from passing"; נְחִתִּים is found only here and its meaning is uncertain (taken from נָחַת "go down"; others read "are hiding").
- **"sent to the place which the man of God told him and warned him of, and saved himself there" → "sent to the place that the man of Elohim had said to him; and he warned him, and he took heed there"** (v10) — the verse follows the reading tradition (qere) וְהִזְהִירוֹ, "and he warned him"; the written text (ketiv) is והזהירה, "and he warned it". וְנִשְׁמַר is the verb of v9, "take heed": "saved himself" is not written.
- **"was sore troubled for this thing" → "was storm-tossed over this thing"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיִּסָּעֵר, from סַעַר "storm". **"which of us is for the king of Israel" → "who of ours"** — Hebrew מִי מִשֶּׁלָּנוּ אֶל־מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל, "who of those that are ours [is] to the king of Israel".
- **"None, my lord, O king: but Elisha" → "No, my lord the king; for Elisha"** (v12) — Hebrew לוֹא … כִּי. **"bedchamber" → "bedroom"** — Hebrew חֲדַר מִשְׁכָּבֶךָ, "the chamber of your lying down" (Exod 8:3).
- **"that I may send and fetch him" → "and I will send and take him"** (v13). "He is" is supplied: the Hebrew has "Behold, in Dothan".
- **"a great host: and they came by night, and compassed the city about" → "a heavy force … and encircled the city"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew חַיִל כָּבֵד, "a heavy force" (many); הִקִּיף עַל in v14, סָבַב in v15: two verbs, "encircle" and "surround".
- **"the servant of the man of God was risen early, and gone forth" → "the minister of the man of Elohim rose early to arise, and he went out"** (v15) — Hebrew מְשָׁרֵת, one who ministers to him (as Joshua to Moses); in the same verse he is נַעֲרוֹ, "his boy / young man". **"how shall we do?"** — Hebrew אֵיכָה נַעֲשֶׂה.
- **"they that be with us are more than they that be with them" → "more are those who are with us than those who are with them"** (v16) — the Hebrew word order.
- **"LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see" → "Yahweh, open, please, his eyes, and let him see"** (v17, 20) — the Hebrew has no "O"; וְיִרְאֶה is a wish form. **"horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha"** — "of fire" may belong to the chariots only or to both.
- **"Smite this people, I pray thee, with blindness" → "Strike, please, this nation with blindness"** (v18) — Hebrew גּוֹי, "nation". סַנְוֵרִים stands only here and in Gen 19:11; it is not the usual word for blindness (עִוָּרוֹן), and its exact sense is uncertain: a sudden blinding or dazzling. The men still walk and follow, and what they need is to have their eyes "opened" (v20). The Hebrew has the article, "with the blindness".
- **"follow me, and I will bring you … But he led them" → "Go after me, and I will lead you … And he led them"** (v19) — Hebrew לְכוּ אַחֲרַי; הוֹלִיךְ, the causing form of "go": "make go". The Hebrew has no "but".
- **"My father, shall I smite them? shall I smite them?" → "Shall I strike down? Shall I strike down, my father?"** (v21, 22) — Hebrew הַאַכֶּה אַכֶּה אָבִי: the verb is הִכָּה, the "strike" of v18, here of killing; no object is written.
- **"wouldest thou smite those whom thou hast taken captive" → "Those whom you have taken captive with your sword and with your bow, are you striking them down?"** (v22) — Hebrew הַאֲשֶׁר שָׁבִיתָ … אַתָּה מַכֶּה, a question with a participle. It can mean "do you strike down even those you have taken yourself?" (so much less these), or "are these men ones you took, that you should strike them?". **"their master" → "their lord"** (v22, 23, 32) — Hebrew אֲדֹנֵיהֶם, אֲדֹנָיו.
- **"he prepared great provision for them" → "he made for them a great feast"** (v23) — Hebrew וַיִּכְרֶה לָהֶם כֵּרָה גְדוֹלָה: verb and noun of one root, found only here in this sense; "feast" is the commonly accepted meaning.
- **"the bands of Syria came no more" → "the raiding bands of Aram came no more again"** (v23) — Hebrew גְּדוּדֵי אֲרָם; וְלֹא־יָסְפוּ עוֹד … לָבוֹא, "did not add any more to come".
- **"gathered all his host" → "gathered all his camp"** (v24) — Hebrew מַחֲנֵהוּ.
- **"an ass's head was sold for fourscore pieces of silver, and the fourth part of a cab of dove's dung for five pieces of silver" → "a donkey's head was at eighty of silver, and the fourth of the qab of dove's dung at five of silver"** (v25) — the Hebrew has no "sold" and names no unit: "at eighty of silver" (shekels are meant). The qab is a dry measure, somewhat more than a litre. "Dove's dung" follows the reading tradition (qere) דִּבְיוֹנִים; the written text (ketiv) has חרי יונים, the coarse word, "doves' shit". Whether real dung is meant or a name for some poor food is not known.
- **"Help, my lord, O king" → "Save, my lord the king!"** (v26, 27) — Hebrew הוֹשִׁיעָה, the verb "save".
- **"If the LORD do not help thee, whence shall I help thee?" → "Let Yahweh not save you! From where shall I save you?"** (v27) — Hebrew אַל־יוֹשִׁעֵךְ יְהוָה: אַל with a wish form is "let … not". It is commonly taken as "If Yahweh does not save you …"; it can also be read "No! Let Yahweh save you". The Hebrew has no "if". **"barnfloor" → "threshing floor"**.
- **"What aileth thee?"** (v28) — Hebrew מַה־לָּךְ, "what is to you?".
- **"So we boiled my son … and she hath hid her son" → "And we boiled … And she hid her son"** (v29) — **"on the next day"** — Hebrew בַּיּוֹם הָאַחֵר, "on the other day".
- **"he rent his clothes; and he passed by upon the wall, and the people looked, and, behold, he had sackcloth within upon his flesh" → "he tore his garments — and he was passing by on the wall — and the people saw, and behold, the sackcloth was on his flesh within"** (v30) — Hebrew מִבָּיִת, "on the inside": under his garments.
- **"God do so and more also to me, if the head of Elisha … shall stand on him this day" → "Thus may Elohim do to me, and thus may he add: the head of Elisha … shall not stand on him today"** (v31) — the oath form: literally "if the head of Elisha … stands on him today", which means that it shall not.
- **"But Elisha sat in his house … and the king sent a man from before him" → "And Elisha was sitting in his house … And he sent a man from before him"** (v32) — the Hebrew does not name the one who sends; "the king" was supplied. In v33 also the speaker is not named.
- **"See ye how this son of a murderer hath sent to take away mine head? … hold him fast at the door" → "Have you seen that this son of the murderer has sent to remove my head? … press him back with the door"** (v32) — Hebrew בֶּן־הַמְרַצֵּחַ, with the article; "son of the murderer" may also mean simply "this murderer". לָחַץ is "press, squeeze": push him back with the door.
- **"the messenger came down unto him: and he said, Behold, this evil is of the LORD; what should I wait for the LORD any longer?" → "the messenger was coming down to him. And he said, Behold, this evil is from Yahweh. Why should I wait for Yahweh any longer?"** (v33) — Hebrew מָה־אוֹחִיל, "what / why should I wait". The Hebrew has "the messenger" (הַמַּלְאָךְ); the words that follow fit the king (7:17 says the king came down), and "the king" (הַמֶּלֶךְ) is often read here: the verse keeps the Hebrew.

### Notes
- **Floating iron** — smallest of Elisha's signs, concerned with a poor man's loss (a tool he had asked for).
- **Dothan** — north of Samaria.
- **"More with us than with them"** — the unseen host. Opened eyes see what was there already.
- **Treatment of captives** — Elisha forbids killing them. They are fed and sent away, and the raids stop (v23).
- **Siege of Samaria** — the king of Israel is not named in the chapter. The famine brings what Lev 26:29 and Deut 28:53–57 threaten.
- **King's sackcloth** — worn under his garments; seen only when he tears them.
- **"Son of the murderer"** — the king is not named; if he is Jehoram, the murderer is Ahab (1 Kgs 21:19).
- **End of the chapter** — the scene goes on in 7:1.
