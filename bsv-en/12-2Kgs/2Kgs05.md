# 2 Kings 5

[1] And Naaman, the commander of the host of the king of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧, was a great man before his lord and one whose face was lifted up, for by him ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ had given salvation to ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧; and the man was a mighty one of ability, a leper.

[2] And ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ had gone out in raiding bands, and they took captive from the land of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ a small girl, and she was before the wife of Naaman.

[3] And she said to her mistress, "Would that my lord were before the prophet who is in ⟦samaria|Shomron|Samaria⟧! Then he would gather him in from his leprosy."

[4] And he went in and told his lord, saying, "Thus and thus has the girl spoken who is from the land of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧."

[5] And the king of ⟦aram|Aram|Syria⟧ said, "Go, come, and let me send a letter to the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧." And he went and took in his hand ten talents of silver and six thousand of gold and ten changes of garments.

[6] And he brought the letter to the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, saying, "And now, when this letter comes to you, behold, I have sent to you Naaman my servant, and you shall gather him in from his leprosy."

[7] And it came to pass, when the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ read the letter, that he tore his garments and said, "Am I ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, to put to death and to make alive, that this one is sending to me to gather in a man from his leprosy? For only know, please, and see that he is seeking an occasion against me."

[8] And it came to pass, when Elisha the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ heard that the king of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ had torn his garments, that he sent to the king, saying, "Why have you torn your garments? Let him come, please, to me, and let him know that there is a prophet in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧."

[9] And Naaman came with his horses and with his chariots and stood at the entrance of the house of Elisha.

[10] And Elisha sent to him a messenger, saying, "Go, and you shall wash seven times in the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧, and let your flesh return to you, and be clean."

[11] And Naaman was wrathful and went, and he said, "Behold, I said, 'To me he will surely come out, and he will stand and call on the name of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ his ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and wave his hand toward the place and gather in the leper.'

[12] Are not ⟦abana|Avanah|Abana⟧ and ⟦pharpar|Parpar|Pharpar⟧, the rivers of ⟦damascus|Dammeseq|Damascus⟧, better than all the waters of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧? Shall I not wash in them and be clean?" And he turned and went in fury.

[13] And his servants drew near and spoke to him and said, "My father, had the prophet spoken a great thing to you, would you not do it? And how much more when he has said to you, 'Wash and be clean'!"

[14] And he went down and dipped in the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧ seven times, according to the word of the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and his flesh returned like the flesh of a small boy, and he was clean.

[15] And he returned to the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, he and all his camp, and he came and stood before him and said, "Behold, please, I know that there is no ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in all the earth except in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧. And now, take, please, a blessing from your servant."

[16] And he said, "As ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ lives, before whom I stand, I will not take." And he pressed him to take, and he refused.

[17] And Naaman said, "And if not, let there be given, please, to your servant a burden of ground for a pair of mules; for your servant will no more offer burnt offering and sacrifice to other gods, except to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[18] For this thing may ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ pardon your servant: when my lord comes into the house of Rimmon to bow down there, and he is leaning on my hand, and I bow down in the house of Rimmon — at my bowing down in the house of Rimmon, may ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ pardon your servant in this thing."

[19] And he said to him, "Go in ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧." And he went from him a stretch of land.

[20] And ⟦gehazi|Gechazi|Gehazi⟧, the young man of Elisha the man of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, said, "Behold, my lord has spared this Naaman the ⟦aramean|Arammi|Syrian⟧, in not taking from his hand what he brought. As ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ lives, I will surely run after him and take something from him."

[21] And ⟦gehazi|Gechazi|Gehazi⟧ pursued after Naaman, and Naaman saw one running after him, and he alighted from upon the chariot to meet him and said, "Is it ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧?"

[22] And he said, "⟦shalom|Shalom|Peace⟧. My lord has sent me, saying, 'Behold, just now there have come to me two young men from the hill country of ⟦ephraim|Efrayim|Ephraim⟧, of the sons of the prophets. Give them, please, a talent of silver and two changes of garments.'"

[23] And Naaman said, "Be willing, take two talents." And he pressed upon him, and he bound two talents of silver in two bags, and two changes of garments, and gave them to two of his young men, and they carried them before him.

[24] And he came to the hill and took them from their hand and deposited them in the house, and he sent the men away, and they went.

[25] And he came in and stood by his lord, and Elisha said to him, "From where, ⟦gehazi|Gechazi|Gehazi⟧?" And he said, "Your servant did not go here or there."

[26] And he said to him, "Did not my heart go, when a man turned from upon his chariot to meet you? Is it a time to take silver and to take garments and olive trees and vineyards and flock and herd and slaves and maidservants?

[27] And the leprosy of Naaman shall cling to you and to your seed forever." And he went out from before him leprous, like snow.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"Syria / Syrians / Syrian" → "Aram / Aramean"** (v1, 2, 5, 20) — Hebrew אֲרָם, the people named as one, also in v2 ("And Aram had gone out … and they took captive"); הָאֲרַמִּי "the Aramean" in v20.
- **"captain of the host of the king of Syria, was a great man with his master, and honourable, because by him the LORD had given deliverance unto Syria: he was also a mighty man in valour, but he was a leper" → "the commander of the host of the king of Aram, was a great man before his lord and one whose face was lifted up, for by him Yahweh had given salvation to Aram; and the man was a mighty one of ability, a leper"** (v1) — Hebrew שַׂר צְבָא; לִפְנֵי אֲדֹנָיו "before his lord"; נְשֻׂא פָנִים, literally "lifted up of face": held in regard; תְּשׁוּעָה "salvation"; גִּבּוֹר חַיִל. "A leper" follows with no "but".
- **"leper … leprosy"** (v1, 3, 6, 7, 11, 27) — Hebrew מְצֹרָע and צָרַעַת, the words of Lev 13–14: a scaly outbreak on the skin that makes unclean. It is not the leprosy of modern medicine; no diagnosis is put in the verse.
- **"had brought away captive out of the land of Israel a little maid; and she waited on Naaman's wife" → "they took captive from the land of Israel a small girl, and she was before the wife of Naaman"** (v2) — Hebrew גְּדוּדִים "raiding bands"; נַעֲרָה קְטַנָּה "a small girl"; וַתְּהִי לִפְנֵי "and she was before": in her service.
- **"Would God my lord were with the prophet … for he would recover him of his leprosy" → "Would that my lord were before the prophet … Then he would gather him in from his leprosy"** (v3, 6, 7, 11) — Hebrew אַחֲלֵי, a wish, with no "God". אָסַף is "gather": to gather a leper in is to take him back among men, as Miriam was "gathered in" (Num 12:14, 15), and so to free him of his leprosy. The verse keeps the word.
- **"And one went in, and told his lord" → "And he went in and told his lord"** (v4) — the Hebrew names no subject: Naaman to the king, or one of the house to Naaman.
- **"Go to, go, and I will send a letter … six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of raiment" → "Go, come, and let me send a letter … six thousand of gold and ten changes of garments"** (v5) — Hebrew סֵפֶר "book", any written document, here a letter; no unit is named with the gold.
- **"that thou mayest recover him" → "and you shall gather him in"** (v6) — Hebrew וַאֲסַפְתּוֹ. The letter begins with וְעַתָּה "And now": what stood before it is not given.
- **"he rent his clothes … Am I God, to kill and to make alive … wherefore consider, I pray you, and see how he seeketh a quarrel against me" → "he tore his garments … Am I *Elohim*, to put to death and to make alive … For only know, please, and see that he is seeking an occasion against me"** (v7) — Hebrew הֵמִית "put to death"; דְּעוּ "know"; מִתְאַנֶּה, of the word "occasion" (Judg 14:4): he is looking for a pretext.
- **"let him come now to me, and he shall know" → "Let him come, please, to me, and let him know"** (v8) — Hebrew נָא "please"; וְיֵדַע.
- **"with his horses and with his chariot, and stood at the door of the house of Elisha" → "with his horses and with his chariots and stood at the entrance of the house of Elisha"** (v9) — the written text has "his horse", the reading tradition "his horses"; רֶכֶב is a collective; פֶּתַח "entrance".
- **"Go and wash in Jordan seven times, and thy flesh shall come again to thee, and thou shalt be clean" → "Go, and you shall wash seven times in the Jordan, and let your flesh return to you, and be clean"** (v10) — Hebrew שׁוּב "return"; וּטְהָר is a command, "and be clean".
- **"Naaman was wroth, and went away, and said, Behold, I thought, He will surely come out to me … and strike his hand over the place, and recover the leper" → "Naaman was wrathful and went, and he said, Behold, I said, To me he will surely come out … and wave his hand toward the place and gather in the leper"** (v11) — Hebrew אָמַרְתִּי "I said"; אֵלַי "to me" may go with "I said" ("I said to myself") or with "he will come out"; יֵצֵא יָצוֹא, literally "coming out he will come out"; הֵנִיף "wave"; הַמְּצֹרָע "the leper".
- **"Abana"** (v12) — the written text has אֲבָנָה "Avanah"; the reading tradition has אֲמָנָה "Amanah". The verse keeps the written name. **"may I not wash in them … So he turned and went away in a rage" → "Shall I not wash in them … And he turned and went in fury"** — Hebrew חֵמָה "fury", literally "heat".
- **"if the prophet had bid thee do some great thing, wouldest thou not have done it?" → "had the prophet spoken a great thing to you, would you not do it?"** (v13) — the Hebrew has no "if": literally "a great thing the prophet has spoken to you — would you not do it?"; the sense is a condition.
- **"dipped himself seven times in Jordan … his flesh came again like unto the flesh of a little child" → "dipped in the Jordan seven times … his flesh returned like the flesh of a small boy"** (v14) — Hebrew טָבַל "dip"; נַעַר קָטֹן, the words of 2 Kgs 2:23.
- **"he and all his company … now I know that there is no God in all the earth, but in Israel: now therefore, I pray thee, take a blessing of thy servant" → "he and all his camp … Behold, please, I know that there is no *Elohim* in all the earth except in Israel. And now, take, please, a blessing from your servant"** (v15) — Hebrew מַחֲנֶה "camp"; בְּרָכָה "blessing": a gift (Gen 33:11).
- **"I will receive none. And he urged him to take it" → "I will not take. And he pressed him to take"** (v16) — the oath is אִם־אֶקָּח, literally "if I take"; the "if" of an oath means a sworn "not". "Before whom I stand" is a perfect, as 1 Kgs 17:1. פָּצַר בְּ "press".
- **"Shall there not then, I pray thee, be given to thy servant two mules' burden of earth? for thy servant will henceforth offer neither burnt offering nor sacrifice unto other gods, but unto the LORD" → "And if not, let there be given, please, to your servant a burden of ground for a pair of mules; for your servant will no more offer burnt offering and sacrifice to other gods, except to Yahweh"** (v17) — Hebrew וָלֹא "and (if) not"; מַשָּׂא צֶמֶד־פְּרָדִים אֲדָמָה, literally "a burden of a pair of mules, ground"; אֲדָמָה "ground"; the verb with the offerings is עָשָׂה "do, make".
- **"In this thing the LORD pardon thy servant, that when my master goeth into the house of Rimmon to worship there … and I bow myself in the house of Rimmon" → "For this thing may Yahweh pardon your servant: when my lord comes into the house of Rimmon to bow down there … and I bow down in the house of Rimmon"** (v18) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה "bow down", three times, of the king and of Naaman alike; "worship" is not a second word. In the last clause the written text has נָא "please", which the reading tradition does not read.
- **"Go in peace. So he departed from him a little way" → "Go in shalom. And he went from him a stretch of land"** (v19) — Hebrew לֵךְ לְשָׁלוֹם, literally "go to peace"; כִּבְרַת־אָרֶץ, a distance of unknown length (Gen 35:16).
- **"the servant of Elisha … my master hath spared Naaman this Syrian, in not receiving at his hands … I will run after him, and take somewhat of him" → "the young man of Elisha … my lord has spared this Naaman the Aramean, in not taking from his hand … I will surely run after him and take something from him"** (v20) — Hebrew נַעַר; חָשַׂךְ "withhold, hold back": he has held him back from giving; the oath כִּי־אִם־רַצְתִּי is a sworn "surely".
- **"So Gehazi followed after Naaman … he lighted down from the chariot to meet him, and said, Is all well?" → "And Gehazi pursued after Naaman … he alighted from upon the chariot to meet him and said, Is it shalom?"** (v21, 22) — Hebrew רָדַף "pursue"; וַיִּפֹּל, literally "and he fell", as Gen 24:64; הֲשָׁלוֹם and the answer שָׁלוֹם, the one word "peace".
- **"Be content, take two talents. And he urged him" → "Be willing, take two talents. And he pressed upon him"** (v23) — Hebrew הוֹאֵל; וַיִּפְרָץ־בּוֹ, literally "and he broke out upon him", another verb than "pressed" in v16. חֲרִטִים "bags".
- **"when he came to the tower, he took them from their hand, and bestowed them in the house: and he let the men go" → "he came to the hill and took them from their hand and deposited them in the house, and he sent the men away"** (v24) — Hebrew הָעֹפֶל, literally "the swelling": a hill or a fortified height; וַיִּפְקֹד, the verb "visit, appoint", here "put in keeping"; שִׁלַּח "send away".
- **"Whence comest thou, Gehazi? … Thy servant went no whither" → "From where, Gehazi? … Your servant did not go here or there"** (v25) — the Hebrew has no verb in the question; אָנֶה וָאָנָה "whither and whither". "From where": two spellings in the written text and the reading tradition.
- **"Went not mine heart with thee, when the man turned again from his chariot to meet thee? Is it a time to receive money … and oliveyards, and vineyards, and sheep, and oxen, and menservants, and maidservants?" → "Did not my heart go, when a man turned from upon his chariot to meet you? Is it a time to take silver … and olive trees and vineyards and flock and herd and slaves and maidservants?"** (v26) — the Hebrew is לֹא־לִבִּי הָלַךְ "not my heart went", with no mark of a question and no "with you"; it is commonly heard as a question. כֶּסֶף "silver"; זֵיתִים "olive trees"; צֹאן וּבָקָר "flock and herd"; עֲבָדִים "slaves".
- **"The leprosy therefore of Naaman shall cleave unto thee, and unto thy seed for ever. And he went out from his presence a leper as white as snow" → "And the leprosy of Naaman shall cling to you and to your seed forever. And he went out from before him leprous, like snow"** (v27) — Hebrew וְ "and", not "therefore"; דָּבַק "cling"; זֶרַע "seed"; מְצֹרָע כַּשָּׁלֶג "leprous, like snow", as Num 12:10; "white" is not written.

### Notes
- **Naaman** — Aramean commander. Aram is Israel's chronic enemy, yet Yahweh gives salvation even to Aram by him (v1).
- **Small Israelite girl** — a captive girl points her master to the prophet. Small witness with massive consequence.
- **Rimmon** — Aramean storm-god (Hadad-Ramman); parallels Baal-Hadad.
- **Seven-fold washing** — ceremonial number; not hygiene but obedience-test. Naaman's pride almost derails him.
- **Naaman's confession** — no *Elohim* in all the earth except in Israel (v15); offerings to Yahweh alone (v17). Jesus cites Naaman in Luke 4:27.
- **A burden of ground for a pair of mules** — wants Israelite soil, it seems for an altar in Damascus — a physical anchor of Yahweh-worship in a foreign land.
- **Gehazi's fall** — private greed, public lie. Naaman's leprosy clings to him as exact reversal: the foreigner becomes clean, the young man of the prophet becomes leprous.
- **"Like snow"** — the look of the skin (Exod 4:6; Num 12:10); the Hebrew does not say "white".
