# Matthew 6

[1] "But take heed not to do your righteousness before men, in order to be beheld by them; otherwise you have no wage with your Father who is in the heavens.

[2] "When therefore you do alms, do not sound the trumpet before you, just as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, so that they may be glorified by men. Amen, I say to you, they have their wage in full.

[3] But you, when doing alms, let not your left hand know what your right hand is doing,

[4] so that your alms may be in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will repay you.

[5] "And when you pray, you shall not be as the hypocrites, because they have affection for praying standing in the synagogues and at the corners of the broad streets, so that they may appear to men. Amen, I say to you, they have their wage in full.

[6] But you, when you pray, enter into your inner room and, having shut your door, pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will repay you.

[7] "And when praying, do not babble just as those of the nations, for they think that in their much speaking they will be heard.

[8] Therefore do not be made like them, for your Father knows what things you have need of before you ask him.

[9] "You, therefore, pray thus:

"Our Father who is in the heavens,
let your name be sanctified,

[10] let your kingdom come,
let your will come to pass,
as in heaven, also upon earth.

[11] Our daily bread give us today,

[12] and forgive us our debts,
as we also have forgiven our debtors;

[13] and do not bring us into trial,
but rescue us from the evil one."

[14] For if you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you;

[15] but if you do not forgive men, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.

[16] "And when you fast, do not become as the hypocrites, sullen, for they disfigure their faces so that they may appear to men as fasting. Amen, I say to you, they have their wage in full.

[17] But you, when fasting, anoint your head and wash your face,

[18] so that you may not appear to men as fasting, but to your Father who is in what is hidden; and your Father who sees in what is hidden will repay you.

[19] "Do not store up for yourselves treasures upon the earth, where moth and eating destroy, and where thieves dig through and steal;

[20] but store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor eating destroys, and where thieves do not dig through nor steal;

[21] for where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.

[22] "The lamp of the body is the eye. If therefore your eye is single, your whole body will be bright;

[23] but if your eye is evil, your whole body will be dark. If therefore the light that is in you is darkness, how great the darkness!

[24] "No one can be a slave to two lords; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will hold to one and despise the other. You cannot be slaves to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and to mammon.

[25] "For this reason I say to you: do not be anxious about your soul, what you shall eat or what you shall drink, nor about your body, what you shall put on. Is not the soul more than food, and the body than clothing?

[26] Look at the birds of the heaven, that they do not sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not worth more than they?

[27] And which of you by being anxious can add one cubit to his span of life?

[28] And about clothing, why are you anxious? Observe the lilies of the field, how they grow; they do not labor nor spin;

[29] but I say to you that not even ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧ in all his glory clothed himself like one of these.

[30] But if ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ so arrays the grass of the field, which is today and tomorrow is cast into the oven, not much more you, you of little faith?

[31] Therefore do not be anxious, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or, 'What shall we drink?' or, 'With what shall we clothe ourselves?'

[32] For all these things the nations seek after; for your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things.

[33] **But seek first the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.**

[34] Therefore do not be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious about itself. Sufficient for the day is its trouble."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv24, 30, 33) — Greek θεός *theos*; divine name preserved in Hebrew form.
- **"Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them" → "But take heed not to do your righteousness before men, in order to be beheld by them"** (v1) — Greek δικαιοσύνην "righteousness" (WH Treg NA28; RP ἐλεημοσύνην "alms"); θεαθῆναι, the verb "behold". δέ "but" is printed (WH NA28; not in Treg, RP).
- **"reward" → "wage"** (vv1, 2, 5, 16) — Greek μισθός, pay for work done. ἀπέχουσιν τὸν μισθόν (vv2, 5, 16) is the wording of a receipt: "they have their wage in full"; nothing more is owed.
- **"of your Father which is in heaven" → "with your Father who is in the heavens"** (v1; also v9) — Greek παρὰ τῷ πατρί, "with, in the keeping of"; ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς is plural. The singular stands in v10, 20 ("in heaven") and v26 ("the birds of the heaven").
- **"doest thine alms" → "do alms"** (vv2–4) — Greek ποιεῖν ἐλεημοσύνην, literally "do an act of mercy": a gift to the poor. "Alms" kept.
- **"that they may have glory of men" → "so that they may be glorified by men"** (v2) — Greek δοξασθῶσιν, the passive of "glorify".
- **"Verily I say unto you" → "Amen, I say to you"** (vv2, 5, 16) — Greek ἀμήν, the Hebrew word left untranslated, as the Greek leaves it.
- **"reward thee openly" → "repay you"** (vv4, 6, 18) — Greek ἀποδώσει σοι, "give back, repay"; "openly" (ἐν τῷ φανερῷ) stands in RP only.
- **"thou shalt not be" → "you shall not be"** (v5) — Greek προσεύχησθε, οὐκ ἔσεσθε, plural (WH Treg NA28; RP singular); a future used as a command.
- **"they love to pray" → "they have affection for praying"** (v5) — Greek φιλοῦσιν … προσεύχεσθαι, the verb φιλέω "have affection for" with an infinitive; not ἀγαπάω "love".
- **"in the corners of the streets" → "at the corners of the broad streets"** (v5) — Greek πλατειῶν, the wide main streets; in v2 ῥύμαις, the ordinary (narrower) streets.
- **"that they may be seen of men" → "so that they may appear to men"** (vv5, 16, 18) — Greek φανῶσιν, "appear, show themselves"; a different verb from "beheld" in v1.
- **"closet" → "inner room"** (v6) — Greek ταμεῖον *tameion*, a storeroom, the innermost room of a house.
- **"use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do" → "do not babble just as those of the nations"** (v7) — Greek βατταλογήσητε, a rare word of uncertain origin: "babble, stammer on"; "vain repetitions" is an interpretation. ἐθνικοί, "those who belong to the nations" (the non-Jewish peoples); in v32 τὰ ἔθνη, "the nations".
- **"for their much speaking" → "in their much speaking"** (v7) — Greek ἐν τῇ πολυλογίᾳ.
- **"Be not ye therefore like unto them" → "Therefore do not be made like them"** (v8) — Greek ὁμοιωθῆτε, passive. "your Father" (Treg NA28 RP; WH "God your Father").
- **"Hallowed be thy name" → "let your name be sanctified"** (v9) — Greek ἁγιασθήτω, the verb ἁγιάζω "sanctify, make or treat as holy"; the three requests of v9–10 are third-person imperatives of the same form ("let … be sanctified", "let … come", "let … come to pass").
- **"Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven" → "let your will come to pass, as in heaven, also upon earth"** (v10) — Greek γενηθήτω, "come to pass, come to be"; ὡς ἐν οὐρανῷ καὶ ἐπὶ γῆς, in this order.
- **"daily" kept** (v11) — Greek ἐπιούσιος is found nowhere before the gospels (here and Luke 11:3) and its meaning is not certain. Three explanations: (1) from ἡ ἐπιοῦσα (ἡμέρα), "for the coming day" — the day now beginning, or tomorrow; (2) from ἐπί + οὐσία, "needful for existence"; (3) "for the present day". The old Latin has *cotidianum* "daily", Jerome in Matthew *supersubstantialem*. "Daily" is the traditional word and covers (1) and (3); it is not certain. Greek order: "Our bread, the ἐπιούσιος one, give us today."
- **"debts" and "debtors"** (v12) — Greek ὀφειλήματα, ὀφειλέταις: what is owed, those who owe. ἀφήκαμεν "we have forgiven" (aorist: WH Treg NA28); RP ἀφίεμεν "we forgive".
- **"lead us not into temptation" → "do not bring us into trial"** (v13) — Greek εἰσενέγκῃς, "carry, bring in"; πειρασμός is one word for "testing, trial" and "temptation" (the verb πειράζω, "tempt", 4:1, 3).
- **"deliver us from evil" → "rescue us from the evil one"** (v13) — Greek ῥῦσαι, "rescue"; ἀπὸ τοῦ πονηροῦ can be masculine, "the evil one" (as 13:19, 38; John 17:15; 1 John 5:18), or neuter, "evil". The form does not decide.
- **Traditional doxology omitted** (v13) — "For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen." stands in RP; WH, Treg, NA28 and SBLGNT do not have it.
- **"if ye forgive not men their trespasses" → "if you do not forgive men"** (v15) — "their trespasses" (τὰ παραπτώματα αὐτῶν: WH Treg RP) is not in NA28 and SBLGNT; on a 2–2 split NA28 decides.
- **"be not, as the hypocrites, of a sad countenance" → "do not become as the hypocrites, sullen"** (v16) — Greek γίνεσθε "become"; σκυθρωποί, "gloomy-faced, sullen".
- **"disfigure" kept; "doth corrupt" → "destroy"** (vv16, 19–20) — one Greek verb, ἀφανίζω, "make unseen, make disappear": they make their faces unseen (ἀφανίζουσιν) so that they may be seen (φανῶσιν), a play on words; moth and eating make treasures disappear.
- **"in secret" → "in what is hidden"** (v18) — Greek ἐν τῷ κρυφαίῳ (WH Treg NA28), a different word from ἐν τῷ κρυπτῷ "in secret" (vv4, 6; RP has it here too).
- **"Lay not up for yourselves treasures" → "Do not store up for yourselves treasures"** (vv19–20) — Greek θησαυρίζετε … θησαυρούς, verb and noun of one root ("treasure up treasures").
- **"rust" → "eating"** (vv19–20) — Greek βρῶσις, "eating" (the word for "food, eating" in John 6:27, 55; Rom 14:17): whatever eats a thing away — rust, or the eating of vermin. "Rust" proper is ἰός (Jas 5:3).
- **"break through" → "dig through"** (vv19–20) — Greek διορύσσουσιν, dig through the (clay) wall of a house.
- **"your heart" (plural) → "your treasure … your heart"** (v21) — Greek σου, singular (WH Treg NA28); RP ὑμῶν, plural.
- **"The light of the body" → "The lamp of the body"** (v22) — Greek λύχνος, a lamp; "light" (φῶς) comes in v23.
- **"single" kept; "full of light" / "full of darkness" → "bright" / "dark"** (vv22–23) — Greek ἁπλοῦς, "single, simple, without a second aim"; also "generous" (ἁπλότης, Rom 12:8; 2 Cor 9:11). Its opposite here is πονηρός "evil": an "evil eye" is a grudging, envious one (20:15; Mark 7:22). φωτεινόν, σκοτεινόν are plain adjectives.
- **"how great is that darkness" → "how great the darkness"** (v23) — Greek τὸ σκότος πόσον, without a verb.
- **"No man can serve two masters" → "No one can be a slave to two lords"** (v24) — Greek δουλεύειν, "be a slave to"; κυρίοις, "lords", the word used of the Lord.
- **"mammon" kept** (v24) — Greek μαμωνᾶς, an Aramaic word (ממונא) left untranslated in the Greek: wealth, property.
- **"Take no thought for your life" → "do not be anxious about your soul"** (v25; also vv27, 28, 31, 34) — Greek μεριμνάω, "be anxious, be full of care"; ψυχή, "soul": the life that eating keeps up. "or what you shall drink" (ἢ τί πίητε: WH Treg NA28; RP καὶ τί πίητε "and what you shall drink"); the printed SBLGNT leaves it out.
- **"meat" → "food"; "raiment" → "clothing"** (vv25, 28) — Greek τροφή, ἔνδυμα.
- **"Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not" → "Look at the birds of the heaven, that they do not sow"** (v26) — Greek ἐμβλέψατε εἰς; τοῦ οὐρανοῦ "of the heaven" (the sky; as Mark 4:32); ὅτι "that".
- **"Are ye not much better than they?" → "Are you not worth more than they?"** (v26) — Greek μᾶλλον διαφέρετε αὐτῶν, "differ from, surpass them the more".
- **"add one cubit unto his stature" → "add one cubit to his span of life"** (v27) — Greek ἡλικία means "age, length of life" (John 9:21; Heb 11:11) and "stature" (Luke 19:3); πῆχυς, "cubit", is a measure of length (about 45 cm). Either: a small stretch added to the length of life, or a cubit added to bodily height. Luke 12:26 calls it "a very little thing", which fits the length of life better; "stature" remains possible.
- **"Consider the lilies … they toil not" → "Observe the lilies … they do not labor"** (v28) — Greek καταμάθετε, "learn thoroughly, observe closely"; κοπιῶσιν, "labor to weariness".
- **"was not arrayed" → "clothed himself"; "clothe" → "arrays"; "Wherewithal shall we be clothed?" → "With what shall we clothe ourselves?"** (vv29–31) — Greek περιεβάλετο, περιβαλώμεθα (περιβάλλω, middle: "put around oneself"); in v30 another verb, ἀμφιέννυσιν.
- **"shall he not much more clothe you" → "not much more you"** (v30) — Greek οὐ πολλῷ μᾶλλον ὑμᾶς, without a verb. "which is today" — Greek σήμερον ὄντα, "being today".
- **"O ye of little faith" → "you of little faith"** (v30) — Greek ὀλιγόπιστοι, one word.
- **"Gentiles" → "the nations"** (v32) — Greek τὰ ἔθνη. The verse has "for" (γάρ) twice.
- **"the kingdom of God" kept** (v33) — τοῦ θεοῦ stands in Treg, NA28 and RP; WH and the printed SBLGNT read "the kingdom and his righteousness" without it. On a 2–2 split NA28 decides.
- **"shall take thought for the things of itself" → "will be anxious about itself"** (v34) — Greek μεριμνήσει ἑαυτῆς (Treg NA28; WH αὑτῆς, the same sense); RP τὰ ἑαυτῆς "its own things".
- **"Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof" → "Sufficient for the day is its trouble"** (v34) — Greek κακία, here "badness" in the sense of hardship, trouble; not moral evil (πονηρός) and not "malice", the sense of the word in the letters.

### Notes
- **v13 "the evil one"** (*tou ponērou*) — masculine (the evil one) or neuter (evil); see Changes.
- **v9–13** — "you" in the prayer's frame (v9 "You … pray") is plural; in vv2–4, 6, 17–18, 21–23 the Greek turns to the singular "you".
