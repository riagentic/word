# Matthew 18

[1] In that hour the disciples approached ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, saying, "Who then is greater in the kingdom of the heavens?"

[2] And calling a young child to himself, he stood it in the midst of them

[3] and said, "Amen, I say to you, unless you turn and become as the young children, you will by no means enter into the kingdom of the heavens.

[4] Whoever therefore will humble himself as this young child, this one is the greater in the kingdom of the heavens;

[5] and whoever accepts one such young child upon my name accepts me.

[6] But whoever makes one of these little ones who believe in me stumble, it is profitable for him that a donkey's millstone be hung around his neck and he be sunk in the deep of the sea.

[7] Woe to the world because of the stumbling blocks! For it is a necessity that the stumbling blocks come, yet woe to the man through whom the stumbling block comes!

[8] And if your hand or your foot makes you stumble, cut it off and cast it from you; it is better for you to enter into life maimed or lame than, having two hands or two feet, to be cast into the eternal fire.

[9] And if your eye makes you stumble, take it out and cast it from you; it is better for you to enter into life one-eyed than, having two eyes, to be cast into the ⟦gehenna|Gehenna|hell⟧ of fire.

[10] See that you do not despise one of these little ones, for I say to you that their angels in the heavens continually see the face of my Father who is in the heavens.

[12] What do you think? If any man comes to have a hundred sheep and one of them goes astray, will he not leave the ninety-nine upon the mountains and, going, seek the one that is going astray?

[13] And if it comes to pass that he finds it, amen, I say to you that he rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine that have not gone astray.

[14] So it is not the will before your Father who is in the heavens that one of these little ones should perish.

[15] But if your brother sins against you, go, reprove him between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother;

[16] but if he does not hear, take along with you one or two more, so that at the mouth of two witnesses or three every word may be established;

[17] and if he pays no heed to them, tell the church; and if he pays no heed even to the church, let him be to you as the one of the nations and the toll collector.

[18] Amen, I say to you, whatever things you bind upon the earth will have been bound in heaven, and whatever things you loose upon the earth will have been loosed in heaven.

[19] Again, amen, I say to you that if two of you agree upon the earth about any matter that they ask, it will come to be for them from my Father who is in the heavens.

[20] **For where two or three are gathered together unto my name, there I am in the midst of them.**"

[21] Then ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧, approaching, said to him, "Lord, how many times shall my brother sin against me and I forgive him? Up to seven times?"

[22] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to him, "I do not say to you up to seven times, but up to **seventy times seven**.

[23] Because of this the kingdom of the heavens was likened to a man, a king, who wanted to settle an account with his slaves.

[24] And when he began to settle, there was brought to him one debtor of ten thousand talents.

[25] And as he did not have the means to repay, the lord commanded him to be sold, and the wife and the children and all that he has, and repayment to be made.

[26] So the slave, falling down, was bowing down to him, saying, 'Be patient with me, and I will repay you all.'

[27] And the lord of that slave, moved with compassion, released him and forgave him the loan.

[28] But that slave, going out, found one of his fellow slaves who owed him a hundred denarii, and taking hold of him he was choking him, saying, 'Repay, if you owe anything.'

[29] So his fellow slave, falling down, was appealing to him, saying, 'Be patient with me, and I will repay you.'

[30] But he was not willing, but going away he cast him into prison until he should repay what was owed.

[31] So his fellow slaves, seeing the things that had happened, were exceedingly grieved, and coming they made plain to their own lord all the things that had happened.

[32] Then his lord, calling him to himself, says to him, 'Evil slave, all that debt I forgave you, since you appealed to me;

[33] was it not necessary for you also to have mercy on your fellow slave, as I also had mercy on you?'

[34] And his lord, being wrathful, delivered him up to the tormentors until he should repay all that was owed.

[35] So also my heavenly Father will do to you, if you do not forgive, each one his brother, from your hearts."

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (vv1, 22) — Hebrew name preserved.
- **"At the same time" → "In that hour"** (v1) — Greek Ἐν ἐκείνῃ τῇ ὥρᾳ.
- **"the greatest" → "greater"** (vv1, 4) — Greek μείζων, a comparative, as Mark 9:34; v4 ὁ μείζων, "the greater".
- **"kingdom of heaven" → "kingdom of the heavens"** (vv1, 3, 4, 23) — Greek τῶν οὐρανῶν, plural.
- **"a little child" → "a young child"; "set him" → "stood it"** (v2) — Greek παιδίον, neuter; ἔστησεν αὐτό, as Mark 9:36. The critical text has no "Jesus".
- **"Verily" → "Amen"** (vv3, 13, 18, 19) — Greek ἀμήν, left untranslated. In v19 it is read by Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28 and the Byzantine text.
- **"Except ye be converted" → "unless you turn"** (v3) — Greek στραφῆτε, "turn" (passive in form), the verb of 16:23.
- **"shall not enter" → "will by no means enter"** (v3) — Greek οὐ μὴ εἰσέλθητε.
- **"shall receive … in my name" → "accepts … upon my name"** (v5) — Greek δέξηται; ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματί μου, as Mark 9:37.
- **"offend" → "make stumble"** (vv6, 8, 9) — Greek σκανδαλίζω; the word does not say "sin".
- **"it were better for him" → "it is profitable for him"** (v6) — Greek συμφέρει αὐτῷ; Mark 9:42 has "it is better for him" (καλόν ἐστιν αὐτῷ μᾶλλον).
- **"a millstone" → "a donkey's millstone"** (v6) — Greek μύλος ὀνικός, the large upper stone turned by a donkey, as Mark 9:42.
- **"about his neck" → "around his neck"** (v6) — the verse reads περί (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28); the printed SBLGNT has εἰς with the Byzantine text. No change in meaning.
- **"drowned in the depth of the sea" → "sunk in the deep of the sea"** (v6) — Greek καταποντισθῇ ἐν τῷ πελάγει τῆς θαλάσσης; πέλαγος is the open sea. Mark 9:42 has "cast into the sea".
- **"offences" → "stumbling blocks"** (v7) — Greek σκάνδαλα, the noun of "make stumble" (vv6, 8, 9): the things over which one stumbles; as 16:23. Only Matthew has this verse.
- **"it must needs be" → "it is a necessity"** (v7) — Greek ἀνάγκη, a noun.
- **"to that man" → "to the man"** (v7) — "that" (ἐκείνῳ) is in the Byzantine text only.
- **"cut them off, and cast them from thee" → "cut it off and cast it from you"** (v8) — Greek αὐτόν, singular (the Byzantine text has the plural). The verb is ἐκκόπτω ("cut out", of a tree "cut down"); Mark 9:43 has ἀποκόπτω.
- **"halt or maimed" → "maimed or lame"** (v8) — the order of Westcott-Hort and NA28; Tregelles and the Byzantine text have "lame or maimed". Matthew joins hand and foot in one saying; Mark 9:43, 45 has two.
- **"everlasting fire" → "the eternal fire"** (v8) — Greek τὸ πῦρ τὸ αἰώνιον.
- **"pluck it out" → "take it out"** (v9) — Greek ἔξελε; Mark 9:47 has "cast it out" (ἔκβαλε).
- **"hell fire" → "the *Gehenna* of fire"** (v9) — Greek τὴν γέενναν τοῦ πυρός; Mark 9:47 has "*Gehenna*" alone and "the kingdom of *Elohim*" where Matthew has "life".
- **"in heaven" → "in the heavens"** (vv10, 14, 19) — Greek ἐν οὐρανοῖς, plural, here without the article; in v18 the Greek has the singular, "in heaven".
- **"do always behold" → "continually see"** (v10) — Greek διὰ παντὸς βλέπουσι.
- **v11 omitted** — KJV v11 ("For the Son of man is come to save that which was lost") is in the Byzantine text only; Westcott-Hort, Tregelles and NA28 do not have it (it stands in Luke 19:10). Omitted.
- **"How think ye?" → "What do you think?"** (v12) — Greek τί ὑμῖν δοκεῖ, "what seems to you?".
- **"if a man have" → "If any man comes to have"** (v12) — Greek ἐὰν γένηταί τινι ἀνθρώπῳ, "if there come to be to some man".
- **"be gone astray" → "goes astray"** (vv12–13) — Greek πλανηθῇ, πλανώμενον, πεπλανημένοις: the verb is "deceive, lead astray"; in the passive, of a sheep, "go astray".
- **"doth he not leave … and goeth … and seeketh" → "will he not leave … and, going, seek"** (v12) — Greek ἀφήσει, a future (the Byzantine text has a participle), and ζητεῖ, a present: "will he not leave … and, going, he seeks".
- **"if so be that he find it" → "if it comes to pass that he finds it"** (v13) — Greek ἐὰν γένηται εὑρεῖν αὐτό.
- **"it is not the will of your Father" → "it is not the will before your Father"** (v14) — Greek οὐκ ἔστιν θέλημα ἔμπροσθεν τοῦ πατρὸς ὑμῶν, "there is not a will before your Father": a reverent Semitic way of saying what God wills. "Your" (ὑμῶν): NA28 and the Byzantine text; Westcott-Hort and Tregelles read "my" (μου).
- **"trespass against thee" → "sins against you"** (v15) — Greek ἁμαρτήσῃ εἰς σέ (Tregelles, NA28, the Byzantine text); Westcott-Hort do not have "against you".
- **"tell him his fault" → "reprove him"** (v15) — Greek ἔλεγξον, "bring to light, convict, reprove".
- **"hear" kept** (vv15–16) — Greek ἀκούσῃ.
- **"in the mouth of two or three witnesses" → "at the mouth of two witnesses or three"** (v16) — Greek ἐπὶ στόματος δύο μαρτύρων ἢ τριῶν σταθῇ πᾶν ῥῆμα (Deut 19:15), as 2 Cor 13:1.
- **"neglect to hear" → "pays no heed to"** (v17) — Greek παρακούσῃ, "hear beside": to hear and take no notice, or to refuse to hear.
- **"church" kept** (v17) — Greek ἐκκλησία, "assembly", as 16:18.
- **"an heathen man and a publican" → "the one of the nations and the toll collector"** (v17) — Greek ὁ ἐθνικὸς καὶ ὁ τελώνης, both with the article.
- **"shall be bound … shall be loosed" → "will have been bound … will have been loosed"** (v18) — Greek ἔσται δεδεμένα, ἔσται λελυμένα: the future of "to be" with a perfect passive participle, a future perfect. The other way of taking it: the participle names only the lasting state, "will be bound", "will be loosed". As 16:19, but here "you" is plural (δήσητε, λύσητε) and "heaven" singular without the article (Westcott-Hort, NA28; Tregelles and the Byzantine text add the article).
- **"as touching any thing that they shall ask" → "about any matter that they ask"** (v19) — Greek περὶ παντὸς πράγματος οὗ ἐὰν αἰτήσωνται.
- **"it shall be done for them of" → "it will come to be for them from"** (v19) — Greek γενήσεται αὐτοῖς παρά.
- **"gathered together in my name" → "gathered together unto my name"** (v20) — Greek εἰς τὸ ἐμὸν ὄνομα, "into, unto my name"; a different preposition from v5.
- **"Then came Peter to him, and said" → "Then *Kefa*, approaching, said to him"** (v21) — the verse reads ὁ Πέτρος εἶπεν αὐτῷ (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28); the printed SBLGNT and the Byzantine text have αὐτῷ ὁ Πέτρος εἶπεν, "approaching him, *Kefa* said".
- **"how oft … till seven times" → "how many times … Up to seven times"** (vv21–22) — Greek ποσάκις; ἕως ἑπτάκις.
- **"saith" → "says"** (vv22, 32) — Greek λέγει, a present; kept.
- **"seventy times seven" kept** (v22) — Greek ἑβδομηκοντάκις ἑπτά, word for word "seventy times seven". It can mean seventy times seven (490) or seventy-seven times: the same two Greek words stand in Gen 4:24 in the Septuagint for the Hebrew "seventy-seven", of Lamech's vengeance, which this saying turns around.
- **"Therefore is the kingdom of heaven likened unto a certain king" → "Because of this the kingdom of the heavens was likened to a man, a king"** (v23) — Greek Διὰ τοῦτο ὡμοιώθη … ἀνθρώπῳ βασιλεῖ: an aorist passive; "man" stands in the Greek.
- **"take account of his servants" → "settle an account with his slaves"** (v23) — Greek συνᾶραι λόγον; δούλων, "slaves" (so vv26–33).
- **"one … which owed him ten thousand talents" → "one debtor of ten thousand talents"** (v24) — Greek εἷς ὀφειλέτης μυρίων ταλάντων. A talent is a weight of silver, some six thousand denarii; μύριοι, ten thousand, is the highest Greek number word.
- **"had not to pay" → "did not have the means to repay"** (v25) — Greek μὴ ἔχοντος … ἀποδοῦναι; ἀποδίδωμι is "repay" throughout (vv25–34).
- **"his lord … his wife … all that he had" → "the lord … the wife … all that he has"** (v25) — the critical text has no "his" (Tregelles has it with "wife") and the present ἔχει.
- **"worshipped him" → "was bowing down to him"** (v26) — Greek προσεκύνει, imperfect; the verb names the act of bowing to the ground.
- **"Lord, have patience with me" → "Be patient with me"** (vv26, 29) — Greek Μακροθύμησον ἐπ' ἐμοί; "Lord" is in the Byzantine text only.
- **"loosed him" → "released him"** (v27) — Greek ἀπέλυσεν, the verb "send away" (19:3).
- **"the debt" → "the loan"** (v27) — Greek τὸ δάνειον; "debt" in v32 is ὀφειλήν.
- **"an hundred pence" → "a hundred denarii"** (v28) — Greek δηνάρια; a denarius is a day's wage (20:2).
- **"took him by the throat" → "was choking him"** (v28) — Greek ἔπνιγεν, imperfect.
- **"Pay me that thou owest" → "Repay, if you owe anything"** (v28) — Greek Ἀπόδος εἴ τι ὀφείλεις; "me" is in the Byzantine text only.
- **"fell down at his feet, and besought him" → "falling down, was appealing to him"** (v29) — Greek παρεκάλει; "at his feet" is in the Byzantine text only.
- **"he would not" → "he was not willing"** (v30) — Greek οὐκ ἤθελεν.
- **"told" → "made plain"** (v31) — Greek διεσάφησαν.
- **"O thou wicked servant" → "Evil slave"** (v32) — Greek Δοῦλε πονηρέ.
- **"Shouldest not thou also have had compassion … as I had pity" → "was it not necessary for you also to have mercy … as I also had mercy"** (v33) — Greek οὐκ ἔδει; ἐλεῆσαι … ἠλέησα, one verb.
- **"was wroth" → "being wrathful"** (v34) — Greek ὀργισθείς.
- **"tormentors" kept** (v34) — Greek βασανισταῖς, from βασανίζω, "torment"; not simply "jailers".
- **"if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses" → "if you do not forgive, each one his brother, from your hearts"** (v35) — "their trespasses" is in the Byzantine text only.
- **"heavenly" kept** (v35) — Greek οὐράνιος (the Byzantine text has ἐπουράνιος).

### Notes
- **v11** absent from the earliest manuscripts ("For the Son of Man came to save the lost" — from Luke 19:10).
- **v22** — cf. Gen 4:24 (reversing Lamech's vengeance).
- **vv23–35** — only in Matthew.
