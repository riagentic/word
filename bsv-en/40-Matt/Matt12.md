# Matthew 12

[1] At that time ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ went on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ through the grainfields; and his disciples were hungry and began to pluck ears and to eat.

[2] But the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧, seeing it, said to him, "Behold, your disciples are doing what is not permitted to do on a ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧."

[3] But he said to them, "Did you not read what David did when he was hungry, and those with him:

[4] how he entered into the house of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and they ate the loaves of the setting forth, which it was not permitted for him to eat, nor for those with him, except for the priests alone?

[5] Or did you not read in the ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧ that on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ the priests in the temple profane the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧ and are guiltless?

[6] But I say to you that something greater than the temple is here.

[7] But if you had known what this is, '**Mercy I want, and not sacrifice**,' you would not have condemned the guiltless.

[8] For the Son of Man is lord of the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧."

[9] And passing on from there he came into their synagogue;

[10] and behold, a man having a withered hand. And they asked him, saying, "Is it permitted to heal on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧?" — that they might accuse him.

[11] But he said to them, "What man will there be of you who will have one sheep, and if this falls into a pit on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧, will he not take hold of it and raise it?

[12] How much more, then, is a man worth than a sheep! So then it is permitted to do well on the ⟦shabbat|Shabbat|Sabbath⟧."

[13] Then he says to the man, "Stretch out your hand." And he stretched it out, and it was restored, healthy as the other.

[14] But going out, the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ took counsel against him, how they might destroy him.

[15] But ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, knowing it, withdrew from there. And many crowds followed him, and he healed them all,

[16] and he rebuked them, that they should not make him manifest,

[17] that what was spoken through ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ the prophet might be fulfilled, saying:

[18] "Behold, my servant whom I chose,
my beloved in whom my soul took pleasure;
I will put my Spirit upon him,
and he will declare judgment to the nations.

[19] He will not strive nor shout,
nor will anyone hear his voice in the streets;

[20] a crushed reed he will not break,
and smoking flax he will not quench,
until he puts forth judgment unto victory;

[21] and in his name nations will hope."

[22] Then there was brought to him a demonized man, blind and mute; and he healed him, so that the mute man spoke and saw.

[23] And all the crowds were amazed and were saying, "Is this perhaps the Son of David?"

[24] But the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧, hearing it, said, "This one does not cast out the demons except by Beelzebul, ruler of the demons."

[25] But knowing their reflections he said to them, "Every kingdom divided against itself is made desolate, and every city or house divided against itself will not stand.

[26] And if Satan casts out Satan, he has been divided against himself; how then will his kingdom stand?

[27] And if I by Beelzebul cast out the demons, by whom do your sons cast them out? Because of this they themselves will be your judges.

[28] But if by the Spirit of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ I cast out the demons, then the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has come upon you.

[29] Or how is anyone able to enter into the house of the strong man and snatch his goods, unless he first binds the strong man? And then he will plunder his house.

[30] "The one who is not with me is against me, and the one who does not gather with me scatters.

[31] Because of this I say to you, every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men, but the blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven.

[32] And whoever says a word against the Son of Man, it will be forgiven him; but whoever speaks against the ⟦ruach-haqodesh|Ruach HaQodesh|Holy Spirit⟧, it will not be forgiven him, neither in this age nor in the coming one.

[33] "Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree rotten and its fruit rotten; for from the fruit the tree is known.

[34] Offspring of vipers, how are you able to speak good things, being evil? For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.

[35] The good man out of the good treasure puts forth good things, and the evil man out of the evil treasure puts forth evil things.

[36] But I say to you that every idle word that men will speak, they will render an account concerning it in the day of judgment;

[37] for from your words you will be justified, and from your words you will be condemned."

[38] Then some of the scribes and ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ answered him, saying, "Teacher, we want to see a sign from you."

[39] But he answered and said to them, "An evil and adulterous generation seeks after a sign, and a sign will not be given to it except the sign of ⟦jonah|Yonah|Jonah⟧ the prophet.

[40] For just as ⟦jonah|Yonah|Jonah⟧ was in the belly of the sea monster three days and three nights, so the Son of Man will be in the heart of the earth three days and three nights.

[41] Men of ⟦nineveh|Ninveh|Nineveh⟧ will rise in the judgment with this generation and will condemn it, because they repented at the proclamation of ⟦jonah|Yonah|Jonah⟧; and behold, something more than ⟦jonah|Yonah|Jonah⟧ is here.

[42] The queen of the south will be raised in the judgment with this generation and will condemn it, because she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧; and behold, something more than ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧ is here.

[43] "Now when the unclean spirit goes out from the man, it passes through waterless places seeking rest, and does not find it.

[44] Then it says, 'Into my house I will return, from where I came out.' And coming, it finds it unoccupied, swept and adorned.

[45] Then it goes and takes along with itself seven other spirits more evil than itself, and entering they dwell there; and the last things of that man become worse than the first. So it will be also for this evil generation."

[46] While he was still speaking to the crowds, behold, his mother and his brothers were standing outside, seeking to speak to him.

[47] And someone said to him, "Behold, your mother and your brothers are standing outside, seeking to speak to you."

[48] But he answered and said to the one telling him, "Who is my mother, and who are my brothers?"

[49] And stretching out his hand over his disciples he said, "Behold, my mother and my brothers!

[50] For whoever does the will of my Father who is in the heavens, he is my brother and sister and mother."

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv4, 28) — Greek θεός *theos*; divine name preserved.
- **"Holy Ghost" → "*Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v32) — Greek τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον *to pneuma to hagion*; in v31 the Greek has "the Spirit" alone.
- **"devils/devil" → "demons/demonized"** (vv22, 24, 27–28) — Greek δαιμόνια *daimonia*; δαιμονιζόμενος "demonized" (no "possessed" in the Greek).
- **"sabbath" → "Sabbath"** (vv1–2, 5, 8, 10–12) — Greek σάββατον *sabbaton* = Hebrew שַׁבָּת *Shabbat*. The plural form τοῖς σάββασιν means the one day ("on the Shabbat"); in v2 ἐν σαββάτῳ has no article, "on a Shabbat".
- **"corn" → "grainfields"; "ears of corn" → "ears"** (v1) — Greek διὰ τῶν σπορίμων, the sown fields; στάχυας "ears". ἐπείνασαν "were hungry" is an aorist: "became hungry".
- **"not lawful" → "not permitted"** (vv2, 4, 10, 12) — Greek οὐκ ἔξεστιν, as Mark 2:24, 26; 3:4.
- **"did eat the shewbread" → "they ate the loaves of the setting forth"** (v4) — Greek τοὺς ἄρτους τῆς προθέσεως, as Mark 2:26 and Heb 9:2; Hebrew לֶחֶם הַפָּנִים *lechem ha-panim*, "bread of the Face". The verb is the plural ἔφαγον "they ate" (WH, NA28); Tregelles and the Byzantine text have ἔφαγεν "he ate".
- **"blameless" → "guiltless"** (vv5, 7) — Greek ἀναίτιος *anaitios*, "without guilt, not chargeable".
- **"one greater than the temple" → "something greater than the temple"** (v6) — Greek μεῖζον, a neuter: "a greater thing". In vv41–42 πλεῖον "more", also a neuter.
- **"I will have mercy" → "Mercy I want"** (v7) — Greek Ἔλεος θέλω καὶ οὐ θυσίαν, the words of Hos 6:6 in the Septuagint; the Hebrew word there is חֶסֶד *chesed*. The Greek is translated as printed. τί ἐστιν "what this is", i.e. what it means; κατεδικάσατε "condemned" is another verb than κατακρίνω "condemn" (vv41–42).
- **"Lord even of the sabbath day" → "lord of the Shabbat"** (v8) — Greek κύριος … τοῦ σαββάτου; Matthew has no "also" (Mark 2:28 καί).
- **"departed" → "passing on"** (v9) — Greek μεταβάς *metabas*, as 11:1.
- **"which had his hand withered"** (v10) — Greek has no verb: "and behold, a man having a withered hand" (WH, Treg, NA28). εἰ introduces the direct question. θεραπεύειν (WH, Treg, RP; NA28 has the aorist θεραπεῦσαι, the same sense).
- **"lay hold on it, and lift it out" → "take hold of it and raise it"** (v11) — Greek κρατήσει … ἐγερεῖ; the verbs "will be", "will have" are futures.
- **"How much then is a man better than a sheep?" → "How much more, then, is a man worth than a sheep!"** (v12) — Greek πόσῳ οὖν διαφέρει ἄνθρωπος προβάτου, literally "by how much does a man differ from a sheep", as 6:26. καλῶς ποιεῖν "to do well" (an adverb); Mark 3:4 has ἀγαθοποιῆσαι "to do good".
- **"saith" → "says"** (v13) — Greek λέγει, a present, kept. ὑγιής "healthy".
- **"held a council" → "took counsel"** (v14) — Greek συμβούλιον ἔλαβον; Mark 3:6 has συμβούλιον ἐδίδουν "were giving counsel".
- **"great multitudes" → "many crowds"** (v15) — Greek ὄχλοι πολλοί (Treg, NA28, RP); Westcott-Hort and the SBLGNT have πολλοί alone, "many followed him".
- **"charged them" → "rebuked them"** (v16) — Greek ἐπετίμησεν αὐτοῖς ἵνα, as Mark 3:12: a stern warning. φανερὸν ποιεῖν "make manifest".
- **"my servant, whom I have chosen" → "my servant whom I chose"** (v18) — Greek ὁ παῖς μου "my servant" (also "child, boy"; the Septuagint's word for the Servant of Isa 42); ᾑρέτισα, εὐδόκησεν are aorists. The quotation (Isa 42:1–4) is translated from Matthew's Greek, which differs from both the Hebrew and the Septuagint. εἰς ὃν "in whom" (NA28, RP; WH ὅν, Treg ἐν ᾧ).
- **"shew judgment to the Gentiles" → "declare judgment to the nations"** (vv18, 20, 21) — Greek κρίσις *krisis*, "judgment" (Hebrew *mishpat*, also "justice"); ἔθνη "nations"; in v21 without the article.
- **"cry" → "shout"** (v19) — Greek κραυγάσει; ἐρίσει "strive" is of one root with ἔρις "strife".
- **"bruised reed … smoking flax" → "crushed reed … smoking flax"** (v20) — Greek συντετριμμένον "crushed"; λίνον τυφόμενον, flax that smokes: a lamp wick about to go out.
- **"send forth judgment" → "puts forth judgment"** (v20, 35) — Greek ἐκβάλλω *ekballō*, the verb "cast out" of vv24–28; of the treasure in v35 "puts forth".
- **"blind, and dumb" → "blind and mute"** (v22) — Greek κωφός *kōphos*, "dull": deaf (11:5) or mute; here he "spoke", so "mute". Passive προσηνέχθη "was brought" (Treg, NA28, RP); Westcott-Hort has the active "they brought".
- **"Is not this the son of David?" → "Is this perhaps the Son of David?"** (v23) — Greek μήτι, a question that expects "no" or leaves it in doubt. ἐξίσταντο "were amazed".
- **"prince of the devils" → "ruler of the demons"** (v24) — Greek ἄρχοντι τῶν δαιμονίων, as Mark 3:22; οὐκ … εἰ μή "does not … except".
- **"thoughts" → "reflections"** (v25) — Greek ἐνθυμήσεις *enthymēseis*, as Heb 4:12. ἐρημοῦται "is made desolate".
- **"is divided" → "has been divided"** (v26) — Greek ἐμερίσθη, an aorist, as Mark 3:26; ἐφʼ ἑαυτόν "against himself" (v25 has καθʼ ἑαυτῆς).
- **"by the Spirit of God"** (v28) — Greek ἐν πνεύματι θεοῦ, without the article. ἔφθασεν "has come, has arrived" (as 1 Thess 2:16).
- **"spoil his goods … spoil his house" → "snatch his goods … plunder his house"** (v29) — Greek ἁρπάσαι "snatch" (WH, Treg, NA28), then διαρπάσει "plunder"; Mark 3:27 has "plunder" twice.
- **"the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost" → "the blasphemy of the Spirit"** (v31) — Greek ἡ τοῦ πνεύματος βλασφημία, a plain genitive: blasphemy against the Spirit (v32 κατά "against"). "Unto men" at the end of the verse is only in the Byzantine text.
- **"in the world to come" → "in the coming one"** (v32) — Greek οὔτε ἐν τούτῳ τῷ αἰῶνι οὔτε ἐν τῷ μέλλοντι, "age".
- **"corrupt" → "rotten"** (v33) — Greek σαπρός *sapros*, as Eph 4:29.
- **"O generation of vipers" → "Offspring of vipers"** (v34) — Greek γεννήματα ἐχιδνῶν, "things begotten of vipers".
- **"give account thereof" → "render an account concerning it"** (v36) — Greek ἀποδώσουσιν … λόγον; ῥῆμα ἀργόν "idle word": ῥῆμα, not λόγος; ἀργός "not working" (Jas 2:20). The sentence breaks off and starts again in the Greek, as here.
- **"by thy words" → "from your words"** (v37) — Greek ἐκ τῶν λόγων σου, as "justified from" elsewhere (Gal 2:16).
- **"Master" → "Teacher"** (v38) — Greek Διδάσκαλε.
- **"seeketh after a sign"** (v39) — Greek ἐπιζητεῖ, the compound; Mark 8:12 has ζητεῖ "seeks".
- **"whale's belly" → "belly of the sea monster"** (v40) — Greek κῆτος *kētos*, a huge sea creature; the Septuagint's word in Jonah 2:1, where the Hebrew has דָּג גָּדוֹל "a great fish".
- **"shall rise … shall rise up" → "will rise … will be raised"** (vv41–42) — Greek ἀναστήσονται (v41), ἐγερθήσεται (v42): two verbs. ἄνδρες Νινευῖται "men, Ninevites", without the article; κήρυγμα "proclamation".
- **"is gone out of a man" → "goes out from the man"** (v43) — Greek ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου with the article (as v45 "of that man"). The unclean spirit is "it": πνεῦμα is neuter; the gender is grammatical.
- **"empty, swept, and garnished" → "unoccupied, swept and adorned"** (v44) — Greek σχολάζοντα "standing idle, vacant"; κεκοσμημένον "adorned, put in order" (as Titus 2:10). "It finds it": the second "it" is supplied; the Greek has no object.
- **"the last state of that man" → "the last things of that man"** (v45) — Greek τὰ ἔσχατα … τῶν πρώτων, neuter plurals.
- **"desiring to speak with him" → "seeking to speak to him"** (vv46–47) — Greek ζητοῦντες. In v46 "Now" (δέ) is only in the Byzantine text.
- **v47** — the verse is in Tregelles, NA28 and the Byzantine text; Westcott-Hort omits it (some old manuscripts lack it). It stays.
- **"toward his disciples" → "over his disciples"** (v49) — Greek ἐπὶ τοὺς μαθητάς.
- **"which is in heaven" → "who is in the heavens"** (v50) — Greek τοῦ ἐν οὐρανοῖς, a plural. Mark 3:35 has "the will of God" and οὗτος "this one"; Matthew has αὐτός "he".
