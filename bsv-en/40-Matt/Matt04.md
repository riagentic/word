# Matthew 4

[1] Then ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ was led up into the wilderness by the Spirit, to be tempted by the devil.

[2] And having fasted forty days and forty nights, afterward he hungered.

[3] And approaching, the tempter said to him, "If you are Son of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, say that these stones should become loaves."

[4] But answering he said, "It is written,

"'Not on bread alone shall man live,
but on every word going out through the mouth of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.'"

[5] Then the devil takes him along into the holy city, and he set him upon the pinnacle of the temple,

[6] and says to him, "If you are Son of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, cast yourself down; for it is written,

"'He will give commandment to his angels concerning you,'
and
"'On hands they will lift you,
lest you strike your foot against a stone.'"

[7] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to him, "Again it is written, 'You shall not tempt ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.'"

[8] Again the devil takes him along onto an exceedingly high mountain, and shows him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory,

[9] and he said to him, "All these things I will give you, if falling down you bow down to me."

[10] Then ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to him, "Go, Satan! For it is written,

"'You shall bow down to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧
and him alone shall you worship.'"

[11] Then the devil leaves him, and behold, angels approached and were serving him.

[12] Now hearing that ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧ was delivered up, he withdrew into ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧.

[13] And leaving behind ⟦nazareth|Natzrat|Nazareth⟧, he came and dwelt in ⟦capernaum|Kfar Nachum|Capernaum⟧, the one by the sea, in the borders of ⟦zebulun|Zevulun|Zebulun⟧ and ⟦naphtali|Naftali|Naphtali⟧,

[14] that what was spoken through ⟦isaiah|Yeshayahu|Isaiah⟧ the prophet might be fulfilled, saying,

[15] "Land of ⟦zebulun|Zevulun|Zebulun⟧ and land of ⟦naphtali|Naftali|Naphtali⟧,
way of the sea, beyond the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧,
⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧ of the nations —

[16] the people sitting in darkness
saw a great light,
and for those sitting in the country and shadow of death,
light rose for them."

[17] From then ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ began to proclaim and to say, "**Repent, for the kingdom of the heavens has drawn near.**"

[18] And walking beside the sea of ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧, he saw two brothers, ⟦simon|Shimon|Simon⟧ the one called ⟦peter|Kefa|Peter⟧ and ⟦andrew|Andreas|Andrew⟧ his brother, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishers.

[19] And he says to them, "Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men."

[20] And they, immediately leaving the nets, followed him.

[21] And going on from there, he saw two other brothers, ⟦james|Yaaqov|James⟧ the son of ⟦zebedee|Zebedaios|Zebedee⟧ and ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧ his brother, in the boat with ⟦zebedee|Zebedaios|Zebedee⟧ their father, mending their nets; and he called them.

[22] And they, immediately leaving the boat and their father, followed him.

[23] And he was going about in all ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧, teaching in their synagogues and proclaiming the gospel of the kingdom and healing every disease and every sickness among the people.

[24] And the report of him went away into all Syria; and they brought to him all who were ill, held by various diseases and torments, demonized and moonstruck and paralytics, and he healed them.

[25] And many crowds followed him from ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧ and Decapolis and ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ and ⟦judea|Yehudah|Judea⟧ and beyond the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved throughout.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv3–4, 6) — Greek θεός *theos* in the temptation narrative; where Yeshua quotes Deuteronomy the underlying Hebrew is *Elohim*, so the Hebrew divine name is retained.
- **"of the Spirit" → "by the Spirit"** (v1) — Greek ὑπὸ τοῦ πνεύματος … ὑπὸ τοῦ διαβόλου, the same preposition twice. Mark 1:12 has "the Spirit casts him out".
- **"when he had fasted … he was afterward an hungred" → "having fasted … afterward he hungered"** (v2) — Greek νηστεύσας … ὕστερον ἐπείνασεν.
- **"when the tempter came to him, he said" → "approaching, the tempter said to him"** (v3) — Greek προσελθών, "approaching" (also v11); ὁ πειράζων, "the one tempting".
- **"If thou be the Son of God, command that these stones be made bread" → "If you are Son of *Elohim*, say that these stones should become loaves"** (v3, 6) — Greek εἰ υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ θεοῦ: υἱός has no article and stands before the verb, so the Greek does not decide between "the Son" and "a son". εἰπὲ ἵνα … ἄρτοι γένωνται: "say", "become", "loaves" (plural).
- **"Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" → "Not on bread alone shall man live, but on every word going out through the mouth of *Elohim*"** (v4) — Greek οὐκ ἐπ' ἄρτῳ μόνῳ … ἀλλ' ἐπὶ παντὶ ῥήματι ἐκπορευομένῳ διὰ στόματος θεοῦ: ἐπί, "on" (Treg ἐν, "in"), διά, "through"; ῥῆμα, "word" (a thing spoken; not λόγος).
- **"taketh him up … setteth him … saith" → "takes him along … set him … says"** (v5–6, 8, 10, 11, 19) — Greek presents in narrative are kept (παραλαμβάνει, λέγει, δείκνυσιν, ἀφίησιν); ἔστησεν (v5) and εἶπεν (v9) are past tenses (RP has presents there). παραλαμβάνω, "take along", as 2:13.
- **"pinnacle" kept** (v5) — Greek τὸ πτερύγιον τοῦ ἱεροῦ, literally "the little wing of the temple": some projecting point of the temple precinct (ἱερόν).
- **"He shall give his angels charge concerning thee: and in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest at any time thou dash thy foot" → "He will give commandment to his angels concerning you, and on hands they will lift you, lest you strike your foot"** (v6) — Greek ἐντελεῖται; ἐπὶ χειρῶν, "on hands", without "their"; ἀροῦσιν, "will lift"; προσκόψῃς. The "and" is part of the quotation (Ps 91:11–12, with words left out between).
- **"Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God" kept** (v7) — Greek ἐκπειράσεις, the verb of v1, 3 (πειράζω, "tempt, put to the test") with ἐκ-.
- **"an exceeding high mountain" kept: "an exceedingly high mountain"** (v8) — Greek εἰς ὄρος ὑψηλὸν λίαν.
- **"if thou wilt fall down and worship me" → "if falling down you bow down to me"; "Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve" → "You shall bow down to *Yahweh* your *Elohim* and him alone shall you worship"** (v9–10) — two Greek verbs: προσκυνέω, "bow down to" (as 2:2, 11), and λατρεύω, the service given to God, "worship".
- **"Get thee hence, Satan" → "Go, Satan"** (v10) — Greek ὕπαγε, Σατανᾶ (WH Treg NA28); RP adds ὀπίσω μου, "behind me" (as 16:23; Mark 8:33).
- **"leaveth him" kept: "leaves him"; "came and ministered unto him" → "approached and were serving him"** (v11) — Greek ἀφίησιν, the verb of 3:15; διηκόνουν, an imperfect, as Mark 1:13.
- **"was cast into prison" → "was delivered up"; "departed" → "withdrew"** (v12) — Greek παρεδόθη, as Mark 1:14: no prison is named; ἀνεχώρησεν, as 2:12–14, 22. RP adds the name "*Yeshua*".
- **"leaving Nazareth, he came and dwelt in Capernaum, which is upon the sea coast, in the borders" → "leaving behind Nazareth, he came and dwelt in Capernaum, the one by the sea, in the borders"** (v13) — Greek καταλιπών; τὴν παραθαλασσίαν; ἐν ὁρίοις. The town is spelled Ναζαρά here (Ναζαρέτ in 2:23).
- **"The land of Zabulon … by the way of the sea … Galilee of the Gentiles" → "Land of Zebulun … way of the sea … Galilee of the nations"** (v15) — the Greek has no articles and no verb: Γῆ Ζαβουλὼν καὶ γῆ Νεφθαλίμ, ὁδὸν θαλάσσης (an accusative), Γαλιλαία τῶν ἐθνῶν; ἔθνη is "nations".
- **"which sat in darkness … in the region and shadow of death light is sprung up" → "sitting in darkness … in the country and shadow of death, light rose for them"** (v16) — Greek ὁ καθήμενος, τοῖς καθημένοις, "sitting", twice; εἶδεν, "saw"; χώρᾳ, "country"; φῶς ἀνέτειλεν αὐτοῖς: ἀνατέλλω, "rise", the verb behind ἀνατολή (2:2). "darkness": σκοτίᾳ WH Treg, σκότει NA28 RP, the same meaning.
- **"From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say" → "From then Yeshua began to proclaim and to say"** (v17) — Greek ἀπὸ τότε; κηρύσσειν καὶ λέγειν. The words are those of *Yochanan* in 3:2.
- **"Simon called Peter" → "Simon the one called *Kefa*"** (v18) — Greek Σίμωνα τὸν λεγόμενον Πέτρον, without brackets. Aramaic *Kefa* = rock; Greek *Petros* = Peter.
- **"fishers" kept; "Follow me" → "Come after me"** (v18, 19) — Greek ἁλιεῖς, one word in both verses; δεῦτε ὀπίσω μου, as Mark 1:17 (where "make you become" stands; here "make you").
- **"straightway left their nets" → "immediately leaving the nets"** (v20, 22) — Greek εὐθέως ἀφέντες τὰ δίκτυα, without "their"; ἀφίημι is "leave" here and "let" in 3:15.
- **"James the son of Zebedee" kept** (v21) — Greek Ἰάκωβον τὸν τοῦ Ζεβεδαίου, "Jacob the one of Zebedee"; "son" is supplied because the verse itself calls Zebedee "their father".
- **"went about all Galilee" → "was going about in all Galilee"** (v23) — Greek περιῆγεν, an imperfect; ἐν ὅλῃ τῇ Γαλιλαίᾳ WH NA28; Treg and RP add the name "*Yeshua*".
- **"all manner of sickness and all manner of disease" → "every disease and every sickness"** (v23) — Greek πᾶσαν νόσον καὶ πᾶσαν μαλακίαν: νόσος, "disease" (as Mark 1:34); μαλακία, "softness, weakness".
- **"his fame went throughout all Syria" → "the report of him went away into all Syria"** (v24) — Greek ἀπῆλθεν ἡ ἀκοὴ αὐτοῦ εἰς ὅλην τὴν Συρίαν; ἀκοή as Mark 1:28.
- **"all sick people that were taken with divers diseases and torments" → "all who were ill, held by various diseases and torments"** (v24) — Greek τοὺς κακῶς ἔχοντας, "those having it badly" (as Mark 1:32, 34); συνεχομένους, "held fast, gripped". The datives can also go with "ill": "ill with various diseases and held by torments". NA28 and RP add "and" after συνεχομένους; WH Treg and the printed text do not.
- **"possessed with devils" → "demonized"** (v24) — Greek δαιμονιζομένους, one verb from δαιμόνιον, "demon"; the Greek has no word "possessed". "Devil" (v1–11) is διάβολος, a different word.
- **"lunatick" → "moonstruck"** (v24) — Greek σεληνιαζομένους, from σελήνη, "moon". The symptoms described in 17:15 are often taken to be epilepsy; the word itself does not say that.
- **"those that had the palsy" → "paralytics"** (v24) — Greek παραλυτικούς, "paralyzed ones".
- **"great multitudes" → "many crowds"** (v25) — Greek ὄχλοι πολλοί.
- **OT quotation v15–16** — Quotes Isa 9:1–2; rendered from Matthew's Greek text as it stands, not from the Hebrew or the Septuagint.

### Notes
- **v4** quotes Deut 8:3.
- **v7** quotes Deut 6:16.
- **v10** quotes Deut 6:13 (the three temptations all countered from Deuteronomy).
- **v15-16** quotes Isa 9:1-2.
