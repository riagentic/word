[2] And two men, commanders of raiding bands, the son of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ had: the name of the one was Baanah, and the name of the second ⟦rechab|Rechav|Rechab⟧, sons of Rimmon the ⟦beeroth|Beeroti|Beerothite⟧, of the sons of ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧; for ⟦beeroth|Beerot|Beeroth⟧ also is reckoned to ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧.

[3] And the ⟦beeroth|Beerotim|Beerothites⟧ fled to ⟦gittaim|Gittayim|Gittaim⟧, and they have been sojourning there to this day.

[4] And ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ the son of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ had a son stricken in the feet. He was five years old when the report of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ came from ⟦jezreel|Yizreel|Jezreel⟧, and his nurse took him up and fled; and it came to pass, in her alarm to flee, that he fell and was made lame. ## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"when Saul's son heard that Abner was dead in Hebron, his hands were feeble, and all the Israelites were troubled" → "the son of Saul heard that Abner had died in Hebron, and his hands went slack, and all Israel were dismayed"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיִּרְפּוּ יָדָיו, "his hands sank, went slack"; נִבְהָלוּ. The Masoretic Text has only "the son of Saul", without a name, here and in v2 (the Septuagint is reported to name him: from memory).
- **"Saul's son had two men that were captains of bands" → "two men, commanders of raiding bands, the son of Saul had"** (v2) — Hebrew וּשְׁנֵי אֲנָשִׁים שָׂרֵי־גְדוּדִים הָיוּ בֶן־שָׁאוּל, literally "and two men, commanders of raiding bands, were the son of Saul": the preposition "to" (לְ) that the sense needs is not in the Masoretic Text. גְּדוּד is "raiding band" (1 Sam 30:8). **"the other" → "the second"** — Hebrew הַשֵּׁנִי. **"of the children of Benjamin" → "of the sons of Benjamin"**. **"was reckoned" → "is reckoned"** — Hebrew תֵּחָשֵׁב.
- **"were sojourners there until this day" → "have been sojourning there to this day"** (v3) — Hebrew גָּרִים, a participle.
- **"lame of his feet" → "stricken in the feet"** (v4) — Hebrew נְכֵה רַגְלַיִם, from the root of הִכָּה "strike" (also 9:3). **"when the tidings came of Saul and Jonathan" → "when the report of Saul and Jonathan came"** — Hebrew שְׁמֻעַת. **"as she made haste to flee" → "in her alarm to flee"** — Hebrew בְּחָפְזָהּ לָנוּס: חָפַז is "hurry away in alarm". **"became lame" → "was made lame"** — Hebrew וַיִּפָּסֵחַ, a passive form.
- **"who lay on a bed at noon" → "he was lying down for the lying of the noonday"** (v5) — Hebrew וְהוּא שֹׁכֵב אֵת מִשְׁכַּב הַצָּהֳרָיִם, "lying the lying of the noon": verb and noun of one root; the midday rest.
- **"they came thither into the midst of the house, as though they would have fetched wheat; and they smote him under the fifth rib" → "they came there, as far as the midst of the house, takers of wheat, and they struck him in the belly"** (v6) — Hebrew וְהֵנָּה בָּאוּ עַד־תּוֹךְ הַבַּיִת לֹקְחֵי חִטִּים. The verse is difficult. וְהֵנָּה as pointed is "and hither" (or the pronoun "they", feminine); לֹקְחֵי חִטִּים is "takers of wheat", with no "as though": whether the two came as men fetching wheat, or with such men, is not said. The Septuagint is reported to have a different verse here (the woman who kept the door was cleaning wheat and fell asleep, and the brothers slipped in: from memory). The Masoretic Text is translated as printed; nothing of the Greek is taken into the verse. חֹמֶשׁ "belly" is uncertain (2:23). The verse tells the blow and the escape, and v7 tells the killing again more fully.
- **"slew him, and beheaded him" → "put him to death and removed his head"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיְמִתֻהוּ; וַיָּסִירוּ אֶת־רֹאשׁוֹ. **"gat them away through the plain" → "went by the way of the Arabah"** — Hebrew דֶּרֶךְ הָעֲרָבָה.
- **"which sought thy life" → "who sought your soul"** (v8) — Hebrew נַפְשֶׁךָ. **"the LORD hath avenged my lord the king this day of Saul, and of his seed" → "Yahweh has given to my lord the king vengeance this day on Saul and on his seed"** — Hebrew וַיִּתֵּן … נְקָמוֹת, "gave vengeances" (plural); מִשָּׁאוּל וּמִזַּרְעוֹ, "from Saul and from his seed".
- **"who hath redeemed my soul out of all adversity" → "who has ransomed my soul out of all distress"** (v9) — Hebrew פָּדָה "ransom" (גָּאַל is "redeem"); צָרָה.
- **"When one told me … thinking to have brought good tidings, I took hold of him, and slew him in Ziklag, who thought that I would have given him a reward for his tidings" → "the one who told me … and he was as a bearer of news in his own eyes — I took hold of him and killed him in Ziklag, which was what I gave him for the news"** (v10) — Hebrew וְהוּא־הָיָה כִמְבַשֵּׂר בְּעֵינָיו: מְבַשֵּׂר is one who brings news (1 Sam 4:17), mostly glad news; "thinking" is not in the Hebrew. אֲשֶׁר לְתִתִּי־לוֹ בְּשֹׂרָה is difficult: literally "which (was) for my giving him news": בְּשֹׂרָה is "news" and also the reward for it (18:22). It can be taken as bitter irony ("that was the reward I gave him"), as here, or "to whom I ought to have given a reward for news". See 1:15.
- **"How much more, when wicked men have slain a righteous person in his own house upon his bed? shall I not therefore now require his blood of your hand, and take you away from the earth?" → "How much more when wicked men have killed a righteous man in his house, on his bed! And now, shall I not seek his blood from your hand and purge you from the earth?"** (v11) — Hebrew אֲבַקֵּשׁ "seek" (require); וּבִעַרְתִּי, the verb "burn away, purge" (Deut 13:5); הָאָרֶץ is "the earth" or "the land".
- **"his young men" → "the young men"** (v12) — Hebrew הַנְּעָרִים. **"slew them" → "killed them"** — Hebrew הָרַג. **"hanged them up over the pool" → "hung them by the pool"** — Hebrew וַיִּתְלוּ עַל־הַבְּרֵכָה: the verb has no object in the Hebrew ("them" is supplied): the bodies, or the hands and the feet. **"sepulchre" → "grave"** — Hebrew קֶבֶר.

### Notes
- **The son of Saul** (v1–2) — named Ish-bosheth from v5 on.
- **Mephibosheth** (v4) — Hebrew מְפִיבֹשֶׁת, as printed; 1 Chr 8:34; 9:40 name a son of Jonathan Merib-baal. He comes again in ch 9; 16; 19; 21.
- **The news-bearer of Ziklag** (v10) — David's own account of 1:2–16.
- **The pool in Hebron** (v12) — the head of Ish-bosheth is buried in the grave of Abner (3:32).
