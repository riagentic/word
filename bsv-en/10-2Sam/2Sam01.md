# 2 Samuel 1

[1] And it came to pass after the death of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, and David had returned from striking down ⟦amalek|Amaleq|Amalek⟧, and David dwelt in ⟦ziklag|Tziqlag|Ziklag⟧ two days.

[2] And it came to pass on the third day that, behold, a man came from the camp, from with ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, and his garments were torn and ground was on his head. And it came to pass, when he came to David, that he fell to the earth and bowed down.

[3] And David said to him, "From where do you come?" And he said to him, "From the camp of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ I have escaped."

[4] And David said to him, "What was the matter? Tell me, please." And he said, "That the people fled from the battle, and also many of the people fell and died, and also ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ his son died."

[5] And David said to the young man who was telling him, "How do you know that ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ has died, and ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ his son?"

[6] And the young man who was telling him said, "I happened by chance to be on Mount Gilboa, and behold, ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ was leaning on his spear, and behold, the chariots and the lords of the horsemen had caught up with him.

[7] And he turned behind him and saw me, and he called to me, and I said, 'Here I am.'

[8] And he said to me, 'Who are you?' And I said to him, 'I am an ⟦amalekites|Amaleqi|Amalekite⟧.'

[9] And he said to me, 'Stand, please, over me and put me to death, for the cramp has taken hold of me, for all my soul is yet in me.'

[10] And I stood over him and put him to death, for I knew that he would not live after his falling. And I took the crown that was on his head and an armlet that was on his arm, and I have brought them to my lord here."

[11] And David took fast hold of his garments and tore them, and also all the men who were with him.

[12] And they lamented and wept and fasted until the evening over ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and over ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ his son and over the people of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ and over the house of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, because they had fallen by the sword.

[13] And David said to the young man who was telling him, "From where are you?" And he said, "I am the son of a sojourner, an ⟦amalekites|Amaleqi|Amalekite⟧."

[14] And David said to him, "How did you not fear to stretch out your hand to destroy the ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧ of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧?"

[15] And David called one of the young men and said, "Draw near, fall upon him." And he struck him, and he died.

[16] And David said to him, "Your blood is on your head, for your mouth has answered against you, saying, 'I have put to death the ⟦anointed|mashiach|anointed⟧ of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.'"

[17] And David chanted this dirge over ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and over ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ his son.

[18] And he said to teach the sons of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ the Bow; behold, it is written on the book of ⟦jashar|Yashar|Jashar⟧:

[19] "The beauty, ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, on your high places is slain!
How the mighty ones have fallen!

[20] Tell it not in ⟦gath|Gat|Gath⟧,
bring not the news in the streets of ⟦ashkelon|Ashqelon|Ashkelon⟧,
lest the daughters of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ rejoice,
lest the daughters of the uncircumcised be jubilant.

[21] Mountains in Gilboa,
let there be no dew and no rain upon you,
and fields of contributions;
for there the shield of the mighty ones was abhorred,
the shield of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, not anointed with oil.

[22] From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty ones,
the bow of ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧ did not draw back,
and the sword of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ would not return empty.

[23] ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧ and ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧, the beloved and the pleasant in their lives,
and in their death they were not parted;
they were swifter than eagles,
they were mightier than lions.

[24] Daughters of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, weep over ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧,
who clothed you in scarlet with delights,
who put ornaments of gold upon your clothing.

[25] How the mighty ones have fallen in the midst of the battle!
⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧, on your high places, slain!

[26] I am in distress over you, my brother ⟦jonathan|Yehonatan|Jonathan⟧;
you were very pleasant to me;
more wonderful was your love to me
than the love of women.

[27] How the mighty ones have fallen,
and the weapons of war have perished!"

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept, and "And it came to pass" (וַיְהִי, v1, 2) is not dropped.
- **"when David was returned from the slaughter of the Amalekites, and David had abode" → "and David had returned from striking down Amalek, and David dwelt"** (v1) — Hebrew וְדָוִד שָׁב מֵהַכּוֹת אֶת־הָעֲמָלֵק וַיֵּשֶׁב: הִכָּה "strike", here of killing; הָעֲמָלֵק is the name of the people, "the Amalek", not "the Amalekites"; יָשַׁב "dwell".
- **"a man came out of the camp from Saul with his clothes rent, and earth upon his head" → "a man came from the camp, from with Saul, and his garments were torn and ground was on his head"** (v2) — Hebrew מִן־הַמַּחֲנֶה מֵעִם שָׁאוּל; בְּגָדִים "garments"; אֲדָמָה "ground" (as 1 Sam 4:12). **"did obeisance" → "bowed down"** — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁתָּחוּ.
- **"Out of the camp of Israel am I escaped" → "From the camp of Israel I have escaped"** (v3) — the Hebrew order; נִמְלַט "escape".
- **"How went the matter?" → "What was the matter?"** (v4) — Hebrew מֶה־הָיָה הַדָּבָר, "what was the word / the thing" (as 1 Sam 4:16). **"That the people …"** — the answer begins with אֲשֶׁר "that". **"are dead also" → "died"** — Hebrew וַיָּמֻתוּ … מֵתוּ, verbs.
- **"the young man that told him" → "the young man who was telling him"** (v5, 6, 13) — Hebrew הַנַּעַר הַמַּגִּיד לוֹ, a participle; נַעַר is "boy", of a grown man or an attendant "young man".
- **"As I happened by chance" → "I happened by chance to be"** (v6) — Hebrew נִקְרֹא נִקְרֵיתִי, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "happening I happened". The usual "surely" of this translation would here affirm the fact instead of the chance, so "by chance" carries the doubling.
- **"the chariots and horsemen followed hard after him" → "the chariots and the lords of the horsemen had caught up with him"** (v6) — Hebrew וּבַעֲלֵי הַפָּרָשִׁים הִדְבִּקֻהוּ. בַּעֲלֵי הַפָּרָשִׁים is literally "the lords / owners of the horsemen" (or "of the steeds": פָּרָשׁ is both); the phrase stands only here and is difficult. הִדְבִּיק is "cling to, catch up with", the verb of 1 Sam 31:2. 1 Sam 31:3 speaks of archers, not of chariots: the two accounts are left as they stand.
- **"when he looked behind him" → "he turned behind him"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיִּפֶן אַחֲרָיו. **"Here am I"** — Hebrew הִנֵּנִי, "behold me".
- **"And I answered him"** (v8) — the written text (ketiv) has "and he said"; the reading tradition (qere) has "and I said", which is translated.
- **"slay me" → "put me to death"** (v9, 10, 16) — Hebrew מוֹתֵת, the intensive stem of מוּת "die" (Judg 9:54; 1 Sam 14:13; 17:51).
- **"anguish is come upon me" → "the cramp has taken hold of me"** (v9) — Hebrew אֲחָזַנִי הַשָּׁבָץ. אָחַז is "take hold of". הַשָּׁבָץ stands only here and its meaning is uncertain: "cramp" (from a root "weave, interlace") is the best-supported sense; "dizziness", "agony" are also given. The Septuagint is reported to have "a terrible darkness" (from memory).
- **"because my life is yet whole in me" → "for all my soul is yet in me"** (v9) — Hebrew כִּי־כָל־עוֹד נַפְשִׁי בִּי, literally "for all yet my soul in me": the word order is unusual; נֶפֶשׁ is "soul".
- **"after that he was fallen" → "after his falling"** (v10) — Hebrew אַחֲרֵי נִפְלוֹ. **"the crown … and the bracelet" → "the crown … and an armlet"** — Hebrew הַנֵּזֶר, the mark of one set apart (Exod 29:6; 2 Kgs 11:12), with the article; אֶצְעָדָה, an arm-band, without the article.
- **"took hold on his clothes, and rent them" → "took fast hold of his garments and tore them"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיַּחֲזֵק בְּ, "hold fast, take a strong hold of" (Exod 4:4; אָחַז in v9 is "take hold of"). The ketiv has "his garment", the qere "his garments".
- **"they mourned" → "they lamented"** (v12) — Hebrew סָפַד, the loud wailing for the dead. **"for Saul, and for …" → "over Saul and over …"** — Hebrew עַל, four times.
- **"the son of a stranger" → "the son of a sojourner"** (v13) — Hebrew בֶּן־אִישׁ גֵּר עֲמָלֵקִי, literally "the son of a man, a sojourner, an Amalekite"; גֵּר is one who lives in a land not his own.
- **"wast thou not afraid" → "did you not fear"** (v14) — Hebrew יָרֵאתָ. **"to destroy the LORD's anointed" → "to destroy the *mashiach* (anointed) of Yahweh"** (v14, 16) — Hebrew לְשַׁחֵת, the verb rendered "ruin" elsewhere in this translation; of the killing of a man "destroy" is kept, as in 1 Sam 26:9. מְשִׁיחַ יְהוָה is "the anointed one of Yahweh".
- **"he smote him that he died" → "he struck him, and he died"** (v15) — Hebrew וַיַּכֵּהוּ וַיָּמֹת. **"Go near"** — Hebrew גַּשׁ. **"fall upon him"** — Hebrew פְּגַע־בּוֹ.
- **"Thy blood be upon thy head; for thy mouth hath testified against thee" → "Your blood is on your head, for your mouth has answered against you"** (v16) — the Hebrew has no verb ("is" or "be"); the ketiv has the plural דמיך "your bloods" (bloodguilt), the qere "your blood". עָנָה בְּ is "answer against": bear witness against.
- **"lamented with this lamentation" → "chanted this dirge"** (v17) — Hebrew וַיְקֹנֵן … אֶת־הַקִּינָה, verb and noun of one root: the קִינָה is the formal song for the dead. "Lament" is kept for סָפַד (v12).
- **"Also he bade them teach the children of Judah the use of the bow" → "And he said to teach the sons of Judah the Bow"** (v18) — Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר לְלַמֵּד בְּנֵי־יְהוּדָה קָשֶׁת, word for word "and he said to teach the sons of Judah bow". "The use of" is not in the Hebrew. The verse is difficult: "Bow" is commonly taken as the name of the song (from v22), or as archery; the Septuagint is reported to lack the word (from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept as it stands. **"written in the book of Jasher" → "written on the book of Yashar"** — Hebrew עַל־סֵפֶר הַיָּשָׁר, "the book of the Upright", as Josh 10:13; "it" is feminine in the Hebrew (the dirge, or the Bow).
- **"The beauty of Israel is slain upon thy high places" → "The beauty, Israel, on your high places is slain"** (v19) — Hebrew הַצְּבִי יִשְׂרָאֵל עַל־בָּמוֹתֶיךָ חָלָל. הַצְּבִי has the article and so is not "the beauty of Israel": Israel is addressed. צְבִי is "beauty, ornament" and also "gazelle"; either may be meant. חָלָל is "one pierced, slain".
- **"the mighty" → "the mighty ones"** (v19, 21, 22, 25, 27) — Hebrew גִּבּוֹרִים.
- **"publish it not" → "bring not the news"** (v20) — Hebrew בִּשֵּׂר, "bring news" (1 Sam 31:9). **"triumph" → "be jubilant"** — Hebrew עָלַז "exult, be jubilant"; "triumph" says a victory.
- **"Ye mountains of Gilboa" → "Mountains in Gilboa"** (v21) — Hebrew הָרֵי בַגִּלְבֹּעַ, "mountains in the Gilboa".
- **"let there be no dew, neither let there be rain, upon you, nor fields of offerings" → "let there be no dew and no rain upon you, and fields of contributions"** (v21) — Hebrew אַל־טַל וְאַל־מָטָר עֲלֵיכֶם וּשְׂדֵי תְרוּמֹת. The last two words are difficult: literally "and fields of contributions" (תְּרוּמָה, what is lifted off for Yahweh, Exod 25:2), with no negation of their own: fields whose yield is given as contributions, on which no dew is to fall — or the text is damaged. Emendations are common ("fields of death", "springs of the deep"; from memory). The Masoretic Text is translated as printed.
- **"vilely cast away" → "was abhorred"** (v21) — Hebrew נִגְעַל, from גָּעַל "abhor" (Lev 26:11); "defiled" is also given.
- **"as though he had not been anointed with oil" → "not anointed with oil"** (v21) — Hebrew בְּלִי מָשִׁיחַ בַּשָּׁמֶן, "without one anointed with oil". It may be said of the shield (no longer rubbed with oil) or of Saul; the form מָשִׁיחַ is that of "the anointed one". The Hebrew does not decide and neither does the verse.
- **"turned not back" → "did not draw back"; "returned not empty" → "would not return empty"** (v22) — Hebrew לֹא נָשׂוֹג אָחוֹר; לֹא תָשׁוּב רֵיקָם, an imperfect: what it used to do.
- **"lovely and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not divided" → "the beloved and the pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not parted"** (v23) — Hebrew הַנֶּאֱהָבִים וְהַנְּעִימִם, both with the article; נִפְרָדוּ "be parted". The Masoretic accents join "in their lives" to the first line. **"stronger than lions" → "mightier than lions"** — Hebrew גָּבֵרוּ, the verb of גִּבּוֹר "mighty one".
- **"weep over Saul"** (v24) — Hebrew אֶל־שָׁאוּל. **"with other delights" → "with delights"** — Hebrew עִם־עֲדָנִים; "other" is not in the Hebrew. **"apparel" → "clothing"** — Hebrew לְבוּשׁ. "Put" is literally "made go up" (הַמַּעֲלֶה).
- **"O Jonathan, thou wast slain in thine high places" → "Jonathan, on your high places, slain"** (v25) — the Hebrew has no verb; "your" is the "your" of v19 (Israel's).
- **"I am distressed for thee" → "I am in distress over you"** (v26) — Hebrew צַר־לִי עָלֶיךָ, literally "it is narrow for me over you".
- **"thy love to me was wonderful, passing the love of women" → "more wonderful was your love to me than the love of women"** (v26) — Hebrew נִפְלְאַתָה אַהֲבָתְךָ לִי מֵאַהֲבַת נָשִׁים. אַהֲבָה is the plain word "love", the same word that is used of a man and a woman, of friends and of Yahweh and Israel; the verse does not say more than that.
- **Lines of the dirge** (v19–27) — one Hebrew colon per line, in the Hebrew order; the earlier text's "O", "it" and "lies" were supplied and are gone.

### Notes
- **Two accounts of the death of Saul** — the Amalekite says that he put Saul to death at his request; 1 Sam 31:4 says that Saul fell upon the sword. The text sets the two side by side and does not say that the Amalekite lies or tells the truth; the translation does not harmonise them.
- **David has the young man struck down** — "the anointed of Yahweh" again (1 Sam 24:6; 26:9). See also 2 Sam 4:10.
- **The dirge over Saul and Jonathan** (v19–27) — a קִינָה, the song for the dead. The refrain "How the mighty ones have fallen" stands in v19, 25, 27.
- **"Tell it not in Gath"** (v20) — compare Mic 1:10.
- **The Bow** (v18) — the song may bear the name "Bow" (v22); the "book of Yashar" is cited also in Josh 10:13.
