[14] And David sent messengers to ⟦ish-bosheth|Ish-Boshet|Ish-bosheth⟧ the son of ⟦saul|Shaul|Saul⟧, saying, "Give my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to myself for a hundred foreskins of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧."

[20] And ⟦abner|Avner|Abner⟧ came to David at ⟦hebron|Chevron|Hebron⟧, and with him twenty men. And David made a feast for ⟦abner|Avner|Abner⟧ and for the men who were with him.

[21] And ⟦abner|Avner|Abner⟧ said to David, "Let me arise and go and gather to my lord the king all ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, and they will cut a covenant with you, and you shall reign over all that your soul desires." And David sent ⟦abner|Avner|Abner⟧ away, and he went in ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v9, 35) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"David waxed stronger and stronger, and the house of Saul waxed weaker and weaker" → "David went on growing stronger, and the house of Saul went on growing weaker"** (v1) — Hebrew הֹלֵךְ וְחָזֵק … הֹלְכִים וְדַלִּים, literally "going and strong … going and low". דַּל is "low, poor" (Gen 41:19); "poor" would say money here, so "weaker" stands.
- **"unto David were sons born"** (v2) — the ketiv and the qere are two forms of the same verb. **"the wife of Nabal the Carmelite"** (v3) — הַכַּרְמְלִי is masculine and belongs to Nabal. **"Adonijah"** (v4) — the Hebrew has here the short form אֲדֹנִיָּה; the name keeps its one form in this translation. The mothers are named with לְ, "of" (v2, 3, 5).
- **"Abner made himself strong for the house of Saul" → "Abner was strengthening himself in the house of Saul"** (v6) — Hebrew מִתְחַזֵּק בְּבֵית שָׁאוּל; בְּ is "in" (as 1 Sam 30:6).
- **"and Ish-bosheth said to Abner" → "And he said to Abner"** (v7) — the Masoretic Text does not name the speaker: וַיֹּאמֶר אֶל־אַבְנֵר. The name is supplied by the KJV (in italics) and by the earlier text; the Septuagint and some Hebrew manuscripts are reported to have it (from memory). The Masoretic Text is kept. **"concubine"** — Hebrew פִּילֶגֶשׁ.
- **"Then was Abner very wroth for the words of Ish-bosheth" → "Abner burned greatly with anger over the words of Ish-bosheth"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיִּחַר לְאַבְנֵר מְאֹד, literally "it burned to Abner greatly".
- **"Am I a dog's head, which against Judah do shew kindness this day" → "Am I a dog's head that is of Judah? Today I do *chesed* (steadfast love)"** (v8) — Hebrew הֲרֹאשׁ כֶּלֶב אָנֹכִי אֲשֶׁר לִיהוּדָה הַיּוֹם אֶעֱשֶׂה־חֶסֶד. אֲשֶׁר לִיהוּדָה is "which belongs to Judah"; the Masoretic accents end the question there. The words are difficult, and the Septuagint is reported to lack "that is of Judah" (from memory). חֶסֶד with עָשָׂה "do" and עִם "with", as 2:5–6.
- **"have not delivered thee into the hand of David" → "have not let you be found in the hand of David"** (v8) — Hebrew וְלֹא הִמְצִיתִךָ בְּיַד־דָּוִד, the causative of מָצָא "find": literally "have not made you find / be found in the hand of David".
- **"thou chargest me to day with a fault concerning this woman" → "you visit upon me the iniquity of the woman today"** (v8) — Hebrew וַתִּפְקֹד עָלַי עֲוֺן הָאִשָּׁה: פָּקַד "visit", עָוֹן "iniquity" (as Exod 20:5). Whether Abner admits or denies the deed the verse does not say.
- **"So do God to Abner, and more also, except, as the LORD hath sworn to David, even so I do to him" → "Thus may Elohim do to Abner, and thus may he add to him: as Yahweh has sworn to David, so I will do for him"** (v9) — Hebrew כֹּה־יַעֲשֶׂה אֱלֹהִים … וְכֹה יֹסִיף לוֹ כִּי כַּאֲשֶׁר … כִּי־כֵן אֶעֱשֶׂה־לּוֹ: the oath formula (1 Sam 14:44; 25:22), with כִּי bringing in what is sworn. No such oath of Yahweh to David is told before.
- **"To translate the kingdom … and to set up the throne" → "to make the kingdom pass over … and to raise up the throne"** (v10) — Hebrew לְהַעֲבִיר; לְהָקִים. **"from Dan even to Beer-sheba" → "from Dan and to Beersheba"** — Hebrew וְעַד.
- **"he could not answer Abner a word again, because he feared him" → "he was not able any more to return Abner a word, because of his fearing him"** (v11) — Hebrew לְהָשִׁיב … דָּבָר; מִיִּרְאָתוֹ אֹתוֹ. The subject is not named.
- **"sent messengers to David on his behalf, saying, Whose is the land? saying also, Make thy league with me" → "sent messengers to David in his place, saying, 'Whose is the land?' saying, 'Cut your covenant with me'"** (v12) — the verse is difficult. תַּחְתָּיו (qere; the ketiv has תחתו) is "under him, in his place": on Abner's behalf, or "where he was", or "at once"; the versions differ (some are reported to read "to Hebron": from memory). לְמִי־אָרֶץ is literally "to whom is a land?", and לֵאמֹר "saying" stands twice. The Masoretic Text is translated as printed. כָּרַת בְּרִית is "cut a covenant" (also v13, 21). **"to bring about all Israel unto thee" → "to turn all Israel around to you"** — Hebrew לְהָסֵב.
- **"Well; I will make a league with thee: but one thing I require of thee, that is, Thou shalt not see my face, except thou first bring" → "Good. I will cut a covenant with you. Only one thing I ask from you, saying, You shall not see my face except you first bring"** (v13) — Hebrew שֹׁאֵל "ask"; כִּי אִם־לִפְנֵי הֱבִיאֲךָ, literally "except before your bringing": the wording is unusual.
- **"Deliver me my wife Michal, which I espoused to me" → "Give my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to myself"** (v14) — Hebrew תְּנָה; "me" is not in the Hebrew. A hundred foreskins is the price Saul named (1 Sam 18:25); 1 Sam 18:27 tells of two hundred.
- **"took her from her husband" → "took her from a man"** (v15) — Hebrew מֵעִם אִישׁ, "from with a man", without "her"; the versions are reported to read "her husband" (from memory), as v16 has. The ketiv has לוש "Lush", the qere לָיִשׁ "Laish" (1 Sam 25:44, where the man is called Palti).
- **"her husband went with her along weeping behind her" → "her man went with her, going and weeping after her"** (v16) — Hebrew אִישָׁהּ, "her man, her husband": the one word of v15; הָלוֹךְ וּבָכֹה.
- **"Abner had communication with the elders of Israel, saying, Ye sought for David in times past" → "the word of Abner had been with the elders of Israel, saying, 'Both yesterday and the day before you were seeking David'"** (v17) — Hebrew וּדְבַר־אַבְנֵר הָיָה עִם; גַּם־תְּמוֹל גַּם־שִׁלְשֹׁם, "both yesterday and the day before": for some time past.
- **"Now then do it: for the LORD hath spoken of David, saying, By the hand of my servant David I will save" → "And now, do it, for Yahweh has said to David, saying, 'By the hand of David my servant, to save'"** (v18) — Hebrew אָמַר אֶל־דָּוִד, "said to David" (אֶל can also be "concerning"). The Masoretic Text has הוֹשִׁיעַ, an infinitive ("to save") or "he has saved"; "I will save" (אוֹשִׁיעַ) is the reading of many Hebrew manuscripts and of the versions (from memory) and of the KJV. The printed form is translated; the verse is difficult.
- **"all that seemed good to Israel" → "all that was good in the eyes of Israel"** (v19) — Hebrew טוֹב בְּעֵינֵי, twice.
- **"I will arise and go, and will gather" → "Let me arise and go and gather"** (v21) — Hebrew cohortatives. **"that they may make a league with thee, and that thou mayest reign over all that thine heart desireth" → "and they will cut a covenant with you, and you shall reign over all that your soul desires"** — Hebrew נַפְשֶׁךָ "your soul". **"in peace" → "in *shalom*"** (v21, 22, 23) — Hebrew בְּשָׁלוֹם.
- **"came from pursuing a troop" → "came from the raid"** (v22) — Hebrew בָּא מֵהַגְּדוּד; גְּדוּד is "a raiding band" (1 Sam 30:8) and here the raid itself; "pursuing" is not in the Hebrew. The verb is singular. **"a great spoil" → "much spoil"** — Hebrew שָׁלָל רָב.
- **"he is quite gone" → "he is surely gone"** (v24) — Hebrew וַיֵּלֶךְ הָלוֹךְ, "and he went, going": the infinitive absolute. In v21–23 the same going is "in *shalom*"; Joab leaves that word out.
- **"to deceive thee" → "to entice you"** (v25) — Hebrew לְפַתֹּתְךָ, "persuade, entice". The ketiv and qere of "your coming in" are two forms of one word.
- **"the well of Sirah" → "the pit of the Sirah"** (v26) — Hebrew בּוֹר הַסִּרָה: בּוֹר is a pit, a cistern (בְּאֵר is "well"); the name has the article. **"but David knew it not" → "and David did not know"**.
- **"took him aside in the gate to speak with him quietly, and smote him there under the fifth rib" → "turned him aside into the midst of the gate, to speak with him in quiet, and he struck him there in the belly"** (v27) — Hebrew וַיַּטֵּהוּ … אֶל־תּוֹךְ הַשַּׁעַר, "to the midst of the gate". בַּשֶּׁלִי stands only here and is uncertain: "in quietness" (from שָׁלָה "be at ease"), so "privately", or "unawares". הַחֹמֶשׁ "the belly" (uncertain, as 2:23) stands here without a preposition. "His brother" is Joab's brother.
- **"I and my kingdom are guiltless before the LORD for ever from the blood of Abner" → "Innocent am I, and my kingdom, before Yahweh forever, of the blood of Abner"** (v28) — Hebrew נָקִי … מֵעִם יְהוָה, literally "clear … from with Yahweh"; דְּמֵי is the plural "bloods": blood shed, bloodguilt.
- **"Let it rest on the head of Joab" → "Let it whirl upon the head of Joab"** (v29) — Hebrew יָחֻלוּ, from חוּל "whirl, turn about" (Jer 23:19): a plural, "let them whirl", the "bloods" of v28. **"let there not fail" → "let there not be cut off"** — Hebrew אַל־יִכָּרֵת.
- **"one that hath an issue, or that is a leper, or that leaneth on a staff" → "one who flows, and a leper, and one who holds fast the spindle"** (v29) — Hebrew זָב, "one who flows": a man with a bodily flow (Lev 15:2). מְצֹרָע "leper" is the word of Lev 13–14; it is not the leprosy of modern medicine. מַחֲזִיק בַּפֶּלֶךְ is uncertain: פֶּלֶךְ is "spindle" in Prov 31:19 (a man fit only for women's work), and elsewhere "district" (Neh 3:9); "staff, crutch" (the KJV; the Septuagint is reported to have "staff": from memory) is the other old understanding. "Spindle" is kept as the attested sense; the five are joined by "and".
- **"slew Abner, because he had slain" → "killed Abner, because he had put to death"** (v30) — Hebrew הָרְגוּ; הֵמִית, two verbs.
- **"Rend your clothes, and gird you with sackcloth, and mourn before Abner" → "Tear your garments and gird on sackcloth and lament before Abner"** (v31) — Hebrew קִרְעוּ בִגְדֵיכֶם; סִפְדוּ, as 1:11–12. **"king David himself followed the bier" → "King David was going after the bier"** — Hebrew הֹלֵךְ אַחֲרֵי הַמִּטָּה; מִטָּה is "bed, couch" (4:7), here that on which the dead man is carried.
- **"the king lamented over Abner" → "the king chanted a dirge for Abner"** (v33) — Hebrew וַיְקֹנֵן, as 1:17.
- **"Died Abner as a fool dieth?" → "As a fool dies, should Abner die?"** (v33) — Hebrew הַכְּמוֹת נָבָל יָמוּת אַבְנֵר, literally "as the death of a fool should Abner die?" נָבָל "fool" is also the name Nabal (1 Sam 25:25).
- **"nor thy feet put into fetters: as a man falleth before wicked men, so fellest thou" → "and your feet were not brought near to fetters of bronze; as one falls before sons of injustice, you have fallen"** (v34) — Hebrew לֹא־לִנְחֻשְׁתַּיִם הֻגָּשׁוּ; נְחֻשְׁתַּיִם is a pair of bronze fetters; בְּנֵי־עַוְלָה "sons of injustice". The lines of the dirge follow the Hebrew: v33 is its first line, v34 the rest (the earlier text had the verse number one line too late).
- **"to cause David to eat meat" → "to make David eat bread"** (v35) — Hebrew לְהַבְרוֹת … לֶחֶם, to give the food of the mourner. **"So do God to me, and more also, if I taste bread, or ought else, till the sun be down" → "Thus may Elohim do to me, and thus may he add: before the sun goes in I will not taste bread or anything at all"** — Hebrew כִּי אִם … אֶטְעַם, literally "if I taste": the form of an oath, meaning "I will not".
- **"took notice of it, and it pleased them: as whatsoever the king did pleased all the people" → "took notice, and it was good in their eyes; as all that the king did was good in the eyes of all the people"** (v36) — Hebrew הִכִּירוּ; וַיִּיטַב בְּעֵינֵיהֶם; בְּעֵינֵי … טוֹב.
- **"understood that day that it was not of the king to slay Abner" → "knew on that day that it had not been from the king to put to death Abner"** (v37) — Hebrew וַיֵּדְעוּ; מֵהַמֶּלֶךְ; לְהָמִית.
- **"a prince and a great man" → "a prince and a great one"** (v38) — Hebrew שַׂר וְגָדוֹל; שַׂר is "prince", of an army "commander" (2:8).
- **"I am this day weak, though anointed king; and these men … be too hard for me" → "I am today soft, and anointed king, and these men … are harder than I"** (v39) — Hebrew רַךְ "soft, tender" set against קָשִׁים "hard"; וּמָשׁוּחַ מֶלֶךְ is "and anointed king", with "and"; קָשִׁים מִמֶּנִּי is "harder than I", which may also be "too hard for me". **"the LORD shall reward the doer of evil according to his wickedness" → "May Yahweh repay the doer of the evil according to his evil"** — Hebrew יְשַׁלֵּם, a wish or a statement; הָרָעָה … כְּרָעָתוֹ, one word twice.

### Notes
- **Sons born in Hebron** (v2–5) — six sons of six mothers; Amnon, Absalom and Adonijah come again in the book (2 Sam 13–18; 1 Kgs 1–2).
- **Abner and Ish-bosheth** (v6–11) — the question about Saul's concubine (compare 16:21–22; 1 Kgs 2:22) turns Abner to David.
- **Michal** (v13–16) — given to David (1 Sam 18:27), then to Palti (1 Sam 25:44).
- **Joab strikes Abner** (v27, 30) — for the blood of Asahel (2:23); the text names Abishai with him in v30.
- **David and the death of Abner** (v28–39) — the oath of innocence, the dirge, the fast.
- **"Harder than I"** (v39) — the sons of Zeruiah; see 16:10; 19:22; 1 Kgs 2:5–6.
