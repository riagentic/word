# Ephesians 5

[1] Become therefore imitators of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, as beloved children,

[2] and walk in love, just as also ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ loved us and delivered himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ for a fragrance of sweet smell.

[3] But sexual immorality and all uncleanness or covetousness, let it not even be named among you, just as is proper for saints,

[4] and shamefulness and foolish talk or jesting, which are not fitting, but rather thanksgiving.

[5] For this you know, knowing that no sexually immoral or unclean or covetous person, which is an idolater, has an inheritance in the kingdom of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[6] Let no one beguile you with empty words, for because of these things the wrath of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is coming upon the sons of disobedience.

[7] Do not therefore become fellow partakers with them;

[8] for you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord; walk as children of light,

[9] for the fruit of the light is in all goodness and righteousness and truth,

[10] testing what is well-pleasing to the Lord;

[11] and do not be fellow sharers in the unfruitful works of the darkness, but rather even reprove them,

[12] for the things done in secret by them it is shameful even to speak of;

[13] but all things being reproved by the light are manifested,

[14] for everything that is manifested is light. Therefore it says,
"**Awake, you who sleep,
and arise from the dead,
and ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ will shine upon you**."

[15] Look therefore carefully how you walk, not as unwise but as wise,

[16] redeeming the time, because the days are evil.

[17] For this reason do not become senseless, but understand what the will of the Lord is;

[18] and do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissoluteness, but be filled in the Spirit,

[19] speaking to each other with psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and singing psalms with your heart to the Lord,

[20] giving thanks always for all things in the name of our Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ to the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and Father,

[21] being subject to one another in the fear of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[22] Wives, to your own husbands as to the Lord,

[23] because a husband is head of the wife as also ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ is head of the church, he himself Savior of the body.

[24] But as the church is subject to ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, so also the wives to their husbands in everything.

[25] **Husbands, love your wives, just as also ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ loved the church and delivered himself up for her**,

[26] that he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of the water in a word,

[27] that he himself might present to himself the church glorious, not having spot or wrinkle or any of such things, but that she might be holy and without blemish.

[28] So ought the husbands also to love their own wives as their own bodies. He who loves his own wife loves himself,

[29] for no one ever hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, just as also ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ the church,

[30] because we are members of his body.

[31] "**For this reason a man will leave father and mother and will be joined to his wife, and the two will be one flesh.**"

[32] This mystery is great; but I speak with regard to ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and to the church.

[33] Nevertheless you also, one by one, let each so love his own wife as himself, and the wife, that she fear the husband.

## Changes

- **"Christ" / "Jesus Christ" → "*Mashiach*" / "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv1, 2, 5, 6, 20) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v20) — in "our Lord *Yeshua* the *Mashiach*."
- **"Be ye therefore followers of God, as dear children" → "Become therefore imitators of *Elohim*, as beloved children"** (v1) — Greek γίνεσθε (4:32); μιμηταί; ἀγαπητά.
- **"hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour" → "delivered himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to *Elohim* for a fragrance of sweet smell"** (v2) — Greek παρέδωκεν, aorist (v25); προσφορὰν καὶ θυσίαν, two nouns; εἰς ὀσμὴν εὐωδίας as Phil 4:18. "Loved us … for us": ἡμᾶς (SBLGNT NA28 RP; WH and Treg read ὑμᾶς, "you": 2–2, NA28 decides), ἡμῶν (SBLGNT Treg NA28 RP; WH ὑμῶν).
- **"fornication, and all uncleanness, or covetousness, let it not be once named among you, as becometh saints" → "sexual immorality and all uncleanness or covetousness, let it not even be named among you, just as is proper for saints"** (v3) — Greek πορνεία, ἀκαθαρσία, πλεονεξία, as Col 3:5; μηδὲ ὀνομαζέσθω, an imperative, not "must".
- **"Neither filthiness, nor foolish talking, nor jesting, which are not convenient" → "and shamefulness and foolish talk or jesting, which are not fitting"** (v4) — Greek αἰσχρότης (αἰσχρολογία in Col 3:8), μωρολογία, εὐτραπελία ("ready wit", here in a bad sense); ἃ οὐκ ἀνῆκεν as Col 3:18. The verse has no verb of its own.
- **"For this ye know, that no whoremonger, nor unclean person, nor covetous man, who is an idolater" → "For this you know, knowing that no sexually immoral or unclean or covetous person, which is an idolater"** (v5) — Greek ἴστε γινώσκοντες, two verbs of knowing; ἴστε is a statement or a command ("know this"). πόρνος is the person of πορνεία (v3); ὅ ἐστιν, "which is", is neuter in the printed text. "Has an inheritance": present tense.
- **"the kingdom of Christ and of God" → "the kingdom of the *Mashiach* and *Elohim*"** (v5) — Greek τοῦ Χριστοῦ καὶ θεοῦ, one article for both nouns.
- **"Let no man deceive you with vain words … cometh the wrath of God upon the children of disobedience" → "Let no one beguile you with empty words … the wrath of *Elohim* is coming upon the sons of disobedience"** (v6) — Greek κενοῖς (Col 2:8); as Col 3:6. ἀπατάω "beguile" (1 Tim 2:14; "deceive" is πλανάω) is the verb of ἀπάτη "deceit" (4:22).
- **"Be not ye therefore partakers with them" → "Do not therefore become fellow partakers with them"** (v7) — Greek γίνεσθε συμμέτοχοι, the word of 3:6.
- **"For the fruit of the Spirit" → "for the fruit of the light"** (v9) — the critical text reads φωτός, "light". The KJV brackets are not in the Greek.
- **"Proving what is acceptable unto the Lord" → "testing what is well-pleasing to the Lord"** (v10) — Greek δοκιμάζοντες, a participle that continues "walk" (v8); εὐάρεστον as Col 3:20. No "try to".
- **"And have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them" → "and do not be fellow sharers in the unfruitful works of the darkness, but rather even reprove them"** (v11) — Greek συγκοινωνεῖτε (Phil 4:14); ἐλέγχετε "reprove", also v13: to show to be wrong, to bring to light. "Them" is supplied.
- **"But all things that are reproved are made manifest by the light" → "but all things being reproved by the light are manifested"** (v13) — Greek τὰ δὲ πάντα ἐλεγχόμενα ὑπὸ τοῦ φωτὸς φανεροῦται: "by the light" stands between the two verbs and can go with either. φανερόω "manifest" as Col 3:4.
- **"for whatsoever doth make manifest is light" → "for everything that is manifested is light"** (v14) — Greek τὸ φανερούμενον, the same passive form as in v13.
- **"Awake thou that sleepest … and Christ shall give thee light" → "Awake, you who sleep … and the *Mashiach* will shine upon you"** (v14) — Greek Ἔγειρε, ὁ καθεύδων; ἐπιφαύσει σοι.
- **"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools" → "Look therefore carefully how you walk, not as unwise"** (v15) — the printed order is ἀκριβῶς πῶς: "carefully" goes with "look"; ἄσοφοι, the negative of "wise".
- **"Redeeming the time" kept** (v16) — Greek ἐξαγοραζόμενοι τὸν καιρόν, "buying up", as Col 4:5.
- **"Wherefore be ye not unwise, but understanding" → "For this reason do not become senseless, but understand"** (v17) — Greek γίνεσθε ἄφρονες; συνίετε, an imperative in the printed text.
- **"wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit" → "in which is dissoluteness, but be filled in the Spirit"** (v18) — Greek ἀσωτία as Titus 1:6; πληροῦσθε ἐν πνεύματι, without the article: "in the Spirit / in spirit", or "by the Spirit". The Greek does not name with what they are to be filled (compare 3:19).
- **"Speaking to yourselves in psalms … singing and making melody in your heart" → "speaking to each other with psalms … singing and singing psalms with your heart"** (v19) — Greek ἑαυτοῖς as Col 3:16; ψάλλοντες is the verb of ψαλμός (Jas 5:13), originally "to pluck strings"; τῇ καρδίᾳ, a dative without "in" in the printed text.
- **"unto God and the Father" → "to the *Elohim* and Father"** (v20) — Greek τῷ θεῷ καὶ πατρί, one article: he who is *Elohim* and Father (1:3). ὑπὲρ πάντων is "for all things" or "for all people".
- **"Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God" → "being subject to one another in the fear of the *Mashiach*"** (v21) — the critical text reads Χριστοῦ; ὑποτασσόμενοι as Col 3:18; φόβος is "fear", not "reverence" (the verb in v33).
- **"Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands" → "Wives, to your own husbands"** (v22) — the verse has no verb (SBLGNT WH NA28; Treg adds ὑποτασσέσθωσαν, "let them be subject", RP ὑποτάσσεσθε): it is carried over from "being subject" in v21. γυναῖκες / ἄνδρες are "women / men" and "wives / husbands".
- **"For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body" → "because a husband is head of the wife as also the *Mashiach* is head of the church, he himself Savior of the body"** (v23) — Greek ἀνήρ and κεφαλή without the article; αὐτὸς σωτὴρ τοῦ σώματος, without a verb and without "his".
- **"Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands" → "But as the church is subject to the *Mashiach*, so also the wives to their husbands"** (v24) — Greek ἀλλά, "but"; the second half has no verb; "own" is not in the critical text.
- **"gave himself for it" → "delivered himself up for her"** (v25) — Greek ἑαυτὸν παρέδωκεν ὑπὲρ αὐτῆς; "church" is feminine in Greek, and the picture is of a bride (v27).
- **"That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word" → "that he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of the water in a word"** (v26) — Greek ἁγιάσῃ καθαρίσας, a verb and a participle; λουτρόν as Titus 3:5; ἐν ῥήματι, without the article: ῥῆμα is a spoken word, a saying (6:17), a different word from λόγος.
- **"That he might present it to himself a glorious church" → "that he himself might present to himself the church glorious"** (v27) — Greek ἵνα παραστήσῃ αὐτὸς ἑαυτῷ ἔνδοξον τὴν ἐκκλησίαν; παρίστημι as Col 1:22; ἄμωμος "without blemish" as 1:4.
- **"So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies" → "So ought the husbands also to love their own wives as their own bodies"** (v28) — the printed text has "also"; ἑαυτῶν, "their own", twice.
- **"but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church" → "but nourishes and cherishes it, just as also the *Mashiach* the church"** (v29) — the critical text reads Χριστός; ἐκτρέφει is the verb of 6:4; θάλπει as 1 Thess 2:7. "Flesh" is σάρξ (v31); "body" in v28, 30 is σῶμα.
- **"For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones" → "because we are members of his body"** (v30) — "of his flesh, and of his bones" is not in the critical text.
- **"For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother … and they two shall be one flesh" → "For this reason a man will leave father and mother … and the two will be one flesh"** (v31) — Greek ἀντὶ τούτου; ἄνθρωπος, "a human being"; "father" and "mother" have the article but no "his"; ἔσονται εἰς σάρκα μίαν, "will be into one flesh", the Hebrew idiom of Gen 2:24.
- **"This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church" → "This mystery is great; but I speak with regard to the *Mashiach* and to the church"** (v32) — Greek τὸ μυστήριον τοῦτο μέγα ἐστίν; μέγα is "great", not "profound"; εἰς Χριστὸν καὶ εἰς τὴν ἐκκλησίαν. The Greek has no "it refers".
- **"let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband" → "you also, one by one, let each so love his own wife as himself, and the wife, that she fear the husband"** (v33) — Greek οἱ καθʼ ἕνα ἕκαστος; ἵνα φοβῆται, the verb of φόβος "fear" (v21); "see" is supplied in the KJV.

### Notes
- **v14** — the source of the quotation is unknown (compare Isa 26:19; 60:1).
- **v31** quotes Gen 2:24.
