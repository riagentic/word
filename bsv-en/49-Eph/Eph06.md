# Ephesians 6

[1] Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is just.

[2] "**Honor your father and mother**," which is the first commandment with a promise,

[3] "**that it may be well with you and you may be long-lived on the earth**."

[4] And fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath, but nourish them in the training and admonition of the Lord.

[5] Slaves, obey your lords according to the flesh with fear and trembling, in singleness of your heart, as to ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧,

[6] not according to eye-service, as men-pleasers, but as slaves of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ doing the will of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, from the soul

[7] with good will being slaves, as to the Lord and not to men,

[8] knowing that each one, if he does anything good, this he will receive back from the Lord, whether slave or free.

[9] And lords, do the same things toward them, giving up the threatening, knowing that both their Lord and yours is in the heavens, and there is no partiality with him.

[10] For the rest, be empowered in the Lord and in the might of his strength.

[11] Put on the full armor of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that you may be able to stand against the schemings of the devil;

[12] **because our wrestling is not against blood and flesh, but against the principalities, against the authorities, against the world rulers of this darkness, against the spiritual things of evil in the heavenlies.**

[13] For this reason take up the full armor of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that you may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having worked out all things, to stand.

[14] Stand therefore, having girded your loins in truth, and having put on the breastplate of righteousness,

[15] and having shod the feet in the readiness of the gospel of peace,

[16] in all things having taken up the shield of the faith, in which you will be able to quench all the flaming arrows of the evil one;

[17] and accept the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[18] through all prayer and supplication, praying at every time in the Spirit, and unto this keeping watch in all perseverance and supplication concerning all the saints,

[19] and on behalf of me, that a word may be given to me in the opening of my mouth, in boldness to make known the mystery of the gospel,

[20] on behalf of which I am an ambassador in a chain, that in it I may speak boldly, as I ought to speak.

[21] But that you also may know the things concerning me, what I am doing, ⟦tychicus|Tychikos|Tychicus⟧ will make all things known to you, the beloved brother and faithful servant in the Lord,

[22] whom I have sent to you for this very thing, that you may know the things concerning us and that he may encourage your hearts.

[23] Peace to the brothers and love with faith from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ the Father and the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[24] Grace be with all those who love our Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ in incorruption.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (vv5, 6) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (vv23, 24) — Greek *Iēsous* transliterates Hebrew/Aramaic *Yeshua*.
- **"for this is right" → "for this is just"** (v1) — Greek δίκαιον, as Col 4:1.
- **"which is the first commandment with promise" kept** (v2) — Greek ἥτις ἐστὶν ἐντολὴ πρώτη ἐν ἐπαγγελίᾳ, "in a promise"; the Greek has no brackets.
- **"and thou mayest live long on the earth" → "and you may be long-lived on the earth"** (v3) — Greek ἔσῃ μακροχρόνιος ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς; γῆ is "earth" or "land" (Exod 20:12: the land).
- **"provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord" → "do not provoke your children to wrath, but nourish them in the training and admonition of the Lord"** (v4) — Greek παροργίζετε (the noun in 4:26); ἐκτρέφετε, the verb of 5:29, used of rearing children; παιδεία "training", νουθεσία "admonition" (Titus 3:10).
- **"Servants, be obedient to them that are your masters according to the flesh" → "Slaves, obey your lords according to the flesh"** (v5) — Greek δοῦλοι; κυρίοις, the same word as "the Lord" in v7–9; as Col 3:22. Not "earthly masters".
- **"in singleness of your heart, as unto Christ" kept** (v5) — Greek ἐν ἁπλότητι τῆς καρδίας ὑμῶν ὡς τῷ Χριστῷ; ἁπλότης "singleness" as in the sister passage Col 3:22 (elsewhere "simplicity").
- **"Not with eyeservice, as menpleasers; but as the servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart" → "not according to eye-service, as men-pleasers, but as slaves of the *Mashiach* doing the will of *Elohim*, from the soul"** (v6) — Greek κατʼ ὀφθαλμοδουλίαν, singular; ἐκ ψυχῆς, "from the soul", as Col 3:23. The printed comma puts "from the soul" with v7; it can also go with "doing the will of *Elohim*".
- **"With good will doing service" → "with good will being slaves"** (v7) — Greek δουλεύοντες, the verb of δοῦλος.
- **"whatsoever good thing any man doeth, the same shall he receive of the Lord, whether he be bond or free" → "each one, if he does anything good, this he will receive back from the Lord, whether slave or free"** (v8) — Greek ἕκαστος ἐάν τι ποιήσῃ ἀγαθόν (WH NA28; SBLGNT and Treg print ἕκαστος, ὃ ἂν ποιήσῃ, "each one, whatever good he does": 2–2, NA28 decides); κομίσεται as Col 3:25.
- **"And, ye masters, do the same things unto them, forbearing threatening: knowing that your Master also is in heaven" → "And lords, do the same things toward them, giving up the threatening, knowing that both their Lord and yours is in the heavens"** (v9) — Greek οἱ κύριοι … ὁ κύριος, one word; καὶ αὐτῶν καὶ ὑμῶν, "both theirs and yours"; ἀνιέντες, "letting go, loosening".
- **"neither is there respect of persons with him" → "there is no partiality with him"** (v9) — Greek προσωπολημψία, as Col 3:25.
- **"Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might" → "For the rest, be empowered in the Lord and in the might of his strength"** (v10) — Greek Τοῦ λοιποῦ, "for the rest" or "from now on"; "my brethren" is not in the critical text; ἐνδυναμοῦσθε (Col 1:11); κράτος and ἰσχύς as 1:19.
- **"the whole armour of God … against the wiles of the devil" → "the full armor of *Elohim* … against the schemings of the devil"** (v11) — Greek πανοπλία, the complete equipment of a heavy-armed soldier; μεθοδείας as 4:14.
- **"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood" → "because our wrestling is not against blood and flesh"** (v12) — Greek οὐκ ἔστιν ἡμῖν ἡ πάλη πρὸς αἷμα καὶ σάρκα, in this order.
- **"against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places" → "against the principalities, against the authorities, against the world rulers of this darkness, against the spiritual things of evil in the heavenlies"** (v12) — Greek ἀρχάς, ἐξουσίας (1:21; 3:10); κοσμοκράτορας, "world rulers"; "of this world" (KJV: τοῦ αἰῶνος) is not in the critical text, and the Greek has no "present". τὰ πνευματικὰ τῆς πονηρίας is a neuter adjective used as a noun ("the spiritual things / forces"); πονηρία is the noun of "evil" (v13, 16). ἐν τοῖς ἐπουρανίοις as 1:3.
- **"having done all, to stand" → "having worked out all things, to stand"** (v13) — Greek ἅπαντα κατεργασάμενοι, the verb of Phil 2:12: "having accomplished" or "having overcome"; no "firm" in the Greek.
- **"having your loins girt about with truth" → "having girded your loins in truth"** (v14) — Greek περιζωσάμενοι τὴν ὀσφὺν ὑμῶν ἐν ἀληθείᾳ; no "belt" in the Greek.
- **"And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace" → "and having shod the feet in the readiness of the gospel of peace"** (v15) — Greek ἐν ἑτοιμασίᾳ τοῦ εὐαγγελίου, a genitive; the Greek has no "given by" and no "shoes".
- **"Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked" → "in all things having taken up the shield of the faith, in which you will be able to quench all the flaming arrows of the evil one"** (v16) — Greek ἐν πᾶσιν (the KJV follows ἐπὶ πᾶσιν); βέλη, missiles; τοῦ πονηροῦ is "of the evil one" or "of evil".
- **"And take the helmet of salvation" → "and accept the helmet of salvation"** (v17) — Greek δέξασθε; τοῦ σωτηρίου, a neuter adjective used as a noun (Isa 59:17 in the Septuagint), not the usual σωτηρία.
- **"which is the word of God" kept** (v17) — Greek ὅ ἐστιν ῥῆμα θεοῦ: "which" is neuter, so it points to "the Spirit" (or to the whole phrase), not to "sword" (feminine); ῥῆμα, a spoken word, as 5:26, without the article.
- **"Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance" → "through all prayer and supplication, praying at every time in the Spirit, and unto this keeping watch in all perseverance"** (v18) — Greek διὰ πάσης προσευχῆς καὶ δεήσεως stands first and can go with what precedes or with "praying"; ἐν πνεύματι, without the article; ἀγρυπνοῦντες, "being sleepless", a different word from "be awake" (γρηγορέω, Col 4:2); προσκαρτέρησις, the noun of "persevere" (Col 4:2). No new sentence in the Greek.
- **"that utterance may be given unto me, that I may open my mouth boldly" → "that a word may be given to me in the opening of my mouth, in boldness to make known"** (v19) — Greek λόγος; ἐν ἀνοίξει τοῦ στόματός μου; ἐν παρρησίᾳ can go with "opening" or with "to make known". γνωρίσαι as 1:9; 3:3.
- **"For which I am an ambassador in bonds: that therein I may speak boldly" → "on behalf of which I am an ambassador in a chain, that in it I may speak boldly"** (v20) — Greek ὑπὲρ οὗ; ἐν ἁλύσει, singular, "a chain" (the "chains" of Col 4:18 are δεσμοί); παρρησιάσωμαι, the verb of παρρησία "boldness"; as Col 4:4.
- **"But that ye also may know my affairs, and how I do" → "But that you also may know the things concerning me, what I am doing"** (v21) — Greek τὰ κατʼ ἐμέ, τί πράσσω; as Col 4:7.
- **"faithful minister" → "faithful servant"** (v21) — Greek διάκονος.
- **"Whom I have sent unto you for the same purpose, that ye might know our affairs, and that he might comfort your hearts" → "whom I have sent to you for this very thing, that you may know the things concerning us and that he may encourage your hearts"** (v22) — the same words as Col 4:8; ἔπεμψα is a past tense written from the readers' point of view; παρακαλέσῃ "encourage".
- **"Peace be to the brethren" → "Peace to the brothers"** (v23) — the Greek has no verb.
- **"that love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity. Amen." → "who love our Lord *Yeshua* the *Mashiach* in incorruption"** (v24) — Greek ἐν ἀφθαρσίᾳ, "in incorruption" (2 Tim 1:10; the root of φθείρω, 4:22): it can go with "love", with "grace", or with "our Lord". Not "with love incorruptible". "Amen" is not in the critical text; "be" is supplied.

### Notes
- **v2-3** quotes Ex 20:12; Deut 5:16.
- **v14-17** — the armor imagery echoes Isa 11:5; 52:7; 59:17.
- **v5–9; 21–22** — close to Col 3:22–4:1 and Col 4:7–8, in part word for word.
