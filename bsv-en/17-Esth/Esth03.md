# Esther 3

[1] After these things King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧ made Haman the son of ⟦hammedatha|Hammedata|Hammedatha⟧ the ⟦agagite|Agagi|Agagite⟧ great, and lifted him up, and set his seat above all the princes who were with him.

[2] And all the king's servants who were in the king's gate were bowing and bowing down to Haman, for so the king had commanded concerning him; but ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ did not bow and did not bow down.

[3] And the king's servants who were in the king's gate said to ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧, "Why are you passing over the commandment of the king?"

[4] And it came to pass, when they spoke to him day by day and he did not listen to them, that they told Haman, to see whether the words of ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ would stand; for he had told them that he was a ⟦jew|Yehudi|Jew⟧.

[5] And Haman saw that ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ was not bowing and bowing down to him, and Haman was filled with fury.

[6] And it was contemptible in his eyes to stretch out a hand against ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧ alone, for they had told him the people of ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧; and Haman sought to destroy all the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ who were in all the kingdom of ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧, the people of ⟦mordecai|Mordechai|Mordecai⟧.

[7] In the first month, that is the month Nisan, in the twelfth year of King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧, he cast Pur, that is the lot, before Haman, from day to day and from month to the twelfth month, that is the month Adar.

[8] And Haman said to King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧, "There is one people, scattered and separated among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom, and their laws are different from every people, and the laws of the king they do not do; and for the king it is not worth it to leave them.

[9] If it is good to the king, let it be written to do away with them; and ten thousand talents of silver I will weigh into the hands of those who do the work, to bring into the king's treasuries."

[10] And the king removed his ring from his hand and gave it to Haman the son of ⟦hammedatha|Hammedata|Hammedatha⟧ the ⟦agagite|Agagi|Agagite⟧, the adversary of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧.

[11] And the king said to Haman, "The silver is given to you, and the people, to do with it as is good in your eyes."

[12] And the king's scribes were called in the first month, on the thirteenth day of it, and it was written according to all that Haman commanded, to the king's satraps and to the governors who were over each province and to the princes of each people, to each province according to its writing and to each people according to its tongue; in the name of King ⟦ahasuerus|Achashverosh|Ahasuerus⟧ it was written, and it was sealed with the king's ring.

[13] And letters were sent by the hand of the runners to all the king's provinces, to destroy, to kill, and to do away with all the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, from boy to old man, little ones and women, in one day, on the thirteenth of the twelfth month, that is the month Adar, and to plunder their spoil.

[14] A copy of the writing, to be given as law in each province, was revealed to all the peoples, that they should be ready for this day.

[15] The runners went out, hastened by the word of the king, and the law was given in ⟦susa|Shushan|Susa⟧ the citadel; and the king and Haman sat down to drink, and the city ⟦susa|Shushan|Susa⟧ was perplexed.

## Changes

- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"promote Haman … and advanced him" → "made Haman … great, and lifted him up"** (v1) — Hebrew גִּדֵּל (piel) "make great"; נִשֵּׂא "lift up". **"princes"** (v1, 12) — שָׂרִים, kept (not "officials"). **"seat"** — כִּסֵּא, of a king "throne".
- **"bowed, and reverenced Haman" → "were bowing and bowing down to Haman"** (v2, 5) — Hebrew כֹּרְעִים וּמִשְׁתַּחֲוִים, two participles: a lasting act. כָּרַע "bow, bend the knee"; הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה "bow down" (to men as to God; not "reverence"). English has one verb for both: the second is "bow down".
- **"transgressest thou" → "are you passing over"** (v3) — Hebrew עוֹבֵר, a participle of עָבַר "pass over". **"commandment"** — מִצְוָה.
- **"when they spake daily unto him" → "And it came to pass, when they spoke to him day by day"** (v4) — וַיְהִי kept. The written text (ketiv) has בְּאָמְרָם, the qere כְּאָמְרָם: both "when they spoke"; no difference in meaning. **"hearkened not" → "did not listen to"** — שָׁמַע אֶל. **"matters" → "words"** — דִּבְרֵי מָרְדֳּכַי, "the words / matters of Mordecai": his reason, that he was a Jew.
- **"full of wrath" → "filled with fury"** (v5) — Hebrew חֵמָה, as 2:1.
- **"he thought scorn to lay hands on Mordecai" → "it was contemptible in his eyes to stretch out a hand against Mordecai"** (v6) — Hebrew וַיִּבֶז בְּעֵינָיו, literally "and he despised in his eyes"; שָׁלַח יָד בְּ, as 2:21. **"wherefore Haman sought to destroy" → "and Haman sought to destroy"** — no "wherefore"; הִשְׁמִיד "destroy".
- **"they cast Pur … to the twelfth month" → "he cast Pur … from day to day and from month to the twelfth month"** (v7) — Hebrew הִפִּיל, singular, with no subject: who cast the lot is not said. פּוּר is a loan word (Akkadian *pūru*), glossed by the text itself: הוּא הַגּוֹרָל "that is the lot". The Hebrew has no "and the lot fell on": וּמֵחֹדֶשׁ לְחֹדֶשׁ שְׁנֵים־עָשָׂר, literally "and from month to month twelve" = to the twelfth month.
- **"a certain people … dispersed … diverse from all people; neither keep they the king's laws: therefore it is not for the king's profit to suffer them" → "one people … separated … different from every people, and the laws of the king they do not do; and for the king it is not worth it to leave them"** (v8) — Hebrew עַם־אֶחָד "one people"; מְפֹרָד "separated"; דָּתֵיהֶם … דָּתֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ "their laws … the laws of the king" (דָּת, a Persian loan word, as 2:8); עֹשִׂים "doing"; אֵין־שֹׁוֶה, literally "there is no being worth" (the same word as 5:13); לְהַנִּיחָם "to leave them" (let them be).
- **"If it please the king" → "If it is good to the king"** (v9) — Hebrew אִם־עַל־הַמֶּלֶךְ טוֹב, literally "if upon the king it is good". **"that they may be destroyed" → "to do away with them"** (v9; 4:7) — Hebrew לְאַבְּדָם, piel of אָבַד "perish": "make perish". **"I will pay … to the hands of those that have the charge of the business … into the king's treasuries" → "I will weigh into the hands of those who do the work … into the king's treasuries"** — Hebrew שָׁקַל "weigh"; עֹשֵׂי הַמְּלָאכָה "doers of the work"; גִּנְזֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ, גְּנָזִים a Persian loan word, "treasuries".
- **"took his ring from his hand" → "removed his ring from his hand"** (v10, 12) — Hebrew הֵסִיר "remove"; טַבַּעַת "ring" (a sealing ring, v12). **"the Jews' enemy" → "the adversary of the Jews"** — צֹרֵר, participle of צָרַר "harass, be hostile": "adversary", as צַר.
- **"to do with them as it seemeth good to thee" → "to do with it as is good in your eyes"** (v11) — Hebrew בּוֹ "with it" (singular, of הָעָם "the people", or of the silver); כַּטּוֹב בְּעֵינֶיךָ.
- **"were called on the thirteenth day of the first month, and there was written … unto the king's lieutenants, and to the governors that were over every province, and to the rulers of every people of every province according to the writing thereof" → "were called in the first month, on the thirteenth day of it … to the king's satraps and to the governors who were over each province and to the princes of each people, to each province according to its writing"** (v12) — Hebrew אֲחַשְׁדַּרְפְּנִים, a Persian loan word, "satraps"; פַּחוֹת "governors"; מְדִינָה וּמְדִינָה, עַם וָעָם, literally "province and province", "people and people" = each one; כִּכְתָבָהּ "according to its writing" (its script), כִּלְשׁוֹנוֹ "according to its tongue". "And it was sealed": נֶחְתָּם.
- **"letters were sent by posts … to destroy, to kill, and to cause to perish … both young and old, little children and women … and to take the spoil of them for a prey" → "letters were sent by the hand of the runners … to destroy, to kill, and to do away with … from boy to old man, little ones and women … and to plunder their spoil"** (v13, 15) — Hebrew סְפָרִים, literally "writings", here letters; בְּיַד הָרָצִים "by the hand of the runners" (the king's couriers and guard); מִנַּעַר וְעַד־זָקֵן; טַף "little ones"; וּשְׁלָלָם לָבוֹז, literally "and their spoil, to plunder". The three verbs as the Hebrew has them: הִשְׁמִיד, הָרַג, אִבֵּד.
- **"The copy of the writing for a commandment to be given in every province was published unto all people" → "A copy of the writing, to be given as law in each province, was revealed to all the peoples"** (v14) — Hebrew פַּתְשֶׁגֶן, a Persian loan word, "copy"; כְּתָב "writing", as v12; דָּת "law"; גָּלוּי "revealed, uncovered"; no verb "was": the clause is a heading, "was" is supplied.
- **"The posts went out, being hastened by the king's commandment, and the decree was given … but the city Shushan was perplexed" → "The runners went out, hastened by the word of the king, and the law was given … and the city Shushan was perplexed"** (v15) — Hebrew דְּחוּפִים, literally "pushed"; דְּבַר הַמֶּלֶךְ "the word of the king"; דָּת "law"; נָבוֹכָה "be perplexed, confused"; "and", not "but".

### Notes
- **Agagite** (v1) — descendant of Agag, Amalekite king Saul failed to destroy (1 Sam 15). Haman vs. Mordecai enactment of ancient Amalek vs Israel conflict (Ex 17, Deut 25:17-19).
- **Mordecai's refusal to bow** — Persian prostration standard; some see Jewish prohibition on prostrating to a man as motive. Others: unwillingness to honor Amalekite. The text gives only "he had told them that he was a Jew" (v4).
- **Pur** — Akkadian *pūru*, "lot." Festival of Purim ("lots") derives its name (9:24-26).
- **Eleven months gap** — the lot reaches the twelfth month (Adar); Haman has eleven months to organize. Jews have eleven months to react.
- **10,000 talents of silver** — enormous sum; Persian annual revenue ~14,000 talents (Herodotus).
- **Thirteenth day of first month** — day before Passover (14 Nisan).
- **"Destroy, kill, do away with"** — triple verb emphasizes the scope. Boy, old man, little ones, women: total extermination.
- **Plunder-clause** — material incentive for participants.
- **King and Haman drink; city perplexed** — ironic juxtaposition.
