# John 2

[1] And on the third day a wedding took place in ⟦cana|Qanah|Cana⟧ of ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧, and the mother of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ was there;

[2] and ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ also was called, and his disciples, to the wedding.

[3] And when wine came short, the mother of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to him, "They have no wine."

[4] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to her, "What have I to do with you, woman? My hour has not yet come."

[5] His mother says to the servants, "Whatever he says to you, do."

[6] Now there were six stone water jars set there, according to the purification of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧, holding two or three measures each.

[7] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ says to them, "Fill the water jars with water." And they filled them up to the top.

[8] And he says to them, "Draw now and bring it to the master of the feast." And they brought it.

[9] And when the master of the feast tasted the water that had become wine, and did not know from where it was — but the servants knew, those who had drawn the water — the master of the feast calls the bridegroom

[10] and says to him, "Every man sets out the good wine first, and when they have become drunk, the lesser; you have kept the good wine until now."

[11] This beginning of the signs ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ did in ⟦cana|Qanah|Cana⟧ of ⟦galilee|Galil|Galilee⟧, and he manifested his glory, and his disciples believed in him.

[12] After this he went down to ⟦capernaum|Kfar Nachum|Capernaum⟧, he and his mother and the brothers and his disciples, and there they remained not many days.

[13] And the ⟦pesach|Pesach|Passover⟧ of the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ was near, and ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ went up to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[14] And he found in the temple those selling oxen and sheep and doves, and the changers of coin sitting,

[15] and having made a whip out of cords, he cast all out of the temple, both the sheep and the oxen, and he poured out the coins of the money-changers and overturned the tables,

[16] and to those selling the doves he said, "Take these things away from here; do not make the house of my Father a house of trade."

[17] His disciples remembered that it is written, "**The zeal of your house will eat me up**."

[18] So the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ answered and said to him, "What sign do you show us, seeing that you do these things?"

[19] ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ answered and said to them, "**Destroy this sanctuary, and in three days I will raise it**."

[20] So the ⟦jews|Yehudim|Jews⟧ said, "In forty-six years this sanctuary was built, and you will raise it in three days?"

[21] But that one was speaking concerning the sanctuary of his body.

[22] So when he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they believed the scripture and the word which ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said.

[23] Now when he was in ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ at the ⟦pesach|Pesach|Passover⟧, at the feast, many believed in his name, beholding his signs which he was doing;

[24] but ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ himself was not entrusting himself to them, because he knew all men

[25] and because he had no need that anyone should testify concerning man, for he himself knew what was in man.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"passover" → "*Pesach*"** (v13, 23) — Greek τὸ πάσχα, the Aramaic form of Hebrew פֶּסַח.
- **"there was a marriage" → "a wedding took place"; "Cana of Galilee" kept** (v1, 11) — Greek γάμος ἐγένετο; ἐν Κανὰ τῆς Γαλιλαίας, a genitive.
- **"was called" kept** (v2) — Greek ἐκλήθη, "was called, invited"; "and his disciples" stands after the verb.
- **"when they wanted wine" → "when wine came short"** (v3) — Greek ὑστερήσαντος οἴνου, without the article; ὑστερέω "come short", as elsewhere.
- **"saith" → "says"** (v3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10) — the Greek present in narrative is kept as a present.
- **"Woman, what have I to do with thee?" → "What have I to do with you, woman?"** (v4) — Greek Τί ἐμοὶ καὶ σοί, γύναι; word for word "What to me and to you, woman?", the Hebrew idiom מַה־לִּי וָלָךְ (Judg 11:12; 1 Kgs 17:18; Mark 1:24): "what is there between me and you?" It can also be taken as "what is that to me and to you?" γύναι is the ordinary address to a woman (4:21; 19:26). The Greek has no "this".
- **"Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it" → "Whatever he says to you, do"** (v5) — Ὅ τι Treg NA28 RP ] Ὅτι WH (the same letters, divided differently).
- **"after the manner of the purifying of the Jews" → "according to the purification of the *Yehudim*"** (v6) — Greek κατὰ τὸν καθαρισμὸν τῶν Ἰουδαίων; the Greek has no "rites" and no adjective "Jewish". κείμεναι is "lying, set".
- **"two or three firkins apiece" → "two or three measures each"** (v6) — Greek μετρητὰς δύο ἢ τρεῖς: a μετρητής is a liquid measure of about 39 litres; "gallons" is not in the Greek.
- **"to the brim" → "to the top"** (v7) — Greek ἕως ἄνω, "up to above".
- **"Draw out now, and bear" → "Draw now and bring it"; "And they bare it" → "And they brought it"** (v8) — Greek Ἀντλήσατε νῦν καὶ φέρετε … οἱ δὲ ἤνεγκαν: one verb, φέρω; the Greek has no object, "it" is supplied twice.
- **"governor of the feast" → "master of the feast"** (v8, 9) — Greek ἀρχιτρίκλινος, the one in charge of the dining room (τρίκλινος, a room of three couches).
- **"the water that was made wine" → "the water that had become wine"** (v9) — Greek γεγενημένον, from γίνομαι. The parentheses of the KJV are not in the Greek; "called" (φωνεῖ) is a present.
- **"when men have well drunk, then that which is worse" → "when they have become drunk, the lesser"** (v10) — Greek ὅταν μεθυσθῶσιν, from μεθύσκω "make drunk" (μέθη "drunkenness"); τὸν ἐλάσσω "the lesser". μεθυσθῶσιν WH NA28 ] + τότε "then" Treg RP. The Greek has no "but" before "you have kept".
- **"This beginning of miracles" → "This beginning of the signs"** (v11) — Greek ταύτην ἐποίησεν ἀρχὴν τῶν σημείων: σημεῖον is "sign" throughout (v18, 23); ἀρχή is "beginning", as 1:1. ἐποίησεν WH Treg NA28 ] + τὴν RP. "Manifested" is ἐφανέρωσεν.
- **"his brethren" → "the brothers"** (v12) — ἀδελφοί SBLGNT WH Treg ] + αὐτοῦ "his brothers" NA28 RP; the "his" after "disciples" can cover both nouns.
- **"they continued there" → "there they remained"** (v12) — Greek ἔμειναν, the verb μένω ("abide" of persons, 1:38–39), here of a stay in a place: "remain".
- **"was at hand" → "was near"** (v13) — Greek ἐγγύς.
- **"changers of money" → "changers of coin" (v14) and "money-changers" (v15)** — two Greek words: κερματιστάς (from κέρμα, "small coin") and κολλυβιστῶν (from κόλλυβος, the fee for changing).
- **"doves" kept** (v14, 16) — Greek περιστεράς, the word of 1:32.
- **"a scourge of small cords" → "a whip out of cords"** (v15) — Greek φραγέλλιον ἐκ σχοινίων, the Latin *flagellum*.
- **"he drove them all out of the temple, and the sheep, and the oxen" → "he cast all out of the temple, both the sheep and the oxen"** (v15) — Greek πάντας ἐξέβαλεν … τά τε πρόβατα καὶ τοὺς βόας. "All" (πάντας) is masculine: it can be the sellers together with the animals, or the animals alone ("all, namely the sheep and the oxen"; "oxen" is masculine). ἐκβάλλω is "cast out", as in Mark.
- **"the changers' money" → "the coins of the money-changers"** (v15) — τὰ κέρματα SBLGNT WH Treg ] τὸ κέρμα "the coin" NA28 RP. "Overturned": ἀνέτρεψεν WH NA28 ] ἀνέστρεψεν Treg RP, the same meaning.
- **"Take these things hence" → "Take these things away from here"** (v16) — Greek Ἄρατε, αἴρω "take away" (1:29). No "and" joins the two commands.
- **"an house of merchandise" → "a house of trade"** (v16) — Greek οἶκον ἐμπορίου, "a house of a market, of trade".
- **"it was written" → "it is written"** (v17) — Greek γεγραμμένον ἐστίν. ἐμνήσθησαν WH Treg NA28 ] + δέ RP.
- **"The zeal of thine house hath eaten me up" → "The zeal of your house will eat me up"** (v17) — Greek Ὁ ζῆλος τοῦ οἴκου σου καταφάγεταί με: a plain genitive ("zeal for your house" is the sense), and a future in all the editions compared; the KJV has a past, as the Hebrew of Ps 69:9.
- **"Then answered the Jews" → "So the *Yehudim* answered"** (v18, 20, 22) — Greek οὖν, "so" in narrative.
- **"seeing that thou doest these things" kept** (v18) — Greek ὅτι ταῦτα ποιεῖς: ὅτι is "that, because, seeing that"; the Greek has no "for doing".
- **"Destroy this temple" → "Destroy this sanctuary"** (v19) — Greek Λύσατε, from λύω "loose, undo" (1 John 3:8 "undo"); of a building "break up, pull down". "Sanctuary" here and in v20–21 is ναός, the temple building itself; "temple" in v14–15 is ἱερόν, the whole temple precinct.
- **"I will raise it up" → "I will raise it"** (v19, 20) — Greek ἐγερῶ, ἐγερεῖς: ἐγείρω "raise", the verb of v22 ("he was raised from the dead").
- **"Forty and six years was this temple in building" → "In forty-six years this sanctuary was built"** (v20) — Greek Τεσσεράκοντα καὶ ἓξ ἔτεσιν οἰκοδομήθη, an aorist passive with a dative of time: the building is looked at as a whole, though it was not finished.
- **"But he spake" → "But that one was speaking"** (v21) — Greek ἐκεῖνος δὲ ἔλεγεν, an imperfect.
- **"was risen" → "was raised"** (v22) — Greek ἠγέρθη, passive. "That he had said this" is ὅτι τοῦτο ἔλεγεν, the imperfect of v21; "believed the scripture" is a dative (τῇ γραφῇ), not εἰς "in" as in v11, 23.
- **"in the feast day" → "at the *Pesach*, at the feast"** (v23) — Greek ἐν τῷ πάσχα ἐν τῇ ἑορτῇ, two phrases.
- **"when they saw the miracles" → "beholding his signs"** (v23) — Greek θεωροῦντες αὐτοῦ τὰ σημεῖα; θεωρέω "behold".
- **"did not commit himself unto them" → "was not entrusting himself to them"** (v24) — Greek οὐκ ἐπίστευεν αὐτὸν αὐτοῖς, an imperfect: the verb of "believed" in v23 (πιστεύω), here "entrust". αὐτόν Treg NA28 ] αὑτόν WH; ἑαυτόν RP: the letters are the same in the old manuscripts, and the sense is "himself". "All men" is πάντας, masculine.
- **"needed not that any should testify of man" → "had no need that anyone should testify concerning man"** (v25) — Greek ἵνα τις μαρτυρήσῃ περὶ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου … ἐν τῷ ἀνθρώπῳ: "the man" with the article, man as such (or the man he was dealing with).

### Notes
- **v17** quotes Ps 69:9 (Septuagint 68:10).
- **v20** — forty-six years from the beginning of Herod's rebuilding (c. 20 BC).
