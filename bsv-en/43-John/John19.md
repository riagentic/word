## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — systematic name transliteration.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v7) — Hebrew divine name preserved.
- **"Golgotha"** (v17) — Aramaic גֻּלְגֹּלְתָּא *Gulgolta* ("skull"); the Greek keeps the Semitic name as Γολγοθα and explains it, Κρανίου Τόπος "Place of a Skull"; "Calvary" (KJV Luke 23:33) is the Latin equivalent, not used here.
- **"Gabbatha"** (v13) — Aramaic גַּבְּתָא *Gabbatha* ("elevated place, ridge"); the Greek keeps the name as Γαββαθα. Λιθόστρωτον, "Stone Pavement" (literally "paved with stone"), is the Greek name of the place, not a translation of Gabbatha.
- **"in the Hebrew" → "in Hebrew"** (v13, 17, 20) — Greek Ἑβραϊστί, "in Hebrew", the language of the Hebrews; the two names are Aramaic in form, and the word may cover Aramaic.
- **"scourged"** (v1) — Greek ἐμαστίγωσεν; the Greek says that Pilatus scourged him, as it says in v19 that he wrote the title.
- **"platted a crown of thorns" → "having plaited a crown out of thorns"** (v2, 5) — Greek στέφανος, a wreath; v5 has the adjective ἀκάνθινος, "thorny".
- **"a purple robe" → "a purple garment"** (v2, 5) — Greek ἱμάτιον, the word of v23.
- **"And said … and they smote him with their hands" → "and they kept coming to him and saying … and they kept giving him slaps"** (v3) — Greek imperfects, ἤρχοντο πρὸς αὐτὸν καὶ ἔλεγον … ἐδίδοσαν; "they kept coming to him" (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, NA28) is lacking in the Byzantine text. ῥαπίσματα, as 18:22.
- **"I find no fault in him" → "I find no cause in him"** (v4, 6) — Greek αἰτία, a cause, a ground for a charge (18:38).
- **"saith" → "says"** (v4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 14, 15, 26, 27, 28) — the Greek present tense in narration is kept.
- **"And Pilate saith unto them, Behold the man!" → "And he says to them, 'Behold, the man!'"** (v5) — the Greek names no speaker; the speaker is Pilatus (v4). Greek Ἰδοὺ ὁ ἄνθρωπος *Idou ho anthrōpos*; the Latin *Ecce homo* (Vulgate) is the traditional form of the words.
- **"chief priests" → "high priests"** (v6, 15, 21) — Greek ἀρχιερεῖς.
- **"they cried out" → "they shouted"** (v6, 12, 15) — Greek κραυγάζω.
- **"Crucify him, crucify him" → "Crucify, crucify"** (v6) — Greek Σταύρωσον σταύρωσον, without object (the Byzantine text adds αὐτόν "him"); the same with "crucify" in Pilatus's answer.
- **"by our law" → "according to the law"** (v7) — "our" (Byzantine ἡμῶν) is not in the critical text.
- **"the Son of God" → "Son of God"** (v7) — Greek υἱὸν θεοῦ, without article: "Son of God" or "a son of God".
- **"that saying" → "this word"** (v8) — Greek λόγος.
- **"judgment hall" → "praetorium"** (v9) — see 18:28.
- **"power" → "authority"** (v10, 11) — Greek ἐξουσία. In v10 the critical text has "release" before "crucify"; the Byzantine text has the reverse order.
- **"no power at all against me, except it were given thee" → "no authority against me at all, if it had not been given to you"** (v11) — Greek κατ' ἐμοῦ "against me"; ἦν δεδομένον is neuter: what was given is the having of authority, not "authority" itself (a feminine word).
- **"he that delivered me unto thee hath the greater sin" → "the one who delivered me up to you has greater sin"** (v11) — Greek ὁ παραδούς, aorist (Westcott-Hort, NA28, SBLGNT; Tregelles and the Byzantine text have the present παραδιδούς, "the one delivering me up"); μείζονα ἁμαρτίαν has no article.
- **"And from thenceforth" → "From this"** (v12) — Greek ἐκ τούτου: "from this time" or "because of this".
- **"speaketh against Caesar" → "contradicts Caesar"** (v12) — Greek ἀντιλέγει.
- **"sat down in the judgment seat" → "sat down on a judgment seat"** (v13) — Greek ἐκάθισεν ἐπὶ βήματος, without article. ἐκάθισεν can also be transitive, "seated him"; the usual sense "sat down" is given.
- **"the preparation of the passover"** (v14, 31, 42) — Greek παρασκευή, "preparation", the name of the day before a Shabbat (Mark 15:42); "Preparation of the Pesach" is then the Friday of Passover week, or it is the day on which the Passover was prepared.
- **"Behold your King!" → "See, your King!"** (v14; also v4, 26, 27) — Greek ἴδε; ἰδού "behold" stands in v5.
- **"But they cried out, Away with him, away with him" → "So those shouted, 'Take away, take away'"** (v15) — Greek ἐκραύγασαν οὖν ἐκεῖνοι (critical text; Byzantine οἱ δὲ ἐκραύγασαν); Ἆρον ἆρον, the imperative of αἴρω "take away", without object (the verb of v31, 38).
- **"And they took Jesus, and led him away" → "So they received Yeshua"** (v16) — Greek παρέλαβον, the counterpart of "delivered up" (as 1:11); "and led him away" (Byzantine καὶ ἤγαγον) is not in the critical text.
- **"bearing his cross" → "bearing the cross for himself"** (v17) — Greek αὑτῷ (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, SBLGNT) or ἑαυτῷ (NA28), a dative, "for himself" (it can also be read αὐτῷ, "bearing the cross himself"); the Byzantine text has τὸν σταυρὸν αὐτοῦ, "his cross".
- **"a place called the place of a skull" → "what is called Place of a Skull"** (v17) — Greek τὸν λεγόμενον Κρανίου Τόπον.
- **"on either side one" → "on this side and on that"** (v18) — Greek ἐντεῦθεν καὶ ἐντεῦθεν, literally "from here and from here".
- **"title"** (v19, 20) — Greek τίτλος, the Latin word *titulus*, a board with an inscription. "And the writing was" → "and there was written" (ἦν γεγραμμένον).
- **"Jesus of Nazareth" → "Yeshua the Nazorean"** (v19) — Greek Ἰησοῦς ὁ Ναζωραῖος (see 18:5).
- **"in Hebrew, and Greek, and Latin" → "in Hebrew, in Latin, in Greek"** (v20) — the order of the critical text (Ἑβραϊστί, Ῥωμαϊστί, Ἑλληνιστί; the Byzantine text has Greek before Latin); Ῥωμαϊστί is literally "in Roman".
- **"Then said" → "were saying"; "that he said" → "that that one said"** (v21) — Greek ἔλεγον, an imperfect; ἐκεῖνος.
- **"coat" → "tunic"** (v23) — Greek χιτών, the garment worn next to the skin. "also his coat" → "and the tunic": the Greek adds it to "took" without a further verb. "woven from the top throughout": ἐκ τῶν ἄνωθεν ὑφαντὸς δι' ὅλου.
- **"Let us not rend it, but cast lots for it" → "Let us not tear it, but let us draw lots for it"** (v24) — Greek λάχωμεν (λαγχάνω, "obtain by lot", here "decide by lot"), a different word from ἔβαλον κλῆρον "they cast a lot" in the quotation.
- **"They parted my raiment among them, and for my vesture they did cast lots" → "They divided my garments among themselves, and upon my clothing they cast a lot"** (v24) — the Greek as printed (it agrees with the Septuagint of Ps 22:18, LXX 21:19): ἱμάτια "garments", ἱματισμός "clothing", κλῆρον singular. "which says" (ἡ λέγουσα) is in Tregelles, NA28, SBLGNT and the Byzantine text; Westcott-Hort omit it.
- **"These things therefore the soldiers did. Now there stood" → "So then the soldiers did these things. But there were standing"** (v24–25) — Greek οἱ μὲν οὖν στρατιῶται … εἱστήκεισαν δέ: the soldiers on the one side, the women on the other.
- **"Mary the wife of Cleophas" → "Miryam the one of Klopas"** (v25) — Greek Μαρία ἡ τοῦ Κλωπᾶ, with no word for "wife": wife (the usual understanding), daughter or mother of Klopas. The Greek list can be read as four women (his mother; her sister; Miryam of Klopas; Miryam the Magdalene) or as three (the sister being Miryam of Klopas); the punctuation leaves it open.
- **"Mary Magdalene" → "Miryam the Magdalene"** (v25) — Greek ἡ Μαγδαληνή, "the woman of Magdala".
- **"his mother"** (v26) — Greek τὴν μητέρα … τῇ μητρί, "the mother", the article standing for the possessive (the Byzantine text adds αὐτοῦ "his" the second time).
- **"unto his own home" → "to his own"** (v27) — Greek εἰς τὰ ἴδια, "to his own things" (as 1:11; 16:32); "home" is not in the Greek.
- **"that all things were now accomplished, that the scripture might be fulfilled" → "that all things have now been finished, that the Scripture might be perfected"** (v28) — Greek τετέλεσται (τελέω "finish", as in v30) and τελειωθῇ (τελειόω "perfect, bring to its end"), not πληρωθῇ "be fulfilled" as in v24, 36. The clause "that the Scripture might be perfected" can go with what stands before it or with "says, 'I thirst'" (cf. Ps 69:21; 22:15); the Greek order is kept and the brackets are dropped.
- **"vinegar"** (v29, 30) — Greek ὄξος, wine vinegar, the sour wine drunk by soldiers and labourers. "a sponge full of the vinegar" follows the critical text (σπόγγον οὖν μεστὸν τοῦ ὄξους; Byzantine "and they, having filled a sponge with vinegar"). "put it upon hyssop" → "having put … around hyssop" (ὑσσώπῳ περιθέντες).
- **"It is finished" — retained** (v30) — Greek τετέλεσται *tetelestai*, a perfect passive: "it has been finished, brought to its end" (also used of debts paid in full).
- **"gave up the ghost" → "delivered up the spirit"** (v30) — Greek παρέδωκεν τὸ πνεῦμα: the verb παραδίδωμι of v11, 16; "the spirit", not "his".
- **"should not remain upon the cross" → "might not remain on the cross"** (v31) — Greek μείνῃ, the verb μένω ("abide" of persons), here of bodies: "remain".
- **"for that sabbath day was an high day" → "for the day of that Shabbat was great"** (v31) — Greek ἦν γὰρ μεγάλη ἡ ἡμέρα ἐκείνου τοῦ σαββάτου. The brackets are replaced by dashes.
- **"pierced his side" → "stabbed his side"** (v34) — Greek ἔνυξεν (νύσσω "prick, stab"), a different verb from ἐξεκέντησαν "pierced" in v37.
- **"And he that saw it bare record, and his record is true: and he knoweth that he saith true" → "And the one who has seen has testified, and his testimony is true, and that one knows that he says true things"** (v35) — Greek perfects ἑωρακώς, μεμαρτύρηκεν; "true" is first ἀληθινή, then ἀληθῆ (neuter plural); ἐκεῖνος "that one" is the one who has seen (others refer it to the Lord). "that you may believe": πιστεύητε, present (Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, SBLGNT; NA28 and the Byzantine text have the aorist πιστεύσητε).
- **"A bone of him shall not be broken" → "A bone of him shall not be crushed"** (v36) — Greek συντριβήσεται (συντρίβω "crush, shatter"), a different verb from κατάγνυμι "break" in v31–33. The Greek as printed; cf. Ex 12:46; Num 9:12; Ps 34:20.
- **"They shall look on him whom they pierced"** (v37) — the Greek as printed, Ὄψονται εἰς ὃν ἐξεκέντησαν; cf. Zech 12:10 (the Hebrew; the Septuagint differs). "another": ἑτέρα, "a different" Scripture.
- **"but secretly for fear of the Jews" → "but a hidden one because of the fear of the Jews"** (v38) — Greek κεκρυμμένος, a perfect passive participle, "hidden"; τὸν φόβον τῶν Ἰουδαίων is a plain genitive. "gave him leave" → "permitted it": the Greek has no object. "took the body of Jesus" → "took away his body" (αὐτοῦ, critical text).
- **"which at the first came to Jesus by night" → "the one who came to him by night at the first"** (v39) — "him" (αὐτόν) in the critical text; the Byzantine text has "Jesus".
- **"about an hundred pound weight"** (v39) — Greek ὡς λίτρας ἑκατόν, about a hundred Roman pounds (*librae*, about 327 g each: some 33 kg). "mixture": μίγμα (Tregelles, NA28, SBLGNT, Byzantine); Westcott-Hort read ἕλιγμα, "a roll".
- **"wound it in linen clothes" → "bound it with linen cloths"** (v40) — Greek ἔδησαν, the verb "bind" of 18:12, 24. "as the manner of the Jews is to bury" → "as is the custom for the Jews to prepare for burial" (ἐνταφιάζειν).
- **"sepulchre" → "tomb"** (v41, 42) — Greek μνημεῖον.
- **"the Jews' preparation day" → "the Preparation of the Jews"** (v42) — Greek τὴν παρασκευὴν τῶν Ἰουδαίων; "day" is not in the Greek.
- **Archaic pronouns and verb forms** modernized throughout.

### Notes
- **v24** quotes Ps 22:18.
- **v36** quotes Ex 12:46; Ps 34:20 (Passover lamb).
- **v37** quotes Zech 12:10.
