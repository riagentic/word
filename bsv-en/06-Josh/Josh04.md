## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **The titles of the ark** — kept as the Hebrew varies them: "the ark of Yahweh your Elohim" (v5), "the ark of the covenant of Yahweh" (v7, 18), "the ark of the covenant" (v9), "the ark" (v10), "the ark of Yahweh" (v11), "the ark of the testimony" (v16; הָעֵדוּת, the name used in Exodus and Numbers, only here in Joshua).
- **The repetitions** — the account tells the same things more than once (the twelve men, v2, 4; the stones, v3, 5, 8, 9, 20; the sons' question, v6, 21; the people's passing over, v1, 10, 11) and each telling is kept in its own words.
- **"when all the people were clean passed over" → "when all the nation had come to an end of passing over"** (v1, 11) — Hebrew תַּמּוּ … לַעֲבוֹר, the verb of 3:16, 17 (so also v10, "came to an end"); הַגּוֹי "the nation" in v1, הָעָם "the people" in v11. עָבַר "pass over" through the chapter; הֶעֱבִיר "make pass over" (v3, 8).
- **"Take you twelve men out of the people, out of every tribe a man" → "Take for yourselves from the people twelve men, one man from each tribe"** (v2, 4) — "take" and "command" (v3) are plural, though Joshua is addressed; אִישׁ־אֶחָד אִישׁ־אֶחָד מִשָּׁבֶט, literally "one man, one man from a tribe".
- **"Take you hence out of the midst of Jordan, out of the place where the priests' feet stood firm, twelve stones" → "Lift up for yourselves from here, from the midst of the Jordan, from the standing place of the feet of the priests, firmly, twelve stones"** (v3) — Hebrew שְׂאוּ, from נָשָׂא "lift up, carry" (so v8); מַצַּב "standing place" (so v9), from נָצַב "stand"; הָכִין, an infinitive, stands after it as in 3:17 ("stood … firmly"); it can also be read as "prepare": "make ready twelve stones". **"ye shall carry them over with you, and leave them in the lodging place" → "make them pass over with you and set them down in the lodging place"** (v3, 8) — Hebrew הִנִּיחַ "set down, deposit"; מָלוֹן, from לוּן "lodge".
- **"whom he had prepared"** (v4) — Hebrew הֵכִין "make ready, appoint", the verb of 1:11.
- **"take you up every man of you a stone upon his shoulder" → "raise for yourselves, each man, one stone on his shoulder"** (v5) — Hebrew הָרִימוּ "raise, lift high", another verb than "lift up" in v3, 8; אֶבֶן אַחַת "one stone". **"into the midst of Jordan" → "to the midst of the Jordan"** — Hebrew אֶל־תּוֹךְ.
- **"when your children ask their fathers in time to come, saying, What mean ye by these stones?" → "When your sons ask tomorrow, saying, 'What are these stones to you?'"** (v6, 21) — Hebrew בְּנֵיכֶם "your sons"; מָחָר "tomorrow", also of any later day (Exod 13:14; Deut 6:20); מָה הָאֲבָנִים הָאֵלֶּה לָכֶם, with no verb. "Their fathers" stands only in v21.
- **"Then ye shall answer them, That the waters of Jordan were cut off before the ark" → "then you shall say to them that the waters of the Jordan were cut off from before the ark"** (v7) — Hebrew וַאֲמַרְתֶּם לָהֶם אֲשֶׁר "you shall say to them that"; נִכְרְתוּ "were cut off", twice (3:13, 16); מִפְּנֵי "from the face of". **"when it passed over Jordan" → "when it passed over in the Jordan"** — Hebrew בְּעָבְרוֹ בַּיַּרְדֵּן.
- **"a memorial unto the children of Israel for ever"** (v7) — Hebrew זִכָּרוֹן, from זָכַר "remember"; עַד־עוֹלָם, literally "to the age": to time out of sight.
- **"took up twelve stones … and carried them over with them unto the place where they lodged, and laid them down there" → "lifted up twelve stones … made them pass over with them to the lodging place and set them down there"** (v8) — as v3. **"as the LORD spake unto Joshua"** — Hebrew דִּבֶּר.
- **"And Joshua set up twelve stones in the midst of Jordan, in the place where the feet of the priests … stood: and they are there unto this day" → "And twelve stones Joshua raised up in the midst of the Jordan, under the standing place of the feet of the priests …; and they have been there to this day"** (v9, 20) — Hebrew הֵקִים, literally "make stand". The object stands first and has no article: "twelve stones", not "the twelve stones"; so the verse tells of a second set, in the river bed. תַּחַת is "under" or "in the place of".
- **"For the priests which bare the ark stood … until every thing was finished" → "And the priests, the carriers of the ark, were standing … until all the word came to an end"** (v10) — Hebrew עֹמְדִים, a participle; עַד תֹּם כָּל־הַדָּבָר: דָּבָר is "word" or "matter". **"hasted"** → "hastened" — Hebrew מִהַר.
- **"the ark of the LORD passed over, and the priests, in the presence of the people" → "the ark of Yahweh passed over, and the priests, before the people"** (v11) — Hebrew לִפְנֵי הָעָם, "before the people": in front of them, or before their eyes.
- **"passed over armed" → "passed over in battle array"** (v12) — Hebrew חֲמֻשִׁים, as 1:14; the meaning is uncertain. **"as Moses spake unto them"** — Num 32:20–27.
- **"About forty thousand prepared for war passed over before the LORD unto battle" → "About forty thousand armed for the host passed over before Yahweh to the war"** (v13) — Hebrew חֲלוּצֵי הַצָּבָא (Num 31:5; 32:27); מִלְחָמָה "war". **"the plains of Jericho"** — Hebrew עַרְבוֹת, the dry steppe of the Jordan valley, the word of "the Arabah" and of "the plains of Moab".
- **"magnified Joshua in the sight of all Israel" → "made Joshua great in the eyes of all Israel"** (v14) — Hebrew גִּדַּל, as 3:7. **"they feared him, as they feared Moses"** kept — Hebrew יָרֵא "fear".
- **"that they come up out of Jordan" → "and let them come up from the Jordan"** (v16) — Hebrew וְיַעֲלוּ, a wish or command in the third person.
- **"when the priests … were come up out of the midst of Jordan, and the soles of the priests' feet were lifted up unto the dry land" → "as the priests … came up from the midst of the Jordan — the soles of the feet of the priests were drawn out to the dry ground —"** (v18) — the reading tradition (qere) כַּעֲלוֹת "as … came up" is translated; the written form (ketiv) is בַּעֲלוֹת "when … came up". נִתְּקוּ, from נָתַק "pull away, tear loose": the feet are drawn out of the river bed; the clause has no "and". חָרָבָה "dry ground" (3:17).
- **"flowed over all his banks, as they did before" → "went as yesterday and the day before over all its banks"** (v18) — Hebrew וַיֵּלְכוּ "and they went"; כִתְמוֹל־שִׁלְשׁוֹם (3:4): as before.
- **"on the tenth day of the first month, and encamped in Gilgal, in the east border of Jericho" → "on the tenth of the first month, and they camped at Gilgal, at the edge of the east of Jericho"** (v19) — Hebrew בֶּעָשׂוֹר "on the tenth", with no "day" (Exod 12:3); בַּגִּלְגָּל, with the article, "the Gilgal" (so v20; the name is explained in 5:9); בִּקְצֵה מִזְרַח יְרִיחוֹ, literally "at the end of the east of Jericho".
- **"those twelve stones, which they took out of Jordan, did Joshua pitch in Gilgal" → "these twelve stones that they had taken from the Jordan Joshua raised up at Gilgal"** (v20) — Hebrew הָאֵלֶּה "these"; הֵקִים, as v9.
- **"Then ye shall let your children know, saying, Israel came over this Jordan on dry land" → "then you shall make known to your sons, saying, 'On the dry land Israel passed over this Jordan'"** (v22) — Hebrew הוֹדַעְתֶּם, the causative of "know"; בַּיַּבָּשָׁה "on the dry land" stands first (Exod 14:22, 29); another word than "dry ground" in 3:17; 4:18.
- **"For the LORD your God dried up" → "in that Yahweh your Elohim dried up"** (v23) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר, "that, in that, because", twice in the verse ("which he dried up"). **"until ye were passed over"** — Hebrew עַד־עָבְרְכֶם. "From before us … until we had passed over": the speaker counts himself among those who came out of Egypt.
- **"That all the people of the earth might know the hand of the LORD, that it is mighty: that ye might fear the LORD your God for ever" → "in order that all the peoples of the earth may know the hand of Yahweh, that it is strong; so that you may fear Yahweh your Elohim all the days"** (v24) — Hebrew לְמַעַן twice, first with an infinitive ("in order that … may know", literally "for the sake of the knowing of"), then with a finite verb ("so that"); עַמֵּי "peoples", plural; חֲזָקָה "strong" (Exod 6:1); כָּל־הַיָּמִים "all the days". יְרָאתֶם is pointed as a perfect, "you have feared"; with other vowels the same consonants give "that they may fear" (the peoples), which is often read (cited from memory); the printed text is kept, with its "you".

### Notes
- **Twelve-stone memorial** — the most prominent aspect of the crossing. The stones are meant to prompt intergenerational questions (v6, 21). Central to Deuteronomic pedagogy: catechesis by monument.
- **Two stone piles** — (1) twelve stones *from* the Jordan, set at Gilgal (v8, 20); (2) twelve stones placed *in* the Jordan bed where the priests stood (v9). Two witnesses to the event.
- **Dated crossing** (v19) — 10th day of 1st month — exactly four days before Passover. Matches timing for the firstborn-sparing Passover lamb to be selected (Ex 12:3). Passover will be celebrated at Gilgal.
- **"Dried up like the Red Sea"** (v23) — explicit parallel. The generation that crossed the Red Sea mostly died in the wilderness; their children cross the Jordan. Exodus begins and ends with miraculous water-crossings.
- **Joshua made great** (v14) — fulfillment of 3:7. The succession is validated by repeating the exodus-miracle.
- **"That all the peoples of the earth may know"** (v24) — missional dimension. The crossing is not merely for Israel's benefit; it has international witness-value.
