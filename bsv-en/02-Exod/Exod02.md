# Exodus 2

[1] And a man from the house of Levi went and took the daughter of Levi.

[2] And the woman conceived and bore a son, and she saw him, that he was good, and she hid him three months.

[3] And she could no longer hide him, and she took for him an ark of papyrus and coated it with bitumen and with pitch, and she put the child in it and put it in the reeds on the bank of the ⟦nile|Yeor|Nile⟧.

[4] And his sister took her stand afar off, to know what would be done to him.

[5] And the daughter of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ came down to wash at the ⟦nile|Yeor|Nile⟧, and her girls were walking beside the ⟦nile|Yeor|Nile⟧. And she saw the ark in the midst of the reeds and sent her slave woman, and she took it.

[6] And she opened it and saw him, the child, and behold, a boy weeping. And she had pity on him and said, "This is one of the children of the ⟦hebrews|Ivrim|Hebrews⟧."

[7] And his sister said to the daughter of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, "Shall I go and call for you a nursing woman from the ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ women, that she may nurse the child for you?"

[8] And the daughter of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ said to her, "Go." And the young woman went and called the mother of the child.

[9] And the daughter of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ said to her, "Take this child away and nurse him for me, and I will give your wages." And the woman took the child and nursed him.

[10] And the child grew, and she brought him to the daughter of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, and he became a son to her. And she called his name ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and said, "Because out of the water I drew him."

[11] And it came to pass in those days that ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ grew, and he went out to his brothers and looked on their burdens. And he saw ⟦egyptian|a Mitzri|an Egyptian⟧ man striking ⟦hebrew|an Ivri|a Hebrew⟧ man, one of his brothers.

[12] And he turned this way and that way and saw that there was no man, and he struck down the ⟦egyptian|Mitzri|Egyptian⟧ and hid him in the sand.

[13] And he went out on the second day, and behold, two ⟦hebrew|Ivri|Hebrew⟧ men were struggling together. And he said to the wicked one, "Why do you strike your neighbor?"

[14] And he said, "Who set you as a man, a prince and a judge, over us? Do you mean to kill me, as you killed the ⟦egyptian|Mitzri|Egyptian⟧?" And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ feared and said, "Surely the matter is known."

[15] And ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ heard this matter and sought to kill ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧. And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ fled from the face of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧ and dwelt in the land of ⟦midian|Midyan|Midian⟧, and he sat by the well.

[16] And the priest of ⟦midian|Midyan|Midian⟧ had seven daughters, and they came and drew and filled the gutters to water the flock of their father.

[17] And the shepherds came and drove them out, and ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ arose and saved them and watered their flock.

[18] And they came to Reuel their father, and he said, "Why have you hastened to come today?"

[19] And they said, "⟦egyptian|A Mitzri|An Egyptian⟧ man delivered us from the hand of the shepherds, and he also surely drew for us and watered the flock."

[20] And he said to his daughters, "And where is he? Why is it that you have left the man? Call him, and let him eat bread."

[21] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ undertook to dwell with the man, and he gave ⟦zipporah|Tzipporah|Zipporah⟧ his daughter to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧.

[22] And she bore a son, and he called his name Gershom, for he said, "A sojourner I have been in a foreign land."

[23] And it came to pass in those many days that the king of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧ died, and the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ sighed because of the service and cried out, and their cry for help went up to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ because of the service.

[24] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ heard their groaning, and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ remembered his covenant with ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, with ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ and with ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧.

[25] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ saw the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ knew.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*. In v23 the Hebrew has the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim"; in v24, 25 it has none.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"took to wife a daughter of Levi" → "took the daughter of Levi"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיִּקַּח אֶת־בַּת־לֵוִי: "took" alone (to take a woman is to marry her), and "the daughter of Levi" with the mark of a definite object. A noun joined to a name is definite in form; the sense may also be "a daughter of Levi", a woman of that tribe. Neither parent is named here (see 6:20).
- **"when she saw him that he was a goodly child" → "she saw him, that he was good"** (v2) — Hebrew וַתֵּרֶא אֹתוֹ כִּי־טוֹב הוּא, the words of Gen 1:4 ("*Elohim* saw the light, that it was good"). טוֹב is "good"; the Hebrew has no word "child" here. **"months"** — Hebrew יְרָחִים, "moons".
- **"an ark of bulrushes" → "an ark of papyrus"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew תֵּבַת גֹּמֶא. תֵּבָה is found only here and of Noah's ark (Gen 6–9): a chest. גֹּמֶא is the papyrus plant.
- **"daubed it with slime and with pitch" → "coated it with bitumen and with pitch"** (v3) — Hebrew וַתַּחְמְרָה בַחֵמָר וּבַזָּפֶת: the verb is made from the noun חֵמָר "bitumen" (Gen 11:3), literally "she bitumened it with bitumen".
- **"in the flags by the river's brink" → "in the reeds on the bank of the Nile"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew בַּסּוּף עַל־שְׂפַת הַיְאֹר; סוּף is reeds, the word of the "Sea of Reeds" (יַם־סוּף, 13:18); שָׂפָה is literally "lip". הַיְאֹר is the Nile.
- **"stood afar off, to wit" → "took her stand afar off, to know"** (v4) — Hebrew וַתֵּתַצַּב … לְדֵעָה: "station oneself", and יָדַע "know", the verb of v25.
- **"to wash herself" → "to wash"** (v5) — Hebrew לִרְחֹץ. **"her maidens" → "her girls"; "her maid" → "her slave woman"** — Hebrew נַעֲרֹתֶיהָ (נַעֲרָה "girl", of attendants) and אֲמָתָהּ (אָמָה "slave woman"): two words. **"by the river's side" → "beside the Nile"** — Hebrew עַל־יַד, literally "at the hand of". **"to fetch it" → "and she took it"** — Hebrew וַתִּקָּחֶהָ, "and she took it"; the Hebrew does not say which of the two women.
- **"she saw the child" → "saw him, the child"** (v6) — Hebrew וַתִּרְאֵהוּ אֶת־הַיֶּלֶד, with the pronoun and then the noun. **"the babe wept" → "a boy weeping"** — Hebrew נַעַר בֹּכֶה; נַעַר "boy" beside יֶלֶד "child" in one verse.
- **"had compassion on" → "had pity on"** (v6) — Hebrew חָמַל; "compassion" is another word (רַחֲמִים).
- **"a nurse of the Hebrew women" → "a nursing woman from the Hebrew women"** (v7) — Hebrew אִשָּׁה מֵינֶקֶת, "a woman, one who suckles"; the same root gives "nurse" three times in v7, 9.
- **"the maid" → "the young woman"** (v8) — Hebrew הָעַלְמָה, a girl of marriageable age (Gen 24:43; Isa 7:14).
- **"Take this child away" kept; "thy wages" kept** (v9) — Hebrew הֵילִיכִי, "make go, carry away" (the form is unusual); וַאֲנִי אֶתֵּן אֶת־שְׂכָרֵךְ, with a stressed "I".
- **"he became her son" → "he became a son to her"** (v10) — Hebrew וַיְהִי־לָהּ לְבֵן.
- **"Moses … Because I drew him out of the water" → "Because out of the water I drew him"** (v10) — Hebrew מֹשֶׁה … כִּי מִן־הַמַּיִם מְשִׁיתִהוּ. The name *Mosheh* is tied to the verb מָשָׁה *mashah* "draw out" (found again only in 2 Sam 22:17 = Ps 18:16). In form *Mosheh* is an active participle, "one who draws out", not "one drawn out"; the wordplay is by sound and cannot be shown in English. The name is also commonly explained from Egyptian *ms* "born, child", as in names like Thutmose (from general knowledge, not from the text).
- **"when Moses was grown" → "that Moses grew"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיִּגְדַּל, the same verb as in v10 ("the child grew").
- **"looked on their burdens" kept** (v11) — Hebrew וַיַּרְא בְּסִבְלֹתָם: "see" with בְּ, to look at with feeling; the same verb follows at once ("and he saw"). סְבָלוֹת as in 1:11.
- **"an Egyptian smiting an Hebrew" → "an Egyptian man striking a Hebrew man"** (v11) — Hebrew אִישׁ מִצְרִי מַכֶּה אִישׁ־עִבְרִי, with "man" twice. **"slew" → "struck down"** (v12) — the same verb, הִכָּה "strike" (v11, 12, 13); in v12 the blow kills (v14 "killed").
- **"looked this way and that way" → "turned this way and that way"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיִּפֶן כֹּה וָכֹה, literally "turned thus and thus". **"no man"** — Hebrew אֵין אִישׁ.
- **"strove together" → "were struggling together"** (v13) — Hebrew נִצִּים, of men who come to blows (21:22; Deut 25:11). **"him that did the wrong" → "the wicked one"** — Hebrew לָרָשָׁע, the usual word "wicked": here the one who is in the wrong. **"thy fellow" → "your neighbor"** — Hebrew רֵעֶךָ.
- **"Who made thee a prince and a judge" → "Who set you as a man, a prince and a judge"** (v14) — Hebrew מִי שָׂמְךָ לְאִישׁ שַׂר וְשֹׁפֵט, with the word "man" before the two titles.
- **"intendest thou to kill me" → "Do you mean to kill me"** (v14) — Hebrew הַלְהָרְגֵנִי אַתָּה אֹמֵר, literally "Is it to kill me that you are saying?": "say" for what one says in one's heart.
- **"this thing" → "the matter", "this matter"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew הַדָּבָר, "the word / the matter".
- **"fled from the face of Pharaoh" kept; "dwelt … and he sat down" → "dwelt … and he sat"** (v15) — Hebrew וַיֵּשֶׁב twice: one verb, יָשַׁב, is "dwell" and "sit". **"a well" → "the well"** — the Hebrew has the article.
- **"troughs" → "gutters"** (v16) — Hebrew רְהָטִים, channels for the water (Gen 30:38). **"drew water"** → "drew" — Hebrew וַתִּדְלֶנָה; "water" is not in the verse. דָּלָה "draw" is another word than שָׁאַב (Gen 24).
- **"drove them away" → "drove them out"** (v17) — Hebrew וַיְגָרְשׁוּם, from גֵּרֵשׁ; the sound of the name Gershom (v22) is close to it. **"helped them" → "saved them"** — Hebrew וַיּוֹשִׁעָן, from יָשַׁע "save".
- **"How is it that ye are come so soon" → "Why have you hastened to come"** (v18) — Hebrew מַדּוּעַ מִהַרְתֶּן בֹּא.
- **"Reuel"** (v18) — the father is Reuel here, Jethro in 3:1 and 18:1, Jether in 4:18; the text does not explain the names.
- **"drew water enough for us" → "surely drew for us"** (v19) — Hebrew דָּלֹה דָלָה, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "drawing he drew".
- **"that he may eat bread" → "and let him eat bread"** (v20) — Hebrew וְיֹאכַל, a wish. The question begins with "And" (וְאַיּוֹ).
- **"was content to dwell" → "undertook to dwell"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיּוֹאֶל, from הוֹאִיל: to resolve, to make up one's mind to a thing, to be willing (Gen 18:27).
- **"I have been a stranger in a strange land" → "A sojourner I have been in a foreign land"** (v22) — Hebrew גֵּר הָיִיתִי בְּאֶרֶץ נָכְרִיָּה, with "sojourner" first. The name Gershom (גֵּרְשֹׁם) is tied by sound to גֵּר *ger* "sojourner"; it has often been heard as "a sojourner there" (שָׁם *sham*), which the verse itself does not say.
- **"in process of time" → "in those many days"** (v23) — Hebrew בַיָּמִים הָרַבִּים הָהֵם.
- **"sighed by reason of the bondage, and they cried, and their cry came up" → "sighed because of the service and cried out, and their cry for help went up"** (v23) — three different words: וַיֵּאָנְחוּ "sighed", וַיִּזְעָקוּ "cried out", שַׁוְעָתָם "their cry for help"; a fourth follows in v24, נַאֲקָתָם "their groaning". עֲבֹדָה is "service", as in 1:14. The Hebrew does not say to whom they cried out.
- **"with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob"** (v24) — Hebrew אֶת three times: "with" (or the mark of the object).
- **"looked upon … and God had respect unto them" → "saw … and *Elohim* knew"** (v24–25) — four verbs with *Elohim* as subject, named four times: וַיִּשְׁמַע "heard", וַיִּזְכֹּר "remembered", וַיַּרְא "saw", וַיֵּדַע "knew". The last has no object in the Hebrew: וַיֵּדַע אֱלֹהִים "and Elohim knew". The Septuagint reads "and he was made known to them" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.

### Notes

- **Moses' three 40-year segments** — later Jewish tradition divides Moses' 120 years into three parts: 40 in Egypt, 40 in Midian, 40 leading Israel (Acts 7:23, 30). Exodus itself gives only his age when he spoke to Pharaoh, eighty years (7:7).
- **"Forty years in the wilderness" as formation** — the years in Midian shape Moses from displaced Egyptian prince into a shepherd-leader. The shepherd's staff he will carry into Pharaoh's court originates here.
- **Four divine verbs close the chapter** — "heard… remembered… saw… knew" (v24–25). The cascade of divine attention after long silence sets up the theophany of ch 3.
