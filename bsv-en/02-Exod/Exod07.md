## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 16) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (v2, 4, 5) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"I have made thee a god to Pharaoh" → "I have made you *Elohim* to Pharaoh"** (v1) — Hebrew נְתַתִּיךָ אֱלֹהִים לְפַרְעֹה, literally "I have given you — Elohim — to Pharaoh". The Hebrew has no "as" and no article; the verse leaves it so. In 4:16 the word has a preposition: "you shall be to him for Elohim" (לֵאלֹהִים). Moses stands before Pharaoh as the one whose word is given to a prophet to speak: "Aaron your brother shall be your prophet".
- **"that he send the children of Israel" → "and he shall let the sons of Israel go"** (v2) — Hebrew וְשִׁלַּח, a plain statement of what follows ("and he shall let go"); it can also be heard as the aim of the speaking ("that he let go"). שִׁלַּח (piel) is "send away, let go"; one rendering in the plague chapters.
- **"I will harden Pharaoh's heart" → "I myself will make hard the heart of Pharaoh"** (v3) — Hebrew וַאֲנִי אַקְשֶׁה, the causative of קָשָׁה "be hard" (the adjective in 6:9, "hard service"); the pronoun "I" is written out. Three different Hebrew verbs are used of Pharaoh's heart in Exodus, and KJV has "harden" for all three: קָשָׁה "make hard" (here; 13:15), חָזַק "be strong / make strong" (v13, 22; 8:19; 9:12, 35; 10:20, 27), כָּבֵד "be heavy / make heavy" (v14; 8:15, 32; 9:7, 34; 10:1). Each keeps its own rendering, and who acts is left as each verse has it: here Yahweh says "I will make hard"; in v13 and v22 no one is named ("the heart of Pharaoh was strong"); in v14 "the heart of Pharaoh is heavy".
- **"signs … wonders"** (v3, 9) — Hebrew אוֹת "sign" and מוֹפֵת "wonder, portent". **"Shew a miracle for you" → "Give a wonder for yourselves"** (v9) — Hebrew תְּנוּ לָכֶם מוֹפֵת, the same word as in v3; the verb is נָתַן "give".
- **"that I may lay my hand upon Egypt" → "and I will put my hand on Egypt"** (v4) — Hebrew וְנָתַתִּי אֶת־יָדִי בְּמִצְרָיִם, literally "and I will give my hand in / against Egypt"; the clause is joined with "and", not "so that". **"armies" → "hosts"** — Hebrew צִבְאֹתַי (6:26). **"by great judgments"** — Hebrew שְׁפָטִים (6:6).
- **"the Egyptians"** (v5, 18, 21, 24) — Hebrew מִצְרַיִם "Egypt" with a plural verb: the people. **"from among them" → "from their midst"** (v5) — Hebrew מִתּוֹכָם.
- **"did as the LORD commanded them, so did they" → "did it; as Yahweh commanded them, so they did"** (v6) — the Hebrew has the verb "did" twice; "it" is supplied for the first.
- **"fourscore years old" → "eighty years old"** (v7) — Hebrew בֶּן־שְׁמֹנִים שָׁנָה, literally "a son of eighty years"; "eighty-three" is in the Hebrew "three and eighty".
- **"serpent" → "dragon"** (v9, 10, 12) — Hebrew תַּנִּין *tannin*, a different word from נָחָשׁ "serpent" in 4:3 and in v15 of this chapter. תַּנִּין is a great serpent-like creature: the "sea monsters" of Gen 1:21, the dragon in the sea (Isa 27:1), and Pharaoh himself as "the great *tannin* that lies in the midst of his Niles" (Ezek 29:3), where many think of the crocodile. What animal the staff became is not certain; the two words are kept apart, as the Hebrew has them. (The word list allows "serpent" for these verses; because נָחָשׁ stands in the same chapter, "dragon" is used.)
- **"cast it before Pharaoh, and it shall become" → "throw it before Pharaoh; let it become"** (v9) — Hebrew יְהִי, a wish form: "let it become".
- **"the wise men and the sorcerers … the magicians of Egypt"** (v11) — Hebrew חֲכָמִים "wise men", מְכַשְּׁפִים "sorcerers" (those who practise כְּשָׁפִים, forbidden arts: 22:18; Deut 18:10) and חַרְטֻמִּים "magicians", the word of Gen 41:8, an Egyptian loanword for the learned men of the court; its exact sense is uncertain.
- **"with their enchantments" → "with their secret arts"** (v11, 22) — Hebrew בְּלַהֲטֵיהֶם (v11) and בְּלָטֵיהֶם (v22; 8:7, 18). The second is from לָט "secrecy". The first, found only here, may be another spelling of the same word or come from לַהַט "flame" (Gen 3:24): the meaning is not certain; one rendering is kept for both.
- **"Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods" → "the staff of Aaron swallowed their staffs"** (v12) — Hebrew מַטֶּה "staff"; the verse says "staff", not "dragon".
- **"And he hardened Pharaoh's heart" → "And the heart of Pharaoh was strong"** (v13, 22) — Hebrew וַיֶּחֱזַק לֵב פַּרְעֹה: the heart is the subject, and the verb is the simple stem of חָזַק "be strong, grow strong". No one is named as making it so. "Strong" here is firm, unyielding. **"as the LORD had said" → "as Yahweh had spoken"** — Hebrew דִּבֶּר.
- **"Pharaoh's heart is hardened" → "The heart of Pharaoh is heavy"** (v14) — Hebrew כָּבֵד לֵב פַּרְעֹה: כָּבֵד "heavy" (the root of כָּבוֹד "glory"), a heart that does not move. **"he refuseth"** — Hebrew מֵאֵן.
- **"lo, he goeth out unto the water" → "behold, he is going out to the water"** (v15) — Hebrew הִנֵּה יֹצֵא. **"thou shalt stand by the river's brink against he come" → "you shall stand to meet him on the bank of the Nile"** — Hebrew נִצַּבְתָּ "take your stand"; שְׂפַת הַיְאֹר, literally "the lip of the Nile". **"the river" → "the Nile"** (v15–25) — Hebrew הַיְאֹר *ha-ye'or*, an Egyptian word for the Nile and its arms.
- **"turned to a serpent"** (v15) — Hebrew נָחָשׁ, the word of 4:3 (the staff in Moses' hand), not the תַּנִּין of v9–12.
- **"that they may serve me"** (v16) — Hebrew וְיַעַבְדֻנִי, literally "and let them serve me". **"hitherto thou wouldest not hear" → "you have not listened until now"** — Hebrew לֹא־שָׁמַעְתָּ עַד־כֹּה, "you have not heard until thus"; the verb has no object.
- **"I will smite" → "I am striking"** (v17) — Hebrew הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי מַכֶּה, a participle: what is about to happen. The speaker is Yahweh, and the staff is "in my hand"; the hand that holds it is Moses' (v15) or Aaron's (v19).
- **"shall lothe to drink" → "shall weary themselves to drink"** (v18) — Hebrew וְנִלְאוּ … לִשְׁתּוֹת, the verb of Gen 19:11 ("they wearied themselves to find the entrance"): they will try in vain, or they will be too weary of it to drink. "Loathe" is one reading of it.
- **"upon their streams, upon their rivers, and upon their ponds, and upon all their pools of water" → "over their rivers, over their Niles and over their pools and over every gathering of their waters"** (v19) — Hebrew נַהֲרֹת "rivers", יְאֹרִים (the plural of יְאֹר: the arms and canals of the Nile), אֲגַמִּים "pools" (reedy standing water), מִקְוֵה מֵימֵיהֶם "the gathering of their waters" (the word of Gen 1:10).
- **"both in vessels of wood, and in vessels of stone" → "both in the wood and in the stone"** (v19) — Hebrew וּבָעֵצִים וּבָאֲבָנִים, literally "and in the woods and in the stones". "Vessels" is not in the Hebrew; it is the usual understanding (wooden and stone vessels). Others: in trees and stones, that is, everywhere.
- **"in the sight of" → "before the eyes of"** (v20) — Hebrew לְעֵינֵי. **"he lifted up the rod" → "he lifted up the staff"** — Hebrew וַיָּרֶם בַּמַּטֶּה, literally "he lifted up with the staff"; who lifted it is not named (Aaron, by v19).
- **"the fish"** (v18, 21) — Hebrew הַדָּגָה, a collective singular.
- **"neither did he set his heart to this also" → "he did not set his heart to this either"** (v23) — Hebrew וְלֹא־שָׁת לִבּוֹ גַּם־לָזֹאת: he paid it no attention.
- **"seven days were fulfilled" kept** (v25) — Hebrew וַיִּמָּלֵא, "were filled".

### Notes

- **Structure of the plague narrative** — the plagues are grouped in threes, followed by the last. First plague in each three: warning at the water in the morning (7:15; 8:20; 9:13). Second: "Go in to Pharaoh" (8:1; 9:1; 10:1). Third: no warning (8:16; 9:8; 10:21).
- **First plague: Nile to blood** — the Nile was the life of Egypt and was honoured as divine. Turning its water to blood strikes at the center of Egyptian life.
- **What water did the magicians use?** (v22) — if all the water was already blood, what did the magicians turn to blood? The text does not say; v24 speaks of water dug around the Nile.
- **Verse numbers** — in the Hebrew Bible this chapter has four more verses (7:26–29); they are 8:1–4 here.
