# Exodus 4

[1] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ answered and said, "And behold, they will not believe me and will not listen to my voice, for they will say, '⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ has not appeared to you.'"

[2] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to him, "What is this in your hand?" And he said, "A staff."

[3] And he said, "Throw it to the earth." And he threw it to the earth, and it became a serpent, and ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ fled from before it.

[4] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, "Stretch out your hand and take hold of its tail." And he stretched out his hand and laid strong hold of it, and it became a staff in his palm.

[6] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to him further, "Bring your hand, please, into your bosom." And he brought his hand into his bosom, and he brought it out, and behold, his hand was leprous, like snow.

[7] And he said, "Return your hand to your bosom." And he returned his hand to his bosom, and he brought it out of his bosom, and behold, it had returned as his flesh.

[8] "And it shall come to pass, if they do not believe you and do not listen to the voice of the first sign, that they will believe the voice of the latter sign.

[9] And it shall come to pass, if they do not believe even these two signs and do not listen to your voice, that you shall take some of the water of the ⟦nile|Yeor|Nile⟧ and pour it out on the dry land, and the water that you take from the ⟦nile|Yeor|Nile⟧, it shall become blood on the dry land."

[10] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ said to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, "Oh, ⟦adonai|Adonai|Lord⟧, I am not a man of words, neither yesterday nor the day before, nor since you have spoken to your servant, for heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue am I."

[11] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to him, "Who has put a mouth for man? Or who makes mute or deaf or seeing or blind? Is it not I, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧?

[13] And he said, "Oh, ⟦adonai|Adonai|Lord⟧, send, please, by the hand of whom you will send."

[14] And the anger of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ burned against ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, and he said, "Is there not ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧ your brother, the ⟦levites|Levi|Levite⟧? I know that he will surely speak. And also, behold, he is coming out to meet you, and he will see you and be glad in his heart.

[15] And you shall speak to him and put the words in his mouth, and I, I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach you what you shall do.

[16] And he shall speak for you to the people, and it shall come to pass that he shall be to you for a mouth, and you shall be to him for ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[17] And this staff you shall take in your hand, with which you shall do the signs."

[18] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ went and returned to ⟦jether|Yeter|Jether⟧ his father-in-law and said to him, "Let me go, please, and return to my brothers who are in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, and see whether they are still living." And ⟦jethro|Yitro|Jethro⟧ said to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, "Go in ⟦shalom|shalom|peace⟧."

[19] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ in ⟦midian|Midyan|Midian⟧, "Go, return to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, for all the men who were seeking your soul have died."

[20] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ took his wife and his sons and made them ride on the donkey, and he returned to the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧. And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ took the staff of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in his hand.

[21] And ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ said to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, "When you go to return to ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, see all the wonders that I have put in your hand, and you shall do them before ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧. And I, I will make his heart strong, and he will not let the people go.

[22] And you shall say to ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, 'Thus says ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧: My son, my firstborn, is ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[28] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ told ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧ all the words of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ with which he had sent him, and all the signs that he had commanded him.

[29] And ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧ went and gathered all the elders of the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[30] And ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧ spoke all the words that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ had spoken to ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧, and he did the signs before the eyes of the people.

[31] And the people believed. And they heard that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ had visited the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ and that he had seen their affliction, and they bowed their heads and bowed down.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Hebrew יהוה. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim", in v20 and v27.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"But, behold, they will not believe me, nor hearken unto my voice" → "And behold, they will not believe me and will not listen to my voice"** (v1) — Hebrew וְהֵן לֹא־יַאֲמִינוּ לִי וְלֹא יִשְׁמְעוּ בְּקֹלִי. הֵן is "behold"; it can also mean "if" ("and if they do not believe me …?"). The Septuagint has a question with "if" (cited from memory).
- **"What is that in thine hand?" → "What is this in your hand?"** (v2) — Hebrew מַה־זֶּה. The consonants are written as one word (מזה); the reading tradition divides them into two. The sense is the same.
- **"rod" → "staff"** (v2, 4, 17, 20) — Hebrew מַטֶּה.
- **"Cast it on the ground" → "Throw it to the earth"** (v3) — Hebrew הַשְׁלִיכֵהוּ אַרְצָה, the verb of 1:22; אֶרֶץ is "earth". **"fled from before it"** — Hebrew מִפָּנָיו, literally "from its face".
- **"Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail. And he put forth his hand, and caught it" → "Stretch out your hand and take hold of its tail. And he stretched out his hand and laid strong hold of it"** (v4) — two different verbs: אָחַז "take hold" in the command, and וַיַּחֲזֶק בּוֹ in the doing, from חָזַק "be strong" (the root of v21): to grip firmly. **"in his hand" → "in his palm"** — Hebrew בְּכַפּוֹ; "hand" (יָד) stands earlier in the verse.
- **"That they may believe" → "So that they may believe"** (v5) — Hebrew לְמַעַן יַאֲמִינוּ. The speech of Yahweh goes on from v4 without a new "and he said"; the sentence is not complete in itself. The order of the Hebrew is kept: "has appeared to you" stands before the three names.
- **"Put now thine hand into thy bosom" → "Bring your hand, please, into your bosom"** (v6) — Hebrew הָבֵא־נָא, "make come", with נָא; the same verb in "he brought". חֵיק is the fold of the garment at the breast.
- **"leprous as snow" → "leprous, like snow"** (v6) — Hebrew מְצֹרַעַת כַּשָּׁלֶג. צָרַעַת is the skin disease of Lev 13–14; it is not certain that it is the leprosy of modern medicine. The Hebrew says "like snow"; "white" is not in the verse.
- **"Put thine hand into thy bosom again … it was turned again as his other flesh" → "Return your hand to your bosom … it had returned as his flesh"** (v7) — one verb three times, שׁוּב "return": הָשֵׁב, וַיָּשֶׁב, שָׁבָה. "Other" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"the voice of the first sign … the voice of the latter sign" kept** (v8) — Hebrew לְקֹל הָאֹת: the signs have a "voice". "Believe the voice" is הֶאֱמִין לְ, as "believe you".
- **"take of the water of the river" → "take some of the water of the Nile"** (v9) — Hebrew מִמֵּימֵי הַיְאֹר, "from the waters of the Nile". **"shall become blood" → "it shall become blood"** — the Hebrew has the verb twice, taking the subject up again after the relative clause: וְהָיוּ הַמַּיִם … וְהָיוּ לְדָם, literally "and the water … shall be, and it shall become blood". **"dry land"** — two forms of one word, הַיַּבָּשָׁה and בַּיַּבָּשֶׁת.
- **"O my Lord" → "Oh, Adonai"** (v10, 13) — Hebrew בִּי אֲדֹנָי. בִּי is literally "upon me": the word of one who asks leave to speak to a superior (Gen 43:20; 44:18). אֲדֹנָי is pointed as the divine title *Adonai*; with other vowels the same consonants are "my lord".
- **"I am not eloquent" → "I am not a man of words"** (v10) — Hebrew לֹא אִישׁ דְּבָרִים אָנֹכִי.
- **"neither heretofore, nor since thou hast spoken" → "neither yesterday nor the day before, nor since you have spoken"** (v10) — Hebrew גַּם מִתְּמוֹל גַּם מִשִּׁלְשֹׁם גַּם מֵאָז דַּבֶּרְךָ, literally "also from yesterday, also from the day before, also from the time of your speaking". **"your servant"** — Hebrew עַבְדֶּךָ; the word is "slave".
- **"slow of speech, and of a slow tongue" → "heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue"** (v10) — Hebrew כְבַד־פֶּה וּכְבַד לָשׁוֹן. כָּבֵד is "heavy", the word used later of the heart of Pharaoh (7:14; 8:15). What kind of heaviness is meant (a defect of speech, or want of skill in speaking) the word does not say.
- **"Who hath made man's mouth?" → "Who has put a mouth for man?"** (v11) — Hebrew מִי שָׂם פֶּה לָאָדָם: שִׂים "put, set" twice in the verse ("who has put … who makes", literally "who sets"); "man" is הָאָדָם, mankind. **"the dumb" → "mute"; "the seeing" kept** — four adjectives without the article; פִּקֵּחַ is "open-eyed, seeing". **"have not I the LORD?" → "Is it not I, Yahweh?"** — Hebrew הֲלֹא אָנֹכִי יְהוָה.
- **"I will be with thy mouth" → "I, I will be with your mouth"** (v12, 15) — Hebrew וְאָנֹכִי אֶהְיֶה עִם־פִּיךָ, with a stressed "I" and the form אֶהְיֶה "I will be" of 3:12, 14. **"teach"** — Hebrew הוֹרָה, the root of *torah*.
- **"send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send" → "send, please, by the hand of whom you will send"** (v13) — Hebrew שְׁלַח־נָא בְּיַד־תִּשְׁלָח, literally "send, please, by the hand you will send". The words say only: send by someone; that he means "someone else" is heard from the answer in v14.
- **"the anger of the LORD was kindled" → "the anger of Yahweh burned"** (v14) — Hebrew וַיִּחַר־אַף; אַף is literally "nose".
- **"Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother?" → "Is there not Aaron your brother, the Levite?"** (v14) — Hebrew הֲלֹא אַהֲרֹן אָחִיךָ הַלֵּוִי, in this order. **"he can speak well" → "he will surely speak"** — Hebrew דַבֵּר יְדַבֵּר הוּא, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "speaking he will speak"; "well" is not in the Hebrew. **"when he seeth thee, he will be glad" → "he will see you and be glad"** — two verbs joined by "and".
- **"will teach you what ye shall do"** (v15) — "you" is plural here: Moses and Aaron.
- **"he shall be thy spokesman … instead of a mouth … instead of God" → "he shall speak for you … for a mouth … for *Elohim*"** (v16) — Hebrew וְדִבֶּר־הוּא לְךָ … יִהְיֶה־לְּךָ לְפֶה וְאַתָּה תִּהְיֶה־לּוֹ לֵאלֹהִים: "be to you for a mouth … be to him for Elohim". The word is אֱלֹהִים without the article: "for *Elohim*" or "for a god" (see 7:1).
- **"Jethro" → "Jether … Jethro"** (v18) — the Masoretic Text has two forms of the name in one verse: יֶתֶר *Yeter* first, then יִתְרוֹ *Yitro*. Both are kept as written. **"father in law"** — Hebrew חֹתֵן.
- **"Let me go, I pray thee, and return … and see whether they be yet alive" → "Let me go, please, and return … and see whether they are still living"** (v18) — three cohortatives with נָא.
- **"Go in peace" → "Go in shalom"** (v18) — Hebrew לֵךְ לְשָׁלוֹם, literally "go to peace".
- **"the men are dead which sought thy life" → "the men who were seeking your soul have died"** (v19) — Hebrew הַמְבַקְשִׁים אֶת־נַפְשֶׁךָ; נֶפֶשׁ is "soul".
- **"set them upon an ass" → "made them ride on the donkey"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיַּרְכִּבֵם עַל־הַחֲמֹר, with the article. **"his sons"** — plural; only Gershom has been named (2:22; see 18:3, 4).
- **"the rod of God" → "the staff of *Elohim*"** (v20) — Hebrew מַטֵּה הָאֱלֹהִים.
- **"see that thou do all those wonders" → "see all the wonders … and you shall do them"** (v21) — Hebrew רְאֵה כָּל־הַמֹּפְתִים … וַעֲשִׂיתָם: "see" has the wonders as its object. מוֹפֵת is "wonder, portent", another word than "sign" (אוֹת, v8, 17).
- **"I will harden his heart" → "I, I will make his heart strong"** (v21) — Hebrew וַאֲנִי אֲחַזֵּק אֶת־לִבּוֹ: the piel of חָזַק "be strong", with a stressed "I". Three different verbs are used of the heart of Pharaoh in this book, and each keeps its own rendering: חָזַק "be strong / make strong" (here; 7:13, 22; 9:12), כָּבֵד "be heavy / make heavy" (7:14; 8:15), קָשָׁה "make hard" (7:3). "Harden" would make them one. Here the one who makes strong is Yahweh; other verses name Pharaoh, or no one: each verse is left as it stands.
- **"let the people go"** (v21, 23) — Hebrew שִׁלַּח, the piel of שָׁלַח "send": "send away, let go".
- **"Israel is my son, even my firstborn" → "My son, my firstborn, is Israel"** (v22) — Hebrew בְּנִי בְכֹרִי יִשְׂרָאֵל, in this order, with no "even". **"Thus saith the LORD" → "Thus says Yahweh"** — Hebrew כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה, the first time this formula stands in the Bible.
- **"And I say unto thee … and if thou refuse … I will slay" → "And I said to you … and you refused … I am killing"** (v23) — Hebrew וָאֹמַר … וַתְּמָאֵן … הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי הֹרֵג: two past forms ("I said … and you refused") and a participle. The words are spoken as from the hour when the refusal is complete. The Hebrew has no "if". **"that he may serve me" → "and let him serve me"** — Hebrew וְיַעַבְדֵנִי. **"thy son, even thy firstborn" → "your son, your firstborn"** — as in v22.
- **"in the inn" → "at the lodging place"** (v24) — Hebrew בַּמָּלוֹן, a place to spend the night. **"sought to kill him" → "sought to put him to death"** — Hebrew הֲמִיתוֹ, the hifil of מוּת. Who "him" is — Moses, or the son — the Hebrew does not say; no name stands in v24–26 except Yahweh and Zipporah.
- **"a sharp stone" → "a flint"** (v25) — Hebrew צֹר.
- **"cast it at his feet" → "made it touch his feet"** (v25) — Hebrew וַתַּגַּע לְרַגְלָיו, the hifil of נָגַע "touch": literally "she made touch at his feet". "It" is supplied; the Hebrew names no object. Whose feet is open: the son's, Moses', or those of the one who met him. "Feet" is elsewhere also a veiled word for the private parts (Isa 7:20); whether it is so here cannot be decided.
- **"Surely a bloody husband art thou to me" → "For a bridegroom of blood you are to me"** (v25) — Hebrew כִּי חֲתַן־דָּמִים אַתָּה לִי. חָתָן is one who becomes kin by marriage: bridegroom, son-in-law (Gen 19:12, 14). דָּמִים is the plural "bloods", used of blood that has been shed. כִּי is "for" or "surely". To whom she says it is open, as in the clause before. What the phrase meant is not known; the verse is left as it stands.
- **"So he let him go" → "And he let go of him"** (v26) — Hebrew וַיִּרֶף מִמֶּנּוּ, literally "and he slackened from him": the same "he" and "him" as in v24.
- **"then she said, A bloody husband thou art, because of the circumcision" → "Then she said, 'A bridegroom of blood,' for the circumcisions"** (v26) — Hebrew אָז אָמְרָה חֲתַן דָּמִים לַמּוּלֹת. "Thou art" is not in the Hebrew. לַמּוּלֹת is "for / with regard to the circumcisions": a plural noun found only here. The sentence seems to explain the saying of v25 ("at that time she said 'bridegroom of blood' with regard to circumcision"); its exact sense is uncertain.
- **"Go into the wilderness to meet Moses" → "Go to meet Moses, to the wilderness"** (v27) — the Hebrew order. **"met"** (v24, 27) — the same verb, פָּגַשׁ, of Yahweh meeting "him" and of Aaron meeting Moses.
- **"all the words of the LORD who had sent him" → "all the words of Yahweh with which he had sent him"** (v28) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר שְׁלָחוֹ: the relative may go with "Yahweh" ("who had sent him") or with "the words" ("with which he had sent him"), like the parallel "the signs that he had commanded him".
- **"gathered together" → "gathered"** (v29) — Hebrew אָסַף. The verb "went" is singular, "gathered" plural.
- **"in the sight of the people" → "before the eyes of the people"** (v30) — Hebrew לְעֵינֵי הָעָם.
- **"and when they heard … then they bowed their heads and worshipped" → "And they heard … and they bowed their heads and bowed down"** (v31) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁמְעוּ … וַיִּקְּדוּ וַיִּשְׁתַּחֲוּוּ, three verbs joined by "and"; הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה is "bow down" to the ground. "Visited" is פָּקַד, as in 3:16; "affliction" עֳנִי, as in 3:7.

### Notes

- **The strange episode (v24–26)** — one of the most cryptic passages in the Hebrew Bible. Yahweh meets "him" (Moses, or possibly his son; the pronouns are ambiguous) and seeks to put him to death, and Zipporah circumcises their son. Interpretive possibilities:
  1. Moses had neglected to circumcise his son; Yahweh demands it before the commissioning proceeds.
  2. The phrase marks a rite of passage; the episode is a fragment of older ritual memory.
  3. A test that parallels Abraham's (ch 22) — radical obedience required at the threshold of commission.
  The text leaves it starkly unexplained.
- **Aaron as spokesman** — from here on, Aaron speaks publicly while Moses receives revelation. The mouth / heart division anticipates later priestly / prophetic distinctions.
- **"My son, my firstborn, is Israel" (v22)** — covenantal naming. The plague of the firstborn (12:29) will mirror this: Egypt killed the Hebrew sons; Yahweh will kill Egyptian firstborn as judgment for refusing to release his firstborn-people.
