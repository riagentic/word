## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים "the Elohim", in v5, 12 (second), 16, 19 (second and third); without it in v1, 4, 12 (first), 15, 19 (first), 21, 23. In v11 the same word with the article means "the gods".
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"heard of all … and that the LORD" → "heard all … that Yahweh"** (v1) — Hebrew כִּי, "that" (or "for", "how"); there is no "and" before it.
- **"after he had sent her back" → "after she had been sent away"** (v2) — Hebrew אַחַר שִׁלּוּחֶיהָ, literally "after her sendings-away", a noun; it does not name who sent her. The noun is used of a parting gift to a bride in 1 Kgs 9:16; the verb שִׁלַּח "send away" is also used of divorce (Deut 24:1). The verse does not say more.
- **"an alien in a strange land" → "a sojourner in a foreign land"** (v3) — Hebrew גֵּר … נָכְרִיָּה; the name *Gershom* is heard as גֵּר שָׁם "a sojourner there" (2:22).
- **"Eliezer"** (v4) — Hebrew אֱלִיעֶזֶר, "my El is help"; the verse gives the reason in other words: אֱלֹהֵי אָבִי בְּעֶזְרִי, literally "the Elohim of my father [was] in / as my help". No gloss stands in the verse. **"the name of the one … the name of the other"** (v3, 4) — Hebrew הָאֶחָד "the one" both times.
- **"came with his sons and his wife unto Moses" → "came, and his sons and his wife, to Moses"** (v5) — the Hebrew order; "his" is Moses'. **"where he encamped at the mount of God" → "where he was camping, the mountain of *Elohim*"** — the Hebrew has no "at": "the mountain of the Elohim" stands beside "the wilderness".
- **"I thy father in law Jethro am come" → "am coming"** (v6) — Hebrew בָּא, a participle. "He said" must be a message sent ahead, since Moses goes out to meet him in v7; the Hebrew says only "said". The Septuagint and the Samaritan text read "Behold" (הִנֵּה) for "I" (אֲנִי): "it was said to Moses, Behold, your father-in-law …" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"did obeisance" → "bowed down"** (v7) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה. **"they asked each other of their welfare" → "they asked each man his neighbor about shalom"** — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁאֲלוּ אִישׁ־לְרֵעֵהוּ לְשָׁלוֹם, the usual greeting: to ask after one's *shalom* (peace, well-being).
- **"told" → "recounted"** (v8) — Hebrew סִפֵּר. **"unto the Egyptians" → "to Egypt"** (v8), **"out of the hand of the Egyptians" → "from the hand of Egypt"** (v9, 10) — Hebrew מִצְרַיִם, the name of the land and its people as one. **"for Israel's sake" → "on account of Israel"** — Hebrew עַל אוֹדֹת.
- **"the travail that had come upon them" → "the hardship that had found them"** (v8) — Hebrew תְּלָאָה "weariness, hardship" and מָצָא "find". **"and how the LORD delivered them" → "and Yahweh delivered them"** — the Hebrew has only "and".
- **"whom he had delivered"** (v9) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר הִצִּילוֹ, "whom he delivered" or "in that he delivered him". וַיִּחַדְּ "rejoiced" is a rare verb (חָדָה).
- **"greater than all gods" → "greater than all the gods"** (v11) — Hebrew מִכָּל־הָאֱלֹהִים, with the article.
- **"for in the thing wherein they dealt proudly he was above them" → "for in the matter in which they acted presumptuously against them —"** (v11) — Hebrew כִּי בַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר זָדוּ עֲלֵיהֶם, word for word "for in the thing / word in which they acted presumptuously against them". The sentence has no main clause; it is left unfinished. KJV supplies "he was above them" (in italics). "They" are the Egyptians (or their gods) and "them" Israel; it is commonly completed "… he has dealt with them" or read as a second "that": "[I know] that in the very thing in which they acted presumptuously against them [he is greater]". זִיד is "act presumptuously, insolently" (lit. "boil up").
- **"took a burnt offering" kept** (v12) — Hebrew וַיִּקַּח "took"; the verb is not "offered". Some old versions have "offered" (cited from memory).
- **"stood by Moses"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew עַל "over, beside": Moses sits and the people stand over him. Two verbs for "stand": עָמַד (v13) and נִצָּב (v14).
- **"to enquire of God" → "to seek *Elohim*"** (v15) — Hebrew לִדְרֹשׁ אֱלֹהִים, "seek": to ask for his word or decision.
- **"When they have a matter, they come unto me" → "it comes to me"** (v16) — Hebrew בָּא אֵלַי, singular: the matter comes (or "one comes"). דָּבָר is "word, matter, thing" throughout the chapter (v11, 14, 16–19, 22, 23, 26): a case to be decided. **"between one and another" → "between a man and his neighbor"** — Hebrew בֵּין אִישׁ וּבֵין רֵעֵהוּ.
- **"his laws" → "his torot"** (v16), **"laws" → "the torot"** (v20) — Hebrew תּוֹרֹת, plural of *torah* "instruction, teaching". **"ordinances" → "statutes"** (v20) — Hebrew חֻקִּים, as in v16.
- **"Thou wilt surely wear away"** kept (v18) — Hebrew infinitive absolute with its verb: נָבֹל תִּבֹּל, literally "withering you will wither"; נָבֵל is said of a leaf or flower that fades and falls. **"thyself alone" → "alone"** — Hebrew לְבַדֶּךָ.
- **"Hearken now unto my voice" → "Now listen to my voice"** (v19) — Hebrew שְׁמַע בְּקֹלִי. **"and God shall be with thee" → "and may *Elohim* be with you"** — Hebrew וִיהִי, a wish (jussive).
- **"to God-ward" → "before *Elohim*"** (v19) — Hebrew מוּל הָאֱלֹהִים, "in front of, facing the Elohim". **"the causes" → "the matters"** — Hebrew הַדְּבָרִים.
- **"thou shalt teach them" → "you shall warn them of"** (v20) — Hebrew הִזְהִיר "warn, admonish"; "teach" is another verb.
- **"provide … able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness" → "look out … men of ability, fearers of *Elohim*, men of truth, haters of unjust gain"** (v21) — Hebrew תֶחֱזֶה "see, behold" (look out, select); אַנְשֵׁי־חַיִל "men of ability" (חַיִל: strength, worth, also "army" and "wealth"); בֶּצַע is gain got by violence or wrong, here a judge's bribe.
- **"rulers of thousands" → "chiefs of thousands"** (v21, 25) — Hebrew שַׂר, "prince, commander, chief": the one set over a body of men.
- **"at all seasons" → "at all times"** (v22, 26) — Hebrew בְּכָל־עֵת. **"so shall it be easier for thyself" → "And make it lighter from upon you"** (v22) — Hebrew וְהָקֵל מֵעָלֶיךָ, an imperative: "lighten [it] from upon yourself". **"bear the burden with thee" → "bear with you"** — the Hebrew has no object.
- **"then thou shalt be able to endure" → "to stand"** (v23) — Hebrew עֲמֹד "stand". **"go to their place in peace" → "come to its place in shalom"** — Hebrew יָבֹא בְשָׁלוֹם; "its" is the people's.
- **"made them heads" → "placed them as heads"** (v25) — Hebrew וַיִּתֵּן "gave, placed". **"the hard causes" → "the hard matter"** (v26) — Hebrew אֶת־הַדָּבָר הַקָּשֶׁה, singular. The verbs of v26 tell what was done as a custom ("they would bring … they would judge").
- **"let his father in law depart; and he went his way" → "sent his father-in-law away, and he got himself to his land"** (v27) — Hebrew וַיְשַׁלַּח "sent away" and וַיֵּלֶךְ לוֹ, literally "he went for himself": the same turn as לֶךְ־לְךָ "get yourself" (Gen 12:1).

### Notes

- **Jethro's confession (v10–11)** — a Midianite priest declares Yahweh greater than all the gods. Monolatrous confession (Yahweh is higher, not that others do not exist). The Midianite-Kenite connection to early Yahwism is a scholarly hypothesis based on Jethro's priestly role and Moses' formation in Midian.
- **Administrative organization (v21–26)** — Jethro's counsel introduces the hierarchical judicial system: chiefs over tens, fifties, hundreds, thousands. Practical wisdom from a foreign priest becomes institutional foundation for Israel's governance.
- **Structural placement** — ch 18 precedes the Sinai theophany (ch 19), which is historically curious: Jethro visits *after* Israel is at the mountain of *Elohim* (v5) but the Sinai events seem to begin in ch 19. Probably structural / thematic arrangement: Jethro's model of delegation prepares for the covenantal structure about to be given.
