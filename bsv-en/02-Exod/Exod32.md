## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v11, 16, 27) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים *Elohim*, where the one God is meant.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept (v1 and v25–26 are chains of main clauses, not "when … then").
- **"delayed to come down" → "was long in coming down"** (v1) — Hebrew בֹשֵׁשׁ, a rare form of בּוֹשׁ ("be ashamed"; here "keep one waiting", as Judg 5:28). "Delay" is another verb (אֵחַר).
- **"gathered themselves together unto Aaron" → "assembled against Aaron"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיִּקָּהֵל … עַל, the verb of קָהָל "assembly". עַל is "upon, against" (so with this verb in Num 16:3), but also "to, around"; the Hebrew leaves it open how hostile the gathering is.
- **"Up, make us gods, which shall go before us" → "Arise, make for us gods who shall go before us"** (v1, 23) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים אֲשֶׁר יֵלְכוּ: the verb is plural, "who shall go" (they). אֱלֹהִים is plural in form and is the same word as "*Elohim*"; with a plural verb it is "gods". The people make one calf; with a singular sense the words would be "make for us a god / an Elohim who shall go before us". The verse translates the plural as it stands.
- **"These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up" → "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up"** (v4, 8) — Hebrew אֵלֶּה אֱלֹהֶיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל אֲשֶׁר הֶעֱלוּךָ: the pronoun ("these") and the verb ("they brought you up") are plural, though there is one calf, and Aaron calls the feast "a feast to Yahweh" (v5). The same words can be heard as "This is your Elohim, Israel, who brought you up": the plural of אֱלֹהִים sometimes takes a plural verb when the one God is meant (Gen 20:13; 35:7). Neh 9:18 retells the words in the singular ("This is your Elohim who brought you up"); 1 Kgs 12:28 has the plural, of two calves. The verse keeps the plural of the Hebrew. "O" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"we wot not what is become of him" → "we do not know what has happened to him"** (v1, 23) — Hebrew מֶה־הָיָה לוֹ, literally "what has been to him".
- **"Break off" → "Tear off"** (v2, 3, 24) — Hebrew פָּרַק; in v3 and v24 the reflexive stem, "tore off from themselves". **"golden earrings" → "rings of gold"** — Hebrew נִזְמֵי הַזָּהָב; נֶזֶם is a ring (of the nose or the ear). "Them" in v2, 3 is supplied for English.
- **"fashioned it with a graving tool"→ "formed it with a graving tool"** (v4) — Hebrew וַיָּצַר אֹתוֹ בַּחֶרֶט. Uncertain. חֶרֶט is a graving tool or stylus (Isa 8:1); the verb is from צוּר "shape" or יָצַר "form". The same letters can be read "and he tied it up in a bag" (צוּר "bind", חָרִיט "bag", 2 Kgs 5:23). "It" has no noun before it (the gold). **"a molten calf" kept** (v4, 8) — Hebrew עֵגֶל מַסֵּכָה, "a calf of cast metal" (נָסַךְ "pour"); עֵגֶל is a young bull.
- **"And when Aaron saw it" → "And Aaron saw"** (v5) — the Hebrew has no object. **"made proclamation" → "called out"** — Hebrew וַיִּקְרָא.
- **"To morrow is a feast to the LORD" → "A feast to Yahweh tomorrow!"** (v5) — Hebrew חַג לַיהוָה מָחָר, three words without a verb. חַג is a pilgrim feast. Aaron names Yahweh, not another god: see the note on v4.
- **"offered burnt offerings" → "offered up burnt offerings"** (v6) — Hebrew וַיַּעֲלוּ עֹלֹת, verb and noun of one root, literally "made go up goings-up". **"brought peace offerings" → "brought near peace offerings"** — Hebrew הִגִּישׁ.
- **"rose up to play" → "arose to play"** (v6) — Hebrew לְצַחֵק, the piel of צָחַק "laugh" (the root of the name Isaac): to play, jest, make sport. The word covers dancing and games (v19), mockery (Gen 21:9; 39:14) and the play of a husband with his wife (Gen 26:8); which is meant here the word does not say. 1 Cor 10:7 cites the verse.
- **"Go, get thee down" → "Go, go down"** (v7) — Hebrew לֶךְ־רֵד, two imperatives. "At once" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"have corrupted themselves" → "have wrought ruin"** (v7) — Hebrew שִׁחֵת, the piel of the verb "ruin" (Gen 6:12, 17), here with no object: has acted ruinously. "Themselves" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"worshipped it" → "bowed down to it"** (v8) — Hebrew הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה.
- **"a stiffnecked people" kept** (v9) — Hebrew עַם־קְשֵׁה־עֹרֶף, literally "a people hard of neck". "It" (v9, 21, 22, 25): the Hebrew speaks of the people in the singular.
- **"let me alone" → "leave me"** (v10) — Hebrew הַנִּיחָה לִּי, from נוּחַ "rest": "let me be, leave me to myself".
- **"that my wrath may wax hot against them" → "that my anger may burn against them"** (v10, 11, 19, 22) — Hebrew חָרָה אַף; אַף is literally "nose": "that my nose may burn". "Hot" is not a separate word. In v12 חֲרוֹן אַפֶּךָ is "the burning of your nose".
- **"consume them" → "make an end of them"** (v10, 12) — Hebrew כִּלָּה "finish, bring to an end", the verb of "finished" in 31:18. "Consume" (eat up) is אָכַל.
- **"besought the LORD" → "besought the face of Yahweh"** (v11) — Hebrew וַיְחַל … אֶת־פְּנֵי יְהוָה, literally "he made the face of Yahweh soft (or: sweet)": to appease, to seek the favor of. "Face" is in the Hebrew.
- **"why doth thy wrath wax hot" → "Why … should your anger burn"** (v11) — Hebrew imperfect; also "why does your anger burn". **"a mighty hand" → "a strong hand"** — Hebrew יָד חֲזָקָה.
- **"Wherefore should the Egyptians speak, and say" → "Why should the Egyptians say, saying"** (v12) — Hebrew מִצְרַיִם "Egypt", with a plural verb.
- **"For mischief did he bring them out" → "With evil he brought them out"** (v12) — Hebrew בְּרָעָה, "in / with evil": with evil intent. **"repent of this evil against thy people" → "relent concerning the evil against your people"** (v12, 14) — Hebrew רָעָה is one word in the three places, "evil": here the harm that is planned, not wrongdoing. In v22 the people is בְרָע "in evil".
- **"repent" / "repented" → "relent" / "relented"** (v12, 14) — Hebrew נִחַם, niphal: to be sorry, to let oneself be moved to give up what was planned. The same verb as Gen 6:6, 7 ("was sorry"): there Yahweh is sorry for what he has done, here he gives up what he has said he would do. "Repent" (turn from sin) is not the sense.
- **"Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel"** (v13) — Hebrew זְכֹר לְ, "remember for Abraham, for Isaac and for Israel": remember in their favor. **"thy servants"** — Hebrew עֲבָדֶיךָ, the word "slave". **"thine own self" → "yourself"** — Hebrew בָּךְ "by you".
- **"seed" kept** (v13) — Hebrew זֶרַע. **"that I have spoken of" → "of which I have said"** — Hebrew אָמַרְתִּי. "It" (twice) is supplied.
- **"which he thought to do" → "that he had spoken of doing"** (v14) — Hebrew דִּבֶּר "spoke".
- **"on both their sides; on the one side and on the other" → "on their two sides; on this side and on that"** (v15) — Hebrew מִשְּׁנֵי עֶבְרֵיהֶם מִזֶּה וּמִזֶּה.
- **"graven upon the tables" → "engraved on the tablets"** (v16) — Hebrew חָרוּת, found only here.
- **"the noise of the people as they shouted" → "the sound of the people in their shouting"** (v17, 18) — Hebrew קוֹל five times, one word ("voice, sound"). The written form בְּרֵעֹה is "in its shouting".
- **"the voice of them that shout for mastery … cry for being overcome … the noise of them that sing" → "the sound of the singing of might … of weakness … the sound of singing"** (v18) — Hebrew עֲנוֹת גְּבוּרָה … עֲנוֹת חֲלוּשָׁה … עַנּוֹת: one root three times (עָנָה "answer, sing in answer"), the third time in the intensive stem with no word after it. גְּבוּרָה is "might" (victory), חֲלוּשָׁה "weakness" (defeat; found only here). Three short lines in the Hebrew.
- **"the dancing" → "dances"** (v19) — Hebrew מְחֹלֹת, plural, without the article. **"out of his hands"** — the reading tradition (qere) is "from his hands"; the written text (ketiv) has "from his hand". **"brake them beneath the mount" → "broke them in pieces beneath the mountain"** — Hebrew שִׁבֵּר, תַּחַת הָהָר.
- **"ground it to powder, and strawed it upon the water" → "ground it until it was fine, and he strewed it on the face of the waters"** (v20) — Hebrew עַד אֲשֶׁר־דָּק; עַל־פְּנֵי הַמַּיִם. **"made the children of Israel drink of it" → "gave the sons of Israel to drink"** — Hebrew וַיַּשְׁקְ; "of it" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"so great a sin" → "a great sin"** (v21, 30, 31) — Hebrew חֲטָאָה גְדֹלָה.
- **"that they are set on mischief" → "that it is in evil"** (v22) — Hebrew כִּי בְרָע הוּא, three words: "that in evil it is". "Set on" is not in the Hebrew. "You yourself" renders the pronoun אַתָּה, which stands first.
- **"Whosoever hath any gold, let them break it off" → "Whoever has gold, tear it off from yourselves"** (v24) — Hebrew לְמִי זָהָב הִתְפָּרָקוּ, "To whom is gold? Tear off!" The verb can also be read as a statement: "whoever had gold, they tore it off". **"there came out this calf" → "this calf came out"** — Hebrew וַיֵּצֵא.
- **"that the people were naked; (for Aaron had made them naked" → "that it was let loose, for Aaron had let it loose"** (v25) — Hebrew פָרֻעַ … פְרָעֹה, the same verb twice. פָּרַע is "let go, let loose" (5:4), also "let the hair hang loose, uncover the head" (Lev 10:6; Num 5:18): a people out of hand. KJV "naked" takes it of the body; that is not the usual sense of the verb.
- **"unto their shame among their enemies" → "for derision among those who rise against them"** (v25) — Hebrew לְשִׁמְצָה בְּקָמֵיהֶם. שִׁמְצָה is found only here and its meaning is uncertain: probably "a whisper" (שֶׁמֶץ, Job 4:12; 26:14), hence a whispered taunt, derision. קָמֵיהֶם is "those rising up against them".
- **"Who is on the LORD's side? let him come unto me" → "Who is for Yahweh? To me!"** (v26) — Hebrew מִי לַיהוָה אֵלָי, four words; no verb "come".
- **"Thus saith the LORD God of Israel" → "Thus says Yahweh, the *Elohim* of Israel"** (v27).
- **"by his side" → "on his thigh"** (v27) — Hebrew עַל־יְרֵכוֹ. **"go in and out" → "Pass through and return"** — Hebrew עִבְרוּ וָשׁוּבוּ. **"slay" → "kill"** — Hebrew הָרַג.
- **"his companion … his neighbour" → "his neighbor … the one near to him"** (v27) — Hebrew רֵעֵהוּ (the fellow man, as in "each man … his neighbor") and קְרֹבוֹ "his near one": near by kinship or by dwelling.
- **"according to the word of Moses" kept** (v28) — Hebrew כִּדְבַר.
- **"Consecrate yourselves to day to the LORD" → "Fill your hand today for Yahweh"** (v29) — Hebrew מִלְאוּ יֶדְכֶם, an imperative as pointed. "To fill the hand" of someone is the Hebrew expression for installing him as priest (28:41; 29:9). The Septuagint has a past tense, "you have filled your hands" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"even every man upon his son, and upon his brother; that he may bestow upon you a blessing this day" → "for each man was against his son and against his brother, and to give a blessing upon you today"** (v29) — Hebrew כִּי אִישׁ בִּבְנוֹ וּבְאָחִיו וְלָתֵת עֲלֵיכֶם הַיּוֹם בְּרָכָה. The clause has no verb ("was" is supplied); בְּ is "against" or "at the cost of". The last words are an infinitive without a subject, "and to give": who gives the blessing is not said.
- **"Ye have sinned" → "You yourselves have sinned"** (v30) — the pronoun אַתֶּם is expressed. **"peradventure I shall make an atonement" → "perhaps I may make atonement"** — Hebrew אוּלַי אֲכַפְּרָה, the form of a wish or resolve; כִּפֶּר is literally "cover".
- **"Oh" → "Ah"** (v31) — Hebrew אָנָּא, a cry of entreaty.
- **"gods of gold" kept** (v31) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי זָהָב, plural in form; also "a god of gold".
- **"if thou wilt forgive their sin—" kept** (v32) — Hebrew אִם־תִּשָּׂא חַטָּאתָם, literally "if you will lift / bear their sin". The sentence is left unfinished in the Hebrew.
- **"blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book" → "blot me out, please, from your book"** (v32, 33) — Hebrew מְחֵנִי נָא מִסִּפְרְךָ. מָחָה is "wipe out" (Gen 6:7; 7:23), of writing "blot out"; סֵפֶר is any written document, "book". What book this is the verse does not say (compare Ps 69:28; Isa 4:3; Dan 12:1).
- **"unto the place of which I have spoken" → "to that of which I have spoken"** (v34) — Hebrew אֶל אֲשֶׁר־דִּבַּרְתִּי; "place" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"mine Angel" → "my angel"** (v34) — Hebrew מַלְאָכִי; the word is "messenger".
- **"in the day when I visit I will visit their sin upon them" → "in the day of my visiting, I will visit their sin upon them"** (v34) — Hebrew פָּקַד twice: to attend to, here for punishment.
- **"plagued the people" → "smote the people"** (v35) — Hebrew וַיִּגֹּף, to strike with a blow from God (12:23, 27); "plague" as a noun is not in the verse.
- **"because they made the calf, which Aaron made" kept** (v35) — the Hebrew has "made" twice.

### Notes

- **The Golden Calf** — the catastrophic sin at the heart of Exodus. While Yahweh is giving Moses the tabernacle plans (Israel-accepted-holy-presence), the people below are making a calf (Israel-rejected-Yahweh's-form). The chapters juxtapose deliberately.
- **"A feast to Yahweh"** (v5) — Aaron proclaims the calf-festival as a feast to Yahweh, not to a foreign god. The sin may be the representing of Yahweh by an image (20:4) rather than the worship of another god; the calf may have been meant as a pedestal for the unseen Yahweh, as with Jeroboam's calves (1 Kgs 12:28). The text does not decide.
- **Moses' intercession (v11–14)** — one of the most remarkable passages of human-divine negotiation. Moses invokes Yahweh's own name, reputation, and covenant-oath. Yahweh "relents." Theological tension: divine sovereignty and responsive engagement with covenantal partner.
- **"Blot me out"** (v32) — Moses asks to be wiped out of Yahweh's book if the people are not forgiven. Compare Paul's wish in Rom 9:3.
- **Levi's loyalty** (v26–29) — the Levites' response to the calf-crisis (by violent zeal against their own kin) becomes the basis for their priestly / Levitical status. Their willingness to put covenant loyalty over kinship earns them the priesthood in perpetuity.
- **"Aaron made the calf" — and Aaron survives** — a puzzling feature. Aaron's complicity in the calf is not punished personally (he continues as high priest). The text lets this tension stand.
