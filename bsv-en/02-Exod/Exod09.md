## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 13, 28, 30) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. **"children of Israel" → "sons of Israel"** (v4, 6, 26, 35) — Hebrew בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
- **"and tell him" → "and you shall speak to him"** (v1) — Hebrew וְדִבַּרְתָּ; in v13 וְאָמַרְתָּ "you shall say".
- **"and wilt hold them still" → "and are still holding them fast"** (v2) — Hebrew מַחֲזִיק בָּם, the causative of חָזַק "be strong": "keep a strong hold on". It is the root used of Pharaoh's heart in v12, 35.
- **"the hand of the LORD is upon thy cattle" → "the hand of Yahweh is on your livestock"** (v3) — Hebrew הוֹיָה, a participle of "be" found only here: "is being, is about to be". מִקְנֶה is "livestock"; בָּקָר "herd" (cattle) and צֹאן "flock" (sheep and goats).
- **"a very grievous murrain" → "a very heavy pestilence"** (v3) — Hebrew דֶּבֶר כָּבֵד מְאֹד. כָּבֵד "heavy" is the word of this chapter: the pestilence (v3), the heart of Pharaoh (v7, 34), the hail (v18, 24).
- **"shall sever" → "will distinguish"** (v4) — Hebrew וְהִפְלָה, as in 8:22. **"there shall nothing die" → "not a thing shall die"** — Hebrew דָּבָר "a thing, a word", which sounds like דֶּבֶר "pestilence" (v3); the same word in v5, 6 ("this thing").
- **"all the cattle of Egypt died"** (v6) — so the Hebrew, כֹּל מִקְנֵה מִצְרָיִם; yet livestock is named again in v9, 19–21. The livestock "in the field" (v3) may be meant, or "all" in the broad sense of "all kinds". The verse says what the Hebrew says.
- **"the heart of Pharaoh was hardened" → "the heart of Pharaoh was heavy"** (v7) — Hebrew וַיִּכְבַּד לֵב פַּרְעֹה, the simple stem of כָּבֵד; no agent is named. In v34 Pharaoh "made his heart heavy" (וַיַּכְבֵּד לִבּוֹ), the causative. See 7:3.
- **"handfuls of ashes of the furnace" → "handfuls of soot of a kiln"** (v8, 10) — Hebrew מְלֹא חָפְנֵיכֶם, literally "the fullness of your two cupped hands"; פִּיחַ "soot" (only here); כִּבְשָׁן "kiln" (Gen 19:28). **"let Moses sprinkle it" → "let Moses toss it"** — Hebrew זָרַק "throw, toss, scatter" (the word used of dashing blood from a bowl).
- **"small dust" → "fine dust"** (v9) — Hebrew אָבָק, another word than עָפָר "dust" (8:16).
- **"a boil breaking forth with blains" → "boils breaking out in blisters"** (v9, 10) — Hebrew שְׁחִין "boil, inflamed sore" (a singular, used of the whole outbreak), פֹּרֵחַ "sprouting, breaking out", אֲבַעְבֻּעֹת, found only here: "blisters, pustules"; the exact sense is not certain.
- **"magicians"** (v11) — Hebrew חַרְטֻמִּים, as in 7:11. **"upon all the Egyptians" → "on all Egypt"** — Hebrew וּבְכָל־מִצְרָיִם.
- **"And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh" → "And Yahweh made strong the heart of Pharaoh"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיְחַזֵּק יְהוָה אֶת־לֵב פַּרְעֹה, the intensive stem (piel) of חָזַק "be strong", with Yahweh as the subject: the first time in the plague account that Yahweh is named as the one who does it (announced in 4:21; 7:3). In v35 the same verb stands in the simple stem with the heart as subject: "the heart of Pharaoh was strong". The verse keeps each as it is.
- **"stand before Pharaoh" → "take your stand before Pharaoh"** (v13) — Hebrew הִתְיַצֵּב.
- **"I will at this time send all my plagues upon thine heart" → "this time I am sending all my plagues to your heart"** (v14) — Hebrew אֲנִי שֹׁלֵחַ, a participle; מַגֵּפֹתַי, from נָגַף "smite": "my blows"; אֶל־לִבְּךָ "to your heart".
- **"For now I will stretch out my hand, that I may smite thee" → "For by now I could have stretched out my hand and struck you"** (v15) — Hebrew כִּי עַתָּה שָׁלַחְתִּי … וָאַךְ … וַתִּכָּחֵד, three past forms: literally "for now I have stretched out my hand and struck you … and you have been annihilated". As v16 shows ("but indeed …"), it is what could have been done and was not. **"cut off from the earth" → "annihilated from the earth"** — Hebrew נִכְחַד "be made to disappear".
- **"for this cause have I raised thee up" → "for the sake of this I have made you stand"** (v16) — Hebrew הֶעֱמַדְתִּיךָ, the causative of עָמַד "stand": "I have made you stand", that is, kept you standing, let you remain (after v15), or set you up. The Septuagint has "you were preserved"; Rom 9:17 cites the verse as "I raised you up" (both cited from memory). The verse keeps the Hebrew word.
- **"for to shew in thee my power" → "for the sake of showing you my power"** (v16) — Hebrew הַרְאֹתְךָ אֶת־כֹּחִי, "to make you see my power". "In thee" is the reading of the Septuagint and of Rom 9:17 (from memory); the Masoretic Text is kept. **"that my name may be declared" → "that my name may be recounted"** — Hebrew סַפֵּר שְׁמִי, an infinitive, "to recount my name"; who recounts is not said.
- **"As yet exaltest thou thyself against my people" → "You are still exalting yourself against my people"** (v17) — Hebrew מִסְתּוֹלֵל, found only here in this form, from סָלַל "heap up, raise (a road, a mound)": "raise yourself up" as a barrier against. The sense is fairly sure, the picture is not.
- **"I will cause it to rain a very grievous hail" → "I am causing a very heavy hail to rain"** (v18) — Hebrew הִנְנִי מַמְטִיר, a participle. **"since the foundation thereof" → "from the day it was founded"** — Hebrew לְמִן־הַיּוֹם הִוָּסְדָה.
- **"Send therefore now, and gather thy cattle" → "And now send, bring to safety your livestock"** (v19) — Hebrew הָעֵז, "bring into a place of refuge". **"shall not be brought home" → "is not gathered into the house"** — Hebrew יֵאָסֵף הַבַּיְתָה. **"man and beast"** (v9, 10, 19, 22, 25) — Hebrew אָדָם, בְּהֵמָה, collective singulars.
- **"made his servants … flee" → "made his slaves … flee"** (v20, 21) — one Hebrew word, עֶבֶד, twice: "the servants of Pharaoh" (the king's men) and "his slaves" (the bondmen of each of them). **"regarded not" → "did not set his heart to"** (v21) — Hebrew לֹא־שָׂם לִבּוֹ אֶל.
- **"toward heaven" → "toward the heavens"** (v22, 23) — Hebrew עַל־הַשָּׁמַיִם, literally "over / upon the heavens". **"herb" → "plant"** (v22, 25) — Hebrew עֵשֶׂב.
- **"the LORD sent thunder" → "Yahweh gave thunders"** (v23, 28, 29, 33, 34) — Hebrew קֹלֹת, literally "voices": the Hebrew word for thunder is the plural of קוֹל "voice". In v28 קֹלֹת אֱלֹהִים is "voices of Elohim": thunder that comes from Elohim, or "mighty thunder" (אֱלֹהִים as a superlative). **"the fire ran along upon the ground" → "fire went to the earth"** (v23) — Hebrew וַתִּהֲלַךְ אֵשׁ אָרְצָה.
- **"fire mingled with the hail" → "fire flashing in the midst of the hail"** (v24) — Hebrew אֵשׁ מִתְלַקַּחַת, literally "fire taking hold of itself" (the reflexive of לָקַח "take"; again only Ezek 1:4): fire that keeps catching, flashing back and forth. The exact sense is not certain.
- **"brake every tree" → "broke in pieces"** (v25) — Hebrew שִׁבֵּר, the intensive of "break".
- **"the LORD is righteous, and I and my people are wicked" → "Yahweh is the righteous one, and I and my people are the wicked ones"** (v27) — the Hebrew has the article on both: הַצַּדִּיק … הָרְשָׁעִים, the words of a court: the one in the right, the ones in the wrong.
- **"Intreat the LORD (for it is enough) that there be no more mighty thunderings" → "Plead with Yahweh, for there has been enough of the thunders of *Elohim*"** (v28) — Hebrew וְרַב מִהְיֹת, literally "and much from there being": it is more than enough. **"ye shall stay no longer"** — Hebrew לֹא תֹסִפוּן לַעֲמֹד, literally "you shall not add to stand".
- **"I will spread abroad my hands" → "I will spread out my palms"** (v29, 33) — Hebrew כַּפַּי, the open palms of one who prays. **"the earth is the LORD's"** — Hebrew לַיהוָה הָאָרֶץ; אֶרֶץ is "earth" or "land".
- **"ye will not yet fear the LORD God" → "you do not yet fear before Yahweh *Elohim*"** (v30) — Hebrew טֶרֶם תִּירְאוּן מִפְּנֵי, literally "you do not yet fear from the face of". Both names stand together, יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים, as in Gen 2–3.
- **"the barley was in the ear, and the flax was bolled" → "the barley was in the ear and the flax was in bud"** (v31) — Hebrew אָבִיב, the fresh young ears (the name of the month of the going out, 13:4); גִּבְעֹל, found only here: "bud" or "seed pod"; uncertain. **"smitten" → "struck"** (v31, 32) — Hebrew נֻכָּה, the passive of הִכָּה "strike".
- **"the rie" → "the spelt"; "they were not grown up" → "they are late"** (v32) — Hebrew כֻּסֶּמֶת, a kind of wheat (emmer or spelt), not rye; אֲפִילֹת "late", found only here. The parentheses of KJV in v31–32 are not in the Hebrew.
- **"the rain was not poured upon the earth" → "rain did not pour down on the earth"** (v33) — Hebrew נִתַּךְ אָרְצָה.
- **"he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart" → "he sinned yet more and made his heart heavy"** (v34) — Hebrew וַיֹּסֶף לַחֲטֹא, literally "he added to sin"; וַיַּכְבֵּד לִבּוֹ, Pharaoh the subject.
- **"the heart of Pharaoh was hardened" → "the heart of Pharaoh was strong"** (v35) — Hebrew וַיֶּחֱזַק. **"by Moses" → "by the hand of Moses"** — Hebrew בְּיַד־מֹשֶׁה.

### Notes

- **"Yahweh made strong the heart of Pharaoh" (v12)** — in the plagues before this the text says "the heart of Pharaoh was strong" (7:13, 22; 8:19), "is heavy / was heavy" (7:14; 9:7) or "Pharaoh made his heart heavy" (8:15, 32). In this chapter all three ways of speaking stand side by side (v7, 12, 34, 35). Paul draws on v16 in Rom 9:17–18.
- **Pharaoh's "I have sinned" (v27)** — said again in 10:16. When the pressure lifts he goes back on it (v34).
- **The barley and the flax (v31–32)** — the note dates the hail to the early part of the year, when barley is in the ear, and prepares for the locusts, which eat what the hail has left (10:5, 15).
