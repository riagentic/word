# Song of Songs 1

[1] The song of the songs, which is ⟦solomon|Shlomo's|Solomon's⟧.

[2] Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth!
For your love is better than wine;

[3] as for smell, your oils are good;
oil poured out is your name;
therefore young women love you.

[4] Draw me after you; let us run.
The king has brought me into his chambers.
Let us exult and rejoice in you;
let us make mention of your love more than wine;
uprightly they love you.

[5] I am black and lovely,
daughters of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧,
like the tents of ⟦kedar|Qedar|Kedar⟧,
like the curtains of ⟦solomon|Shlomo|Solomon⟧.

[6] Do not look at me, that I am blackish,
that the sun has looked upon me.
The sons of my mother burned with anger against me;
they set me as keeper of the vineyards;
my vineyard, my own, I have not kept.

[7] Tell me, you whom my soul loves,
where you pasture,
where you make them lie down at noon;
for why should I be like one who veils herself
beside the droves of your comrades?

[8] If you yourself do not know, most beautiful among women,
get yourself out in the tracks of the flock,
and pasture your kids
beside the dwellings of the shepherds.

[9] To my mare among the chariots of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧
I have likened you, my companion.

[10] Lovely are your cheeks with circlets,
your neck with strings of beads.

[11] Circlets of gold we will make for you,
with studs of silver.

[12] While the king was at his table,
my nard gave its smell.

[13] A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me;
between my breasts he lodges.

[14] A cluster of henna is my beloved to me,
in the vineyards of ⟦en-gedi|Ein Gedi|En-gedi⟧.

[15] Behold, you are beautiful, my companion;
behold, you are beautiful;
your eyes are doves.

[16] Behold, you are beautiful, my beloved, yes, pleasant;
yes, our couch is luxuriant;

[17] the beams of our houses are cedars;
our rafters are cypresses.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order. The blank line inside v4 is taken out: the Hebrew has no break there, and the Hebrew marks no speakers.
- **"The song of songs, which is Solomon's" → "The song of the songs, which is Solomon's"** (v1) — Hebrew שִׁיר הַשִּׁירִים אֲשֶׁר לִשְׁלֹמֹה; the article on "the songs" kept; the construct "song of the songs" is the Hebrew superlative ("the finest song"). לְ "of / for / belonging to" (as לְדָוִד in the Psalm headings): the verse has "which is Solomon's", the other senses are open.
- **"thy love" → "your love"** (v2, 4) — Hebrew דֹּדֶיךָ, plural "your loves" (דֹּדִים: love as shown, caresses); English uses the singular. אַהֲבָה, the other Hebrew word for "love", does not stand in this chapter.
- **"Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth"** (v2) — Hebrew מִנְּשִׁיקוֹת, literally "from the kisses" (some of his kisses). The speaker turns from "him" to "you" within the verse: as printed.
- **"Because of the savour of thy good ointments" → "as for smell, your oils are good"** (v3) — Hebrew לְרֵיחַ שְׁמָנֶיךָ טוֹבִים, "for smell your oils are good"; רֵיחַ "smell" (as Gen 8:21), שֶׁמֶן "oil".
- **"thy name is as ointment poured forth" → "oil poured out is your name"** (v3) — Hebrew שֶׁמֶן תּוּרַק שְׁמֶךָ, Hebrew order, no "as"; תּוּרַק is feminine beside the masculine שֶׁמֶן (a hard form; MT kept).
- **"the virgins" → "young women"** (v3) — Hebrew עֲלָמוֹת, plural of עַלְמָה "young woman" (בְּתוּלָה is "virgin"). **"love thee"** — Hebrew perfect אֲהֵבוּךָ, "have loved you", said of what holds; also v4.
- **"the king hath brought me"** (v4) — Hebrew perfect הֱבִיאַנִי; some take it as a wish ("may the king bring me"). חֲדָרִים "chambers" (inner rooms).
- **"we will be glad and rejoice … we will remember" → "Let us exult and rejoice … let us make mention of"** (v4) — Hebrew cohortatives נָגִילָה וְנִשְׂמְחָה … נַזְכִּירָה; זָכַר hifil "make remembered, call to mind" (to praise). **"the upright love thee" → "uprightly they love you"** — Hebrew מֵישָׁרִים "uprightness" (as Ps 9:8), used as an adverb ("with uprightness", "rightly"); "the upright" as subject is also read.
- **"I am black, but comely" → "I am black and lovely"** (v5) — Hebrew שְׁחוֹרָה אֲנִי וְנָאוָה; וְ "and", as printed ("but" reads a contrast into it). **"O daughters" → "daughters"** — no vocative word in the Hebrew.
- **"as the curtains of Solomon" → "like the curtains of Solomon"** (v5) — Hebrew יְרִיעוֹת, the lengths of tent cloth (as Exod 26). Some read "Salmah" (a tribe) for שְׁלֹמֹה; the MT has "Solomon".
- **"Look not upon me, because I am black" → "Do not look at me, that I am blackish"** (v6) — Hebrew שְׁחַרְחֹרֶת, the doubled form of שָׁחוֹר "black": "blackish" or "very black" (uncertain which; the root kept). שֶׁ "that" (= because).
- **"the sun hath looked upon me"** (v6) — Hebrew שְׁזָפַתְנִי, "has looked on me" (שָׁזַף, as Job 20:9; 28:7); "scorched" is the sense often given.
- **"my mother's children were angry with me" → "The sons of my mother burned with anger against me"** (v6) — Hebrew בְּנֵי אִמִּי "sons of my mother"; נִחֲרוּ בִי, nifal of חָרָה "burn" (lit. "burned against me").
- **"they made me the keeper … mine own vineyard have I not kept" → "they set me as keeper … my vineyard, my own, I have not kept"** (v6) — Hebrew שָׂמֻנִי "set me"; נָטַר "keep" both times (keeper / kept); כַּרְמִי שֶׁלִּי "my vineyard, which is mine", Hebrew order.
- **"where thou feedest, where thou makest thy flock to rest" → "where you pasture, where you make them lie down"** (v7) — Hebrew תִרְעֶה "you pasture" and תַּרְבִּיץ "you make lie down", both without an object: "thy flock" is not written; "them" is supplied because English cannot stand without an object. צָהֳרַיִם "noon".
- **"one that turneth aside" → "one who veils herself"** (v7) — Hebrew כְּעֹטְיָה, participle of עָטָה "wrap, cover" (as Lev 13:45). A reading with the letters turned about, "like one who strays" (טֹעִיָּה), is followed by some (from memory); MT kept. שַׁלָּמָה "for why".
- **"by the flocks of thy companions" → "beside the droves of your comrades"** (v7) — Hebrew עַל עֶדְרֵי חֲבֵרֶיךָ; עֵדֶר "drove" (a single herd or flock); חָבֵר "comrade" (kept apart from רַעְיָה "companion", v9, 15).
- **"If thou know not, O thou fairest among women" → "If you yourself do not know, most beautiful among women"** (v8) — Hebrew אִם־לֹא תֵדְעִי לָךְ, with לָךְ "for yourself"; הַיָּפָה בַּנָּשִׁים, literally "the beautiful among women" (the Hebrew superlative).
- **"go thy way forth by the footsteps of the flock" → "get yourself out in the tracks of the flock"** (v8) — Hebrew צְאִי־לָךְ, imperative with לָךְ (the "Get yourself" of Gen 12:1); עִקְּבֵי "heels" (tracks); צֹאן "flock".
- **"feed thy kids beside the shepherds' tents" → "pasture your kids beside the dwellings of the shepherds"** (v8) — Hebrew גְּדִיֹּתַיִךְ (feminine plural of גְּדִי "kid"); מִשְׁכְּנוֹת "dwellings" (מִשְׁכָּן, not אֹהֶל "tent").
- **"I have compared thee, O my love, to a company of horses in Pharaoh's chariots" → "To my mare among the chariots of Pharaoh I have likened you, my companion"** (v9) — Hebrew לְסֻסָתִי, "to my mare" (the -י may also be an old ending without "my": uncertain); רֶכֶב "chariots" (a collective); דִּמִּיתִיךְ "I have likened you". רַעְיָתִי "my companion" (also v15), not "my love".
- **"Thy cheeks are comely with rows of jewels, thy neck with chains of gold" → "Lovely are your cheeks with circlets, your neck with strings of beads"** (v10) — Hebrew נָאווּ "are lovely" (the verb of נָאוָה, v5); תֹּרִים "circlets" (rows, rings: the ornament is uncertain); חֲרוּזִים only here, "strings (of beads)", uncertain; "of gold" is not written. לְחִי "cheek" (the "jawbone" of Judg 15 is the same word).
- **"We will make thee borders of gold with studs of silver" → "Circlets of gold we will make for you, with studs of silver"** (v11) — Hebrew תּוֹרֵי זָהָב, the word of v10; Hebrew order; הַכָּסֶף has the article ("the silver").
- **"While the king sitteth at his table, my spikenard sendeth forth the smell thereof" → "While the king was at his table, my nard gave its smell"** (v12) — Hebrew בִּמְסִבּוֹ, from סָבַב "go around": his round table, or the couch where those at table lie (uncertain); נֵרְדְּ "nard" (spikenard); נָתַן "gave".
- **"A bundle of myrrh is my wellbeloved unto me; he shall lie all night betwixt my breasts" → "A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me; between my breasts he lodges"** (v13) — Hebrew צְרוֹר הַמֹּר, with the article ("of the myrrh": the kind, as English says "of myrrh"); יָלִין "lodges, spends the night" (לוּן); its subject may be the beloved or the bundle (both masculine).
- **"a cluster of camphire" → "a cluster of henna"** (v14) — Hebrew אֶשְׁכֹּל הַכֹּפֶר; כֹּפֶר here is the henna plant (the same letters as כֹּפֶר "ransom", Exod 30:12); אֶשְׁכֹּל "cluster" (as of grapes).
- **"Behold, thou art fair, my love … thou hast doves' eyes" → "Behold, you are beautiful, my companion … your eyes are doves"** (v15) — Hebrew הִנָּךְ יָפָה; עֵינַיִךְ יוֹנִים "your eyes, doves": no "like".
- **"yea, pleasant: also our bed is green" → "yes, pleasant; yes, our couch is luxuriant"** (v16) — Hebrew אַף … אַף "yes … yes"; נָעִים "pleasant"; עֶרֶשׂ "couch"; רַעֲנָן "luxuriant" (fresh, full of leaves: of a couch in the open, among leaves, or of its look; not the colour word יָרֹק).
- **"The beams of our house are cedar, and our rafters of fir" → "the beams of our houses are cedars; our rafters are cypresses"** (v17) — Hebrew בָּתֵּינוּ, plural "our houses"; אֲרָזִים "cedars". **Qere**: רַהִיטֵנוּ "our rafters"; the written form רחיטנו has ח for ה, the same word (the sense "rafters, panels" is uncertain; another word than רַהַט "gutter", Gen 30:38). בְּרוֹתִים = בְּרוֹשִׁים "cypresses" (another spelling; the tree is not certain: juniper or fir).

### Notes
- **Speakers** — the Hebrew has no speaker names; the grammar shows a woman speaking in v2–7, 12–14, 16–17 (feminine "I", "my breasts", "my beloved") and a man addressing a woman in v9–10, 15 ("my companion", feminine "you"); v8 and v11 address her with no speaker marked. The verse carries none of this.
- **Allegory** — Jewish reading: Yahweh and Israel; Christian reading: Christ and the church. The plain sense is a love poem between a man and a woman.
