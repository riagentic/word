# Song of Songs 5

[1] I have come to my garden, my sister, bride;
I have plucked my myrrh with my spice,
I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey,
I have drunk my wine with my milk.

Eat, friends;
drink and be drunk, beloved ones!

[2] I was asleep, and my heart was awake.
The voice of my beloved, knocking:

"Open to me, my sister, my companion,
my dove, my flawless one,
for my head is filled with dew,
my locks with the drops of night."

[3] I have put off my tunic;
how shall I put it on?
I have washed my feet;
how shall I soil them?

[4] My beloved stretched out his hand from the hole,
and my inward parts were in turmoil over him.

[5] I myself rose to open to my beloved,
and my hands dripped myrrh,
and my fingers liquid myrrh,
upon the handles of the lock.

[6] I myself opened to my beloved,
and my beloved had turned away, had passed on.
My soul went out when he spoke.
I sought him, and I did not find him;
I called him, and he did not answer me.

[7] The watchmen found me,
who go around in the city;
they struck me, they wounded me;
they lifted my veil from upon me,
the watchmen of the walls.

[8] I make you swear, daughters of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧:
if you find my beloved,
what shall you tell him?
That I am sick with love.

[9] What is your beloved more than a beloved,
most beautiful among women?
What is your beloved more than a beloved,
that you have thus made us swear?

[10] My beloved is radiant and ruddy,
distinguished above ten thousand.

[11] His head is gold, fine gold;
his locks are wavy, black as the raven.

[12] His eyes are like doves
by channels of water,
washing in milk,
sitting upon fullness.

[13] His cheeks are like the bed of spice,
towers of perfumes;
his lips are lilies,
dripping liquid myrrh.

[14] His hands are rings of gold,
filled with beryl;
his inward parts are a polished work of ivory,
covered with sapphires.

[15] His legs are pillars of marble,
founded upon sockets of fine gold;
his appearance is like ⟦lebanon|Levanon|Lebanon⟧,
choice as the cedars.

[16] His palate is sweetness,
and all of him is desirable.
This is my beloved, and this is my friend,
daughters of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; v13 and v15 now follow the accents.
- **"I am come … I have gathered … I have eaten … I have drunk" → "I have come … I have plucked … I have eaten … I have drunk"** (v1) — Hebrew perfects. אָרִיתִי is from אָרָה "pluck" (Ps 80:12), kept apart from לָקַט "gather" (6:2). כַּלָּה has no suffix: "bride", not "my bride". יַעַר is "honeycomb" here (as יַעְרַת הַדְּבַשׁ 1 Sam 14:27; elsewhere "forest").
- **"my spice"** (v1) — Hebrew בֹּשֶׂם (singular; plural בְּשָׂמִים "spices", 8:14), the spice plant or its perfume.
- **"drink, yea, drink abundantly, O beloved" → "drink and be drunk, beloved ones"** (v1) — Hebrew שְׁתוּ וְשִׁכְרוּ דּוֹדִים: שָׁכַר is "be drunk". דּוֹדִים stands beside רֵעִים "friends" and is taken as the same kind of word, a vocative "beloved ones" (plural of דּוֹד); it can also be the noun דֹּדִים "love" (lit. "loves"): "be drunk with love" — but then "with" must be supplied. The previous "be drunk with love" took the second reading.
- **"I sleep, but my heart waketh" → "I was asleep, and my heart was awake"** (v2) — two clauses without a verb (participle יְשֵׁנָה, adjective עֵר); the time is taken from the past verbs of v3–6. וְ "and".
- **"It is the voice of my beloved that knocketh" → "The voice of my beloved, knocking"** (v2) — Hebrew קוֹל דּוֹדִי דוֹפֵק; קוֹל "voice", here the sound of his knocking (as 2:8 "The voice of my beloved"); no verb.
- **"my love" → "my companion"** (v2) — Hebrew רַעְיָתִי. **"my undefiled" → "my flawless one"** — Hebrew תַּמָּתִי, feminine of תָּם (the row's "blameless", lit. "whole, complete"); said of her whole person, not of a moral record, so "flawless"; kept against the row (also 6:9).
- **"my head is filled with dew"** (v2) — Hebrew נִמְלָא, nifal of מָלֵא "fill" (was "wet with"). **"the drops of the night" → "the drops of night"** — לַיְלָה has no article.
- **"coat" → "tunic"** (v3) — Hebrew כֻּתֹּנֶת (row כְּתֹנֶת). Perfects "I have put off … I have washed"; "how shall I" for the imperfects. "defile" → "soil": טִנֵּף, "make dirty".
- **"put in his hand by the hole of the door" → "stretched out his hand from the hole"** (v4) — Hebrew שָׁלַח יָדוֹ מִן־הַחֹר; מִן "from": whether the hand goes in through the hole or is drawn back from it is not said. "of the door" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"my bowels were moved for him" → "my inward parts were in turmoil over him"** (v4) — Hebrew מֵעַי הָמוּ עָלָיו; מֵעִים "inward parts" (row; lit. "bowels"), הָמָה "be in turmoil" (as Ps 42:5); עָלָיו "over him, because of him".
- **"I rose up" → "I myself rose"; "I opened" → "I myself opened"** (v5, 6) — Hebrew קַמְתִּי אֲנִי, פָּתַחְתִּי אֲנִי: the pronoun after the verb is emphatic.
- **"sweet smelling myrrh" → "liquid myrrh"** (v5, 13) — Hebrew מוֹר עֹבֵר, lit. "passing myrrh": myrrh that flows (עָבַר "pass"); not "flowing myrrh", which is kept for מָר־דְּרוֹר (Exod 30:23). "and my fingers" — the וְ is kept.
- **"upon the handles of the lock"** (v5) — Hebrew כַּפּוֹת הַמַּנְעוּל, lit. "the palms of the lock" (כַּף "palm"); מַנְעוּל from נָעַל "lock".
- **"had withdrawn himself, and was gone" → "had turned away, had passed on"** (v6) — Hebrew חָמַק עָבָר, two verbs without "and". **"my soul failed when he spake" → "My soul went out when he spoke"** — Hebrew נַפְשִׁי יָצְאָה בְדַבְּרוֹ, lit. "in his speaking". **"he gave me no answer" → "he did not answer me"** — וְלֹא "and not" twice (was "but").
- **"The watchmen that went about the city" → "The watchmen found me, who go around in the city"** (v7) — Hebrew order; participle with the article, as 3:3. **"smote me, they wounded me"** — הִכָּה "strike", פָּצַע "wound". **"took away my veil from me" → "lifted my veil from upon me"** — נָשָׂא "lift"; רְדִיד is found again only in Isa 3:23 (a veil or wrap: uncertain). **"the keepers of the walls" → "the watchmen of the walls"** — the same שֹׁמְרִים as the first line.
- **"I charge you" → "I make you swear"** (v8, 9) — Hebrew הִשְׁבַּעְתִּי, hifil of נִשְׁבַּע, lit. "I make you swear" (row "make swear"), as the refrain 2:7; 3:5; 8:4. **"that ye tell him" → "what shall you tell him? That I am sick with love"** — Hebrew מַה־תַּגִּידוּ לוֹ, "what shall you tell him", answered by שֶׁ "that". No "O" before the vocative.
- **"more than another beloved" → "more than a beloved"** (v9) — Hebrew מִדּוֹד, no "another". **"O thou fairest among women" → "most beautiful among women"** — Hebrew הַיָּפָה בַּנָּשִׁים, lit. "the beautiful one among women" (a superlative). **"that thou dost so charge us" → "that you have thus made us swear"** — perfect.
- **"white and ruddy" → "radiant and ruddy"** (v10) — Hebrew צַח, "dazzling, bright" (Lam 4:7). **"the chiefest among ten thousand" → "distinguished above ten thousand"** — Hebrew דָּגוּל מֵרְבָבָה; דָּגוּל only here, from the root of דֶּגֶל "standard" (2:4; 6:4, 10): "marked out as by a standard" (uncertain); מִן "more than".
- **"the most fine gold" → "gold, fine gold"** (v11) — Hebrew כֶּתֶם פָּז, two words for gold (rows: כֶּתֶם "gold", פָּז "fine gold"). **"bushy" → "wavy"** — Hebrew תַּלְתַּלִּים, only here: uncertain ("curls", or "date clusters"). **"as a raven" → "as the raven"** — the article.
- **"by the rivers of waters" → "by channels of water"** (v12) — Hebrew אֲפִיקֵי מָיִם (reviewer row: אָפִיק "channel"); עַל lit. "upon". **"washed with milk" → "washing in milk"** — active participle. **"fitly set" → "sitting upon fullness"** — Hebrew יֹשְׁבוֹת עַל־מִלֵּאת; מִלֵּאת only here, from מָלֵא "fill": a full pool, or a setting filled with a stone (as מִלֻּאָה Exod 28:17): uncertain, the word kept.
- **"as a bed of spices" → "like the bed of spice"** (v13) — Hebrew כַּעֲרוּגַת הַבֹּשֶׂם, singular, with the article. **"as sweet flowers" → "towers of perfumes"** — Hebrew מִגְדְּלוֹת מֶרְקָחִים as the Masoretes point it ("towers"); מֶרְקָח from רָקַח "blend perfume". Others read the same letters as a participle, "producing perfumes"; not the MT.
- **"His hands are as gold rings set with the beryl" → "His hands are rings of gold, filled with beryl"** (v14) — Hebrew יָדָיו "his hands" (not "arms"); גְּלִילִים "rings" (or "rods": uncertain, as Esth 1:6); מְמֻלָּאִים "filled" (מִלֵּא of stones); תַּרְשִׁישׁ "beryl" (the "Tarshish stone": the identification is traditional, not sure). **"his belly is as bright ivory overlaid with sapphires" → "his inward parts are a polished work of ivory, covered with sapphires"** — מֵעִים "inward parts" (row; the outward belly is meant here: "body" removed); עֶשֶׁת only here: "a work" or "plate", "polished" from עָשַׁת "be sleek" (Jer 5:28): uncertain; שֵׁן "ivory", lit. "tooth"; מְעֻלֶּפֶת "covered"; סַפִּיר "sapphire" (probably lapis lazuli).
- **"pillars of marble, set upon sockets of fine gold"** (v15) — Hebrew עַמּוּדֵי שֵׁשׁ מְיֻסָּדִים עַל־אַדְנֵי־פָז: שֵׁשׁ of stone "marble" (or alabaster: uncertain), "founded" (יָסַד), אֶדֶן "socket". "legs" — שׁוֹק. **"excellent as the cedars" → "choice as the cedars"** — בָּחוּר. Hebrew כַּלְּבָנוֹן has the article ("the Lebanon").
- **"His mouth is most sweet: yea, he is altogether lovely" → "His palate is sweetness, and all of him is desirable"** (v16) — Hebrew חִכּוֹ מַמְתַקִּים וְכֻלּוֹ מַחֲמַדִּים: חֵךְ "palate" (mouth as the organ of taste and speech); both nouns are plural ("sweetnesses", "desirable things": the plural of fullness). "This is my beloved, and this is my friend": רֵעַ "friend" (row; as רֵעִים v1).

### Notes
- **Who speaks** — the Hebrew marks the speaker only by grammar: v1 a man ("my sister, bride"); v2–8 a woman (יְשֵׁנָה, feminine); v9 a group speaks to the woman (דּוֹדֵךְ "your", feminine; "made us swear"); v10–16 the woman.
