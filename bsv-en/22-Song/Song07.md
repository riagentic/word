# Song of Songs 7

[1] How beautiful are your feet in sandals,
daughter of a noble!
The curves of your thighs are like ornaments,
the work of the hands of a craftsman.

[2] Your navel is a bowl of roundness;
may it not lack the mixed wine!
Your belly is a heap of wheat,
fenced about with lilies.

[3] Your two breasts are like two fawns,
twins of a gazelle.

[4] Your neck is like the tower of ivory;
your eyes are pools in ⟦heshbon|Cheshbon|Heshbon⟧,
by the gate of ⟦bath-rabbim|Bat-Rabbim|Bath-rabbim⟧;
your nose is like the tower of ⟦lebanon|Levanon|Lebanon⟧,
watching the face of ⟦damascus|Dammeseq|Damascus⟧.

[5] Your head upon you is like ⟦carmel|Karmel|Carmel⟧,
and the hanging hair of your head is like purple;
a king is bound in the tresses.

[6] How beautiful you are, and how pleasant,
love, in the delights!

[7] This your stature is like a palm tree,
and your breasts like clusters.

[8] I said, I will go up into the palm tree;
let me take hold of its fruit stalks;
and now may your breasts be like clusters of the vine,
and the smell of your nose like apples,

[9] and your palate like the best wine.

It goes to my beloved smoothly,
gliding over the lips of sleepers.

[10] I am my beloved's,
and upon me is his desire.

[11] Come, my beloved, let us go out to the field;
let us lodge in the villages.

[12] Let us rise early to the vineyards;
let us see whether the vine has budded,
whether the bloom has opened,
whether the pomegranates have blossomed;
there I will give my love to you.

[13] The mandrakes have given a smell,
and over our entrances are all choice things,
new and also old;
my beloved, I have hidden them for you.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order.
- **"with shoes, O prince's daughter" → "in sandals, daughter of a noble"** (v1; Heb 7:2) — Hebrew בַּת־נָדִיב, lit. "daughter of a noble one" (נָדִיב, as 6:12); no "O". פַּעַם "foot" (row; "step" where walking is meant). **"The joints of thy thighs" → "The curves of your thighs"** — חַמּוּקִים only here, from חָמַק "turn" (5:6): "turnings, curves". **"jewels" → "ornaments"** — חֲלָאִים (Prov 25:12 חֲלִי). **"the work of the hands of a cunning workman" → "of a craftsman"** — אָמָּן only here.
- **"Thy navel is like a round goblet, which wanteth not liquor" → "Your navel is a bowl of roundness; may it not lack the mixed wine!"** (v2) — Hebrew שָׁרְרֵךְ אַגַּן הַסַּהַר: שֹׁר "navel" (Ezek 16:4; some take it of the parts below); סַהַר only here ("roundness", or "moon": uncertain); no "like". אַל־יֶחְסַר is a negative jussive, a wish ("may it not lack"), not a statement; מֶזֶג "mixed wine", only here, with the article. **"set about with lilies" → "fenced about with lilies"** — סוּגָה, "hedged, fenced".
- **"two young roes that are twins" → "two fawns, twins of a gazelle"** (v3) — עֳפָרִים "fawns" (Song 1–4 row; as 4:5; also 8:14), צְבִיָּה "gazelle" (female; row צְבִי).
- **"a tower of ivory" → "the tower of ivory"** (v4) — Hebrew כְּמִגְדַּל הַשֵּׁן, with the article; צַוָּאר "neck" (row). **"fishpools" → "pools"** — בְּרֵכוֹת. **"thy nose" — kept** — Hebrew אַף, the body meant (standing decision: "nose"). **"which looketh toward Damascus" → "watching the face of Damascus"** — צוֹפֶה "watching" (the participle of the "watchman" row), פְּנֵי "the face of" (= facing, looking over toward).
- **"Thine head upon thee is like Carmel" — kept, word for word** (v5) — Hebrew רֹאשֵׁךְ עָלַיִךְ כַּכַּרְמֶל; Carmel is the mountain (also "orchard land"; the like-sounding כַּרְמִיל "crimson", 2 Chr 2:7, is another word). **"the hair of thine head like purple" → "the hanging hair of your head is like purple"** — דַּלָּה, what hangs loose (threads, Isa 38:12); אַרְגָּמָן "purple" (row). **"the king is held in the galleries" → "a king is bound in the tresses"** — מֶלֶךְ without the article; אָסוּר "bound" (אָסַר); רְהָטִים elsewhere only "troughs, gutters" for watering the flock (Gen 30:38, 41; row "gutter"); here of the hair, its flowing locks: the row's word would say a water-channel in a description of the head, so "tresses", kept against the row (lit. "runnels").
- **"O love, for delights!" → "love, in the delights!"** (v6) — Hebrew אַהֲבָה בַּתַּעֲנוּגִים: the vocative "love" (not "loved one"), בְּ "in", the article kept; perfects of state "how beautiful you are, and how pleasant" (נָעַם, row נָעִים "pleasant").
- **"This thy stature is like to a palm tree" → "This your stature is like a palm tree"** (v7) — Hebrew זֹאת קוֹמָתֵךְ דָּמְתָה, "this, your stature, resembles"; "and your breasts like clusters": the verb serves both lines; אֶשְׁכֹּל "cluster" (row).
- **"I said, I will go up to the palm tree, I will take hold of the boughs thereof" → "I said, I will go up into the palm tree; let me take hold of its fruit stalks"** (v8) — אָמַרְתִּי perfect; אֹחֲזָה cohortative; סַנְסִנִּים only here ("fruit stalks", "branches": uncertain). **"now also thy breasts shall be as clusters of the vine" → "and now may your breasts be like clusters of the vine"** — וְיִהְיוּ־נָא, jussive with נָא "now". **"the smell of thy nose like apples" — the Hebrew אַף "nose" restored** (was "breath"); רֵיחַ "smell" (row); תַּפּוּחַ "apple" (the kind of fruit is not certain).
- **"And the roof of thy mouth like the best wine" → "and your palate like the best wine"** (v9; Heb 7:10) — חֵךְ "palate" (as 5:16), lit. "like the wine of the good". **"that goeth down sweetly" → "It goes to my beloved smoothly"** — Hebrew הוֹלֵךְ לְדוֹדִי לְמֵישָׁרִים, "going to my beloved, for straightness"; מֵישָׁרִים is "uprightness" in its row (Ps 9:8, of justice); of wine it is "smoothly", as Prov 23:31: kept against the row. The man's words end in the woman's "my beloved" with no mark of a new speaker. **"causing the lips of those that are asleep to speak" → "gliding over the lips of sleepers"** — Hebrew דּוֹבֵב (only here: "gliding, flowing", or "making to murmur") and יְשֵׁנִים "sleepers", as the MT reads; the previous "lips and teeth" follows another reading (from memory: שִׂנַּיִם "teeth" for יְשֵׁנִים, as some of the old versions), not the MT.
- **"his desire is toward me" → "upon me is his desire"** (v10) — Hebrew order; עַל "upon"; תְּשׁוּקָה "desire" (row; as Gen 3:16).
- **"into the field" → "to the field"** (v11) — הַשָּׂדֶה singular; "let us lodge in the villages" — כְּפָרִים "villages" (row; the same letters are "henna plants" in 1:14, 4:13: the other sense is possible here).
- **"Let us get up early to the vineyards" → "Let us rise early"** (v12) — הִשְׁכִּים (row). **"whether the vine flourish, whether the tender grape appear" → "whether the vine has budded, whether the bloom has opened"** — פָּרַח "bud" (row), סְמָדַר "bloom" (Song 1–4 row "in bloom", 2:13, 15; uncertain: the opening bud or blossom of the vine; here the subject of the verb, so the noun), פִּתַּח "opened". **"the pomegranates bud forth" → "have blossomed"** — as 6:11. **"there will I give thee my loves" → "there I will give my love to you"** — Hebrew דֹּדַי, lit. "my loves" (WISDOM: "love").
- **"The mandrakes give a smell" → "have given a smell"** (v13) — perfect נָתְנוּ; דּוּדָאִים "mandrakes" (row: the usual identification, not certain; root דּוֹד, a play on דֹּדַי v12). **"at our gates are all manner of pleasant fruits" → "over our entrances are all choice things"** — עַל "over, at"; פֶּתַח "entrance" (row); מְגָדִים "choice things" (row: no "fruits"). **"new and old" → "new and also old"** — גַּם "also". **"which I have laid up for thee, O my beloved" → "my beloved, I have hidden them for you"** — Hebrew order; צָפַן "hide" (row: also "store up"); "them" supplied, the Hebrew has no object; no "O".

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Song 7:1–13 is Heb 7:2–14 (our 6:13 is Heb 7:1). Our numbers are kept.
- **Who speaks** — v1–9a a man to a woman (feminine "your"); v9b–13 the woman ("my beloved", masculine "you").
