# Song of Songs 2

[1] I am a crocus of the Sharon,
a lily of the valleys.

[2] As a lily among the thistles,
so is my companion among the daughters.

[3] As an apple tree among the trees of the forest,
so is my beloved among the sons.
In his shade I took delight and sat,
and his fruit was sweet to my palate.

[4] He has brought me to the house of the wine,
and his standard over me is love.

[5] Sustain me with the raisin cakes;
refresh me with the apples,
for sick with love am I.

[6] His left is under my head,
and his right embraces me.

[7] I make you swear, daughters of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧,
by the gazelles or by the does of the field,
that you do not stir up and do not awaken love
until it pleases.

[8] The voice of my beloved!
Behold, there he comes,
leaping over the mountains,
bounding over the hills.

[9] My beloved is like a gazelle or a fawn of the deer.
Behold, there he stands behind our wall,
gazing through the windows,
peering through the lattices.

[10] My beloved answered and said to me,
"Get yourself up, my companion, my beautiful one,
and get yourself away;

[11] for behold, the winter has passed by;
the rain is over, it has gone its way.

[12] The blossoms have appeared in the land,
the time of singing has arrived,
and the voice of the turtledove
is heard in our land.

[13] The fig tree has ripened its early figs,
and the vines in bloom
have given smell.
Get yourself up, my companion, my beautiful one,
and get yourself away.

[14] My dove, in the clefts of the crag,
in the hiding place of the steep,
let me see your appearance,
let me hear your voice,
for your voice is pleasing,
and your appearance is lovely.

[15] Seize for us foxes,
little foxes,
that ruin vineyards,
and our vineyards are in bloom."

[16] My beloved is mine, and I am his,
who pastures among the lilies.

[17] Until the day breathes
and the shadows flee,
turn, my beloved, make yourself like a gazelle
or a fawn of the deer on the mountains of Bether.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; v10, 13 keep the break inside the line where the accents mark a pause. The blank line inside v10 is taken out: the Hebrew has none.
- **"I am the rose of Sharon" → "I am a crocus of the Sharon"** (v1) — Hebrew חֲבַצֶּלֶת הַשָּׁרוֹן; חֲבַצֶּלֶת (also Isa 35:1) is a meadow flower, "crocus" or "asphodel": uncertain; not a rose. הַשָּׁרוֹן has the article: the plain of the Sharon.
- **"the lily of the valleys" → "a lily of the valleys"** (v1, 2, 16) — Hebrew שׁוֹשַׁנָּה; the flower is not certain (lily, lotus or another bloom); "lily" kept throughout the book.
- **"among thorns … among the daughters" → "among the thistles … among the daughters"** (v2) — Hebrew הַחוֹחִים, with the article (חוֹחַ "thistle", as 2 Kgs 14:9); בָּנוֹת "daughters". **"my love" → "my companion"** — Hebrew רַעְיָתִי (also v10, 13).
- **"among the sons"** (v3) — Hebrew בָּנִים "sons" (not "young men"). תַּפּוּחַ "apple tree": the fruit is not certain (apple, apricot or quince).
- **"I sat down under his shadow with great delight" → "In his shade I took delight and sat"** (v3) — Hebrew בְּצִלּוֹ חִמַּדְתִּי וְיָשַׁבְתִּי, two verbs: piel of חָמַד "desire, take delight" and "sat"; Hebrew order. **"to my taste" → "to my palate"** — Hebrew חִכִּי.
- **"He brought me to the banqueting house" → "He has brought me to the house of the wine"** (v4) — Hebrew אֶל־בֵּית הַיָּיִן, word for word. **"his banner" → "his standard"** — Hebrew דִּגְלוֹ, דֶּגֶל "standard" (the sense "intent, look", from a related word, is also defended: uncertain).
- **"Stay me with flagons, comfort me with apples" → "Sustain me with the raisin cakes; refresh me with the apples"** (v5) — Hebrew אֲשִׁישׁוֹת "raisin cakes" (as 2 Sam 6:19), both nouns with the article; רַפְּדוּנִי, from רָפַד "spread (a bed)" (Job 17:13): "refresh" or "spread out for me", uncertain. The imperatives are plural (to more than one).
- **"for I am sick of love" → "for sick with love am I"** (v5) — Hebrew כִּי־חוֹלַת אַהֲבָה אָנִי, Hebrew order. Here and v7 the word is אַהֲבָה "love"; in 1:2, 4 it is דֹּדִים.
- **"His left hand is under my head, and his right hand doth embrace me" → "His left is under my head, and his right embraces me"** (v6) — Hebrew שְׂמֹאלוֹ … וִימִינוֹ, "his left … his right": "hand" is not written. תְּחַבְּקֵנִי may also be a wish ("and may his right embrace me").
- **"I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, till he please" → "I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or by the does of the field, that you do not stir up and do not awaken love until it pleases"** (v7) — Hebrew הִשְׁבַּעְתִּי "I make you swear" (perfect, said as the oath is given); צְבָאוֹת "gazelles", אַיְלוֹת "does"; אוֹ "or". The oath form אִם־תָּעִירוּ וְאִם־תְּעוֹרְרוּ, literally "if you stir up and if you awaken", means "that you do not"; עוּר hifil "stir up", polel "awaken" (one root). אֶת־הָאַהֲבָה "the love" (the article left out in English: love itself, not "my love"); חָפֵץ "delight, desire" ("until it pleases"). The pronouns are masculine plural, said to the daughters. The same words in 3:5.
- **"The voice of my beloved! behold, he cometh" → "The voice of my beloved! Behold, there he comes"** (v8) — Hebrew הִנֵּה־זֶה בָּא, "behold, this one comes": "there" for זֶה (also v9).
- **"like a roe or a young hart" → "like a gazelle or a fawn of the deer"** (v9, 17) — Hebrew לִצְבִי אוֹ לְעֹפֶר הָאַיָּלִים; עֹפֶר "fawn", אַיָּלִים "deer" (as Deut 14:5).
- **"he standeth behind our wall, he looketh forth at the windows, shewing himself through the lattice" → "there he stands behind our wall, gazing through the windows, peering through the lattices"** (v9) — Hebrew כָּתְלֵנוּ (כֹּתֶל, only here: a house wall); מַשְׁגִּיחַ "gazing", מֵצִיץ "peering", both participles; מִן "from, through"; הַחֲרַכִּים plural "the lattices".
- **"My beloved spake, and said unto me" → "My beloved answered and said to me"** (v10) — Hebrew עָנָה … וְאָמַר.
- **"Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away" → "Get yourself up, my companion, my beautiful one, and get yourself away"** (v10, 13) — Hebrew קוּמִי לָךְ … וּלְכִי־לָךְ, both imperatives with לָךְ ("for yourself"): the reflexive English of the "Get yourself" row (Gen 12:1). יָפָתִי "my beautiful one". In v13 the written form is לכי, the qere לָךְ (as v10): the qere is translated; the written form would read "Arise, go, my companion".
- **"For, lo, the winter is past, the rain is over and gone" → "for behold, the winter has passed by; the rain is over, it has gone its way"** (v11) — Hebrew written הסתו, qere הַסְּתָיו (the same word in two spellings), עָבָר "has passed by", חָלַף "is over (has passed on)", הָלַךְ לוֹ "has gone its way" (lit. "gone for itself"): three verbs kept apart.
- **"The flowers appear on the earth" → "The blossoms have appeared in the land"** (v12) — Hebrew הַנִּצָּנִים נִרְאוּ בָאָרֶץ (perfect); אֶרֶץ "land" here, by the parallel "our land". **"the time of the singing of birds is come" → "the time of singing has arrived"** — Hebrew עֵת הַזָּמִיר; "of birds" is not written; זָמִיר may also be "pruning" (זָמַר): uncertain. **"the turtle" → "the turtledove"** — Hebrew הַתּוֹר.
- **"The fig tree putteth forth her green figs" → "The fig tree has ripened its early figs"** (v13) — Hebrew חָנְטָה פַגֶּיהָ; חָנַט is elsewhere "embalm" (Gen 50:2): here "ripen, make sweet", uncertain; פַּגִּים, only here, the early or unripe figs. **"and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell" → "and the vines in bloom have given smell"** — Hebrew סְמָדַר (also v15; 7:12): the blossom or the first small grape, uncertain; רֵיחַ "smell"; "good" is not written.
- **"O my dove, that art in the clefts of the rock, in the secret places of the stairs" → "My dove, in the clefts of the crag, in the hiding place of the steep"** (v14) — Hebrew בְּחַגְוֵי הַסֶּלַע, סֶלַע "crag"; סֵתֶר "hiding place" (singular); מַדְרֵגָה "steep place" (a terraced slope, as Ezek 38:20).
- **"let me see thy countenance … thy countenance is comely" → "let me see your appearance … your appearance is lovely"** (v14) — Hebrew מַרְאַיִךְ / מַרְאֵיךְ, from רָאָה "see": "appearance, form" (not "face": that is פָּנִים). הַרְאִינִי "make me see". **"thy voice is sweet" → "your voice is pleasing"** — Hebrew עָרֵב (מָתוֹק "sweet" stands in v3).
- **"Take us the foxes, the little foxes, that spoil the vines: for our vines have tender grapes" → "Seize for us foxes, little foxes, that ruin vineyards, and our vineyards are in bloom"** (v15) — Hebrew אֶחֱזוּ־לָנוּ, plural imperative; no article on any noun; שׁוּעָל "fox" (jackals are also named for the word); חִבֵּל "ruin"; כְּרָמִים "vineyards"; וּכְרָמֵינוּ סְמָדַר "and our vineyards — in bloom", with "and". Where the speech opened in v10 ends is not marked in the Hebrew; the quotation mark stands as before.
- **"he feedeth among the lilies" → "who pastures among the lilies"** (v16) — Hebrew הָרֹעֶה, participle with the article ("the one who pastures"); no object: he grazes his flock there, or the word is said of him as of the fawns in 4:5.
- **"Until the day break" → "Until the day breathes"** (v17) — Hebrew יָפוּחַ "breathes, blows" (the evening breeze or the dawn wind: not decided). **"be thou like a roe" → "make yourself like a gazelle"** — Hebrew דְּמֵה־לְךָ, imperative with לְךָ. **"upon the mountains of Bether"** — Hebrew עַל־הָרֵי בָתֶר; בֶּתֶר is "half, piece" (Gen 15:10): a place name, or "the mountains of separation / cleft", uncertain.

### Notes
- **Speakers** — the Hebrew has no speaker names: v1, 3, 5–6, 8–10a, 16–17 have a woman's voice ("my beloved", feminine "I"), v2 and v10b–14 a man addressing a woman ("my companion", feminine "you"); v7 is said to the daughters of Jerusalem; v15 has no marked speaker.
