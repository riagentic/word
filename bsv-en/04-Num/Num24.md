## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v2) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים. **"God" → "El"** (v4, 8, 16, 23) — Hebrew אֵל. **"the Almighty" → "Shaddai"** (v4, 16) — Hebrew שַׁדַּי; the meaning of the name is not certain. **"the most High" → "Elyon"** (v16) — Hebrew עֶלְיוֹן "highest". Each name stands where the Hebrew has it.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"when Balaam saw … he went not, as at other times, to seek for enchantments, but he set" → "Balaam saw … and he did not go, as time after time, to meet auguries, and he set"** (v1) — Hebrew כְּפַעַם־בְּפַעַם, literally "as time in time": as each time before; לִקְרַאת נְחָשִׁים "to meet auguries", omens that are looked for (נַחַשׁ, 23:23). Chapter 23 says only that he went to meet Yahweh (23:3, 15).
- **"abiding in his tents according to their tribes; and the spirit of God came upon him" → "abiding by its tribes; and the spirit of *Elohim* was upon him"** (v2) — Hebrew שֹׁכֵן לִשְׁבָטָיו: "in his tents" is not in the Hebrew. וַתְּהִי עָלָיו is "was upon him" or "came to be upon him".
- **"took up his parable" → "lifted up his parable"** (v3, 15, 20, 21, 23) — Hebrew וַיִּשָּׂא מְשָׁלוֹ, as 23:7.
- **"Balaam the son of Beor hath said, and the man whose eyes are open hath said" → "The declaration of Balaam son of Beor, and the declaration of the mighty man whose eye is opened"** (v3, 15) — Hebrew נְאֻם, the noun otherwise used of Yahweh's word ("declares Yahweh"); הַגֶּבֶר "the mighty man". שְׁתֻם הָעָיִן: שְׁתֻם is found only in these two verses and its meaning is uncertain: "opened" (so the verse, from a later Hebrew and Aramaic use, "bore open") or "closed" (as if סתם; so the Vulgate, cited from memory); others divide the letters and read "whose eye is perfect"; the Septuagint has "who truly sees" (cited from memory). "Eye" is singular.
- **"which heard the words of God, which saw the vision of the Almighty, falling into a trance, but having his eyes open" → "him who hears the sayings of El, who beholds the vision of Shaddai, falling, and with eyes uncovered"** (v4, 16) — Hebrew אִמְרֵי־אֵל "sayings of El" (אֵמֶר, another word than דָּבָר "word"); מַחֲזֵה … יֶחֱזֶה "beholds the vision", noun and verb of one root; נֹפֵל "falling": "into a trance" is not in the Hebrew, and whether he falls in awe, in sleep or in ecstasy is not said; גְלוּי עֵינָיִם "uncovered of eyes", the verb of 22:31. In v4 the second line begins with אֲשֶׁר "who"; in v16 it does not.
- **"How goodly are thy tents, O Jacob, and thy tabernacles, O Israel" → "How good are your tents, Jacob, your dwellings, Israel"** (v5) — Hebrew מַה־טֹּבוּ; מִשְׁכְּנֹתֶיךָ, the plural of מִשְׁכָּן "dwelling" (the word used of the dwelling of Yahweh).
- **"As the valleys are they spread forth" → "Like wadis they are stretched out"** (v6) — Hebrew כִּנְחָלִים נִטָּיוּ: נַחַל is the wadi, the stream bed; some take the word here as "palm trees". **"the trees of lign aloes" → "aloes"** — Hebrew אֲהָלִים, a fragrant tree; which tree is not certain.
- **"He shall pour the water out of his buckets" → "Water shall flow from his buckets"** (v7) — Hebrew יִזַּל־מַיִם מִדָּלְיָו: "water" is the subject; דָּלְיָו is a dual, "his two buckets". **"and his seed shall be in many waters"** — the Hebrew has no verb. The Septuagint has a different text in this line ("a man shall come forth from his seed and rule many nations"; cited from memory).
- **"his king shall be higher than Agag, and his kingdom shall be exalted" → "let his king be higher than Agag, and let his kingdom be lifted up"** (v7) — Hebrew וְיָרֹם, the short form of the verb, a wish. "Agag" is the Masoretic Text (the name of the Amalekite king in 1 Sam 15:8); the Septuagint and the Samaritan text read "Gog" (cited from memory).
- **"God brought him forth out of Egypt; he hath as it were the strength of an unicorn" → "El is bringing him out from Egypt; he has as it were the horns of the wild ox"** (v8) — as 23:22, here with "him" for "them"; תּוֹעֲפֹת is uncertain ("horns", "strength").
- **"he shall eat up the nations his enemies, and shall break their bones, and pierce them through with his arrows" → "He shall devour nations, his adversaries, and their bones he shall gnaw, and with his arrows he shall strike through"** (v8) — Hebrew יֹאכַל "eat"; צָרָיו "his adversaries" (צַר, another word than "enemies", v10, 18, and than "adversary" in 22:22); יְגָרֵם, a verb made from גֶּרֶם "bone": "gnaw" or "crush the bones", uncertain; וְחִצָּיו יִמְחָץ is literally "and his arrows he shall strike through": "his arrows" may be the means ("with his arrows") or the object ("shatter his arrows"); uncertain.
- **"He couched, he lay down as a lion, and as a great lion: who shall stir him up?" → "He stooped, he lay down like a lion, and like a lioness — who will rouse him?"** (v9) — Hebrew כָּרַע שָׁכַב כַּאֲרִי וּכְלָבִיא מִי יְקִימֶנּוּ, nearly the words of Gen 49:9 and rendered alike. The verbs are perfects.
- **"Blessed is he that blesseth thee, and cursed is he that curseth thee" → "Blessed be those who bless you, and cursed be those who curse you"** (v9) — Hebrew מְבָרֲכֶיךָ בָרוּךְ וְאֹרְרֶיךָ אָרוּר: "those who bless you" is plural, "blessed" singular (each of them); there is no verb, so "are" is as possible as "be". Compare Gen 12:3; 27:29.
- **"smote his hands together" → "struck his palms together"** (v10) — Hebrew וַיִּסְפֹּק אֶת־כַּפָּיו. **"I called thee to curse mine enemies, and, behold, thou hast altogether blessed them" → "To lay a curse on my enemies I called you, and behold, you have surely blessed"** — Hebrew לָקֹב (22:11); בֵּרַכְתָּ בָרֵךְ, the verb with its infinitive absolute, as 23:11. "Three times" is here שָׁלֹשׁ פְּעָמִים.
- **"flee thou to thy place: I thought to promote thee unto great honour; but, lo, the LORD hath kept thee back from honour" → "flee to your place. I said, 'I will surely honor you'; and behold, Yahweh has held you back from honor"** (v11) — Hebrew בְּרַח־לְךָ, "flee" with the reflexive לְךָ; כַּבֵּד אֲכַבֶּדְךָ, infinitive absolute with its verb, the words of 22:17; מִכָּבוֹד: כָּבוֹד is the word elsewhere rendered "glory", here the honor Balak promised.
- **"to do either good or bad of mine own mind" → "to do good or evil from my own heart"** (v13) — Hebrew טוֹבָה אוֹ רָעָה מִלִּבִּי. "Pass over the mouth of Yahweh" as 22:18, where the words were "a small thing or a great" and "Yahweh my Elohim".
- **"I will advertise thee" → "let me counsel you"** (v14) — Hebrew אִיעָצְךָ, from יָעַץ "counsel". **"in the latter days" kept** — Hebrew בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים, literally "in the after-part of the days" (Gen 49:1).
- **"and knew the knowledge of the most High" → "and knows the knowledge of Elyon"** (v16) — Hebrew וְיֹדֵעַ דַּעַת עֶלְיוֹן.
- **"I shall see him, but not now: I shall behold him, but not nigh" → "I see him, but not now; I watch him, but not near"** (v17) — Hebrew אֶרְאֶנּוּ … אֲשׁוּרֶנּוּ, the two verbs of 23:9, imperfects: "I see" or "I shall see". "Him" (or "it") is not named.
- **"there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel" → "A star has trodden forth from Jacob, and a scepter shall rise from Israel"** (v17) — Hebrew דָּרַךְ כּוֹכָב מִיַּעֲקֹב וְקָם שֵׁבֶט מִיִּשְׂרָאֵל, word for word "has trodden — a star — from Jacob — and shall rise (or: and has risen) — a rod — from Israel". דָּרַךְ is a perfect, "has trodden, has marched"; the verbs that follow are perfects joined by "and", the form that in a saying about what is to come carries on as a future. The tenses are given as the forms stand; "not now … not near" shows that what is seen is still to come. שֵׁבֶט is a "rod", the rod of one who rules, "scepter" (Gen 49:10); the same word also means "tribe". The Hebrew has no capital letters; the verse does not say who is meant.
- **"and shall smite the corners of Moab, and destroy all the children of Sheth" → "and it shall strike through the edges of Moab, and tear down all the sons of Sheth"** (v17) — Hebrew וּמָחַץ, the verb of v8; פַּאֲתֵי מוֹאָב, a dual, "the two edges of Moab": the two sides of the head, the temples (Lev 19:27), or the borders of the land. וְקַרְקַר is uncertain: as pointed it is taken as "tear down" (Isa 22:5); Jer 48:45, which takes up this line, has קָדְקֹד "the crown of the head", and the Samaritan text reads so here (cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is translated as printed. "The sons of Sheth" (בְּנֵי־שֵׁת) are not known: a people, or Seth the son of Adam (Gen 4:25), or a common noun ("sons of tumult", as Jer 48:45 has שָׁאוֹן).
- **"Seir also shall be a possession for his enemies; and Israel shall do valiantly" → "and a possession shall be Seir, his enemies; and Israel is doing valiantly"** (v18) — Hebrew יְרֵשָׁה שֵׂעִיר אֹיְבָיו: "his enemies" stands beside "Seir" without a preposition; whose enemies (Israel's, or his who rules) is not said. עֹשֶׂה חָיִל, a participle: חַיִל is strength, ability, wealth, an army.
- **"Out of Jacob shall come he that shall have dominion, and shall destroy him that remaineth of the city" → "And let him have dominion from Jacob, and he shall destroy the survivor from the city"** (v19) — Hebrew וְיֵרְדְּ מִיַּעֲקֹב: the short form of the verb, a wish; no subject is named, and "from Jacob" may itself be the subject ("one from Jacob"). שָׂרִיד מֵעִיר is "a survivor from a city", without the article; some take עִיר as a name (Ar, 21:28).
- **"And when he looked on Amalek" → "And he saw Amalek"** (v20, 21) — Hebrew וַיַּרְא.
- **"Amalek was the first of the nations; but his latter end shall be that he perish for ever" → "The first of nations is Amalek, and his end is unto perishing"** (v20) — Hebrew רֵאשִׁית גּוֹיִם "first / beginning of nations" (in time or in rank) set against וְאַחֲרִיתוֹ "and his end"; עֲדֵי אֹבֵד is literally "unto one perishing": a hard form, also in v24; KJV "for ever" takes עֲדֵי as the noun עַד "perpetuity"; as pointed it is the preposition "unto". Uncertain.
- **"Strong is thy dwellingplace, and thou puttest thy nest in a rock" → "Enduring is your dwelling place, and set in the crag is your nest"** (v21) — Hebrew אֵיתָן "ever-flowing, enduring"; סֶלַע "crag"; קִנֶּךָ "your nest" (*qen*) plays on the name of the Kenite (*qeni*) and of Kain (*qayin*, v22).
- **"Nevertheless the Kenite shall be wasted, until Asshur shall carry thee away captive" → "Yet Kain shall be for burning; until when shall Asshur take you captive?"** (v22) — Hebrew כִּי אִם־יִהְיֶה לְבָעֵר קָיִן עַד־מָה אַשּׁוּר תִּשְׁבֶּךָּ, word for word "but / for if — shall be — for burning (or: for consuming) — Kain — until what — Asshur — shall take you captive". The verse is uncertain: כִּי אִם may be "yet", "but", or "for if"; עַד־מָה "until what, how long" may ask how long it will be until Asshur comes, or how long the captivity will last. *Kain* is the name of the people of the Kenite (Judg 4:11).
- **"Alas, who shall live when God doeth this!" → "Woe! Who shall live when El sets this?"** (v23) — Hebrew מִי יִחְיֶה מִשֻּׂמוֹ אֵל, literally "who shall live from his setting it — El": שִׂים "put, set, appoint"; מִן is "from, after, because of, apart from". The sense is uncertain.
- **"And ships shall come from the coast of Chittim, and shall afflict Asshur, and shall afflict Eber, and he also shall perish for ever" → "And ships from the side of Kittim, and they shall afflict Asshur, and they shall afflict Eber; and he also is unto perishing"** (v24) — Hebrew וְצִים מִיַּד כִּתִּים: there is no verb; מִיַּד is literally "from the hand of", the side or coast. *Kittim* is Cyprus and the coastlands of the western sea (Gen 10:4). Who "he also" is (Kittim, Asshur or Eber) the Hebrew does not say. The verse is uncertain and is translated as the Masoretic Text stands.
- **"went his way" → "went on his way"** (v25) — Hebrew הָלַךְ לְדַרְכּוֹ.
- **Poetry** (v3–9, 15–24) — set one Hebrew line (colon) to a printed line.

### Notes
- **The reading of v17** — "a star … from Jacob, a scepter … from Israel" was read of a coming ruler from early times: the Targums, the Dead Sea Scrolls (*1QM*, *4QTestimonia*), the name Bar Kokhba ("son of the star"). The verse itself names no one; within the Old Testament David's victories over Moab and Edom (2 Sam 8:2, 14) are the first to answer to it.
- **No auguries** (v1–2) — this time Balaam does not go to meet auguries; the spirit of Elohim is upon him.
- **The short sayings** (v20–24) — on Amalek, the Kenite, and Asshur, Eber and Kittim. Their text is hard and their reference debated; later readers applied v24 to the Greeks and to Rome (Dan 11:30).
- **Balaam's "opened eye"** (v3, 15) — the meaning of *shetum* is debated: "opened" or "closed". Ancient versions differ.
- **After the sayings** — Num 31:16 says that the matter of Peor (ch 25) came through the word of Balaam; he is killed in 31:8.
