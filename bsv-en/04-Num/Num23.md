## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — the Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v4, 21, 27) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים; in v27 הָאֱלֹהִים, with the article. **"God" → "El"** (v8, 19, 22, 23) — Hebrew אֵל. Each divine name stands where the Hebrew has it: in v3–5 "Yahweh … Elohim … Yahweh"; in v8 El beside Yahweh.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"oxen" → "bulls"** (v1, 29), **"a bullock" → "a bull"** (v2, 4, 14, 30) — Hebrew פַּר.
- **"offered on every altar a bullock and a ram" → "offered up a bull and a ram on each altar"** (v2, 4, 14, 30) — Hebrew וַיַּעַל … בַּמִּזְבֵּחַ, literally "made go up … on the altar": the singular after "seven altars" means each of them. The verb is of one root with עֹלָה "burnt offering" (v3).
- **"Stand by thy burnt offering" → "Take your stand by your burnt offering"** (v3, 15) — Hebrew הִתְיַצֵּב עַל.
- **"peradventure the LORD will come to meet me" → "perhaps Yahweh will happen to meet me"** (v3) — Hebrew יִקָּרֵה יְהוָה לִקְרָאתִי: נִקְרָה "happen upon, let oneself be met", with לִקְרָאתִי "to meet me". The same verb is "met with" in v4, 16 and "I will be met" in v15.
- **"whatsoever he sheweth me" → "whatever he shows me"** (v3) — Hebrew וּדְבַר מַה־יַּרְאֵנִי, literally "and the word / matter of what he shows me".
- **"he went to an high place" → "he went to a bare height"** (v3) — Hebrew שֶׁפִי: the meaning is uncertain ("a bare height", as the plural שְׁפָיִם in Jer 3:2; others "alone" or "quietly").
- **"I have prepared seven altars" → "The seven altars I have arranged"** (v4) — Hebrew אֶת־שִׁבְעַת הַמִּזְבְּחֹת עָרַכְתִּי, with the article and the object first.
- **"took up his parable" → "lifted up his parable"** (v7, 18) — Hebrew וַיִּשָּׂא מְשָׁלוֹ. מָשָׁל is a saying built on a likeness: a proverb, a figurative poem, a taunt song (21:27); the Septuagint has παραβολή (cited from memory). The Hebrew does not name Balaam in v7.
- **"Balak the king of Moab hath brought me from Aram, out of the mountains of the east" → "From Aram Balak leads me, the king of Moab from the mountains of the east"** (v7) — Hebrew יַנְחֵנִי, an imperfect of נָחָה "lead"; in old poetry the form can tell of the past ("led me"). הַרְרֵי־קֶדֶם "the mountains of the east" (קֶדֶם is also "of old").
- **"curse me Jacob … defy Israel" → "curse Jacob for me … denounce Israel"** (v7), **"defy" → "denounce"** (v8) — Hebrew אָרָה "curse" and זֹעֲמָה, from זָעַם "be indignant at, speak wrath against".
- **"How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?" → "How shall I lay a curse on him on whom El has laid no curse?"** (v8) — Hebrew מָה אֶקֹּב לֹא קַבֹּה אֵל, literally "what shall I curse? El has not cursed him": קָבַב, the second verb for "curse" (22:11), kept apart from אָרַר (v7).
- **"crags" / "rocks" → "rocks"** (v9) — Hebrew צֻרִים. **"I behold him" → "I watch him"** — Hebrew אֲשׁוּרֶנּוּ (שׁוּר), another verb than "see"; so 24:17.
- **"the people shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations" → "a people that abides alone, and among the nations it does not reckon itself"** (v9) — Hebrew יִשְׁכֹּן "abides"; יִתְחַשָּׁב, a reflexive form found only here: "reckons itself" or "is reckoned".
- **"Who can count the dust of Jacob, and the number of the fourth part of Israel?" → "Who has counted the dust of Jacob, and the number of the fourth part of Israel?"** (v10) — Hebrew מִי מָנָה, a perfect. וּמִסְפָּר אֶת־רֹבַע יִשְׂרָאֵל is translated as the Masoretic Text stands: מִסְפָּר is the noun "number", followed by the object marker; the Septuagint and the Samaritan text read a verb, "and who has numbered" (cited from memory). רֹבַע "fourth part" is uncertain (some connect it with a word for "dust cloud").
- **"Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his" → "Let my soul die the death of the upright, and let my end be like his"** (v10) — Hebrew תָּמֹת נַפְשִׁי "let my soul die"; יְשָׁרִים "upright ones" (plural); כָּמֹהוּ "like him" (singular). אַחֲרִית is "end, what comes after".
- **"I took thee to curse mine enemies, and, behold, thou hast blessed them altogether" → "To lay a curse on my enemies I took you, and behold, you have surely blessed"** (v11) — Hebrew word order; בֵּרַכְתָּ בָרֵךְ, the verb with its infinitive absolute; no object is named.
- **"Must I not take heed to speak" → "shall I not take care to speak that?"** (v12) — Hebrew אֹתוֹ אֶשְׁמֹר לְדַבֵּר, literally "that I shall keep to speak".
- **"thou shalt see but the utmost part of them, and shalt not see them all" → "nothing except its end you will see, and all of it you will not see"** (v13) — Hebrew אֶפֶס קָצֵהוּ: the people is spoken of in the singular. Whether this describes the new place or the place where they stand the Hebrew does not say. "Come" follows the reading tradition (qere לְכָה; written לך); the meaning is the same.
- **"the top of Pisgah" → "the top of the Pisgah"** (v14) — Hebrew הַפִּסְגָּה, with the article; שְׂדֵה צֹפִים is "the field of the watchers".
- **"while I meet the LORD yonder" → "and I, I will be met yonder"** (v15) — Hebrew וְאָנֹכִי אִקָּרֶה כֹּה: the verb has no object; "the LORD" is not in the Hebrew. כֹּה stands twice: "here … yonder".
- **"Rise up, Balak, and hear; hearken unto me, thou son of Zippor" → "Rise, Balak, and hear; give ear to me, son of Zippor"** (v18) — Hebrew הַאֲזִינָה עָדַי.
- **"God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent" → "El is not a man, that he should lie, nor a son of man, that he should relent"** (v19) — Hebrew אִישׁ "man" and בֶּן־אָדָם "son of man (*adam*)"; וְיִתְנֶחָם, from נחם "be sorry, relent", not "repent" of sin (compare 1 Sam 15:29). **"shall he not make it good?" → "will he not establish it?"** — Hebrew יְקִימֶנָּה, literally "make it stand".
- **"I have received commandment to bless: and he hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it" → "to bless I have received; and he has blessed, and I shall not turn it back"** (v20) — Hebrew הִנֵּה בָרֵךְ לָקָחְתִּי, literally "behold, to bless I have taken": no word "commandment". וְלֹא אֲשִׁיבֶנָּה "and I shall not turn it back": "cannot" is not said.
- **"He hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob, neither hath he seen perverseness in Israel" → "He has not looked on trouble in Jacob, and he has not seen toil in Israel"** (v21) — Hebrew אָוֶן and עָמָל. אָוֶן is "wickedness" and also the "trouble" that comes of it; עָמָל is "toil, hardship", also "mischief" (the two together: Ps 90:10; Hab 1:3). Whether the line says that he sees no wrong in Israel or no misfortune for Israel, the Hebrew leaves open. The subject "he" is not named: El, or an unnamed "one".
- **"the shout of a king is among them" → "the shouting of a king is in him"** (v21) — Hebrew תְרוּעַת מֶלֶךְ בּוֹ: תְּרוּעָה is the loud shout or blast of acclaim and of alarm (Num 10:5; 29:1); "in him" is singular, the people.
- **"God brought them out of Egypt; he hath as it were the strength of an unicorn" → "El is bringing them out from Egypt; he has as it were the horns of the wild ox"** (v22) — Hebrew אֵל מוֹצִיאָם, a participle: "is bringing them out" ("them" is plural here, "him" in 24:8). כְּתוֹעֲפֹת רְאֵם לוֹ is literally "like the *toafot* of a wild ox are his": תּוֹעָפוֹת is uncertain ("horns", "strength", "heights": Ps 95:4; Job 22:25); "his" may be El's or the people's. רְאֵם is the wild ox (the aurochs); "unicorn" comes from the Septuagint's rendering.
- **"Surely there is no enchantment against Jacob, neither is there any divination against Israel" → "For there is no augury in Jacob, and no divination in Israel"** (v23) — Hebrew נַחַשׁ, the reading of omens (the "auguries" of 24:1), and קֶסֶם "divination" (22:7). בְּ is "in" or "against": that no such arts are practised in Israel, or that none avails against Israel.
- **"according to this time it shall be said of Jacob and of Israel, What hath God wrought!" → "at the time it is said to Jacob and to Israel, 'What has El wrought!'"** (v23) — Hebrew כָּעֵת "at the time, now"; יֵאָמֵר an imperfect, "is said / shall be said"; לְ is "to" or "of"; מַה־פָּעַל אֵל is "what El has wrought" or the cry "What has El wrought!". The lines are divided as the Hebrew accents have them.
- **"the people shall rise up as a great lion, and lift up himself as a young lion" → "a people: like a lioness it rises, and like a lion it lifts itself up"** (v24) — Hebrew לָבִיא and אֲרִי, two words for the lion; לָבִיא is rendered "lioness" as in Gen 49:9; 24:9 (its gender is not certain). **"eat of the prey" → "eats prey"**.
- **"Neither curse them at all, nor bless them at all" → "Neither shall you surely lay a curse on it, nor shall you surely bless it"** (v25) — Hebrew infinitive absolute with its verb, twice: גַּם־קֹב לֹא תִקֳּבֶנּוּ גַּם־בָּרֵךְ לֹא תְבָרֲכֶנּוּ, literally "both cursing you shall not curse it, and blessing you shall not bless it".
- **"Told not I thee, saying, All that the LORD speaketh, that I must do?" → "Did I not speak to you, saying, 'All that Yahweh speaks, that I shall do'?"** (v26) — Hebrew אֶעֱשֶׂה, a plain imperfect.
- **"peradventure it will please God that thou mayest curse me them" → "perhaps it will be right in the eyes of *Elohim*, and you will lay a curse on it for me"** (v27) — Hebrew יִישַׁר בְּעֵינֵי, "be upright, right in the eyes of".
- **"the top of Peor, that looketh toward Jeshimon" → "the top of the Peor, which looks down over the face of the Jeshimon"** (v28) — Hebrew רֹאשׁ הַפְּעוֹר הַנִּשְׁקָף עַל־פְּנֵי הַיְשִׁימֹן, as 21:20; *yeshimon* is "the waste".
- **"had said"** (v30) — Hebrew אָמַר; in v2 דִּבֶּר "had spoken".
- **Poetry** (v7–10, 18–24) — set one Hebrew line (colon) to a printed line.

### Notes
- **Balaam's parables** — chs 23–24 hold four longer sayings (23:7–10, 18–24; 24:3–9, 15–19) and three short ones (24:20, 21–22, 23–24). Brought to curse, Balaam blesses.
- **First saying** (v7–10) — a people abiding alone, beyond counting; the speaker wishes for himself the death of the upright.
- **Second saying** (v18–24) — El does not lie or relent; what he has blessed is not turned back; the people as a lion.
- **"El is not a man, that he should lie"** (v19) — compare 1 Sam 15:29.
- **Seven altars, seven bulls, seven rams** (v1, 14, 29) — the rite is Balaam's own; the Torah prescribes nothing like it.
- **The names of God** — the narrator and Balaam say "Yahweh"; the sayings use "El", "Yahweh", "Elohim", and in ch 24 "Shaddai" and "Elyon".
