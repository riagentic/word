[1] ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, a prisoner of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, and ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧ the brother, to Philemon, our beloved one and fellow worker,

[2] and to Apphia the sister, and to ⟦archippus|Archippos|Archippus⟧ our fellow soldier, and to the church at your house:

[3] Grace to you and peace from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ our Father and the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[4] I give thanks to my ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ always, making mention of you in my prayers,

[7] For I had much joy and encouragement over your love, because the inward parts of the saints have been refreshed through you, brother.

[8] Therefore, though I have much boldness in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ to order you to do what is fitting,

[9] because of love I rather appeal — being such a one as ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, an old man, and now also a prisoner of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ —

[10] I appeal to you concerning my child, whom I begot in my chains, ⟦onesimus|Onesimos|Onesimus⟧,

[11] who once was useless to you, but now is useful to you and to me,

[12] whom I have sent back to you — him, that is, my own inward parts.

[13] I wished to keep him with myself, so that on your behalf he might serve me in the chains of the gospel,

[14] but without your consent I was willing to do nothing, so that your good deed would not be as from necessity but of free will.

[15] For perhaps for this reason he was separated from you for a short time, that you might have him back forever,

[16] no longer as a slave, but above a slave, a beloved brother — most of all to me, but how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord.

[19] I, ⟦paul|Paulus|Paul⟧, have written this with my own hand: I will repay — not to mention to you that you owe me your very self besides.

[20] Yes, brother, may I have this benefit from you in the Lord; refresh my inward parts in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[21] Trusting in your obedience I have written to you, knowing that you will do even beyond what I say.

[22] And at the same time also prepare lodging for me, for I hope that through your prayers I will be granted to you.

[23] Epaphras, my fellow captive in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, greets you,

[24] as do ⟦mark|Marcus|Mark⟧, ⟦aristarchus|Aristarchos|Aristarchus⟧, Demas, ⟦luke|Loukas|Luke⟧, my fellow workers.

[25] The grace of the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ be with your spirit.

## Changes

- **"Christ Jesus" / "Jesus Christ" → "*Mashiach* *Yeshua*" / "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv3, 4) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (vv5, 25) — Greek *Iēsous* transliterates Hebrew/Aramaic *Yeshua*.
- **"prisoner of" kept** (v1, 9) — Greek δέσμιος Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ, a plain genitive: "prisoner of", not "prisoner for".
- **"in Christ Jesus" → "unto the *Mashiach*"** (v6) — Greek εἰς Χριστόν *eis Christon* (NA28); "Jesus" is not in the critical text. The verse has no verb of praying; it continues v4.
- **"bowels" → "inward parts"** (v7, 12, 20) — Greek σπλάγχνα *splanchna*, the inner organs as the seat of deep feeling; "heart" is a different Greek word.
- **"begotten in my bonds" → "begot in my chains"** (v10) — Greek ἐγέννησα ἐν τοῖς δεσμοῖς, γεννάω of the father, "beget" (as 1 Cor 4:15); also v13, "the chains of the gospel".
- **"servant" → "slave"** (v16) — Greek δοῦλος *doulos*.
- **"for a season" → "for a short time"** (v15) — Greek πρὸς ὥραν, literally "for an hour".
- **"let me have joy of thee" → "may I have this benefit from you"** (v20) — Greek ὀναίμην *onaimēn*, a wish; a second wordplay on the name *Onesimos*.
- **"fellowprisoner" → "fellow captive"** (v23) — Greek συναιχμάλωτος *synaichmalōtos*, a captive of war; a different word from "prisoner" (δέσμιος) in v1, 9.
- **"your" / "you"** (v3, 22, 25) — plural in Greek (ὑμῖν, ὑμῶν): the whole house church; elsewhere "you" is Philemon alone.

### Notes
- **v11** — a wordplay: the name *Onesimos* means "useful"; formerly "useless" (*achrestos*), now "useful" (*euchrestos*).
- **v10–12** — one long sentence in Greek; the name Onesimos comes last in v10, after "whom I begot in my chains".
