# Deuteronomy 24

[1] "When a man takes a wife and becomes her husband, and it shall be, if she does not find favor in his eyes, for he has found in her the nakedness of a thing, and he writes for her a document of cutting off and gives it into her hand and sends her away from his house,

[2] and she goes out from his house and goes and becomes another man's,

[3] and the latter man hates her and writes for her a document of cutting off and gives it into her hand and sends her away from his house, or if the latter man dies, who took her to himself as a wife,

[4] then her first husband, who sent her away, cannot return to take her to be his wife, after she has been defiled, for that is an abomination before ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧; and you shall not cause to sin the land that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is giving to you as an inheritance.

[5] When a man takes a new wife, he shall not go out in the host, and nothing shall pass over upon him for any matter; he shall be clear for his house one year, and he shall gladden his wife whom he has taken.

[6] No one shall take in pledge the two millstones or the upper millstone, for it is a soul that he takes in pledge.

[7] When a man is found stealing a soul from his brothers, from the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, and he deals tyrannically with him and sells him, then that thief shall die; and you shall purge the evil from your midst.

[8] Take heed in the plague of leprosy, to keep very much and to do according to all that the priests, the ⟦levites|Leviyim|Levites⟧, teach you; as I commanded them, you shall keep to do.

[9] Remember what ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ did to ⟦miriam|Miryam|Miriam⟧ on the way, when you came out of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[10] When you lend your neighbor a loan of anything, you shall not come into his house to take his pledge.

[11] Outside you shall stand, and the man to whom you are lending shall bring the pledge out to you, outside.

[12] And if he is an afflicted man, you shall not lie down in his pledge;

[13] you shall surely return the pledge to him when the sun sets, and he shall lie down in his cloak and bless you; and to you it shall be righteousness before ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[14] You shall not extort from a hired man, afflicted and needy, of your brothers or of your sojourner who is in your land, in your gates.

[15] On his day you shall give his wages, and the sun shall not set upon it, for he is afflicted, and to it he lifts up his soul; and he shall not call against you to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, and there would be sin in you.

[16] Fathers shall not be put to death on account of sons, and sons shall not be put to death on account of fathers; each man in his sin they shall be put to death.

[17] You shall not turn aside the judgment of a sojourner, a fatherless one, and you shall not take in pledge the garment of a widow.

[18] And you shall remember that you were a slave in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, and ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ ransomed you from there; therefore I am commanding you to do this thing.

[19] When you reap your harvest in your field and forget a sheaf in the field, you shall not return to take it; for the sojourner, for the fatherless and for the widow it shall be, so that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ may bless you in all the work of your hands.

[20] When you beat your olive tree, you shall not go over the boughs after you; for the sojourner, for the fatherless and for the widow it shall be.

[21] When you pick your vineyard, you shall not glean after you; for the sojourner, for the fatherless and for the widow it shall be.

[22] And you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧; therefore I am commanding you to do this thing.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וְ "and"; the chain is kept. "You" is one person (singular) throughout, except "teach you … you shall keep to do" in v8 and "when you came out" in v9, which are plural.
- **The sentence of v1–4** — in the Hebrew v1–3 are one long string of "and" clauses that set out the case ("when a man takes … and he writes … and sends her away … and she goes … and the latter man hates her …"); the ruling comes only in v4 ("then her first husband … cannot"). The KJV makes v1 a command ("then let him write her a bill of divorcement"); the Hebrew וְכָתַב is a plain "and he writes", one more step of the case. The law does not command or set up the sending away; it takes the practice as it finds it and rules on one thing, the return to the first husband.
- **"hath taken a wife, and married her" → "takes a wife and becomes her husband"** (v1) — Hebrew וּבְעָלָהּ, the verb of בַּעַל "owner, husband" (21:13). **"and it come to pass"** → **"and it shall be"** — Hebrew וְהָיָה.
- **"because he hath found some uncleanness in her" → "for he has found in her the nakedness of a thing"** (v1) — Hebrew עֶרְוַת דָּבָר, word for word: עֶרְוָה is "nakedness", דָּבָר "word, thing, matter". The same two words stand in 23:14 of something exposed in the camp that ought to be covered. What is meant here was disputed among the Jewish teachers: something shameful of a sexual kind, or anything displeasing (so the schools of Shammai and of Hillel, cited from memory). The verse keeps the words and does not decide. Matt 5:32; 19:9 speak to this dispute.
- **"a bill of divorcement" → "a document of cutting off"** (v1, 3) — Hebrew סֵפֶר כְּרִיתֻת: סֵפֶר is "book", any written document; כְּרִיתוּת is from כָּרַת "cut, cut off": a writing that cuts the bond (Isa 50:1; Jer 3:8). Matt 19:7 has it in Greek as "a document of divorce". **"send her out of his house" → "sends her away from his house"** — Hebrew שִׁלַּח, "send away" (22:19, 29).
- **"she may go and be another man's wife" → "and goes and becomes another man's"** (v2) — Hebrew וְהָלְכָה וְהָיְתָה לְאִישׁ־אַחֵר: a plain "and … becomes", not a leave given; "wife" is not written.
- **"the latter husband" → "the latter man"** (v3) — Hebrew הָאִישׁ הָאַחֲרוֹן both times; "husband" in v4 is בַּעַל. **"hate"** — Hebrew שָׂנֵא, as 22:13.
- **"may not take her again" → "cannot return to take her"** (v4) — Hebrew לֹא־יוּכַל … לָשׁוּב לְקַחְתָּהּ: "is not able", is not free to (21:16; 22:19). **"after that she is defiled" → "after she has been defiled"** — Hebrew הֻטַּמָּאָה, a rare passive-reflexive form of טִמֵּא "make unclean": "has been made to defile herself", or "has been declared defiled". She is so in regard to her first husband; the verse does not call the second union wrong. **"thou shalt not cause the land to sin, which the LORD thy God giveth thee" → "you shall not cause to sin the land that Yahweh your Elohim is giving to you"** — Hebrew וְלֹא תַחֲטִיא אֶת־הָאָרֶץ, literally so: bring sin upon the land; the clause that follows is the book's fixed "the land that Yahweh your Elohim is giving to you" and is worded so.
- **"he shall not go out to war, neither shall he be charged with any business" → "he shall not go out in the host, and nothing shall pass over upon him for any matter"** (v5) — Hebrew לֹא יֵצֵא בַּצָּבָא וְלֹא־יַעֲבֹר עָלָיו לְכָל־דָּבָר: צָבָא "host"; the second clause is literally "and it shall not pass over upon him as to any matter": no duty shall be laid on him. **"he shall be free at home" → "he shall be clear for his house"** — Hebrew נָקִי יִהְיֶה לְבֵיתוֹ; נָקִי is "clear, free of obligation". **"cheer up" → "gladden"** — Hebrew וְשִׂמַּח.
- **"No man shall take the nether or the upper millstone to pledge: for he taketh a man's life to pledge" → "No one shall take in pledge the two millstones or the upper millstone, for it is a soul that he takes in pledge"** (v6) — Hebrew רֵחַיִם, a dual: the hand mill of two stones; רֶכֶב, literally "rider": the upper stone; the Hebrew joins them with "and". נֶפֶשׁ is "soul": the life of those who live by the mill. "Take in pledge" here and in v17 is חָבַל.
- **"stealing any of his brethren of the children of Israel" → "stealing a soul from his brothers, from the sons of Israel"** (v7) — Hebrew גֹּנֵב נֶפֶשׁ מֵאֶחָיו מִבְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל. **"maketh merchandise of him, or selleth him" → "deals tyrannically with him and sells him"** — Hebrew וְהִתְעַמֶּר־בּוֹ וּמְכָרוֹ: "and", not "or"; הִתְעַמֵּר is found only here and in 21:14 and its meaning is not certain ("deal as a master with, treat as a slave", or "trade in"). **"thou shalt put evil away from among you" → "you shall purge the evil from your midst"** — as 21:21.
- **"that thou observe diligently" → "to keep very much"** (v8) — Hebrew לִשְׁמֹר מְאֹד. **"the plague of leprosy"** — Hebrew נֶגַע־הַצָּרַעַת, as Lev 13–14: נֶגַע is "a touch, a stroke"; צָרַעַת is a scaly or mouldy outbreak on skin, cloth or walls that makes unclean; it is not the leprosy of modern medicine. **"the priests the Levites"** — Hebrew הַכֹּהֲנִים הַלְוִיִּם. **"so ye shall observe to do" → "you shall keep to do"** — Hebrew תִּשְׁמְרוּ לַעֲשׂוֹת.
- **"by the way, after that ye were come forth" → "on the way, when you came out"** (v9) — Hebrew בְּצֵאתְכֶם. See Num 12:10–15.
- **"When thou dost lend thy brother any thing" → "When you lend your neighbor a loan of anything"** (v10) — Hebrew כִּי־תַשֶּׁה בְרֵעֲךָ מַשַּׁאת מְאוּמָה; רֵעַ is "neighbor". **"to fetch his pledge" → "to take his pledge"** — Hebrew לַעֲבֹט עֲבֹטוֹ, literally "to pledge his pledge". "Pledge" in v10–13 is עֲבוֹט, another root than "take in pledge" (חָבַל) in v6, 17; English has one word.
- **"abroad" → "outside"** (v11) — Hebrew בַּחוּץ … הַחוּצָה, said twice.
- **"if the man be poor" → "if he is an afflicted man"** (v12), **"poor" → "afflicted"** (v14, 15) — Hebrew עָנִי, the poor man bowed down. **"sleep with his pledge" → "lie down in his pledge"** — Hebrew תִשְׁכַּב בַּעֲבֹטוֹ: the pledge is the cloak he lies down in (v13).
- **"In any case thou shalt deliver him the pledge again" → "you shall surely return the pledge to him"** (v13) — Hebrew הָשֵׁב תָּשִׁיב, literally "returning you shall return". **"raiment" → "cloak"** — Hebrew שַׂלְמָה, the same word as שִׂמְלָה. "When the sun sets" is literally "as the sun goes in".
- **"Thou shalt not oppress an hired servant" → "You shall not extort from a hired man"** (v14) — Hebrew עָשַׁק, "press out, withhold what is due" (Lev 19:13); שָׂכִיר "hired man". **"of thy strangers that are in thy land within thy gates" → "of your sojourner who is in your land, in your gates"** — Hebrew מִגֵּרְךָ, singular.
- **"At his day thou shalt give him his hire" → "On his day you shall give his wages"** (v15) — Hebrew בְּיוֹמוֹ. **"setteth his heart upon it" → "to it he lifts up his soul"** — Hebrew וְאֵלָיו הוּא נֹשֵׂא אֶת־נַפְשׁוֹ, literally so. **"lest he cry against thee" → "and he shall not call against you"** — Hebrew וְלֹא־יִקְרָא, "and … not", with the force of "that he may not". **"and it be sin unto thee" → "and there would be sin in you"** — as 23:21.
- **"The fathers shall not be put to death for the children … every man shall be put to death for his own sin" → "Fathers shall not be put to death on account of sons … each man in his sin they shall be put to death"** (v16) — Hebrew לֹא־יוּמְתוּ אָבוֹת עַל־בָּנִים וּבָנִים לֹא־יוּמְתוּ עַל־אָבוֹת אִישׁ בְּחֶטְאוֹ יוּמָתוּ, word for word. בָּנִים is "sons". עַל is "upon, on account of" (it can also say "together with"); בְּחֶטְאוֹ is "in his sin", with another preposition; "own" is not written. The last verb is plural.
- **"pervert the judgment of the stranger, nor of the fatherless" → "turn aside the judgment of a sojourner, a fatherless one"** (v17) — Hebrew לֹא תַטֶּה מִשְׁפַּט גֵּר יָתוֹם: הִטָּה "turn aside", that is, pervert; the two nouns stand side by side without "and" (also read "a fatherless sojourner"). **"raiment" → "garment"** — Hebrew בֶּגֶד.
- **"bondman" → "slave"** (v18, 22) — Hebrew עֶבֶד. **"redeemed" → "ransomed"** (v18) — Hebrew פָּדָה. **"I command" → "I am commanding"** — Hebrew אָנֹכִי מְצַוְּךָ, a participle. "But" at the head of v18 is "And" in the Hebrew.
- **"thou shalt not go again to fetch it" → "you shall not return to take it"** (v19) — Hebrew לֹא תָשׁוּב לְקַחְתּוֹ. **"the stranger" → "the sojourner"** (v19–21) — Hebrew גֵּר. **"it shall be for the stranger, for the fatherless, and for the widow" → "for the sojourner, for the fatherless and for the widow it shall be"** — the Hebrew order, the same words three times.
- **"thou shalt not go over the boughs again" → "you shall not go over the boughs after you"** (v20), **"thou shalt not glean it afterward" → "you shall not glean after you"** (v21) — Hebrew אַחֲרֶיךָ "after you", behind yourself. פֵּאֵר (only here) is a verb made from the word for "bough". **"gatherest the grapes of thy vineyard" → "pick your vineyard"** — Hebrew תִבְצֹר; "grapes" is not written.

### Notes
- **Sending away a wife** (v1–4) — the law assumes the practice and the written document; it rules only that the first husband cannot take her back after she has become another man's. Yeshua is asked about this passage (Matt 19:3–9; Mark 10:2–9): "Moses, with regard to your hardness of heart, permitted you to send away your wives; but from the beginning it has not been so" (Matt 19:8).
- **The new wife** (v5) — for one year the man does not go out in the host and no duty is laid on him.
- **Millstones** (v6) — the mill made the daily bread; to take it in pledge is to take "a soul" in pledge.
- **Wages on the day** (v14–15) — the hired man lives from day to day; compare Lev 19:13 and Jas 5:4.
- **Each man in his sin** (v16) — fathers and sons are not put to death for one another (2 Kgs 14:6 cites it; compare Ezek 18; Jer 31:29–30).
- **The forgotten sheaf, the olive tree, the vineyard** (v19–22) — what is left is for the sojourner, the fatherless and the widow (Lev 19:9–10; Ruth 2).
