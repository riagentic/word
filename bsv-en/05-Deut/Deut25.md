# Deuteronomy 25

[1] "When there is a case between men, and they draw near to the judgment, and they judge them, and they justify the righteous and condemn the wicked,

[2] then it shall be, if the wicked man deserves to be struck, that the judge shall make him fall, and he shall strike him before his face, as much as his wickedness requires, by number.

[3] Forty times he may strike him; he shall not add; lest he add to strike him beyond these with many blows, and your brother be made light of before your eyes.

[4] You shall not muzzle an ox in its threshing.

[5] When brothers dwell together, and one of them dies, and he has no son, the wife of the dead man shall not become a strange man's, outside; her husband's brother shall go in to her and take her to himself as wife and do the brother-in-law's duty to her.

[6] And it shall be that the firstborn whom she bears shall stand upon the name of his brother who is dead, and his name shall not be blotted out from ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧.

[7] And if the man has no delight to take his brother's widow, then his brother's widow shall go up to the gate, to the elders, and say, 'My husband's brother refuses to raise up for his brother a name in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧; he is not willing to do the brother-in-law's duty to me.'

[8] And the elders of his city shall call him and speak to him; and he shall stand and say, 'I have no delight to take her.'

[9] Then his brother's widow shall draw near to him before the eyes of the elders, and she shall pull his sandal off his foot and spit in his face; and she shall answer and say, 'So shall it be done to the man who does not build the house of his brother.'

[10] And his name shall be called in ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧, 'The house of him whose sandal was pulled off.'

[11] When men struggle together, a man and his brother, and the wife of the one comes near to deliver her husband from the hand of the one striking him, and she stretches out her hand and holds fast his private parts,

[12] then you shall cut off her palm; your eye shall not pity.

[13] You shall not have in your bag a weight and a weight, a large and a small.

[14] You shall not have in your house an ephah and an ephah, a large and a small.

[15] A whole and righteous weight you shall have; a whole and righteous ephah you shall have, so that your days may be long upon the ground that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is giving to you.

[16] For an abomination to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is everyone who does these things, everyone who does injustice.

[17] Remember what ⟦amalek|Amaleq|Amalek⟧ did to you on the way, when you came out from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧,

[18] how he met you on the way and cut off your rear, all who were worn out behind you, and you were weary and spent; and he did not fear ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[19] And it shall be, when ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ gives you rest from all your enemies all around, in the land that ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ your ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is giving to you as an inheritance to possess it, you shall blot out the remembrance of ⟦amalek|Amaleq|Amalek⟧ from under the heavens; you shall not forget.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"**, **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — the Hebrew names kept.
- **"If there be a controversy between men, and they come unto judgment, that the judges may judge them; then they shall justify the righteous, and condemn the wicked" → "When there is a case between men, and they draw near to the judgment, and they judge them, and they justify the righteous and condemn the wicked,"** (v1) — Hebrew רִיב, a contention at law. The Hebrew names no "judges": "they judge them". הִצְדִּיק and הִרְשִׁיעַ are "declare righteous" and "declare wicked" (Exod 22:9; 23:7). The four clauses are one chain of "and"; the main clause begins in v2 ("then it shall be").
- **"if the wicked man be worthy to be beaten, that the judge shall cause him to lie down, and to be beaten before his face, according to his fault, by a certain number" → "if the wicked man deserves to be struck, that the judge shall make him fall, and he shall strike him before his face, as much as his wickedness requires, by number"** (v2) — Hebrew בִּן הַכּוֹת, literally "a son of striking". הִפִּיל is "make fall". וְהִכָּהוּ לְפָנָיו "and he shall strike him before him": the Hebrew names no second person who strikes; it is usually understood that the man is struck in the judge's presence. כְּדֵי רִשְׁעָתוֹ is "according to the sufficiency of his wickedness". "Strike" (v2, 3, 11) is one verb, הִכָּה; "blows" (v3) is its noun.
- **"Forty stripes he may give him, and not exceed: lest, if he should exceed, and beat him above these with many stripes, then thy brother should seem vile unto thee" → "Forty times he may strike him; he shall not add; lest he add to strike him beyond these with many blows, and your brother be made light of before your eyes"** (v3) — the Hebrew is "forty he shall strike him" ("times" is supplied); יֹסִיף "add" twice; מַכָּה רַבָּה is a singular, "a great blow, much beating". נִקְלָה is "be made light, be dishonored" (the root of "makes light of" in 27:16).
- **"Thou shalt not muzzle the ox when he treadeth out the corn" → "You shall not muzzle an ox in its threshing"** (v4) — Hebrew לֹא־תַחְסֹם שׁוֹר בְּדִישׁוֹ, four words; שׁוֹר has no article. 1 Cor 9:9 and 1 Tim 5:18 cite it in Greek with a participle, "a threshing ox".
- **"the wife of the dead shall not marry without unto a stranger: her husband's brother shall go in unto her, and take her to him to wife, and perform the duty of an husband's brother unto her" → "the wife of the dead man shall not become a strange man's, outside; her husband's brother shall go in to her and take her to himself as wife and do the brother-in-law's duty to her"** (v5) — Hebrew לֹא־תִהְיֶה … הַחוּצָה לְאִישׁ זָר "shall not be, to the outside, a strange man's"; "the family" is not written. יְבָמָהּ "her husband's brother" (יָבָם) and וְיִבְּמָהּ "do the brother-in-law's duty to her" (יִבֵּם) are one root, as "his brother's widow" (יְבָמָה, v7, 9); the same words as Gen 38:8. "Go in to her" is literally "come upon her" (עָלֶיהָ). "Son" is בֵּן; the Septuagint has "seed" (from memory), and Matt 22:24 "children".
- **"shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead, that his name be not put out of Israel" → "shall stand upon the name of his brother who is dead, and his name shall not be blotted out from Israel"** (v6) — Hebrew יָקוּם עַל־שֵׁם "rise, stand upon the name of"; "his brother" is the brother of the man of v5. מָחָה is "wipe out", of a name "blot out" (so v19).
- **"if the man like not to take his brother's wife … he will not perform the duty of my husband's brother" → "if the man has no delight to take his brother's widow … he is not willing to do the brother-in-law's duty to me"** (v7, 8) — Hebrew חָפֵץ "delight in"; אָבָה "be willing"; מֵאֵן "refuse". "Raise up a name" is the verb of Gen 38:8 ("raise up seed").
- **"Then the elders of his city shall call him, and speak unto him: and if he stand to it, and say" → "And the elders of his city shall call him and speak to him; and he shall stand and say"** (v8) — the Hebrew has no "if" and no "to it": וְעָמַד וְאָמַר.
- **"loose his shoe from off his foot … that will not build up his brother's house" → "pull his sandal off his foot … who does not build the house of his brother"** (v9, 10) — Hebrew חָלַץ "draw off"; נַעַל "sandal". "In his face" (בְּפָנָיו) may also be "before his face". "The house of him whose sandal was pulled off" is literally "the house of the pulled-off of the sandal".
- **"When men strive together one with another … taketh him by the secrets" → "When men struggle together, a man and his brother … holds fast his private parts"** (v11) — Hebrew נִצָּה "struggle" (Exod 21:22); אִישׁ וְאָחִיו "a man and his brother". הֶחֱזִיק בְּ is "take a strong hold of" (22:25). מְבֻשִׁים is found only here: from בּוֹשׁ "be ashamed", literally "his parts of shame".
- **"cut off her hand, thine eye shall not pity her" → "cut off her palm; your eye shall not pity"** (v12) — Hebrew כַּף "palm", another word than the "hand" (יָד) of v11; the Hebrew has no "her" after "pity".
- **"divers weights … divers measures" → "a weight and a weight … an ephah and an ephah"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew אֶבֶן וָאָבֶן, literally "a stone and a stone" (weights were stones; so Lev 19:36), and אֵיפָה וְאֵיפָה.
- **"a perfect and just weight" → "A whole and righteous weight"** (v15) — Hebrew שְׁלֵמָה "whole" (27:6) and צֶדֶק "righteousness" (Lev 19:36). **"that thy days may be lengthened in the land" → "so that your days may be long upon the ground"** — Hebrew הָאֲדָמָה, as Exod 20:12.
- **"all that do such things, and all that do unrighteously" → "everyone who does these things, everyone who does injustice"** (v16) — Hebrew עָוֶל, as Lev 19:15, 35. "An abomination to Yahweh" is literally "the abomination of Yahweh".
- **"when ye were come forth" → "when you came out"** (v17) — "you" is plural here only; elsewhere in v17–19 it is singular.
- **"How he met thee by the way, and smote the hindmost of thee, even all that were feeble behind thee, when thou wast faint and weary; and he feared not God" → "how he met you on the way and cut off your rear, all who were worn out behind you, and you were weary and spent; and he did not fear Elohim"** (v18) — Hebrew קָרְךָ "happened upon you". וַיְזַנֵּב is a verb made from זָנָב "tail" (28:13, 44): "he tailed you", struck at the tail. הַנֶּחֱשָׁלִים is found only here; the meaning is uncertain ("worn out", "lagging"). "And he did not fear Elohim" is said of Amalek as the accents divide the verse; the words could also be read of Israel ("and not fearing Elohim").
- **"blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven; thou shalt not forget it" → "blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under the heavens; you shall not forget"** (v19) — the words of Exod 17:14 (זֵכֶר); the Hebrew has no "it". "All around" is literally "from round about" (12:10). "To possess it" is the verb יָרַשׁ.

### Notes

- **Striking before the judge** (v1–3) — at most forty. 2 Cor 11:24 speaks of "forty less one".
- **The ox** (v4) — cited in 1 Cor 9:9 and 1 Tim 5:18.
- **The husband's brother** (v5–10) — so Gen 38:8; the question of Matt 22:24–28 rests on this law. Ruth 4:7–8 tells of a sandal drawn off in another matter, redemption and exchange.
- **The hand and the palm** (v11–12) — the only place in the laws of Deuteronomy where a part of the body is cut off as a penalty.
- **Weights and measures** (v13–16) — so Lev 19:35–36; Prov 11:1; 20:10.
- **Amalek** (v17–19) — Exod 17:8–16; 1 Sam 15. Esth 3:1 calls Haman "the Agagite"; Agag is the king of Amalek in 1 Sam 15.
