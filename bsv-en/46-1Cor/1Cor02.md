# 1 Corinthians 2

[1] And I, having come to you, brothers, came not according to eminence of word or of wisdom, proclaiming to you the mystery of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[2] For I did not judge to know anything among you except ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, and this one crucified.

[3] And I came to be with you in weakness and in fear and in much trembling,

[4] and my word and my proclamation were not in persuasive words of wisdom, but in a showing forth of Spirit and of power,

[5] that your faith might not be in the wisdom of men but in the power of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[6] But we speak wisdom among the perfect, yet a wisdom not of this age nor of the rulers of this age, who are being abolished;

[7] but we speak ⟦elohim|Elohim's|God's⟧ wisdom in a mystery, the hidden one, which ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ predestined before the ages unto our glory,

[8] which none of the rulers of this age has known; for if they had known, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory;

[9] but just as it is written:

"**What eye did not see and ear did not hear
and did not come up upon the heart of man,
as many things as ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ prepared for those who love him**" —

[10] but to us ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ revealed them through the Spirit; for the Spirit searches all things, even the depths of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[11] For who among men knows the things of the man except the spirit of the man that is in him? So also the things of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ no one has known except the Spirit of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[12] But we received not the spirit of the world but the Spirit that is from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that we might know the things granted to us by ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[13] which we also speak, not in words taught by human wisdom but in words taught by the Spirit, comparing spiritual things with spiritual.

[14] But a soulish man does not accept the things of the Spirit of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, for they are foolishness to him, and he is not able to know them, because they are spiritually examined.

[15] But the spiritual one examines all things, yet he himself is examined by no one.

[16] "**For who has known the mind of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧, who will instruct him?**" But we have the mind of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v2) — see note on 1 Cor 1.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (vv2, 16) — see note on 1 Cor 1.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — see note on 1 Cor 1.
- **"when I came to you, came not with excellency of speech or of wisdom" → "having come to you, came not according to eminence of word or of wisdom"** (v1) — Greek ἐλθὼν … ἦλθον οὐ καθʼ ὑπεροχὴν λόγου ἢ σοφίας. ὑπεροχή is a standing above others (1 Tim 2:2 "high position"); λόγος is "word" (1:17).
- **"declaring unto you the testimony of God" → "proclaiming to you the mystery of *Elohim*"** (v1) — the verse follows μυστήριον, "mystery" (WH, NA28); the printed SBLGNT reads μαρτύριον, "testimony" (Treg, NIV, RP), which the KJV also follows. "Proclaiming" here is καταγγέλλων; "proclamation" in v4 is κήρυγμα, another word.
- **"I determined not to know any thing" → "I did not judge to know anything"** (v2) — Greek οὐ γὰρ ἔκρινά τι εἰδέναι; κρίνω is "judge" (as 2 Cor 2:1). The "not" stands with "judge" in the Greek; the sense can also be "I judged to know nothing".
- **"and him crucified" → "and this one crucified"** (v2) — Greek καὶ τοῦτον ἐσταυρωμένον.
- **"I was with you" → "I came to be with you"** (v3) — Greek ἐγενόμην πρὸς ὑμᾶς.
- **"my speech and my preaching was not with enticing words of man's wisdom" → "my word and my proclamation were not in persuasive words of wisdom"** (v4) — Greek ὁ λόγος μου καὶ τὸ κήρυγμά μου (as 1:21). The verse follows ἐν πειθοῖς σοφίας λόγοις, "in persuasive words of wisdom" (WH, Treg, NA28); the printed SBLGNT reads ἐν πειθοῖ σοφίας, "in persuasion of wisdom" (Holmes); the KJV follows πειθοῖς ἀνθρωπίνης σοφίας λόγοις, "of human wisdom" (RP). The Greek has no verb ("were" is supplied).
- **"in demonstration of the Spirit and of power" → "in a showing forth of Spirit and of power"** (v4) — Greek ἐν ἀποδείξει πνεύματος καὶ δυνάμεως, without articles; the verb of the same root in 4:9 ("showed forth"). "Spirit" has no article: it can also be "of spirit".
- **"should not stand in" → "might not be in"** (v5) — Greek μὴ ᾖ ἐν; the Greek has no "rest" or "stand".
- **"them that are perfect" → "the perfect"** (v6) — Greek τοῖς τελείοις, as Phil 3:15; Heb 5:14; "mature" is an interpretation.
- **"of this world … the princes of this world, that come to nought" → "of this age … the rulers of this age, who are being abolished"** (v6) — Greek τοῦ αἰῶνος τούτου, twice, "age"; ἀρχόντων "rulers"; τῶν καταργουμένων, present participle of καταργέω "abolish" (1:28). The Greek has no "doomed".
- **"the wisdom of God in a mystery, even the hidden wisdom" → "*Elohim's* wisdom in a mystery, the hidden one"** (v7) — Greek θεοῦ σοφίαν ἐν μυστηρίῳ, τὴν ἀποκεκρυμμένην: "of God" stands first, and "in a mystery" is a phrase of its own, not the adjective "secret".
- **"ordained before the world" → "predestined before the ages"** (v7) — Greek προώρισεν (as Eph 1:5, 11) πρὸ τῶν αἰώνων.
- **"knew" → "has known"; "had they known it" → "if they had known"** (v8) — Greek ἔγνωκεν, perfect; εἰ γὰρ ἔγνωσαν, without an object. γινώσκω is "know"; "understand" is another word.
- **"Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither have entered into the heart of man, the things which God hath prepared" → "What eye did not see and ear did not hear and did not come up upon the heart of man, as many things as *Elohim* prepared"** (v9) — Greek ἃ ὀφθαλμὸς οὐκ εἶδεν … καὶ ἐπὶ καρδίαν ἀνθρώπου οὐκ ἀνέβη, ὅσα ἡτοίμασεν ὁ θεός: past tenses; "imagined" is not in the Greek. The printed text reads ὅσα, "as many things as" (WH, Treg); NA28 and RP read ἅ, "what". The quotation is a string of relative clauses with no main verb, and the verse leaves it so.
- **"But God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit" → "but to us *Elohim* revealed them through the Spirit"** (v10) — the verse follows δέ, "but" (Treg, NA28, RP); the printed SBLGNT reads γάρ, "for" (WH). "To us" stands first in the Greek. The Greek has no object ("them" is supplied: English needs one) and no "his" (WH, Treg, NA28; the KJV follows + αὐτοῦ, RP).
- **"the deep things" → "the depths"** (v10) — Greek τὰ βάθη.
- **"what man knoweth the things of a man, save the spirit of man" → "who among men knows the things of the man except the spirit of the man"** (v11) — Greek τίς … ἀνθρώπων τὰ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου … τὸ πνεῦμα τοῦ ἀνθρώπου; the Greek has no "thoughts". "That is in him" renders τὸ ἐν αὐτῷ, an article and not a pronoun.
- **"knoweth no man" → "no one has known"** (v11) — Greek ἔγνωκεν, perfect of γινώσκω (WH, Treg, NA28); the first "knows" in the verse is οἶδεν, which the KJV's Greek has here too (RP).
- **"the spirit which is of God" → "the Spirit that is from *Elohim*"** (v12) — Greek τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἐκ τοῦ θεοῦ.
- **"the things that are freely given to us" → "the things granted to us"** (v12) — Greek τὰ … χαρισθέντα, from χαρίζομαι "grant", root χάρις "grace"; "know" is εἰδῶμεν.
- **"not in the words which man's wisdom teacheth, but which the Holy Ghost teacheth" → "not in words taught by human wisdom but in words taught by the Spirit"** (v13) — Greek ἐν διδακτοῖς ἀνθρωπίνης σοφίας λόγοις, ἀλλʼ ἐν διδακτοῖς πνεύματος: "Spirit" without article and without "Holy" (WH, Treg, NA28; the KJV follows + ἁγίου, RP); the second "words" is supplied from the first.
- **"comparing spiritual things with spiritual" kept** (v13) — Greek πνευματικοῖς πνευματικὰ συγκρίνοντες. συγκρίνω is "compare" in 2 Cor 10:12; it can also mean "combine" or "interpret". πνευματικοῖς can be neuter ("with spiritual things / words") or masculine ("to spiritual men"). The verse keeps the open form.
- **"the natural man" → "a soulish man"** (v14) — Greek ψυχικὸς ἄνθρωπος, without the article; ψυχικός is of the root of ψυχή "soul" (as Jas 3:15; Jude 19), set against πνευματικός "spiritual", of πνεῦμα "spirit".
- **"receiveth not" → "does not accept"** (v14) — Greek οὐ δέχεται; "received" in v12 is λαμβάνω.
- **"spiritually discerned" → "spiritually examined"; "judgeth" / "is judged" → "examines" / "is examined"** (v14, 15) — Greek ἀνακρίνω, three times: to examine, to question as in a hearing (also 4:3–4); "judge" is κρίνω (v2; 4:5).
- **"he that is spiritual" → "the spiritual one"** (v15) — Greek ὁ πνευματικός; the Greek has no "person".
- **"that he may instruct him" → "who will instruct him"** (v16) — Greek ὃς συμβιβάσει αὐτόν, a relative clause with a future verb. The Greek has κύριος, "Lord"; the wording is that of Isa 40:13 in Greek, where it is the mind of Yahweh.

### Notes
- **v9** echoes Isa 64:4; 65:17.
- **v16** quotes Isa 40:13.
