# 1 Corinthians 14

[1] Pursue love, and be zealous for the spiritual things, but rather that you may prophesy.

[2] For he who speaks in a tongue speaks not to men but to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; for no one hears, but in spirit he speaks mysteries;

[3] but he who prophesies speaks to men building up and encouragement and comfort.

[4] He who speaks in a tongue builds up himself; but he who prophesies builds up the church.

[5] Now I want you all to speak in tongues, but rather that you may prophesy; and greater is he who prophesies than he who speaks in tongues, unless he interprets, that the church may receive building up.

[6] But now, brothers, if I come to you speaking in tongues, what shall I profit you, unless I speak to you either in revelation or in knowledge or in prophecy or in teaching?

[7] Even the things without soul that give a voice, whether flute or harp, if they give no distinction to the sounds, how will it be known what is played on the flute or on the harp?

[8] For also if a trumpet gives an uncertain voice, who will prepare himself for war?

[9] So also you, if through the tongue you do not give a clear word, how will it be known what is spoken? For you will be speaking into the air.

[10] There are, it may be, so many kinds of voices in the world, and none is without voice;

[11] if then I do not know the power of the voice, I shall be a barbarian to him who speaks, and he who speaks a barbarian to me.

[12] So also you, since you are zealous for spirits, seek that you may abound unto the building up of the church.

[13] Therefore let him who speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.

[14] For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays, but my mind is unfruitful.

[15] What is it then? I will pray with the spirit, and I will pray also with the mind; I will sing psalms with the spirit, and I will sing psalms also with the mind.

[16] Otherwise, if you bless in spirit, how will he who fills the place of the unskilled say the Amen at your thanksgiving, since he does not know what you are saying?

[17] For you indeed give thanks well, but the other is not built up.

[18] I give thanks to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧: I speak in tongues more than all of you;

[19] but in church I want to speak five words with my mind, that I may instruct others also, rather than ten thousand words in a tongue.

[20] Brothers, do not become young children in understanding; but in malice be infants, and in understanding become perfect.

[21] In the ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧ it is written, "**By men of other tongues and by lips of others I will speak to this people, and not even so will they listen to me, says the Lord**."

[22] So then the tongues are for a sign, not for those who believe but for the unbelievers; but prophecy is not for the unbelievers but for those who believe.

[23] If then the whole church comes together to the same place and all speak in tongues, and unskilled ones or unbelievers come in, will they not say that you are mad?

[24] But if all prophesy, and some unbeliever or unskilled one comes in, he is reproved by all, he is examined by all,

[25] the hidden things of his heart become manifest, and so, falling on his face, he will bow down to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, reporting that ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is really among you.

[26] What is it then, brothers? When you come together, each one has a psalm, has a teaching, has a revelation, has a tongue, has an interpretation. Let all things be done unto building up.

[27] If anyone speaks in a tongue, let it be by two or at the most three, and in turn, and let one interpret;

[28] but if there is no interpreter, let him be silent in church, and let him speak to himself and to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[29] And let prophets speak, two or three, and let the others discern;

[30] but if something is revealed to another who is sitting, let the first be silent.

[31] For you can all prophesy one by one, that all may learn and all may be encouraged

[32] (and spirits of prophets are subject to prophets,

[33] for ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is not of unrest but of peace), as in all the churches of the saints.

[34] Let the women be silent in the churches, for it is not permitted to them to speak; but let them be subject, as also the ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧ says.

[35] And if they want to learn anything, let them ask their own husbands at home, for it is shameful for a woman to speak in church.

[36] Or did the word of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ go out from you? Or did it reach to you only?

[37] If anyone thinks he is a prophet or spiritual, let him recognize that the things which I write to you are a commandment of the Lord;

[38] but if anyone does not know, he is not known.

[39] So then, my brothers, be zealous to prophesy, and do not forbid the speaking in tongues;

[40] but let all things be done becomingly and according to order.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v2, 18, 25, 28, 33, 36) — systematic.
- **"law" → "Torah"** (v21, 34) — Greek ὁ νόμος, with the article. In v21 the words quoted are from the prophet Isaiah: "the Torah" here names the Scriptures more widely.
- **"edifying" / "edification" / "edify" → "building up" / "build up"** (v3, 4, 5, 12, 17, 26) — Greek οἰκοδομή, οἰκοδομέω, the building of a house.
- **"unknown tongue" → "tongue"** (throughout) — Greek γλώσσῃ, γλώσσαις, a dative without a preposition (v19 alone has ἐν γλώσσῃ); KJV adds "unknown", which is not in the Greek. γλῶσσα is the tongue and a language; the same word in v9 ("through the tongue") and in the quotation of v21.
- **"Follow after charity, and desire spiritual gifts" → "Pursue love, and be zealous for the spiritual things"** (v1) — Greek διώκετε, ζηλοῦτε δὲ τὰ πνευματικά: a neuter plural adjective, "the spiritual things" (as 12:1); no word "gifts". ζηλόω is "be zealous" also in v39 and 12:31, and "is jealous" in 13:4.
- **"but rather that ye may prophesy" kept** (v1, 5) — Greek μᾶλλον δὲ ἵνα προφητεύητε, the same words in both verses.
- **"no man understandeth him; howbeit in the spirit he speaketh mysteries" → "no one hears, but in spirit he speaks mysteries"** (v2) — Greek οὐδεὶς γὰρ ἀκούει, "hears" (in the sense "understands"); the Greek has no "him". πνεύματι has no article and no "his": "in spirit", "by the Spirit" or "with his spirit" (v14 "my spirit").
- **"speaketh unto men to edification, and exhortation, and comfort" → "speaks to men building up and encouragement and comfort"** (v3) — Greek λαλεῖ οἰκοδομὴν καὶ παράκλησιν καὶ παραμυθίαν: three objects of "speaks", no "for their". παράκλησις is "encouragement" or "exhortation" (so also the verb in v31).
- **"the church" kept** (v4) — Greek ἐκκλησίαν, without the article: "a church".
- **"except he interpret" → "unless he interprets"** (v5) — Greek ἐκτὸς εἰ μὴ διερμηνεύῃ: the subject is the one who speaks in tongues; the Greek has no "someone".
- **"that the church may receive edifying" → "that the church may receive building up"** (v5) — Greek ἵνα ἡ ἐκκλησία οἰκοδομὴν λάβῃ.
- **"except I shall speak to you either by revelation…" → "unless I speak to you either in revelation or in knowledge or in prophecy or in teaching"** (v6) — Greek ἐὰν μὴ ὑμῖν λαλήσω ἢ ἐν ἀποκαλύψει …: "speak in", four times ἐν; διδαχή is "teaching".
- **"things without life giving sound" → "the things without soul that give a voice"** (v7) — Greek τὰ ἄψυχα φωνὴν διδόντα: ἄψυχος is "without soul (ψυχή)", lifeless. φωνή, "voice, sound", is one word in v7, 8, 10 and 11; in v10–11 it is used of languages.
- **"pipe or harp … what is piped or harped" → "flute or harp … what is played on the flute or on the harp"** (v7) — Greek αὐλός, κιθάρα (as Rev 5:8), τὸ αὐλούμενον ἢ τὸ κιθαριζόμενον; "sounds" is φθόγγοις, a different word from "voice".
- **"if the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself to the battle" → "if a trumpet gives an uncertain voice, who will prepare himself for war"** (v8) — Greek ἄδηλον σάλπιγξ φωνὴν … εἰς πόλεμον. The verse follows the word order σάλπιγξ φωνήν (WH NA28; φωνὴν σάλπιγξ Treg RP), which does not change the translation.
- **"words easy to be understood" → "a clear word"** (v9) — Greek εὔσημον λόγον, "well-marked"; λόγος is "word", singular.
- **"none of them is without signification" → "none is without voice"** (v10) — Greek οὐδὲν ἄφωνον, "voiceless": a play on φωνῶν. εἰ τύχοι is "if it should so happen, it may be" (also 15:37).
- **"the meaning of the voice" → "the power of the voice"** (v11) — Greek τὴν δύναμιν τῆς φωνῆς: δύναμις, "power", here the force or meaning of what is said.
- **"barbarian" kept; "unto me" → "to me"** (v11) — Greek βάρβαρος, one whose speech is not understood (as Col 3:11); ἐν ἐμοί, literally "in me": in my case, in my eyes.
- **"zealous of spiritual gifts" → "zealous for spirits"** (v12) — Greek ζηλωταί ἐστε πνευμάτων: the plural of πνεῦμα, "spirits", not the adjective of v1. It is usually understood of workings of the Spirit; the verse keeps the word.
- **"seek that ye may excel to the edifying of the church" → "seek that you may abound unto the building up of the church"** (v12) — Greek περισσεύητε, "abound"; in the Greek "unto the building up of the church" stands first.
- **"my understanding" → "my mind"** (v14, 15, 19) — Greek νοῦς; "understanding" in v20 is a different word.
- **"I will sing" → "I will sing psalms"** (v15) — Greek ψαλῶ, the verb of "psalm" (v26; as Eph 5:19; Jas 5:13). τῷ πνεύματι, τῷ νοΐ have the article and no "my".
- **"when thou shalt bless with the spirit" → "if you bless in spirit"** (v16) — the verse follows ἐν πνεύματι (WH NA28); the other readings are πνεύματι (Treg; the printed SBLGNT text) and τῷ πνεύματι (RP).
- **"he that occupieth the room of the unlearned" → "he who fills the place of the unskilled"** (v16, 23, 24) — Greek ἰδιώτης, a private person, one without training or skill (as 2 Cor 11:6); here one who does not know the tongue, or one who stands outside. "The Amen" has the article in Greek.
- **"I thank my God, I speak with tongues" → "I give thanks to *Elohim*: I speak in tongues"** (v18) — Greek εὐχαριστῶ τῷ θεῷ, … λαλῶ: two statements side by side, with no "that"; "my" is not in the critical text.
- **"I had rather speak" → "I want to speak … rather than"** (v19) — Greek θέλω … ἤ, "I want … than". κατηχήσω is "instruct" (as Gal 6:6).
- **"be not children in understanding: howbeit in malice be ye children, but in understanding be men" → "do not become young children in understanding; but in malice be infants, and in understanding become perfect"** (v20) — Greek παιδία, νηπιάζετε (the verb of νήπιος, "infant", 13:11), τέλειοι ("perfect", as 13:10; of persons "full-grown"); γίνεσθε is "become". ταῖς φρεσίν (only here in the New Testament) is the seat of thought.
- **"With men of other tongues and other lips" → "By men of other tongues and by lips of others"** (v21) — Greek ἐν ἑτερογλώσσοις καὶ ἐν χείλεσιν ἑτέρων, translated as printed; the wording differs from both the Hebrew and the Septuagint of Isa 28:11–12. ἐν is "in, by".
- **"and yet for all that will they not hear me" → "and not even so will they listen to me"** (v21) — Greek οὐδʼ οὕτως εἰσακούσονταί μου.
- **"prophesying serveth not for them that believe not" → "prophecy is not for the unbelievers"** (v22) — Greek ἡ δὲ προφητεία οὐ τοῖς ἀπίστοις ἀλλὰ τοῖς πιστεύουσιν: the second half has no verb and does not repeat "for a sign". The datives ("for") can also be read "to".
- **"into one place" → "to the same place"** (v23) — Greek ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτό, "to the same", "together".
- **"he is convinced of all, he is judged of all" → "he is reproved by all, he is examined by all"** (v24) — Greek ἐλέγχεται, ἀνακρίνεται (the verb of 2:14–15; 4:3–4).
- **"the secrets of his heart made manifest" → "the hidden things of his heart become manifest"** (v25) — Greek τὰ κρυπτὰ … φανερὰ γίνεται.
- **"he will worship God, and report that God is in you of a truth" → "he will bow down to *Elohim*, reporting that *Elohim* is really among you"** (v25) — Greek προσκυνήσει, to bow down before; ὄντως, "really"; ἐν ὑμῖν is "among you" or "in you".
- **"hath a doctrine" → "has a teaching"** (v26) — Greek διδαχήν. "Has" stands five times in the Greek; there is no "or".
- **"by course" → "in turn"** (v27) — Greek ἀνὰ μέρος. The Greek has no verb in "by two or at the most three".
- **"let him keep silence" / "hold his peace" / "keep silence" → "be silent"** (v28, 30, 34) — Greek σιγάω, one verb in all three places.
- **"let the other judge" → "let the others discern"** (v29) — Greek οἱ ἄλλοι διακρινέτωσαν, plural; the verb of 11:29, 31 (the noun in 12:10). The Greek names no object.
- **"If any thing be revealed" → "if something is revealed"** (v30) — Greek ἀποκαλυφθῇ, a passive without a subject; "something" is supplied.
- **"comforted" → "encouraged"** (v31) — Greek παρακαλῶνται, "be encouraged" or "be exhorted".
- **"God is not the author of confusion" → "*Elohim* is not of unrest"** (v33) — Greek οὐ γάρ ἐστιν ἀκαταστασίας ὁ θεὸς ἀλλὰ εἰρήνης: "God" stands once, with two genitives; no "author". ἀκαταστασία as 2 Cor 12:20; Jas 3:16.
- **"as in all churches of the saints"** (v33) — the printed text (SBLGNT) sets v32–33a in brackets and closes the sentence after these words, so that they go with what precedes (v31, or v33a). They can also begin the sentence of v34: "As in all the churches of the saints, let the women be silent in the churches." The old manuscripts have no punctuation.
- **"Let your women keep silence … but they are commanded to be under obedience" → "Let the women be silent … but let them be subject"** (v34) — Greek αἱ γυναῖκες … σιγάτωσαν … ὑποτασσέσθωσαν; "your" and the infinitive ὑποτάσσεσθαι are the later text (RP). γυνή is "woman" and "wife", ἀνήρ (v35) "man" and "husband": "their own men" in v35 shows that wives are in view there; whether v34 means all women or wives is not decided by the word. The verse names no object of "be subject" and no passage of the Torah. What kind of speaking is meant (λαλεῖν is the verb used of tongues and prophecy throughout the chapter; 11:5 has a woman praying and prophesying) is a matter of interpretation.
- **"it is a shame for women" → "it is shameful for a woman"** (v35) — Greek αἰσχρὸν … γυναικί, singular.
- **"What? came the word of God out from you? or came it unto you only?" → "Or did the word of *Elohim* go out from you? Or did it reach to you only?"** (v36) — Greek ἢ … ἢ …; "you only" (ὑμᾶς μόνους) is masculine plural.
- **"let him acknowledge" → "let him recognize"** (v37) — Greek ἐπιγινωσκέτω, the verb of 13:12 ("know fully") and 16:18.
- **"the commandments of the Lord" → "a commandment of the Lord"** (v37) — the verse follows κυρίου ἐστὶν ἐντολή (WH Treg NA28); the printed SBLGNT text has κυρίου ἐστίν, "are of the Lord" (Holmes), and RP εἰσὶν ἐντολαί, "are commandments".
- **"if any man be ignorant, let him be ignorant" → "if anyone does not know, he is not known"** (v38) — the printed text has ἀγνοεῖται, "he is not known, not recognized" (WH NA28); the other reading is ἀγνοείτω, "let him not know" (Treg RP). One verb twice; by whom he is not known is not said.
- **"covet to prophesy, and forbid not to speak with tongues" → "be zealous to prophesy, and do not forbid the speaking in tongues"** (v39) — Greek ζηλοῦτε τὸ προφητεύειν, καὶ τὸ λαλεῖν μὴ κωλύετε γλώσσαις.
- **"decently and in order" → "becomingly and according to order"** (v40) — Greek εὐσχημόνως (as 1 Thess 4:12; the opposite verb in 13:5) καὶ κατὰ τάξιν.

### Notes
- **v21** quotes Isa 28:11-12.
