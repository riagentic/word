# 1 Corinthians 7

[1] Now concerning the things of which you wrote: it is good for a man not to touch a woman;

[2] but because of the sexual immoralities let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her own husband.

[3] Let the husband render to the wife the debt, and likewise also the wife to the husband.

[4] The wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband; and likewise also the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife.

[5] Do not deprive one another, unless perhaps by agreement for a time, that you may have leisure for prayer and be together again, that Satan may not tempt you because of your lack of self-control.

[6] But this I say by way of concession, not by way of command.

[7] But I want all people to be as also myself; but each has his own gift from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, one thus, and another thus.

[8] But I say to the unmarried and to the widows: it is good for them if they abide as I also;

[9] but if they do not exercise self-control, let them marry, for it is better to marry than to burn.

[10] But to those who have married I charge — not I, but the Lord — that a wife not separate from a husband —

[11] but if she does separate, let her abide unmarried or be reconciled to the husband — and that a husband not leave a wife.

[12] But to the rest I say — I, not the Lord: if any brother has an unbelieving wife, and she consents to dwell with him, let him not leave her;

[13] and if any woman has an unbelieving husband, and this one consents to dwell with her, let her not leave the husband.

[14] For the unbelieving husband has been sanctified in the wife, and the unbelieving wife has been sanctified in the brother; since in that case your children are unclean, but now they are holy.

[15] But if the unbeliever separates, let him separate; the brother or the sister has not been enslaved in such things; but in peace ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has called you.

[16] For how do you know, wife, whether you will save the husband? Or how do you know, husband, whether you will save the wife?

[17] Only, as the Lord apportioned to each, as ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has called each, so let him walk; and so I ordain in all the churches.

[18] Was anyone called having been circumcised? Let him not become uncircumcised. Has anyone been called in uncircumcision? Let him not be circumcised.

[19] Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[20] Each one, in the calling in which he was called, in this let him abide.

[21] Were you called as a slave? Let it not be a care to you; but if you are also able to become free, rather use it.

[22] For the one called in the Lord as a slave is a freedman of the Lord; likewise the one called as a free man is a slave of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧.

[23] You were bought with a price; do not become slaves of men.

[24] Each one, in that in which he was called, brothers, in this let him abide with ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[25] Now concerning the virgins I have no command of the Lord, but I give an opinion, as one who has received mercy from the Lord to be faithful.

[26] I suppose therefore that this is good because of the present necessity, that it is good for a man to be thus.

[27] Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek to be loosed. Are you loosed from a wife? Do not seek a wife.

[28] But if you do marry, you have not sinned; and if the virgin marries, she has not sinned. But such ones will have affliction in the flesh, and I am sparing you.

[29] But this I say, brothers: the time has been shortened; henceforth, that even those who have wives be as not having,

[30] and those who weep as not weeping, and those who rejoice as not rejoicing, and those who buy as not possessing,

[31] and those who use the world as not using it to the full; for the fashion of this world is passing away.

[32] But I want you to be without anxiety. The unmarried man is anxious about the things of the Lord, how he may please the Lord;

[33] but the one who has married is anxious about the things of the world, how he may please the wife,

[34] and he is divided. And the unmarried woman and the virgin is anxious about the things of the Lord, that she may be holy both in the body and in the spirit; but she who has married is anxious about the things of the world, how she may please the husband.

[35] But this I say for your own profit, not that I may throw a noose on you, but for what is becoming and for waiting well upon the Lord without distraction.

[36] But if anyone supposes that he is behaving unbecomingly toward his virgin, if past the peak, and so it ought to be, let him do what he wants; he does not sin; let them marry.

[37] But he who stands firm in his heart, not having necessity, but has authority concerning his own will, and has judged this in his own heart, to keep his own virgin, will do well;

[38] so then both he who gives his own virgin in marriage does well, and he who does not give in marriage will do better.

[39] A wife is bound for as long a time as her husband lives; but if the husband falls asleep, she is free to be married to whom she wants, only in the Lord;

[40] but she is more blessed if she abides thus, according to my opinion; and I think that I also have the Spirit of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v22) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"wife" / "woman", "husband" / "man"** (throughout) — Greek has one word for each pair: γυνή is "woman" and "wife", ἀνήρ is "man" and "husband". Where the verse says "wife" or "husband" the Greek word is the same as in "woman" (v1, 2, 13) and "man". Greek often has only the article where English says "his wife", "her husband"; the verse keeps "the".
- **"whereof ye wrote unto me" → "of which you wrote"** (v1) — Greek περὶ δὲ ὧν ἐγράψατε (WH NA28); "to me" (μοι) is added by Treg and RP.
- **"It is good for a man not to touch a woman" kept, without quotation marks** (v1) — Greek καλὸν ἀνθρώπῳ γυναικὸς μὴ ἅπτεσθαι; "touch" is the Greek verb, an indirect word for sexual union (as Gen 20:6 in the Septuagint). Many take the sentence as a saying from the Corinthians' letter that Paul answers; the Greek does not mark it, and it can equally be Paul's own statement.
- **"to avoid fornication" → "because of the sexual immoralities"** (v2) — Greek διὰ δὲ τὰς πορνείας, a plural with the article: the acts of sexual immorality. No "temptation" and no "to avoid" in the Greek.
- **"due benevolence" → "the debt"** (v3) — Greek τὴν ὀφειλήν, "what is owed" (WH Treg NA28); the KJV follows ὀφειλομένην εὔνοιαν (RP). The Greek does not say what is owed; v4–5 show it.
- **"hath not power of" → "does not have authority over"** (v4) — Greek ἐξουσιάζει, the verb of ἐξουσία (v37), as 6:12.
- **"Defraud ye not" → "Do not deprive"** (v5) — Greek ἀποστερεῖτε, as 6:7–8.
- **"that ye may give yourselves to fasting and prayer; and come together again" → "that you may have leisure for prayer and be together again"** (v5) — Greek ἵνα σχολάσητε τῇ προσευχῇ καὶ πάλιν ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτὸ ἦτε: σχολάζω is "have leisure, be free for"; "be together" is literally "be at the same". "Fasting and" and "come together" (συνέρχησθε) are later readings (RP). "Be together again" depends on "that", like "have leisure".
- **"for a time" kept** (v5) — Greek πρὸς καιρόν; καιρός again in v29. No "limited" in the Greek.
- **"incontinency" → "lack of self-control"** (v5) — Greek ἀκρασίαν, the opposite of ἐγκράτεια "self-control"; v9 has its verb.
- **"by permission, and not of commandment" → "by way of concession, not by way of command"** (v6) — Greek κατὰ συγγνώμην, οὐ κατʼ ἐπιταγήν. "This" can point back (v5, or v2–5) or forward (v7).
- **"For I would that all men were" → "But I want all people to be"** (v7) — Greek θέλω δέ (WH Treg NA28; RP γάρ, "for"); θέλω is "want".
- **"his proper gift" → "his own gift"** (v7) — Greek ἴδιον χάρισμα, a gift of grace (χάρις). "One thus, and another thus" is ὁ μὲν οὕτως, ὁ δὲ οὕτως.
- **"unmarried" kept** (v8, 11, 32, 34) — Greek ἄγαμος, "not married" (ἀ- + γάμος "marriage"), of a man or a woman. The word does not say why: never married, widowed, or separated (so v11, of a wife who has left). In v8 τοῖς ἀγάμοις is masculine plural and stands beside "the widows"; it can be all the unmarried, or the unmarried men, or widowers. The verse says only "unmarried". The Greek has no "single".
- **"if they abide even as I" → "if they abide as I also"** (v8) — Greek ἐὰν μείνωσιν ὡς κἀγώ, without a verb after "I". What Paul's own state was (never married, widowed, or left by a wife) the Greek does not say.
- **"if they cannot contain" → "if they do not exercise self-control"** (v9) — Greek εἰ δὲ οὐκ ἐγκρατεύονται, a present tense; the Greek has no "cannot".
- **"to burn" kept** (v9) — Greek πυροῦσθαι, "be set on fire", as 2 Cor 11:29; no "with passion" in the Greek. It is taken of desire, or by some of the fire of judgment.
- **"the married" → "those who have married"** (v10) — Greek τοῖς γεγαμηκόσιν, a perfect participle of γαμέω "marry".
- **"I command" → "I charge"** (v10) — Greek παραγγέλλω.
- **"Let not the wife depart from her husband" → "that a wife not separate from a husband"** (v10, 11, 15) — Greek χωρισθῆναι, χωρισθῇ, χωρίζεται. The form in v10–11 is passive ("be separated"), but the verb is used in this form for "separate oneself, go away" (so v15). Without articles: "a wife", "a husband".
- **"put away" → "leave"** (v11, 12, 13) — Greek ἀφίημι, "let go, leave, send away". The same verb is said of the husband (v11, 12) and of the wife (v13); the KJV has "put away" and "leave". It can mean sending the other away or going away oneself. The usual word for sending a wife away with a divorce is another one (ἀπολύω), which is not in this chapter.
- **"that believeth not" → "unbelieving"** (v12, 13, 14) — Greek ἄπιστος; in v15 "the unbeliever".
- **"be pleased to dwell with him" → "consents to dwell with him"** (v12, 13) — Greek συνευδοκεῖ, "agree together with".
- **"And the woman which hath" → "and if any woman has"** (v13) — Greek καὶ γυνὴ εἴ τις ἔχει (NA28, also SBLGNT); WH, Treg and RP read ἥτις, "a woman who has". "The husband" is τὸν ἄνδρα (WH Treg NA28); RP has "him".
- **"is sanctified by the wife … by the husband" → "has been sanctified in the wife … in the brother"** (v14) — Greek ἡγίασται, a perfect; ἐν, "in", which can also mean "through" or "in the person of". "The brother" is ἀδελφῷ (WH Treg NA28); "the husband" (ἀνδρί) is the later reading (RP). ἁγιάζω "sanctify" is the verb of ἅγιος "holy" at the end of the verse.
- **"else were your children unclean" → "since in that case your children are unclean"** (v14) — Greek ἐπεὶ ἄρα … ἐστιν, as 5:10; the Greek verb is "are".
- **"A brother or a sister is not under bondage in such cases" → "the brother or the sister has not been enslaved in such things"** (v15) — Greek οὐ δεδούλωται, a perfect of δουλόω "make a slave"; a different verb from "bound" (δέω) in v27, 39. ἐν τοῖς τοιούτοις is "in such things" or "among such people". Whether "not enslaved" means free to marry another, or only free from the duty to hold the marriage, the Greek does not say.
- **"God hath called us to peace" → "in peace *Elohim* has called you"** (v15) — Greek ἐν δὲ εἰρήνῃ, "in peace". The verse follows ὑμᾶς "you" (WH NA28); ἡμᾶς "us" is read by SBLGNT, Treg, NIV and RP.
- **"what knowest thou" → "how do you know"** (v16) — Greek τί οἶδας, twice, literally "what do you know". The question can expect "perhaps you will" or "you cannot know that you will".
- **"But as God hath distributed to every man, as the Lord hath called every one" → "Only, as the Lord apportioned to each, as *Elohim* has called each"** (v17) — Greek εἰ μή, "except, only". "The Lord … *Elohim*" in this order is read by WH, Treg and NA28; RP has them the other way round. ἐμέρισεν "apportioned" (NA28 RP, also SBLGNT); WH and Treg read μεμέρικεν, "has apportioned". μερίζω is the verb of "divided" in v34.
- **"let him not become uncircumcised" kept** (v18) — Greek μὴ ἐπισπάσθω, literally "let him not draw over": the word for an operation that hid the circumcision. The Greek has no object.
- **"servant" → "slave"** (v21, 22, 23) — Greek δοῦλος.
- **"care not for it" → "Let it not be a care to you"** (v21) — Greek μή σοι μελέτω.
- **"but if thou mayest be made free, use it rather" → "but if you are also able to become free, rather use it"** (v21) — Greek ἀλλʼ εἰ καὶ δύνασαι ἐλεύθερος γενέσθαι, μᾶλλον χρῆσαι. The verb "use" has no object in the Greek; "it" is supplied because English needs one. It is either "rather use the freedom" or "rather use your slavery". εἰ καί is "if also, if indeed" or "even if"; the second goes with the second reading.
- **"the Lord's freeman" → "a freedman of the Lord"** (v22) — Greek ἀπελεύθερος κυρίου, a slave who has been set free; "free man" in the second half is ἐλεύθερος.
- **"be not ye the servants of men" → "do not become slaves of men"** (v23) — Greek μὴ γίνεσθε.
- **"concerning virgins" → "concerning the virgins"** (v25) — Greek περὶ δὲ τῶν παρθένων. παρθένος is "virgin", a person who has had no sexual union; in v28, 34, 36–38 it is feminine. The genitive plural here does not show the gender (the word is said of men in Rev 14:4). The Greek has no "betrothed".
- **"I give my judgment" → "I give an opinion"** (v25, 40) — Greek γνώμην, as 2 Cor 8:10.
- **"as one that hath obtained mercy of the Lord to be faithful" → "as one who has received mercy from the Lord to be faithful"** (v25) — Greek ἠλεημένος ὑπὸ κυρίου πιστὸς εἶναι; πιστός is "faithful" or "trustworthy".
- **"for the present distress" → "because of the present necessity"** (v26) — Greek διὰ τὴν ἐνεστῶσαν ἀνάγκην. ἀνάγκη is "necessity", also in v37; here it can mean distress. ἐνεστῶσαν is "present" or "that has set in, impending".
- **"that it is good for a man so to be" → "that it is good for a man to be thus"** (v26) — Greek τὸ οὕτως εἶναι; "thus" is "as he is" (v27) or "as I am" (v8). No "remain" in the Greek.
- **"Art thou bound unto a wife? … Art thou loosed from a wife?" kept** (v27) — Greek δέδεσαι γυναικί; … λέλυσαι ἀπὸ γυναικός; two perfects, "are you in the state of having been bound / loosed". "Seek not to be loosed" is μὴ ζήτει λύσιν, "do not seek a loosing", of the same root as "loosed". "Loosed from a wife" can mean one whose marriage has ended (by death or divorce), or one who is simply not bound; γυνή can also be a woman to whom a man is promised.
- **"if a virgin marry" → "if the virgin marries"** (v28) — Greek ἡ παρθένος, with the article.
- **"trouble in the flesh" → "affliction in the flesh"** (v28) — Greek θλῖψιν … τῇ σαρκί; no "worldly" in the Greek. "Such ones" is οἱ τοιοῦτοι.
- **"but I spare you" → "and I am sparing you"** (v28) — Greek ἐγὼ δὲ ὑμῶν φείδομαι, a present tense.
- **"the time is short" → "the time has been shortened"** (v29) — Greek ὁ καιρὸς συνεσταλμένος ἐστίν, "drawn together, shortened"; καιρός is a set time, a season. No "appointed" and no "very" in the Greek.
- **"it remaineth, that" → "henceforth, that"** (v29) — Greek τὸ λοιπὸν ἵνα, "for the rest, from now on". The printed text puts the stop before τὸ λοιπόν; it can also be joined to what precedes ("the time is shortened from now on"). RP has the order τὸ λοιπόν ἐστιν.
- **"as though they possessed not" → "as not possessing"** (v30) — Greek ὡς μὴ κατέχοντες; κατέχω is "hold fast" elsewhere, "possess" here as in 2 Cor 6:10.
- **"they that use this world, as not abusing it" → "those who use the world as not using it to the full"** (v31) — Greek οἱ χρώμενοι τὸν κόσμον ὡς μὴ καταχρώμενοι: one verb, the second time with κατα-, "use up, use to the full". "The world" (WH Treg NA28); RP has "this world".
- **"fashion" kept** (v31) — Greek σχῆμα, the outward shape, as Phil 2:8. No "present" in the Greek.
- **"without carefulness" → "without anxiety"; "careth for" → "is anxious about"** (v32–34) — Greek ἀμερίμνους, μεριμνᾷ, one root; as Phil 2:20; 4:6.
- **"he that is married" → "the one who has married"** (v33, 34) — Greek ὁ γαμήσας, ἡ γαμήσασα, past participles of γαμέω.
- **"the things that are of the world" → "the things of the world"** (v33, 34) — Greek τὰ τοῦ κόσμου, as "the things of the Lord".
- **"There is difference also between a wife and a virgin. The unmarried woman careth" → "and he is divided. And the unmarried woman and the virgin is anxious"** (v34) — the verse follows καὶ μεμέρισται. καὶ ἡ γυνὴ ἡ ἄγαμος καὶ ἡ παρθένος μεριμνᾷ (WH Treg NA28): "and he is divided" ends the sentence of v33, and two women are named, "the woman who is unmarried" and "the virgin", with one verb in the singular. The KJV follows the later text (RP), which has no first "and" and reads "the wife and the virgin are divided; the unmarried woman is anxious".
- **"both in body and in spirit" → "both in the body and in the spirit"** (v34) — Greek καὶ τῷ σώματι καὶ τῷ πνεύματι (WH Treg NA28).
- **"not that I may cast a snare upon you" → "not that I may throw a noose on you"** (v35) — Greek βρόχον, a noose, a halter.
- **"for that which is comely, and that ye may attend upon the Lord without distraction" → "for what is becoming and for waiting well upon the Lord without distraction"** (v35) — Greek πρὸς τὸ εὔσχημον καὶ εὐπάρεδρον τῷ κυρίῳ ἀπερισπάστως. εὐπάρεδρον is "sitting well beside", constant attendance; ἀπερισπάστως is "without being drawn away". εὔσχημον is the opposite of "behaving unbecomingly" (v36).
- **"his virgin"** (v36, 37, 38) — Greek τὴν παρθένον αὐτοῦ, τὴν ἑαυτοῦ παρθένον. The Greek says only "his virgin". It is taken as (1) a father's or guardian's unmarried daughter, whom he gives or does not give in marriage; (2) the virgin to whom a man is betrothed; (3) by some, a woman with whom a man lives without union. The verse adds neither "daughter" nor "betrothed".
- **"if she pass the flower of her age" → "if past the peak"** (v36) — Greek ἐὰν ᾖ ὑπέρακμος. The clause has no expressed subject, and the adjective has one form for masculine and feminine: it is "if she is" (the virgin) or "if he is" (the man). ὑπέρακμος is ὑπέρ "beyond, over" + ἀκμή "peak, prime": "past the prime of age" or "over the peak" in the sense of strong desire. The verse keeps both open.
- **"and need so require" → "and so it ought to be"** (v36) — Greek καὶ οὕτως ὀφείλει γίνεσθαι; ὀφείλω "owe, ought", the root of "debt" (v3).
- **"let him do what he will, he sinneth not: let them marry" → "let him do what he wants; he does not sin; let them marry"** (v36) — Greek γαμείτωσαν, a plural: the virgin and the man who marries her (reading 1), or the man and his virgin (reading 2).
- **"having no necessity, but hath power over his own will" → "not having necessity, but has authority concerning his own will"** (v37) — Greek μὴ ἔχων ἀνάγκην, ἐξουσίαν δὲ ἔχει περὶ τοῦ ἰδίου θελήματος; ἐξουσία is "authority" (in 8:9 and chapter 9 "right").
- **"hath so decreed in his heart that he will keep his virgin" → "has judged this in his own heart, to keep his own virgin"** (v37) — Greek κέκρικεν, from κρίνω "judge"; τηρεῖν is "keep, guard". No "as his betrothed" in the Greek.
- **"doeth well" → "will do well"; "doeth better" → "will do better"** (v37, 38) — Greek ποιήσει, future (WH Treg NA28); RP has the present.
- **"he that giveth her in marriage" → "he who gives his own virgin in marriage"** (v38) — Greek ὁ γαμίζων τὴν ἑαυτοῦ παρθένον (WH NA28; Treg has the same words in another order; RP has no object). γαμίζω is "give in marriage" (as Matt 24:38); the verb for "marry" is γαμέω (v9, 28, 36, 39). Those who follow reading 2 take γαμίζω here as "marry". "Both … and" is καὶ … καί (WH Treg NA28).
- **"The wife is bound by the law" → "A wife is bound"** (v39) — Greek γυνὴ δέδεται; "by the law" (νόμῳ) is a later addition (RP).
- **"if her husband be dead" → "if the husband falls asleep"** (v39) — Greek κοιμηθῇ.
- **"she is at liberty" → "she is free"** (v39) — Greek ἐλευθέρα, as v21–22.
- **"happier" → "more blessed"** (v40) — Greek μακαριωτέρα, from μακάριος.
- **"if she so abide" → "if she abides thus"** (v40) — Greek ἐὰν οὕτως μείνῃ; μένω is "abide" in v8, 11, 20, 24, 40.
- **"the Spirit of God" → "the Spirit of *Elohim*"** (v40) — Greek πνεῦμα θεοῦ, without the article. "And I think" is δοκῶ δέ (Treg NA28 RP); WH reads γάρ, "for I think".

### Notes
- **v10–11** — "not I, but the Lord": compare the sayings of *Yeshua* in Mark 10:11–12.
- **v19** — "the commandments of *Elohim*": ἐντολῶν θεοῦ, without articles.
- **v23** — the same sentence as 6:20.
