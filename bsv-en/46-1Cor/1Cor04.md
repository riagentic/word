# 1 Corinthians 4

[1] Thus let a man consider us, as assistants of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ and stewards of mysteries of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[2] Here, moreover, it is sought in stewards that one be found faithful.

[3] But to me it is a very small thing that I should be examined by you or by a human day; but neither do I examine myself;

[4] for I am conscious of nothing against myself, yet not in this have I been justified; but the one who examines me is the Lord.

[5] So then do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, who will also bring to light the hidden things of the darkness and manifest the counsels of the hearts; and then the praise will come to each from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[6] Now these things, brothers, I refashioned unto myself and Apollos because of you, that in us you may learn the "Not beyond the things that are written," that you be not puffed up, one on behalf of the one against the other.

[7] For who distinguishes you? And what do you have that you did not receive? And if you did receive it, why do you boast as not having received it?

[8] Already you are sated, already you became rich, without us you came to reign; and would indeed that you did reign, that we also might reign with you.

[9] For I think, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ showed forth us the apostles as last, as men doomed to death, because we became a spectacle to the world, and to angels and to men.

[10] We are fools because of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, but you are sensible in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧; we are weak, but you strong; you glorious, but we without honor.

[11] Until the present hour we both hunger and thirst and are naked and are buffeted and have no settled place

[12] and labor, working with our own hands; being reviled, we bless; being persecuted, we bear with it;

[13] being defamed, we appeal; we became as the scourings of the world, the offscouring of all, until now.

[14] Not shaming you do I write these things, but admonishing you as my beloved children;

[15] for if you should have ten thousand tutors in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, yet not many fathers; for in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ through the gospel I begot you.

[16] I exhort you therefore, become imitators of me.

[17] Because of this I sent to you ⟦timothy|Timotheos|Timothy⟧, who is my child, beloved and faithful in the Lord, who will remind you of my ways that are in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, just as I teach everywhere in every church.

[18] But as though I were not coming to you, some were puffed up;

[19] but I will come to you quickly, if the Lord wills, and I will know not the word of those who are puffed up but the power,

[20] for the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is not in word but in power.

[21] What do you want? Shall I come to you with a rod, or in love and a spirit of meekness?

## Changes

- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (throughout) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Greek *theos* renders the Hebrew divine name *Elohim*.
- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v15, 17) — Hebrew/Aramaic name he was actually called; Greek *Iēsous* is a transliteration.
- **"Let a man so account of us" → "Thus let a man consider us"** (v1) — Greek οὕτως ἡμᾶς λογιζέσθω ἄνθρωπος.
- **"ministers of Christ" → "assistants of the *Mashiach*"** (v1) — Greek ὑπηρέτας, one who serves under another's orders, an assistant or officer; a different word from "servants" (διάκονοι, 3:5) and "slaves" (δοῦλοι).
- **"the mysteries of God" → "mysteries of *Elohim*"** (v1) — Greek μυστηρίων θεοῦ, without articles.
- **"Moreover it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful" → "Here, moreover, it is sought in stewards that one be found faithful"** (v2) — Greek ὧδε λοιπὸν ζητεῖται (WH, Treg, NA28 ὧδε, "here"; the KJV follows ὃ δέ, RP); ζητέω is "seek" (1:22).
- **"judged" / "judge" / "judgeth" → "examined" / "examine" / "examines"** (v3, 4) — Greek ἀνακρίνω, as 2:14–15; "judge" in v5 is κρίνω.
- **"of man's judgment" → "by a human day"** (v3) — Greek ὑπὸ ἀνθρωπίνης ἡμέρας, literally "a human day": a day of hearing set by men, set against "the day" of 3:13. The Greek has no "court".
- **"yea, I judge not mine own self" → "but neither do I examine myself"** (v3) — Greek ἀλλʼ οὐδὲ ἐμαυτὸν ἀνακρίνω.
- **"I know nothing by myself" → "I am conscious of nothing against myself"** (v4) — Greek οὐδὲν ἐμαυτῷ σύνοιδα, "I know nothing with myself", the verb of συνείδησις "conscience". "Against" is supplied: English needs it.
- **"yet am I not hereby justified" → "yet not in this have I been justified"** (v4) — Greek οὐκ ἐν τούτῳ δεδικαίωμαι, perfect of δικαιόω "justify"; not "acquitted".
- **"judge nothing" → "do not judge anything"; "until the Lord come" kept** (v5) — Greek μὴ … τι κρίνετε, ἕως ἂν ἔλθῃ ὁ κύριος.
- **"the hidden things of darkness" → "the hidden things of the darkness"; "make manifest" → "manifest"** (v5) — Greek τὰ κρυπτὰ τοῦ σκότους, a genitive; the Greek has no "now". φανερώσει. βουλάς is "counsels" (as Eph 1:11).
- **"then shall every man have praise of God" → "then the praise will come to each from *Elohim*"** (v5) — Greek ὁ ἔπαινος γενήσεται ἑκάστῳ ἀπὸ τοῦ θεοῦ: "the praise", with the article and without "his"; ἔπαινος is "praise" (Phil 1:11).
- **"I have in a figure transferred to myself and to Apollos for your sakes" → "I refashioned unto myself and Apollos because of you"** (v6) — Greek μετεσχημάτισα εἰς ἐμαυτὸν καὶ Ἀπολλῶν διʼ ὑμᾶς. μετασχηματίζω is "give another form (σχῆμα) to" (as 2 Cor 11:13–15; Phil 3:21): he has put what he says into another form, as said of himself and Apollos. The Greek has no "all" and no "applied".
- **"that ye might learn in us not to think of men above that which is written" → "that in us you may learn the 'Not beyond the things that are written'"** (v6) — Greek ἵνα ἐν ἡμῖν μάθητε τό· Μὴ ὑπὲρ ἃ γέγραπται: the article τό makes the words a saying; it has no verb (WH, Treg, NA28). The KJV follows the reading that adds φρονεῖν, "to think" (RP).
- **"that no one of you be puffed up for one against another" → "that you be not puffed up, one on behalf of the one against the other"** (v6) — Greek ἵνα μὴ εἷς ὑπὲρ τοῦ ἑνὸς φυσιοῦσθε κατὰ τοῦ ἑτέρου; φυσιόω "puff up" also in v18, 19.
- **"who maketh thee to differ from another?" → "who distinguishes you?"** (v7) — Greek τίς γάρ σε διακρίνει; "you" is singular in the whole verse. διακρίνω (elsewhere in this letter "discern") is here "set apart, make a difference between"; the Greek has no "from another" and no "sees".
- **"why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it" → "why do you boast as not having received it"** (v7) — Greek καυχᾶσαι ὡς μὴ λαβών, a participle; "it" is supplied twice.
- **"Now ye are full, now ye are rich, ye have reigned as kings without us" → "Already you are sated, already you became rich, without us you came to reign"** (v8) — Greek ἤδη κεκορεσμένοι ἐστέ, ἤδη ἐπλουτήσατε, χωρὶς ἡμῶν ἐβασιλεύσατε: a perfect and two past tenses that speak of entering a state. The verse can be read as statements, as questions or as exclamations.
- **"I would to God ye did reign" → "would indeed that you did reign"; "reign with you" kept** (v8) — Greek ὄφελόν γε ἐβασιλεύσατε; the Greek has no "God". συμβασιλεύσωμεν is "reign together with"; the Greek has no "share the rule".
- **"For I think that God hath set forth us the apostles last, as it were appointed to death" → "For I think, *Elohim* showed forth us the apostles as last, as men doomed to death"** (v9) — Greek δοκῶ γάρ, ὁ θεὸς … ἀπέδειξεν, without "that" (WH, Treg, NA28; NIV and RP add ὅτι); ἀποδείκνυμι "show forth" (2:4); ἐπιθανατίους, "appointed to death".
- **"for we are made a spectacle unto the world, and to angels, and to men" → "because we became a spectacle to the world, and to angels and to men"** (v9) — Greek θέατρον ἐγενήθημεν τῷ κόσμῳ καὶ ἀγγέλοις καὶ ἀνθρώποις: "world" has the article, "angels" and "men" have none; the sense can be "to the world, both to angels and to men".
- **"for Christ's sake" → "because of the *Mashiach*"; "wise" → "sensible"** (v10) — Greek διὰ Χριστόν; φρόνιμοι (as 2 Cor 11:19), not σοφοί "wise".
- **"ye are honourable, but we are despised" → "you glorious, but we without honor"** (v10) — Greek ἔνδοξοι, of δόξα "glory"; ἄτιμοι, "without honor" (τιμή). The verbs are supplied only where English needs them.
- **"have no certain dwellingplace" → "have no settled place"** (v11) — Greek ἀστατοῦμεν, "be unsettled, without a fixed abode". γυμνιτεύομεν is "are naked" (as 2 Cor 11:27 "nakedness"), κολαφιζόμεθα "are buffeted" (as 2 Cor 12:7).
- **"we suffer it" → "we bear with it"** (v12) — Greek ἀνεχόμεθα, as 2 Cor 11:19; "endure" is ὑπομένω.
- **"Being defamed, we intreat" → "being defamed, we appeal"** (v13) — Greek δυσφημούμενοι (WH, NA28; Treg and RP read βλασφημούμενοι, "being blasphemed") παρακαλοῦμεν: παρακαλέω, here of a friendly word in answer, hence "appeal".
- **"we are made as the filth of the world, and are the offscouring of all things unto this day" → "we became as the scourings of the world, the offscouring of all, until now"** (v13) — Greek ὡς περικαθάρματα τοῦ κόσμου ἐγενήθημεν, πάντων περίψημα ἕως ἄρτι: what is cleaned off all around, and what is wiped off. πάντων is "of all things" or "of all men".
- **"I write not these things to shame you, but as my beloved sons I warn you" → "Not shaming you do I write these things, but admonishing you as my beloved children"** (v14) — Greek οὐκ ἐντρέπων … ἀλλʼ … νουθετῶν, two participles (WH, NA28); Treg and RP read νουθετῶ, "I admonish". τέκνα is "children".
- **"instructers" → "tutors"** (v15) — Greek παιδαγωγούς, the slave who had charge of a boy (as Gal 3:24). μυρίους is "ten thousand"; the second clause has no verb.
- **"I have begotten you" → "I begot you"** (v15) — Greek ἐγέννησα, a simple past; "I" is stressed (ἐγώ). The Greek has no "became your father".
- **"Wherefore I beseech you, be ye followers of me" → "I exhort you therefore, become imitators of me"** (v16) — Greek παρακαλῶ οὖν; μιμηταί μου γίνεσθε.
- **"my beloved son, and faithful in the Lord" → "my child, beloved and faithful in the Lord"** (v17) — Greek μου τέκνον ἀγαπητὸν καὶ πιστὸν ἐν κυρίῳ.
- **"my ways which be in Christ" → "my ways that are in the *Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v17) — the printed text reads ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ (WH, NA28); Treg and RP omit Ἰησοῦ, as the KJV does.
- **"Now some are puffed up, as though I would not come to you" → "But as though I were not coming to you, some were puffed up"** (v18) — Greek ὡς μὴ ἐρχομένου δέ μου … ἐφυσιώθησαν, a past tense.
- **"shortly" → "quickly"; "the speech" → "the word"** (v19, 20) — Greek ταχέως; τὸν λόγον, ἐν λόγῳ: λόγος is "word" throughout. θελήσῃ is the verb "want" of v21.
- **"the kingdom of God is not in word" kept** (v20) — the Greek has no verb; "consist" is not in it.
- **"What will ye?" → "What do you want?"** (v21) — Greek τί θέλετε;
- **"with a rod, or in love, and in the spirit of meekness" → "with a rod, or in love and a spirit of meekness"** (v21) — Greek ἐν ῥάβδῳ … ἢ ἐν ἀγάπῃ πνεύματί τε πραΰτητος: the same preposition ἐν both times; "spirit" has no article. πραΰτης is "meekness" (as Gal 6:1).
