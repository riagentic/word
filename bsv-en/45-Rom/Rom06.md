# Romans 6

[1] What then shall we say? Shall we remain in sin, that grace may increase?

[2] May it not be! We who died to sin, how shall we still live in it?

[3] Or are you ignorant that as many of us as were ⟦immerse|immersed|baptized⟧ into ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ were ⟦immerse|immersed|baptized⟧ into his death?

[4] We were buried therefore with him through the ⟦immersion|immersion|baptism⟧ into death, that, just as ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we also might walk in newness of life.

[5] For if we have become grown together with the likeness of his death, we shall certainly also be of the resurrection;

[6] knowing this, that our old man was crucified with him, that the body of sin might be abolished, so that we should no longer be slaves to sin;

[7] for he who has died has been justified from sin.

[8] But if we died with ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, we believe that we shall also live with him;

[9] knowing that ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, having been raised from the dead, dies no more; death is lord over him no more.

[10] For what he died, he died to sin once for all; but what he lives, he lives to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[11] So you also, consider yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but living to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧.

[12] Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body, so that you obey its desires,

[13] nor present your members to sin as weapons of unrighteousness, but present yourselves to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ as living from the dead, and your members to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ as weapons of righteousness.

[14] For sin will not be lord over you, for you are not under ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ but under grace.

[15] What then? Shall we sin, because we are not under ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ but under grace? May it not be!

[16] Do you not know that to whom you present yourselves as slaves unto obedience, you are slaves to him whom you obey, whether of sin unto death or of obedience unto righteousness?

[17] But thanks be to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ that you were slaves of sin, but you obeyed from the heart the pattern of teaching unto which you were delivered up,

[18] and having been set free from sin, you were enslaved to righteousness.

[19] I speak in a human way because of the weakness of your flesh. For just as you presented your members as slaves to uncleanness and to lawlessness unto lawlessness, so now present your members as slaves to righteousness unto sanctification.

[20] For when you were slaves of sin, you were free in regard to righteousness.

[21] What fruit then did you have at that time in the things of which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death;

[22] but now, having been set free from sin and enslaved to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, you have your fruit unto sanctification, and the end, eternal life.

[23] For **the wages of sin is death, but the gift of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is eternal life in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ our Lord**.

## Changes

- **"Jesus Christ" / "Christ Jesus" → "*Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v3, 11, 23) — systematic; the Greek order is Χριστὸς Ἰησοῦς in all three verses.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"baptized" → "immersed"** (v3, 4) — βαπτίζω *baptizō* = to immerse/dip; "baptize" is a transliteration that has become a technical term for a ritual; "immerse" restores the physical meaning of full submersion.
- **"law" → "Torah"** (v14, 15) — Greek ὑπὸ νόμον, without the article, as Gal 4:21; 5:18.
- **"Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?" → "Shall we remain in sin, that grace may increase?"** (v1) — Greek ἐπιμένωμεν (as Col 1:23); πλεονάσῃ, the verb of 5:20. ἐπιμένωμεν "shall we remain" WH Treg NA28 ] ἐπιμένομεν RP.
- **"God forbid" → "May it not be!"** (v2, 15) — Greek μὴ γένοιτο.
- **"How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein?" → "We who died to sin, how shall we still live in it?"** (v2) — Greek ἀπεθάνομεν, aorist: "died"; ζήσομεν, future; the Greek order is kept.
- **"Know ye not" → "Or are you ignorant"** (v3) — Greek ἢ ἀγνοεῖτε (also 7:1); a different verb from "do you not know" (οὐκ οἴδατε, v16).
- **"by baptism into death" → "through the immersion into death"; "by the glory of the Father" → "through the glory of the Father"** (v4) — Greek διά twice; συνετάφημεν, "buried together" (as Col 2:12). εἰς τὸν θάνατον has the article ("the death", that of v3), as "death" and "sin" have it through most of the chapter.
- **"planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall be also in the likeness of his resurrection" → "grown together with the likeness of his death, we shall certainly also be of the resurrection"** (v5) — Greek σύμφυτοι, "grown together" (of things that grow as one), with γεγόναμεν, "have become". τῷ ὁμοιώματι is "with the likeness" or "(with him) in the likeness". The second half has only ἀλλὰ καὶ τῆς ἀναστάσεως ἐσόμεθα: "in the likeness" and "his" are KJV supplies; the genitive may depend on an understood "likeness" or on "grown together".
- **"our old man is crucified with him" → "our old man was crucified with him"** (v6) — Greek συνεσταυρώθη, aorist, "was crucified together"; "him" is supplied (as Gal 2:20, where the name stands). ὁ παλαιὸς ἡμῶν ἄνθρωπος is "our old man" (as Col 3:9; Eph 4:22), not "self".
- **"might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin" → "might be abolished, so that we should no longer be slaves to sin"** (v6) — Greek καταργηθῇ (as Gal 3:17; 5:11); δουλεύειν, "be a slave to".
- **"he that is dead is freed from sin" → "he who has died has been justified from sin"** (v7) — Greek δεδικαίωται, the verb "justify" (5:1, 9); not the verb "set free" (ἐλευθερόω, v18, 22).
- **"Now if we be dead with Christ" → "But if we died with the *Mashiach*"** (v8) — Greek ἀπεθάνομεν, aorist.
- **"dieth no more; death hath no more dominion over him" → "dies no more; death is lord over him no more"** (v9, 14) — Greek οὐκέτι ἀποθνῄσκει, present; κυριεύει, from κύριος "lord", kept apart from "reign" (βασιλεύω, v12; 5:14–21).
- **"in that he died, he died unto sin once: but in that he liveth, he liveth unto God" → "what he died, he died to sin once for all; but what he lives, he lives to *Elohim*"** (v10) — Greek ὃ ἀπέθανεν … ὃ δὲ ζῇ, "that which he died / lives" (the construction of Gal 2:20); ἐφάπαξ is "once for all". The Greek has no "the death" and no "the life".
- **"reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord" → "consider yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but living to *Elohim* in the *Mashiach* *Yeshua*"** (v11) — Greek λογίζεσθε, here of a judgment; ζῶντας, a participle; ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ, "in". Ἰησοῦ WH Treg NA28 ] + τῷ κυρίῳ ἡμῶν "our Lord" RP. εἶναι "to be" WH Treg NA28 RP ] omitted NIV.
- **"that ye should obey it in the lusts thereof" → "so that you obey its desires"** (v12) — Greek εἰς τὸ ὑπακούειν ταῖς ἐπιθυμίαις αὐτοῦ: αὐτοῦ is the body's (neuter), not sin's. ὑπακούειν WH Treg NA28 ] + αὐτῇ ἐν "it in" RP.
- **"Neither yield ye your members as instruments of unrighteousness unto sin: but yield yourselves unto God, as those that are alive from the dead" → "nor present your members to sin as weapons of unrighteousness, but present yourselves to *Elohim* as living from the dead"** (v13) — Greek παρίστημι, "present" (also v16, 19; Col 1:22), first a present tense, then an aorist; ὅπλα is "weapons" (as 13:12; 2 Cor 6:7; 10:4); ὡσεὶ ἐκ νεκρῶν ζῶντας, "as if living from the dead": the Greek has no "brought … to life".
- **"shall not have dominion over you" → "will not be lord over you"** (v14) — see v9.
- **"to whom ye yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants ye are to whom ye obey" → "to whom you present yourselves as slaves unto obedience, you are slaves to him whom you obey"** (v16) — Greek δούλους εἰς ὑπακοήν; δοῦλος is "slave" throughout (v16–20). The Greek has no "which leads to": εἰς θάνατον, εἰς δικαιοσύνην are "unto death", "unto righteousness".
- **"God be thanked, that ye were the servants of sin, but ye have obeyed from the heart that form of doctrine which was delivered you" → "thanks be to *Elohim* that you were slaves of sin, but you obeyed from the heart the pattern of teaching unto which you were delivered up"** (v17) — Greek χάρις τῷ θεῷ (as 2 Cor 8:16); the Greek has no "once" and no "though". εἰς ὃν παρεδόθητε τύπον διδαχῆς: it is the hearers who were delivered up to the pattern, not the teaching to them; παραδίδωμι is the verb of 1:24 ("delivered up"), τύπος "pattern" as 5:14, διδαχή "teaching".
- **"Being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness" → "having been set free from sin, you were enslaved to righteousness"** (v18) — Greek ἐλευθερωθέντες (as Gal 5:1), ἐδουλώθητε, passive (as Gal 4:3); also v22.
- **"I speak after the manner of men because of the infirmity of your flesh" → "I speak in a human way because of the weakness of your flesh"** (v19) — Greek ἀνθρώπινον λέγω, "I say a human thing"; τὴν ἀσθένειαν τῆς σαρκὸς ὑμῶν: "flesh" is in the Greek.
- **"servants to uncleanness and to iniquity unto iniquity … servants to righteousness unto holiness" → "slaves to uncleanness and to lawlessness unto lawlessness … slaves to righteousness unto sanctification"** (v19) — Greek δοῦλα, an adjective, "enslaved"; ἀνομία "lawlessness" twice, the second with the article; the Greek has no "more"; ἁγιασμός "sanctification" (also v22).
- **"ye were free from righteousness" → "you were free in regard to righteousness"** (v20) — Greek ἐλεύθεροι ἦτε τῇ δικαιοσύνῃ, a dative.
- **"What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed?" → "What fruit then did you have at that time in the things of which you are now ashamed?"** (v21) — Greek ἐφʼ οἷς νῦν ἐπαισχύνεσθε. The printed text puts the question mark after "ashamed"; it can also stand after "at that time" ("What fruit then did you have at that time? Things of which you are now ashamed").
- **"being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life" → "having been set free from sin and enslaved to *Elohim*, you have your fruit unto sanctification, and the end, eternal life"** (v22) — Greek δουλωθέντες, passive; ζωὴν αἰώνιον is an accusative, a second object of "you have".
- **"the gift of God" kept** (v23) — Greek χάρισμα, a gift of grace (χάρις), the word of 5:15–16; "free" is not in the Greek. ὀψώνια is "wages", a soldier's pay, a plural; the Greek has no verb in either half ("is" is supplied twice).
