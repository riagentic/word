# Romans 7

[1] Or are you ignorant, brothers — for I speak to those who know law — that the law is lord over a man for as long a time as he lives?

[2] For the married woman is bound by law to the living husband; but if the husband dies, she is discharged from the law of the husband.

[3] So then, while the husband lives, she will be called an adulteress if she becomes another man's; but if the husband dies, she is free from the law, so that she is not an adulteress, having become another man's.

[4] Therefore, my brothers, you also were put to death to the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ through the body of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, that you might become another's, his who was raised from the dead, that we might bear fruit to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[5] For when we were in the flesh, the passions of sins, which were through the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧, were working in our members so as to bear fruit to death;

[6] but now we were discharged from the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧, having died to that in which we were held, so that we are slaves in newness of Spirit and not in oldness of letter.

[7] What then shall we say? Is the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ sin? May it not be! But I did not come to know sin except through ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧; for I would not have known desire if the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ had not said, "**You shall not desire**."

[8] But sin, taking occasion through the commandment, worked out in me every desire; for apart from ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ sin is dead.

[9] And I was once alive apart from ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧; but when the commandment came, sin came to life again, and I died;

[10] and the commandment that was unto life, this was found for me to be unto death;

[11] for sin, taking occasion through the commandment, utterly beguiled me and through it killed me.

[12] So then the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and righteous and good.

[13] Did the good, then, become death to me? May it not be! But sin, that it might appear as sin, working out death for me through the good — that sin might become exceedingly sinful through the commandment.

[14] For we know that the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ is spiritual; but I am of flesh, sold under sin.

[15] For what I work out, I do not know; for not what I want, this I practice, but what I hate, this I do.

[16] But if what I do not want, this I do, I agree with the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ that it is good.

[17] But now it is no longer I who work it out, but the sin dwelling in me.

[18] For I know that good does not dwell in me, that is, in my flesh; for to want is present with me, but to work out the good is not;

[19] for I do not do the good that I want, but the evil that I do not want, this I practice.

[20] But if what I do not want, this I do, it is no longer I who work it out, but the sin dwelling in me.

[21] I find then the law, for me who want to do the good, that the evil is present with me;

[22] for I delight in the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ according to the inner man,

[23] but I see a different law in my members, warring against the law of my mind and taking me captive in the law of sin which is in my members.

[24] Wretched man that I am! Who will rescue me out of the body of this death?

[25] But thanks be to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ through ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ our Lord. So then I myself with the mind am a slave to the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, but with the flesh to the law of sin.

## Changes

- **"Jesus Christ" → "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (v25) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v4, 22, 25) — systematic.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v4) — systematic.
- **"law" → "Torah"** (v4–9, 12, 14, 16, 22, 25) — Greek νόμος, one word throughout the chapter. It is rendered "Torah" where the law of *Moshe* is meant (v4–9, 12, 14, 16, 22, 25); in v1–3 (the marriage illustration, with "the law of the husband", v2) and in v21, 23, 25b ("a different law", "the law of my mind", "the law of sin") it is left as "law". "The" stands where the Greek has the article; νόμον (v1), νόμῳ (v2), διὰ νόμου (v7), χωρὶς νόμου (v8, 9) have none. In v25 νόμῳ θεοῦ and νόμῳ ἁμαρτίας have no article either; English needs "the".
- **"Know ye not" → "Or are you ignorant"; "the law hath dominion over a man as long as he liveth" → "the law is lord over a man for as long a time as he lives"** (v1) — Greek ἢ ἀγνοεῖτε (as 6:3); κυριεύει (as 6:9, 14); τοῦ ἀνθρώπου, "the man", a human being; ἐφʼ ὅσον χρόνον (as Gal 4:1). The Greek has no "only".
- **"the woman which hath an husband is bound by the law to her husband so long as he liveth … she is loosed from the law of her husband" → "the married woman is bound by law to the living husband … she is discharged from the law of the husband"** (v2) — Greek ἡ ὕπανδρος γυνή, "the woman under a husband"; τῷ ζῶντι ἀνδρί; the Greek has no "her" and no "of marriage". κατήργηται ἀπό (also v6) is καταργέω, "abolish, make of no effect" (6:6), here with ἀπό, "away from" (as Gal 5:4): "abolish" cannot be said of a person in English, so "discharged".
- **"if … she be married to another man … though she be married to another man" → "if she becomes another man's … having become another man's"** (v3) — Greek ἐὰν γένηται ἀνδρὶ ἑτέρῳ, γενομένην ἀνδρὶ ἑτέρῳ: "come to belong to another man"; the Greek has no "lives with" and no "marries". χρηματίσει is "will bear the name". τοῦ μὴ εἶναι is "so that she is not".
- **"ye also are become dead to the law … that ye should be married to another, even to him who is raised from the dead" → "you also were put to death to the *Torah* … that you might become another's, his who was raised from the dead"** (v4) — Greek ἐθανατώθητε, passive: "were put to death"; γενέσθαι ὑμᾶς ἑτέρῳ, the wording of v3; ἐγερθέντι, aorist. "Bear fruit to *Elohim*" is a dative (also "to death", v5).
- **"the motions of sins, which were by the law, did work in our members to bring forth fruit unto death" → "the passions of sins, which were through the *Torah*, were working in our members so as to bear fruit to death"** (v5) — Greek τὰ παθήματα τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν τὰ διὰ τοῦ νόμου: πάθημα is "suffering" elsewhere (8:18) and "passion" here and in Gal 5:24; "of sins" is a plain genitive, not "sinful"; the Greek has no "aroused" and no "living". ἐνηργεῖτο, "were at work".
- **"we are delivered from the law, that being dead wherein we were held; that we should serve in newness of spirit, and not in the oldness of the letter" → "we were discharged from the *Torah*, having died to that in which we were held, so that we are slaves in newness of Spirit and not in oldness of letter"** (v6) — Greek κατηργήθημεν, aorist (see v2); ἀποθανόντες ἐν ᾧ κατειχόμεθα, "having died (to that) in which we were held": "to that" is supplied. δουλεύειν, "be slaves"; καινότητι πνεύματος, παλαιότητι γράμματος, without articles: "spirit" can also be written with a small letter; γράμμα is "letter" (as 2:27, 29; 2 Cor 3:6), with no "written code" and no "way".
- **"God forbid" → "May it not be!"** (v7, 13) — Greek μὴ γένοιτο.
- **"I had not known sin, but by the law: for I had not known lust, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet" → "I did not come to know sin except through *Torah*; for I would not have known desire if the *Torah* had not said, 'You shall not desire'"** (v7) — Greek ἔγνων (γινώσκω), then ᾔδειν (οἶδα): two verbs of knowing. ἐπιθυμία "desire" and ἐπιθυμήσεις "you shall desire" are one root, kept visible; the command is that of Ex 20:17; Deut 5:21, where the sense is "covet". The first clause can also be read "I would not have known".
- **"taking occasion by the commandment, wrought in me all manner of concupiscence. For without the law sin was dead" → "taking occasion through the commandment, worked out in me every desire; for apart from *Torah* sin is dead"** (v8) — Greek ἀφορμὴν λαβοῦσα (as 2 Cor 5:12; Gal 5:13); κατειργάσατο; πᾶσαν ἐπιθυμίαν. The last clause has no verb ("is" is supplied). "Through the commandment" can go with "taking occasion" or with "worked out" (also v11).
- **"sin revived, and I died" → "sin came to life again, and I died"** (v9) — Greek ἀνέζησεν, "lived again". The critical editions count "and I died" as the beginning of v10; the verse numbers here are those of the KJV.
- **"the commandment, which was ordained to life, I found to be unto death" → "the commandment that was unto life, this was found for me to be unto death"** (v10) — Greek ἡ ἐντολὴ ἡ εἰς ζωήν, without a verb: "the commandment, the one unto life"; εὑρέθη μοι, passive. The Greek has no "promised".
- **"deceived me, and by it slew me" → "utterly beguiled me and through it killed me"** (v11) — Greek ἐξηπάτησεν (as 2 Cor 11:3), stronger than "deceive" (πλανάω).
- **"Wherefore the law is holy" → "So then the *Torah* indeed is holy"** (v12) — Greek ὥστε ὁ μὲν νόμος ἅγιος: μέν, "indeed", expects a "but" that does not follow. The verse has no verb.
- **"Was then that which is good made death unto me? … But sin, that it might appear sin, working death in me by that which is good" → "Did the good, then, become death to me? … But sin, that it might appear as sin, working out death for me through the good"** (v13) — Greek τὸ ἀγαθόν … ἐγένετο θάνατος; the answer has no finite verb ("It was" is not in the Greek). μοι is "for me, to me". καθʼ ὑπερβολήν is "exceedingly" (as 2 Cor 1:8; Gal 1:13). ἐγένετο WH Treg NA28 ] γέγονεν RP.
- **"I am carnal" → "I am of flesh"** (v14) — Greek σάρκινος, "made of flesh" (as 2 Cor 3:3; 1 Cor 3:1) WH Treg NA28 ] σαρκικός "fleshly" RP.
- **"that which I do I allow not: for what I would, that do I not; but what I hate, that do I" → "what I work out, I do not know; for not what I want, this I practice, but what I hate, this I do"** (v15) — three verbs of doing, kept apart through v15–21: κατεργάζομαι "work out" (v8, 13, 15, 17, 18, 20), πράσσω "practice" (v15, 19), ποιέω "do" (v15, 16, 19, 20, 21). γινώσκω is "know"; "understand" and "approve" are interpretations. θέλω is "want".
- **"I consent unto the law that it is good" → "I agree with the *Torah* that it is good"** (v16) — Greek σύμφημι, "say together with"; καλός, "good, fine". καλός (v16, 18, 21) and ἀγαθός (v12, 13, 18, 19) are both "good".
- **"it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me" → "it is no longer I who work it out, but the sin dwelling in me"** (v17, 20) — Greek κατεργάζομαι; ἡ οἰκοῦσα ἐν ἐμοὶ ἁμαρτία. v17 οἰκοῦσα Treg NA28 RP ] ἐνοικοῦσα WH.
- **"in me (that is, in my flesh,) dwelleth no good thing: for to will is present with me; but how to perform that which is good I find not" → "good does not dwell in me, that is, in my flesh; for to want is present with me, but to work out the good is not"** (v18) — Greek οὐκ οἰκεῖ … ἀγαθόν; τὸ θέλειν παράκειταί μοι, "lies beside me" (also v21); the Greek has no "desire" as a noun and no "ability". οὔ "not" WH Treg NA28 ] οὐχ εὑρίσκω "I do not find" RP, which the KJV follows.
- **"the good that I would I do not: but the evil which I would not, that I do" → "I do not do the good that I want, but the evil that I do not want, this I practice"** (v19) — Greek ποιῶ, then πράσσω; κακόν "evil, bad". The Greek has no "keep on".
- **"if I do that I would not" kept in sense** (v20) — θέλω WH Treg ] + ἐγώ "I" NA28 RP; no difference in English.
- **"I find then a law, that, when I would do good, evil is present with me" → "I find then the law, for me who want to do the good, that the evil is present with me"** (v21) — Greek εὑρίσκω ἄρα τὸν νόμον τῷ θέλοντι ἐμοὶ ποιεῖν τὸ καλὸν ὅτι ἐμοὶ τὸ κακὸν παράκειται: τὸν νόμον has the article ("this law", the rule that follows, or the *Torah*); "me" stands twice.
- **"I delight in the law of God after the inward man" → "I delight in the *Torah* of *Elohim* according to the inner man"** (v22) — Greek συνήδομαι, "rejoice together with"; κατὰ τὸν ἔσω ἄνθρωπον (as Eph 3:16; 2 Cor 4:16), not "inner being".
- **"another law … bringing me into captivity to the law of sin" → "a different law … taking me captive in the law of sin"** (v23) — Greek ἕτερον νόμον (ἕτερος as Gal 1:6; "another" in v3–4); ἀντιστρατευόμενον, "warring against"; αἰχμαλωτίζοντα (as 2 Cor 10:5); ἐν τῷ νόμῳ, "in" or "by". ἐν WH Treg NA28 ] omitted RP. The Greek has τῷ ὄντι, "which is", not "dwells".
- **"who shall deliver me from the body of this death?" → "Who will rescue me out of the body of this death?"** (v24) — Greek ῥύσεται; ἐκ τοῦ σώματος τοῦ θανάτου τούτου: "this" can go with "death" or with "body" ("this body of death").
- **"I thank God" → "But thanks be to *Elohim*"** (v25) — the verse follows χάρις δέ (WH NA28): χάρις Treg ] χάρις δὲ WH NA28; εὐχαριστῶ "I thank" RP. SBLGNT prints χάρις without "but".
- **"with the mind I myself serve the law of God; but with the flesh the law of sin" → "I myself with the mind am a slave to the *Torah* of *Elohim*, but with the flesh to the law of sin"** (v25) — Greek δουλεύω, once; the Greek has no second "I serve".

### Notes
- **v7** quotes Ex 20:17; Deut 5:21.
