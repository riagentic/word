# Romans 14

[1] But welcome the one who is weak in the faith, not unto discernings of reasonings.

[2] One has faith to eat all things, but the one who is weak eats vegetables.

[3] Let not the one who eats despise the one who does not eat, and let not the one who does not eat judge the one who eats, for ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has welcomed him.

[4] Who are you, the one judging another's household slave? To his own lord he stands or falls. And he will be made to stand, for the Lord is powerful to make him stand.

[5] For one judges a day above a day, and another judges every day. Let each be fully convinced in his own mind.

[6] The one who minds the day minds it to the Lord. And the one who eats, eats to the Lord, for he gives thanks to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧; and the one who does not eat, to the Lord he does not eat, and he gives thanks to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[7] For none of us lives to himself, and none dies to himself;

[8] for whether we live, we live to the Lord, or whether we die, we die to the Lord. Whether therefore we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's.

[9] For unto this ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ died and lived, that he might be lord over both dead and living.

[10] But you, why do you judge your brother? Or you also, why do you despise your brother? For we shall all stand before the judgment seat of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[11] for it is written:

"**As I live, says ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧, to me every knee shall bow,
and every tongue shall confess to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.**"

[12] So then each of us will give account concerning himself to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[13] Let us therefore no longer judge one another; but judge this rather: not to put a cause of stumbling before the brother, or a stumbling block.

[14] I know and am persuaded in the Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ that nothing is common through itself; except to the one who considers anything to be common — to that one it is common.

[15] For if because of food your brother is grieved, you are no longer walking according to love. Do not by your food destroy that one for whom ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ died.

[16] Let not then your good be blasphemed.

[17] For the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is not eating and drinking, but righteousness and peace and joy in the ⟦ruach-haqodesh|Ruach HaQodesh|Holy Spirit⟧;

[18] for the one who in this is a slave to ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ is well-pleasing to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and approved by men.

[19] So then let us pursue the things of peace and the things of the building up that is for one another.

[20] Do not for the sake of food tear down the work of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧. All things indeed are clean, but it is evil for the man who eats with stumbling.

[21] It is good not to eat meat nor to drink wine nor anything in which your brother stumbles;

[22] you, the faith that you have, have by yourself before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧. Blessed is the one who does not judge himself in what he approves;

[23] but the one who doubts, if he eats, has been condemned, because it is not from faith; and everything that is not from faith is sin.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" / "Christ" → "*Yeshua*" / "*Mashiach*"** (v9, 14, 15, 18) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v3, 6, 10, 12, 17, 18, 20, 22) — systematic.
- **"receive ye, but not to doubtful disputations" → "welcome … not unto discernings of reasonings"** (v1) — Greek προσλαμβάνεσθε (also v3); μὴ εἰς διακρίσεις διαλογισμῶν, literally "not unto discernings (or: judgings, disputes) of reasonings (or: thoughts)"; διάκρισις is from the root of "judge" (κρίνω), which runs through the chapter. The Greek has no verb here and does not say whose reasonings.
- **"believeth that he may eat" → "has faith to eat"** (v2) — Greek πιστεύει φαγεῖν πάντα, the verb of "faith" (v1, 22, 23). "Only" is not in the Greek.
- **"eateth not" kept: "does not eat"** (v3, 6) — Greek ὁ μὴ ἐσθίων; not "abstains".
- **"another man's servant … master" → "another's household slave … lord"** (v4) — Greek οἰκέτης; κύριος, the same word as "the Lord" at the end of the verse.
- **"shall be holden up: for God is able" → "will be made to stand, for the Lord is powerful"** (v4) — σταθήσεται (passive of "stand"; also simply "will stand"), δυνατεῖ. "The Lord" follows ὁ κύριος (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28); KJV "God" follows θεός (RP).
- **"esteemeth" → "judges"** (v5) — Greek κρίνει, the verb "judge" of v3, 4, 10, 13: "judges a day above a day (ἡμέραν παρ' ἡμέραν) … judges every day". "Alike" is not in the Greek. "For" follows the reading + γάρ (WH, NA28); SBLGNT, Treg and RP have no γάρ.
- **"regardeth" → "minds"** (v6) — Greek φρονέω, "think, set the mind on". The datives are bare: "to the Lord" (κυρίῳ), not "in honor of". KJV's "and he that regardeth not the day, to the Lord he doth not regard it" (RP) is not in SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28.
- **"both died, and rose, and revived" → "died and lived"** (v9) — Greek ἀπέθανεν καὶ ἔζησεν (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28); RP adds καὶ ἀνέστη. "Be lord over" is the verb κυριεύσῃ.
- **"judgment seat of Christ" → "judgment seat of *Elohim*"** (v10) — Greek θεοῦ (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28); KJV follows χριστοῦ (RP).
- **"As I live, saith the Lord, every knee shall bow to me, and every tongue shall confess to God" → "As I live, says the Lord, to me every knee shall bow, and every tongue shall confess to *Elohim*"** (v11) — OT quotation from Isa 45:23, translated from Paul's Greek. Literally "I live, says the Lord, that to me …" (Ζῶ ἐγώ … ὅτι), an oath formula. ἐξομολογήσεται τῷ θεῷ is "confess to" or "give praise to".
- **"shall give account of himself to God" kept** (v12) — the verse follows ἄρα οὖν … δώσει τῷ θεῷ (WH, NA28, RP); the printed SBLGNT has no οὖν and no τῷ θεῷ, "to God".
- **"judge this rather"** (v13) — Greek κρίνατε, the same verb as "let us judge" in the first clause; plural imperative. πρόσκομμα "cause of stumbling" (the root of "stumbles", v21; also v20), σκάνδαλον "stumbling block" (the noun of σκανδαλίζω "make stumble"): two different Greek words.
- **"unclean" → "common"** (v14) — Greek κοινός *koinos*, "common", that is, profane, not set apart; "unclean" is a different Greek word (ἀκάθαρτος). "Through itself" follows δι' ἑαυτοῦ (SBLGNT, WH, NA28); Treg and RP read δι' αὐτοῦ, "through itself" or "through him".
- **"with thy meat" → "by your food"; "charitably" → "according to love"** (v15) — Greek βρῶμα, κατὰ ἀγάπην. "That one" is ἐκεῖνον.
- **"evil spoken of" → "blasphemed"** (v16) — Greek βλασφημείσθω; "your" is plural (ὑμῶν).
- **"meat and drink" → "eating and drinking"** (v17) — Greek βρῶσις καὶ πόσις, the acts; the Greek has no "a matter of".
- **"Holy Ghost" → "*Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v17) — systematic; the Greek has no article (ἐν πνεύματι ἁγίῳ).
- **"in these things serveth" → "in this is a slave to"** (v18) — Greek ἐν τούτῳ (singular; RP τούτοις) δουλεύων.
- **"edify" → "building up"** (v19) — οἰκοδομή *oikodomē*; literally "the things of the building up that is unto one another".
- **"destroy" → "tear down"** (v20) — Greek κατάλυε, the opposite of "building up" in v19; a different word from "destroy" (ἀπόλλυε) in v15.
- **"who eateth with offence" → "who eats with stumbling"** (v20) — Greek διὰ προσκόμματος. It is open whether the eater himself stumbles or his eating makes another stumble.
- **"stumbleth, or is offended, or is made weak" → "stumbles"** (v21) — the verse follows the text without ἢ σκανδαλίζεται ἢ ἀσθενεῖ (WH, NA28); SBLGNT, Treg and RP have the words, "or is offended or is weak". The Greek has no verb with "anything in which".
- **"Hast thou faith? have it to thyself" → "you, the faith that you have, have by yourself"** (v22) — Greek σὺ πίστιν ἣν ἔχεις κατὰ σεαυτὸν ἔχε; ἥν "that" (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28), absent in RP, which gives the question.
- **"condemneth not" → "does not judge"** (v22) — Greek κρίνων, as in the rest of the chapter.
- **"damned" → "has been condemned"** (v23) — κατακέκριται, perfect. "The eating" is not in the Greek: "because [it is] not from faith".

### Notes
- **v11** quotes Isa 45:23.
- **v23** — RP places the doxology (our 16:25–27) after this verse; our text has it at 16:25–27.
