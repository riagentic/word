# Psalm 11

To the overseer. Of David.

[1] In ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ I have taken refuge;
how do you say to my soul,
"Flee, bird, to your mountain;

[2] for behold, the wicked tread the bow;
they have set their arrow on the string,
to shoot in gloom at the upright of heart.

[3] For the foundations are torn down;
the righteous — what has he wrought?"

[4] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ is in his holy temple;
⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ — in the heavens is his throne;
his eyes behold,
his eyelids try the sons of man.

[5] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ tries the righteous;
and the wicked and the one who loves violence, his soul hates.

[6] Let him cause traps to rain on the wicked;
fire and sulfur and a wind of scorchings are the portion of their cup.

[7] For righteous is ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧; righteous deeds he loves;
his face beholds the upright.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David" → "To the overseer. Of David."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ לְדָוִד: "To the overseer" (נִצַּח "oversee", 1 Chr 15:21; traditionally "the chief musician"); "A psalm" (מִזְמוֹר) is not in this heading. לְדָוִד "of David": לְ can say "by", "for" or "belonging to". The heading and v1 are one verse in the Hebrew; the numbering is the same.
- **"In the LORD put I my trust: how say ye to my soul, Flee as a bird to your mountain?" → "In Yahweh I have taken refuge; how do you say to my soul, 'Flee, bird, to your mountain'"** (v1) — Hebrew חָסִיתִי, perfect of חָסָה "take refuge". "Flee" is the qere נוּדִי, feminine singular, spoken to the soul (נֶפֶשׁ is feminine), and צִפּוֹר "bird" (feminine) stands beside it without "as": the soul is called a bird. The ketiv has נודו, plural ("flee, you"). הַרְכֶם "your mountain" with a plural "your". Where the words of "you" end (v1, 2 or 3) is not marked; they are taken to end with v3.
- **"the wicked bend their bow, they make ready their arrow upon the string, that they may privily shoot at the upright in heart" → "the wicked tread the bow; they have set their arrow on the string, to shoot in gloom at the upright of heart"** (v2) — Hebrew הִנֵּה "behold"; יִדְרְכוּן קֶשֶׁת "they tread the bow" (it is strung with the foot; "their" is not written); כּוֹנְנוּ "they have set (made firm)"; יֶתֶר "string"; בְּמוֹ־אֹפֶל "in gloom" (unseen); יִשְׁרֵי־לֵב "the upright of heart".
- **"If the foundations be destroyed, what can the righteous do?" → "For the foundations are torn down; the righteous — what has he wrought?"** (v3) — Hebrew כִּי "for" (as v2; also "when, if"); הַשָּׁתוֹת "the foundations" (only here and Isa 19:10; uncertain); יֵהָרֵסוּן "are torn down" (הָרַס); צַדִּיק מַה־פָּעָל "a righteous one, what has he wrought?" — the perfect: what has he done (to deserve it), or what can he do; both readings in use.
- **"the LORD's throne is in heaven: his eyes behold, his eyelids try, the children of men" → "Yahweh — in the heavens is his throne; his eyes behold, his eyelids try the sons of man"** (v4) — Hebrew יְהוָה בַּשָּׁמַיִם כִּסְאוֹ "Yahweh, in the heavens his throne" (Yahweh is named first); שָׁמַיִם "the heavens"; חָזָה "behold"; בָּחַן "try" (also v5; נִסָּה is "test"); בְּנֵי אָדָם "the sons of man". The accents divide the verse after "his throne" and after "his eyes behold": the lines are set so.
- **"but the wicked and him that loveth violence his soul hateth" → "and the wicked and the one who loves violence his soul hates"** (v5) — Hebrew וְ "and"; the Hebrew order kept (the object first); חָמָס "violence".
- **"Upon the wicked he shall rain snares, fire and brimstone, and an horrible tempest: this shall be the portion of their cup" → "Let him cause traps to rain on the wicked; fire and sulfur and a wind of scorchings are the portion of their cup"** (v6) — Hebrew יַמְטֵר, the short form (jussive) of הִמְטִיר "cause to rain": a wish. פַּחִים "traps" (the fowler's trap, פַּח) as the text has it; the reading פַּחֲמֵי "coals of" is a change of the letters and is not followed (from memory). The accents set "traps" in the first half, fire and sulfur in the second. גָּפְרִית "sulfur"; רוּחַ זִלְעָפוֹת "a wind of scorchings" (burning heat); מְנָת "portion". "this shall be" is not written.
- **"For the righteous LORD loveth righteousness; his countenance doth behold the upright" → "For righteous is Yahweh; righteous deeds he loves; his face beholds the upright"** (v7) — Hebrew כִּי־צַדִּיק יְהוָה "for righteous is Yahweh"; צְדָקוֹת, plural of צְדָקָה "righteousness": "righteous deeds"; יָשָׁר יֶחֱזוּ פָנֵימוֹ: the plural verb agrees with פָּנִים "face", which is the subject, as "his eyes behold" in v4 (KJV); פָנֵימוֹ, with the poetic suffix ־מוֹ, here "his". Many read "the upright shall behold his face" (יָשָׁר as subject with a plural verb); both readings are possible; the Hebrew order is "upright — they behold — his face".

### Notes
- **Refuge** — the psalm answers the counsel to flee (v1–3) with Yahweh on his throne, who tries and sees (v4–7).
