# Psalm 109

To the overseer. Of David. A psalm.

[1] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of my praise, do not be silent.

[2] For the mouth of the wicked and the mouth of deceit they have opened against me;
they have spoken with me with a tongue of falsehood.

[3] And with words of hatred they have surrounded me,
and they fought against me without cause.

[4] For my love they are adversaries to me,
and I am prayer.

[5] And they have laid on me evil for good,
and hatred for my love.

[6] Appoint over him a wicked one,
and let an adversary stand at his right hand.

[7] When he is judged, let him go out wicked,
and let his prayer become sin.

[8] Let his days be few;
let another take his oversight.

[9] Let his sons be fatherless,
and his wife a widow.

[10] And let his sons surely roam and beg,
and let them seek out of their ruins.

[11] Let the creditor ensnare all that he has,
and let strangers plunder his labor.

[12] Let there be none for him who draws out ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧,
and let there be none who shows favor to his fatherless ones.

[13] Let his posterity be for cutting off;
in another generation let their name be blotted out.

[14] Let the iniquity of his fathers be remembered to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧,
and the sin of his mother, let it not be blotted out.

[15] Let them be before ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ continually,
and let him cut off their remembrance from the earth,

[16] because he did not remember to do ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧,
and he pursued an afflicted and needy man,
and the stricken of heart, to put to death.

[17] And he loved a curse, and it came to him;
and he did not delight in blessing, and it was far from him.

[18] And he put on a curse like his clothing,
and it came like water into his inward part,
and like oil into his bones.

[19] Let it be to him like a garment he wraps himself in,
and for a belt he girds on continually.

[20] This is the wage of my adversaries from ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧,
and of those who speak evil against my soul.

[21] And you, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|GOD⟧ ⟦adonai|Adonai|my Lord⟧, deal with me on account of your name;
because your ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ is good, deliver me.

[22] For afflicted and needy am I,
and my heart is pierced within me.

[23] Like a shadow as it stretches out I am gone;
I am shaken off like locusts.

[24] My knees have stumbled from fasting,
and my flesh has grown lean, without fatness.

[25] And I, I have become a reproach to them;
they see me, they shake their head.

[26] Help me, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧ my ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;
save me according to your ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧,

[27] and let them know that this is your hand;
you, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, have done it.

[28] Let them curse, and you, you will bless;
they have risen, and they are put to shame, and your servant shall be glad.

[29] Let my adversaries put on dishonor,
and let them wrap themselves in their shame as in a robe.

[30] I will give thanks to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ greatly with my mouth,
and in the midst of many I will praise him.

[31] For he stands at the right hand of the needy,
to save from those who judge his soul.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 26) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v14, 15, 20, 26, 27, 30) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"GOD the Lord" → "Yahweh Adonai"** (v21) — Hebrew יְהוִה אֲדֹנָי, in the MT order: the name יהוה pointed to be read *Elohim* beside אֲדֹנָי.
- **No "O"** (v1, 21, 26, 27) — the Hebrew vocatives have none.
- **Heading "To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David" → "To the overseer. Of David. A psalm."** — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ לְדָוִד מִזְמוֹר, in this order. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer", from the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21 (traditional "to the chief musician"). לְדָוִד: the לְ may mean "by", "for" or "belonging to" David; "Of David" leaves it open.
- **"Hold not thy peace, O God of my praise" → "Elohim of my praise, do not be silent"** (v1) — Hebrew אֱלֹהֵי תְהִלָּתִי אַל־תֶּחֱרַשׁ, in this order; חָרַשׁ "be silent".
- **"the mouth of the wicked and the mouth of the deceitful are opened against me: they have spoken against me with a lying tongue" → "the mouth of the wicked and the mouth of deceit they have opened against me; they have spoken with me with a tongue of falsehood"** (v2) — Hebrew פִי רָשָׁע וּפִי־מִרְמָה עָלַי פָּתָחוּ, active, "they have opened" (the subject is unnamed); מִרְמָה "deceit" (a noun, "the mouth of deceit"). דִּבְּרוּ אִתִּי "they have spoken with me" (אֵת "with"; KJV "against"); לְשׁוֹן שָׁקֶר "a tongue of falsehood".
- **"They compassed me about also with words of hatred; and fought against me without a cause" → "And with words of hatred they have surrounded me, and they fought against me without cause"** (v3) — Hebrew וְדִבְרֵי שִׂנְאָה סְבָבוּנִי, "words of hatred" first; וַיִּלָּחֲמוּנִי, a narrative form (wayyiqtol); חִנָּם "without cause" (lit. "for nothing").
- **"For my love they are my adversaries: but I give myself unto prayer" → "For my love they are adversaries to me, and I am prayer"** (v4) — Hebrew תַּחַת־אַהֲבָתִי יִשְׂטְנוּנִי, "in the place of my love" (תַּחַת, "for" as in v5: in return for); יִשְׂטְנוּנִי, the verb of שָׂטָן "adversary" (as Ps 38:20; the noun in v6, the participle "my adversaries" v20, 29: one root; צַר "adversary" is another word, 112:8). וַאֲנִי תְפִלָּה, "and I — prayer", without a verb; "give myself unto" is not in the Hebrew. It is commonly taken as "I am (all) prayer" or "I pray (for them)".
- **"And they have rewarded me evil for good" → "And they have laid on me evil for good"** (v5) — Hebrew וַיָּשִׂימוּ עָלַי רָעָה, שִׂים עַל "set, lay on"; רָעָה "evil".
- **"Set thou a wicked man over him: and let Satan stand at his right hand" → "Appoint over him a wicked one, and let an adversary stand at his right hand"** (v6) — Hebrew הַפְקֵד עָלָיו רָשָׁע, הִפְקִיד עַל "appoint over"; רָשָׁע "a wicked one" ("man" is not in the Hebrew). וְשָׂטָן without the article: a common noun, "an adversary" (as Num 22:22; 1 Kgs 11:14); Hebrew *satan*, traditional "Satan" (KJV), which the Hebrew does not make a name here. At the right hand stands the accuser in a court (Zech 3:1). Who speaks v6–19 (the psalmist against his enemy, or the enemies' words against him, quoted) the Hebrew does not mark.
- **"When he shall be judged, let him be condemned: and let his prayer become sin" → "When he is judged, let him go out wicked, and let his prayer become sin"** (v7) — Hebrew בְּהִשָּׁפְטוֹ יֵצֵא רָשָׁע, "let him go out (of the court) wicked", i.e. found guilty: רָשָׁע is one word, "wicked" (v2, 6), here as the verdict. תִּהְיֶה לַחֲטָאָה "let it become (be for) sin".
- **"let another take his office" → "let another take his oversight"** (v8) — Hebrew פְּקֻדָּתוֹ, פְּקֻדָּה "oversight" (lit. "a being set over"; as Num 4:16), of the root of הִפְקִיד "appoint" in v6. Acts 1:20 quotes the line from the Greek, τὴν ἐπισκοπὴν αὐτοῦ λαβέτω ἕτερος, "his office of overseer let another take", of Judas; the verse keeps the Hebrew.
- **"Let his children be fatherless" → "Let his sons be fatherless"** (v9, 10) — Hebrew בָנָיו "his sons"; יָתוֹם "fatherless".
- **"Let his children be continually vagabonds, and beg: let them seek their bread also out of their desolate places" → "And let his sons surely roam and beg, and let them seek out of their ruins"** (v10) — Hebrew וְנוֹעַ יָנוּעוּ, literally "and roaming let them roam" (infinitive absolute with its verb, rendered "surely"); נוּעַ "roam" (as Gen 4:12 נָע; in v25 the hifil "shake" of the head: one root). וְשִׁאֵלוּ, piel of שָׁאַל "ask": "beg". וְדָרְשׁוּ מֵחָרְבוֹתֵיהֶם "and let them seek out of their ruins": "bread" (KJV) and "food" are not in the Hebrew; what they seek is not said. The Greek ("let them be cast out of their ruins", reading another verb) from memory; the verse keeps the MT.
- **"Let the extortioner catch all that he hath; and let the strangers spoil his labour" → "Let the creditor ensnare all that he has, and let strangers plunder his labor"** (v11) — Hebrew יְנַקֵּשׁ, piel of נָקַשׁ "lay a snare": "ensnare"; נוֹשֶׁה "creditor" (as Exod 22:25); לְכָל־אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ "all that is his". בָּזַז "plunder"; זָרִים "strangers" (no article); יְגִיעוֹ "his labor" (what he has gained by toil).
- **"Let there be none to extend mercy unto him: neither let there be any to favour his fatherless children" → "Let there be none for him who draws out chesed, and let there be none who shows favor to his fatherless ones"** (v12) — Hebrew אַל־יְהִי־לוֹ מֹשֵׁךְ חָסֶד, "let there not be for him one drawing out chesed" (מָשַׁךְ "draw out, prolong", as Jer 31:3; Ps 36:10). חוֹנֵן, participle of חָנַן "show favor"; לִיתוֹמָיו "to his fatherless" ("children" is not in the Hebrew; "ones" is supplied for English).
- **"Let his posterity be cut off; and in the generation following let their name be blotted out" → "Let his posterity be for cutting off; in another generation let their name be blotted out"** (v13) — Hebrew יְהִי־אַחֲרִיתוֹ לְהַכְרִית, "let his after-part (what comes after him) be for cutting off"; אַחֲרִית "latter end, posterity"; הִכְרִית "cut off". בְּדוֹר אַחֵר "in another generation" (not "second", "following"). יִמַּח, nifal of מָחָה "wipe out"; of a name "blot out" (so also v14).
- **"Let the iniquity of his fathers be remembered with the LORD" → "Let the iniquity of his fathers be remembered to Yahweh"** (v14) — Hebrew אֶל־יְהוָה "to Yahweh". וְחַטַּאת אִמּוֹ אַל־תִּמָּח, "and the sin of his mother" first.
- **"that he may cut off the memory of them from the earth" → "and let him cut off their remembrance from the earth"** (v15) — Hebrew וְיַכְרֵת, a wish ("and let him cut off"), no "that"; זִכְרָם, זֵכֶר "remembrance". The plural "them" (v15) and "their" (v13) after "him": as the Hebrew.
- **"Because that he remembered not to shew mercy, but persecuted the poor and needy man, that he might even slay the broken in heart" → "because he did not remember to do chesed, and he pursued an afflicted and needy man, and the stricken of heart, to put to death"** (v16) — Hebrew יַעַן אֲשֶׁר "because"; עֲשׂוֹת חֶסֶד "to do chesed"; וַיִּרְדֹּף "and he pursued" (רָדַף "pursue"); אִישׁ־עָנִי וְאֶבְיוֹן "an afflicted and needy man" (עָנִי "afflicted", אֶבְיוֹן "needy"); וְנִכְאֵה לֵבָב "and the stricken of heart" (נִכְאֶה "stricken, crushed"); לְמוֹתֵת, polel infinitive, "to put to death", without an object.
- **"As he loved cursing, so let it come unto him: as he delighted not in blessing, so let it be far from him" → "And he loved a curse, and it came to him; and he did not delight in blessing, and it was far from him"** (v17) — Hebrew וַיֶּאֱהַב קְלָלָה וַתְּבוֹאֵהוּ ... וַתִּרְחַק מִמֶּנּוּ: as pointed, narrative forms (wayyiqtol), "and it came", "and it was far": the verse tells what happened, it does not wish it. The wish ("let it come", KJV and the versions) reads the same letters with other vowels (from memory); the verse keeps the MT. קְלָלָה "curse" (the noun of קִלֵּל "curse", v28); חָפֵץ בְּ "delight in"; בְּרָכָה "blessing".
- **"As he clothed himself with cursing like as with his garment, so let it come into his bowels like water, and like oil into his bones" → "And he put on a curse like his clothing, and it came like water into his inward part, and like oil into his bones"** (v18) — Hebrew וַיִּלְבַּשׁ ... וַתָּבֹא, narrative forms as in v17. לָבַשׁ "put on" (as v29). כְּמַדּוֹ, מַד "clothing" (a cloth garment; another word than בֶּגֶד "garment" of v19). בְּקִרְבּוֹ "into his inward part" (קֶרֶב). שֶׁמֶן "oil".
- **"Let it be unto him as the garment which covereth him, and for a girdle wherewith he is girded continually" → "Let it be to him like a garment he wraps himself in, and for a belt he girds on continually"** (v19) — Hebrew תְּהִי־לוֹ, a wish (from here on the forms are wishes again). כְּבֶגֶד יַעְטֶה, בֶּגֶד "garment", עָטָה "wrap oneself" (as v29). וּלְמֵזַח, מֵזַח "belt" (only here; מָזִיחַ Job 12:21); יַחְגְּרֶהָ, חָגַר "gird on"; תָּמִיד "continually".
- **"Let this be the reward of mine adversaries from the LORD" → "This is the wage of my adversaries from Yahweh"** (v20) — Hebrew זֹאת פְּעֻלַּת שֹׂטְנַי, without a verb: "this (is) the wage"; it may also be read as a wish ("let this be"), or as the summing up of what the adversaries say (v6–19). פְּעֻלָּה "work, wage" (what a deed earns). שֹׂטְנַי, participle of שָׂטַן "be an adversary" (v4). וְהַדֹּבְרִים רָע עַל־נַפְשִׁי "and of those who speak evil against my soul".
- **"But do thou for me, O GOD the Lord, for thy name's sake: because thy mercy is good, deliver thou me" → "And you, Yahweh Adonai, deal with me on account of your name; because your chesed is good, deliver me"** (v21) — Hebrew וְאַתָּה "and you" (stressed); עֲשֵׂה־אִתִּי "do with me" ("deal with me"); לְמַעַן שְׁמֶךָ "on account of your name" (as Ps 23:3); כִּי־טוֹב חַסְדְּךָ; הִצִּיל "deliver".
- **"For I am poor and needy, and my heart is wounded within me" → "For afflicted and needy am I, and my heart is pierced within me"** (v22) — Hebrew כִּי־עָנִי וְאֶבְיוֹן אָנֹכִי, in this order (as v16); חָלַל, the root of חָלָל "pierced, slain": "is pierced".
- **"I am gone like the shadow when it declineth: I am tossed up and down as the locust" → "Like a shadow as it stretches out I am gone; I am shaken off like locusts"** (v23) — Hebrew כְּצֵל־כִּנְטוֹתוֹ, "like a shadow when it stretches out" (נָטָה "stretch out": the long shadow of evening; "evening" is not in the Hebrew). נֶהֱלָכְתִּי, nifal of הָלַךְ, "I am made to go, I am gone". נִנְעַרְתִּי, nifal of נָעַר "shake off" (as one shakes locusts from a garment). אַרְבֶּה "locusts" (a collective).
- **"My knees are weak through fasting; and my flesh faileth of fatness" → "My knees have stumbled from fasting, and my flesh has grown lean, without fatness"** (v24) — Hebrew כָּשְׁלוּ "have stumbled" (כָּשַׁל "stumble, totter"); מִצּוֹם "from fasting". וּבְשָׂרִי כָּחַשׁ מִשָּׁמֶן, "and my flesh has grown lean from fatness" (= so that fatness is gone): שֶׁמֶן is "oil" in v18 and "fatness" here (one word, two senses); "lean, without oil" (no anointing in mourning) is also read.
- **"I became also a reproach unto them: when they looked upon me they shaked their heads" → "And I, I have become a reproach to them; they see me, they shake their head"** (v25) — Hebrew וַאֲנִי הָיִיתִי חֶרְפָּה לָהֶם, "I" stressed; חֶרְפָּה "reproach". יִרְאוּנִי יְנִיעוּן רֹאשָׁם, two clauses without "when": "they see me, they shake their head" (singular, as the Hebrew); יְנִיעוּן, hifil of נוּעַ (v10).
- **"Help me, O LORD my God: O save me according to thy mercy" → "Help me, Yahweh my Elohim; save me according to your chesed"** (v26) — Hebrew עָזְרֵנִי ... הוֹשִׁיעֵנִי כְחַסְדֶּךָ; עָזַר "help", הוֹשִׁיעַ "save".
- **"That they may know that this is thy hand; that thou, LORD, hast done it" → "and let them know that this is your hand; you, Yahweh, have done it"** (v27) — Hebrew וְיֵדְעוּ, "and let them know" (a wish joined with "and"; no "that they may"); כִּי־יָדְךָ זֹּאת "that this is your hand"; אַתָּה יְהוָה עֲשִׂיתָהּ, a new clause, "you" stressed.
- **"Let them curse, but bless thou: when they arise, let them be ashamed; but let thy servant rejoice" → "Let them curse, and you, you will bless; they have risen, and they are put to shame, and your servant shall be glad"** (v28) — Hebrew יְקַלְלוּ־הֵמָּה וְאַתָּה תְבָרֵךְ, "let them, they, curse, and you, you will bless" (both pronouns stressed; תְבָרֵךְ an imperfect, or a wish "and may you bless"). קָמוּ וַיֵּבֹשׁוּ, a perfect and a narrative form: "they have risen and are put to shame"; there is no "when", and the forms are not wishes (KJV "let them be ashamed"; a change of the vowels, "may my assailants be put to shame", is proposed, from memory). וְעַבְדְּךָ יִשְׂמָח "and your servant shall be glad" (עֶבֶד, lit. "slave", of the speaker before Yahweh: "servant"; שָׂמַח "be glad").
- **"Let mine adversaries be clothed with shame, and let them cover themselves with their own confusion, as with a mantle" → "Let my adversaries put on dishonor, and let them wrap themselves in their shame as in a robe"** (v29) — Hebrew יִלְבְּשׁוּ שׂוֹטְנַי כְּלִמָּה, לָבַשׁ "put on" (as v18); כְּלִמָּה "dishonor" (as Ps 35:26); וְיַעֲטוּ "and let them wrap themselves" (as v19); בָּשְׁתָּם "their shame" (בֹּשֶׁת; "own" is not in the Hebrew); מְעִיל "robe".
- **"I will greatly praise the LORD with my mouth; yea, I will praise him among the multitude" → "I will give thanks to Yahweh greatly with my mouth, and in the midst of many I will praise him"** (v30) — Hebrew אוֹדֶה, הוֹדָה "give thanks to" (beside הִלֵּל "praise" in the second line: two verbs); וּבְתוֹךְ רַבִּים "and in the midst of many".
- **"For he shall stand at the right hand of the poor, to save him from those that condemn his soul" → "For he stands at the right hand of the needy, to save from those who judge his soul"** (v31) — Hebrew כִּי־יַעֲמֹד לִימִין אֶבְיוֹן, an imperfect ("he stands", or "will stand"): Yahweh stands where the adversary was to stand (v6). אֶבְיוֹן "needy". לְהוֹשִׁיעַ "to save", without an object; מִשֹּׁפְטֵי נַפְשׁוֹ "from the judges of his soul", "those who judge his soul" (שָׁפַט "judge"; not "condemn", and "to death" is not in the Hebrew).

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — the same as ours: the heading is the first words of v1.
- **Poetic structure** — the psalmist's complaint (v1–5), the curses on one man (v6–19; whether they are the psalmist's words or the enemies' words, quoted, is debated), the plea (v20–31).
- **Imprecatory psalm** — severe curses on the enemy.
- **v8 cited Acts 1:20** — applied to Judas.
