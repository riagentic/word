# Psalm 144

Of David.

[1] Blessed is ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, my Rock,
who teaches my hands for battle,
my fingers for war;

[2] my ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ and my stronghold,
my secure height and my rescuer, mine,
my shield, and in him I take refuge,
who subdues my people under me.

[3] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, what is man, that you know him,
the ⟦ben-enosh|ben-enosh|son of man⟧, that you think of him?

[4] ⟦adam|Adam|Man⟧ is like the vapor;
his days are like a passing shadow.

[5] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, bow your heavens and come down;
touch the mountains, and let them smoke.

[6] Flash forth lightning, and scatter them;
send your arrows, and throw them into confusion.

[7] Stretch out your hands from on high;
set me free and deliver me from many waters,
from the hand of foreigners,

[8] whose mouth has spoken emptiness,
and whose right hand is a right hand of falsehood.

[9] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, a new song let me sing to you;
with a harp of ten strings let me make music to you,

[10] who gives salvation to kings,
who sets free David his servant from the evil sword.

[11] Set me free and deliver me
from the hand of foreigners,
whose mouth has spoken emptiness,
and whose right hand is a right hand of falsehood;

[12] that our sons may be like plants
grown great in their youth,
our daughters like corners
cut in the pattern of a temple;

[13] our storehouses full,
furnishing from kind to kind;
our flock bringing forth thousands,
made ten thousands in our open places;

[14] our oxen laden;
no breach and no going out,
and no outcry in our squares.

[15] Happy is the people for whom it is so;
happy is the people whose ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 3, 5, 15) — Tetragrammaton. **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v9, 15) — Hebrew *Elohim*. No vocative "O": the Hebrew has no such word.
- **The words of Ps 18 / 2 Sam 22 and Ps 8:4** (v1–7, 10) — where the Hebrew is the same, the wording of the finished song is used; every difference is rendered as this psalm has it and named below. The psalm takes up the song's words but gives them other forms: imperatives (v5–7) where the song tells what Yahweh did.
- **"Of David"** (heading) — Hebrew לְדָוִד, the first word of v1 in the Hebrew. לְ "by / for / belonging to": "of" leaves it open.
- **"Blessed be the LORD my strength, which teacheth my hands to war, and my fingers to fight" → "Blessed is Yahweh, my Rock, who teaches my hands for battle, my fingers for war"** (v1) — Hebrew בָּרוּךְ יְהוָה צוּרִי, no verb: "is" (as Ps 18:46 "blessed is my Rock"; Ps 28:6); צוּר "Rock", with a capital where it names Yahweh (its row; KJV "strength"). הַמְלַמֵּד יָדַי לַקְרָב, as Ps 18:34 "He teaches my hands for war", but here קְרָב "battle" (a poetic word), and the second line אֶצְבְּעוֹתַי לַמִּלְחָמָה "my fingers for war" (מִלְחָמָה, the word of Ps 18:34); no "and".
- **"My goodness, and my fortress; my high tower, and my deliverer; my shield, and he in whom I trust; who subdueth my people under me" → "my chesed and my stronghold, my secure height and my rescuer, mine, my shield, and in him I take refuge, who subdues my people under me"** (v2) — Hebrew חַסְדִּי "my chesed" (token; Yahweh himself named as the speaker's *chesed*, as Ps 59:10, 17 "the Elohim of my chesed"); מְצוּדָה "stronghold", מִשְׂגָּב "secure height", מְפַלֵּט "rescuer", מָגֵן "shield" (the names of 2 Sam 22:2–3; Ps 18:2); וּמְפַלְטִי לִי "and my rescuer, mine", as 2 Sam 22:2 (Ps 18 has no לִי). וּבוֹ חָסִיתִי "and in him I take refuge" (perfect; חָסָה בְּ). הָרוֹדֵד עַמִּי תַחְתָּי: רָדַד "beat down, subdue" (Ps 18:47 has another verb, וַיַּדְבֵּר; English has one word); עַמִּי "my people" as printed; some manuscripts and the versions read עַמִּים "peoples", as Ps 18:47; 2 Sam 22:48 (from memory): the Masoretic Text is kept.
- **"LORD, what is man, that thou takest knowledge of him! or the son of man, that thou makest account of him!" → "Yahweh, what is man, that you know him, the ben-enosh (son of man), that you think of him?"** (v3) — Hebrew מָה־אָדָם וַתֵּדָעֵהוּ בֶּן־אֱנוֹשׁ וַתְּחַשְּׁבֵהוּ: the words of Ps 8:4 in the other order — there אֱנוֹשׁ "mortal man" first and בֶּן־אָדָם "son of man"; here אָדָם "man" first and בֶּן־אֱנוֹשׁ, literally "son of mortal man" (token `ben-enosh`). The verbs differ too: יָדַע "know" (Ps 8:4 "remember"), חִשֵּׁב "think of, take account of" (Ps 8:4 "visit"). No "or" and no "and" between the lines.
- **"Man is like to vanity: his days are as a shadow that passeth away" → "Man is like the vapor; his days are like a passing shadow"** (v4) — Hebrew אָדָם לַהֶבֶל דָּמָה: הֶבֶל "vapor" (its row; "breath", "vanity" in other versions), with the article; דָּמָה "be like", a perfect. כְּצֵל עוֹבֵר "like a passing shadow"; the line has no verb ("are" supplied).
- **"Bow thy heavens, O LORD, and come down: touch the mountains, and they shall smoke" → "Yahweh, bow your heavens and come down; touch the mountains, and let them smoke"** (v5) — Hebrew יְהוָה הַט־שָׁמֶיךָ וְתֵרֵד, the name first: the request of what Ps 18:9 tells, "And he bowed the heavens and came down" (נָטָה); here "your heavens". גַּע בֶּהָרִים "touch the mountains" (נָגַע בְּ); וְיֶעֱשָׁנוּ "and let them smoke" (עָשַׁן, an imperfect after the imperative: what is to follow; "and they will smoke").
- **"Cast forth lightning, and scatter them: shoot out thine arrows, and destroy them" → "Flash forth lightning, and scatter them; send your arrows, and throw them into confusion"** (v6) — Hebrew בְּרוֹק בָּרָק, verb and noun of one root, literally "lighten lightning"; וּתְפִיצֵם and וּתְהֻמֵּם, imperfects with "and" after imperatives (also "and you will scatter them … throw them into confusion"); the words of Ps 18:14 ("And he sent his arrows and scattered them, and lightnings, many, and threw them into confusion"): שָׁלַח "send", הֵפִיץ "scatter", הָמַם "throw into confusion" (not "destroy").
- **"Send thine hand from above; rid me, and deliver me out of great waters, from the hand of strange children" → "Stretch out your hands from on high; set me free and deliver me from many waters, from the hand of foreigners"** (v7) — Hebrew שְׁלַח יָדֶיךָ מִמָּרוֹם: יָדֶיךָ plural "your hands"; שָׁלַח יָד "stretch out the hand" (its row); מִמָּרוֹם "from on high" (as Ps 18:16, which has no "hands": "He sent from on high"). פְּצֵנִי, from פָּצָה, literally "open" (of the mouth); in the late and Aramaic sense "set free" (also v10, 11). מִמַּיִם רַבִּים "from many waters" (as Ps 18:16). בְּנֵי נֵכָר "foreigners", literally "sons of foreignness" (as Ps 18:44, 45).
- **"Whose mouth speaketh vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of falsehood" → "whose mouth has spoken emptiness, and whose right hand is a right hand of falsehood"** (v8, 11) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר פִּיהֶם דִּבֶּר־שָׁוְא: פֶּה singular "mouth"; דִּבֶּר a perfect; שָׁוְא as an object "emptiness" (its row; Ps 12:2). וִימִינָם יְמִין שָׁקֶר "and their right hand is a right hand of falsehood"; שֶׁקֶר "falsehood". "whose" is carried on from אֲשֶׁר.
- **"I will sing a new song unto thee, O God: upon a psaltery and an instrument of ten strings will I sing praises unto thee" → "Elohim, a new song let me sing to you; with a harp of ten strings let me make music to you"** (v9) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים first (the one place in the psalm without the name Yahweh); אָשִׁירָה, אֲזַמְּרָה cohortatives, "let me"; בְּנֵבֶל עָשׂוֹר "with a harp of ten (strings)", as Ps 33:2 ("strings" supplied; KJV makes two instruments); זִמֵּר "make music".
- **"It is he that giveth salvation unto kings: who delivereth David his servant from the hurtful sword" → "who gives salvation to kings, who sets free David his servant from the evil sword"** (v10) — Hebrew הַנּוֹתֵן … הַפּוֹצֶה, participles with the article ("who gives … who sets free"), joined to v9; "It is he" is not written. תְּשׁוּעָה "salvation"; לַמְּלָכִים, with the article ("to the kings"); Ps 18:50 has "the salvations of his king". מֵחֶרֶב רָעָה "from an evil sword" (רַע "evil", no article; the English article supplied); the accents may also join it to v11 ("from the evil sword set me free").
- **"Rid me, and deliver me" → "Set me free and deliver me"** (v11) — Hebrew פְּצֵנִי וְהַצִּילֵנִי, as v7; the verse repeats v7b–8.
- **"That our sons may be as plants grown up in their youth; that our daughters may be as corner stones, polished after the similitude of a palace" → "that our sons may be like plants grown great in their youth, our daughters like corners cut in the pattern of a temple"** (v12) — Hebrew אֲשֶׁר בָּנֵינוּ כִּנְטִעִים מְגֻדָּלִים בִּנְעוּרֵיהֶם: no verb; אֲשֶׁר "that" (as אֲשֶׁר יִיטַב "that it may go well"), and the wish "may be" is supplied from it; many take it as a statement ("our sons are like plants …", "happy are we, whose sons …"), and some emend the word (from memory). נְטִעִים "plants" (things planted); מְגֻדָּלִים, pual of גָּדַל, "made great, grown great". זָוִיֹּת "corners" (Zech 9:15 has the word of the corners of the altar, from memory; "corner pillars" interprets); מְחֻטָּבוֹת, pual of חָטַב "cut, hew" ("polished" is not the word); תַּבְנִית "pattern" (its row); הֵיכָל "temple" (its row; of a king's house "palace": which is meant here is not said).
- **"That our garners may be full, affording all manner of store: that our sheep may bring forth thousands and ten thousands in our streets" → "our storehouses full, furnishing from kind to kind; our flock bringing forth thousands, made ten thousands in our open places"** (v13) — Hebrew מְזָוֵינוּ, a word found only here, "our storehouses" (uncertain); מְפִיקִים "furnishing, bringing out" (הֵפִיק); מִזַּן אֶל־זַן "from kind to kind" (זַן, a late word). צֹאן "flock" (sheep and goats); מַאֲלִיפוֹת, hifil of אָלַף, "making thousands"; מְרֻבָּבוֹת, pual of רָבַב, "made ten thousands". בְּחוּצוֹתֵינוּ, literally "in our outsides": the open country outside the town ("streets" elsewhere, as Ps 18:42; here the flock is meant to be in the fields).
- **"That our oxen may be strong to labour; that there be no breaking in, nor going out; that there be no complaining in our streets" → "our oxen laden; no breach and no going out, and no outcry in our squares"** (v14) — Hebrew אַלּוּפֵינוּ "our oxen" (אַלּוּף of cattle, another sense than the "chief" of Edom: a different sense, so the "chief" row does not bind); מְסֻבָּלִים, pual of סָבַל "carry a load": "laden", either with burdens or with young (KJV "strong to labour", others "heavy with young": the word does not decide). אֵין־פֶּרֶץ "no breach" (פֶּרֶץ, its row: a break in the wall); וְאֵין יוֹצֵאת "and no going out" (a feminine participle: a going out into exile, a sortie, or a miscarriage: not said); צְוָחָה "outcry" (a loud cry of distress; another word than צְעָקָה); רְחֹבוֹת "squares" (the open places of the town).
- **"Happy is that people, that is in such a case: yea, happy is that people, whose God is the LORD" → "Happy is the people for whom it is so; happy is the people whose Elohim is Yahweh"** (v15) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵי "happy" (never "blessed": that is בָּרוּךְ, v1); הָעָם with the article; שֶׁכָּכָה לּוֹ, literally "that thus is to it" (שֶׁ, the short form of אֲשֶׁר); no "yea".

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — the heading is part of v1 in the Hebrew; the verse numbers are the same.
- **Ps 18 / 2 Sam 22 and Ps 8** — v1–2 take up the names of Yahweh from the song of David; v3 the question of Ps 8:4; v5–7 the coming down of Ps 18:9–16. The psalm's own words differ in many small points: each is translated as printed here.
- **v12–14** — the blessing on sons, daughters, stores and herds is one long sentence with no main verb; v15 sums it up.
