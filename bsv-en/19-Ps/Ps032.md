# Psalm 32

Of David. A Maskil.

[1] Happy is the one whose transgression is forgiven,
whose sin is covered.

[2] Happy is the man to whom ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ does not reckon iniquity,
and in whose spirit there is no deceit.

[3] When I was silent, my bones wore out
through my roaring all the day.

[4] For day and night your hand was heavy upon me;
my sap was turned in the droughts of summer. Selah.

[5] My sin I made known to you,
and my iniquity I did not cover;
I said, "I will confess my transgressions to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧,"
and you forgave the iniquity of my sin. Selah.

[6] For this let every faithful one pray to you
at a time of finding;
surely at the flood of many waters,
to him they shall not reach.

[7] You are a hiding place for me;
from distress you guard me;
with ringing cries of rescue you encircle me. Selah.

[8] I will give you insight and teach you in the way that you shall go;
I will counsel, my eye upon you.

[9] Do not be like a horse, like a mule, without understanding,
with bridle and halter, its ornament, to curb it;
there is no coming near to you.

[10] Many are the pains of the wicked,
but the one who trusts in ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧, ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ shall encircle him.

[11] Rejoice in ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ and be glad, righteous ones,
and shout for joy, all upright of heart!

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v2, 5, 10, 11) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"Maschil of David" → "Of David. A Maskil."** (heading) — Hebrew לְדָוִד מַשְׂכִּיל, in this order. לְדָוִד: the לְ may mean "by", "for" or "belonging to" David; "of" leaves it open. מַשְׂכִּיל is a hifil participle of שָׂכַל ("give insight, act with insight"); as a heading term its meaning is uncertain ("a psalm of insight", "a skilful psalm"); the word is kept. The same root returns in v8 אַשְׂכִּילְךָ "I will give you insight".
- **"Blessed" → "Happy"** (v1, 2) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵי, "happy" (as Deut 33:29; 1 Kgs 10:8); "blessed" is בָּרוּךְ. Rom 4:7–8 cites v1–2 from the Greek, μακάριοι, and in the plural ("whose lawless deeds are forgiven"); the Hebrew is singular.
- **"whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered" — kept** (v1) — Hebrew נְשׂוּי־פֶּשַׁע כְּסוּי חֲטָאָה, two passive participles: literally "lifted of transgression, covered of sin". נָשָׂא of sin is "forgive" (literally "lift, bear, take away"), so also v5 נָשָׂאתָ "you forgave".
- **"unto whom the LORD imputeth not iniquity" → "to whom Yahweh does not reckon iniquity"** (v2) — Hebrew לֹא יַחְשֹׁב יְהוָה לוֹ עָוֹן; חָשַׁב "reckon" (as Gen 15:6). אָדָם "man" (a human being). רְמִיָּה "deceit" (34:13 מִרְמָה, another word, has the same English).
- **"When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long" → "When I was silent, my bones wore out through my roaring all the day"** (v3) — Hebrew כִּי־הֶחֱרַשְׁתִּי בָּלוּ עֲצָמָי בְּשַׁאֲגָתִי כָּל־הַיּוֹם. כִּי may be "when" or "for" (giving the reason for v1–2). בָּלָה "wear out"; שְׁאָגָה "roaring" (the lion's word).
- **"my moisture is turned into the drought of summer" → "my sap was turned in the droughts of summer"** (v4) — Hebrew נֶהְפַּךְ לְשַׁדִּי בְּחַרְבֹנֵי קַיִץ. תִּכְבַּד "was heavy" is an imperfect: what went on day and night. לְשַׁד is found only here and Num 11:8; its sense is uncertain ("juice, sap, vigour"). WORDS-HE gives "dainty" for Num 11:8 (a rich morsel of oil); of a man's own body that would say nothing, so "sap" here. חַרְבֹנִים "droughts" (plural, only here); בְּ is "in" (often taken as "into" or "as by").
- **"I acknowledged my sin unto thee" → "My sin I made known to you"** (v5) — Hebrew חַטָּאתִי אוֹדִיעֲךָ, "my sin" first; the verb is an imperfect, set in the past by "I did not cover", "I said", "you forgave" (it may also be read "I will make known"). אוֹדֶה עֲלֵי פְשָׁעַי: הוֹדָה of sin "confess", עֲלֵי "concerning" (English "confess" takes the object directly).
- **"in a time when thou mayest be found" → "at a time of finding"** (v6) — Hebrew לְעֵת מְצֹא, an infinitive with no object or subject: who finds or is found is not said. עַל־זֹאת "for this". חָסִיד "faithful one" (one who keeps *chesed*). רַק, literally "only", here asserting: "surely". שֵׁטֶף "flood" (from שָׁטַף "overflow"). The last line keeps the Hebrew order: "to him they shall not reach".
- **"thou shalt preserve me from trouble; thou shalt compass me about with songs of deliverance" → "from distress you guard me; with ringing cries of rescue you encircle me"** (v7) — Hebrew מִצַּר תִּצְּרֵנִי, a play of sounds; צַר here "distress" (it may also be "adversary"); נָצַר "guard". רָנֵּי פַלֵּט "ringing cries of rescue": רֹן only here; פַלֵּט is a form of the root of "rescue" (פִּלֵּט); the phrase is uncertain. סוֹבֵב "encircle", also v10. סֵתֶר "hiding place".
- **"I will instruct thee and teach thee … I will guide thee with mine eye" → "I will give you insight and teach you … I will counsel, my eye upon you"** (v8) — Hebrew אַשְׂכִּילְךָ וְאוֹרְךָ … אִיעֲצָה עָלֶיךָ עֵינִי. הִשְׂכִּיל "give insight" (the root of מַשְׂכִּיל); הוֹרָה "teach"; אִיעֲצָה "I will counsel" (cohortative). "My eye upon you" stands beside the verb without a joining word: it may describe the counsel or be a clause of its own. The speaker is not named.
- **"Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule … whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee" → "Do not be like a horse, like a mule … with bridle and halter, its ornament, to curb it; there is no coming near to you"** (v9) — Hebrew אַל־תִּהְיוּ כְּסוּס כְּפֶרֶד אֵין הָבִין בְּמֶתֶג־וָרֶסֶן עֶדְיוֹ לִבְלוֹם בַּל קְרֹב אֵלֶיךָ. The command is plural (v8 and אֵלֶיךָ are singular). עֶדְיוֹ is "its ornament" (עֲדִי), the harness; its sense here is uncertain ("its mouth", "its jaws", "its spirit" are guesses; "mouth" is from memory of the versions). לִבְלוֹם "to curb", without an object: "it" supplied. בַּל קְרֹב "no coming near": either it will not come near unless curbed, or it must be curbed so that it does not come at you; the Hebrew does not decide. מֶתֶג "bridle", רֶסֶן "halter".
- **"Many sorrows shall be to the wicked: but he that trusteth in the LORD, mercy shall compass him about" → "Many are the pains of the wicked, but the one who trusts in Yahweh, steadfast love shall encircle him"** (v10) — Hebrew מַכְאוֹב "pain"; the trusting one stands first and is taken up by "him", as in the Hebrew. חֶסֶד *chesed*.
- **"Be glad … and rejoice … and shout for joy" → "Rejoice … and be glad … and shout for joy"** (v11) — Hebrew שִׂמְחוּ "rejoice", גִּילוּ "be glad", הַרְנִינוּ "shout for joy", the rows of the word list. כָּל־יִשְׁרֵי־לֵב "all upright of heart".

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — the heading stands in Hebrew v1 with the first words of the psalm: the numbers do not shift.
- **Penitential psalm** — second of the seven.
- **v1–2 cited by Paul** (Rom 4:7–8) on justification.
