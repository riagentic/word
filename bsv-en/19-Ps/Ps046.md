# Psalm 46

To the overseer. Of the sons of ⟦korah|Qorach|Korah⟧. Upon ⟦alamoth|Alamot|Alamoth⟧. A song.

[1] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is for us a refuge and strength,
a help in distresses, found very much.

[2] Therefore we will not fear when the earth changes,
and when the mountains totter in the heart of the seas.

[3] Its waters roar, they foam;
mountains quake at its rising. Selah.

[4] A river — its channels gladden the city of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,
the holy place of the dwellings of ⟦elyon|Elyon|the Most High⟧.

[5] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is in her midst; she shall not be shaken;
⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ will help her at the turning of the morning.

[6] Nations roared, kingdoms tottered;
he gave his voice, the earth melts away.

[7] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ of Hosts is with us;
a secure height for us is the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧. Selah.

[8] Come, behold the works of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧,
who has set things that appall in the earth.

[9] He makes wars cease to the end of the earth;
the bow he breaks in pieces, and he cuts up the spear;
wagons he burns in the fire.

[10] "Leave off, and know that I am ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;
I will be exalted among the nations,
I will be exalted in the earth."

[11] ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The LORD⟧ of Hosts is with us;
a secure height for us is the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧. Selah.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 4, 5, 7, 10, 11) — Hebrew *Elohim*; an Elohistic psalm: the divine names as printed. **"LORD of hosts" → "Yahweh of Hosts"** (v7, 11) — Hebrew יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת: the Tetragrammaton stands here and in v8, also in this Elohistic psalm.
- **"To the chief Musician for the sons of Korah, A Song upon Alamoth" → "To the overseer. Of the sons of Korah. Upon Alamot. A song."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ לִבְנֵי־קֹרַח עַל־עֲלָמוֹת שִׁיר, in this order. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer" (the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21; traditional "to the chief musician"); לִבְנֵי: לְ "by", "for" or "belonging to", "of" leaves it open; עֲלָמוֹת (as 1 Chr 15:20) is commonly taken from עַלְמָה "young woman" (of high voices, or a tune): meaning uncertain; שִׁיר "a song".
- **"God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble" → "Elohim is for us a refuge and strength, a help in distresses, found very much"** (v1) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים לָנוּ מַחֲסֶה וָעֹז, "for us"; מַחֲסֶה "refuge" (noun of חָסָה "take refuge"); עֹז "strength"; עֶזְרָה בְצָרוֹת נִמְצָא מְאֹד: צָרוֹת plural "distresses"; נִמְצָא "found" (nifal: he has proved himself, he is readily found), מְאֹד "very much"; "present" is not written.
- **"though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea" → "when the earth changes, and when the mountains totter in the heart of the seas"** (v2) — Hebrew בְּהָמִיר אָרֶץ, הֵמִיר "change"; וּבְמוֹט הָרִים, מוֹט qal "totter" (v6; the nifal "be shaken" v5); בְּלֵב יַמִּים "in the heart of the seas", plural, with בְּ "in" (not "into"); "though" is not written: בְּ with the infinitive is "when".
- **"Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof" → "Its waters roar, they foam; mountains quake at its rising"** (v3) — Hebrew יֶהֱמוּ יֶחְמְרוּ מֵימָיו, two verbs before the subject, no "though" (the line may still go on from the "when" of v2); הָמָה "roar" (also of the nations, v6); רָעַשׁ "quake"; גַּאֲוָה "rising" (lit. "exaltation", of the sea's surge: the row "exaltation" would speak of honor here).
- **"There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High" → "A river — its channels gladden the city of Elohim, the holy place of the dwellings of Elyon"** (v4) — Hebrew נָהָר פְּלָגָיו יְשַׂמְּחוּ: "there is" is not written; פְּלָגִים "channels" (as Ps 1:3); שִׂמַּח "gladden"; קְדֹשׁ מִשְׁכְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן "the holy place of the dwellings of Elyon" (מִשְׁכָּן plural "dwellings"); the Greek has "has sanctified his dwelling" (from memory).
- **"she shall not be moved: God shall help her, and that right early" → "she shall not be shaken; Elohim will help her at the turning of the morning"** (v5) — Hebrew בַּל־תִּמּוֹט, מוֹט nifal "be shaken"; לִפְנוֹת בֹּקֶר "at the turning of the morning" (= at daybreak).
- **"The heathen raged, the kingdoms were moved: he uttered his voice, the earth melted" → "Nations roared, kingdoms tottered; he gave his voice, the earth melts away"** (v6) — Hebrew הָמוּ גוֹיִם, הָמָה "roar" (as v3); מָטוּ, מוֹט qal "totter" (as v2); נָתַן בְּקוֹלוֹ, lit. "he gave with his voice" (= gave his voice, as 2 Sam 22:14); תָּמוּג imperfect after three perfects, "melts away" (מוּג).
- **"the God of Jacob is our refuge" → "a secure height for us is the Elohim of Jacob"** (v7, 11) — Hebrew מִשְׂגָּב־לָנוּ אֱלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב, "a secure height for us" first; מִשְׂגָּב "secure height" (2 Sam 22:3; not "refuge": that is מַחֲסֶה v1).
- **"Come, behold the works of the LORD, what desolations he hath made in the earth" → "Come, behold the works of Yahweh, who has set things that appall in the earth"** (v8) — Hebrew לְכוּ־חֲזוּ, חָזָה "behold"; מִפְעָלוֹת "works" (root פָּעַל, as פֹּעַל Ps 44:1); אֲשֶׁר־שָׂם שַׁמּוֹת, שִׂים "set"; שַׁמּוֹת "things that appall" (שַׁמָּה, root שָׁמֵם "be desolate / appalled"; "desolations" in the other sense of the root).
- **"he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder; he burneth the chariot in the fire" → "the bow he breaks in pieces, and he cuts up the spear; wagons he burns in the fire"** (v9) — Hebrew מַשְׁבִּית מִלְחָמוֹת, a participle ("He" supplied for English); קֶשֶׁת יְשַׁבֵּר, the object first; שִׁבֵּר "break in pieces", קִצֵּץ "cut up"; עֲגָלוֹת "wagons" (carts; "chariots" is רֶכֶב); the Greek has "shields" (reading עֲגִלוֹת "round shields", from memory); בָּאֵשׁ "in the fire".
- **"Be still, and know" → "Leave off, and know"** (v10) — Hebrew הַרְפּוּ, hifil of רָפָה "let go, slacken": "leave off, desist" (addressed to the nations at war); "be still" is a reading of the sense. The quotation marks show the change of speaker; the Hebrew has none.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1–11 = Heb v2–12.
- **Poetic structure** — three stanzas (v1-3, v4-7, v8-11); the refrain v7 = v11.
- **Classic refuge-psalm** — basis of Luther's "A Mighty Fortress."
