# Psalm 13

To the overseer. A psalm of David.

[1] How long, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧, will you forget me forever?
How long will you hide your face from me?

[2] How long shall I set counsels in my soul,
sorrow in my heart by day?
How long shall my enemy be exalted over me?

[3] Look, answer me, ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|LORD⟧ my ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;
give light to my eyes, lest I sleep the death,

[4] lest my enemy say, "I have overcome him,"
lest my adversaries be glad when I am shaken.

[5] But I — in your ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ I have trusted;
let my heart be glad in your salvation.

[6] Let me sing to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧,
for he has dealt with me.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 3, 6) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v3) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David" → "To the overseer. A psalm of David."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer", from the verb "oversee" of 1 Chr 15:21 (traditional "to the chief musician"). לְדָוִד: the לְ may mean "by", "for" or "belonging to" David; "of" leaves it open.
- **"How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever?" → "How long, Yahweh, will you forget me forever?"** (v1) — Hebrew עַד־אָנָה יְהוָה תִּשְׁכָּחֵנִי נֶצַח, one question: "forever" (נֶצַח, "to the end, perpetually") belongs to the same clause; it is not a second question.
- **"take counsel in my soul, having sorrow in my heart daily" → "set counsels in my soul, sorrow in my heart by day"** (v2) — Hebrew אָשִׁית עֵצוֹת, "set (place) counsels", plural; the second line has no verb of its own (שִׁית governs both). יוֹמָם "by day" (KJV "daily").
- **"Consider and hear me" → "Look, answer me"** (v3) — Hebrew הַבִּיטָה עֲנֵנִי, two imperatives without "and"; עָנָה "answer".
- **"lighten mine eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death" → "give light to my eyes, lest I sleep the death"** (v3) — Hebrew הָאִירָה עֵינַי פֶּן־אִישַׁן הַמָּוֶת, "make my eyes shine"; "the death" is the object of "sleep", with the article: there is no second word "sleep".
- **"I have prevailed against him" → "I have overcome him"** (v4) — Hebrew יְכָלְתִּיו, "I have been able for him" (the יָכֹל row: "overcome").
- **"those that trouble me rejoice when I am moved" → "my adversaries be glad when I am shaken"** (v4) — Hebrew צָרַי יָגִילוּ כִּי אֶמּוֹט; צַר "adversary"; גִּיל "be glad"; מוֹט nifal "be shaken" (as Ps 15:5; 16:8). כִּי may also be "because".
- **"But I have trusted in thy mercy" → "But I — in your steadfast love I have trusted"** (v5) — Hebrew וַאֲנִי בְּחַסְדְּךָ בָטַחְתִּי, "I" set first and "in your *chesed*" before the verb, as in the Hebrew.
- **"my heart shall rejoice" → "let my heart be glad"** (v5) — Hebrew יָגֵל, the short (jussive) form of גִּיל, as in Ps 14:7 "let Jacob be glad"; it may also be read as a future, "my heart shall be glad".
- **"I will sing unto the LORD" → "Let me sing to Yahweh"** (v6) — Hebrew אָשִׁירָה, cohortative ("let me sing", "I will sing").
- **"because he hath dealt bountifully with me" → "for he has dealt with me"** (v6) — Hebrew כִּי גָמַל עָלָי, "for he has dealt upon me". "Bountifully" is not in the Hebrew: the verb alone, which in the Psalms is said of good done (Ps 116:7; 119:17), with no adverb.

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1–4 = Heb v2–5; our v5–6 together = Heb v6.
- **Classic lament** — four "how longs" move to trust.
- **Short but dense** — pattern of lament moving to praise.
