# Psalm 67

To the overseer, with stringed instruments. A psalm, a song.

[1] May ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ show us favor and bless us;
may he make his face shine with us. Selah.

[2] To know your way on the earth,
among all nations your salvation.

[3] Let peoples give thanks to you, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;
let peoples give thanks to you, all of them.

[4] Let folks rejoice and shout for joy,
for you judge peoples with equity,
and folks on the earth — you lead them. Selah.

[5] Let peoples give thanks to you, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;
let peoples give thanks to you, all of them.

[6] The earth has given its produce;
⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, shall bless us.

[7] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ shall bless us,
and all the ends of the earth shall fear him.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 3, 5, 6, 7) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים *Elohim*. An Elohistic psalm: אֱלֹהִים as printed.
- **No "O"** (v3, 5) — the Hebrew vocatives have none.
- **"To the chief Musician on Neginoth, A Psalm or Song" → "To the overseer, with stringed instruments. A psalm, a song."** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ בִּנְגִינֹת מִזְמוֹר שִׁיר: לַמְנַצֵּחַ, participle of נִצַּח "oversee" (1 Chr 15:21), traditional "To the chief musician"; בִּנְגִינוֹת "with stringed instruments" (as Ps 4); מִזְמוֹר שִׁיר "a psalm, a song", in this order; there is no "or".
- **"God be merciful unto us, and bless us" → "May Elohim show us favor and bless us"** (v1) — Hebrew יְחָנֵּנוּ וִיבָרְכֵנוּ, a wish; חָנַן "show favor" (not "mercy", which is רַחֲמִים). **"and cause his face to shine upon us" → "may he make his face shine with us"** — Hebrew יָאֵר פָּנָיו אִתָּנוּ, a short form (jussive) with no "and"; אִתָּנוּ "with us" (Num 6:25 has אֵלֶיךָ "to you"). The verse ends at "Selah"; v2 continues the thought.
- **"That thy way may be known upon earth, thy saving health among all nations" → "To know your way on the earth, among all nations your salvation"** (v2) — Hebrew לָדַעַת בָּאָרֶץ דַּרְכֶּךָ בְּכָל־גּוֹיִם יְשׁוּעָתֶךָ: an infinitive, "to know", with no subject named (= so that your way may be known); the second line in the Hebrew order; יְשׁוּעָה "salvation".
- **"Let the people praise thee, O God; let all the people praise thee" → "Let peoples give thanks to you, Elohim; let peoples give thanks to you, all of them"** (v3, 5) — Hebrew יוֹדוּךָ עַמִּים … יוֹדוּךָ עַמִּים כֻּלָּם: הוֹדָה "give thanks to" (beside הִלֵּל "praise"); עַמִּים plural, without the article; כֻּלָּם "all of them" at the end. The imperfect is a wish here; "peoples shall give thanks" is also possible.
- **"O let the nations be glad and sing for joy" → "Let folks rejoice and shout for joy"** (v4) — Hebrew יִשְׂמְחוּ וִירַנְּנוּ לְאֻמִּים: שָׂמַח "rejoice", רִנֵּן "shout for joy"; לְאֹם "folk" (beside עַם "people", גּוֹי "nation"). **"for thou shalt judge the people righteously" → "for you judge peoples with equity"** — Hebrew מִישׁוֹר, literally "level ground": what is even, fair. **"and govern the nations upon earth" → "and folks on the earth — you lead them"** — Hebrew וּלְאֻמִּים בָּאָרֶץ תַּנְחֵם, "folks" first, then "you lead them" (נָחָה "lead").
- **"Then shall the earth yield her increase" → "The earth has given its produce"** (v6) — Hebrew אֶרֶץ נָתְנָה יְבוּלָהּ, a perfect: "has given"; no "then"; יְבוּל "produce". **"and God, even our own God, shall bless us" → "Elohim, our Elohim, shall bless us"** — Hebrew יְבָרְכֵנוּ אֱלֹהִים אֱלֹהֵינוּ, no "and"; the imperfect may also be read as a wish ("may Elohim bless us"), as in v7.
- **"and all the ends of the earth shall fear him" kept** (v7) — Hebrew וְיִירְאוּ אֹתוֹ כָּל־אַפְסֵי־אָרֶץ; also read "and let all the ends of the earth fear him".

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1 = Heb v2, and so on to v7 = Heb v8.
- **The blessing of the priests** — v1 takes up Num 6:24–26 ("bless", "make his face shine", "show favor").
- **Refrain** — v3 = v5.
