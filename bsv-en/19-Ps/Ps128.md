# Psalm 128

A song of the ascents.

[1] Happy is everyone who fears ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧,
who walks in his ways.

[2] The labor of your palms you shall surely eat;
happy are you, and it is good for you.

[3] Your wife is like a fruitful vine
in the remote parts of your house;
your sons like shoots of olive trees
around your table.

[4] Behold, surely thus shall a man be blessed
who fears ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧.

[5] May ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ bless you from ⟦zion|Tziyon|Zion⟧,
and see the good of ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧
all the days of your life;

[6] and see sons of your sons!
⟦shalom|Shalom|Peace⟧ upon ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧!

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (v1, 4, 5) — Tetragrammaton.
- **"A Song of degrees" → "A song of the ascents"** (heading) — Hebrew שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת, the first words of v1; מַעֲלָה "a going up, a step", with the article.
- **"Blessed is every one that feareth the LORD" → "Happy is everyone who fears Yahweh"** (v1) — Hebrew אַשְׁרֵי "happy" (also v2; "blessed" is בָּרוּךְ, and the verb בֵּרַךְ stands in v4, 5); יְרֵא יְהוָה "one who fears Yahweh".
- **"For thou shalt eat the labour of thine hands" → "The labor of your palms you shall surely eat"** (v2) — Hebrew יְגִיעַ כַּפֶּיךָ כִּי תֹאכֵל: the object first, as in the Hebrew; כִּי here makes the statement strong ("surely"); "when you eat" is the other reading. יְגִיעַ "labor" (what toil gains); כַּף "palm", not יָד "hand". **"happy shalt thou be, and it shall be well with thee" → "happy are you, and it is good for you"** — Hebrew אַשְׁרֶיךָ וְטוֹב לָךְ, no verb: "your happiness", "and good to you".
- **"Thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of thine house" → "Your wife is like a fruitful vine in the remote parts of your house"** (v3) — Hebrew אֶשְׁתְּךָ כְּגֶפֶן פֹּרִיָּה, no verb ("is" supplied; "shall be" is not written); בְּיַרְכְּתֵי בֵיתֶךָ, literally "in the flanks of your house", its inner, far rooms (as Ps 48:2 "the remote parts of the north"). **"thy children like olive plants" → "your sons like shoots of olive trees"** — Hebrew בָּנֶיךָ "your sons"; שְׁתִלֵי זֵיתִים "plantings / shoots of olive trees".
- **"Behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the LORD" → "Behold, surely thus shall a man be blessed who fears Yahweh"** (v4) — Hebrew הִנֵּה כִי־כֵן, כִּי making "thus" strong; יְבֹרַךְ, pual "be blessed"; גֶּבֶר "a man" (no article; elsewhere "mighty man", here alone).
- **"The LORD shall bless thee out of Zion: and thou shalt see the good of Jerusalem" → "May Yahweh bless you from Zion, and see the good of Jerusalem"** (v5) — Hebrew יְבָרֶכְךָ, a wish; then וּרְאֵה, an imperative, "and see" (= so that you see); also v6. טוּב "good".
- **"Yea, thou shalt see thy children's children, and peace upon Israel" → "and see sons of your sons! Shalom upon Israel!"** (v6) — Hebrew וּרְאֵה־בָנִים לְבָנֶיךָ, literally "and see sons to your sons"; שָׁלוֹם עַל־יִשְׂרָאֵל, no verb and no "and" (as Ps 125:5).

### Notes
- **Hebrew numbering** — the heading is part of v1 in the Hebrew too; the numbers agree.
- **Poetic structure** — the one who fears Yahweh and his labor (v1–2), wife and sons (v3–4), the blessing from Zion (v5–6).
