# Psalm 114

[1] When ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ went out from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧,
the house of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ from a people of strange speech,

[2] ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ became his holiness,
⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ his dominions.

[3] The sea saw and fled;
the ⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧ turned backward.

[4] The mountains skipped like rams,
hills like sons of the flock.

[5] What is it, sea, that you flee,
⟦jordan|Yarden|Jordan⟧, that you turn backward,

[6] mountains, that you skip like rams,
hills, like sons of the flock?

[7] From before the Lord writhe, earth,
from before the ⟦eloah|Eloah|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧,

[8] who turns the rock into a pool of water,
flint into a spring of water.

## Changes

- **"God" → "Eloah"** (v7) — Hebrew אֱלוֹהַּ *Eloah*.
- **"from a people of strange language" → "from a people of strange speech"** (v1) — Hebrew מֵעַם לֹעֵז, a participle found only here: a people "speaking unintelligibly" (foreign to Israel's ears).
- **"Judah was his sanctuary, and Israel his dominion" → "Judah became his holiness, Israel his dominions"** (v2) — Hebrew הָיְתָה יְהוּדָה לְקָדְשׁוֹ, "became for his holiness": קֹדֶשׁ "holiness / holy thing" (his holy possession; "sanctuary", "holy place" is the other sense of the word, and מִקְדָּשׁ is not written). The verb is feminine (Judah as a land). מַמְשְׁלוֹתָיו is plural, "his dominions" (the realm he rules).
- **"The sea saw it, and fled: Jordan was driven back" → "The sea saw and fled; the Jordan turned backward"** (v3) — Hebrew הַיָּם רָאָה וַיָּנֹס, no object "it"; הַיַּרְדֵּן יִסֹּב לְאָחוֹר "turns around backward" (an imperfect, beside the past "saw … fled": given as past), active, not "was driven". The article of "the Jordan" kept (also v5, where it is a vocative).
- **"the little hills like lambs" → "hills like sons of the flock"** (v4, 6) — Hebrew גְּבָעוֹת כִּבְנֵי־צֹאן, "hills" without the article; בְּנֵי־צֹאן "sons of the flock" (= lambs; בֵּן row).
- **"What ailed thee, O thou sea, that thou fleddest? thou Jordan, that thou wast driven back?" → "What is it, sea, that you flee, Jordan, that you turn backward"** (v5) — Hebrew מַה־לְּךָ הַיָּם כִּי תָנוּס, "what to you, sea, that you flee" ("What is it?", as the מַה־לָּךְ row); no "O". The imperfects תָנוּס, תִּסֹּב, תִּרְקְדוּ (v6) are given as present ("flee … turn … skip": the poet asks as though he saw it; past is also possible). The one כִּי "that" carries all four lines; English repeats "that".
- **"Tremble, thou earth, at the presence of the Lord" → "From before the Lord writhe, earth"** (v7) — Hebrew מִלִּפְנֵי אָדוֹן חוּלִי אָרֶץ, in the Hebrew order; חוּל / חִיל "writhe" (as Ps 29:8–9; of the earth: shake as in labor). אָדוֹן "Lord" (the plain word "lord", not אֲדֹנָי, no article; so no token). Line 2 has no verb: "writhe" governs both.
- **"Which turned the rock into a standing water, the flint into a fountain of waters" → "who turns the rock into a pool of water, flint into a spring of water"** (v8) — Hebrew הַהֹפְכִי, a participle ("who turns"; the ‑ִי an old poetic ending); אֲגַם "pool" (reedy standing water, its row), מַעְיָן "spring" (row); חַלָּמִישׁ "flint" without the article.

### Notes
- **Exodus poetic celebration** — part of the Egyptian Hallel (Pss 113–118).
- **Hebrew numbering** — the same as ours.
- **"his" in v2** — no name of God stands before v7: the psalm holds it back until "the Lord … Eloah of Jacob".
