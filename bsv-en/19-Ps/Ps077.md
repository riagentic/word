# Psalm 77

To the overseer, upon ⟦jeduthun|Yedutun|Jeduthun⟧. Of ⟦asaph|Asaf|Asaph⟧, a psalm.

[1] My voice is to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and I will cry out;
my voice is to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and he will give ear to me.

[2] In the day of my distress ⟦adonai|Adonai|the Lord⟧ I sought;
my hand by night was stretched out and did not grow numb;
my soul refused to be comforted.

[3] I will remember ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and I will moan;
I will muse, and my spirit grows faint. Selah.

[4] You held my eyelids;
I was troubled, and I did not speak.

[5] I considered the days from of old,
the years of the ages.

[6] I will remember my song in the night;
with my heart I will muse,
and my spirit searched:

[7] "Will ⟦adonai|Adonai|the Lord⟧ cast off for the ages,
and will he no more again be pleased?

[8] Is his ⟦steadfast-love|chesed|steadfast love⟧ gone forever?
Has speech come to an end to generation and generation?

[9] Has ⟦el|El|God⟧ forgotten to show favor?
Or has he shut up in anger his compassion?" Selah.

[10] And I said, "This is my sickness:
the years of the right hand of ⟦elyon|Elyon|the Most High⟧."

[11] I will remember the deeds of ⟦yah|Yah|the LORD⟧;
yes, I will remember your wonder from of old.

[12] And I will softly recite all your work,
and on your doings I will muse.

[13] ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, in holiness is your way;
who is a great ⟦el|el|god⟧ like ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧?

[14] You are the ⟦el|El|God⟧ who does wonder;
you have made known among the peoples your strength.

[15] You redeemed with the arm your people,
the sons of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ and ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧. Selah.

[16] The waters saw you, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧;
the waters saw you, they writhed;
even the deeps trembled.

[17] The clouds poured out water;
the skies gave a voice;
even your arrows went to and fro.

[18] The voice of your thunder was in the whirl;
lightnings lit up the world;
the earth trembled and quaked.

[19] In the sea was your way,
and your path in great waters,
and your footprints were not known.

[20] You led your people like a flock
by the hand of ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and ⟦aaron|Aharon|Aaron⟧.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v1, 3, 13, 16) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים, as printed. **"Lord" → "Adonai"** (v2, 7) — Hebrew אֲדֹנָי. **"God" → "El"** (v9, 14) — Hebrew אֵל; in v13 אֵל is the common noun, "a great god" (the `el` token with its plain forms, as Deut 32:12). **"LORD" → "Yah"** (v11) — Hebrew יָהּ, the short form of the Name. **"the most High" → "Elyon"** (v10) — Hebrew עֶלְיוֹן.
- **"To the chief Musician, to Jeduthun, A Psalm of Asaph" → "To the overseer, upon Yedutun. Of Asaph, a psalm"** (heading) — Hebrew לַמְנַצֵּחַ עַל־יְדוּתוּן לְאָסָף מִזְמוֹר, in this order. לַמְנַצֵּחַ "to the overseer" (the verb of 1 Chr 15:21; traditional "to the chief musician"). עַל "upon", as with the tune names (Ps 6 "upon the Sheminit"; Ps 39 has לִ "to"): what "upon Jeduthun" means (his manner of performance, or an instrument or tune named after him) is not known. יְדוּתוּן is the qere; the ketiv ידיתון reads the name *Yedithun*. לְאָסָף: by, for or belonging to Asaph; "of" leaves it open.
- **"I cried unto God with my voice, even unto God with my voice; and he gave ear unto me" → "My voice is to Elohim, and I will cry out; my voice is to Elohim, and he will give ear to me"** (v1) — Hebrew קוֹלִי אֶל־אֱלֹהִים, without a verb ("is" supplied); וְאֶצְעָקָה, cohortative, "and I will cry out" (the earlier "aloud" turns the noun into an adverb); וְהַאֲזִין, an unusual form of הֶאֱזִין "give ear" (its row): read as a perfect with "and", "and he will give ear" (KJV "gave ear"); it may also be an infinitive used as a command, "and give ear to me".
- **"In the day of my trouble I sought the Lord: my sore ran in the night, and ceased not" → "In the day of my distress Adonai I sought; my hand by night was stretched out and did not grow numb"** (v2) — Hebrew צָרָה "distress" (its row); אֲדֹנָי דָּרָשְׁתִּי, perfect, Adonai first (the earlier "I seek" is present); יָדִי לַיְלָה נִגְּרָה, word for word "my hand by night flowed / was poured out" (נָגַר, of water 2 Sam 14:14; the hifil "pour out" in Ps 75:8): the hand stretched out in prayer; KJV "my sore ran" takes יָד as a wound. וְלֹא תָפוּג "and did not grow numb" (פּוּג, as Ps 38:8). מֵאֲנָה, perfect, "refused" (מֵאֵן); הִנָּחֵם "be comforted" (the נִחַם row).
- **"I remembered God, and was troubled: I complained, and my spirit was overwhelmed" → "I will remember Elohim, and I will moan; I will muse, and my spirit grows faint"** (v3) — Hebrew אֶזְכְּרָה … וְאֶהֱמָיָה … אָשִׂיחָה, three cohortatives ("I will", as v6, 11, 12: the resolve of the moment; "When I remember … I moan" makes them a condition); הָמָה "moan" (of a person; the row "be in uproar" is of a town); שִׂיחַ "muse" (as 1 Kgs 18:27); וְתִתְעַטֵּף "and grows faint" (עָטַף hitpael, Ps 107:5; 142:3).
- **"Selah"** (v3, 9, 15) — Hebrew סֶלָה, a term of the psalm's performance; its meaning is not known.
- **"Thou holdest mine eyes waking: I am so troubled that I cannot speak" → "You held my eyelids; I was troubled, and I did not speak"** (v4) — Hebrew אָחַזְתָּ, perfect, "you held"; שְׁמֻרוֹת עֵינָי, literally "the guards (or watches) of my eyes", only here: the eyelids, held so that he cannot sleep (the earlier "open" is supplied); נִפְעַמְתִּי "I was troubled" (פָּעַם, as Gen 41:8); וְלֹא אֲדַבֵּר "and I did not (could not) speak": two clauses, not "so … that".
- **"I have considered the days of old, the years of ancient times" → "I considered the days from of old, the years of the ages"** (v5) — Hebrew חִשַּׁבְתִּי, piel of חָשַׁב "think over, consider" (another sense than the "reckon" row); יָמִים מִקֶּדֶם "days from of old"; שְׁנוֹת עוֹלָמִים "the years of the ages" (עוֹלָם plural).
- **"I call to remembrance my song in the night: I commune with mine own heart: and my spirit made diligent search" → "I will remember my song in the night; with my heart I will muse, and my spirit searched"** (v6) — Hebrew אֶזְכְּרָה, cohortative (no "I said" in the Hebrew: the earlier quotation frame is removed); נְגִינָתִי "my song", from נִגֵּן "play a stringed instrument" (the song sung to strings); בַּלַּיְלָה goes with the first line by the accents; עִם־לְבָבִי אָשִׂיחָה "with my heart I will muse"; וַיְחַפֵּשׂ רוּחִי, narrative form, "and my spirit searched": the Hebrew moves from the resolve to what then happened. v7–9 are the questions of that search (quotation marks supplied).
- **"Will the Lord cast off for ever? and will he be favourable no more?" → "Will Adonai cast off for the ages, and will he no more again be pleased?"** (v7) — Hebrew הַלְעוֹלָמִים "for the ages" (plural); זָנַח "cast off" (its row; no object written); וְלֹא־יֹסִיף לִרְצוֹת עוֹד, literally "and will he not add to be pleased again" (the יָסַף row: "no more again"); רָצָה "be pleased" (its row).
- **"Is his mercy clean gone for ever? doth his promise fail for evermore?" → "Is his chesed gone forever? Has speech come to an end to generation and generation?"** (v8) — Hebrew הֶאָפֵס "is it gone" (אָפֵס, its row); לָנֶצַח "forever"; חַסְדּוֹ with the `steadfast-love` token (was plain "steadfast love"); גָּמַר "come to an end" (intransitive; the row's "complete", Ps 57:2, is of one who brings a thing to its end); אֹמֶר "speech" (its row, Ps 19:2), with no "his": the word spoken (KJV "promise" interprets); לְדֹר וָדֹר "to generation and generation" (as Ps 33:11).
- **"Hath God forgotten to be gracious? hath he in anger shut up his tender mercies?" → "Has El forgotten to show favor? Or has he shut up in anger his compassion?"** (v9) — Hebrew חַנּוֹת, infinitive of חָנַן "show favor" (its row; not "be gracious"); אִם "or"; קָפַץ "shut up, draw together"; בְּאַף "in anger" (אַף, also "nose"); רַחֲמָיו "his compassion" (plural in the Hebrew, the רַחֲמִים row).
- **"And I said, This is my infirmity: but I will remember the years of the right hand of the most High" → "And I said, 'This is my sickness: the years of the right hand of Elyon'"** (v10) — Hebrew וָאֹמַר, narrative form, "And I said"; חַלּוֹתִי הִיא, an infinitive of חָלָה "be sick", "my being sick is this" (KJV "infirmity"; the earlier "I will appeal to this" reads another verb, חִלָּה "entreat", and supplies "to"); שְׁנוֹת "the years", the word of v5 (KJV adds "but I will remember"). The same letters and vowels are also the infinitive of שָׁנָה "change": "This is my sickness: the changing of the right hand of Elyon" (that the right hand no longer acts as of old). The verse is one of the hardest in the psalm; neither reading is certain.
- **"I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old" → "I will remember the deeds of Yah; yes, I will remember your wonder from of old"** (v11) — the qere אֶזְכּוֹר "I will remember" is translated; the ketiv אזכיר reads the hifil, "I will make mention of" (the הִזְכִּיר "mention" row). מַעַלְלֵי "the deeds"; כִּי "yes" (it confirms; "for" would give no sense); אֶזְכְּרָה, cohortative; פִּלְאֶךָ "your wonder", singular (פֶּלֶא; KJV "wonders").
- **"I will meditate also of all thy work, and talk of thy doings" → "And I will softly recite all your work, and on your doings I will muse"** (v12) — Hebrew וְהָגִיתִי "And I will softly recite" (הָגָה, the reviewer row; as Ps 1:2, the בְּ of the object not rendered; "meditate" names only thinking); פָּעֳלֶךָ "your work" (פֹּעַל); עֲלִילוֹתֶיךָ "your doings"; אָשִׂיחָה "I will muse" (as v3, 6; KJV "talk").
- **"Thy way, O God, is in the sanctuary: who is so great a God as our God?" → "Elohim, in holiness is your way; who is a great god like Elohim?"** (v13) — Hebrew אֱלֹהִים בַּקֹּדֶשׁ דַּרְכֶּךָ, the vocative first; בַּקֹּדֶשׁ "in holiness" (or "in the holy place", KJV "sanctuary": the word allows both); מִי־אֵל גָּדוֹל כֵּאלֹהִים "who is a great god like Elohim" — no "our" (KJV's italic word) and no "so". No "O".
- **"Thou art the God that doest wonders: thou hast declared thy strength among the people" → "You are the El who does wonder; you have made known among the peoples your strength"** (v14) — Hebrew הָאֵל with the article; עֹשֵׂה פֶלֶא "doing wonder" (singular, as v11); הוֹדַעְתָּ "you have made known"; בָעַמִּים "among the peoples" (plural); עֻזֶּךָ "your strength" (עֹז, its row; the earlier "might").
- **"Thou hast with thine arm redeemed thy people" → "You redeemed with the arm your people"** (v15) — Hebrew גָּאַלְתָּ בִּזְרוֹעַ עַמֶּךָ: בִּזְרוֹעַ "with the arm", without "your" (the arm is his; the word stands alone, as the "strong hand … outstretched arm" of the Exodus); גָּאַל "redeem" (its row); בְּנֵי "the sons of".
- **"The waters saw thee, O God, the waters saw thee; they were afraid: the depths also were troubled" → "The waters saw you, Elohim; the waters saw you, they writhed; even the deeps trembled"** (v16) — Hebrew רָאוּךָ twice (the earlier "When" is not written); יָחִילוּ, from חִיל "writhe" (as Ps 29:8; not "be afraid"); אַף "even"; תְהֹמוֹת "the deeps" (plural of תְּהוֹם, its row); יִרְגְּזוּ "trembled" (רָגַז, as 2 Sam 22:8 = Ps 18:7). The imperfects are given as past, in the frame of v15–20.
- **"The clouds poured out water: the skies sent out a sound: thine arrows also went abroad" → "The clouds poured out water; the skies gave a voice; even your arrows went to and fro"** (v17) — Hebrew זֹרְמוּ מַיִם עָבוֹת "the clouds poured out water" (זָרַם, of a downpour; עָב "cloud"); קוֹל נָתְנוּ שְׁחָקִים "the skies gave a voice" (נָתַן קוֹל, as Ps 18:13 "gave his voice": the thunder; "thunder" itself comes in v18); חֲצָצֶיךָ "your arrows" (the lightning); יִתְהַלָּכוּ, hitpael of הָלַךְ, "went to and fro" (walked about).
- **"The voice of thy thunder was in the heaven: the lightnings lightened the world" → "The voice of your thunder was in the whirl; lightnings lit up the world"** (v18) — Hebrew בַּגַּלְגַּל "in the whirl" (גַּלְגַּל "wheel, whirl": the whirling storm, or the wheels of his chariot; KJV "heaven", the earlier "whirlwind" interpret); בְרָקִים "lightnings", with no "your"; תֵּבֵל "world"; רָגְזָה וַתִּרְעַשׁ "trembled and quaked" (רָגַז as v16, רָעַשׁ "quake" as Ps 18:7).
- **"Thy way is in the sea, and thy path in the great waters, and thy footsteps are not known" → "In the sea was your way, and your path in great waters, and your footprints were not known"** (v19) — Hebrew בַּיָּם דַּרְכֶּךָ "in the sea your way" (the earlier "through" adds); the qere וּשְׁבִילְךָ "and your path" (singular) is translated; the ketiv ושביליך reads "and your paths". בְּמַיִם רַבִּים "in great (many) waters"; וְעִקְּבוֹתֶיךָ "and your footprints" (lit. "heel-marks"; the earlier "yet" is not written); לֹא נֹדָעוּ "were not known" (the earlier "unseen" changes the verb).
- **"Thou leddest thy people like a flock" → "You led your people like a flock"** (v20) — Hebrew נָחִיתָ "you led" (נָחָה, its row); כַצֹּאן "like a flock" (צֹאן, sheep and goats); בְּיַד "by the hand of", kept.

### Notes
- **Numbering** — Hebrew counts the heading as v1: our v1 = Heb v2, and so on to v20 = Heb v21.
- **Structure** — the night of the lament (v1–6), the questions (v7–9), the turn (v10), the remembrance of the deeds of old (v11–15), the waters and the storm at the crossing of the sea (v16–20). The tenses move as in the Hebrew: past (v2, 4, 5), resolve (v3, 6, 11, 12), questions (v7–9).
- **"Asaph"** — Ps 50 and 73–83 bear his name; they use אֱלֹהִים where other psalms have the Name.
