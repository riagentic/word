# Luke 20

[1] And it came to pass on one of the days, as he was teaching the people in the temple and preaching the gospel, that the high priests and the scribes with the elders came upon him,

[2] and they spoke, saying to him, "Tell us by what authority you are doing these things, or who is the one who gave you this authority."

[3] And answering, he said to them, "I also will ask you a word, and tell me:

[4] the ⟦immersion|immersion|baptism⟧ of ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧ — was it from heaven or from men?"

[5] And they reasoned together among themselves, saying, "If we say, 'From heaven,' he will say, 'Why did you not believe him?'

[6] But if we say, 'From men,' all the people will stone us, for they are persuaded that ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧ is a prophet."

[7] And they answered that they did not know from where.

[8] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to them, "Neither do I tell you by what authority I do these things."

[9] And he began to tell the people this parable: "A man planted a vineyard and let it out to farmers and went away from home for a considerable time.

[10] And at the time he sent to the farmers a slave, that they should give him some of the fruit of the vineyard; but the farmers sent him out empty, having beaten him.

[11] And he went on to send another slave; but they, having beaten that one also and dishonored him, sent him out empty.

[12] And he went on to send a third; but they, having wounded this one also, cast him out.

[13] And the lord of the vineyard said, 'What shall I do? I will send my beloved son; perhaps they will respect this one.'

[14] But seeing him, the farmers were reasoning with one another, saying, 'This is the heir; let us kill him, that the inheritance may become ours.'

[15] And having cast him out outside the vineyard, they killed him. What then will the lord of the vineyard do to them?

[16] He will come and destroy these farmers and will give the vineyard to others." And having heard, they said, "May it not be!"

[17] But he, looking at them, said, "What then is this that is written:

'**The stone which the builders rejected,
this became the head of the corner**'?

[18] Everyone who falls upon that stone will be shattered; but upon whomever it falls, it will winnow him."

[19] And the scribes and the high priests sought to lay hands on him in that very hour, and they feared the people, for they knew that he spoke this parable with regard to them.

[20] And having watched closely, they sent spies who pretended that they themselves were righteous, that they might lay hold of a word of his, so as to deliver him up to the rule and the authority of the governor.

[21] And they asked him, saying, "Teacher, we know that you speak and teach rightly and do not receive a face, but in truth you teach the way of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[22] Is it permitted for us to give tribute to Caesar or not?"

[23] But having observed their craftiness, he said to them,

[24] "Show me a denarius. Whose image and inscription does it have?" And they said, "Caesar's."

[25] And he said to them, "Therefore render the things of Caesar to Caesar, and the things of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧."

[26] And they did not have strength to lay hold of a word of his before the people, and marveling at his answer they were silent.

[27] And some of the ⟦sadducees|Tzedukim|Sadducees⟧ approaching, those who say in contradiction that there is no resurrection, asked him,

[28] saying, "Teacher, ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ wrote for us, if someone's brother dies having a wife, and this one is childless, that his brother should take the wife and raise up seed for his brother.

[29] There were therefore seven brothers; and the first, having taken a wife, died childless;

[30] and the second

[31] and the third took her, and likewise also the seven did not leave behind children, and died.

[32] Afterward the woman also died.

[33] The woman therefore, in the resurrection, of which of them does she become wife? For the seven had her as wife."

[34] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to them, "The sons of this age marry and are given in marriage,

[35] but those counted worthy to attain that age and the resurrection from the dead neither marry nor are given in marriage;

[36] for neither are they able to die any more, for they are equal to angels, and they are sons of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, being sons of the resurrection.

[37] But that the dead are raised, even ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ disclosed at the bush, as he calls ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧ the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧.

[38] But he is not ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of the dead, but of the living, for all live to him."

[39] And some of the scribes, answering, said, "Teacher, you spoke well."

[40] For they no longer were daring to ask him anything.

[41] And he said to them, "How do they say that the ⟦mashiach|Mashiach|Christ⟧ is David's son?

[42] For David himself says in the book of Psalms,

'**⟦yhwh|Yahweh|The Lord⟧ said to my Lord,
"Sit at my right hand,
[43] until I set your enemies as a footstool of your feet."**'

[44] David therefore calls him Lord, and how is he his son?"

[45] And as all the people were hearing, he said to the disciples,

[46] "Take heed of the scribes, who want to walk about in robes and have affection for greetings in the marketplaces and first seats in the synagogues and first couches at the suppers,

[47] who devour the houses of the widows and for a pretext pray long; these will receive a more abundant sentence."

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v8, 34) — Hebrew name preserved. In v1 the Greek has only αὐτοῦ, "as he was teaching": no name stands there.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv21, 25, 36, 37, 38) — divine name retained.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v41) — Hebrew Anointed. Greek τὸν χριστόν, with the article.
- **"baptism" → "immersion"** (v4) — Greek βάπτισμα; consistent rendering.
- **"on one of those days, as he taught the people in the temple, and preached the gospel, the chief priests and the scribes came upon him with the elders" → "on one of the days, as he was teaching the people in the temple and preaching the gospel, that the high priests and the scribes with the elders came upon him"** (v1) — "those" (RP ἐκείνων) is not in the critical text. Greek εὐαγγελιζομένου, "preaching the gospel, bringing good news"; ἐπέστησαν, "came upon, stood over" (as Acts 4:1); ἀρχιερεῖς, "high priests" (WH Treg NA28; RP "priests").
- **"Tell us, by what authority doest thou these things? or who is he that gave thee this authority?" → "Tell us by what authority you are doing these things, or who is the one who gave you this authority"** (v2) — Greek εἶπαν λέγοντες πρὸς αὐτόν, "they spoke, saying to him"; ἐν ποίᾳ ἐξουσίᾳ, "in what kind of authority" (as Mark 11:28); τίς ἐστιν ὁ δούς. The printed text ends the sentence with a question mark; in English the request "tell us" carries it.
- **"I will also ask you one thing; and answer me" → "I also will ask you a word, and tell me"** (v3) — Greek ἐρωτήσω ὑμᾶς κἀγὼ λόγον: λόγος, "word"; "one" (RP ἕνα, and Mark 11:29) is not in the critical text. εἴπατέ μοι, "tell me" (Mark: "answer me").
- **"was it from heaven, or of men?" → "was it from heaven or from men?"** (v4, 6) — Greek ἐξ οὐρανοῦ … ἐξ ἀνθρώπων, the same preposition twice; "men" is plural. The words are those of Mark 11:30.
- **"they reasoned with themselves … Why then believed ye him not?" → "they reasoned together among themselves … 'Why did you not believe him?'"** (v5) — Greek συνελογίσαντο, "reckoned together" (only here; Mark 11:31 has διελογίζοντο); "then" is not in the Greek here (Mark 11:31 has οὖν).
- **"all the people will stone us: for they be persuaded that John was a prophet" → "all the people will stone us, for they are persuaded that *Yochanan* is a prophet"** (v6) — Greek καταλιθάσει, "will stone down, stone to death" (λιθάζω with κατα-, only here); πεπεισμένος ἐστίν, a singular, of the people as one; Ἰωάννην προφήτην εἶναι, "John to be a prophet": the infinitive has no time of its own.
- **"they answered, that they could not tell whence it was" → "they answered that they did not know from where"** (v7) — Greek μὴ εἰδέναι πόθεν, without "it was".
- **"Neither tell I you" → "Neither do I tell you"** (v8) — Greek οὐδὲ ἐγὼ λέγω ὑμῖν, a present, word for word Mark 11:33.
- **"A certain man planted a vineyard, and let it forth to husbandmen, and went into a far country for a long time" → "A man planted a vineyard and let it out to farmers and went away from home for a considerable time"** (v9) — Editions: Ἄνθρωπος WH Treg (and the printed SBLGNT) ] + τις NA28, "a certain man" — the verse follows the majority. Greek γεωργοῖς, "farmers, tillers of the ground"; ἀπεδήμησεν, "went away from home" (as Mark 12:1): no "far country" is in the word; χρόνους ἱκανούς, literally "considerable times". πρὸς τὸν λαὸν λέγειν, "to say to the people".
- **"at the season he sent a servant … but the husbandmen beat him, and sent him away empty" → "at the time he sent to the farmers a slave … but the farmers sent him out empty, having beaten him"** (v10) — Greek καιρῷ, "at (the) time"; δοῦλον, "slave"; ἵνα … δώσουσιν, "that they should give" (a future); ἐξαπέστειλαν αὐτὸν δείραντες κενόν (WH NA28 order): ἐξαποστέλλω, "send out" (Mark 12:3 ἀπέστειλαν); δέρω, "beat".
- **"again he sent another servant: and they beat him also, and entreated him shamefully" → "he went on to send another slave; but they, having beaten that one also and dishonored him"** (v11) — Greek προσέθετο ἕτερον πέμψαι, literally "he added to send another" (the Hebrew turn of 19:11; Acts 12:3); ἀτιμάσαντες, "having dishonored" (as Mark 12:4).
- **"again he sent a third: and they wounded him also, and cast him out" → "he went on to send a third; but they, having wounded this one also, cast him out"** (v12) — Greek προσέθετο τρίτον πέμψαι; τραυματίσαντες (as Acts 19:16); ἐξέβαλον.
- **"it may be they will reverence him when they see him" → "perhaps they will respect this one"** (v13) — Greek ἴσως, "perhaps" (only here in the New Testament); ἐντραπήσονται, "will respect" (as 18:2; Mark 12:6); "when they see him" (RP ἰδόντες) is not in the critical text. ὁ κύριος τοῦ ἀμπελῶνος, "the lord of the vineyard" (v13, 15).
- **"they reasoned among themselves, saying, This is the heir: come, let us kill him, that the inheritance may be ours" → "were reasoning with one another, saying, 'This is the heir; let us kill him, that the inheritance may become ours'"** (v14) — Greek διελογίζοντο πρὸς ἀλλήλους, an imperfect (WH Treg NA28); "come" (RP δεῦτε, and Mark 12:7) is not in the critical text; ἵνα ἡμῶν γένηται.
- **"So they cast him out of the vineyard, and killed him" → "And having cast him out outside the vineyard, they killed him"** (v15) — Greek ἐκβαλόντες αὐτὸν ἔξω τοῦ ἀμπελῶνος ἀπέκτειναν: cast out first, then killed (Mark 12:8 has the other order).
- **"when they heard it, they said, God forbid" → "having heard, they said, 'May it not be!'"** (v16) — Greek μὴ γένοιτο, a wish, "may it not come to be" (as Rom 3:4): the word "God" is not in the Greek. τοὺς γεωργοὺς τούτους, "these farmers".
- **"he beheld them … the same is become the head of the corner" → "he, looking at them … this became the head of the corner"** (v17) — Greek ἐμβλέψας αὐτοῖς; the quotation is Ps 118:22 in the Greek, word for word as Mark 12:10: οὗτος ἐγενήθη εἰς κεφαλὴν γωνίας, "this one became the head of the corner" — the chief stone of the corner, whether at the foundation or at the top; "cornerstone" would decide for one of them.
- **"Whosoever shall fall upon that stone shall be broken; but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder" → "Everyone who falls upon that stone will be shattered; but upon whomever it falls, it will winnow him"** (v18) — Greek συνθλασθήσεται, "will be shattered, crushed together"; λικμήσει, "will winnow": scatter him as chaff (as Matt 21:44; the image of Dan 2:35, 44); many render it "crush".
- **"the chief priests and the scribes the same hour sought to lay hands on him; and they feared the people: for they perceived that he had spoken this parable against them" → "the scribes and the high priests sought to lay hands on him in that very hour, and they feared the people, for they knew that he spoke this parable with regard to them"** (v19) — the order "scribes … high priests" is that of WH Treg NA28. Greek ἐν αὐτῇ τῇ ὥρᾳ; καὶ ἐφοβήθησαν τὸν λαόν, "and they feared the people" (the "and" has the force of "but"); ἔγνωσαν, "they knew"; πρὸς αὐτούς, "with regard to them, at them" (as Mark 12:12).
- **"they watched him, and sent forth spies, which should feign themselves just men, that they might take hold of his words, that so they might deliver him unto the power and authority of the governor" → "having watched closely, they sent spies who pretended that they themselves were righteous, that they might lay hold of a word of his, so as to deliver him up to the rule and the authority of the governor"** (v20) — Greek παρατηρήσαντες, "having watched closely" (as 6:7; 14:1), without an object. ἐγκαθέτους, men "let down" into hiding, set to lie in wait: hired spies (only here). ὑποκρινομένους ἑαυτοὺς δικαίους εἶναι, "playing the part that they themselves were righteous" (the verb of ὑποκριτής, "hypocrite"); δίκαιος, "righteous". ἵνα ἐπιλάβωνται αὐτοῦ λόγου: "lay hold of a word of his", or "lay hold of him by a word"; the Greek allows both (so also v26). τῇ ἀρχῇ καὶ τῇ ἐξουσίᾳ τοῦ ἡγεμόνος: ἀρχή, "rule", ἐξουσία, "authority" (the pair of 12:11).
- **"Master, we know that thou sayest and teachest rightly, neither acceptest thou the person of any, but teachest the way of God truly" → "Teacher, we know that you speak and teach rightly and do not receive a face, but in truth you teach the way of *Elohim*"** (v21) — Greek διδάσκαλε; ὀρθῶς λέγεις καὶ διδάσκεις; οὐ λαμβάνεις πρόσωπον, "you do not receive a face" (the Hebrew idiom for showing partiality, as Gal 2:6); ἐπ' ἀληθείας τὴν ὁδὸν τοῦ θεοῦ διδάσκεις, as Mark 12:14.
- **"Is it lawful for us to give tribute unto Caesar, or no?" → "Is it permitted for us to give tribute to Caesar or not?"** (v22) — Greek ἔξεστιν, "is it permitted"; φόρος, "tribute", the tax paid by a subject people (Mark 12:14 has κῆνσος, the poll tax).
- **"he perceived their craftiness, and said unto them, Why tempt ye me?" → "having observed their craftiness, he said to them"** (v23) — Greek κατανοήσας, "having observed, taken note of"; πανουργία, "craftiness" (Mark: "hypocrisy"). "Why tempt ye me?" (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"Shew me a penny. Whose image and superscription hath it? They answered and said" → "'Show me a denarius. Whose image and inscription does it have?' And they said"** (v24) — Greek δηνάριον, the Roman silver coin; εἰκών, "image"; ἐπιγραφή, "inscription". Editions: ἀποκριθέντες δὲ εἶπαν Treg RP (and the printed SBLGNT), "and answering they said" ] οἱ δὲ εἶπαν WH NA28 — 2–2 among the four editions, NA28 decides.
- **"Render therefore unto Caesar the things which be Caesar's, and unto God the things which be God's" → "Therefore render the things of Caesar to Caesar, and the things of *Elohim* to *Elohim*"** (v25) — Greek τοίνυν ἀπόδοτε τὰ Καίσαρος Καίσαρι καὶ τὰ τοῦ θεοῦ τῷ θεῷ: ἀποδίδωμι, "render, give back what is owed" (as Mark 12:17; Rom 13:7); τοίνυν, "therefore, well then", stands first (WH Treg NA28).
- **"they could not take hold of his words before the people: and they marvelled at his answer, and held their peace" → "they did not have strength to lay hold of a word of his before the people, and marveling at his answer they were silent"** (v26) — Greek οὐκ ἴσχυσαν, "had no strength"; ἐπιλαβέσθαι αὐτοῦ ῥήματος. Editions: τοῦ ῥήματος WH (and the printed SBLGNT), "lay hold of the word" ] αὐτοῦ ῥήματος Treg NA28 RP — "a word of his" (or "of him by a word", as v20). ῥῆμα, "word, thing said" (in v20 λόγος); ἐσίγησαν, "were silent".
- **"Then came to him certain of the Sadducees, which deny that there is any resurrection; and they asked him" → "And some of the *Tzedukim* approaching, those who say in contradiction that there is no resurrection, asked him"** (v27) — Greek οἱ ἀντιλέγοντες ἀνάστασιν μὴ εἶναι (NA28 RP; WH Treg λέγοντες, "who say" — 2–2 among the four editions, NA28 decides): ἀντιλέγω, "speak against, contradict", with μή: they contradict, saying there is none.
- **"If any man's brother die, having a wife, and he die without children, that his brother should take his wife" → "if someone's brother dies having a wife, and this one is childless, that his brother should take the wife"** (v28) — Greek καὶ οὗτος ἄτεκνος ᾖ (WH Treg NA28), "and this one be childless"; τὴν γυναῖκα, "the wife, the woman"; ἐξαναστήσῃ σπέρμα, "raise up seed" (Deut 25:5–6; Gen 38:8). γυνή is both "woman" and "wife" throughout v28–33.
- **"the first took a wife, and died without children" → "the first, having taken a wife, died childless"** (v29) — Greek λαβὼν γυναῖκα ἀπέθανεν ἄτεκνος.
- **"And the second took her to wife, and he died childless. And the third took her; and in like manner the seven also: and they left no children, and died" → "and the second and the third took her, and likewise also the seven did not leave behind children, and died"** (v30–31) — in the critical text v30 is only καὶ ὁ δεύτερος, "and the second"; the rest (RP) is not in WH Treg NA28. ὡσαύτως, "likewise"; οὐ κατέλιπον τέκνα, "did not leave behind children".
- **"Last of all the woman died also" → "Afterward the woman also died"** (v32) — Greek ὕστερον καὶ ἡ γυνὴ ἀπέθανεν (WH Treg NA28); "of all" (RP πάντων) is not in the critical text.
- **"Therefore in the resurrection whose wife of them is she? for seven had her to wife" → "The woman therefore, in the resurrection, of which of them does she become wife? For the seven had her as wife"** (v33) — Greek ἡ γυνὴ οὖν ἐν τῇ ἀναστάσει (WH NA28; Treg RP without "the woman"); γίνεται, "becomes", a present (Mark 12:23 ἔσται, "will be"); οἱ γὰρ ἑπτά, "the seven".
- **"Jesus answering said unto them, The children of this world marry" → "*Yeshua* said to them, 'The sons of this age marry'"** (v34) — "answering" (RP) is not in the critical text. Greek οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ αἰῶνος τούτου, "the sons of this age" (as 16:8); γαμίσκονται, "are given in marriage".
- **"they which shall be accounted worthy to obtain that world" → "those counted worthy to attain that age"** (v35) — Greek οἱ καταξιωθέντες, a past participle (as Acts 5:41; 2 Thess 1:5); τυχεῖν, "attain"; τοῦ αἰῶνος ἐκείνου, "that age"; τῆς ἀναστάσεως τῆς ἐκ νεκρῶν, "the resurrection, the one from among the dead".
- **"Neither can they die any more: for they are equal unto the angels; and are the children of God, being the children of the resurrection" → "for neither are they able to die any more, for they are equal to angels, and they are sons of *Elohim*, being sons of the resurrection"** (v36) — Greek οὐδὲ γὰρ … ἰσάγγελοι γάρ, "for" twice; ἰσάγγελοι, one word, "angel-equal" (only here; Mark 12:25: "as angels"); υἱοί … θεοῦ, "sons of God", without articles.
- **"Moses shewed at the bush, when he calleth the Lord the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" → "*Moshe* disclosed at the bush, as he calls *Yahweh* the *Elohim* of *Avraham* and *Elohim* of *Yitzchaq* and *Elohim* of *Yaaqov*"** (v37) — Greek ἐμήνυσεν, "disclosed, made known" (as Acts 23:30); ἐπὶ τῆς βάτου, "at the bush", that is, in the passage about the bush (Exod 3); ὡς λέγει κύριον, "as he calls (the) Lord": κύριος, without the article, stands for the name Yahweh of Exod 3:15. The article stands only before the first "God" (WH Treg NA28).
- **"For he is not a God of the dead, but of the living: for all live unto him" → "But he is not *Elohim* of the dead, but of the living, for all live to him"** (v38) — Greek θεὸς δὲ οὐκ ἔστιν νεκρῶν ἀλλὰ ζώντων, δέ, "but"; without articles: "he is not God of dead ones but of living ones", or "God is not of dead ones". πάντες γὰρ αὐτῷ ζῶσιν, "for all live to him (for him, in his sight)", a clause only Luke has.
- **"Master, thou hast well said" → "Teacher, you spoke well"** (v39) — Greek διδάσκαλε, καλῶς εἶπας, a simple past.
- **"And after that they durst not ask him any question at all" → "For they no longer were daring to ask him anything"** (v40) — Greek οὐκέτι γὰρ ἐτόλμων, "for" (WH Treg NA28; RP "and"), an imperfect; ἐπερωτᾶν αὐτὸν οὐδέν.
- **"How say they that Christ is David's son?" → "How do they say that the *Mashiach* is David's son?"** (v41) — Greek πῶς λέγουσιν τὸν χριστὸν εἶναι Δαυὶδ υἱόν, with "David" before "son" (WH NA28).
- **"And David himself saith … The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand, Till I make thine enemies thy footstool" → "For David himself says … '*Yahweh* said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand, until I set your enemies as a footstool of your feet"'"** (v42–43) — Greek αὐτὸς γὰρ Δαυίδ, "for" (WH NA28; Treg RP "and"). The quotation is Ps 110:1 in the Greek, word for word as Acts 2:34–35: κύριος without the article stands for Yahweh (Hebrew נְאֻם יְהוָה לַאדֹנִי); ἐκ δεξιῶν μου, "at my right"; ἕως ἂν θῶ … ὑποπόδιον τῶν ποδῶν σου, "until I set … a footstool of your feet" (Mark 12:36 has ὑποκάτω, "beneath").
- **"David therefore calleth him Lord, how is he then his son?" → "David therefore calls him Lord, and how is he his son?"** (v44) — Greek καὶ πῶς αὐτοῦ υἱός ἐστιν, "and how".
- **"in the audience of all the people he said unto his disciples" → "as all the people were hearing, he said to the disciples"** (v45) — Greek ἀκούοντος δὲ παντὸς τοῦ λαοῦ. Editions: μαθηταῖς WH Treg (and the printed SBLGNT) ] + αὐτοῦ NA28 RP, "his disciples" — the verse follows the majority.
- **"Beware of the scribes, which desire to walk in long robes, and love greetings in the markets, and the highest seats in the synagogues, and the chief rooms at feasts" → "Take heed of the scribes, who want to walk about in robes and have affection for greetings in the marketplaces and first seats in the synagogues and first couches at the suppers"** (v46) — Greek προσέχετε ἀπό, "take heed of" (as 12:1; Mark 12:38 has βλέπετε ἀπό, "beware of"); ἐν στολαῖς, "in robes": "long" is not in the Greek (the στολή is a long, stately robe); φιλούντων, "having affection for" (φιλέω, not ἀγαπάω "love"), a word Luke adds to Mark's sentence; πρωτοκαθεδρίας, πρωτοκλισίας, "first seats", "first couches" (the places of honor at table); δείπνοις, "suppers".
- **"Which devour widows' houses, and for a shew make long prayers: the same shall receive greater damnation" → "who devour the houses of the widows and for a pretext pray long; these will receive a more abundant sentence"** (v47) — Greek προφάσει, "for a pretext"; μακρὰ προσεύχονται; περισσότερον κρίμα: κρίμα is the sentence of a judge, περισσότερον "more abundant", as Mark 12:40.

### Notes
- **v17** quotes Ps 118:22.
- **v37** echoes Ex 3:6.
- **v42-43** quotes Ps 110:1.
