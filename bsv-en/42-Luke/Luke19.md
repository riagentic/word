# Luke 19

[1] And having entered, he was passing through ⟦jericho|Yericho|Jericho⟧.

[2] And behold, a man called by name ⟦zacchaeus|Zakkai|Zacchaeus⟧, and he was a chief toll collector, and he was rich;

[3] and he was seeking to see ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, who he is, and he was not able because of the crowd, for he was small in stature.

[4] And running ahead to the front, he went up on a sycamore tree that he might see him, because he was about to pass through that way.

[5] And when he came to the place, ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, looking up, said to him, "⟦zacchaeus|Zakkai|Zacchaeus⟧, make haste and come down, for today I must remain in your house."

[6] And he made haste and came down, and received him, rejoicing.

[7] And seeing it, all were murmuring, saying, "He has gone in to take lodging with a sinful man."

[8] But ⟦zacchaeus|Zakkai|Zacchaeus⟧, standing, said to the Lord, "Behold, the half of what belongs to me, Lord, I give to the poor, and if I took anything from anyone by false accusation, I repay fourfold."

[9] And ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ said to him, "Today salvation has come to this house, because he also is a son of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧;

[10] **for the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which is lost.**"

[11] And as they were hearing these things, he went on and spoke a parable, because he was near ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧ and they thought that the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is about to come into sight instantly.

[12] He said therefore, "A certain man of noble birth went into a far country to receive for himself a kingdom and to return.

[13] And having called ten slaves of his own, he gave them ten minas and said to them, 'Do business while I am coming.'

[14] But his citizens were hating him, and they sent an embassy after him, saying, 'We do not want this one to reign over us.'

[15] And it came to pass, when he came back, having received the kingdom, that he said that these slaves to whom he had given the silver be called to him, that he might know what they had gained by doing business.

[16] And the first arrived, saying, 'Lord, your mina worked ten minas besides.'

[17] And he said to him, 'Well done, good slave! Because in a very little you proved faithful, be one having authority over ten cities.'

[18] And the second came, saying, 'Your mina, Lord, made five minas.'

[19] And he said to this one also, 'And you, be over five cities.'

[20] And the other came, saying, 'Lord, behold, your mina, which I was keeping laid away in a kerchief;

[21] for I was fearing you, because you are an austere man: you take up what you did not lay down, and you reap what you did not sow.'

[22] He says to him, 'Out of your mouth I judge you, evil slave! You knew that I am an austere man, taking up what I did not lay down and reaping what I did not sow?

[23] And why did you not give my silver upon a bankers' table? And I, having come, would have exacted it with interest.'

[24] And to those standing by he said, 'Take the mina from him, and give it to the one who has the ten minas' —

[25] and they said to him, 'Lord, he has ten minas' —

[26] 'I say to you that to everyone who has, it will be given, but from the one who does not have, even what he has will be taken away.

[27] Yet these enemies of mine, who did not want me to reign over them, bring here and slaughter them before me.'"

[28] And having said these things, he was going on in front, going up to ⟦jerusalem|Yerushalayim|Jerusalem⟧.

[29] And it came to pass, when he drew near to ⟦bethphage|Beit Pagei|Bethphage⟧ and ⟦bethany|Bethania|Bethany⟧, at the mount that is called ⟦mount-of-olives|HaZeitim|Olivet⟧, that he sent two of the disciples,

[30] saying, "Go into the village opposite, in which, entering, you will find a colt tied, upon which no one of men ever sat; and having loosed it, bring it.

[31] And if anyone asks you, 'Why are you loosing it?' thus you shall say: 'The Lord has need of it.'"

[32] And those who had been sent, going away, found even as he said to them.

[33] And as they were loosing the colt, its lords said to them, "Why are you loosing the colt?"

[34] And they said, "The Lord has need of it."

[35] And they brought it to ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧, and casting their garments upon the colt, they set ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ on it.

[36] And as he was going, they were spreading their own garments under him in the way.

[37] And as he was now drawing near, at the descent of ⟦mount-of-olives|Har HaZeitim|the Mount of Olives⟧, all the multitude of the disciples began, rejoicing, to praise ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ with a loud voice concerning all the powers which they saw,

[38] saying, "**Blessed is the one coming, the King, in the name of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧!** In heaven peace, and glory in the highest!"

[39] And some of the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ from the crowd said to him, "Teacher, rebuke your disciples."

[40] And answering, he said, "I say to you, if these will be silent, the stones will cry out."

[41] And when he drew near, seeing the city, he wept over it,

[42] saying, "If you had known in this day, even you, the things pertaining to peace — but now they were hidden from your eyes.

[43] For days will come upon you, and your enemies will throw up a palisade against you and will encircle you and will hold you in from every side,

[44] and they will dash you to the ground, and your children within you, and they will not leave stone upon stone in you, because you did not know the time of your visitation."

[45] And entering into the temple, he began to cast out those selling,

[46] saying to them, "It is written, '**And my house shall be a house of prayer**,' but you made it **a cave of robbers**."

[47] And he was teaching day by day in the temple; but the high priests and the scribes were seeking to destroy him, and the first men of the people,

[48] and they were not finding what they might do, for all the people were hanging upon him, hearing.

## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (throughout) — Hebrew name preserved. In v1 the Greek has no name (KJV: "Jesus entered").
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v11, 37) — divine name retained.
- **"den of thieves" → "cave of robbers"** (v46) — Greek σπήλαιον λῃστῶν, "a cave of robbers" (λῃστής, a robber, not κλέπτης "thief"), as Mark 11:17.
- **"entered and passed through Jericho" → "having entered, he was passing through *Yericho*"** (v1) — Greek εἰσελθὼν διήρχετο, an imperfect: he was on his way through the city.
- **"there was a man named Zacchaeus, which was the chief among the publicans" → "a man called by name *Zakkai*, and he was a chief toll collector"** (v2) — Greek ἀνὴρ ὀνόματι καλούμενος Ζακχαῖος, without a verb; ἀρχιτελώνης, one word, "chief toll collector" (τελώνης with ἀρχι-, as in ἀρχιερεύς), found only here. καὶ αὐτός stands twice.
- **"he sought to see Jesus who he was; and could not for the press, because he was little of stature" → "he was seeking to see *Yeshua*, who he is, and he was not able because of the crowd, for he was small in stature"** (v3) — Greek ἐζήτει, ἠδύνατο, imperfects; τίς ἐστιν, a present; ἀπὸ τοῦ ὄχλου, literally "from the crowd"; ἡλικία here is bodily stature ("span of life" in 12:25).
- **"he ran before, and climbed up into a sycomore tree … for he was to pass that way" → "running ahead to the front, he went up on a sycamore tree … because he was about to pass through that way"** (v4) — Greek προδραμὼν εἰς τὸ ἔμπροσθεν (WH Treg NA28); ἀνέβη ἐπί; συκομορέα, the sycamore fig (a different word from συκάμινος, 17:6); ἤμελλεν διέρχεσθαι (the verb of v1).
- **"he looked up, and saw him, and said … make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house" → "looking up, said … make haste and come down, for today I must remain in your house"** (v5) — "and saw him" (RP) is not in the critical text. Greek σπεύσας κατάβηθι, "hastening, come down"; δεῖ με μεῖναι, the verb μένω ("abide" of persons), here of a stay in a place: "remain".
- **"received him joyfully" → "received him, rejoicing"** (v6) — Greek ὑπεδέξατο αὐτὸν χαίρων (ὑποδέχομαι, to receive under one's roof, as Acts 17:7).
- **"they all murmured, saying, That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner" → "all were murmuring, saying, 'He has gone in to take lodging with a sinful man'"** (v7) — Greek διεγόγγυζον, an imperfect of διαγογγύζω (γογγύζω "murmur" with δια-: murmuring through the crowd, as 15:2); καταλῦσαι, "to take lodging" (literally "unloose", unyoke for the night; "tear down" elsewhere); παρὰ ἁμαρτωλῷ ἀνδρί, "with a man, a sinner".
- **"Zacchaeus stood … the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold" → "*Zakkai*, standing … the half of what belongs to me, Lord, I give to the poor, and if I took anything from anyone by false accusation, I repay fourfold"** (v8) — Greek σταθείς (as 18:11, 40); τὰ ἡμίσιά μου τῶν ὑπαρχόντων (τὰ ὑπάρχοντα, "what belongs to" one); εἴ τινός τι ἐσυκοφάντησα: συκοφαντέω, "accuse falsely, extort by false accusation" (as 3:14), with εἰ and a past tense: the case is taken as real. δίδωμι, ἀποδίδωμι are presents: "I give, I repay" — a resolve taken now, or what he already does; the Greek allows both. ἀποδίδωμι is "repay, give back"; τετραπλοῦν, "fourfold" (Exod 22:1).
- **"This day is salvation come to this house, forsomuch as he also is a son of Abraham" → "Today salvation has come to this house, because he also is a son of *Avraham*"** (v9) — Greek σωτηρία … ἐγένετο, "salvation came to be for this house"; καθότι, "because, inasmuch as" (as Acts 2:24). εἶπεν πρὸς αὐτόν is "said to him"; it can also be taken "said with regard to him", since the words speak of him in the third person.
- **"the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" → "the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which is lost"** (v10) — Greek ἦλθεν, "came"; τὸ ἀπολωλός, a neuter perfect participle: "that which has been lost and is lost" (ἀπόλλυμι, as 15:4, 6, 24).
- **"as they heard these things, he added and spake a parable, because he was nigh to Jerusalem, and because they thought that the kingdom of God should immediately appear" → "as they were hearing these things, he went on and spoke a parable, because he was near *Yerushalayim* and they thought that the kingdom of *Elohim* is about to come into sight instantly"** (v11) — Greek προσθεὶς εἶπεν, literally "adding, he said", a Hebrew turn for going on to do something (as 20:11, 12; Acts 12:3); παραχρῆμα, "instantly"; μέλλει, a present; ἀναφαίνεσθαι, "to be brought to light, come into sight" (as Acts 21:3, "having sighted"): not ἐπιφαίνω "appear" nor φανερόω "manifest".
- **"A certain nobleman" → "A certain man of noble birth"** (v12) — Greek ἄνθρωπός τις εὐγενής (εὐγενής, "well-born", as Acts 17:11; 1 Cor 1:26).
- **"he called his ten servants, and delivered them ten pounds, and said unto them, Occupy till I come" → "having called ten slaves of his own, he gave them ten minas and said to them, 'Do business while I am coming'"** (v13) — Greek δέκα δούλους ἑαυτοῦ, "ten slaves of his", not "his ten"; μνᾶ, the mina, a sum of a hundred drachmas (a sixtieth of a talent), kept as "mina"; πραγματεύσασθε, "do business, trade"; ἐν ᾧ ἔρχομαι (WH Treg NA28), literally "in (the time in) which I am coming": while I am on my way and back; RP has ἕως, "until".
- **"hated him, and sent a message after him, saying, We will not have this man to reign over us" → "were hating him, and they sent an embassy after him, saying, 'We do not want this one to reign over us'"** (v14) — Greek ἐμίσουν, an imperfect; πρεσβεία, "an embassy" (as 14:32); οὐ θέλομεν τοῦτον βασιλεῦσαι.
- **"when he was returned … then he commanded these servants to be called unto him, to whom he had given the money, that he might know how much every man had gained by trading" → "when he came back … that he said that these slaves to whom he had given the silver be called to him, that he might know what they had gained by doing business"** (v15) — Greek ἐν τῷ ἐπανελθεῖν αὐτόν (ἐπανέρχομαι, as 10:35); εἶπεν φωνηθῆναι, "he said (for them) to be called"; τὸ ἀργύριον, "the silver", that is, the money; τί διεπραγματεύσαντο (WH Treg NA28), "what they had gained by doing business", plural; RP has "who had gained what".
- **"Then came the first, saying, Lord, thy pound hath gained ten pounds" → "And the first arrived, saying, 'Lord, your mina worked ten minas besides'"** (v16) — Greek παρεγένετο, "arrived"; προσηργάσατο, "worked in addition" (ἐργάζομαι "work" with προσ-): it is the mina that did the work. In v18 ἐποίησεν, "made".
- **"Well, thou good servant: because thou hast been faithful in a very little, have thou authority" → "Well done, good slave! Because in a very little you proved faithful, be one having authority"** (v17) — Greek εὖγε (WH Treg NA28; RP and Matt 25:21 εὖ); ἐν ἐλαχίστῳ, "in a least thing" (as 16:10); πιστὸς ἐγένου, "you became, proved faithful"; ἴσθι ἐξουσίαν ἔχων, "be having authority".
- **"Be thou also over five cities" → "And you, be over five cities"** (v19) — Greek καὶ σὺ ἐπάνω γίνου πέντε πόλεων.
- **"another came … here is thy pound, which I have kept laid up in a napkin" → "the other came … your mina, which I was keeping laid away in a kerchief"** (v20) — Greek ὁ ἕτερος, "the other", with the article (WH Treg NA28); εἶχον ἀποκειμένην, "I was having (it) laid away"; σουδάριον, a cloth for the face or neck (as John 11:44; 20:7). "here is" is not in the Greek.
- **"I feared thee, because thou art an austere man: thou takest up that thou layedst not down" → "I was fearing you, because you are an austere man: you take up what you did not lay down"** (v21) — Greek ἐφοβούμην, an imperfect; αὐστηρός, "austere, harsh, exacting" (Matt 25:24 has σκληρός, "hard"); αἴρεις ὃ οὐκ ἔθηκας.
- **"Out of thine own mouth will I judge thee, thou wicked servant" → "Out of your mouth I judge you, evil slave"** (v22) — Greek κρίνω, printed with the accent of a present, "I judge"; with another accent (κρινῶ) it is a future, "I will judge": the old manuscripts have no accents. πονηρὲ δοῦλε, "evil slave". λέγει, "he says", a present. ᾔδεις …; is printed as a question; it can also be read as a statement.
- **"Wherefore then gavest not thou my money into the bank, that at my coming I might have required mine own with usury?" → "And why did you not give my silver upon a bankers' table? And I, having come, would have exacted it with interest"** (v23) — Greek ἔδωκας … ἐπὶ τράπεζαν, "gave … upon a table": τράπεζα, "table" (as Mark 11:15), is the money changers' and bankers' table, hence a bank; "bankers'" is supplied to say which table. κἀγὼ ἐλθὼν σὺν τόκῳ ἂν αὐτὸ ἔπραξα: πράσσω here is "exact, collect" (as 3:13); τόκος, "interest". The second clause is a statement, not a purpose.
- **"Take from him the pound" → "Take the mina from him"** (v24–25) — the sentence of the king is broken off by the bystanders (v25) and goes on in v26; the dashes are those of the printed text.
- **"For I say unto you, That unto every one which hath shall be given; and from him that hath not, even that he hath shall be taken away from him" → "I say to you that to everyone who has, it will be given, but from the one who does not have, even what he has will be taken away"** (v26) — "For" (Treg RP γάρ) and the final "from him" (Treg RP) are not in WH NA28.
- **"But those mine enemies, which would not that I should reign over them, bring hither, and slay them before me" → "Yet these enemies of mine, who did not want me to reign over them, bring here and slaughter them before me"** (v27) — Greek πλήν, "yet"; τούτους, "these" (WH Treg NA28); κατασφάξατε, "slaughter, cut down" (σφάζω with κατα-).
- **"when he had thus spoken, he went before, ascending up to Jerusalem" → "having said these things, he was going on in front, going up to *Yerushalayim*"** (v28) — Greek ἐπορεύετο ἔμπροσθεν, an imperfect; ἀναβαίνων.
- **"at the mount called the mount of Olives, he sent two of his disciples" → "at the mount that is called *HaZeitim*, that he sent two of the disciples"** (v29) — Greek τὸ ὄρος τὸ καλούμενον Ἐλαιῶν: with the printed accent, "the mount called 'of Olives'"; with another accent (Ἐλαιών) it is a name, "Olive Grove, Olivet" (as Acts 1:12, Ἐλαιῶνος). In v37 the full name stands, τὸ Ὄρος τῶν Ἐλαιῶν. "his" (Treg RP) is not in WH NA28.
- **"Go ye into the village over against you; in the which at your entering ye shall find a colt tied, whereon yet never man sat: loose him, and bring him hither" → "Go into the village opposite, in which, entering, you will find a colt tied, upon which no one of men ever sat; and having loosed it, bring it"** (v30) — Greek εἰς τὴν κατέναντι κώμην, without "you"; οὐδεὶς πώποτε ἀνθρώπων, "no one of men ever"; λύσαντες αὐτὸν ἀγάγετε (Mark 11:2: "loose it and bring it"). πῶλος is the young of a horse or a donkey.
- **"Why do ye loose him? thus shall ye say unto him, Because the Lord hath need of him" → "'Why are you loosing it?' thus you shall say: 'The Lord has need of it'"** (v31, 34) — "unto him" (RP) is not in the critical text. Greek ὅτι Ὁ κύριος αὐτοῦ χρείαν ἔχει, the words of Mark 11:3: ὅτι introduces the words spoken, or it is "because", answering "why". Also possible: "its lord has need", since αὐτοῦ can go with κύριος (so "its lords", v33).
- **"the owners thereof" → "its lords"** (v33) — Greek οἱ κύριοι αὐτοῦ, the plural of the word "lord" in v31, 34.
- **"they cast their garments upon the colt, and they set Jesus thereon" kept as "casting their garments upon the colt, they set *Yeshua* on it"** (v35) — Greek ἐπιρίψαντες (as 1 Pet 5:7; Mark 11:7 has ἐπιβάλλουσιν, "throw upon"); ἐπεβίβασαν, "made to mount" (as Acts 23:24).
- **"as he went, they spread their clothes in the way" → "as he was going, they were spreading their own garments under him in the way"** (v36) — Greek ὑπεστρώννυον, an imperfect, "were spreading under"; τὰ ἱμάτια ἑαυτῶν (WH Treg, the printed text; NA28 RP αὐτῶν, "their").
- **"when he was come nigh, even now at the descent of the mount of Olives, the whole multitude of the disciples began to rejoice and praise God … for all the mighty works that they had seen" → "as he was now drawing near, at the descent of *Har HaZeitim*, all the multitude of the disciples began, rejoicing, to praise *Elohim* … concerning all the powers which they saw"** (v37) — Greek ἐγγίζοντος … ἤδη; χαίροντες αἰνεῖν, "rejoicing, to praise"; περὶ πασῶν ὧν εἶδον δυνάμεων: δυνάμεις, "powers", that is, deeds of power.
- **"Blessed be the King that cometh in the name of the Lord: peace in heaven" → "Blessed is the one coming, the King, in the name of *Yahweh*! In heaven peace"** (v38) — Editions: βασιλεὺς Treg RP (and the printed SBLGNT), "the coming King" ] ὁ βασιλεὺς WH NA28 — 2–2 among the four editions, NA28 decides: ὁ ἐρχόμενος ὁ βασιλεύς, "the one coming, the King". The words are Ps 118:26 (εὐλογημένος ὁ ἐρχόμενος ἐν ὀνόματι κυρίου, as Mark 11:9), where κύριος stands for the name Yahweh; "the King" is added to the psalm's words. ἐν οὐρανῷ εἰρήνη, "in heaven peace" (WH Treg NA28 order). Luke has no "Hosanna".
- **"from among the multitude said unto him, Master" → "from the crowd said to him, 'Teacher'"** (v39) — Greek ἀπὸ τοῦ ὄχλου; διδάσκαλε.
- **"I tell you that, if these should hold their peace, the stones would immediately cry out" → "I say to you, if these will be silent, the stones will cry out"** (v40) — Greek ἐὰν … σιωπήσουσιν, a future after ἐάν; κράξουσιν, "will cry out" (WH Treg NA28). "that" (ὅτι Treg RP) is not in WH NA28; "immediately" is not in the Greek; "unto them" (Treg RP) is not in WH NA28.
- **"he beheld the city, and wept over it" → "seeing the city, he wept over it"** (v41) — Greek ἰδών; ἔκλαυσεν (κλαίω, "weep").
- **"If thou hadst known, even thou, at least in this thy day, the things which belong unto thy peace! but now they are hid from thine eyes" → "If you had known in this day, even you, the things pertaining to peace — but now they were hidden from your eyes"** (v42) — Greek εἰ ἔγνως ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ταύτῃ καὶ σύ (WH NA28): "at least" and "thy day" (RP) are not in the critical text, nor "thy" with "peace" (Treg RP σου). The sentence is broken off without its main clause. τὰ πρὸς εἰρήνην, "the things toward, pertaining to peace" (as 14:32); ἐκρύβη, a past passive, "were hidden" (KJV: "are hid").
- **"the days shall come upon thee, that thine enemies shall cast a trench about thee, and compass thee round, and keep thee in on every side" → "days will come upon you, and your enemies will throw up a palisade against you and will encircle you and will hold you in from every side"** (v43) — Greek ἥξουσιν ἡμέραι, without the article; καί, "and"; παρεμβαλοῦσιν … χάρακά σοι (WH Treg NA28): χάραξ is a stake, and then a palisade or rampart of stakes, the siege-work of an army; συνέξουσιν, "hold in, press together" (συνέχω).
- **"shall lay thee even with the ground, and thy children within thee; and they shall not leave in thee one stone upon another" → "they will dash you to the ground, and your children within you, and they will not leave stone upon stone in you"** (v44) — Greek ἐδαφιοῦσίν σε καὶ τὰ τέκνα σου: ἐδαφίζω, from ἔδαφος "ground": to level a city to the ground and to dash persons to the ground (Ps 137:9 in the Greek); one verb for both objects. λίθον ἐπὶ λίθον, as Mark 13:2. ἀνθ' ὧν, "because" (as Acts 12:23); ἐπισκοπή, "visitation" (as 1 Pet 2:12).
- **"began to cast out them that sold therein, and them that bought" → "began to cast out those selling"** (v45) — "therein, and them that bought" (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"My house is the house of prayer: but ye have made it" → "And my house shall be a house of prayer … but you made it"** (v46) — Greek καὶ ἔσται ὁ οἶκός μου οἶκος προσευχῆς (WH Treg NA28; RP has no "and … shall be" and adds ἐστίν, "is"): the Greek of Isa 56:7 has "shall be called", as Mark 11:17. ἐποιήσατε, a simple past (Mark: "have made").
- **"he taught daily in the temple. But the chief priests and the scribes and the chief of the people sought to destroy him" → "he was teaching day by day in the temple; but the high priests and the scribes were seeking to destroy him, and the first men of the people"** (v47) — Greek ἦν διδάσκων τὸ καθ' ἡμέραν; ἀρχιερεῖς, "high priests"; ἐζήτουν, an imperfect; καὶ οἱ πρῶτοι τοῦ λαοῦ, "and the first of the people", stands after the verb, as an afterthought.
- **"could not find what they might do: for all the people were very attentive to hear him" → "they were not finding what they might do, for all the people were hanging upon him, hearing"** (v48) — Greek οὐχ εὕρισκον τὸ τί ποιήσωσιν; ὁ λαὸς … ἅπας ἐξεκρέματο αὐτοῦ ἀκούων, literally "all the people hung from him, hearing": ἐκκρεμάννυμι, "hang from", found only here.

### Notes
- **v38** echoes Ps 118:26.
- **v46** quotes Isa 56:7 + Jer 7:11.
