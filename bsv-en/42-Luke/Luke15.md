# Luke 15

[1] Now all the toll collectors and the sinners were drawing near to him to hear him.

[2] And both the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧ and the scribes were murmuring, saying, "This one receives sinners and eats with them."

[3] But he said to them this parable, saying,

[4] "What man of you, having a hundred sheep and having lost one of them, does not leave behind the ninety-nine in the wilderness and go after the lost one until he finds it?

[5] And having found it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing,

[6] and coming into the house, he calls together the friends and the neighbors, saying to them, 'Rejoice with me, because I found my sheep, the lost one.'

[7] I say to you that so there will be joy in heaven over one sinner repenting, more than over ninety-nine righteous ones who have no need of repentance.

[8] "Or what woman, having ten drachmas, if she loses one drachma, does not light a lamp and sweep the house and seek carefully until she finds it?

[9] And having found it, she calls together the women who are her friends and neighbors, saying, 'Rejoice with me, because I found the drachma which I lost.'

[10] So, I say to you, there comes joy before the angels of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ over one sinner repenting."

[11] And he said, "A certain man had two sons.

[12] And the younger of them said to the father, 'Father, give me the share of the substance that falls to me.' And he distributed to them the livelihood.

[13] And after not many days, the younger son, having gathered all together, went away from home into a far country, and there he scattered his substance, living dissolutely.

[14] And when he had spent all, there came a strong famine throughout that country, and he himself began to be in lack.

[15] And going, he was joined to one of the citizens of that country, and he sent him into his fields to feed pigs.

[16] And he was desiring to be filled from the pods which the pigs were eating, and no one was giving to him.

[17] "But coming to himself, he said, 'How many hired men of my father abound in loaves, but I am perishing here with famine!

[18] Rising, I will go to my father and I will say to him, "Father, I sinned against heaven and before you;

[19] I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Make me as one of your hired men."'

[20] And rising, he came to his own father. But while he was still far off, his father saw him and was moved with compassion, and running, fell upon his neck and kissed him.

[21] And the son said to him, 'Father, I sinned against heaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son.'

[22] But the father said to his slaves, 'Quickly bring out a robe, the first one, and put it on him, and give a ring for his hand and sandals for the feet,

[23] and bring the fattened calf, kill it, and let us eat and make merry,

[24] because this son of mine was dead and came to life again, he was lost and was found.' And they began to make merry.

[25] "Now his elder son was in a field; and as, coming, he drew near to the house, he heard music and dances,

[26] and calling to himself one of the boys, he was inquiring what these things might be.

[27] And he said to him, 'Your brother has come, and your father killed the fattened calf, because he received him back healthy.'

[28] But he was wrathful and did not want to go in. And his father, coming out, was appealing to him.

[29] But answering, he said to his father, 'Behold, so many years I am a slave to you, and I never passed by a commandment of yours, and to me you never gave a kid, that I might make merry with my friends;

[30] but when this son of yours came, who devoured your livelihood with prostitutes, you killed for him the fattened calf.'

[31] But he said to him, 'Child, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours;

[32] but it was necessary to make merry and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and lived, and was lost and was found.'"

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v10) — divine name retained; "joy before the angels of *Elohim*."
- **"publicans" → "toll collectors"** (v1) — Greek τελῶναι.
- **"murmured" → "were murmuring"; "This man receiveth" → "This one receives"** (v2) — Greek διεγόγγυζον, an imperfect and a strengthened form of γογγύζω ("murmur"); οὗτος, with no "man".
- **"if he lose one of them" → "having lost one of them"; "leave" → "leave behind"; "that which is lost" → "the lost one"** (v4, 6) — Greek ἀπολέσας (WH, NA28, Byzantine; Tregelles ἀπολέσῃ, "if he loses"); καταλείπει; τὸ ἀπολωλός. "Lose", "lost" and "perish" (v17) are one verb, ἀπόλλυμι.
- **"when he cometh home" → "coming into the house"; "his friends" → "the friends"** (v6) — Greek εἰς τὸν οἶκον; τοὺς φίλους καὶ τοὺς γείτονας, with no "his".
- **"joy shall be in heaven … more than over" → "there will be joy in heaven … more than over"** (v7) — Greek χαρὰ … ἢ ἐπί, "joy … than over": the Greek has no word for "more"; it is supplied, because "than" cannot stand alone in English. μετανοοῦντι is a present participle, "repenting".
- **"ten pieces of silver" → "ten drachmas"** (v8, 9) — Greek δραχμή, the coin name kept; a drachma was about a day's wage.
- **"diligently" → "carefully"; "her friends and her neighbours" → "the women who are her friends and neighbors"** (v8, 9) — Greek ἐπιμελῶς; τὰς φίλας καὶ γείτονας, feminine: English has no feminine form, so "women" is added.
- **"there is joy in the presence of" → "there comes joy before"** (v10) — Greek γίνεται χαρὰ ἐνώπιον.
- **"the portion of goods that falleth to me. And he divided unto them his living" → "the share of the substance that falls to me. And he distributed to them the livelihood"** (v12) — Greek τὸ ἐπιβάλλον μέρος τῆς οὐσίας; διεῖλεν αὐτοῖς τὸν βίον. Two words: οὐσία, "substance, what one has" (v12, 13), and βίος, "livelihood, what one lives on" (v12, 30).
- **"took his journey … wasted his substance with riotous living" → "went away from home … scattered his substance, living dissolutely"** (v13) — Greek ἀπεδήμησεν; διεσκόρπισεν, "scattered" (as Matt 25:24); ζῶν ἀσώτως, an adverb from ἄσωτος, "unsaved, past saving": a wasteful, abandoned life (the noun ἀσωτία, "dissoluteness", Eph 5:18; Titus 1:6). The verse does not say in what the dissoluteness lay; v30 is the brother's word.
- **"a mighty famine … to be in want" → "a strong famine … to be in lack"** (v14) — Greek λιμὸς ἰσχυρά; ὑστερεῖσθαι.
- **"joined himself to a citizen" → "was joined to one of the citizens"** (v15) — Greek ἐκολλήθη, "was glued to, clung to"; nothing is said of hiring. "Swine" → "pigs", as Mark 5:11.
- **"he would fain have filled his belly with the husks" → "he was desiring to be filled from the pods"** (v16) — Greek ἐπεθύμει χορτασθῆναι ἐκ τῶν κερατίων (WH, NA28); Tregelles and the Byzantine text read γεμίσαι τὴν κοιλίαν αὐτοῦ ἀπό, "to fill his belly from". κεράτια, "little horns": the pods of the carob tree.
- **"hired servants … have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger" → "hired men … abound in loaves, but I am perishing here with famine"** (v17, 19) — Greek μίσθιοι; περισσεύονται ἄρτων; λιμῷ, the word of v14; ὧδε "here" (WH, NA28, Tregelles; not in the Byzantine text).
- **"I will arise and go" → "Rising, I will go"; "I have sinned" → "I sinned"** (v18, 20, 21) — Greek ἀναστὰς πορεύσομαι; ἥμαρτον, an aorist. εἰς τὸν οὐρανόν, "against heaven" (literally "unto heaven"): heaven stands for God.
- **v21** — WH alone adds "make me as one of your hired men" from v19; Tregelles, NA28 and the Byzantine text do not have it.
- **"had compassion" → "was moved with compassion"; "fell on his neck" → "fell upon his neck"** (v20) — Greek ἐσπλαγχνίσθη, from σπλάγχνα, "inward parts"; ἐπέπεσεν ἐπὶ τὸν τράχηλον, as Acts 20:37; κατεφίλησεν, a strengthened form of "kiss".
- **"servants" → "slaves"; "Bring forth the best robe" → "Quickly bring out a robe, the first one"; "shoes" → "sandals"** (v22) — Greek δούλους; ταχύ (WH, Tregelles, NA28; not in the Byzantine text); στολὴν τὴν πρώτην, "the first", that is, first in rank; ὑποδήματα.
- **"be merry" → "make merry"; "is alive again … is found" → "came to life again … was found"** (v23, 24, 29, 32) — Greek εὐφραίνομαι; ἀνέζησεν, εὑρέθη, aorists. In v32 the verb is the simple ἔζησεν, "lived" (WH, Tregelles, NA28; the Byzantine text repeats ἀνέζησεν), here "came to life".
- **"musick and dancing" → "music and dances"** (v25) — Greek συμφωνίας καὶ χορῶν; συμφωνία is a sounding together (of instruments or voices), χοροί bands of dancers or dances.
- **"one of the servants" → "one of the boys"** (v26) — Greek παίδων, "boys", used of young servants; a different word from "slaves" (v22) and "hired men" (v17). ἐπυνθάνετο, "was inquiring".
- **"safe and sound" → "healthy"** (v27) — Greek ὑγιαίνοντα.
- **"he was angry, and would not go in … intreated him" → "he was wrathful and did not want to go in … was appealing to him"** (v28) — Greek ὠργίσθη; οὐκ ἤθελεν; παρεκάλει, an imperfect.
- **"do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment" → "I am a slave to you, and I never passed by a commandment of yours"** (v29) — Greek δουλεύω σοι; ἐντολήν σου παρῆλθον.
- **"thy living with harlots" → "your livelihood with prostitutes"** (v30) — Greek τὸν βίον; πορνῶν.
- **"Son" → "Child"** (v31) — Greek τέκνον, not υἱός as in the rest of the parable.
- **"It was meet that we should make merry" → "it was necessary to make merry"** (v32) — Greek ἔδει, "it was necessary"; the Greek does not say who had to ("we" is not in the text).

### Notes
- **v4–7** — compare Matt 18:12–14, in different Greek ("goes astray", "upon the mountains").
