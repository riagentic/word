# Luke 16

[1] And he was saying also to the disciples, "A certain man was rich, who had a steward, and this one was accused to him as scattering what belonged to him.

[2] And calling him, he said to him, 'What is this I hear about you? Render the account of your stewardship, for you can no longer be steward.'

[3] And the steward said within himself, 'What shall I do, because my lord is taking away the stewardship from me? To dig I do not have strength; to beg I am ashamed.

[4] I know what I will do, so that when I am removed from the stewardship they may accept me into their own houses.'

[5] And calling to himself each one of the debtors of his own lord, he was saying to the first, 'How much do you owe my lord?'

[6] And he said, 'A hundred baths of oil.' And he said to him, 'Accept your writing, and sitting down, quickly write fifty.'

[7] Then to another he said, 'And you, how much do you owe?' And he said, 'A hundred kors of wheat.' He says to him, 'Accept your writing and write eighty.'

[8] And the lord praised the steward of unrighteousness because he had acted sensibly; because the sons of this age are more sensible than the sons of the light toward their own generation.

[9] And I say to you, make for yourselves friends out of the mammon of unrighteousness, so that when it fails they may accept you into the eternal tents.

[10] "The one faithful in the least is faithful also in much, and the one unrighteous in the least is unrighteous also in much.

[11] If therefore you did not become faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will entrust to you the true?

[12] And if you did not become faithful in what is another's, who will give you what is your own?

[13] **No household slave can be a slave to two lords; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will hold to one and despise the other. You cannot be slaves to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and to mammon.**"

[14] Now the ⟦pharisees|Perushim|Pharisees⟧, being lovers of money, were hearing all these things, and they were turning up their noses at him.

[15] And he said to them, "You are those who justify themselves before men, but ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ knows your hearts; because what is high among men is an abomination before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[16] "The ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧ and the prophets were until ⟦john|Yochanan|John⟧; from then the kingdom of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is preached as good news, and everyone forces his way into it.

[17] But it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of the ⟦torah|Torah|Law⟧ to fall.

[18] "Everyone who sends away his wife and marries another commits adultery, and the one who marries a woman sent away from a husband commits adultery.

[19] "Now a certain man was rich, and he clothed himself in purple and fine linen, making merry day by day splendidly.

[20] And a certain poor man, by name ⟦lazarus|Elazar|Lazarus⟧, had been laid at his gateway, covered with sores,

[21] and desiring to be filled from the things falling from the table of the rich man; but even the dogs, coming, were licking his sores.

[22] And it came to pass that the poor man died and was carried away by the angels into the bosom of ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧; and the rich man also died and was buried.

[23] And in Hades, lifting up his eyes, being in torments, he sees ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ from afar and ⟦lazarus|Elazar|Lazarus⟧ in his bosom.

[24] And he, calling, said, 'Father ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, have mercy on me and send ⟦lazarus|Elazar|Lazarus⟧, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, because I am pained in this flame.'

[25] But ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ said, 'Child, remember that you received your good things in your life, and ⟦lazarus|Elazar|Lazarus⟧ in like manner the bad things; but now here he is comforted, and you are pained.

[26] And in all these things, between us and you a great chasm has been fixed, so that those wanting to cross over from here to you may not be able, nor may they pass over from there to us.'

[27] And he said, 'I request you then, father, that you send him to the house of my father,

[28] for I have five brothers, that he may solemnly testify to them, so that they also may not come into this place of torment.'

[29] But ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ says, 'They have ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and the prophets; let them hear them.'

[30] But he said, 'No, father ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, but if someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent.'

[31] But he said to him, '**If they do not hear ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded if someone rises from the dead.**'"

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv13, 15) — divine name retained.
- **"steward" / "stewardship" kept** (v1–8) — Greek οἰκονόμος, οἰκονομία, οἰκονομεῖν: the one who runs a house and its goods for the owner.
- **"was accused unto him that he had wasted his goods" → "was accused to him as scattering what belonged to him"** (v1) — Greek διεβλήθη, "was accused" (often of hostile or secret accusation; the verb does not say whether it was true); διασκορπίζων, "scattering", the verb of 15:13; τὰ ὑπάρχοντα αὐτοῦ.
- **"give an account" → "Render the account"** (v2) — Greek ἀπόδος τὸν λόγον, with the article.
- **"my lord" kept; "I cannot dig" → "To dig I do not have strength"** (v3) — Greek ὁ κύριός μου; σκάπτειν οὐκ ἰσχύω, ἐπαιτεῖν αἰσχύνομαι, the infinitives first.
- **"I am resolved what to do" → "I know what I will do"** (v4) — Greek ἔγνων τί ποιήσω, an aorist: "I have come to know". "They" has no stated subject (the debtors of v5); δέξωνται, "accept, welcome". "Their own" (ἑαυτῶν: WH, Tregelles) or "their" (αὐτῶν: NA28, Byzantine).
- **"measures of oil" / "measures of wheat" → "baths of oil" / "kors of wheat"** (v6, 7) — Greek βάτους, κόρους, the Hebrew measures *bath* (liquid, about 35–40 litres) and *kor* (dry, about ten times as much), kept as Luke keeps them; two different words.
- **"Take thy bill" → "Accept your writing"** (v6, 7) — Greek δέξαι σου τὰ γράμματα, literally "your letters", the written record of the debt.
- **"the lord commended the unjust steward, because he had done wisely" → "the lord praised the steward of unrighteousness because he had acted sensibly"** (v8) — Greek τὸν οἰκονόμον τῆς ἀδικίας, a genitive, as "the mammon of unrighteousness" in v9; φρονίμως, φρονιμώτεροι, "sensibly, more sensible" (not σοφός, "wise"). ὁ κύριος is the lord of the steward (v3, 5); some take it as "the Lord", Yeshua, with the parable ending at v7.
- **"the children of this world are in their generation wiser than the children of light" → "the sons of this age are more sensible than the sons of the light toward their own generation"** (v8) — Greek οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ αἰῶνος τούτου … ὑπὲρ τοὺς υἱοὺς τοῦ φωτὸς εἰς τὴν γενεὰν τὴν ἑαυτῶν.
- **"Make to yourselves friends of the mammon of unrighteousness; that, when ye fail, they may receive you into everlasting habitations" → "make for yourselves friends out of the mammon of unrighteousness, so that when it fails they may accept you into the eternal tents"** (v9) — Greek ἐκ τοῦ μαμωνᾶ τῆς ἀδικίας, "out of / from", the source or means; μαμωνᾶς is the Aramaic word for wealth, kept as the Greek keeps it. ἐκλίπῃ, "it fails, gives out" (WH, Tregelles, NA28); the Byzantine text reads ἐκλίπητε, "you fail" (that is, die), which the KJV follows. "They" is not named. σκηνάς, "tents".
- **"unjust" → "unrighteous"; "that which is least" → "the least"** (v10, 11) — Greek ἄδικος, of one root with ἀδικία (v8, 9); ἐν ἐλαχίστῳ.
- **"have not been faithful … the true riches" → "did not become faithful … the true"** (v11, 12) — Greek πιστοὶ οὐκ ἐγένεσθε; τὸ ἀληθινόν, a neuter adjective with no noun ("riches" is not in the Greek). πιστεύσει, "entrust", is of one root with πιστός, "faithful".
- **"that which is your own"** (v12) — Greek τὸ ὑμέτερον (Tregelles, NA28, Byzantine); WH reads τὸ ἡμέτερον, "what is our own".
- **"No servant can serve two masters … Ye cannot serve God and mammon" → "No household slave can be a slave to two lords … You cannot be slaves to *Elohim* and to mammon"** (v13) — Greek οἰκέτης; δουλεύειν; κυρίοις. Word for word Matt 6:24, except that Luke adds οἰκέτης.
- **"who were covetous … derided him" → "being lovers of money … were turning up their noses at him"** (v14) — Greek φιλάργυροι; ἐξεμυκτήριζον, from μυκτήρ, "nostril": to sneer at.
- **"highly esteemed … in the sight of God" → "high … before *Elohim*"** (v15) — Greek ὑψηλόν; ἐνώπιον, the same word as "before men".
- **"since that time the kingdom of God is preached, and every man presseth into it" → "from then the kingdom of *Elohim* is preached as good news, and everyone forces his way into it"** (v16) — the first clause has no verb in Greek ("were" is supplied); Matt 11:13 has "prophesied". εὐαγγελίζεται, "is preached as gospel". βιάζεται is the verb of Matt 11:12 ("suffers violence") and, as there, can be passive ("is forced, is pressed with violence into it") or middle ("forces his way into it", "uses violence to enter it"); in Matthew the kingdom is the subject and the passive stands; here the subject is "everyone", with εἰς αὐτήν "into it", and the verse has the middle. The passive, "everyone is forced into it", is equally what the form allows.
- **"one tittle of the law to fail" → "one stroke of the *Torah* to fall"** (v17) — Greek κεραίαν, "little horn", a stroke of a letter (Matt 5:18); πεσεῖν, "fall".
- **"putteth away" → "sends away"** (v18) — Greek ἀπολύων, ἀπολελυμένην, the verb for dismissing a wife ("divorce"), as Mark 10:11; ἀπὸ ἀνδρός, "from a husband".
- **"was clothed … fared sumptuously every day" → "clothed himself … making merry day by day splendidly"** (v19) — Greek ἐνεδιδύσκετο, an imperfect (his habit); εὐφραινόμενος, the verb of 15:23–32; λαμπρῶς, "brightly, splendidly".
- **"a certain beggar … was laid at his gate, full of sores" → "a certain poor man … had been laid at his gateway, covered with sores"** (v20) — Greek πτωχός, "poor"; ἐβέβλητο, pluperfect passive, "had been cast / laid"; πυλῶνα; εἱλκωμένος.
- **"to be fed with the crumbs which fell" → "to be filled from the things falling"** (v21) — Greek χορτασθῆναι ἀπὸ τῶν πιπτόντων (WH, NA28); "the crumbs" (τῶν ψιχίων: Tregelles, Byzantine) is not in them. ἀλλὰ καί, "but even".
- **"Abraham's bosom" → "the bosom of Abraham"** (v22, 23) — Greek εἰς τὸν κόλπον Ἀβραάμ; in v23 ἐν τοῖς κόλποις αὐτοῦ, a plural. κόλπος is the breast, or the fold of the garment over it; the one who reclines next to another at table lies "in his bosom" (John 13:23).
- **"in hell" → "in Hades"** (v23) — Greek ᾅδης, the place of the dead; not γέεννα. "He sees" (ὁρᾷ) is a present.
- **"he cried and said" → "he, calling, said"; "I am tormented" → "I am pained"** (v24, 25) — Greek φωνήσας; ὀδυνῶμαι, ὀδυνᾶσαι (Acts 20:38), a different word from βάσανος, "torment" (v23, 28).
- **"Son … receivedst … evil things" → "Child … received … the bad things"** (v25) — Greek τέκνον; ἀπέλαβες, a compound of "receive" (15:27 "received back", 18:30): to receive what is one's due; τὰ κακά.
- **"beside all this … a great gulf fixed" → "in all these things … a great chasm has been fixed"** (v26) — Greek ἐν πᾶσι τούτοις (WH, NA28; Tregelles and the Byzantine text ἐπί, "besides"); χάσμα, a yawning opening; ἐστήρικται, perfect passive, "has been set fast".
- **"I pray thee" → "I request you"; "testify" → "solemnly testify"** (v27, 28) — Greek ἐρωτῶ; διαμαρτύρηται.
- **"be persuaded, though one rose from the dead" → "be persuaded if someone rises from the dead"** (v31) — Greek πεισθήσονται; ἐκ νεκρῶν ἀναστῇ; in v30 ἀπὸ νεκρῶν πορευθῇ, "goes from the dead".

### Notes
- **v16–18** — parallels: Matt 11:12–13; 5:18; 5:32; Mark 10:11–12.
- **v20** — *Lazaros* is the Greek form of the Hebrew name *Elazar*, "God has helped". The rich man has no name in the text.
