## Changes

- **"Jesus" → "*Yeshua*"** (v21, 23) — Hebrew name preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv2, 6, 8, 38) — divine name retained.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v15) — Hebrew Anointed.
- **"baptism of repentance for the remission of sins" → "immersion of repentance unto forgiveness of sins"** (v3) — Greek βάπτισμα μετανοίας εἰς ἄφεσιν ἁμαρτιῶν, word for word as Mark 1:4.
- **"baptized" → "immersed"** (vv7, 12, 16, 21) — Greek *baptizō*.
- **"Holy Ghost" → "*Ruach HaQodesh*"** (v16, 22) — Hebrew divine Spirit. In v16 the Greek has no article (ἐν πνεύματι ἁγίῳ), in v22 it has (τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον).
- **"reign" → "rule"; "Pontius Pilate being governor"** (v1) — Greek τῆς ἡγεμονίας Τιβερίου Καίσαρος, ἡγεμονεύοντος Ποντίου Πιλάτου: one root for both, "rule" and "be governor" (2:2). τετρααρχοῦντος, "being tetrarch", stands three times. τῆς Ἰτουραίας καὶ Τραχωνίτιδος χώρας is "of the Ituraean and Trachonitic country".
- **"Annas and Caiaphas being the high priests, the word of God came unto John" → "in the time of the high priest Annas and Caiaphas, a word of Elohim came upon John"** (v2) — Greek ἐπὶ ἀρχιερέως Ἅννα καὶ Καϊάφα: "high priest" is singular, with two names (the construction of Mark 2:26). ῥῆμα θεοῦ has no article; ἐπὶ Ἰωάννην is "upon" (Jer 1:1 in the Greek).
- **"the country about Jordan" → "the surrounding region of the Jordan"** (v3) — Greek περίχωρος (Matt 3:5).
- **"The voice … Prepare ye" → "A voice … Make ready"** (v4) — Greek φωνὴ βοῶντος, without the article; ἑτοιμάσατε: word for word as Mark 1:3; Matt 3:3. ἐν βίβλῳ λόγων has no article.
- **"Every valley … shall be brought low; and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough ways shall be made smooth" → "Every ravine … shall be made low, and the crooked shall become straight, and the rough ways shall become smooth ways"** (v5) — Greek φάραγξ, a ravine, gorge; ταπεινωθήσεται, the verb "humble" (1:52; 14:11), here of ground; ἔσται … εἰς, "shall become". Editions: εἰς εὐθείαν, singular, NA28 RP SBLGNT ] εἰς εὐθείας, plural, WH Treg — 2–2 among the four editions, NA28 decides; no difference in English.
- **"the salvation of God" kept** (v6) — Greek τὸ σωτήριον τοῦ θεοῦ, the neuter word of 2:30 (Isa 40:5 in the Greek; the Hebrew has "all flesh shall see it together").
- **"Then said he to the multitude that came forth … O generation of vipers, who hath warned you" → "He was saying therefore to the crowds going out … Offspring of vipers, who showed you"** (v7) — Greek ἔλεγεν, imperfect; ὄχλοις, "crowds"; γεννήματα ἐχιδνῶν, τίς ὑπέδειξεν ὑμῖν φυγεῖν ἀπὸ τῆς μελλούσης ὀργῆς: the saying is word for word as Matt 3:7, where it is said to Pharisees and Sadducees.
- **"Bring forth therefore fruits worthy of repentance, and begin not to say" → "Make therefore fruits worthy of repentance; and do not begin to say"** (v8) — Greek ποιήσατε, "make, do" (so v9, 10, 11, 12, 14); καρποὺς ἀξίους, plural (Matt 3:8 singular); μὴ ἄρξησθε, "do not begin" (Matt 3:9 "do not think"). The rest of v8 is word for word as Matt 3:9.
- **"And now also the axe is laid unto the root" → "And already the axe also lies at the root"** (v9) — Greek ἤδη δὲ καὶ ἡ ἀξίνη … κεῖται; Luke has καί, "also, even", which Matt 3:10 has not. ἐκκόπτεται, βάλλεται are presents; εἰς πῦρ has no article.
- **"asked him" → "were asking him"; "What shall we do then" → "What then should we do"** (v10; also v12, 14) — Greek ἐπηρώτων, imperfect; ποιήσωμεν, a deliberative subjunctive (WH Treg NA28; RP the future ποιήσομεν, "shall we do").
- **"He that hath two coats, let him impart to him that hath none; and he that hath meat" → "Let the one having two tunics impart to the one not having, and let the one having foods"** (v11) — Greek χιτών, the garment worn next to the skin (Matt 5:40); μεταδότω (Rom 1:11); βρώματα, plural, "foods".
- **"publicans" → "toll collectors"; "Master" → "Teacher"** (v12) — Greek τελῶναι; διδάσκαλε.
- **"Exact no more than that which is appointed you" → "Exact nothing more beyond what has been ordained for you"** (v13) — Greek μηδὲν πλέον παρὰ τὸ διατεταγμένον ὑμῖν πράσσετε. πράσσω is "do, practice" (Rom 1:32); of money "exact, collect" (19:23): "practice" would not be English here.
- **"the soldiers likewise demanded of him … Do violence to no man, neither accuse any falsely" → "men serving as soldiers also were asking him … Shake down no one, nor accuse falsely"** (v14) — Greek στρατευόμενοι, a participle without article, "men on military service"; διασείσητε, literally "shake thoroughly": extort by force or threat; συκοφαντήσητε, "accuse falsely for gain" (19:8). ὀψώνια is a soldier's pay (Rom 6:23).
- **"were in expectation, and all men mused in their hearts of John, whether he were the Christ, or not" → "were expecting, and all were reasoning in their hearts concerning John, whether perhaps he might be the Mashiach"** (v15) — Greek προσδοκῶντος (1:21); διαλογιζομένων (1:29); μήποτε αὐτὸς εἴη ὁ χριστός. "or not" is not in the Greek.
- **"I indeed baptize you with water; but one mightier than I cometh, the latchet of whose shoes I am not worthy to unloose: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire" → "I indeed immerse you with water; but there comes the one stronger than I, the strap of whose sandals I am not fit to loose; he will immerse you in Ruach HaQodesh and fire"** (v16) — Greek ὕδατι, a dative without preposition (Mark 1:8; Matt 3:11 has ἐν, "in water"); ὁ ἰσχυρότερός μου, with the article; ἱκανός, "fit", not ἄξιος "worthy"; ἐν πνεύματι ἁγίῳ καὶ πυρί, one "in" for both. Luke has neither Mark's "after me" and "stoop down" nor Matthew's "carry".
- **"Whose fan is in his hand, and he will throughly purge his floor, and will gather" → "whose winnowing fork is in his hand, to thoroughly cleanse his threshing floor and to gather"** (v17) — Editions: διακαθᾶραι … συναγαγεῖν, infinitives of purpose, WH NA28 SBLGNT ] διακαθαριεῖ … συνάξει, futures ("he will thoroughly cleanse … and will gather", as Matt 3:12), Treg RP. "the wheat into his barn": Matt 3:12 has "his wheat into the barn". The last clause is word for word as Matt 3:12.
- **"And many other things in his exhortation preached he unto the people" → "So then, exhorting with many other things also, he was preaching the gospel to the people"** (v18) — Greek πολλὰ μὲν οὖν καὶ ἕτερα παρακαλῶν εὐηγγελίζετο τὸν λαόν: παρακαλέω, here "exhort"; εὐαγγελίζομαι with the people as its object (Acts 8:25; 14:21).
- **"for Herodias his brother Philip's wife, and for all the evils which Herod had done" → "concerning Herodias, the wife of his brother, and concerning all the evil things which Herod did"** (v19) — "Philip" (RP) is not in the critical text. ἐλεγχόμενος is "being reproved".
- **"Added yet this above all, that he shut up John" → "added this also to them all: he also shut up John"** (v20) — Greek προσέθηκεν καὶ τοῦτο ἐπὶ πᾶσιν, καὶ κατέκλεισεν; the second καί Treg NA28 RP SBLGNT, not WH ("added this also to them all, he shut up").
- **"when all the people were baptized, it came to pass, that Jesus also being baptized, and praying, the heaven was opened" → "it came to pass, when all the people were immersed, and Yeshua also had been immersed and was praying, that the heaven was opened"** (v21) — Greek ἐν τῷ βαπτισθῆναι ἅπαντα τὸν λαόν; τὸν οὐρανόν, singular (Mark 1:10 and Matt 3:16 have the plural, and Mark "being torn").
- **"descended in a bodily shape like a dove upon him, and a voice came from heaven, which said, Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased" → "came down in bodily form as a dove upon him, and a voice came out of heaven, 'You are my Son, the beloved; in you I have taken pleasure'"** (v22) — Greek σωματικῷ εἴδει, "in bodily form, appearance", only in Luke; ἐξ οὐρανοῦ, singular. The words of the voice are word for word as Mark 1:11: ὁ υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός; ἐν σοὶ εὐδόκησα, a past tense. "which said" (RP) is not in the critical text.
- **"And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli" → "And Yeshua himself was, when beginning, about thirty years old, being son, as was supposed, of Joseph, of Heli"** (v23) — Greek καὶ αὐτὸς ἦν Ἰησοῦς ἀρχόμενος ὡσεὶ ἐτῶν τριάκοντα: "was … beginning"; the Greek does not say what he began (his work: Acts 1:22; 10:37). ὢν υἱός, ὡς ἐνομίζετο, Ἰωσὴφ τοῦ Ἠλί: the word "son" (υἱός) stands once, before "as was supposed"; every name after Joseph is a bare genitive with the article (τοῦ Ἠλί, "of Heli"). The verses therefore say "of" each time and supply no "son" (v23–38).
- **"which was the son of God" → "of Elohim"** (v38) — Greek τοῦ Ἀδὰμ τοῦ θεοῦ, the same bare genitive as all the others.
- **"Juda … Joseph … Semei" → "Joda … Josech … Semein"; "Jose" → "Joshua"; "Salmon" → "Sala"; "Aminadab, which was the son of Aram" → "Amminadab, of Admin, of Arni"** (v26, 29, 32, 33) — the names of the critical text. v29 Ἰησοῦ (WH Treg NA28) is the name "Yeshua / Joshua"; RP Ἰωσή. v32: Σαλά WH NA28 SBLGNT ] Σαλμών Treg RP. v33: τοῦ Ἀμιναδὰβ τοῦ Ἀδμὶν τοῦ Ἀρνί NA28 SBLGNT ] τοῦ Ἀδμὶν τοῦ Ἀρνί WH; τοῦ Ἀμιναδὰβ τοῦ Ἀράμ Treg RP — the three editions differ three ways; the verse follows the printed text.

### Notes
- **v4-6** quotes Isa 40:3-5 (LXX).
- **v23-38** — the list runs from Joseph back to Adam and to God, and differs from Matt 1:1–16 between David and Joseph (here through Nathan, there through Solomon; Joseph's father is here Heli, there Jacob). Since antiquity this has been explained in several ways: that this is Mary's line (with Joseph as son-in-law of Heli), or that one list gives the legal and the other the natural descent. The Greek names only Joseph and says "as was supposed".
- **v36** — "Cainan" between Shelah and Arphaxad stands in the Greek Old Testament (Gen 11:12 LXX), not in the Hebrew text.
