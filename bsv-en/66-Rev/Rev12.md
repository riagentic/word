# Revelation 12

[1] And a great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars,

[2] and being with child; and she cries out, being in birth pains and tormented to bear.

[3] And another sign appeared in heaven, and behold, a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns, and on his heads seven diadems,

[4] and his tail drags the third of the stars of heaven, and he cast them to the earth. And the dragon stands before the woman who is about to bear, so that when she bears he may devour her child.

[5] And she bore a son, a male, **who is about to shepherd all the nations with a rod of iron**; and her child was snatched up to ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and to his throne.

[6] And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she has there a place made ready from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, that there they may nourish her a thousand two hundred sixty days.

[7] And there came to be war in heaven: Michael and his angels, to war with the dragon. And the dragon warred, and his angels,

[8] and he did not have the strength, nor was their place found any longer in heaven.

[9] And the great dragon was cast, the ancient serpent, the one called Devil and the Satan, the one deceiving the whole inhabited earth — he was cast to the earth, and his angels were cast with him.

[10] And I heard a loud voice in heaven saying, "Now the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and the authority of his ⟦mashiach|Mashiach|Christ⟧ have come to be, because the accuser of our brothers was cast, the one accusing them before our ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ day and night.

[11] **And they overcame him because of the blood of the Lamb and because of the word of their testimony, and they did not love their soul unto death.**

[12] Because of this rejoice, heavens and those tenting in them. Woe to the earth and the sea, because the devil has come down to you, having great rage, knowing that he has little time."

[13] And when the dragon saw that he was cast to the earth, he pursued the woman who bore the male.

[14] And there were given to the woman the two wings of the great eagle, that she might fly into the wilderness, into her place, where she is nourished there a time and times and half a time, from the face of the serpent.

[15] And the serpent cast out of his mouth after the woman water as a river, that he might make her carried away by the river.

[16] And the earth helped the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed up the river which the dragon cast out of his mouth.

[17] And the dragon was wrathful at the woman, and went away to make war with the rest of her seed, those keeping the commandments of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ and having the testimony of ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧. And he stood upon the sand of the sea.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v5, 6, 10, 17) — systematic.
- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v10) — systematic.
- **"And" kept at the head of the sentences** (throughout) — Greek καί begins almost every sentence of the chapter; it is not turned into "but", "now" or "then".
- **"there appeared a great wonder" → "a great sign appeared"** (v1, 3) — Greek σημεῖον "sign"; ὤφθη is a passive, "was seen".
- **"crown" kept** (v1) — Greek στέφανος, the wreath; the "diadems" of v3 are διαδήματα, the royal headband.
- **"And she being with child cried, travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered" → "and being with child; and she cries out, being in birth pains and tormented to bear"** (v2) — Greek ἐν γαστρὶ ἔχουσα "having in the womb", a participle that continues v1; κράζει is a present; βασανιζομένη "tormented", the word of 14:10. Reading καὶ κράζει WH NA28 ] κράζει Treg ("she cries out", no "and"); ἔκραζεν RP ("she was crying out").
- **"drew the third part … did cast … stood" → "drags the third … cast … stands"** (v4) — Greek σύρει, a present; ἔβαλεν, an aorist; ἕστηκεν, a perfect with present sense as printed. With the other breathing (ἔστηκεν, imperfect of στήκω) it reads "stood"; the old manuscripts have no breathings.
- **"a man child, who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron" → "a son, a male, who is about to shepherd all the nations with a rod of iron"** (v5) — Greek υἱόν, ἄρσεν: the neuter "male" stands in apposition to "son", against the gender. μέλλει ποιμαίνειν "is about to shepherd" (Ps 2:9 in its Greek form); ἐν ῥάβδῳ σιδηρᾷ "with (in) a rod of iron".
- **"caught up" → "snatched up"** (v5) — Greek ἡρπάσθη.
- **"where she hath a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there" → "where she has there a place made ready from *Elohim*, that there they may nourish her"** (v6) — Greek ὅπου … ἐκεῖ, "where … there", a doubled adverb in the Hebrew manner, kept (also v14); ἀπὸ τοῦ θεοῦ "from God"; τρέφωσιν, "they may nourish", has no named subject.
- **"Michael and his angels fought against the dragon" → "Michael and his angels, to war with the dragon"** (v7) — Greek ὁ Μιχαὴλ καὶ οἱ ἄγγελοι αὐτοῦ τοῦ πολεμῆσαι: a nominative followed by an infinitive with the article, without a finite verb. The broken grammar is kept. πολεμέω "war" (v7), ποιῆσαι πόλεμον "make war" (v17).
- **"prevailed not; neither was their place found any more" → "he did not have the strength, nor was their place found any longer"** (v8) — Greek οὐκ ἴσχυσεν, singular (WH NA28 RP; ἴσχυσαν "they did not have the strength" Treg); τόπος … αὐτῶν "their place".
- **"cast out" → "cast"** (v9, 10, 13) — Greek ἐβλήθη, the plain verb, the same as in v4, 15, 16; no "out" or "down".
- **"that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world" → "the ancient serpent, the one called Devil and the Satan, the one deceiving the whole inhabited earth"** (v9) — Greek ὁ ὄφις ὁ ἀρχαῖος "the ancient serpent"; Διάβολος without the article, ὁ Σατανᾶς with it (ὁ WH Treg NA28; not in RP); τὴν οἰκουμένην ὅλην "the whole inhabited earth", not κόσμος "world".
- **"Now is come salvation, and strength" → "Now the salvation and the power … have come to be"** (v10) — Greek ἐγένετο "came to be"; δύναμις "power"; ἐξουσία "authority".
- **"the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them" → "the accuser of our brothers was cast, the one accusing them"** (v10) — Greek ὁ κατηγορῶν, a present participle.
- **"they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death" → "they overcame him because of the blood of the Lamb and because of the word of their testimony, and they did not love their soul unto death"** (v11) — Greek διά with the accusative, "because of", not "by means of"; τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτῶν "their soul", singular.
- **"ye that dwell in them" → "those tenting in them"** (v12) — Greek σκηνοῦντες, from σκηνή "tent" (John 1:14); not κατοικέω "dwell".
- **"Woe to the inhabiters of the earth and of the sea" → "Woe to the earth and the sea"** (v12) — Greek οὐαὶ τὴν γῆν καὶ τὴν θάλασσαν, "woe" with an accusative (WH Treg NA28; RP has the dative); "the inhabiters" is not in the Greek.
- **"having great wrath, because he knoweth that he hath but a short time" → "having great rage, knowing that he has little time"** (v12) — Greek θυμός "rage" (ὀργή is "wrath"); εἰδώς, a participle; ὀλίγον καιρόν "little time".
- **"persecuted" → "pursued"** (v13) — Greek ἐδίωξεν; the verb means both. The woman flees and the serpent sends a river after her, so "pursued".
- **"the man child" → "the male"** (v13) — Greek τὸν ἄρσενα.
- **"where she is nourished for a time, and times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent" → "where she is nourished there a time and times and half a time, from the face of the serpent"** (v14) — Greek ἀπὸ προσώπου τοῦ ὄφεως stands at the end; it can go with "nourished" or with "fly".
- **"water as a flood … carried away of the flood" → "water as a river … carried away by the river"** (v15, 16) — Greek ποταμός "river" and ποταμοφόρητον "river-borne", one root.
- **"was wroth" → "was wrathful"** (v17) — Greek ὠργίσθη, the verb of ὀργή "wrath".
- **"the remnant of her seed … have the testimony of Jesus Christ" → "the rest of her seed … having the testimony of *Yeshua*"** (v17) — Greek τῶν λοιπῶν "the rest"; σπέρμα "seed"; "Christ" is not in the Greek. The genitive is left open: the testimony Yeshua gave, or the testimony about him.
- **"And I stood upon the sand of the sea" (KJV 13:1) → "And he stood upon the sand of the sea"** (v17) — Greek ἐστάθη "he stood", the dragon (WH Treg NA28); ἐστάθην "I stood" RP. The Greek editions number this sentence 12:18.
