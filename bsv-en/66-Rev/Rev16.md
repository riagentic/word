# Revelation 16

[1] And I heard a loud voice out of the sanctuary saying to the seven angels, "Go and pour out the seven bowls of the rage of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ into the earth."

[2] And the first went away and poured out his bowl into the earth; and there came a bad and evil sore upon the men having the mark of the beast and those bowing down to its image.

[3] And the second poured out his bowl into the sea; and it became blood as of a dead man, and every soul of life died, the things in the sea.

[4] And the third poured out his bowl into the rivers and the springs of the waters; and it became blood.

[5] And I heard the angel of the waters saying,

"**Righteous are you, the one who is and the one who was, the holy one,
because you judged these things,
[6] because they poured out the blood of saints and prophets,
and blood you have given them to drink;
they are worthy.**"

[7] And I heard the altar saying,

"**Yes, Lord, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, the Almighty,
true and righteous are your judgments.**"

[8] And the fourth poured out his bowl upon the sun; and it was given to it to scorch men with fire.

[9] And men were scorched with a great scorching, and they blasphemed the name of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, who has the authority over these plagues, and they did not repent to give him glory.

[10] And the fifth poured out his bowl upon the throne of the beast; and its kingdom became darkened, and they were gnawing their tongues from the pain,

[11] and they blasphemed the ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ of heaven from their pains and from their sores, and they did not repent of their works.

[12] And the sixth poured out his bowl upon the great river, the ⟦euphrates|Perat|Euphrates⟧; and its water was dried up, that the way of the kings from the rising of the sun might be made ready.

[13] And I saw out of the mouth of the dragon and out of the mouth of the beast and out of the mouth of the false prophet three unclean spirits, as frogs;

[14] for they are spirits of demons doing signs, which go out upon the kings of the whole inhabited earth, to gather them together unto the war of the great day of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ the Almighty.

[15] ("Behold, I am coming as a thief. Blessed is the one who is awake and keeps his garments, that he may not walk naked and they see his shame.")

[16] And they gathered them together into the place called in ⟦hebrew|Ivrit|Hebrew⟧ ⟦armageddon|Har Megiddo|Armageddon⟧.

[17] And the seventh poured out his bowl upon the air; and a loud voice came out of the sanctuary from the throne, saying, "**It has come to pass.**"

[18] And there came lightnings and voices and thunders, and there came a great earthquake, such as had not come since man came to be upon the earth, so mighty an earthquake, so great.

[19] And the great city came to be in three parts, and the cities of the nations fell; and ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ the great was remembered before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, to give her the cup of the wine of the rage of his wrath.

[20] And every island fled, and mountains were not found.

[21] And great hail, as of a talent's weight, comes down out of heaven upon men; and men blasphemed ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ from the plague of the hail, because its plague is exceedingly great.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"And" kept at the head of the sentences** (throughout) — Greek καί begins every one of the seven bowls; not turned into "then" or "so".
- **"temple" → "sanctuary"** (v1, 17) — Greek ναός.
- **"vials" → "bowls"** (v1–4, 8, 10, 12, 17) — Greek φιάλη, a shallow bowl.
- **"the vials of the wrath of God upon the earth" → "the bowls of the rage of *Elohim* into the earth"** (v1, 2) — Greek θυμός "rage"; εἰς "into" with the first three bowls (v1–4), ἐπί "upon" with the last four.
- **"the first went" → "the first went away"; "angel" not supplied** (v2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 12, 17) — Greek ἀπῆλθεν ὁ πρῶτος; the Greek says "the first", "the second" … without "angel" (WH Treg NA28; RP adds ἄγγελος in v3, 8).
- **"there fell a noisome and grievous sore" → "there came a bad and evil sore"** (v2) — Greek ἐγένετο ἕλκος κακὸν καὶ πονηρόν: ἕλκος is singular; κακός "bad", πονηρός "evil".
- **"which worshipped his image" → "those bowing down to its image"** (v2) — Greek προσκυνοῦντας, a present participle.
- **"it became as the blood of a dead man: and every living soul died in the sea" → "it became blood as of a dead man, and every soul of life died, the things in the sea"** (v3) — Greek αἷμα ὡς νεκροῦ; πᾶσα ψυχὴ ζωῆς "every soul of life" (WH Treg NA28; ζῶσα "living" RP); τὰ ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ, a neuter plural set beside the feminine singular: kept as an apposition.
- **"they became blood" → "it became blood"** (v4) — Greek ἐγένετο, singular; it can also be read "there came blood".
- **"Thou art righteous, O Lord, which art, and wast, and shalt be, because thou hast judged thus" → "Righteous are you, the one who is and the one who was, the holy one, because you judged these things"** (v5) — Greek ὁ ὢν καὶ ὁ ἦν, ὁ ὅσιος: no "O Lord", no "and shalt be". ὁ ὅσιος "the holy one" (ὁ WH NA28 RP; without the article, "holy", Treg); on ὅσιος see 15:4. ταῦτα ἔκρινας "you judged these things".
- **"they have shed the blood" → "they poured out the blood"** (v6) — Greek ἐξέχεαν, the verb used of the bowls.
- **"for they are worthy" → "they are worthy"** (v6) — Greek ἄξιοί εἰσιν, without "for".
- **"I heard another out of the altar say" → "I heard the altar saying"** (v7) — Greek ἤκουσα τοῦ θυσιαστηρίου λέγοντος: the altar itself speaks.
- **"Lord God Almighty, true and righteous are thy judgments" → "Lord, *Elohim*, the Almighty, true and righteous are your judgments"** (v7) — Greek κύριε, ὁ θεός, ὁ παντοκράτωρ; ἀληθιναί; κρίσεις "judgments".
- **"power was given unto him to scorch men with fire" → "it was given to it to scorch men with fire"** (v8) — Greek ἐδόθη αὐτῷ: "to it" (the sun) or "to him" (the angel).
- **"men were scorched with great heat" → "men were scorched with a great scorching"** (v9) — Greek ἐκαυματίσθησαν … καῦμα μέγα, verb and noun of one root.
- **"blasphemed" kept** (v9, 11, 21) — Greek ἐβλασφήμησαν; not "cursed".
- **"which hath power over these plagues: and they repented not to give him glory" → "who has the authority over these plagues, and they did not repent to give him glory"** (v9) — Greek τὴν ἐξουσίαν; οὐ μετενόησαν δοῦναι, an infinitive.
- **"his kingdom was full of darkness; and they gnawed their tongues for pain" → "its kingdom became darkened, and they were gnawing their tongues from the pain"** (v10) — Greek ἐγένετο … ἐσκοτωμένη; ἐμασῶντο, an imperfect with no named subject ("people" is not in the Greek); ἐκ τοῦ πόνου. πόνος is "toil" elsewhere in this translation; here and in v11 it is bodily pain.
- **"because of their pains and their sores, and repented not of their deeds" → "from their pains and from their sores, and they did not repent of their works"** (v11) — Greek ἐκ three times, literally "out of"; ἔργα "works".
- **"the great river Euphrates … that the way of the kings of the east might be prepared" → "the great river, the Euphrates … that the way of the kings from the rising of the sun might be made ready"** (v12) — Greek ἀπὸ ἀνατολῆς ἡλίου "from the rising of the sun"; ἑτοιμασθῇ "be made ready".
- **"I saw three unclean spirits like frogs come out of the mouth" → "I saw out of the mouth … three unclean spirits, as frogs"** (v13) — the Greek has no verb "come"; ὡς βάτραχοι "as frogs" is a nominative beside the accusative "spirits".
- **"the spirits of devils, working miracles, which go forth unto the kings of the earth and of the whole world, to gather them to the battle of that great day" → "spirits of demons doing signs, which go out upon the kings of the whole inhabited earth, to gather them together unto the war of the great day"** (v14) — Greek δαιμονίων "demons"; σημεῖα "signs"; τῆς οἰκουμένης ὅλης "the whole inhabited earth" ("of the earth and" is not in the Greek); πόλεμος "war"; "that" (ἐκείνης) only in RP.
- **"Blessed is he that watcheth, and keepeth his garments, lest he walk naked, and they see his shame" → "Blessed is the one who is awake and keeps his garments, that he may not walk naked and they see his shame"** (v15) — Greek γρηγορῶν "being awake"; τηρῶν "keeping"; βλέπωσιν "they see", with no named subject. The verse breaks into the vision in the first person; the brackets mark this and are not in the manuscripts.
- **"he gathered them together" → "they gathered them together"** (v16) — Greek συνήγαγεν, a singular verb. Its subject is most naturally the neuter plural "spirits" of v14 (a neuter plural takes a singular verb), so "they"; it can also be read "he gathered".
- **"Armageddon"** (v16) — Greek Ἁρμαγεδών, *Harmagedōn*, given as a Hebrew name (Ἑβραϊστί "in Hebrew").
- **"into the air … a great voice out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, It is done" → "upon the air … a loud voice came out of the sanctuary from the throne, saying, 'It has come to pass'"** (v17) — Greek ἐπὶ τὸν ἀέρα; "of heaven" only in RP; Γέγονεν, a perfect of γίνομαι, the verb that follows four times in v18–19.
- **"there were voices, and thunders, and lightnings; and there was a great earthquake, such as was not since men were upon the earth" → "there came lightnings and voices and thunders, and there came a great earthquake, such as had not come since man came to be upon the earth"** (v18) — Greek ἐγένοντο … ἐγένετο … οὐκ ἐγένετο … ἐγένετο, one verb; φωναί "voices". Reading: ἄνθρωπος ἐγένετο "man came to be" Treg NA28; ἄνθρωποι ἐγένοντο "men came to be" WH (and the SBLGNT); οἱ ἄνθρωποι ἐγένοντο RP.
- **"the great city was divided into three parts" → "the great city came to be in three parts"** (v19) — Greek ἐγένετο … εἰς τρία μέρη.
- **"great Babylon came in remembrance before God, to give unto her the cup of the wine of the fierceness of his wrath" → "Babylon the great was remembered before *Elohim*, to give her the cup of the wine of the rage of his wrath"** (v19) — Greek ἐμνήσθη ἐνώπιον τοῦ θεοῦ, a passive: God is not the subject. τοῦ θυμοῦ τῆς ὀργῆς αὐτοῦ: θυμός "rage" and ὀργή "wrath", two words side by side.
- **"the mountains were not found" → "mountains were not found"** (v20) — Greek ὄρη without the article.
- **"there fell upon men a great hail out of heaven, every stone about the weight of a talent" → "great hail, as of a talent's weight, comes down out of heaven upon men"** (v21) — Greek χάλαζα μεγάλη ὡς ταλαντιαία καταβαίνει, a present; a talent is a weight of roughly 26–34 kg.
- **"for the plague thereof was exceeding great" → "because its plague is exceedingly great"** (v21) — Greek ἐστίν, a present.
