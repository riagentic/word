# Revelation 18

[1] After these things I saw another angel coming down out of heaven, having great authority, and the earth was illumined from his glory.

[2] And he cried out in a strong voice, saying,

"**Fallen, fallen is ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ the great!**
And she has become a dwelling place of demons,
and a prison of every unclean spirit,
and a prison of every unclean bird,
and a prison of every unclean and hated beast,
[3] because from the wine of the rage of her sexual immorality
all the nations have fallen,
and the kings of the earth committed sexual immorality with her,
and the merchants of the earth grew rich
from the power of her wantonness."

[4] And I heard another voice out of heaven, saying,

"**Come out, my people, out of her,
so that you may not be fellow sharers in her sins,
and so that you may not receive of her plagues;
[5] because her sins have been joined together as far as heaven,
and ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has remembered her wrongdoings**.
[6] Repay her as she herself also repaid,
and double the double according to her works;
in the cup in which she mixed, mix for her double.
[7] As much as she glorified herself and lived wantonly,
so much give her of torment and mourning,
because in her heart she says,
'I sit a queen,
and a widow I am not,
and mourning I shall by no means see.'
[8] Because of this, in one day her plagues will come,
death and mourning and famine,
and she will be burned up in fire;
because strong is the Lord ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ who judged her."

[9] And the kings of the earth, who committed sexual immorality and lived wantonly with her, will weep and wail over her, when they see the smoke of her burning,

[10] standing afar off because of the fear of her torment, saying,

"Woe, woe, the great city,
⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧, the strong city!
Because in one hour your judgment came."

[11] And the merchants of the earth weep and mourn over her, because no one buys their cargo any more,

[12] cargo of gold and of silver and of precious stone and of pearls and of fine linen and of purple and of silk and of scarlet, and every citron wood and every vessel of ivory and every vessel of most precious wood and of bronze and of iron and of marble,

[13] and cinnamon and amomum and incenses and ointment and frankincense and wine and oil and fine flour and wheat and cattle and sheep, and of horses and of carriages and of bodies, and souls of men.

[14] And the ripe fruit of the desire of your soul has gone away from you, and all the rich things and the bright things have perished from you, and they will by no means find them any more.

[15] The merchants of these things, who grew rich from her, will stand afar off because of the fear of her torment, weeping and mourning,

[16] saying, "Woe, woe, the great city,
she who was clothed in fine linen and purple and scarlet,
and gilded with gold and precious stone and pearl,
[17] because in one hour such great riches were made desolate."

And every helmsman and everyone who sails to a place, and sailors and as many as work the sea, stood afar off

[18] and were crying out, seeing the smoke of her burning, saying, "Who is like the great city?"

[19] And they cast dust on their heads and were crying out, weeping and mourning, saying,

"Woe, woe, the great city,
in which all who had the ships in the sea
grew rich from her costliness,
because in one hour she was made desolate.
[20] Be glad over her, heaven,
and you saints and you apostles and you prophets,
because ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ has judged your sentence out of her."

[21] And one strong angel took up a stone as a great millstone and cast it into the sea, saying,

"Thus with a rush will ⟦babylon|Bavel|Babylon⟧ the great city be cast,
and will by no means be found any more;
[22] and the voice of harpists and musicians and flute players and trumpeters
will by no means be heard in you any more,
and every craftsman of every craft
will by no means be found in you any more,
and the voice of a mill
will by no means be heard in you any more,
[23] and the light of a lamp
will by no means shine in you any more,
and the voice of bridegroom and bride
will by no means be heard in you any more;
because your merchants were the great men of the earth,
because by your sorcery all the nations were deceived,
[24] and in her was found the blood of prophets and of saints,
and of all who have been slaughtered on the earth."

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v5, 8, 20) — systematic.
- **"Lord God" → "Lord *Elohim*"** (v8) — systematic.
- **"Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen" → "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great!"** (v2) — Greek Ἔπεσεν, ἔπεσεν Βαβυλὼν ἡ μεγάλη: the verb stands first, twice (Isa 21:9). The second ἔπεσεν is read by SBLGNT, WH and NA28; Treg omits it.
- **"the hold … a cage" → "a prison … a prison"** (v2) — Greek φυλακή, one word three times: a place of guarding, a prison (as 20:7); it can also mean a watch-post, a haunt.
- **"a cage of every unclean and hateful bird" → "a prison of every unclean bird, and a prison of every unclean and hated beast"** (v2) — the printed text (SBLGNT, NA28) has the words καὶ φυλακὴ παντὸς θηρίου ἀκαθάρτου, "and a prison of every unclean beast"; WH and Treg do not have them and read "a prison of every unclean and hated bird". On a tie NA28 decides. μεμισημένου is "hated" (μισέω, "hate").
- **"have drunk of the wine" → "from the wine … have fallen"** (v3) — the printed text reads πέπτωκαν, "have fallen" (SBLGNT, WH, Treg); NA28 reads πέπωκαν, "have drunk": "all the nations have drunk of the wine of the rage of her sexual immorality". ἐκ is "from, out of": the wine is the cause of the fall.
- **"the wine of the wrath of her fornication" → "the wine of the rage of her sexual immorality"** (v3) — Greek θυμός, "rage" ("wrath" is ὀργή); it can also mean passion. πορνεία, "sexual immorality"; πορνεύω, "commit sexual immorality" (v3, 9).
- **"the abundance of her delicacies" → "the power of her wantonness"** (v3) — Greek ἐκ τῆς δυνάμεως τοῦ στρήνους αὐτῆς; δύναμις is "power". στρῆνος is unbridled, wanton luxury; the verb στρηνιάω, "live wantonly" (v7, 9).
- **"Come out of her, my people" → "Come out, my people, out of her"** (v4) — the Greek order, Ἐξέλθατε, ὁ λαός μου, ἐξ αὐτῆς; the verb is plural (Jer 51:45).
- **"that ye be not partakers of her sins" → "so that you may not be fellow sharers in her sins"** (v4) — Greek συγκοινωνέω, the verb of συγκοινωνός, "fellow sharer".
- **"have reached unto heaven" → "have been joined together as far as heaven"** (v5) — Greek ἐκολλήθησαν, "were glued, joined": the sins cling one to another in a heap that touches heaven.
- **"iniquities" → "wrongdoings"** (v5) — Greek ἀδικήματα, wrong deeds.
- **"Reward her even as she rewarded you" → "Repay her as she herself also repaid"** (v6) — Greek ἀποδίδωμι, "repay"; the Greek has no object ("you", "others").
- **"double unto her double" → "double the double"** (v6) — Greek διπλώσατε τὰ διπλᾶ.
- **"How much she hath glorified herself" → "As much as she glorified herself"** (v7) — the printed text has αὑτήν, "herself"; other editions print αὐτήν with the smooth breathing, the same letters, here with the same sense.
- **"sorrow" → "mourning"** (v7, 8) — Greek πένθος; πενθέω, "mourn" (v11, 15, 19).
- **"shall see no sorrow" → "mourning I shall by no means see"** (v7) — Greek οὐ μή, the strong denial; also v14, 21–23.
- **"strong is the Lord God who judgeth her" → "who judged her"** (v8) — Greek ὁ κρίνας, an aorist participle.
- **"bewail her, and lament for her" → "weep and wail over her"** (v9) — Greek κλαίω, "weep"; κόπτομαι, to beat one's breast in grief (as 1:7).
- **"Alas, alas" → "Woe, woe"** (v10, 16, 19) — Greek οὐαί, the same word as "woe" everywhere else. The city is named in the nominative: "the great city", not "you great city".
- **"mighty" → "strong"** (v2, 8, 10, 21) — Greek ἰσχυρός.
- **"merchandise" → "cargo"** (v11, 12) — Greek γόμος, a ship's load.
- **"thyine wood" → "citron wood"** (v12) — Greek ξύλον θύϊνον, the scented wood of the citron tree (*thyia*).
- **"odours" → "amomum … incenses"** (v13) — Greek ἄμωμον, an Indian spice plant (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28 have the word; the Byzantine text does not); θυμιάματα, plural.
- **"chariots, and slaves, and souls of men" → "of carriages and of bodies, and souls of men"** (v13) — Greek ῥεδῶν, four-wheeled carriages; σωμάτων, "bodies", the trade word for slaves; ψυχὰς ἀνθρώπων, "souls of men" (Ezek 27:13). The cases change in the Greek: "of horses, of carriages, of bodies" depends on "cargo", "souls" is an accusative like "cinnamon".
- **"the fruits that thy soul lusted after" → "the ripe fruit of the desire of your soul"** (v14) — Greek ἡ ὀπώρα σου τῆς ἐπιθυμίας τῆς ψυχῆς; ὀπώρα is the fruit of late summer, a different word from καρπός, "fruit". The verse speaks to the city; the Greek does not say who speaks.
- **"dainty and goodly" → "the rich things and the bright things"** (v14) — Greek τὰ λιπαρά, "fat, rich"; τὰ λαμπρά, "bright" (as 19:8; 22:1, 16).
- **"thou shalt find them no more at all" → "they will by no means find them any more"** (v14) — Greek εὑρήσουσιν, third person plural (SBLGNT, WH, Treg, NA28); the Byzantine text has "you will find".
- **"decked with gold" → "gilded with gold"** (v16) — Greek κεχρυσωμένη ἐν χρυσίῳ, literally "made golden in gold"; ἐν is read by WH and NA28, not by Treg and SBLGNT; the sense is the same.
- **"is come to nought" / "is she made desolate" → "was made desolate"** (v17, 19) — Greek ἠρημώθη, one verb.
- **"all the company in ships" → "everyone who sails to a place"** (v17) — Greek πᾶς ὁ ἐπὶ τόπον πλέων.
- **"trade by sea" → "work the sea"** (v17) — Greek τὴν θάλασσαν ἐργάζονται.
- **"cried" → "were crying out"** (v18, 19) — the verse follows ἔκραζον, imperfect (Treg, NA28); WH and SBLGNT read ἔκραξαν, "cried out".
- **"What city is like unto this great city" → "Who is like the great city?"** (v18) — Greek Τίς ὁμοία τῇ πόλει τῇ μεγάλῃ; τίς is feminine here, as "city" is; the same form of question as 13:4.
- **"by reason of her costliness" → "from her costliness"** (v19) — Greek ἐκ τῆς τιμιότητος αὐτῆς; τιμιότης is from τίμιος, "precious" (v12, 16).
- **"God hath avenged you on her" → "God has judged your sentence out of her"** (v20) — Greek ἔκρινεν ὁ θεὸς τὸ κρίμα ὑμῶν ἐξ αὐτῆς, literally "God judged your sentence from her". It can mean that God has given judgment in your cause and exacted it from her, or that God has passed on her the sentence which she passed on you.
- **"with violence shall … be thrown down" → "with a rush will … be cast"** (v21) — Greek ὁρμήματι, a rushing onset; βληθήσεται, "will be cast", the same verb as "cast" in v19 and 21a; the Greek has no "down".
- **"the sound of a millstone" → "the voice of a mill"** (v22) — Greek φωνή, one word in v22–23 (harpists, mill, bridegroom and bride); μύλος, the mill.
- **"harpers" → "harpists"** (v22) — Greek κιθαρῳδός, one who sings to the *kithara*.
- **"sorceries" → "sorcery"** (v23) — Greek φαρμακεία, singular: the use of drugs and spells.
- **"slain" → "slaughtered"** (v24) — Greek ἐσφαγμένων, from σφάζω, "slaughter".
- **"lighted" → "illumined"** (v1) — Greek ἐφωτίσθη, from φωτίζω, "enlighten"; here of light on the earth.
