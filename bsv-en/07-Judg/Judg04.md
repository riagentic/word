## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v6, 23) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. **"children of" → "sons of"** — Hebrew בְּנֵי.
- **"again did evil in the sight of the LORD, when Ehud was dead" → "again did the evil in the eyes of Yahweh; and Ehud was dead"** (v1) — the standing sentence of the book (2:11; 3:12); "again" is literally "added to do". וְאֵהוּד מֵת is a clause of its own, "and Ehud was dead / had died".
- **"the captain of whose host was Sisera, which dwelt in Harosheth of the Gentiles" → "the commander of his host was Sisera, and he was dwelling in Harosheth-hagoyim"** (v2, 7) — Hebrew שַׂר־צְבָאוֹ; שַׂר is "prince", of an army "commander"; צָבָא "host". חֲרֹשֶׁת הַגּוֹיִם is "Harosheth of the nations".
- **"he mightily oppressed" → "he oppressed … with force"** (v3) — Hebrew בְּחָזְקָה, "with strength, by force". "He" is not named: Sisera, or Jabin. **"chariots of iron"** — רֶכֶב בַּרְזֶל, as 1:19. The cry is וַיִּצְעֲקוּ here, וַיִּזְעֲקוּ in 3:9, 15: two spellings of one verb.
- **"Deborah, a prophetess" → "Deborah, a woman, a prophetess"** (v4) — Hebrew אִשָּׁה נְבִיאָה, word for word; the Hebrew piles up the feminine words: "a woman, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, she". אֵשֶׁת לַפִּידוֹת is "the wife of Lappidoth"; the same words can be heard as "a woman of torches" (לַפִּיד "torch"). The name Deborah means "bee".
- **"she dwelt under the palm tree of Deborah" → "she was sitting under the palm tree of Deborah"** (v5) — Hebrew יוֹשֶׁבֶת, "sitting" (as a judge sits) or "dwelling". "Ramah" has the article. **"came up to her for judgment" → "went up to her for the judgment"** — Hebrew לַמִּשְׁפָּט, with the article.
- **"Go and draw toward mount Tabor" → "Go, and draw out on Mount Tabor"** (v6) — Hebrew וּמָשַׁכְתָּ בְּהַר תָּבוֹר: מָשַׁךְ is "draw, pull"; here without an object: draw (men) along, or march out; בְּ is "on / at". The same verb is Yahweh's in v7, "I will draw out to you … Sisera". "Has not Yahweh commanded" is a question in the Hebrew; "you" is not in it.
- **"with his chariots and his multitude" → "and his chariots and his multitude"** (v7) — Hebrew וְאֶת … וְאֶת.
- **"notwithstanding the journey that thou takest shall not be for thine honour" → "only that the glory will not be yours on the way that you are going"** (v9) — Hebrew אֶפֶס כִּי לֹא תִהְיֶה תִּפְאַרְתְּךָ עַל־הַדֶּרֶךְ, literally "only that your glory will not be upon the way". תִּפְאֶרֶת is "beauty, adornment, glory": the thing a man may pride himself on. "I will surely go" is the infinitive absolute with its verb (הָלֹךְ אֵלֵךְ, "going I will go"). The object stands first: "into the hand of a woman".
- **"called Zebulun and Naphtali" → "called out Zebulun and Naphtali"** (v10, 13) — Hebrew וַיַּזְעֵק, the causative of "cry out": to call men together for war. **"he went up with ten thousand men at his feet" → "there went up at his feet ten thousand men"** — Hebrew וַיַּעַל בְּרַגְלָיו עֲשֶׂרֶת אַלְפֵי אִישׁ; the verb is singular: "he went up, at his feet ten thousand men", or "ten thousand men went up at his feet" (behind him).
- **"Heber the Kenite, which was of the children of Hobab the father in law of Moses, had severed himself from the Kenites" → "Heber the Kenite had parted from Kain, from the sons of Hobab, the father-in-law of Moses"** (v11) — Hebrew נִפְרָד מִקַּיִן: קַיִן "Kain" is the name of the people of the Kenites (Num 24:22). חֹתֵן is the wife's father; in Num 10:29 Hobab is the son of Reuel, the father-in-law of Moses, and the consonants here can also be read "brother-in-law" (חָתָן, one related by marriage). The Masoretic pointing is translated. **"unto the plain of Zaanaim" → "as far as the oak in Zaanannim"** — Hebrew אֵלוֹן, a great tree. The written text has בצענים; the reading tradition, בְּצַעֲנַנִּים, is translated (as Josh 19:33).
- **"they shewed Sisera" → "they told Sisera"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיַּגִּדוּ.
- **"all the people that were with him"** (v13) — Hebrew הָעָם, "the people": his fighting men.
- **"Up; for this is the day" → "Arise, for this is the day"** (v14) — Hebrew קוּם.
- **"the LORD discomfited Sisera, and all his chariots, and all his host" → "Yahweh threw into confusion Sisera and all the chariots and all the camp"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew וַיָּהָם, as Exod 14:24; Josh 10:10. מַחֲנֶה is "camp", also of the host in it; "host" (v2, 7) is another word. "With the edge of the sword" is literally "to the mouth of the sword". **"lighted down off his chariot, and fled away on his feet" → "went down from the chariot and fled on his feet"**.
- **"there was not a man left" → "not even one was left"** (v16) — Hebrew לֹא נִשְׁאַר עַד־אֶחָד.
- **"there was peace" → "there was *shalom*"** (v17) — Hebrew שָׁלוֹם.
- **"fear not" → "do not fear"; "she covered him with a mantle" → "she covered him with the rug"** (v18) — Hebrew בַּשְּׂמִיכָה, with the article; the word is found only here and its meaning is not certain: a thick covering, rug or curtain.
- **"a bottle of milk" → "the skin of milk"** (v19) — Hebrew נֹאד, a skin for liquids; חָלָב "milk". He asks for water; she gives milk (5:25). "Give me to drink, please" has נָא.
- **"Stand in the door of the tent … Is there any man here? … No" → "Stand at the entrance of the tent … Is there a man here? … There is not"** (v20) — Hebrew פֶּתַח "entrance"; אִישׁ "a man" twice; אָיִן "there is not". The command "Stand" (עֲמֹד) is a masculine form, though a woman is addressed.
- **"took a nail of the tent, and took an hammer in her hand, and went softly unto him, and smote the nail into his temples, and fastened it into the ground" → "took the peg of the tent and put the hammer in her hand, and she came to him softly and thrust the peg into his temple, and it went down into the earth"** (v21) — Hebrew יְתַד הָאֹהֶל, a tent peg; וַתָּשֶׂם "put"; וַתִּתְקַע "thrust, drove", the verb of 3:21; רַקָּה is the temple of the head. וַתִּצְנַח is the rare verb of 1:14 ("alighted"): its subject here is the peg, or she herself ("she drove it down"); "went down" is the sense the context gives. Uncertain.
- **"for he was fast asleep and weary. So he died" → "and he was in a deep sleep, and he was weary, and he died"** (v21) — Hebrew וְהוּא־נִרְדָּם וַיָּעַף וַיָּמֹת, three clauses joined by "and". נִרְדָּם is the deep sleep of תַּרְדֵּמָה (Gen 2:21). וַיָּעַף is pointed as a form of "be weary, faint": "and he was weary" (the reason for his sleep), or, following the blow, "and he grew faint, and he died". Both are possible; the order of the Hebrew is kept.
- **"as Barak pursued Sisera" → "Barak was pursuing Sisera"** (v22) — a main clause. **"when he came into her tent" → "he came in to her"** — Hebrew וַיָּבֹא אֵלֶיהָ; "tent" is not in the Hebrew. **"Sisera lay dead, and the nail was in his temples" → "Sisera was fallen, dead, and the peg was in his temple"** — Hebrew נֹפֵל מֵת, as 3:25.
- **"So God subdued on that day Jabin" → "And *Elohim* brought low on that day Jabin"** (v23) — Hebrew וַיַּכְנַע, the verb of 3:30 ("was brought low"). The narrator says *Elohim* here, not Yahweh.
- **"the hand of the children of Israel prospered, and prevailed against Jabin" → "the hand of the sons of Israel went on, harder and harder, upon Jabin"** (v24) — Hebrew וַתֵּלֶךְ … הָלוֹךְ וְקָשָׁה, literally "went, going and being hard". **"until they had destroyed Jabin" → "until they had cut off Jabin"** — Hebrew הִכְרִיתוּ.

### Notes
- **Deborah** — the only woman among the judges. Both prophetess and judge.
- **Barak's condition** (v8) — it is answered by Deborah's word: Yahweh will sell Sisera "into the hand of a woman" (v9). Heb 11:32 names Barak among those who acted by faith.
- **Jael** (v17–22) — the woman. Wife of Heber, whose house had *shalom* with Jabin. Celebrated in ch 5's song.
- **"Chariots of iron"** (v3, 13) — the Canaanite advantage on the plain. Sisera's host is strong on level ground; the song tells how the wadi Kishon swept them away (5:20–21).
- **Wadi Kishon** (v7, 13) — runs through the Jezreel valley, past Mount Tabor.
- **Hospitality inversion** — Sisera expects shelter; receives death.
