# Judges 15

[1] And it came to pass after some days, in the days of the wheat harvest, that ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ visited his wife with a kid of the goats, and he said, "Let me go in to my wife, into the chamber." And her father did not let him go in.

[2] And her father said, "I surely said that you surely hated her, and I gave her to your friend. Is not her younger sister better than she? Let her be yours, please, in her place."

[3] And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ said to them, "This time I am clear of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ when I do evil with them."

[4] And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ went and captured three hundred foxes, and he took torches and turned tail to tail and put one torch between the two tails, in the middle.

[5] And he kindled fire in the torches and let them go into the standing grain of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧, and he burned from the stacked grain to the standing grain and to the olive orchard.

[6] And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ said, "Who has done this?" And they said, "⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧, the son-in-law of the ⟦timnite|Timni|Timnite⟧, because he took his wife and gave her to his friend." And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ went up and burned her and her father with fire.

[7] And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ said to them, "If you do like this, I will surely avenge myself on you, and afterward I will cease."

[8] And he struck them, leg upon thigh, a great blow. And he went down and dwelt in the cleft of the crag of ⟦etam|Eitam|Etam⟧.

[9] And the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ went up and camped in ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧, and they spread out in ⟦lehi|Lechi|Lehi⟧.

[10] And the men of ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ said, "Why have you come up against us?" And they said, "To bind ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ we have come up, to do to him as he did to us."

[11] And three thousand men from ⟦judah|Yehudah|Judah⟧ went down to the cleft of the crag of ⟦etam|Eitam|Etam⟧ and said to ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧, "Do you not know that the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ are ruling over us? And what is this that you have done to us?" And he said to them, "As they did to me, so I have done to them."

[12] And they said to him, "To bind you we have come down, to give you into the hand of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧." And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ said to them, "Swear to me, lest you fall upon me yourselves."

[13] And they said to him, saying, "No, but we will surely bind you and give you into their hand, but we will surely not put you to death." And they bound him with two new cords and brought him up from the crag.

[14] He came as far as ⟦lehi|Lechi|Lehi⟧, and the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ shouted to meet him. And the spirit of ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ rushed upon him, and the cords that were on his arms became like flax that has burned with fire, and his bonds melted from off his hands.

[15] And he found a fresh jawbone of a donkey, and he stretched out his hand and took it and struck down with it a thousand men.

[16] And ⟦samson|Shimshon|Samson⟧ said,

"With the jawbone of the donkey,
a heap, two heaps;
with the jawbone of the donkey
I have struck down a thousand men."

[17] And it came to pass, when he had finished speaking, that he threw the jawbone from his hand, and he called that place ⟦ramath-lehi|Ramat Lechi|Ramath-lehi⟧.

[18] And he was very thirsty, and he called to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the LORD⟧ and said, "You have given by the hand of your servant this great salvation, and now I shall die of thirst and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised."

[19] And ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ split the hollow that is in ⟦lehi|Lechi|Lehi⟧, and water came out of it, and he drank, and his spirit returned, and he revived. Therefore he called its name ⟦en-hakkore|Ein HaQore|En-hakkore⟧, which is in ⟦lehi|Lechi|Lehi⟧ to this day.

[20] And he judged ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ in the days of the ⟦philistines|Pelishtim|Philistines⟧ twenty years.

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v19) — Hebrew *Elohim*. Samson calls to Yahweh (v18); the narrator says "*Elohim* split".
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"within a while after, in the time of wheat harvest" → "after some days, in the days of the wheat harvest"** (v1) — Hebrew מִיָּמִים, literally "from days" (an unstated length of time), and בִּימֵי "in the days of".
- **"a kid" → "a kid of the goats"** (v1) — Hebrew גְּדִי עִזִּים.
- **"I will go in" → "Let me go in"** (v1) — Hebrew אָבֹאָה, the form of wish or resolve (cohortative); it may also be heard as "I will go in". **"would not suffer him" → "did not let him"** — Hebrew וְלֹא־נְתָנוֹ, literally "did not give him to go in".
- **"I verily thought that thou hadst utterly hated her" → "I surely said that you surely hated her"** (v2) — two infinitives absolute with their verbs: אָמֹר אָמַרְתִּי, literally "saying I said" (he said it to himself: he thought), and שָׂנֹא שְׂנֵאתָהּ "hating you hated her". The same form in v13: אָסֹר נֶאֱסָרְךָ "binding we will bind you", הָמֵת לֹא נְמִיתֶךָ "putting to death we will not put you to death".
- **"companion" → "friend"** (v2, 6) — Hebrew מֵרֵעַ, as in Gen 26:26; the man of 14:20.
- **"fairer" → "better"** (v2) — Hebrew טוֹבָה "good"; the word does not name beauty. **"take her, I pray thee, instead of her" → "Let her be yours, please, in her place"** — Hebrew תְּהִי־נָא לְךָ תַּחְתֶּיהָ: a wish ("let her be"), with נָא "please".
- **"Now shall I be more blameless than the Philistines, though I do them a displeasure" → "This time I am clear of the Philistines when I do evil with them"** (v3) — Hebrew נִקֵּיתִי הַפַּעַם מִפְּלִשְׁתִּים: the verb is a perfect, "I am clear" (free of guilt) "from the Philistines"; no comparison is made. כִּי may be "when" or "for"; the participle עֹשֶׂה is "am doing / am about to do". רָעָה is "evil" (harm done). "To them": the Hebrew has the plural and does not say who is addressed.
- **"caught" → "captured"** (v4) — Hebrew לָכַד. **"foxes"** — Hebrew שׁוּעָלִים; the word may also cover the jackal, which goes in packs. **"a firebrand in the midst between two tails" → "one torch between the two tails, in the middle"** — Hebrew לַפִּיד אֶחָד בֵּין־שְׁנֵי הַזְּנָבוֹת בַּתָּוֶךְ; the verse does not say "foxes" a second time ("turned tail to tail").
- **"when he had set the brands on fire, he let them go" → "he kindled fire in the torches and let them go"** (v5) — two main verbs; "them" is supplied (the Hebrew has no object). "Kindled" and "burned" are one Hebrew verb, הִבְעִיר; וַיְשַׁלַּח is "sent off, let go".
- **"burnt up both the shocks, and also the standing corn, with the vineyards and olives" → "burned from the stacked grain to the standing grain and to the olive orchard"** (v5) — Hebrew מִגָּדִישׁ וְעַד־קָמָה וְעַד־כֶּרֶם זָיִת, literally "from stack and to standing grain and to vineyard of olive". The Masoretic Text has "vineyard of olive", one thing, an olive orchard; "vineyards and olives" is the reading of the Greek (cited from memory).
- **"answered" → "said"** (v6, 10) — Hebrew אָמַר. **"came up" → "went up"** (v6, 9).
- **"her and her father"** (v6) — so the Masoretic Text; some Hebrew manuscripts and the Greek read "her and her father's house" (cited from memory; cf. 14:15).
- **"Though ye have done this, yet will I be avenged of you" → "If you do like this, I will surely avenge myself on you"** (v7) — Hebrew אִם־תַּעֲשׂוּן כָּזֹאת כִּי אִם־נִקַּמְתִּי בָכֶם: after "if you do like this" comes the form of an oath, literally "(I swear) that … if I have not avenged myself on you". **"cease"** — Hebrew חָדַל.
- **"smote them hip and thigh with a great slaughter" → "struck them, leg upon thigh, a great blow"** (v8) — Hebrew שׁוֹק עַל־יָרֵךְ מַכָּה גְדוֹלָה. שׁוֹק is the leg (the lower leg, or of an animal the upper hind leg), יָרֵךְ the thigh. "Leg upon thigh" is a saying found only here; what it pictures is not known. It is given word for word.
- **"top of the rock Etam" → "the cleft of the crag of Etam"** (v8, 11) — Hebrew סְעִיף סֶלַע עֵיטָם; סְעִיף is a cleft, סֶלַע a crag. **"dwelt"** — Hebrew יָשַׁב.
- **"pitched" → "camped"** (v9) — Hebrew חָנָה. **"spread themselves in Lehi"** — Hebrew וַיִּנָּטְשׁוּ בַּלֶּחִי. Lehi is the Hebrew word for "jawbone" (לְחִי); here and in v19 it has the article, "the Lehi". The place bears the name before the story explains it (v17).
- **"the men of Judah"** (v10) — Hebrew אִישׁ יְהוּדָה, a singular used of the whole ("the man of Judah"), with a plural verb.
- **"are rulers over us" → "are ruling over us"** (v11) — Hebrew participle מֹשְׁלִים.
- **"that ye will not fall upon me yourselves" → "lest you fall upon me yourselves"** (v12) — Hebrew פֶּן "lest".
- **"we will bind thee fast … we will not kill thee" → "we will surely bind you … we will surely not put you to death"** (v13) — infinitive absolute, see v2; הֵמִית is "put to death". **"cords"** (v13, 14) — Hebrew עֲבֹתִים, the word of 16:11, 12. **"from the rock" → "from the crag"** — סֶלַע.
- **"when he came unto Lehi" → "He came as far as Lehi"** (v14) — Hebrew הוּא־בָא עַד־לֶחִי, a clause that stands by itself; עַד is "as far as". **"shouted against him" → "shouted to meet him"** — Hebrew הֵרִיעוּ לִקְרָאתוֹ: the shout of men going to battle or of triumph.
- **"came mightily upon him" → "rushed upon him"** (v14) — Hebrew וַתִּצְלַח עָלָיו רוּחַ יְהוָה, the verb of 14:6, 19. רוּחַ is "spirit", "wind" or "breath"; in v19 the same word is Samson's own spirit that returns.
- **"became as flax that was burnt with fire, and his bands loosed from off his hands" → "became like flax that has burned with fire, and his bonds melted from off his hands"** (v14) — Hebrew וַיִּמַּסּוּ "melted"; אֱסוּרָיו "his bonds" is from אָסַר "bind" (v10, 12, 13).
- **"a new jawbone of an ass, and put forth his hand" → "a fresh jawbone of a donkey, and he stretched out his hand"** (v15) — Hebrew טְרִיָּה "fresh, moist": not yet dry and brittle. **"slew" → "struck down"** (v15, 16) — Hebrew הִכָּה "strike".
- **"With the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps" → "With the jawbone of the donkey, a heap, two heaps"** (v16) — Hebrew בִּלְחִי הַחֲמוֹר חֲמוֹר חֲמֹרָתָיִם. The saying is a verse of four short lines and a play on one sound: חֲמוֹר *chamor* is "donkey", and the same word, as it is pointed here, is "heap" (elsewhere חֹמֶר); חֲמֹרָתָיִם is its dual, "two heaps". Word for word: "with the jawbone of the *chamor*, a *chamor*, two *chamors*". The Greek reads the same consonants as a verb, "I have utterly heaped them" (cited from memory). The Masoretic Text is translated as it is pointed.
- **"when he had made an end of speaking, that he cast away the jawbone out of his hand, and called that place Ramath-lehi" → "… he threw the jawbone from his hand, and he called that place Ramath-lehi"** (v17) — Hebrew רָמַת לֶחִי: "the height of the jawbone" (רָמָה "height"); the sound also recalls רָמָה "throw". The name is kept; its meaning is given only here.
- **"he was sore athirst, and called on the LORD" → "he was very thirsty, and he called to Yahweh"** (v18) — Hebrew וַיִּקְרָא אֶל־יְהוָה.
- **"Thou hast given this great deliverance into the hand of thy servant: and now shall I die for thirst" → "You have given by the hand of your servant this great salvation, and now I shall die of thirst"** (v18) — Hebrew תְּשׁוּעָה "salvation"; בְּיַד is "by the hand of" Samson and then "into the hand of" the uncircumcised: the same two words. "Your servant" is עַבְדְּךָ, the word "slave". The Hebrew has no mark of a question; the words may be read as a complaint ("and now I shall die") or as a question ("and now shall I die …?").
- **"God clave an hollow place that was in the jaw" → "*Elohim* split the hollow that is in Lehi"** (v19) — Hebrew הַמַּכְתֵּשׁ אֲשֶׁר־בַּלֶּחִי. מַכְתֵּשׁ is a mortar (Prov 27:22), and so a hollow shaped like one. בַּלֶּחִי is "in the Lehi", the place (as in v9 and at the end of this verse), or "in the jawbone": the Hebrew word is the same.
- **"when he had drunk, his spirit came again" → "and he drank, and his spirit returned"** (v19) — the chain of verbs kept.
- **"he called the name thereof En-hakkore" → "he called its name En-hakkore"** (v19) — Hebrew עֵין הַקּוֹרֵא: "the spring of the one who calls" (v18 "he called to Yahweh"); הַקּוֹרֵא is also the name of a bird, the partridge (1 Sam 26:20). The name is kept; its meaning is given only here. The subject of "called" is not named: "he" or "one".
- **"judged Israel … twenty years"** (v20) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁפֹּט אֶת־יִשְׂרָאֵל; said again in 16:31.

### Notes
- **Samson's feud** — the Philistines burn his wife + father-in-law; he strikes in retaliation; they camp in Judah; he strikes down 1,000 with a donkey jawbone.
- **Foxes and torches** (v4–5) — 300 animals, tied tail to tail in pairs with a torch between, let go into the standing grain at harvest time.
- **Judah delivers him up** (v11–13) — Judah is under Philistine rule and does not defend Samson. Three thousand men of Judah bind one Israelite to hand him to the oppressor. The tribes do not support their own judge.
- **Jawbone of a donkey** — the jawbone is "fresh" (v15), so not yet dry and brittle.
- **Samson's prayer** (v18) — rare moment of prayer. Water comes out of the hollow. A glimpse of a relationship beyond brute exploits.
- **"Twenty years"** (v20) — Samson's judgeship. Repeated at 16:31 — closing formula of his career.
