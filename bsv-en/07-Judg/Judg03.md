## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v7, 20) — Hebrew *Elohim*. Of the gods of the nations (v6) the same word is "gods".
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept. **"children of" → "sons of"** — Hebrew בְּנֵי.
- **"Now these are the nations which the LORD left, to prove Israel by them, even as many of Israel as had not known all the wars of Canaan" → "And these are the nations that Yahweh left, to test Israel by them, all who had not known all the wars of Canaan"** (v1) — Hebrew אֵת כָּל־אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יָדְעוּ, "all who had not known": "of Israel", "Israelites" is not in the Hebrew. נִסָּה is "test".
- **"Only that the generations of the children of Israel might know, to teach them war, at the least such as before knew nothing thereof" → "only so that the generations of the sons of Israel might know, to teach them war, only those who formerly had not known them"** (v2) — Hebrew רַק לְמַעַן דַּעַת דֹּרוֹת … לְלַמְּדָם מִלְחָמָה רַק אֲשֶׁר־לְפָנִים לֹא יְדָעוּם, word for word: רַק "only" stands twice; "them" at the end is plural (the wars). The sentence is hard, and it is left as hard as it is.
- **"the Canaanites, and the Sidonians, and the Hivites" → "the Canaanite, and the Sidonian, and the Hivite"** (v3, 5) — the Hebrew has each people in the singular with the article, as in ch. 1. "Lords" is סַרְנֵי, the Philistines' own word for their five rulers. **"in mount Lebanon"** — "Mount Lebanon" has the article in the Hebrew ("the Lebanon"). **"unto the entering in of Hamath" → "as far as Lebo-hamath"** — Hebrew לְבוֹא חֲמָת, "the coming in of Hamath", or a place name.
- **"to know whether they would hearken unto the commandments" → "to know whether they would hear the commandments"** (v4) — Hebrew הֲיִשְׁמְעוּ אֶת; שָׁמַע is "hear".
- **"dwelt among" → "dwelt in the midst of"** (v5) — Hebrew בְּקֶרֶב. The Hebrew has no "and" before "the Hittite".
- **"did evil in the sight of the LORD" → "did the evil in the eyes of Yahweh"** (v7, 12) — the standing sentence of the book (2:11). **"again"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיֹּסִפוּ … לַעֲשׂוֹת, literally "added to do".
- **"served Baalim and the groves" → "served the Baals and the Asheroth"** (v7) — Hebrew הָאֲשֵׁרוֹת, a feminine plural of Asherah (the usual plural is אֲשֵׁרִים "Asherim"): the goddess, or the wooden objects of her worship; "groves" is the rendering of the Greek and Latin. The word is kept. Two Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac and Latin read "the Ashtaroth", as 2:13 (cited from memory).
- **"the anger of the LORD was hot" → "the anger of Yahweh burned"** (v8) — as 2:14; no "therefore" in the Hebrew. **"Chushanrishathaim king of Mesopotamia" → "Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram-naharaim"** — Hebrew אֲרַם נַהֲרַיִם, "Aram of the two rivers". The second half of the king's name, רִשְׁעָתַיִם, sounds as "double wickedness". In v10 the Hebrew has only "king of Aram".
- **"when the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer … who delivered them" → "the sons of Israel cried out to Yahweh, and Yahweh raised up a savior … and he saved them"** (v9, 15) — main clauses in the Hebrew. מוֹשִׁיעַ "savior" and וַיּוֹשִׁיעֵם "saved them" are one root (יָשַׁע), the root of the names Joshua and Yeshua. Who "he" is that saved them, Yahweh or the savior, the Hebrew does not say. **"Caleb's younger brother" → "the brother of Caleb, the one younger than he"** — as 1:13.
- **"the Spirit of the LORD came upon him" → "the spirit of Yahweh was upon him"** (v10) — Hebrew וַתְּהִי עָלָיו, the verb "be", as Num 24:2. רוּחַ is "spirit", "wind" and "breath". **"his hand prevailed against" → "his hand was strong over"** — Hebrew וַתָּעָז.
- **"the land had rest" → "the land was quiet"** (v11, 30) — Hebrew שָׁקַט, as Josh 11:23.
- **"strengthened Eglon" → "made Eglon … strong"** (v12) — Hebrew וַיְחַזֵּק, the piel of חָזַק "be strong". "Because" is עַל כִּי.
- **"smote Israel, and possessed the city of palm trees" → "struck Israel, and they took possession of the city of the palm trees"** (v13) — Hebrew וַיִּירְשׁוּ, plural; the city as 1:16.
- **"a Benjamite, a man lefthanded" → "the Benjaminite, a left-handed man"** (v15) — "left-handed" is kept, as in 20:16 (the same phrase, of seven hundred slingers): "bound in his right hand" would say in English that he was crippled, which the Hebrew does not say. Hebrew בֶּן־הַיְמִינִי, "the son of the Yemini" (the right-hand one); אִישׁ אִטֵּר יַד־יְמִינוֹ, literally "a man shut / bound as to his right hand", only here and in 20:16 (of seven hundred Benjaminite slingers). It is commonly understood as "left-handed": a man who does not use his right hand; whether by defect or by training the Hebrew does not say. The Greek has "ambidextrous" (cited from memory).
- **"a present" → "an offering"** (v15, 17, 18) — Hebrew מִנְחָה, a gift brought to a superior; the tribute of a subject people is meant. "Sent by his hand" is literal.
- **"a dagger which had two edges, of a cubit length" → "a sword, and it had two edges, a short cubit its length"** (v16, 21, 22) — Hebrew חֶרֶב, the usual word for "sword"; "edges" is literally "mouths" (פֵּיוֹת). גֹּמֶד is found only here; its length is not known. It is not the usual word for cubit (אַמָּה); it is commonly taken as a short cubit, from the elbow to the knuckles. Uncertain. **"under his raiment" → "under his garments"** — Hebrew מַדָּיו.
- **"brought the present" → "brought the offering near"** (v17, 18) — Hebrew הִקְרִיב, the verb used of bringing an offering to the altar. **"a very fat man"** — Hebrew בָּרִיא, "fat", the word used of the fat cows of Gen 41; the name Eglon is like עֵגֶל "calf".
- **"the people that bare the present" → "the people, the carriers of the offering"** (v18).
- **"the quarries that were by Gilgal" → "the carved images that are by Gilgal"** (v19, 26) — Hebrew הַפְּסִילִים, the plural used for פֶּסֶל "carved image" (Deut 7:5, 25): carved stones or idols. "Gilgal" has the article. **"I have a secret errand unto thee, O king: who said, Keep silence" → "I have a secret word for you, O king. And he said, Hush!"** — Hebrew דְּבַר־סֵתֶר, "a word (or matter) of secrecy"; הָס "hush!". The Hebrew does not name the one who says "Hush": the king.
- **"he was sitting in a summer parlour, which he had for himself alone … I have a message from God unto thee" → "he was sitting in the upper room of coolness that was his, alone … I have a word of *Elohim* for you"** (v20) — Hebrew עֲלִיַּת הַמְּקֵרָה, an upper room (on the roof) "of the cooling"; מְקֵרָה is found only here and in v24. דְּבַר־אֱלֹהִים is "a word of Elohim": Ehud says *Elohim*, not Yahweh, to the Moabite; it can also be heard as "a word of a god" or "a matter of the gods". דָּבָר is "word" and "matter": the "word" Ehud has is the sword. **"out of his seat" → "from the seat"** — Hebrew הַכִּסֵּא, "the seat / the throne".
- **"put forth his left hand" → "stretched out his left hand"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיִּשְׁלַח. **"thrust it into his belly"** — Hebrew וַיִּתְקָעֶהָ, the verb used of driving in a peg (4:21) and of blowing the shofar (v27).
- **"the haft also went in after the blade" → "the hilt also went in after the blade"** (v22) — Hebrew הַנִּצָּב, "what stands", the hilt, only here in this sense; לַהַב is "flame", then "blade". **"so that he could not draw the dagger" → "for he did not draw the sword"** — Hebrew כִּי לֹא שָׁלַף, "for he did not draw": Ehud left it.
- **"and the dirt came out" → "and the dung came out"** (v22) — Hebrew וַיֵּצֵא הַפַּרְשְׁדֹנָה. The last word is found only here and its meaning is not known. The rendering follows the old understanding (Targum, Vulgate: cited from memory) that connects it with פֶּרֶשׁ "dung": the contents of the bowels came out. Others take it as a part of the building, like the word of v23 that looks like it ("he went out to the *parshedon*"), or as a part of the body ("it came out at the back"). The verb is masculine, so its subject is not "the sword" (a feminine word). Uncertain.
- **"went forth through the porch" → "went out to the porch"** (v23) — Hebrew הַמִּסְדְּרוֹנָה, found only here; from the root "order, row": a colonnade or porch. Uncertain. The Hebrew form means "to the …". **"shut the doors of the parlour upon him" → "closed the doors of the upper room behind him"** — Hebrew בַּעֲדוֹ: behind Ehud, or upon Eglon.
- **"Surely he covereth his feet in his summer chamber" → "Surely he is covering his feet in the chamber of coolness"** (v24) — Hebrew מֵסִיךְ הוּא אֶת־רַגְלָיו, the Hebrew's own decent way of saying that a man is emptying his bowels (1 Sam 24:3): the garment falls about the feet. חֲדַר is an inner room. "His servants" is עֲבָדָיו, the "slaves" of a king.
- **"they tarried till they were ashamed" → "they waited until they were ashamed"** (v25) — Hebrew עַד־בּוֹשׁ: to the point of shame, or until they were at a loss. **"their lord was fallen down dead on the earth" → "their lord was fallen to the earth, dead"** — Hebrew נֹפֵל אַרְצָה מֵת.
- **"escaped while they tarried" → "escaped while they lingered"** (v26) — Hebrew הִתְמַהְמְהָם, as Gen 19:16. **"Seirath" → "Seirah"** — Hebrew הַשְּׂעִירָתָה, "to the Seirah".
- **"blew a trumpet in the mountain of Ephraim" → "blew on the shofar in the hill country of Ephraim"** (v27) — Hebrew שׁוֹפָר, the ram's horn. הַר is "hill country" both times in the verse.
- **"Follow after me: for the LORD hath delivered your enemies the Moabites" → "Pursue after me, for Yahweh has given your enemies, Moab"** (v28) — Hebrew רִדְפוּ אַחֲרַי, "pursue after me" (the Greek has "come down after me": cited from memory); נָתַן "give"; "Moab", the name of the people as one. **"took the fords of Jordan toward Moab, and suffered not a man to pass over" → "captured the fords of the Jordan toward Moab, and they did not allow a man to pass over"** — Hebrew לְמוֹאָב: "toward Moab", "belonging to Moab" or "against Moab".
- **"all lusty, and all men of valour" → "every stout one and every man of ability"** (v29) — Hebrew כָּל־שָׁמֵן, literally "every fat one" (stout, well-fed, robust): the word looks back to the fat king; אִישׁ חָיִל, a man of strength and standing.
- **"So Moab was subdued that day" → "And Moab was brought low on that day"** (v30) — Hebrew וַתִּכָּנַע, the nifal of כָּנַע "bring low". **"fourscore" → "eighty"**.
- **"which slew of the Philistines six hundred men with an ox goad: and he also delivered Israel" → "and he struck down the Philistines, six hundred men, with an oxgoad; and he also saved Israel"** (v31) — Hebrew מַלְמַד הַבָּקָר, "the goad of the herd" (מַלְמָד only here, from לָמַד "teach, train"); וַיֹּשַׁע "saved".

### Notes
- **Cycle established** — ch 3 launches the first round: sin → oppression → cry → savior → quiet → sin again.
- **Othniel** (v7–11) — Caleb's nephew or younger brother (the Hebrew can mean either). First judge; minimal narrative detail.
- **Ehud's assassination of Eglon** (v15–30) — told with plain, coarse detail. Eglon's name puns on "calf." A left-handed man (literally "bound as to his right hand") from Benjamin ("son of the right hand"). The Moabite king who takes Israel's "offering" becomes the one slaughtered.
- **Shamgar** (v31) — the briefest judge. Only 1 verse. "Son of Anath" (a Canaanite goddess name) suggests a possibly non-Israelite warrior-ally. Six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad.
- **Eighty years of quiet** (v30) — the longest such period in Judges.
