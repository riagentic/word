# 2 Corinthians 12

[1] It is necessary to boast; it is not profitable, indeed, but I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord.

[2] I know a man in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧, fourteen years ago — whether in the body I do not know, or outside the body I do not know, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ knows — such a one snatched up as far as the third heaven.

[3] And I know such a man — whether in the body or apart from the body I do not know, ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ knows —

[4] that he was snatched up into the paradise and heard unspeakable words, which it is not permitted for a man to speak.

[5] On behalf of such a one I will boast, but on behalf of myself I will not boast, except in the weaknesses.

[6] For if I should want to boast, I will not be senseless, for I will speak truth; but I spare, lest anyone consider unto me above what he sees me to be or hears anything from me,

[7] and by the excess of the revelations. Therefore, that I might not exalt myself overmuch, there was given to me a thorn for the flesh, an angel of Satan, that he might buffet me, that I might not exalt myself overmuch.

[8] About this, three times I appealed to the Lord that it might depart from me;

[9] and he has said to me, "**My grace is enough for you; for power is brought to completion in weakness.**" Most gladly therefore I will rather boast in my weaknesses, that the power of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ may pitch its tent upon me.

[10] Therefore I take pleasure in weaknesses, in mistreatments, in necessities, in persecutions and straits, on behalf of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧; **for when I am weak, then I am powerful.**

[11] I have become senseless; you compelled me; for I ought to have been commended by you. For in nothing did I come short of the superlative apostles, even if I am nothing;

[12] the signs indeed of the apostle were worked out among you in all endurance, by both signs and wonders and powers.

[13] For what is it in which you were made less than the rest of the churches, except that I myself was not a burden to you? Forgive me this unrighteousness.

[14] Behold, this third time I am ready to come to you, and I will not be a burden; for I seek not the things of yours but you; for the children ought not to store up treasure for the parents, but the parents for the children.

[15] But I most gladly will spend and be utterly spent on behalf of your souls. If loving you more abundantly, am I loved less?

[16] But be it so: I did not weigh you down; but being crafty, I took you with guile.

[17] Any one of those whom I have sent to you — did I defraud you through him?

[18] I exhorted Titus and sent the brother with him; did Titus defraud you? Did we not walk in the same spirit? Not in the same steps?

[19] Have you been thinking all this while that we are defending ourselves to you? Before ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ we speak; but all things, beloved, are on behalf of your building up,

[20] for I fear lest somehow, having come, I find you not such as I want, and I be found by you such as you do not want; lest somehow there be strife, jealousy, rages, selfish ambitions, slanders, whisperings, puffings up, disorders;

[21] lest, when I come again, my ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ humble me before you, and I mourn many of those who have sinned before and did not repent over the uncleanness and sexual immorality and licentiousness which they practiced.

## Changes

- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (throughout) — Greek *Christos* = Hebrew *Mashiach*, "Anointed One."
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (vv2, 3, 19, 21) — Greek *theos* renders Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"It is not expedient for me doubtless to glory. I will come" → "It is necessary to boast; it is not profitable, indeed, but I will come"** (v1) — the printed text: καυχᾶσθαι δεῖ· οὐ συμφέρον μέν, ἐλεύσομαι δέ. The KJV follows another reading (δή, "doubtless", and no δεῖ). κυρίου, "of the Lord": visions and revelations from him, or of him.
- **"I knew a man … such an one caught up to the third heaven" → "I know a man … such a one snatched up as far as the third heaven"** (v2) — Greek οἶδα, present in sense; ἁρπαγέντα, "seized, snatched" (1 Thess 4:17); ἕως, "as far as". The Greek order is kept: the parenthesis stands between "fourteen years ago" and "snatched up". ἐν σώματι has no article; ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματος, "outside the body".
- **"out of the body" → "apart from the body"** (v3) — the printed text has χωρίς here (v2 ἐκτός).
- **"How that he was caught up into paradise, and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter" → "that he was snatched up into the paradise and heard unspeakable words, which it is not permitted for a man to speak"** (v4) — Greek εἰς τὸν παράδεισον, with the article; ἄρρητα ῥήματα, "words not spoken" or "not to be spoken"; οὐκ ἐξόν, "not permitted"; λαλῆσαι.
- **"Of such an one will I glory: yet of myself I will not glory, but in mine infirmities" → "On behalf of such a one I will boast, but on behalf of myself I will not boast, except in the weaknesses"** (v5) — Greek ὑπέρ twice; ἐν ταῖς ἀσθενείαις, with no "my" in the printed text. ἀσθένεια, ἀσθενέω "weakness, be weak" throughout (v9, 10; 11:21, 29, 30; 13:3, 4, 9).
- **"I shall not be a fool; for I will say the truth: but now I forbear, lest any man should think of me above that which he seeth me to be, or that he heareth of me" → "I will not be senseless, for I will speak truth; but I spare, lest anyone consider unto me above what he sees me to be or hears anything from me"** (v6) — Greek ἄφρων (11:16); φείδομαι "spare" (1:23; 13:2) with no object; εἰς ἐμὲ λογίσηται, "reckon to my account"; ἀκούει τι ἐξ ἐμοῦ. "To be" is supplied.
- **"And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations" → "and by the excess of the revelations. Therefore, that I might not exalt myself overmuch"** (v7) — in the printed text καὶ τῇ ὑπερβολῇ τῶν ἀποκαλύψεων ends the sentence of v6 (how it joins is not clear: "and that because of the excess of the revelations"), and a new sentence begins with διό, "therefore", which the KJV's text lacks. ὑπεραίρωμαι twice, middle or passive, as 2 Thess 2:4; ὑπερβολή "excess" as 4:7.
- **"a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me" → "a thorn for the flesh, an angel of Satan, that he might buffet me"** (v7) — Greek σκόλοψ τῇ σαρκί: σκόλοψ is anything pointed, a thorn, a splinter or a stake; the dative is "for the flesh" or "in the flesh". ἄγγελος Σατανᾶ, without the article: "messenger" or "angel", the same word as 11:14. κολαφίζῃ, "strike with the fist".
- **"For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me" → "About this, three times I appealed to the Lord that it might depart from me"** (v8) — Greek ὑπὲρ τούτου, "about this" or "about this one"; παρεκάλεσα (as 10:1); ἀποστῇ, "it" or "he" (the angel).
- **"he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness" → "he has said to me, "My grace is enough for you; for power is brought to completion in weakness""** (v9) — Greek εἴρηκεν, perfect; ἡ γὰρ δύναμις … τελεῖται: the printed text has no "my", and the verb is τελέω, "bring to an end, complete", not "make perfect" (τελειόω, the reading the KJV follows). δύναμις is "power" throughout (also "the power of the *Mashiach*" in this verse; 13:4).
- **"that the power of Christ may rest upon me" → "that the power of the *Mashiach* may pitch its tent upon me"** (v9) — Greek ἐπισκηνώσῃ ἐπʼ ἐμέ, from σκηνή "tent": "take up its dwelling as in a tent upon me".
- **"I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong" → "I take pleasure in weaknesses, in mistreatments, in necessities, in persecutions and straits, on behalf of the *Mashiach*; for when I am weak, then I am powerful"** (v10) — Greek εὐδοκῶ; ὕβρεσιν, insolent and violent treatment (the verb in 1 Thess 2:2); ἀνάγκαις, στενοχωρίαις as 6:4; δυνατός, the adjective of δύναμις "power" (v9), as 10:4; 13:9. Not "content with".
- **"I am become a fool in glorying … in nothing am I behind the very chiefest apostles, though I be nothing" → "I have become senseless … in nothing did I come short of the superlative apostles, even if I am nothing"** (v11) — the printed text has no "in glorying"; ὑστέρησα, τῶν ὑπερλίαν ἀποστόλων as 11:5. The sentence runs on into v12.
- **"Truly the signs of an apostle were wrought among you in all patience, in signs, and wonders, and mighty deeds" → "the signs indeed of the apostle were worked out among you in all endurance, by both signs and wonders and powers"** (v12) — Greek τοῦ ἀποστόλου, with the article and no "true"; κατειργάσθη (4:17; 7:10); ὑπομονῇ "endurance"; δυνάμεσιν, the plural of "power" (v9): deeds of power.
- **"what is it wherein ye were inferior to other churches, except it be that I myself was not burdensome to you? forgive me this wrong" → "what is it in which you were made less than the rest of the churches, except that I myself was not a burden to you? Forgive me this unrighteousness"** (v13) — Greek ἡσσώθητε ὑπέρ, "were made less, were worsted" (the verb of 2 Pet 2:19–20, "be defeated", in another sense here); κατενάρκησα as 11:9; ἀδικία.
- **"the third time I am ready to come to you" → "this third time I am ready to come to you"** (v14) — Greek τρίτον τοῦτο: the third time of being ready, or of coming (13:1). "I will not be a burden" has no "to you" in the printed text.
- **"I seek not yours … the children ought not to lay up for the parents" → "I seek not the things of yours … the children ought not to store up treasure for the parents"** (v14) — Greek τὰ ὑμῶν; θησαυρίζειν (Jas 5:3).
- **"I will very gladly spend and be spent for you; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved" → "I most gladly will spend and be utterly spent on behalf of your souls. If loving you more abundantly, am I loved less?"** (v15) — Greek ὑπὲρ τῶν ψυχῶν ὑμῶν; ἐκδαπανηθήσομαι, "be spent out"; the printed text has a question with the participle ἀγαπῶν.
- **"I did not burden you: nevertheless, being crafty, I caught you with guile" → "I did not weigh you down; but being crafty, I took you with guile"** (v16) — Greek κατεβάρησα, a different verb from v13–14; πανοῦργος (11:3); δόλῳ ὑμᾶς ἔλαβον (the verb of 11:20). The Greek has no "you say": the words give what was said of him.
- **"Did I make a gain of you by any of them whom I sent unto you?" → "Any one of those whom I have sent to you — did I defraud you through him?"** (v17) — the Greek sentence breaks off and starts again; μή expects the answer "no". πλεονεκτέω as 2:11; 7:2 (also v18).
- **"I desired Titus, and with him I sent a brother … walked we not in the same spirit? walked we not in the same steps?" → "I exhorted Titus and sent the brother with him … Did we not walk in the same spirit? Not in the same steps?"** (v18) — Greek παρεκάλεσα (8:6); τὸν ἀδελφόν, with the article; τῷ αὐτῷ πνεύματι can also be "by the same Spirit". The Greek has no "to go" and no second "walk".
- **"Again, think ye that we excuse ourselves unto you? we speak before God in Christ: but we do all things, dearly beloved, for your edifying" → "Have you been thinking all this while that we are defending ourselves to you? Before *Elohim* in the *Mashiach* we speak; but all things, beloved, are on behalf of your building up"** (v19) — the printed text has πάλαι, "for a long time" (KJV πάλιν, "again"), and a question; κατέναντι θεοῦ; the last clause has no verb ("are" is supplied); οἰκοδομή as 10:8; 13:10. The sentence runs on into v20.
- **"debates, envyings, wraths, strifes, backbitings, whisperings, swellings, tumults" → "strife, jealousy, rages, selfish ambitions, slanders, whisperings, puffings up, disorders"** (v20) — Greek ἔρις, ζῆλος (singular in the printed text; ζῆλος as 11:2), θυμοί (Col 3:8), ἐριθεῖαι (Phil 1:17), καταλαλιαί ("speakings against", Jas 4:11), ψιθυρισμοί, φυσιώσεις (Col 2:18), ἀκαταστασίαι (6:5; the singular is "unrest" in Jas 3:16).
- **"I shall be found unto you such as ye would not" → "I be found by you such as you do not want"** (v20) — Greek κἀγὼ εὑρεθῶ ὑμῖν, passive.
- **"lest, when I come again, my God will humble me among you, and that I shall bewail many which have sinned already, and have not repented of the uncleanness and fornication and lasciviousness which they have committed" → "lest, when I come again, my *Elohim* humble me before you, and I mourn many of those who have sinned before and did not repent over the uncleanness and sexual immorality and licentiousness which they practiced"** (v21) — Greek πάλιν can go with "come" or with "humble"; πρὸς ὑμᾶς, "before you, in relation to you"; πενθήσω with a plain object; προημαρτηκότων (13:2); ἐπὶ τῇ ἀκαθαρσίᾳ καὶ πορνείᾳ καὶ ἀσελγείᾳ. The Greek has no "I may have to".

### Notes
- **v2–4** — one sentence in Greek; "I know" has two objects, "a man … snatched up" (v2) and "such a man … that he was snatched up" (v3–4).
