# 2 Corinthians 3

[1] Are we beginning again to commend ourselves? Or do we need, as some, letters of commendation to you or from you?

[2] You are our letter, inscribed in our hearts, known and read by all men,

[3] being manifested that you are a letter of ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ served by us, inscribed not with ink but with the Spirit of the living ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, not in tablets of stone but in tablets that are hearts of flesh.

[4] And such confidence we have through ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ toward ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[5] Not that we are sufficient of ourselves to consider anything as from ourselves, but our sufficiency is from ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧,

[6] who also made us sufficient as servants of a new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit; **for the letter kills, but the Spirit makes alive.**

[7] But if the service of death, engraved in letters on stones, came to be in glory, so that the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ could not gaze into the face of ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ because of the glory of his face, which was being abolished,

[8] how will the service of the Spirit not rather be in glory?

[9] For if there was glory for the service of condemnation, much more does the service of righteousness abound in glory.

[10] For indeed what has been glorified has not been glorified in this respect, on account of the exceeding glory.

[11] For if what is being abolished was through glory, much more what remains is in glory.

[12] Having therefore such a hope, we use much boldness,

[13] and not as ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ used to put a veil upon his face, so that the sons of ⟦israel|Yisrael|Israel⟧ should not gaze unto the end of what was being abolished.

[14] But their thoughts were hardened. For until this very day the same veil remains upon the reading of the old covenant, not being unveiled, because in ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ it is abolished;

[15] but until today, whenever ⟦moses|Moshe|Moses⟧ is read, a veil lies upon their heart;

[16] but whenever it turns to ⟦yhwh|Yahweh|the Lord⟧, the veil is stripped away.

[17] Now the Lord is the Spirit; and **where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty**.

[18] But we all, with unveiled face seeing as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory unto glory, even as from the Lord, the Spirit.

## Changes

- **"Christ" → "*Mashiach*"** (v3, 4, 14) — systematic.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v3, 4, 5) — systematic.
- **"epistles of commendation" → "letters of commendation"; "as some others" → "as some"** (v1) — Greek συστατικῶν ἐπιστολῶν, of the root of συνιστάνειν "commend" in the same verse; ὥς τινες. The second question (μή) expects the answer no. "Letters of commendation" stands once in the printed text.
- **"Ye are our epistle written in our hearts" → "You are our letter, inscribed in our hearts"** (v2) — Greek ἐγγεγραμμένη, "written in", the same word in v3. The Greek has no "of recommendation" and no "yourselves". "Known and read" is γινωσκομένη καὶ ἀναγινωσκομένη, two verbs of one root (as 1:13).
- **"Forasmuch as ye are manifestly declared to be the epistle of Christ ministered by us" → "being manifested that you are a letter of the *Mashiach* served by us"** (v3) — Greek φανερούμενοι ὅτι ἐστὲ ἐπιστολὴ Χριστοῦ διακονηθεῖσα ὑφʼ ἡμῶν. Χριστοῦ is a plain genitive, not "from". διακονέω is the verb of διακονία "service" (v7–9) and διάκονος "servant" (v6): the letter is what our service brought about and carried; the same construction in 8:19–20.
- **"the Spirit of the living God" kept** (v3) — Greek πνεύματι θεοῦ ζῶντος, without the article.
- **"not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart" → "not in tablets of stone but in tablets that are hearts of flesh"** (v3) — the printed text reads ἐν πλαξὶν καρδίαις σαρκίναις: "hearts" stands beside "tablets" in the same case. σάρκινος is "made of flesh", as λίθινος is "made of stone"; the Greek has no "human".
- **"such trust" → "such confidence"** (v4) — Greek πεποίθησιν, as 1:15.
- **"to think any thing as of ourselves; but our sufficiency is of God" → "to consider anything as from ourselves, but our sufficiency is from *Elohim*"** (v5) — Greek λογίσασθαι, "consider, reckon"; ἀφʼ ἑαυτῶν "of ourselves", then ἐξ αὑτῶν and ἐκ τοῦ θεοῦ, "from". The Greek has no "claim".
- **"hath made us able ministers of the new testament" → "made us sufficient as servants of a new covenant"** (v6) — Greek ἱκάνωσεν, the root of "sufficient" and "sufficiency" in v5 (and 2:16); διακόνους, "servants"; καινῆς διαθήκης, "of a new covenant", without the article. διαθήκη is "covenant".
- **"not of the letter, but of the spirit" kept, with "Spirit"** (v6) — Greek οὐ γράμματος ἀλλὰ πνεύματος, both without the article; the second half of the verse has the article with both. γράμμα is what is written, a written character (plural "letters" in v7). English has the one word "letter" for γράμμα and for ἐπιστολή (v1–3); they are different words.
- **"giveth life" → "makes alive"** (v6) — Greek ζῳοποιεῖ (as Col 2:13).
- **"ministration" → "service"** (v7, 8, 9) — Greek διακονία, four times.
- **"written and engraven in stones" → "engraved in letters on stones"** (v7) — Greek ἐν γράμμασιν ἐντετυπωμένη λίθοις; λίθοις is plural.
- **"was glorious" → "came to be in glory"; "be rather glorious" → "rather be in glory"** (v7, 8) — Greek ἐγενήθη ἐν δόξῃ; ἔσται ἐν δόξῃ. The Greek has no "such" and no "even more".
- **"the children of Israel could not stedfastly behold the face of Moses" → "the sons of *Yisrael* could not gaze into the face of *Moshe*"** (v7, 13) — Greek τοὺς υἱοὺς Ἰσραήλ, "the sons of Israel"; ἀτενίσαι εἰς, "look intently into".
- **"for the glory of his countenance" → "because of the glory of his face"** (v7) — Greek πρόσωπον, twice in the verse, "face" both times.
- **"which glory was to be done away" / "that which is done away" / "that which is abolished" / "is done away" → "which was being abolished" / "what is being abolished" / "what was being abolished" / "is abolished"** (v7, 11, 13, 14) — Greek καταργέω, four times, one English word; in v7, 11, 13 a present participle: the abolishing is going on.
- **"For if the ministration of condemnation be glory" → "For if there was glory for the service of condemnation"** (v9) — the printed text reads τῇ διακονίᾳ, a dative, "for the service"; the Greek has no verb ("there was" is supplied).
- **"exceed in glory" → "abound in glory"** (v9) — Greek περισσεύει, as 1:5.
- **"For even that which was made glorious had no glory in this respect, by reason of the glory that excelleth" → "For indeed what has been glorified has not been glorified in this respect, on account of the exceeding glory"** (v10) — Greek οὐ δεδόξασται τὸ δεδοξασμένον, the same verb twice, both perfect; ὑπερβαλλούσης, the verb of ὑπερβολή "excess" (4:7, 17).
- **"was glorious … is glorious" → "was through glory … is in glory"** (v11) — Greek διὰ δόξης … ἐν δόξῃ, two different prepositions, and no verb ("was", "is" are supplied).
- **"that which remaineth" → "what remains"** (v11) — Greek τὸ μένον; μένω is "abide" of persons, "remain" of a thing as here and in v14.
- **"we use great plainness of speech" → "we use much boldness"** (v12) — Greek πολλῇ παρρησίᾳ χρώμεθα.
- **"which put a vail over his face" → "used to put a veil upon his face"** (v13) — Greek ἐτίθει, imperfect: he did it repeatedly (Exod 34:33–35).
- **"could not stedfastly look to the end" → "should not gaze unto the end"** (v13) — Greek πρὸς τὸ μὴ ἀτενίσαι … εἰς τὸ τέλος, a purpose. τέλος is "end"; it can be the finish or the goal.
- **"their minds were blinded" → "their thoughts were hardened"** (v14) — Greek ἐπωρώθη τὰ νοήματα αὐτῶν; πωρόω is "make hard, callous" (a different word from σκληρύνω, also "harden"); νοήματα as 2:11; 4:4.
- **"until this day remaineth the same vail untaken away in the reading of the old testament; which vail is done away in Christ" → "until this very day the same veil remains upon the reading of the old covenant, not being unveiled, because in the *Mashiach* it is abolished"** (v14) — Greek ἄχρι … τῆς σήμερον ἡμέρας; ἐπὶ τῇ ἀναγνώσει, "upon the reading"; μὴ ἀνακαλυπτόμενον, ὅτι ἐν Χριστῷ καταργεῖται. The last words can also be read "it not being unveiled that in the *Mashiach* it is abolished". "It" can be the veil or the old covenant. The Greek has no "only" and no "they read".
- **"the vail is upon their heart" → "a veil lies upon their heart"** (v15) — Greek κάλυμμα … κεῖται, without the article; καρδίαν is singular.
- **"when it shall turn to the Lord, the vail shall be taken away" → "whenever it turns to *Yahweh*, the veil is stripped away"** (v16) — Greek ἡνίκα δὲ ἐὰν ἐπιστρέψῃ πρὸς κύριον, περιαιρεῖται τὸ κάλυμμα, present tense. The verb has no stated subject: "it" (their heart, v15), or "he" (*Moshe*, or anyone). The words follow Exod 34:34 in the Septuagint, where κύριος stands for the divine name; here κύριον has no article.
- **"the Lord is that Spirit" → "the Lord is the Spirit"; "liberty" kept** (v17) — Greek ὁ δὲ κύριος τὸ πνεῦμά ἐστιν; ἐλευθερία. The second half has no verb ("is … there is" is supplied).
- **"with open face beholding as in a glass" → "with unveiled face seeing as in a mirror"** (v18) — Greek ἀνακεκαλυμμένῳ προσώπῳ, the verb of v14; κατοπτριζόμενοι, from κάτοπτρον "mirror": "seeing as in a mirror", or "reflecting as a mirror".
- **"are changed into the same image from glory to glory" → "are being transformed into the same image from glory unto glory"** (v18) — Greek μεταμορφούμεθα, present tense; ἀπὸ δόξης εἰς δόξαν. The Greek has no "one degree".
- **"even as by the Spirit of the Lord" → "even as from the Lord, the Spirit"** (v18) — Greek καθάπερ ἀπὸ κυρίου πνεύματος, two genitives without articles. Also possible: "from the Lord of the Spirit", "from the Spirit of the Lord", "from the Spirit who is Lord". The Greek has no "this comes".
- **"tables of stone" → "tablets of stone"** (v3) — πλαξὶν λιθίναις; modernized.

### Notes
- **v7–13** — the account of the face of *Moshe* is Exod 34:29–35.
- **v2–3, 6–7** — "letter" in v1–3 is ἐπιστολή, a letter that is sent; in v6–7 it is γράμμα, what is written.
