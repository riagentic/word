# Proverbs 27

[1] Do not boast of the day of tomorrow,
for you do not know what a day will bring forth.

[2] Let a stranger praise you, and not your mouth;
a foreigner, and not your lips.

[3] The heaviness of a stone and the weight of the sand,
and the vexation of a senseless one is heavier than the two of them.

[4] The cruelty of fury and the flood of anger,
and who can stand before jealousy?

[5] Better is reproof uncovered
than love hidden.

[6] Faithful are the wounds of one who loves,
and profuse are the kisses of one who hates.

[7] A sated soul tramples a honeycomb,
and to a hungry soul everything bitter is sweet.

[8] Like a bird wandering from its nest,
so is a man wandering from his place.

[9] Oil and incense make the heart glad,
and the sweetness of one's friend is from counsel of the soul.

[10] Your friend and the friend of your father do not forsake,
and the house of your brother do not enter in the day of your calamity.
Better is a neighbor near than a brother far off.

[11] Be wise, my son, and make my heart glad,
that I may return a word to the one who taunts me.

[12] The shrewd one sees evil and hides himself;
the simple pass on and are punished.

[13] Take his garment, for he has become surety for a stranger,
and for a foreign woman take him in pledge.

[14] One who blesses his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning,
a curse it shall be reckoned to him.

[15] A continual dripping on a day of downpour
and a woman of strifes are alike.

[16] Those who hide her hide wind,
and the oil of his right hand calls out.

[17] Iron is sharpened by iron,
and a man sharpens the face of his friend.

[18] One who guards a fig tree will eat its fruit,
and one who keeps his master will be honored.

[19] As in water face is to face,
so the heart of man is to man.

[20] Sheol and ⟦abaddon|Avaddon|Abaddon⟧ are not sated,
and the eyes of man are not sated.

[21] The crucible for silver and the furnace for gold,
and a man according to his praise.

[22] If you pound the senseless one in the mortar, among the groats, with the pestle,
his folly will not depart from upon him.

[23] You shall surely know the face of your flock;
set your heart to the droves,

[24] for a store is not forever,
and is a crown for generation and generation?

[25] The grass has gone, and the vegetation has appeared,
and the plants of the mountains are gathered.

[26] Lambs for your clothing,
and the price of a field, he-goats,

[27] and enough goats' milk for your bread,
for the bread of your house,
and life for your girls.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — one Hebrew line (colon) per printed line, in the Hebrew order; v10 and v27 keep three lines, where the Hebrew first colon has a strong inner pause.
- **"Boast not thyself of to morrow" → "Do not boast of the day of tomorrow"** (v1) — Hebrew אַל־תִּתְהַלֵּל בְּיוֹם מָחָר "do not boast in the day of tomorrow" (hitpael of הִלֵּל "praise", as 25:14 "boasting"; v2 "praise"); מַה־יֵּלֶד יוֹם "what a day will bring forth" (יָלַד "bear").
- **"Let another man praise thee … a stranger" → "Let a stranger praise you … a foreigner"** (v2) — Hebrew זָר "stranger", נָכְרִי "foreigner" (WORDS-HE); יְהַלֶּלְךָ a wish: "let … praise". "own" is not written.
- **"A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool's wrath is heavier than them both" → "The heaviness of a stone and the weight of the sand, and the vexation of a senseless one is heavier than the two of them"** (v3) — Hebrew כֹּבֶד "heaviness", נֵטֶל "weight" (nouns, not verbs); וְ "and" (not "but"); כַּעַס "vexation" (WORDS-HE, the vexation he causes); אֱוִיל "senseless one" (WISDOM.md; not כְּסִיל "fool"); כָּבֵד מִשְּׁנֵיהֶם "heavier than the two of them".
- **"Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy?" → "The cruelty of fury and the flood of anger, and who can stand before jealousy?"** (v4) — Hebrew אַכְזְרִיּוּת חֵמָה "cruelty of fury" (חֵמָה "fury", WORDS-HE), שֶׁטֶף אַף "a flood of anger" (אַף "anger"); nouns with no verb; וּמִי "and who"; קִנְאָה "jealousy" (WORDS-HE: "envy" of a rival only with a note).
- **"Open rebuke is better than secret love" → "Better is reproof uncovered than love hidden"** (v5) — Hebrew order kept; תּוֹכַחַת "reproof" (WISDOM.md); מְגֻלָּה "uncovered" (pual of גָּלָה, 26:26); מְסֻתָּרֶת "hidden".
- **"Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful" → "Faithful are the wounds of one who loves, and profuse are the kisses of one who hates"** (v6) — Hebrew אוֹהֵב "one who loves", שׂוֹנֵא "one who hates" (participles of אָהַב, שָׂנֵא; not רֵעַ "friend", אֹיֵב "enemy"); פֶּצַע "wound"; נַעְתָּרוֹת is uncertain: "abundant, profuse" (from עָתַר II, Ezek 35:13), or "importunate", or "deceitful" (KJV, a guess from the context).
- **"The full soul loatheth an honeycomb" → "A sated soul tramples a honeycomb"** (v7) — Hebrew תָּבוּס "tramples" (בּוּס, as Ps 44:5; "loathes" is the sense); נֹפֶת "honeycomb" (flowing honey); נֶפֶשׁ "soul" both times; no "but".
- **"As a bird that wandereth from her nest, so is a man that wandereth from his place"** (v8) — kept; נָדַד "wander" (another verb than נוּד "flit", 26:2); מְקוֹמוֹ "his place" (not "home").
- **"Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart: so doth the sweetness of a man's friend by hearty counsel" → "Oil and incense make the heart glad, and the sweetness of one's friend is from counsel of the soul"** (v9) — Hebrew שֶׁמֶן "oil", קְטֹרֶת "incense" (WORDS-HE: the word of the altar incense); רֵעֵהוּ "his friend" ("one's" for the general "his"); מֵעֲצַת־נָפֶשׁ "from counsel of soul" is uncertain: "from heartfelt counsel", or with מִן of comparison "more than one's own counsel" (= a friend's advice is sweeter than your own). עֵצָה "counsel", נֶפֶשׁ "soul".
- **"Thine own friend, and thy father's friend, forsake not" → "Your friend and the friend of your father do not forsake"** (v10) — qere וְרֵעַ "and the friend of"; the written form ורעה is another spelling of the same word. The objects stand first, as kept; עָזַב "forsake"; אֵיד "calamity". רֵעַ "friend" in this chapter (v9, 10, 14, 17), the word that is "neighbor" in 25:8–18 (one word, two English words by the context: friendship here, a neighbor at law there); שָׁכֵן (v10) is "neighbor", one who dwells near.
- **"Better is a neighbour that is near than a brother far off"** (v10) — kept; שָׁכֵן קָרוֹב, אָח רָחוֹק.
- **"that I may answer him that reproacheth me" → "that I may return a word to the one who taunts me"** (v11) — Hebrew וְאָשִׁיבָה … דָבָר, cohortative: "and let me return a word" (הֵשִׁיב דָּבָר "bring back word", WORDS-HE); חֹרְפִי "the one who taunts me" (חָרַף, noun חֶרְפָּה "reproach").
- **"A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished" → "The shrewd one sees evil and hides himself; the simple pass on and are punished"** (v12) — Hebrew עָרוּם "shrewd" (WORDS-HE, WISDOM.md); רָאָה "sees"; רָעָה "evil"; נִסְתָּר "hides himself"; פְּתָאיִם "the simple"; נֶעֱנָשׁוּ "are punished" (nifal of עָנַשׁ, the "fine" of Exod 21:22: a penalty laid on them; as 21:11; 22:3). The Hebrew has no "and" and no "but" (perfects without a link: "sees, hides … pass on, are punished"); English needs the "and". The same saying stands at 22:3, where the Hebrew has "and" twice (qere וְנִסְתָּר, וּפְתָיִים … וְנֶעֱנָשׁוּ, there "but the simple"): one wording otherwise.
- **"Take his garment that is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman" → "Take his garment, for he has become surety for a stranger, and for a foreign woman take him in pledge"** (v13) — Hebrew כִּי "for"; עָרַב "be surety for" (WORDS-HE); זָר "stranger"; נָכְרִיָּה "foreign woman" (WISDOM.md); חַבְלֵהוּ "take him in pledge" (חָבַל, WORDS-HE). The same saying stands at 20:16, where the written form is נכרים "foreigners" and the qere נָכְרִיָּה, as here.
- **"He that blesseth his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted a curse to him"** (v14) — kept, in the Hebrew order; קוֹל גָּדוֹל "loud voice"; הַשְׁכֵּים "rising early" (WORDS-HE); קְלָלָה "curse"; תֵּחָשֶׁב לוֹ "it shall be reckoned to him" (חָשַׁב, WORDS-HE).
- **"A continual dropping in a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike" → "A continual dripping on a day of downpour and a woman of strifes are alike"** (v15) — Hebrew דֶּלֶף טוֹרֵד "a continual dripping" (as 19:13); סַגְרִיר "downpour" (only here); qere מִדְיָנִים "strifes" (as 25:24; the written form מדונים); נִשְׁתָּוָה "are alike" (a rare form of שָׁוָה "be equal", 26:4).
- **"Whosoever hideth her hideth the wind, and the ointment of his right hand, which bewrayeth itself" → "Those who hide her hide wind, and the oil of his right hand calls out"** (v16) — Hebrew צֹפְנֶיהָ צָפַן־רוּחַ "those who hide her have hidden wind" (צָפַן "hide away"; plural subject, singular verb); וְשֶׁמֶן יְמִינוֹ יִקְרָא "and the oil of his right hand calls out" (= it cannot be hidden), or "and his right hand meets oil" (קָרָא II, "meet": he cannot hold it). The verse is obscure; "restrain her … grasp oil" (the existing text) changes both verbs.
- **"Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend" → "Iron is sharpened by iron, and a man sharpens the face of his friend"** (v17) — Hebrew יָחַד, from חָדַד "be sharp" (as pointed: "is sharpened"), and יַחַד "sharpens"; פְּנֵי "the face of" (kept; = the presence, the person); וְ "and".
- **"Whoso keepeth the fig tree … so he that waiteth on his master shall be honoured" → "One who guards a fig tree … and one who keeps his master will be honored"** (v18) — Hebrew נֹצֵר "guards", שֹׁמֵר "keeps" (WORDS-HE: two verbs, kept apart; of a master: watch over, attend); אֲדֹנָיו "his master" (a plural form used of one master, as Gen 24:9; contrast 25:13); יְכֻבָּד "will be honored" (כִּבֵּד).
- **"As in water face answereth to face, so the heart of man to man" → "As in water face is to face, so the heart of man is to man"** (v19) — Hebrew כַּמַּיִם הַפָּנִים לַפָּנִים "as the water: the face to the face"; כֵּן לֵב־הָאָדָם לָאָדָם "so the heart of the man to the man" (no verb: "answereth", "reflects" are supplied by others; "is" here). Whether one's heart shows oneself or another man's heart is meant is not said.
- **"Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied" → "Sheol and Abaddon are not sated, and the eyes of man are not sated"** (v20) — Hebrew שְׁאוֹל "Sheol" (OT.md); qere אֲבַדּוֹ, a short form of אֲבַדּוֹן *Abaddon* ("destruction", the place of the dead; token kept); the written form ואבדה is another spelling. לֹא תִשְׂבַּעְנָה "are not sated", the same verb twice ("never" is not written).
- **"As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise" → "The crucible for silver and the furnace for gold, and a man according to his praise"** (v21) — Hebrew מַצְרֵף "crucible" (from צָרַף "refine", the "refiner" of 25:4); כּוּר "furnace" (WORDS-HE); the first line = 17:3a; לְפִי מַהֲלָלוֹ "according to (literally 'by the mouth of') his praise": a man is tried by what he is praised for, or by how he bears it; "tested" is not written.
- **"Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his foolishness depart from him" → "If you pound the senseless one in the mortar, among the groats, with the pestle, his folly will not depart from upon him"** (v22) — Hebrew אִם "if"; כָּתַשׁ "pound"; הָאֱוִיל "the senseless one" (WISDOM.md); הָרִיפוֹת "groats" (crushed grain; uncertain); עֱלִי "pestle"; סוּר מֵעָלָיו "depart from upon him" (WORDS-HE "depart from"); אִוֶּלֶת "folly".
- **"Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds" → "You shall surely know the face of your flock; set your heart to the droves"** (v23) — Hebrew יָדֹעַ תֵּדַע, infinitive absolute with its verb: "you shall surely know" (literally "knowing you shall know"); פְּנֵי צֹאנֶךָ "the face of your flock" (צֹאן sheep and goats, singular); שִׁית לִבְּךָ לַעֲדָרִים "set your heart to the droves" (WORDS-HE: עֵדֶר "drove"; לֵב "heart").
- **"For riches are not for ever: and doth the crown endure to every generation?" → "for a store is not forever, and is a crown for generation and generation?"** (v24) — Hebrew חֹסֶן "store" (laid-up strength, abundance; Isa 33:6; not עֹשֶׁר "riches", הוֹן "wealth"); לְעוֹלָם "forever"; וְאִם "and if / or": a question ("and is …?"); qere לְדוֹר וָדוֹר "for generation and generation", the written form לדור דור without "and". "endure" is not written. נֵזֶר "crown" (WORDS-HE).
- **"The hay appeareth, and the tender grass sheweth itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered" → "The grass has gone, and the vegetation has appeared, and the plants of the mountains are gathered"** (v25) — Hebrew גָּלָה חָצִיר: גָּלָה qal is "go away, go into exile" (WORDS-HE), so "the grass has gone" (cut and taken in); "appears" (KJV) reads it as "uncovers itself". נִרְאָה "has appeared" (literally "is seen"); דֶּשֶׁא "vegetation", עֵשֶׂב "plant" (WORDS-HE); perfects, kept in the past. v25 sets the time for v26–27.
- **"The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of the field" → "Lambs for your clothing, and the price of a field, he-goats"** (v26) — Hebrew כְּבָשִׂים "lambs" (male lambs); מְחִיר שָׂדֶה "the price of a field" (no article); עַתּוּדִים "he-goats" (WORDS-HE); no verbs.
- **"And thou shalt have goats' milk enough for thy food, for the food of thy household, and for the maintenance for thy maidens" → "and enough goats' milk for your bread, for the bread of your house, and life for your girls"** (v27) — Hebrew וְדֵי "and enough"; לֶחֶם "bread" twice (WORDS-HE: "food" only with a note: here bread stands for food); בֵּיתֶךָ "your house"; חַיִּים "life" (= living, sustenance); נַעֲרוֹת "girls" (WORDS-HE: also of attendants).

### Notes
- **v23–27** — a small poem on the herds: they outlast stored wealth and a crown.
