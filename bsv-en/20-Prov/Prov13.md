# Proverbs 13

[1] A wise son is a father's discipline,
but a scoffer does not hear rebuke.

[2] From the fruit of the mouth of a man he eats good,
but the soul of the treacherous, violence.

[3] He who guards his mouth keeps his soul;
he who opens wide his lips, ruin is his.

[4] The soul of the sluggard craves, and there is nothing,
but the soul of the diligent is made fat.

[5] A word of falsehood the righteous hates,
but the wicked makes a stink and brings shame.

[6] Righteousness guards the one blameless of way,
but wickedness overturns sin.

[7] There is one who makes himself rich, and there is nothing at all;
one who makes himself destitute, and wealth is great.

[8] The ransom of the soul of a man is his riches,
but the destitute does not hear rebuke.

[9] The light of the righteous rejoices,
but the lamp of the wicked goes out.

[10] Only by presumption comes a struggle,
but with those who take counsel is wisdom.

[11] Wealth from vapor grows less,
but he who gathers by hand increases.

[12] Expectation drawn out is sickness of heart,
but a tree of life is desire that has come.

[13] He who despises the word will be held in pledge to it,
but he who fears the commandment, he will be repaid.

[14] The ⟦torah|torah|teaching⟧ of the wise is a fountain of life,
to turn aside from the snares of death.

[15] Good insight gives favor,
but the way of the treacherous is enduring.

[16] Every shrewd one acts with knowledge,
but a fool spreads out folly.

[17] A wicked messenger falls into evil,
but an envoy of faithfulness is healing.

[18] Poverty and disgrace — he who lets discipline go;
but he who keeps reproof is honored.

[19] Desire that has come to be is sweet to the soul,
but the abomination of fools is to turn aside from evil.

[20] He who walks with the wise becomes wise,
but he who keeps company with fools suffers harm.

[21] Evil pursues sinners,
but good repays the righteous.

[22] The good one makes sons of sons inherit,
but stored up for the righteous is the wealth of the sinner.

[23] Much food is the fallow ground of the destitute,
but there is what is swept away without judgment.

[24] He who withholds his rod hates his son,
but he who loves him seeks him eagerly with discipline.

[25] The righteous eats to the satiety of his soul,
but the belly of the wicked lacks.

## Changes

- **Layout** (throughout) — two lines (cola) to a saying, in the Hebrew order, as the accents divide them. The second line opens with וְ in most verses: "but" where the two lines are set against each other (as Ps 1:6), "and" where they run together; where the Hebrew has no וְ (v3, 7b, 14) none is written.
- **Words of the chapter** — מוּסָר "discipline" (v1, 18, 24), תּוֹכַחַת "reproof" (v18), גְּעָרָה "rebuke" (v1, 8; as 2 Sam 22:16), לֵץ "scoffer", כְּסִיל "fool", אִוֶּלֶת "folly", עָרוּם "shrewd" (v16), שֵׂכֶל "insight" (v15), דַּעַת "knowledge", נֶפֶשׁ "soul" (v2, 3, 4, 8, 19, 25: the Hebrew word also says "appetite, desire, life"; one word kept), בֹּגְדִים "the treacherous" (v2, 15; participle of בָּגַד "deal treacherously"), רָשׁ / רֹאשׁ "destitute" (v8, 23; beside דַּל "poor"; its verb in v7), הוֹן "wealth" (v7, 11), עֹשֶׁר "riches" (v8), תַּאֲוָה "desire" (v12, 19; elsewhere "craving": here a good wish, so "desire"). The Hebrew perfects of general truth (v1, 8 שָׁמַע) are English presents.
- **"A wise son heareth his father's instruction" → "A wise son is a father's discipline"** (v1) — Hebrew בֵּן חָכָם מוּסַר אָב: the line has no verb; "is" is supplied. Read as it stands, the wise son is what a father's discipline has made; KJV supplies "heareth" from the second line (a change of one letter, אֹהֵב "loves", is also proposed). **"a scorner heareth not rebuke" → "a scoffer does not hear rebuke"** — Hebrew לֵץ, גְּעָרָה.
- **"A man shall eat good by the fruit of his mouth: but the soul of the transgressors shall eat violence" → "From the fruit of the mouth of a man he eats good, but the soul of the treacherous, violence"** (v2) — Hebrew מִפְּרִי פִי־אִישׁ יֹאכַל טוֹב, the Hebrew order. The second line has no verb: "eats" of the first line is understood, and the English leaves it out the same way; נֶפֶשׁ may also be "appetite" ("the appetite of the treacherous is violence"). בֹּגְדִים "the treacherous" (not "transgressors").
- **"He that keepeth his mouth keepeth his life: but he that openeth wide his lips shall have destruction" → "He who guards his mouth keeps his soul; he who opens wide his lips, ruin is his"** (v3) — Hebrew נֹצֵר "guards", שֹׁמֵר "keeps" (two verbs); נַפְשׁוֹ "his soul"; פֹּשֵׂק "opens wide" (only here and Ezek 16:25); מְחִתָּה־לוֹ, literally "ruin to him" (מְחִתָּה "ruin", as Prov 10:14). No "but" in the Hebrew.
- **"The soul of the sluggard desireth, and hath nothing" → "The soul of the sluggard craves, and there is nothing"** (v4) — Hebrew מִתְאַוָּה וָאַיִן נַפְשׁוֹ עָצֵל, word for word "craving, and there is not, his soul, the sluggard": הִתְאַוָּה "crave" (as Num 11:4), וָאַיִן "and there is nothing". **"shall be made fat" → "is made fat"** — Hebrew תְּדֻשָּׁן, pual of דָּשֵׁן "grow fat"; חָרֻצִים "diligent".
- **"A righteous man hateth lying: but a wicked man is loathsome, and cometh to shame" → "A word of falsehood the righteous hates, but the wicked makes a stink and brings shame"** (v5) — Hebrew דְּבַר־שֶׁקֶר "a word of falsehood" stands first. יַבְאִישׁ hifil "makes stink" (as Gen 34:30; "acts odiously" is the usual sense), וְיַחְפִּיר hifil of חָפֵר "be abashed": "brings shame". Neither verb has an object: whether the wicked shames himself or others is not said.
- **"Righteousness keepeth him that is upright in the way: but wickedness overthroweth the sinner" → "Righteousness guards the one blameless of way, but wickedness overturns sin"** (v6) — Hebrew תִּצֹּר "guards"; תָּם־דָּרֶךְ "blameless of way" (תָּם "blameless", not "upright": that is יָשָׁר); תְּסַלֵּף "overturns" (as Exod 23:8); חַטָּאת "sin", not "the sinner" (the abstract stands for the one who sins, or "sin" is the subject: "sin overturns wickedness"; the Hebrew allows both).
- **"There is that maketh himself rich, yet hath nothing: there is that maketh himself poor, yet hath great riches" → "There is one who makes himself rich, and there is nothing at all; one who makes himself destitute, and wealth is great"** (v7) — Hebrew מִתְעַשֵּׁר, מִתְרוֹשֵׁשׁ: hitpaels, "make oneself rich / destitute" (pretending, or making oneself so by spending or hoarding: the form allows both); וְאֵין כֹּל "and there is not anything"; וְהוֹן רָב "and wealth is much". The second line has no "there is" (it carries over).
- **"The ransom of a man's life are his riches: but the poor heareth not rebuke" → "The ransom of the soul of a man is his riches, but the destitute does not hear rebuke"** (v8) — Hebrew כֹּפֶר "ransom", נֶפֶשׁ־אִישׁ "the soul of a man", עָשְׁרוֹ "his riches"; רָשׁ "destitute". The destitute man hears no threat: no one demands a ransom of him.
- **"The light of the righteous rejoiceth: but the lamp of the wicked shall be put out" → "… but the lamp of the wicked goes out"** (v9) — Hebrew יִשְׂמָח "rejoices"; יִדְעָךְ, qal of דָּעַךְ "be extinguished", not a passive.
- **"Only by pride cometh contention: but with the well advised is wisdom" → "Only by presumption comes a struggle, but with those who take counsel is wisdom"** (v10) — Hebrew רַק־בְּזָדוֹן יִתֵּן מַצָּה, literally "only by presumption he gives a struggle" (the verb has no named subject; English "he gives" would point to a person, so "comes"); זָדוֹן "presumption" (as Deut 17:12); מַצָּה "struggle" (from נָצָה "struggle"; beside מָדוֹן "strife", רִיב "contention"). נוֹעָצִים, nifal of יָעַץ: "those who take counsel (together)".
- **"Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by labour shall increase" → "Wealth from vapor grows less, but he who gathers by hand increases"** (v11) — Hebrew מֵהֶבֶל "from vapor" (הֶבֶל "vapor, breath"; traditional "vanity": wealth got from nothing, or by empty means; the Greek reads מְבֹהָל "in haste": not followed, MT kept). עַל־יָד, literally "upon hand": by hand, little by little. יַרְבֶּה "increases" has no object.
- **"Hope deferred maketh the heart sick: but when the desire cometh, it is a tree of life" → "Expectation drawn out is sickness of heart, but a tree of life is desire that has come"** (v12) — Hebrew תּוֹחֶלֶת מְמֻשָּׁכָה "expectation drawn out" (תּוֹחֶלֶת, noun of יִחֵל "hope", beside תִּקְוָה "hope", as 10:28; pual of מָשַׁךְ), מַחֲלָה־לֵב "sickness of heart" (a noun, not a verb); the second line in the Hebrew order: וְעֵץ חַיִּים תַּאֲוָה בָאָה.
- **"Whoso despiseth the word shall be destroyed: but he that feareth the commandment shall be rewarded" → "He who despises the word will be held in pledge to it, but he who fears the commandment, he will be repaid"** (v13) — Hebrew יֵחָבֶל לוֹ, nifal of חָבַל: "be taken in pledge" (the verb of Exod 22:26), so "held in pledge to it", in debt to the word he despised; חָבַל is also "ruin" (KJV "destroyed"): uncertain. הוּא יְשֻׁלָּם, pual of שִׁלֵּם "repay", with the pronoun "he" written; "rewarded" says more.
- **"The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death" → "The torah of the wise is a fountain of life, to turn aside from the snares of death"** (v14) — Hebrew תּוֹרַת חָכָם: the token keeps the word as written ("teaching" here: the instruction of a wise man, not the Torah of Moses). מְקוֹר "fountain" (as Lev 12:7); לָסוּר "to turn aside"; מוֹקְשֵׁי מָוֶת "the snares of death" (as 2 Sam 22:6). No "and" in the Hebrew.
- **"Good understanding giveth favour: but the way of transgressors is hard" → "Good insight gives favor, but the way of the treacherous is enduring"** (v15) — Hebrew שֵׂכֶל־טוֹב "good insight"; חֵן "favor". אֵיתָן "lasting, enduring" (as Deut 21:4 "ever-flowing"; Num 24:21 "enduring"); KJV "hard" takes the sense "firm, rough"; the Greek has "in destruction", and many change the Hebrew to אֵידָם "their calamity": not followed. Uncertain.
- **"Every prudent man dealeth with knowledge: but a fool layeth open his folly" → "Every shrewd one acts with knowledge, but a fool spreads out folly"** (v16) — Hebrew כָּל־עָרוּם "every shrewd one" (no "man"); יַעֲשֶׂה בְדָעַת "acts with knowledge"; יִפְרֹשׂ "spreads out" (as a cloth or a net); אִוֶּלֶת "folly", no "his".
- **"A wicked messenger falleth into mischief: but a faithful ambassador is health" → "A wicked messenger falls into evil, but an envoy of faithfulness is healing"** (v17) — Hebrew יִפֹּל בְּרָע "falls into evil" (pointed יַפִּל it would be "makes fall"); צִיר אֱמוּנִים "an envoy of faithfulness" (plural noun); מַרְפֵּא "healing".
- **"Poverty and shame shall be to him that refuseth instruction" → "Poverty and disgrace — he who lets discipline go"** (v18) — Hebrew רֵישׁ וְקָלוֹן פּוֹרֵעַ מוּסָר: no verb and no "to"; the dash keeps the gap (the sense "are his"). פָּרַע "let loose, let go" (as Exod 5:4); קָלוֹן "disgrace". **"he that regardeth reproof shall be honoured" → "he who keeps reproof is honored"** — Hebrew שׁוֹמֵר "keeps"; יְכֻבָּד, pual of כִּבֵּד "honor".
- **"The desire accomplished is sweet to the soul: but it is abomination to fools to depart from evil" → "Desire that has come to be is sweet to the soul, but the abomination of fools is to turn aside from evil"** (v19) — Hebrew תַּאֲוָה נִהְיָה, nifal of הָיָה "come to be"; תֶּעֱרַב "is sweet" (עָרֵב "be pleasant"); תּוֹעֲבַת כְּסִילִים "the abomination of fools"; סוּר מֵרָע "to turn aside from evil" (as Job 1:1).
- **"He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed" → "He who walks with the wise becomes wise, but he who keeps company with fools suffers harm"** (v20) — qere הוֹלֵךְ … יֶחְכָּם "he who walks … becomes wise"; the written form (ketiv) הלוך … וחכם reads "Walk with the wise and be wise". רֹעֶה "one who keeps company with" (רָעָה "associate with", Judg 14:20; the same letters as רֹעֶה "shepherd"); יֵרוֹעַ, nifal of רָעַע "be bad": "suffers harm" (literally "is made bad"), a play on רֹעֶה.
- **"Evil pursueth sinners: but to the righteous good shall be repayed" → "Evil pursues sinners, but good repays the righteous"** (v21) — Hebrew חַטָּאִים תְּרַדֵּף רָעָה, the object first ("sinners evil pursues"); וְאֶת־צַדִּיקִים יְשַׁלֶּם־טוֹב, piel of שִׁלֵּם "repay": "good" is its subject, or the verb has an unnamed subject ("he repays good to the righteous"); not a passive.
- **"A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children's children: and the wealth of the sinner is laid up for the just" → "The good one makes sons of sons inherit, but stored up for the righteous is the wealth of the sinner"** (v22) — Hebrew טוֹב "the good one" (no "man"); יַנְחִיל "makes inherit" (hifil of נָחַל); בְּנֵי־בָנִים "sons of sons", no "his"; צָפוּן "stored up", first in the line; חֵיל חוֹטֵא "the wealth of the sinner" (חַיִל, here of goods; not the הוֹן of v7, 11).
- **"Much food is in the tillage of the poor: but there is that is destroyed for want of judgment" → "Much food is the fallow ground of the destitute, but there is what is swept away without judgment"** (v23) — Hebrew רָב־אֹכֶל נִיר רָאשִׁים, no "in": "much food" is the subject (נִיר "fallow ground", newly broken land, Jer 4:3; Hos 10:12); רָאשִׁים "the destitute" (spelled with א, the רָשׁ of v8). נִסְפֶּה "what is swept away" (or "one who is swept away": nifal of סָפָה, as Gen 19:15); בְּלֹא מִשְׁפָּט "without judgment". The line is hard; the sense is debated.
- **"He that spareth his rod hateth his son: but he that loveth him chasteneth him betimes" → "He who withholds his rod hates his son, but he who loves him seeks him eagerly with discipline"** (v24) — Hebrew חוֹשֵׂךְ "withholds"; שֵׁבֶט "rod"; שִׁחֲרוֹ מוּסָר "seeks him eagerly (with) discipline" (שִׁחֵר, root of שַׁחַר "dawn": KJV "betimes", "early"; "with" is supplied; or "seeks discipline for him").
- **"The righteous eateth to the satisfying of his soul: but the belly of the wicked shall want" → "The righteous eats to the satiety of his soul, but the belly of the wicked lacks"** (v25) — Hebrew לְשֹׂבַע נַפְשׁוֹ "to the satiety of his soul"; בֶּטֶן "belly"; תֶּחְסָר "lacks".

### Notes
- **Two-line sayings** — most verses of Proverbs 10–15 set the two lines against each other (the righteous and the wicked, the wise and the fool).
- **v13–14** — "the word", "the commandment" and the "torah of the wise" are the teacher's words; the same pairs stand of Yahweh's words elsewhere.
