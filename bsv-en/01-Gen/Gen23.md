# Genesis 23

[1] And the life of Sarah was a hundred and twenty-seven years, the years of the life of Sarah.

[2] And Sarah died in ⟦kiriath-arba|Qiryat Arba|Kiriath-Arba⟧, that is ⟦hebron|Chevron|Hebron⟧, in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧, and ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ came to lament for Sarah and to weep for her.

[3] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ arose from before his dead and spoke to the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧, saying,

[4] "A sojourner and a settler am I with you. Give me a possession of a grave with you, that I may bury my dead from before me."

[5] And the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧ answered ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, saying to him,

[6] "Hear us, my lord. A chieftain of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ are you in our midst. In the choice of our graves bury your dead; not a man of us will keep back his grave from you, from burying your dead."

[7] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ arose and bowed down to the people of the land, to the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧.

[8] And he spoke with them, saying, "If it is with your soul that I bury my dead from before me, hear me, and entreat for me ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧ the son of ⟦zohar|Tzochar|Zohar⟧,

[9] that he may give me the cave of Machpelah, which is his, which is at the end of his field. For full silver let him give it to me in your midst, for a possession of a grave."

[10] And ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧ was sitting in the midst of the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧, and ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧ the ⟦hittite|Chitti|Hittite⟧ answered ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ in the ears of the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧, of all who went in at the gate of his city, saying,

[11] "No, my lord, hear me. The field I give to you, and the cave that is in it, to you I give it; before the eyes of the sons of my people I give it to you. Bury your dead."

[12] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ bowed down before the people of the land.

[13] And he spoke to ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧ in the ears of the people of the land, saying, "But if you — oh that you would hear me! I give the silver of the field; take it from me, that I may bury my dead there."

[14] And ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧ answered ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧, saying to him,

[15] "My lord, hear me. Land of four hundred shekels of silver, between me and you, what is that? And bury your dead."

[16] And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ listened to ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧, and ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ weighed out for ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧ the silver that he had spoken in the ears of the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧, four hundred shekels of silver, current with the trader.

[17] And the field of ⟦ephron|Efron|Ephron⟧ that was in Machpelah, that was before Mamre, the field and the cave that was in it and all the trees that were in the field, that were in all its border round about, was made sure

[18] to ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ as a purchase before the eyes of the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧, among all who went in at the gate of his city.

[19] And after this ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah, facing Mamre, that is ⟦hebron|Chevron|Hebron⟧, in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧.

[20] And the field and the cave that was in it were made sure to ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ for a possession of a grave from the sons of ⟦heth|Chet|Heth⟧.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v6) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; the chain is kept.
- **"And Sarah was an hundred and seven and twenty years old" → "And the life of Sarah was a hundred and twenty-seven years"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיִּהְיוּ חַיֵּי שָׂרָה, "the life of Sarah was"; the number stands in the Hebrew as "a hundred years and twenty years and seven years", with "year(s)" after each part; the verse ends by repeating "the years of the life of Sarah".
- **"Kirjath-arba; the same is Hebron" → "Kiriath-Arba, that is Hebron"** (v2) — Hebrew קִרְיַת אַרְבַּע *Qiryat Arba*, "city of Arba" (or "city of four"); הִוא חֶבְרוֹן "that is Hebron" is part of the Hebrew text, here and in v19.
- **"to mourn for Sarah" → "to lament for Sarah"** (v2) — Hebrew סָפַד, the loud wailing for the dead; "mourn" is another word (אָבַל).
- **"stood up from before his dead" → "arose from before his dead"** (v3) — Hebrew מֵעַל פְּנֵי מֵתוֹ, literally "from upon the face of his dead". "His dead" (מֵתוֹ) is a masculine form, "his dead one", used of Sarah throughout (v4, 6, 8, 11, 13, 15).
- **"sons of Heth" → "sons of Chet"** (v3, 5, 7, 10, 16, 18, 20) — Hebrew בְּנֵי־חֵת *bene-Chet*, word for word; Chet is the son of Canaan (10:15). In v10 Ephron is called הַחִתִּי "the Hittite", the name of the people drawn from Chet. How these people of the hill country are related to the Hittite empire of Anatolia is debated.
- **"a stranger and a sojourner" → "a sojourner and a settler"** (v4) — Hebrew גֵּר־וְתוֹשָׁב *ger we-toshav*: גֵּר, one who lives among a people not his own; תּוֹשָׁב, one settled in a place without land of his own. Abraham owns no land and must buy a grave.
- **"a possession of a buryingplace" → "a possession of a grave"** (v4, 9, 20) — Hebrew אֲחֻזַּת־קֶבֶר *achuzzat-qever*: אֲחֻזָּה is land held as one's own; קֶבֶר is "grave", the same word as in v6.
- **"that I may bury my dead out of my sight" → "… from before me"** (v4, 8) — Hebrew מִלְּפָנָי, literally "from before my face".
- **"thou art a mighty prince among us" → "A chieftain of *Elohim* are you in our midst"** (v6) — Hebrew נְשִׂיא אֱלֹהִים אַתָּה בְּתוֹכֵנוּ, in the Hebrew order. נָשִׂיא is "one lifted up", a chieftain. "Of Elohim" is what the Hebrew says; it may mean a chieftain whom Elohim has set up, or be a way of saying "a very great chieftain" (KJV "mighty").
- **"in the choice of our sepulchres" → "In the choice of our graves"** (v6) — Hebrew בְּמִבְחַר קְבָרֵינוּ, the best of them. **"none of us shall withhold" → "not a man of us will keep back"** — Hebrew אִישׁ מִמֶּנּוּ … לֹא־יִכְלֶה.
- **"communed" → "spoke"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיְדַבֵּר.
- **"if it be your mind" → "If it is with your soul"** (v8) — Hebrew אִם־יֵשׁ אֶת־נַפְשְׁכֶם, word for word: נֶפֶשׁ *nephesh* "soul", here the seat of will and wish ("if you are willing"). "That I bury" is an infinitive in the Hebrew ("to bury my dead").
- **"intreat for me to Ephron" → "entreat for me Ephron"** (v8) — Hebrew פִּגְעוּ־לִי בְּעֶפְרוֹן: פָּגַע בְּ is "meet, fall upon" someone, here with a plea.
- **"the cave of Machpelah"** (v9, 17, 19) — the Hebrew has the article, הַמַּכְפֵּלָה "the Machpelah": the name of the place where the field and the cave lie (v17, "in Machpelah"). The word comes from כָּפַל "double".
- **"for as much money as it is worth" → "For full silver"** (v9) — Hebrew בְּכֶסֶף מָלֵא *be-khesef male*, "for full silver": the whole price. כֶּסֶף is "silver"; there were no coins, and the silver is weighed in v16.
- **"dwelt among" → "was sitting in the midst of"** (v10) — Hebrew יֹשֵׁב, "sitting" or "dwelling"; Ephron is present among them at the gate.
- **"in the audience of" → "in the ears of"** (v10, 13, 16) — Hebrew בְּאָזְנֵי; and **"in the presence of" → "before the eyes of"** (v11, 18) — Hebrew לְעֵינֵי. The sale is made before witnesses who hear and see it.
- **"all that went in at the gate of his city" kept** (v10, 18) — Hebrew בָּאֵי שַׁעַר־עִירוֹ: those who have a place in the gate, where the business of a city is done.
- **"the field give I thee" → "The field I give to you"** (v11, 13) — Hebrew נָתַתִּי, a perfect ("I have given"): in a legal declaration the thing is spoken of as done. English says "I give".
- **"But if thou wilt give it, I pray thee, hear me" → "But if you — oh that you would hear me!"** (v13) — Hebrew אַךְ אִם־אַתָּה לוּ שְׁמָעֵנִי, a broken sentence: "only if you —", then the wish-particle לוּ "oh that", then "hear me". "Wilt give it" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"I will give thee money for the field" → "I give the silver of the field"** (v13) — Hebrew כֶּסֶף הַשָּׂדֶה: its price in silver.
- **"the land is worth four hundred shekels of silver" → "Land of four hundred shekels of silver"** (v15) — Hebrew אֶרֶץ אַרְבַּע מֵאֹת שֶׁקֶל־כֶּסֶף, with no verb. A שֶׁקֶל *sheqel* is a weight (from שָׁקַל "weigh", v16), not a coin. The Hebrew has "And bury your dead."
- **"hearkened unto" → "listened to"** (v16) — Hebrew שָׁמַע אֶל. **"which he had named" → "that he had spoken"** — Hebrew דִּבֶּר.
- **"current money with the merchant" → "current with the trader"** (v16) — Hebrew עֹבֵר לַסֹּחֵר *over la-socher*, literally "passing over to the trader": silver of the weight and kind that traders accept. "Money" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"were made sure" kept** (v17, 20) — Hebrew וַיָּקָם, literally "and it rose / stood": the field stood as Abraham's, the sale was settled. The verb is singular in both verses.
- **"all the trees"** (v17) — Hebrew הָעֵץ, singular, "the tree", for all of them. **"in all the borders round about" → "in all its border round about"** — Hebrew בְּכָל־גְּבֻלוֹ סָבִיב.
- **"for a possession" → "as a purchase"** (v18) — Hebrew לְמִקְנָה, a thing bought; "possession" (אֲחֻזָּה) is the word in v4, 9, 20. **"before all" → "among all"** — Hebrew בְּכֹל.
- **"before Mamre"** (v17) **/ "facing Mamre"** (v19) — Hebrew לִפְנֵי מַמְרֵא and עַל־פְּנֵי מַמְרֵא, literally "before the face of" and "upon the face of" Mamre: over against it (often understood as "east of").

### Notes

- **Why a chapter on a property transaction?** The text devotes unusual space to the purchase of the Machpelah cave. Reason: Abraham's first actual foothold in the promised land is a grave. The patriarch whose seed will possess the whole land owns only a burial cave during his life — a tension the chapter highlights. It is also a legal record (detailed formula of the transfer: field, cave, trees, borders, witnesses) serving as a title-deed in narrative form.
- **Oriental bargaining** — the exchange follows Near Eastern custom: Ephron appears to offer the land free, Abraham insists on paying, Ephron names a price (400 shekels — a very substantial sum), Abraham pays in full. The apparent generosity is part of the bargaining ritual; the price is steep.
- **Machpelah's later significance** — Sarah (ch 23), Abraham (25:9), Isaac (35:29), Rebekah (49:31), Leah (49:31), and Jacob (50:13) are all buried there. The traditional site in modern Hebron (Tomb of the Patriarchs / al-Haram al-Ibrahimi) is contested sacred space for Jews, Christians, and Muslims.
