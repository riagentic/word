# Genesis 12

and I will bless you,

## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And" kept at the head of the clauses** (throughout) — Hebrew וַ / וְ.
- **"Get thee out of thy country" → "Get yourself from your land"** (v1) — Hebrew לֶךְ־לְךָ *lekh-lekha*: the imperative "go" with לְךָ, the preposition לְ and "you", which turns the command back on the one addressed (as the old English "get thee"). The English reflexive "yourself" carries the לְךָ; "for" is not in the Hebrew. The same form addresses Abraham in 22:2.
- **"thy kindred" → "your kindred"** (v1) — Hebrew מוֹלֶדֶת *moledet*, as 11:28.
- **"I will make of thee a great nation" → "I will make you into a great nation"** (v2) — Hebrew וְאֶעֶשְׂךָ לְגוֹי גָּדוֹל, "I will make you (in)to a great nation".
- **"thou shalt be a blessing" → "be a blessing"** (v2) — Hebrew וֶהְיֵה, an imperative: "and be a blessing". After the three first-person verbs the imperative can state the purpose or outcome ("so that you will be"); the form itself is a command.
- **"curse him that curseth thee" → "him who makes light of you I will curse"** (v3) — Hebrew וּמְקַלֶּלְךָ אָאֹר *u-meqallelkha a'or*: two different verbs. קִלֵּל (piel of "be light") is "make light of, revile, curse"; אָרַר is "curse". "Those who bless you" is plural, "him who makes light of you" is singular.
- **"shall be blessed" → "will be blessed"** (v3) — Hebrew וְנִבְרְכוּ *we-nivrekhu*, niphal of בָּרַךְ. The niphal is a passive ("will be blessed") or a reflexive ("will bless themselves", that is, will wish for themselves a blessing like Abram's); the form allows both. The passive is in the verse. The parallel promises 22:18 and 26:4 have the hitpael הִתְבָּרְכוּ.
- **"families of the earth" → "clans of the ground"** (v3) — Hebrew מִשְׁפְּחֹת הָאֲדָמָה: מִשְׁפָּחָה "clan", אֲדָמָה "ground" (not אֶרֶץ "earth / land").
- **"departed out of Haran" → "went out from Haran"** (v4) — Hebrew בְּצֵאתוֹ, "in his going out"; "seventy-five years old" is literally "a son of five years and seventy years": the Hebrew puts the smaller number first and repeats "years".
- **"substance that they had gathered" → "goods which they had acquired"** (v5) — Hebrew רְכוּשָׁם אֲשֶׁר רָכָשׁוּ, noun and verb of one root.
- **"the souls that they had gotten" → "the souls that they had made"** (v5) — Hebrew הַנֶּפֶשׁ אֲשֶׁר־עָשׂוּ *ha-nephesh asher-asu*: נֶפֶשׁ "soul" (singular, used for the whole group: persons), עָשָׂה "make". "Made" is commonly understood as "gotten" (slaves and dependents); the old Jewish reading is "won" (for Abram's faith). The verse keeps the Hebrew words.
- **"into the land of Canaan they came" → "and they came to the land of Canaan"** (v5) — Hebrew וַיָּבֹאוּ אַרְצָה כְּנָעַן, a full clause that ends the verse; it is not joined to v6 as "when they came".
- **"Sichem" → "Shechem"** (v6) — Hebrew שְׁכֶם *Shekhem*.
- **"unto the place … unto the plain of Moreh" → "as far as the place … as far as the oak of Moreh"** (v6) — Hebrew עַד … עַד. אֵלוֹן *elon* is a great tree; "oak" is the usual rendering, the kind of tree is not certain (others: terebinth). KJV "plain" is not the Hebrew word. *Moreh* = "teacher".
- **"the Canaanite" kept** (v6) — Hebrew הַכְּנַעֲנִי, singular for the people.
- **"seed" kept** (v7) — Hebrew זֶרַע *zera*, a singular noun used for all one's offspring; "seed" keeps the singular.
- **"he removed from thence unto a mountain" → "he moved on from there to the mountain"** (v8) — Hebrew וַיַּעְתֵּק מִשָּׁם הָהָרָה; הַר is "mountain" (of a region: the hill country). "On the west" is literally "from the sea" (מִיָּם).
- **"Hai" → "Ai"** (v8) — Hebrew הָעַי *ha-Ai*; KJV transliterates the article as part of the name.
- **"going on still toward the south" → "going on and journeying toward the Negev"** (v9) — Hebrew הָלוֹךְ וְנָסוֹעַ הַנֶּגְבָּה, two infinitives absolute after "journeyed": he kept moving by stages. הַנֶּגֶב is the dry region in the south of the land.
- **"sojourn" kept** (v10) — Hebrew לָגוּר, to stay as a sojourner (גֵּר).
- **"grievous" → "heavy"** (v10) — Hebrew כָּבֵד.
- **"Behold now" kept** (v11) — Hebrew הִנֵּה־נָא; "Say, please" (v13) is אִמְרִי־נָא.
- **"a fair woman to look upon" → "a woman beautiful in appearance"** (v11) — Hebrew אִשָּׁה יְפַת־מַרְאֶה.
- **"my soul shall live" → "my soul may live"** (v13) — Hebrew וְחָיְתָה נַפְשִׁי; נֶפֶשׁ "soul" stands for the living person, "I will stay alive". "For your sake" (בַּעֲבוּרֵךְ) and "because of you" (בִּגְלָלֵךְ) are two Hebrew words.
- **"beheld the woman that she was very fair" → "saw the woman, that she was very beautiful"** (v14) — the Hebrew order is kept.
- **"princes" kept** (v15) — Hebrew שָׂרֵי פַרְעֹה, שַׂר "prince, chief".
- **"entreated Abram well" → "to Abram he did good"** (v16) — Hebrew וּלְאַבְרָם הֵיטִיב; the subject is Pharaoh and is not named. The second "Abram" of the older text is not in the Hebrew ("and he had").
- **"sheep, and oxen" → "flocks and herds"** (v16) — Hebrew צֹאן (sheep and goats) and בָּקָר (cattle).
- **"he asses… she asses" → "donkeys… she-donkeys"** (v16) — Hebrew חֲמֹרִים and אֲתֹנֹת.
- **"menservants, and maidservants" → "male slaves and maidservants"** (v16) — Hebrew עֲבָדִים וּשְׁפָחֹת.
- **"plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues" kept, in the Hebrew order** (v17) — Hebrew וַיְנַגַּע … נְגָעִים גְּדֹלִים וְאֶת־בֵּיתוֹ: verb and noun of one root (נגע, "touch, strike"). "Because of the matter of Sarai" is עַל־דְּבַר שָׂרַי (דָּבָר "word, matter").
- **"so I might have taken her" → "and I took her to myself as a wife"** (v19) — Hebrew וָאֶקַּח אֹתָהּ לִי לְאִשָּׁה, a plain past: "and I took her".
- **"take her, and go thy way" → "take and go"** (v19) — Hebrew קַח וָלֵךְ, two imperatives without an object.
- **"commanded his men" → "commanded men"** (v20) — Hebrew אֲנָשִׁים, "men", without "his".

### Notes

- **The Abrahamic call (v1–3)** — the pivot of Genesis. After the universal prehistory (chs 1–11), the narrative narrows to one family through whom Yahweh will work for the blessing of all.
- **v3 in the New Testament** — Acts 3:25 and Gal 3:8 cite the promise in Greek with the passive ἐνευλογηθήσονται, "will be blessed" (the Septuagint form, from memory).
- **"Wife-sister" episode (v10–20)** — the first of three: Abraham/Sarah in Egypt (12), Abraham/Sarah with Abimelech (20), Isaac/Rebekah with Abimelech (26).
- **Abram's half-truth** — Sarai was in fact Abram's half-sister (20:12). The narrator does not justify or explicitly condemn.
