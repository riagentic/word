## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (v11) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses and its lists of names with וַ / וְ "and"; it is kept.
- **"Then again Abraham took a wife" → "And Abraham again took a wife"** (v1) — Hebrew וַיֹּסֶף … וַיִּקַּח, literally "and Abraham added and took". The Hebrew does not say when.
- **"begat" → "begot"** (v3, 19) — Hebrew יָלַד / הוֹלִיד of the father. **"children of Keturah" → "sons of Keturah"** (v4) — Hebrew בְּנֵי.
- **"the concubines, which Abraham had" kept** (v6) — Hebrew הַפִּילַגְשִׁים אֲשֶׁר לְאַבְרָהָם, plural; Keturah is called "wife" in v1 and "concubine" in 1 Chr 1:32. **"eastward, unto the east country" → "eastward, to the land of the east"** — Hebrew קֵדְמָה אֶל־אֶרֶץ קֶדֶם; קֶדֶם is literally "the front".
- **"an hundred threescore and fifteen years" → "a hundred and seventy-five years"** (v7, 17) — the Hebrew gives the numbers as "a hundred years and seventy years and five years", "a hundred years and thirty years and seven years".
- **"gave up the ghost" → "expired"** (v8, 17) — Hebrew גָּוַע *gava*, a second word beside "died".
- **"an old man, and full of years" → "old and sated"** (v8) — Hebrew זָקֵן וְשָׂבֵעַ; "of years" is not in the Masoretic Text (the Samaritan text and the Septuagint have "full of days"; cited from memory). "A good old age" is literally "good gray hair" (שֵׂיבָה).
- **"gathered to his people" → "gathered to his peoples"** (v8, 17) — Hebrew וַיֵּאָסֶף אֶל־עַמָּיו: עַמָּיו is plural, "his peoples", his kinsfolk who died before him. It is said before the burial (v9) and is not the same as being buried: Abraham's fathers lie in another land.
- **"which is before Mamre" → "which faces Mamre"** (v9) — Hebrew עַל־פְּנֵי, literally "upon the face of", as in 23:19; and of Shur and Egypt in v18.
- **"purchased" → "bought"** (v10) — Hebrew קָנָה.
- **"dwelt" kept** (v11) — Hebrew יָשַׁב; in v18 "abode" is שָׁכַן.
- **"Sarah's handmaid" → "the maidservant of Sarah"** (v12) — Hebrew שִׁפְחָה.
- **"by their towns, and by their castles" → "by their villages and by their encampments"** (v16) — Hebrew חַצְרֵיהֶם, unwalled settlements, and טִירֹתָם, camps ringed with a wall or fence.
- **"twelve princes according to their nations" → "twelve chieftains according to their tribes"** (v16) — Hebrew נְשִׂיאִם, as in 17:20 and 23:6; אֻמֹּת, a rare word for peoples of one stock.
- **"before Egypt, as thou goest toward Assyria" → "which faces Egypt, as you come toward Ashur"** (v18) — Hebrew בֹּאֲכָה אַשּׁוּרָה, literally "your coming toward Ashur".
- **"he died in the presence of all his brethren" → "In the face of all his brothers he settled"** (v18) — Hebrew עַל־פְּנֵי כָל־אֶחָיו נָפָל, literally "upon the face of all his brothers he fell". "Fall" here is to settle, to lie encamped (as in Judg 7:12), and the words answer 16:12 (עַל־פְּנֵי כָל־אֶחָיו יִשְׁכֹּן); KJV took it of his death. "In the face of" may be "alongside" or "against". The verse begins with a plural ("they abode") and ends with a singular.
- **"forty years old" / "threescore years old"** (v20, 26) — literally "a son of forty years", "a son of sixty years".
- **"the Syrian of Padan-aram" → "the Aramean from Paddan Aram"** (v20) — Hebrew הָאֲרַמִּי מִפַּדַּן אֲרָם. **"to wife" → "to himself as a wife"** — Hebrew לוֹ לְאִשָּׁה.
- **"intreated the LORD for his wife … and the LORD was intreated of him" → "pleaded with Yahweh on behalf of his wife … and Yahweh let himself be pleaded with by him"** (v21) — Hebrew וַיֶּעְתַּר … וַיֵּעָתֶר לוֹ: one verb, first active, then in the niphal. "On behalf of" is לְנֹכַח, literally "in front of, over against" his wife.
- **"the children struggled together" → "the sons crushed one another"** (v22) — Hebrew וַיִּתְרֹצֲצוּ הַבָּנִים: the verb is רָצַץ "crush", here of two doing it to each other; בָּנִים "sons".
- **"If it be so, why am I thus?" → "If it is so, why then am I?"** (v22) — Hebrew אִם־כֵּן לָמָּה זֶּה אָנֹכִי, word for word "if so, why then I?" The sentence is cut short and its sense is not certain ("why am I like this?", "why do I live?"; the Syriac adds "living": cited from memory). Nothing is supplied. **"to enquire of the LORD" → "to seek Yahweh"** — Hebrew לִדְרֹשׁ אֶת־יְהוָה.
- **"in thy womb" → "in your belly"** (v23, 24) — Hebrew בֶּטֶן. **"two manner of people shall be separated from thy bowels" → "two folks from your inward parts shall be parted"** — Hebrew לְאֻמִּים, a poetic word for "peoples" beside גּוֹיִם "nations"; מֵעַיִךְ "your inward parts". In "nations" the written text (ketiv) is גיים, read (qere) גוֹיִם: the same word.
- **"the one people shall be stronger than the other people" → "folk shall be stronger than folk"** (v23) — Hebrew וּלְאֹם מִלְאֹם יֶאֱמָץ.
- **"the elder shall serve the younger" kept** (v23) — Hebrew וְרַב יַעֲבֹד צָעִיר *we-rav ya'avod tsa'ir*, literally "and great shall serve young / small": רַב "great" for the elder is found only here. Neither word has the object marker, so the line can also be construed "the elder, the younger shall serve"; the order of the words and the story that follows favor "the elder shall serve the younger" (so Rom 9:12).
- **"red, all over like an hairy garment" → "red, all of him like a mantle of hair"** (v25) — Hebrew אַדְמוֹנִי "red, ruddy" (the sound of *Edom*, v30) and אַדֶּרֶת שֵׂעָר "a mantle of hair" (*se'ar*, the sound of *Seir*, Esau's land). The name עֵשָׂו *Esav* itself is not explained by the verse.
- **"his name was called Jacob"** (v26) — Hebrew וַיִּקְרָא, "and he called" (or "one called"). יַעֲקֹב *Ya'aqov* sounds like עָקֵב *aqev* "heel" (cf. 27:36). **"when she bare them"** — Hebrew בְּלֶדֶת אֹתָם, "at the bearing of them", with no subject.
- **"a cunning hunter" → "a man knowing hunting"** (v27) — Hebrew אִישׁ יֹדֵעַ צַיִד.
- **"a plain man" → "a blameless man"** (v27) — Hebrew אִישׁ תָּם *ish tam*. תָּם is "whole, complete", and of a person "blameless": the word used of Job (Job 1:1, 8), of one root with תָּמִים (Noah, 6:9; Abraham, 17:1). "Plain", "quiet", "mild" are guesses drawn from the contrast with Esau the hunter; the word does not mean that elsewhere. What the narrator means by calling Jacob תָּם here is not explained.
- **"because he did eat of his venison" → "because game was in his mouth"** (v28) — Hebrew כִּי־צַיִד בְּפִיו, word for word. "Loved" is first a narrative past (וַיֶּאֱהַב), then a participle (אֹהֶבֶת, "was loving").
- **"sod pottage" → "boiled a stew"** (v29) — Hebrew וַיָּזֶד … נָזִיד, verb and noun of one root. **"faint" → "weary"** (v29, 30) — Hebrew עָיֵף.
- **"Feed me, I pray thee, with that same red pottage" → "Let me gulp down, please, some of the red, this red"** (v30) — Hebrew הַלְעִיטֵנִי נָא מִן־הָאָדֹם הָאָדֹם הַזֶּה. The verb is found only here ("let me swallow, gulp"); "pottage" is not in the Hebrew, which has only "the red, the red, this". *Edom* sounds like אָדֹם *adom* "red".
- **"Sell me this day thy birthright"** (v31, 33) — Hebrew כַיּוֹם, literally "as the day": "today", "now", or "first of all".
- **"I am at the point to die: and what profit shall this birthright do to me?" → "I am going to die, and what then is a birthright to me?"** (v32) — Hebrew הוֹלֵךְ לָמוּת "going to die"; לָמָּה־זֶּה לִי בְּכֹרָה, literally "why then to me a birthright?"
- **"rose up, and went his way" → "arose and went"** (v34) — four verbs in a row in the Hebrew. **"despised his birthright" → "despised the birthright"** — Hebrew בָּזָה "despise"; הַבְּכֹרָה "the birthright".

### Notes

- **Abraham's late marriage** — Keturah's sons become eponymous ancestors of Arabian tribes (Midian, Sheba, Dedan). Gen 25 ends the Abraham cycle by placing his full legacy: Isaac (covenant), Ishmael (promised nation), and the Keturah line (Arab peoples).
- **Esau and Jacob** — set up by birth (v22–26) as rivals. Later Israelite ethnography reads Esau as Edom and Jacob as Israel — the contrast is national, not only personal.
- **Birthright sale (v29–34)** — the right of the firstborn traded for a single meal. The narrative says of Esau that he "despised the birthright"; on Jacob's bargaining it passes no judgment.
