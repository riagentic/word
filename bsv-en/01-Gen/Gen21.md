## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*.
- **"And"** (throughout) — the Hebrew joins its clauses with וַ / וְ "and"; it is kept and the chain of clauses is not turned into "when … then" or "so … but".
- **"visited" kept** (v1) — Hebrew פָּקַד *paqad* = visit, attend to (for good, as here, or to punish). Not merely "went to see"; Yahweh acts on what he had said. "said" (אָמַר) and "spoken" (דִּבֶּר) are two verbs.
- **"in his old age"** (v2, 7) — Hebrew לִזְקֻנָיו, literally "to / for his old age".
- **"his son"** (v3) — Hebrew בְּנוֹ; and in v4, 5 the order "Isaac his son" is the Hebrew's.
- **"being eight days old" → "when he was eight days old"; "an hundred years old" → "a hundred years old"** (v4, 5) — literally "a son of eight days", "a son of a hundred years": the Hebrew way of giving an age.
- **"God hath made me to laugh" → "Laughter *Elohim* has made for me"** (v6) — Hebrew צְחֹק עָשָׂה לִי אֱלֹהִים: the noun "laughter" (צְחֹק) stands first. **"will laugh with me" → "will laugh for me"** — Hebrew יִצְחַק־לִי, "laugh" with the preposition לְ "to / for": the words can mean "laugh with me", "laugh for my sake" or "laugh at me"; the Hebrew does not say which.
- **"Who would have said … that Sarah should have given children suck?" → "Who would have uttered … 'Sarah has nursed sons'?"** (v7) — Hebrew מִי מִלֵּל, a perfect ("who has uttered"), with מִלֵּל, a rare word, not the usual "say"; הֵינִיקָה is a perfect, "has nursed"; בָנִים "sons". The clause that follows begins with כִּי "for"; "him" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"Isaac" (*Yitschaq*) and the laughter theme (v3, 6, 9)** — the name means "he laughs" and keeps resounding: Abraham's laughter (17:17), Sarah's (18:12), now "laughter" and "will laugh" (21:6), and in v9 Ishmael is *metsacheq* (same root).
- **"mocking" → "laughing"** (v9) — Hebrew מְצַחֵק *metsacheq*, piel participle of צָחַק "laugh", the root of the name Isaac; the verse has this one word and no object. The piel of this verb covers: laughing, jesting (19:14), playing (Exod 32:6), fondling (26:8, with "his wife"), making sport of someone (39:14, 17, with בְּ). KJV "mocking" is one of these senses chosen for the reader; Paul speaks of "persecuting" (Gal 4:29). "Laughing" keeps the root that the hearer of the Hebrew hears. The Septuagint adds "with Isaac her son" (cited from memory); the Masoretic Text has no such words.
- **"Cast out this bondwoman" → "Drive out this slave woman"** (v10, 12, 13) — Hebrew אָמָה *amah*, a female slave; a different word from שִׁפְחָה *shiphchah* "maidservant" (16:1).
- **"was very grievous in Abraham's sight" → "was very bad in the eyes of Abraham"** (v11, 12) — Hebrew וַיֵּרַע … בְּעֵינֵי, literally "was bad / evil in the eyes of": it displeased him deeply. "The thing" is הַדָּבָר, "the word" (what Sarah said) or "the matter".
- **"lad" → "boy"** (v12, 17, 18, 19, 20) — Hebrew נַעַר *na'ar*, a boy or young man; in v8, 14, 15, 16 the word is יֶלֶד *yeled* "child". Both are used of Ishmael.
- **"hearken unto her voice" → "listen to her voice"** (v12) — Hebrew שְׁמַע בְּקֹלָהּ.
- **"in Isaac shall thy seed be called" → "in Isaac shall seed be called for you"** (v12) — Hebrew בְיִצְחָק יִקָּרֵא לְךָ זָרַע: "in Isaac", niphal of *qara* "be called", "for you", and "seed" without "your". **"seed" kept** (v12, 13) — Hebrew זֶרַע *zera*, a singular noun used of one descendant or of all.
- **"gave them … putting them"** (v14) — the Hebrew has no object: "and gave to Hagar — he put on her shoulder — and the child". "Them" is supplied twice for English. The words "and the child" follow "on her shoulder"; whether the child was also set on her shoulder or only given to her, the Hebrew does not say.
- **"bottle" → "skin"** (v14, 15, 19) — Hebrew חֵמֶת *chemet* = skin / leather water-bag. Not a glass / ceramic bottle. Typical nomadic water container.
- **"the water was spent in the bottle" → "the water from the skin was finished"** (v15) — Hebrew וַיִּכְלוּ הַמַּיִם מִן־הַחֵמֶת.
- **"cast the child under one of the shrubs" (v15)** — Hebrew וַתַּשְׁלֵךְ אֶת־הַיֶּלֶד *wa-tashlekh et ha-yeled*. *Shalakh* (hiphil) = throw / cast.
- **"sat her down over against him a good way off, as it were a bowshot" → "sat herself down opposite, far off, about a bowshot away"** (v16) — Hebrew מִנֶּגֶד "opposite" (twice; "him" is not in the Hebrew), הַרְחֵק "far off", כִּמְטַחֲוֵי קֶשֶׁת literally "like shooters of a bow". The one who lifts up the voice and weeps is "she" in the Hebrew (the Septuagint has the child weep; cited from memory).
- **"angel of God" → "angel of *Elohim*"** (v17) — Hebrew מַלְאַךְ אֱלֹהִים; the word מַלְאָךְ *mal'akh* means "messenger".
- **"What aileth thee" → "What ails you"** (v17) — Hebrew מַה־לָּךְ, literally "What to you?"
- **"God hath heard the voice" → "*Elohim* has listened to the voice"** (v17) — first שָׁמַע אֶת־קוֹל "heard the voice", then שָׁמַע אֶל־קוֹל "listened to the voice". The name *Ishmael* means "El hears" (16:11).
- **"hold him in thine hand" → "hold him fast with your hand"** (v18) — Hebrew הַחֲזִיקִי אֶת־יָדֵךְ בּוֹ, literally "make your hand strong on him".
- **"dwelt" kept** (v20, 21) — Hebrew יָשַׁב. **"archer"** (v20) — Hebrew רֹבֶה קַשָּׁת, two words; the first is found only here and its exact sense is not certain ("a shooter, a bowman").
- **"it came to pass … spake … saying" kept** (v22) — Hebrew וַיְהִי … וַיֹּאמֶר … לֵאמֹר. **"chief captain of his host" → "commander of his host"** — Hebrew שַׂר־צְבָאוֹ: שַׂר "prince", of an army its commander; צָבָא "host", an army.
- **"that thou wilt not deal falsely"** (v23) — Hebrew אִם־תִּשְׁקֹר, literally "if you deal falsely …": the oath formula, meaning "you will not".
- **"with my son, nor with my son's son" → "with my offspring or with my posterity"** (v23) — Hebrew וּלְנִינִי וּלְנֶכְדִּי *u-le-nini u-le-nekhdi*, a fixed pair of two rare words for those who come after a man (also Job 18:19; Isa 14:22).
- **"kindness" → "*chesed*"** (v23) — Hebrew חֶסֶד *chesed*, loyal love between those bound to one another; "that I have done with you, you shall do with me" is the Hebrew wording (עָשָׂה עִם).
- **"I will swear"** (v24) — the Hebrew has the pronoun as well (אָנֹכִי אִשָּׁבֵעַ): "I, for my part, will swear."
- **"reproved" kept** (v25) — Hebrew הוֹכִחַ. **"servants"** — Hebrew עֲבָדִים, the word for "slaves", here of a king's men. **"violently taken away" → "seized"** — Hebrew גָּזְלוּ.
- **"I wot not … neither didst thou tell me, neither yet heard I of it, but to day" → "I do not know …; you also did not tell me, and I also have not heard of it, except today"** (v26) — Hebrew וְגַם … וְגַם … בִּלְתִּי הַיּוֹם.
- **"sheep and oxen" → "flock and herd"** (v27) — Hebrew צֹאן וּבָקָר: small animals (sheep and goats) and cattle.
- **"made a covenant" → "cut a covenant"** (v27, 32) — Hebrew כָּרַת בְּרִית.
- **"What mean these seven ewe lambs" → "What are these seven ewe lambs"** (v29) — Hebrew מָה הֵנָּה, "what are they".
- **"For these seven ewe lambs shalt thou take … that they may be a witness" → "Because you shall take the seven ewe lambs … so that it may be a witness"** (v30) — Hebrew כִּי opens the answer ("because", or only a mark of the speech); the verb תִּהְיֶה is singular, "it shall be": the taking of the lambs is the witness.
- **"he called that place" → "that place was called"** (v31) — Hebrew קָרָא, "he / one called", with no named subject. **"they sware both of them" → "the two of them swore"** — the explanation "well of the oath / well of seven" that stood in the verse is not in the Hebrew; see Notes.
- **"And Abraham planted" → "And he planted"** (v33) — the Masoretic Text names no subject. (The Septuagint and the Samaritan text name Abraham; cited from memory.)
- **"grove" → "tamarisk"** (v33) — Hebrew אֵשֶׁל *eshel*. Specific tree species (*Tamarix*), a low-water desert tree. KJV "grove" misses the specific botany and confuses it with the Asherah-pole groves (which would be pagan cult). Corrected.
- **"the everlasting God" → "the everlasting El"** (v33) — Hebrew אֵל עוֹלָם *El Olam*, literally "El of the age": עוֹלָם is a noun, time reaching out of sight, past or to come. Another El-compound divine name, like El Elyon (14), El Shaddai (17), El Roi (16). *El* kept as Hebrew, the epithet translated. The words can be read "Yahweh, the everlasting El" (as here) or "Yahweh El Olam" as one name.
- **"the Philistines" (v32, 34)** — anachronism: Philistines as an organized people in this region are attested only from the 12th century BCE onwards (Iron Age), centuries after Abraham. Likely a later editorial update for readers familiar with the current regional politics, or a broader use of "Philistine" for coastal Aegean-origin peoples of the 2nd millennium.

### Notes

- **Beersheba's double etymology** — the name *Be'er-Shava* plays on both *shava* (oath) and *sheva* (seven): "well of the oath" / "well of seven". Both motifs are in the text (the swearing, v31, + seven lambs). The author holds both together rather than choosing.
- **Ishmael's preserved blessing** — though driven out, Ishmael receives divine care (v17–21) and a national future (v18, fulfilled in ch 25:12–18). Paul allegorizes Hagar and Sarah as two covenants (Gal 4:21–31), but the Genesis text treats Hagar and Ishmael with more sympathy.
- **Hagar's theology again** — "*Elohim* opened her eyes, and she saw a well" (v19) parallels her earlier revelation as the woman who sees and is seen (16:13). Theological vision and saving provision fuse in the water for the mother and child.
