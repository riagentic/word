## Changes

- **"LORD" → "Yahweh"** (throughout) — Tetragrammaton preserved.
- **"And" kept** (throughout) — Hebrew וַ / וְ at the head of each clause; the chain of clauses is not turned into "so", "when", "then" (v3, 4, 5).
- **"bare him no children" → "had not borne to him"** (v1, 2) — Hebrew לֹא יָלְדָה לוֹ; מִלֶּדֶת "from bearing". The Hebrew has no object: "children" is not in the text.
- **"handmaid" / "maid" → "maidservant"** (v1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8) — Hebrew שִׁפְחָה *shiphchah*, a woman servant belonging to her mistress (v6 "in your hand"). A different word from אָמָה *amah* "slave woman" (21:10); the two are kept apart.
- **"the LORD hath restrained me from bearing" → "Yahweh has kept me from bearing"** (v2) — Hebrew עֲצָרַנִי *atsarani* = held me back / shut me up. Childlessness attributed to Yahweh's direct action.
- **"I may obtain children by her" → "I shall be built up from her"** (v2) — Hebrew אִבָּנֶה מִמֶּנָּה *ibbaneh mimmennah*. Niphal of *banah* ("to build") — same verb used of building the woman from the human's side (2:22). Wordplay: ben (son) ← banah (build). מִמֶּנָּה = "from her".
- **"after Abram had dwelt ten years" → "at the end of ten years of Abram's dwelling"** (v3) — Hebrew מִקֵּץ עֶשֶׂר שָׁנִים לְשֶׁבֶת אַבְרָם, in the Hebrew order of the clauses; "to him for a wife" (לוֹ לְאִשָּׁה) is kept beside "to Abram her husband".
- **"her mistress was despised in her eyes" → "her mistress was lightly esteemed in her eyes"** (v4, 5) — Hebrew וַתֵּקַל גְּבִרְתָּהּ בְּעֵינֶיהָ *wa-tekal gevirtah be-eyneha*, literally "her mistress was light in her eyes". *Qalal* = be light / of little weight; the same root as "make light of / curse" (12:3). Hagar's view of Sarai.
- **"My wrong be upon thee" → "The violence done to me is upon you"** (v5) — Hebrew חֲמָסִי עָלֶיךָ, literally "my violence upon you". חָמָס *chamas* is "violence" (6:11); the suffix names the one who suffers it. The Hebrew has no verb: "is" (a statement) or "be" (a wish) are both possible.
- **"I have given" → "I myself gave"** (v5) — Hebrew אָנֹכִי נָתַתִּי, the pronoun is written out for emphasis.
- **"into thy bosom" kept** (v5) — Hebrew בְּחֵיקֶךָ *be-cheyqekha* = in your lap / bosom. Idiom for intimate / wife-position.
- **"as it pleaseth thee" → "what is good in your eyes"** (v6) — Hebrew הַטּוֹב בְּעֵינָיִךְ, literally "the good in your eyes".
- **"dealt hardly with her" → "afflicted her"** (v6) — Hebrew וַתְּעַנֶּהָ *wa-te'anneha* = afflicted / oppressed / humbled her. Same verb of affliction used in 15:13 for Egypt's oppression of Israel — and ironically Hagar is Egyptian. The same root returns in v9 ("humble yourself", הִתְעַנִּי, literally "let yourself be afflicted") and v11 ("your affliction", עָנְיֵךְ).
- **"angel of the LORD" → "the angel of Yahweh"** (v7, 9, 10, 11) — Hebrew מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה *mal'akh Yahweh*. The word *mal'akh* means "messenger" / envoy (one sent); it names a task, not a kind of being, and is used of human messengers too (32:3). This fixed phrase is rendered "the angel of Yahweh" throughout the Old Testament. The *mal'akh Yahweh* here speaks as Yahweh in the first person (v10), and v13 says that it was Yahweh who was speaking to her.
- **"fountain" → "spring"** (v7) — Hebrew עַיִן *ayin* = eye / spring. Natural spring, not a built fountain.
- **"under her hands" kept** (v9) — Hebrew תַּחַת יָדֶיהָ, plural.
- **"I will multiply thy seed exceedingly" → "I will surely multiply your seed"** (v10) — Hebrew הַרְבָּה אַרְבֶּה, the infinitive absolute with its verb, literally "multiplying I will multiply". זֶרַע *zera* = "seed", a singular: "it shall not be counted".
- **"Ishmael — Hebrew etymology preserved"** (v11) — Hebrew יִשְׁמָעֵאל *Yishma'el* = "El (God) hears" — verb *shama* (hear) + *El* (God). The narrative makes the wordplay explicit: "call his name *Yishma'el* because Yahweh has *shama* to your affliction." שָׁמַע אֶל = "listened to".
- **"wild man" → "wild donkey of a man"** (v12) — Hebrew פֶּרֶא אָדָם *pere adam*. *Pere* = wild ass / onager (cf. Job 39:5). "Wild ass of a man" — the Hebrew image is specifically of the free, undomesticable desert onager, not "wild man" in a generic sense.
- **"his hand will be against every man" → "his hand against all"** (v12) — Hebrew יָדוֹ בַכֹּל וְיַד כֹּל בּוֹ has no verb; בְּ is "in / against".
- **"dwell in the presence of all his brethren" → "before the face of all his brothers he shall abide"** (v12) — Hebrew עַל־פְּנֵי כָל־אֶחָיו יִשְׁכֹּן *al-pene kol-echaw yishkon*. *Al-pene* = "upon the face of" — ambiguous: "in the presence of / alongside" or "over against / in defiance of". Both are possible; the Hebrew is kept. יִשְׁכֹּן is שָׁכַן "abide", a different verb from יָשַׁב "dwell" (v3).
- **"Thou God seest me" → "You are El of seeing"** (v13) — Hebrew אַתָּה אֵל רֳאִי *Attah El Ro'i*. As the Masoretic Text points it, רֳאִי *ro'i* is a noun, "seeing / sight / appearance" (as 1 Sam 16:12): "El of seeing". It can mean the El who sees, or the El who lets himself be seen. With other vowels the same letters are "who sees me" (רֹאִי, as at the end of the verse), which is how the name is often translated. A name given to Yahweh by a human character — a foreign maidservant.
- **"Have I also here looked after him that seeth me?" → "Have I also here seen after the one who sees me?"** (v13) — Hebrew הֲגַם הֲלֹם רָאִיתִי אַחֲרֵי רֹאִי *ha-gam halom ra'iti acharey ro'i*, word for word: "Have-also here I-have-seen after my-seer?" The Hebrew is hard and is translated as it stands. Possible meanings:
  1. "Have I even here looked after (= caught sight of the back of) the one who sees me?" — cf. Ex 33:23, where Moses sees Yahweh's back (אֲחֹרָי).
  2. "Have I even here seen (and gone on seeing) after he saw me?" — that is, remained alive after the meeting (cf. 32:30; Ex 33:20). The words "and lived", which stood in this verse before, come from a proposed change of the Hebrew text and are not in the Masoretic Text.
- **"Beer-lahai-roi"** (v14) — Hebrew בְּאֵר לַחַי רֹאִי *Be'er Lachai Ro'i* = "well of the Living One who sees me" (or "well of the living one, my seer"). The meaning of the name is not written in the verse. קָרָא "one called" has no named subject: "was called". הִנֵּה "behold" is kept.
- **"fourscore and six years old" → "eighty-six years old"** (v16) — Hebrew בֶּן־שְׁמֹנִים שָׁנָה וְשֵׁשׁ שָׁנִים, literally "a son of eighty years and six years", the Hebrew way of giving an age.

### Notes

- **Hagar names Yahweh** — a human gives a name to God here, and it is a foreign, pregnant maidservant. Striking feature of the text usually under-noted.
- **Ishmael's destiny (v12)** — the imagery of the wild onager / desert nomad / independence fits historic Ishmaelite peoples (N. Arabian nomads). "Hand against all, the hand of all against him" — the proud, conflicted freedom of the desert.
- **Customs of surrogate motherhood** — the practice of the barren wife giving her slave to her husband to produce a child is attested in Nuzi and Mari texts (2nd millennium BCE), confirming the cultural realism of this episode.
