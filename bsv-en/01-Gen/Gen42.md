[1] And ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ saw that there was grain in ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧, and ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ said to his sons, "Why do you look at one another?"

[3] And the brothers of ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ went down, ten of them, to buy grain from ⟦egypt|Mitzrayim|Egypt⟧.

[4] But ⟦benjamin|Binyamin|Benjamin⟧, the brother of ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧, ⟦jacob|Yaaqov|Jacob⟧ did not send with his brothers, for he said, "Lest harm befall him."

[6] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧, he was the ruler over the land; he was the one selling grain to all the people of the land. And the brothers of ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ came and bowed down to him with their faces to the earth.

[7] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ saw his brothers and recognized them, and he made himself a stranger to them and spoke hard things with them. And he said to them, "From where have you come?" And they said, "From the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧, to buy food."

[8] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him.

[9] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ remembered the dreams that he had dreamed about them, and he said to them, "You are spies; to see the nakedness of the land you have come."

[10] And they said to him, "No, my lord; your servants have come to buy food.

[11] All of us are sons of one man. We are honest; your servants have not been spies."

[12] And he said to them, "No, but the nakedness of the land you have come to see."

[13] And they said, "Your servants are twelve brothers; we are sons of one man in the land of ⟦canaan|Kenaan|Canaan⟧. And behold, the youngest is with our father today, and the one is not."

[14] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ said to them, "It is what I spoke to you, saying, 'You are spies.'

[15] By this you shall be tested: by the life of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, you shall not go out from here unless your youngest brother comes here.

[16] Send one of you, and let him take your brother; and you, be bound, and let your words be tested, whether truth is with you. And if not, by the life of ⟦pharaoh|Paroh|Pharaoh⟧, surely you are spies."

[17] And he gathered them into custody for three days.

[18] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ said to them on the third day, "Do this and live; I fear ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[19] If you are honest, let one of your brothers be bound in the house of your custody; and you, go, bring grain for the hunger of your houses.

[20] And your youngest brother you shall bring to me, and your words will be proved true, and you will not die." And they did so.

[21] And they said, each man to his brother, "Truly we are guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the distress of his soul when he pleaded with us, and we did not hear. Therefore this distress has come to us."

[22] And ⟦reuben|Reuven|Reuben⟧ answered them, saying, "Did I not say to you, saying, 'Do not sin against the child'? And you did not hear. And also his blood, behold, it is required."

[23] And they did not know that ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ was hearing, for the interpreter was between them.

[24] And he turned away from them and wept. And he returned to them and spoke to them, and he took ⟦simeon|Shimon|Simeon⟧ from them and bound him before their eyes.

[25] And ⟦joseph|Yosef|Joseph⟧ commanded, and they filled their vessels with grain, and to return their silver, each man's to his sack, and to give them provisions for the way. And he did so for them.

[26] And they lifted their grain onto their donkeys and went from there.

[27] And the one opened his sack to give fodder to his donkey at the lodging place, and he saw his silver, and behold, it was in the mouth of his bag.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — Hebrew *Elohim*; in v18 with the article, הָאֱלֹהִים, placed first in its clause: "the *Elohim* I fear".
- **"corn" → "grain"** (v1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 19, 25, 26) — two Hebrew words: שֶׁבֶר (v1, 2, 19, 26) and בָּר (v3, 25). "Buy grain" (v2, 3, 5) and "selling grain" (v6) are the verb of the first word, שָׁבַר / הִשְׁבִּיר; in v7, 10 it has the object "food" (אֹכֶל), "to buy food".
- **"mischief" → "harm"** (v4, 38) — Hebrew אָסוֹן *ason* = a fatal accident, harm (Exod 21:22–23).
- **"governor" → "ruler"** (v6) — Hebrew הַשַּׁלִּיט.
- **"with their faces to the earth"** (v6) — Hebrew אַפַּיִם אָרְצָה, literally "nostrils to the earth".
- **"made himself strange" → "made himself a stranger"; "roughly" → "hard things"** (v7, 30) — Hebrew וַיִּתְנַכֵּר, from the same root as "recognized" (וַיַּכִּרֵם): a play on the two; קָשׁוֹת, "hard things".
- **"thy servants"** (v10, 11, 13) — Hebrew עֲבָדֶיךָ, the word for "slaves", as the polite way of speaking of oneself before a ruler.
- **"the nakedness of the land" kept** (v9, 12) — Hebrew עֶרְוַת הָאָרֶץ: its uncovered, undefended places.
- **"true men" → "honest"** (v11, 19, 31, 33, 34) — Hebrew כֵּנִים *kenim*, "right, honest"; "men" is not in the Hebrew.
- **"one is not" → "the one is not"** (v13, 32; also v36) — Hebrew הָאֶחָד אֵינֶנּוּ, literally so.
- **"By the life of Pharaoh ye shall not go forth" kept** (v15, 16) — Hebrew חֵי פַרְעֹה, "the life of Pharaoh", an oath; literally "by the life of Pharaoh, if you go out from here": the oath form אִם = "you shall not". In v16 "surely" is כִּי after the oath.
- **"kept in prison" → "be bound"** (v16, 19) — Hebrew אָסַר "bind", the same verb as in v24. "The house of your custody" (v19) is בֵּית מִשְׁמַרְכֶם. "Gathered" (v17) is וַיֶּאֱסֹף.
- **"the famine of your houses" → "the hunger of your houses"** (v19, 33) — Hebrew רַעֲבוֹן, a different word from "famine" (רָעָב). In v33 the Hebrew has only "and the hunger of your houses take"; "grain" (v19) is not repeated there and is not supplied.
- **"verified" → "proved true"** (v20) — Hebrew וְיֵאָמְנוּ, from the root of "faithful".
- **"one to another" → "each man to his brother"** (v21, 28) — Hebrew אִישׁ אֶל־אָחִיו.
- **"verily" → "truly"** (v21) — Hebrew אֲבָל.
- **"anguish of his soul … this distress" → "distress … distress"** (v21) — Hebrew צָרָה both times; נֶפֶשׁ "soul". "Pleaded" is הִתְחַנֵּן, "sought favor".
- **"would not hear" → "did not hear"** (v21, 22), **"understood" → "was hearing"** (v23) — Hebrew שָׁמַע "hear" all three times: the brothers did not hear, and Joseph hears (that is, understands their speech).
- **"the child" kept** (v22) — Hebrew הַיֶּלֶד.
- **"his blood is required" kept** (v22) — Hebrew דָּמוֹ … נִדְרָשׁ, "is sought", as 9:5.
- **"interpreter"** (v23) — Hebrew הַמֵּלִיץ, one who speaks between two tongues; a different word from "interpret" a dream (פָּתַר, ch 41).
- **"sacks" → "vessels"; "sack" / "bag"** (v25, 27, 28, 35) — Hebrew כְּלֵיהֶם "their vessels" (v25); שַׂק "sack" (v25, 27, 35); אַמְתַּחַת "bag" (v27, 28). In v25 "And he did so for them" is singular in the Hebrew.
- **"money" → "silver"** (v25, 27, 28, 35) — Hebrew כֶּסֶף *kesef*: silver by weight, here the price of the grain.
- **"their heart failed them" kept** (v28) — Hebrew וַיֵּצֵא לִבָּם, literally "their heart went out". "Trembled, each man to his brother" is literal.
- **"took us for spies" → "treated us as spies"** (v30) — Hebrew וַיִּתֵּן אֹתָנוּ כִּמְרַגְּלִים, literally "gave us as spies". "The lord of the land" (v30, 33) is אֲדֹנֵי הָאָרֶץ.
- **"traffick in the land" → "trade in the land"** (v34) — Hebrew תִּסְחָרוּ; the verb also means "go about".
- **"Me have ye bereaved of my children" → "Me you have bereaved"** (v36) — Hebrew אֹתִי שִׁכַּלְתֶּם; the verb means to make childless. "Upon me have all these things come" is literally "upon me have been all of them".
- **"Slay my two sons" → "My two sons you shall put to death"** (v37) — Hebrew תָּמִית. "Into my hand" is עַל־יָדִי, "upon my hand".
- **"if mischief befall him" → "should harm befall him"** (v38) — the Hebrew has no "if": "and harm befalls him … and you bring down".
- **"gray hairs with sorrow to the grave" → "gray hair in sorrow to Sheol"** (v38) — Hebrew שֵׂיבָתִי "my gray hair" (singular); בְּיָגוֹן; שְׁאוֹלָה "to Sheol", as in 37:35.

### Notes

- **Joseph's dreams fulfilled (v6, 9)** — his dreams of sheaves and stars bowing down (37:7, 9) come true, though his brothers do not know it. Joseph alone perceives the fulfillment; he "remembered" the dreams.
- **The brothers' admission (v21)** — more than two decades later they acknowledge guilt, and specifically the distress of Joseph as he pleaded with them — a detail the account in ch 37 did not give. Joseph hears it, though they think the interpreter stands between them (v23).
- **Joseph weeps** (v24) — the first of several times during the brothers' visits (42:24; 43:30; 45:2, 14–15; 46:29; 50:17).
