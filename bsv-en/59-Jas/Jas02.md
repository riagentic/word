# James 2

[1] My brothers, do you hold the faith of our Lord ⟦yeshua|Yeshua|Jesus⟧ ⟦mashiach|the Mashiach|Christ⟧ of glory with respect of persons?

[2] For if there comes into your synagogue a man with a gold ring, in bright clothing, and there comes in also a poor man in filthy clothing,

[3] and you look upon the one wearing the bright clothing and say, "You sit here well," and to the poor man you say, "You stand, or sit there under my footstool,"

[4] were you not divided in yourselves, and did you not become judges of evil reasonings?

[5] Hear, my beloved brothers. Did not ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ choose the poor as to the world, rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom which he promised to those who love him?

[6] But you dishonored the poor man. Do not the rich oppress you, and do not they themselves drag you into courts?

[7] Do not they themselves blaspheme the good name that was called upon you?

[8] If indeed you carry out the royal ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ according to the Scripture, "**You shall love your neighbor as yourself**," you do well;

[9] but if you have respect of persons, you work sin, being reproved by the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ as transgressors.

[10] For whoever keeps the whole ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ but stumbles in one thing has become guilty of all.

[11] For he who said, "**Do not commit adultery**," said also, "**Do not murder**." Now if you do not commit adultery but do murder, you have become a transgressor of the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧.

[12] So speak and so do, as those about to be judged through the ⟦torah|Torah|law⟧ of liberty.

[13] **For the judgment is without mercy to him who did not do mercy; mercy boasts against judgment.**

[14] What is the profit, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can the faith save him?

[15] If a brother or a sister are naked and lacking the daily food,

[16] and one of you says to them, "Go in peace, be warmed and be filled," but you do not give them the things needful for the body, what is the profit?

[17] So also faith, if it does not have works, is dead, by itself.

[18] But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith apart from the works, and I will show you the faith by my works.

[19] Do you believe that ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧ is one? You do well; the demons also believe, and shudder.

[20] But do you wish to know, O empty person, that faith apart from the works is idle?

[21] Was not ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ our father justified by works, having offered up ⟦isaac|Yitzchaq|Isaac⟧ his son upon the altar?

[22] You see that faith was working together with his works, and by the works faith was perfected,

[23] and the Scripture was fulfilled that says, "**And ⟦abraham|Avraham|Abraham⟧ believed ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness**," and he was called a friend of ⟦elohim|Elohim|God⟧.

[24] You see that by works a person is justified, and not by faith only.

[25] And likewise was not also ⟦rahab|Rachav|Rahab⟧ the prostitute justified by works, having received the messengers and sent them out by another way?

[26] For just as the body apart from spirit is dead, so also faith apart from works is dead.

## Changes

- **"God" → "*Elohim*"** (throughout) — systematic.
- **"Jesus Christ" → "*Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"** (v1) — systematic.
- **"have not the faith … with respect of persons" → "do you hold the faith … with respect of persons?"** (v1, 9) — Greek ἐν προσωπολημψίαις *en prosōpolēmpsiais*, plural, literally "in receivings of face": judging a person by his outward appearance. SBLGNT prints the verse as a question; with μή it expects the answer "no" ("you do not hold …, do you?").
- **NA28: a command** (v1) — NA28 punctuates the same words as a prohibition, "do not hold the faith … with respect of persons" (from memory).
- **"the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory" → "the faith of our Lord *Yeshua* the *Mashiach* of glory"** (v1) — Greek τὴν πίστιν τοῦ κυρίου ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ τῆς δόξης. A genitive, "faith of", not "faith in". The second "the Lord" is not in the Greek: "of glory" can go with "our Lord" ("our Lord of glory, *Yeshua* the *Mashiach*"), with the whole name, or mean "the Glory" as a name for him.
- **"assembly" → "synagogue"** (v2) — Greek συναγωγὴν ὑμῶν *synagōgēn hymōn*; "church" (ἐκκλησία) is in 5:14.
- **"goodly apparel" / "gay clothing" → "bright clothing"** (v2, 3) — Greek ἐσθῆτι λαμπρᾷ *esthēti lampra*, "bright, shining clothing"; one phrase in both verses.
- **"vile raiment" → "filthy clothing"** (v2) — Greek ἐν ῥυπαρᾷ ἐσθῆτι *en rhypara esthēti*; the root of "filthiness" (ῥυπαρία) in 1:21.
- **"have respect to" → "look upon"** (v3) — Greek ἐπιβλέψητε ἐπί.
- **"Sit thou here in a good place" → "You sit here well"** (v3) — Greek κάθου ὧδε καλῶς; καλῶς is the adverb "well" (as in v8, 19): "in a good place" or "please".
- **"Stand thou there, or sit here under my footstool" → "You stand, or sit there under my footstool"** (v3) — NA28 and SBLGNT στῆθι ἢ κάθου ἐκεῖ ὑπὸ τὸ ὑποπόδιόν μου; other witnesses have "stand there, or sit" (so NA27). "At my feet" is not what the Greek says.
- **"Are ye not then partial in yourselves" → "were you not divided in yourselves"** (v4) — Greek διεκρίθητε ἐν ἑαυτοῖς, the verb of 1:6 ("doubt"): "were you not divided (wavering) within yourselves" or "did you not make distinctions among yourselves". NA28 reads καὶ οὐ διεκρίθητε, with καί opening the main clause; no change of meaning.
- **"judges of evil thoughts" → "judges of evil reasonings"** (v4) — Greek κριταὶ διαλογισμῶν πονηρῶν, a genitive: judges marked by evil reasonings.
- **"the poor of this world rich in faith" → "the poor as to the world, rich in faith"** (v5) — NA28 τοὺς πτωχοὺς τῷ κόσμῳ, a dative: poor in relation to the world. The Greek has no "to be"; "rich in faith and heirs" can state what they were chosen to be, or what they are.
- **"despised" → "dishonored"** (v6) — Greek ἠτιμάσατε.
- **"draw you before the judgment seats" → "drag you into courts"** (v6) — Greek ἕλκουσιν ὑμᾶς εἰς κριτήρια.
- **"that worthy name by the which ye are called" → "the good name that was called upon you"** (v7) — Greek τὸ καλὸν ὄνομα τὸ ἐπικληθὲν ἐφʼ ὑμᾶς, the name spoken over a person to mark whose he is (Amos 9:12; Deut 28:10).
- **"If ye fulfil" → "If indeed you carry out"** (v8) — Greek εἰ μέντοι … τελεῖτε; μέντοι is "indeed, really" or "however". τελέω is "carry out to the end" ("fulfilled" in v23 is πληρόω); "keeps" in v10 is τηρέω.
- **"Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself" → "You shall love your neighbor as yourself"** (v8) — OT quotation (Lev 19:18).
- **"ye commit sin, and are convinced of the law" → "you work sin, being reproved by the law"** (v9) — Greek ἁμαρτίαν ἐργάζεσθε, ἐλεγχόμενοι (a participle): "reproved, shown to be in the wrong, convicted".
- **"offend in one point" → "stumbles in one thing"** (v10) — Greek πταίσῃ ἐν ἑνί; the verb of 3:2. "Guilty of all" (πάντων) is plural.
- **"Do not commit adultery … Do not kill" → "Do not commit adultery … Do not murder"** (v11) — Greek μὴ μοιχεύσῃς … μὴ φονεύσῃς; φονεύω is "murder" (Hebrew רָצַח *ratsach*), not all killing.
- **"so do" kept; "by the law of liberty" → "through the law of liberty"** (v12) — Greek ποιεῖτε; διὰ νόμου ἐλευθερίας, without the article; cf. 1:25.
- **"he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy" → "the judgment is without mercy to him who did not do mercy"** (v13) — Greek ἡ γὰρ κρίσις ἀνέλεος τῷ μὴ ποιήσαντι ἔλεος; no verb, and ποιέω "do" as in v12.
- **"mercy rejoiceth against judgment" → "mercy boasts against judgment"** (v13) — Greek κατακαυχᾶται, the verb of 3:14; the root of "boast" in 1:9.
- **"can faith save him?" → "Can the faith save him?"** (v14) — Greek ἡ πίστις, with the article pointing back: the faith just spoken of. The question (μή) expects the answer "no". Elsewhere in the chapter the article with "faith" (v17, 20, 22, 26) is not rendered.
- **"naked, and destitute of daily food" → "naked and lacking the daily food"** (v15) — Greek γυμνοί, "naked", also used of one poorly clothed; λειπόμενοι as 1:4–5.
- **"notwithstanding ye give them not" → "but you do not give them"** (v16) — Greek μὴ δῶτε, plural: the hearers, not only the speaker.
- **"is dead, being alone" → "is dead, by itself"** (v17) — Greek νεκρά ἐστιν καθʼ ἑαυτήν; the words stand last and can go with "dead" (dead in itself) or with "faith" (faith on its own).
- **"shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works" → "your faith apart from the works … the faith by my works"** (v18) — NA28 χωρὶς τῶν ἔργων … τὴν πίστιν, without "your" and "my". The Greek has no quotation marks; where the words of "someone" end is disputed.
- **"by works"** (v18, 21, 22, 24, 25) — Greek ἐκ, "out of, from".
- **"Thou believest that there is one God" → "Do you believe that *Elohim* is one?"** (v19) — Greek εἷς ἐστιν ὁ θεός (Deut 6:4); SBLGNT prints the clause as a question.
- **NA28: a statement** (v19) — NA28 punctuates "You believe that *Elohim* is one; you do well" (from memory).
- **"tremble" → "shudder"** (v19) — Greek φρίσσουσιν.
- **"O vain man" → "O empty person"** (v20) — Greek ὦ ἄνθρωπε κενέ.
- **"dead" → "idle"** (v20) — NA28 ἀργή, "not working" (ἀ + ἔργον), a play on "works"; KJV follows the reading νεκρά.
- **"when he had offered" → "having offered up"** (v21) — Greek ἀνενέγκας, a participle; so also v25.
- **"wrought with … made perfect" → "was working together with … was perfected"** (v22) — Greek συνήργει, imperfect, "was working together with" (again the root of "works"); ἐτελειώθη, the root of "perfect" (τέλειος, 1:4).
- **"Abraham believed God" → "And Abraham believed *Elohim*"** (v23) — OT quotation (Gen 15:6); Greek Ἐπίστευσεν δέ. "Imputed" → "reckoned" (ἐλογίσθη): the word of an account, "put to his credit"; the usual rendering of λογίζομαι, "consider", does not fit the passive with εἰς here.
- **"the Friend of God" → "a friend of *Elohim*"** (v23) — Greek φίλος θεοῦ, without the article.
- **"a man" → "a person"** (v24) — Greek ἄνθρωπος. "Not by faith only" kept: μόνον is an adverb.
- **"harlot" → "prostitute"** (v25) — Greek πόρνη. "Sent them out" is ἐκβαλοῦσα, literally "thrust out".
- **"without the spirit" → "apart from spirit"** (v26) — Greek χωρὶς πνεύματος, without the article: "spirit" or "breath". χωρίς is "apart from" throughout (v18, 20, 26).

### Notes
- **v8** quotes Lev 19:18.
- **v11** quotes Ex 20:13-14.
- **v23** quotes Gen 15:6.
